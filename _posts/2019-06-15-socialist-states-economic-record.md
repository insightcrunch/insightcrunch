The economic record of the state socialist countries is usually argued from numbers, and the numbers are where the argument should begin, because the figures quoted on every side of this dispute were never built to be compared with one another. The statistics of the Soviet bloc rested on a national accounting concept devised for a planned economy, a concept that counted a different thing from the gross domestic product used in market economies, priced that thing by administrative decision rather than by exchange, and concealed a portion of its own price movement inside the categories it reported. Independent reconstructions of the same economies, built by Western intelligence analysts, by an independent Soviet economist working from physical output, and by later international projects, produced results that diverge from the official series and from each other by margins large enough to reverse a verdict. A reader who has met contradictory growth claims about these economies has not met carelessness. The reader has met a measurement problem that sits underneath every assessment, and until that problem is stated plainly, no figure in this field means anything at all.

![A desk with statistical yearbooks, a physical output ledger and the measurement and comparison table, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-15.webp)

This article therefore proceeds in an order that polemics on both sides reverse. Measurement comes first, before any judgement of success or failure, because a judgement formed from series whose construction is unknown is not a judgement about an economy. What can be reasonably established comes second, stated as direction rather than as decimal, with the basis of each claim named beside it. The social indicators, including a reversal in life expectancy that any honest account must place near the centre, come third. The comparison problem comes fourth, and it is the article's centre of gravity: compared with what, over which years, from which starting level, measured how. The claim the whole structure supports can be stated in advance. The comparison base decides. Verdicts on this record vary less because scholars dispute the measurements, though they do, than because they select different countries and different dates to compare, and the two cases in which a single people was divided between the two systems supply the closest controls the subject allows. Everything that follows is an attempt to make that claim good, and to concede, along the way, everything the record genuinely concedes to its defenders.

## The economic record begins with an accounting problem

A national accounting system is not a neutral camera pointed at an economy. It is a set of decisions about what counts as production, what counts as a service worth recording, how the things counted are to be valued, and which movements of price are to be treated as changes in quantity. Market economies settled those decisions in one direction during the middle decades of the twentieth century, building the system of national accounts whose headline aggregate is gross domestic product. The Soviet bloc settled them in another direction, for reasons internal to planning itself: a state that allocates physical quantities has reason to count physical production, and less reason to build an elaborate statistical apparatus around activities it regards as redistribution rather than production. Neither system was designed to deceive the other. Each was designed to serve the administrative needs of the economy that built it. The deception enters later, when readers on either side lift a figure from one system and set it against a figure from the other as though the two were readings from a single instrument.

The consequences of that habit are not minor. A growth comparison between a net material product series and a gross domestic product series compares a part with a whole, since the first excludes most services and the second includes them, and in economies where services expand as income rises the exclusion alone bends the comparison. A level comparison inherits the price problem: values aggregated at administratively fixed prices record the planner's valuations, not the relative scarcities that market prices, whatever their distortions, at least attempt to register. A welfare comparison inherits both problems and adds a third, since the things households actually consumed were rationed by availability as much as by income, so that money magnitudes in either system understate the distance between nominal provision and goods in hand. None of this makes assessment impossible. It makes assessment conditional, and the conditions have to be carried in the open, attached to each claim, rather than buried in a footnote that the headline figure then escapes.

### Why do the growth figures for these economies differ so much?

because they were built differently. Official series used a national accounting concept excluding most services, with administratively set prices and hidden inflation. Western reconstructions and independent estimates using physical indicators produced substantially different results. The dispersion is methodological rather than factual.

That answer deserves expansion, because each of its clauses conceals a literature. The exclusion of services was not an oversight but a doctrine: the material product concept descended from a reading of Marx in which value originates in material production, so that transport of goods counted while transport of passengers largely did not, and wholesale and retail trade counted as a continuation of production while health, education, housing services, finance, public administration, and defence were treated as claims upon the material product rather than additions to it. The administrative price system was likewise structural. Prices were set by committees, held for long periods, and used as accounting conventions and instruments of policy, subsidising bread and housing, taxing consumer manufactures through turnover taxes, and bearing no systematic relation to cost or scarcity. Hidden inflation followed from the interaction of fixed official prices with the enterprise's incentive to present novelty: a product declared new could carry a new price, and the statistical apparatus, recording quantities at the prices assigned, converted part of what was price increase into apparent output growth. Each mechanism is examined in its own section below. Together they explain why honest scholars, given the same underlying reality, published numbers that cannot be reconciled, and why the argument about the record so often degenerates into rival figures whose provenance neither side states.

There is a further preliminary that belongs here rather than later, because it disciplines everything after it. This article asserts no growth rates, no output levels, no life expectancy figures, no investment shares, and no income comparisons anywhere in its pages. That abstention is deliberate and it is not evasion. Where the underlying series diverge as widely as these do, a precise figure performs a confidence the evidence cannot support, and the performance is the standard vice of writing on this subject, in apologetics and in denunciation alike. Every empirical claim in what follows is therefore directional: it states that something rose, fell, stagnated, widened, or narrowed, over a stated period, on a stated measurement basis, against a stated comparison. A reader who wants decimals knows where the reconstructions live, and this article names them. A reader who wants to know what can responsibly be said without them is the reader this article is for.

## Net material product: counting production and excluding services

The material product system, used across the Soviet bloc with national variations, took as its object the net material product: the value of output produced in the material sphere, meaning industry, agriculture, construction, freight transport, and the productive portion of trade, after deducting the material inputs consumed in producing it. The deduction matters. Net material product is a net concept, net of intermediate consumption and of depreciation in its usual presentation, whereas gross domestic product is gross of depreciation though net of intermediates in its value added construction. Comparing the two aggregates without adjustment thus stacks a coverage difference on top of a netting difference, and early popular comparisons did exactly that, in both directions, depending on which error flattered the author's side.

The exclusions deserve to be listed in prose because their cumulative weight is the point. A surgeon's work did not add to the measured product, though the manufacture of her instruments did. A teacher's work did not add, though the printing of textbooks did. The carrying of passengers by rail or bus was treated as a service consumed out of the product rather than as production, while the carrying of coal was production itself. Rents, banking, insurance, legal services, the civil service, and the armed forces stood outside the aggregate. In an economy at an early stage of industrialisation, where the material sphere dominates employment, the exclusion distorts less. In a maturing economy, where health, education, and administration absorb a growing share of the labour force, the exclusion grows with the very development the figures are meant to track, and measured growth is correspondingly understated relative to a gross domestic product basis even before any question of falsification arises. Defenders of the official series made this point with justice, and it is one of the few points in the whole dispute that cuts in the official series' favour.

### What did net material product leave out of the economy it measured?

It left out most services: the clinic and the classroom, the rented flat, the passenger train, the bank, the ministry, and the army were all treated as redistribution of the material product rather than as production, so the aggregate counted a shrinking share of activity as those sectors grew with development.

The doctrine behind the boundary was not arbitrary, and understanding it prevents a common misreading. Within the framework the bloc's economists inherited, productive labour was labour that issued in a material object or in the movement and storage of material objects toward their use. Services were understood as maintained out of the surplus generated in the material sphere, in the way that a household's domestic labour is maintained out of wages without entering the wage accounts. One can reject the doctrine, and most Western economists did, while recognising that it produced a coherent accounting system rather than a fraud. The fraud question is separate, and it is taken up later in this article, where the answer turns out to be more interesting than either side's slogan. What the boundary produced, fraud or no fraud, was an aggregate that answered a planner's question, how much material output the economy commands, rather than the question a later reader asks of it, how well the economy performed by the standards of a different system.

National variations complicate the picture in ways that reward a sentence. The East European states adapted the material product system to their own statistical traditions, and several maintained parallel calculations on Western lines for internal or comparative use, so that the bloc's statistics were never as monolithic as outside commentary assumed. Yugoslavia, outside the bloc's discipline after its break with Moscow, developed its own conventions again. China, during the decades when it followed the Soviet model, used the material product system and later reconstructed its earlier series when it adopted the Western framework, an exercise that exposed, in the most concrete way available, how much of any growth story is an artefact of the framework chosen. The reconstructions performed at that transition are among the most instructive documents in the field, since the same statistical service measured the same economy twice and published both answers.

The practical consequence for a reader is a rule rather than a number. Whenever a growth claim about these economies is stated without its accounting basis, the claim is incomplete in a way that no precision can repair, and the first question to ask of it is not whether it is true but what it counts. That rule sounds elementary. Applied consistently, it dissolves a large fraction of the published argument on this subject, including much of the argument conducted at the highest levels of Cold War controversy, where aggregates built on unlike concepts were deployed as though they were laps in a single race. The race metaphor did real damage. It encouraged readers to treat two accounting conventions as two runners, and to ask who was winning, when the prior question, what distance each runner's course actually measures, had never been put.

## Prices set by committee: valuation without scarcity

An aggregate of values is only as informative as the prices that compose it. In a market economy, prices are noisy, manipulated, and distorted by monopoly, taxation, and fashion, yet they carry one indispensable kind of information: they move, however imperfectly, with the relative scarcity of the things priced, because sellers who misjudge scarcity lose money and buyers who ignore it pay more than they must. In the planned economies, industrial prices were set by state committees, often on a cost plus convention, and then held constant for years at a time, sometimes across an entire span between price revisions. The resulting valuations recorded what the price authority had decided a thing should cost, at the moment of the last revision, under the policy preferences then prevailing. They did not record what the thing was worth relative to everything else the economy might have produced instead, because no mechanism existed that could have generated that information in priced form.

The distortions this introduced were systematic rather than random, and their direction can be described without figures. Goods whose prices had been set low relative to cost, whether from subsidy or from the inertia of an old price list, appeared in the aggregates at less than the resources they absorbed. Goods carrying heavy turnover taxes, a category that fell disproportionately on consumer manufactures, appeared at more. An economy shifting resources toward the undervalued categories would show less growth in value terms than the same physical shift deserved, while an economy expanding its taxed consumer sector would show more. Since the planned economies were, across most of their history, shifting resources toward heavy industry and away from consumption, the interaction between the price structure and the growth path was not a wash. It was a standing invitation to misread the aggregates, and scholars who reconstructed the series at alternative price weights, an exercise associated above all with the index number problem discussed below, obtained materially different growth paths from identical physical data.

The index number problem deserves its own paragraph, because it is the point where the price difficulty becomes a theorem rather than a complaint. When quantities and prices both change, the measured growth of an economy depends on whether the quantities are weighted by the prices of the starting year or of the ending year. Where relative prices and relative quantities move in opposite directions, as they characteristically do during industrialisation, with the fast growing goods becoming relatively cheaper, starting year weights yield faster measured growth than ending year weights. This is sometimes called the Gerschenkron effect, after the economic historian Alexander Gerschenkron, who identified it in the Soviet case: the official practice of valuing output at the prices of an early base year flattered the growth of precisely those branches, machinery above all, whose relative prices fell as they expanded. No dishonesty is required. The arithmetic of index numbers does the work, and it does it in any economy, which is why the same effect appears in the early industrial statistics of market economies that happened to use early weights. What distinguishes the Soviet case is the scale of the structural change being measured and the length of time the early weights were retained.

### Why do administratively set prices defeat direct comparison with market economies?

Because a value aggregate inherits the valuations of its prices. Prices fixed by committee and held for years record administrative decisions and policy preferences, not relative scarcity, so the same physical output composes into a different total, and growth weighted at those prices follows a different path.

The reader should notice what this section has and has not claimed. It has not claimed that market prices are true values, a proposition no serious economist holds. It has claimed that two aggregates built from incommensurable valuation systems cannot be set side by side as measurements of one quantity, and that the failure to observe this elementary point accounts for a share of the dispute about the record that has nothing to do with ideology. A comparison can still be made, but it has to be made deliberately: either by revaluing one economy's output at the other's prices, with the Gerschenkron effect then appearing in mirror image, or by abandoning value aggregates for the physical and welfare indicators examined later in this article. Each route has costs. The honest literature is the literature that pays them visibly.

## Hidden inflation: the new product that was not new

The third measurement mechanism is the most concrete of the three, and the one most often mistaken for simple falsification. Enterprises in the planned economies were judged, rewarded, and financed against plan targets expressed substantially in value terms. An enterprise that could raise the recorded value of its output without raising its physical output thereby improved its standing at no cost in effort or materials. The readiest instrument was the new product. A machine, a fabric, a shoe, resubmitted with a minor alteration, a changed assortment code, a decorative variation, could be registered as a novel item and assigned a new and higher price, on the argument that novelty commands a premium during the period before its price is normalised into the list. Statistical practice then recorded the higher priced item as additional real output, since the price indices, built from the prices of items deemed unchanged, registered no inflation in the category. The increase was real neither as quantity nor as quality. It was a price rise wearing the costume of growth, and Western analysts gave it the name it still carries: hidden inflation.

The mechanism's importance lies in its distribution. It was not a directive from a ministry ordering statisticians to lie, though pressure on statistical organs existed and is documented. It was an emergent property of the incentive structure, replicated by thousands of enterprise directors each responding rationally to the indicators by which they were judged, and it is therefore better evidence about the system than any single act of fabrication would be. A measurement regime that rewards the appearance of growth will be supplied with appearances, in quantities that grow with the pressure applied. The same logic, in fairness, operates wherever indicators govern rewards, and market economies know it in their own statistical controversies. What differed in the planned case was the absence of a countervailing check: no competing statistical service, no opposition press with access to the raw returns, no academic community free to publish a rival series under its own name, until the one remarkable exception examined in a later section.

### How did hidden inflation enter the official series without any order to falsify?

Through the new product. An existing item resubmitted with a minor alteration could be registered as novel and priced higher, while price indices tracked only items deemed unchanged, so the increase was recorded as real output growth rather than as the price rise it substantially was.

Estimating the size of the hidden component is where the reconstructions diverge most sharply, and this article follows its protocol in declining to state a magnitude. What can be said directionally is that the phenomenon was recognised inside the system as well as outside it, that Soviet economists themselves debated the pricing of new products in print within the limits permitted, and that any reconstruction which takes official prices at face value for the later decades, when the easy gains from structural change were exhausted and the pressure on indicators intensified, will overstate real growth by some margin that the rival methods bound from different sides. The official series are therefore best read as an upper bound constructed by a particular method, not as a fabrication and not as a measurement. That formulation, an upper bound by construction, is the one the evidence supports, and it is more damaging to naive uses of the figures than the cruder charge of invention, because it survives the demonstration that the statisticians were, in the main, doing their jobs as their instructions defined them.

There is a human corollary worth recording before the article moves to the reconstructions themselves. The statisticians of the bloc were professionals working inside a conceptual scheme they had inherited and a political environment that punished certain results. Some falsified under pressure, particularly at the level of enterprise and regional reporting, where plan fulfilment determined careers. Some resisted at personal cost. The aggregate picture, in which method matters more than mendacity, should not be allowed to launder the cases where returns were invented outright, any more than those cases should be allowed to stand for the whole. Precision about where distortion entered, at the price committee, at the index, at the enterprise return, is what separates an account of the measurement problem from a morality tale about it, and this article aims at the account.

## The Western reconstructions: rebuilding the accounts from outside

Faced with series they could not use directly, Western scholars and intelligence analysts rebuilt the Soviet accounts from the components up. The enterprise is associated in its founding generation with Abram Bergson, whose reconstruction of Soviet national income on Western concepts set the methodological standard: take the published physical and value data, reclassify them into the categories of the Western system, add the excluded services, adjust the prices where adjustment was possible, and present the result with its assumptions exposed. The intelligence agencies institutionalised the work, maintaining large analytical staffs whose estimates of Soviet output and growth informed Western policy across the Cold War. These reconstructions were serious scholarship by any standard that can be applied to them. They were also, unavoidably, built substantially from the official data they sought to correct, since no alternative census of the Soviet economy existed, and that dependence is the hinge on which the later criticism of them turns.

The reconstructions converged on a picture that differed from the official one in a consistent direction. Growth, on the rebuilt series, was slower than the official series claimed, in some periods much slower, while remaining, for the early decades, rapid by any international standard then available. The level of output per head was lower relative to the leading Western economy than official comparisons implied, and the gap in consumption per head was wider than the gap in production, reflecting the investment and defence claims on output that the reconstructions made visible. For a generation this rebuilt picture was the working consensus of Western Sovietology, cited by critics of the system as the sober truth behind the propaganda and resented by defenders of the record as a hostile construction. Both receptions missed the methodological point that the reconstruction's own authors stated plainly: the estimates carried wide margins, rested on judgement at every stage of adjustment, and were offered as the best available reading of defective data rather than as measurements in the laboratory sense.

### What did the Western intelligence reconstructions actually do to the Soviet data?

They rebuilt the accounts on Western concepts. Analysts reclassified the published material, restored the excluded services, adjusted prices where adjustment was possible, and presented the results with assumptions stated, producing growth slower than the official series and levels lower than official comparisons implied.

Criticism of the reconstructions came, in time, from two directions that rarely agree about anything else. From one side, scholars sympathetic to the record argued that the adjustments were applied asymmetrically, that quality change in Western output was credited generously while quality change in Soviet output was discounted, and that the choice of price weights for the rebuilt aggregates imported the Gerschenkron effect in reverse, systematically depressing the measured Soviet path. From the other side, and more consequentially, critics argued after the archives opened that the reconstructions had erred in the opposite direction: that by building on official physical data they had absorbed the overstatements embedded in those data, and that the Soviet economy had in fact been smaller and its growth slower than the intelligence estimates held. The second criticism gained force from the retrospective judgements of the estimators themselves, several of whom acknowledged that the enterprise and regional reporting on which the physical series rested had been more corrupt than the reconstruction methods assumed. An estimate can thus have been simultaneously the best available and substantially wrong, and the history of these reconstructions is the standing illustration of that uncomfortable combination.

### Why were the intelligence estimates later criticised as having been too high?

Because they were built from official physical data whose overstatement they could not fully purge. Enterprise and regional reporting had inflated the underlying returns, so a reconstruction that corrected concepts and prices still inherited corrupted quantities, and the economy proved smaller than the estimates suggested.

The episode carries a lesson that this article will meet again in other forms, and it is worth naming here in its local shape. The direction of an estimate's error is not settled by the politics of the estimator. The intelligence reconstructions were produced by institutions with every incentive, in the cruder tellings, to exaggerate the adversary's strength for budgetary reasons and equally, in other tellings, to minimise it for reasons of reassurance. What the record shows is a professional apparatus producing estimates that were methodologically serious, broadly right about direction and deceleration, and wrong about level in the direction of generosity to the system they were measuring. Readers who assume that an estimate's provenance determines its bias will misread this episode, and will misread most of the others in the field, where the scholar who produced the lowest growth estimates of all was a Soviet economist working in Moscow with no access to Western data and no protection beyond his professional reputation. His work is the subject of the section after next. Before it, the article pauses to set out its single table, in which the measurement choices reviewed so far, and the comparison choices reviewed later, are gathered into one findable object.

## The measurement and comparison table

The measurement and comparison table gathers the choices on which every verdict about this record silently depends. Each row names a choice, states what the choice is, describes how it moves the result, and identifies the claim the choice tends to support. A reader equipped with the table can interrogate any figure offered in this dispute by asking which row the figure has suppressed.

| Choice | What the choice is | How it affects the result | Which claim it supports |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The accounting concept | Net material product, excluding most services, or gross domestic product, including them | The narrower concept understates the weight of the growing service sectors and is not commensurable with the broader one | Whichever side compares its preferred aggregate against the other system without adjustment |
| The price basis | Administratively fixed prices, early base year weights, or market prices of a stated year | Early weights flatter the growth of branches whose relative prices fall as they expand; administrative prices record policy rather than scarcity | The official growth path, when early weights are retained; the critical path, when the effect is reversed without comment |
| Hidden inflation | Price increases registered as new products, or purged by physical indicators | Unpurged, it converts part of price movement into apparent real growth, increasingly in the later decades | The official series read as measurement rather than as an upper bound |
| The reconstruction method | Official series, intelligence reconstruction, physical indicator reconstruction, or international project estimates | Each method corrects some distortions and inherits others, and the spread between them is large | The verdict selected in advance, since a method can be found to suit most verdicts |
| The comparison base | The starting point, Western Europe, countries at comparable levels, or the divided countries | The verdict reverses as the base changes, more completely than it responds to any measurement correction | The comparison base decides, and accounts on both sides choose their base rather than defending it |

The table's last row is the article's claim in tabular form, and the sections on comparison below are devoted to it. The earlier rows are not preliminaries to be cleared away before the real argument starts. They are the reason the real argument takes the shape it does. Because measurement disputes, though genuine, move the verdict within a range, while comparison choices move it across the whole distance between triumph and failure, the centre of gravity of any honest assessment sits in the comparison rows rather than the measurement rows. That ordering surprises readers formed by the polemical literature, in which statistics are accused and defended as though the verdict hung on their integrity. It hangs, far more, on a choice that is rarely even acknowledged to be a choice.

## Khanin and the physical indicators: the reconstruction from inside

The most damaging reconstruction of the Soviet growth record was produced neither in Washington nor in any Western university. It was produced in Novosibirsk and Moscow by Grigory Khanin, a Soviet economist who, distrusting the value aggregates for reasons internal to his own professional experience of how they were compiled, rebuilt the growth path from physical indicators: tonnages, freight movement, energy output, and the other material series that an economy cannot easily conjure on paper because they are constrained by one another. A fabricated value can float free. A fabricated tonnage collides with the freight statistics, the energy balance, and the input returns of the industries that supposedly consumed it, and the collisions are detectable by a sufficiently patient reader of the system's own publications. Khanin's patience was legendary among those who knew the work. His reward, across long stretches of his career, was the near impossibility of publishing his results in his own country, where they circulated in the twilight of typescript and seminar discussion before reaching print in the years of openness at the system's end.

His conclusions, stated directionally as this article's protocol requires, were that Soviet growth across the postwar decades had been far slower than the official series claimed, slower than the Western reconstructions allowed, and in some periods close to stagnation well before stagnation became the acknowledged condition of the final decade. The early industrialisation decades survived his method in better shape than the later ones, a pattern the reader will meet repeatedly: the physical record of the first transformation is corroborated from too many independent directions to be argued away, while the claims of the mature system rest on precisely the value aggregates his method bypassed. Khanin's work thus cuts against both polemical camps at once. Against the official account, it removes the growth miracle of the postwar decades. Against the crudest critical account, it confirms that something real and large happened in the decades of industrialisation, because the physical transformation is there in his own tables.

### What method did Khanin use to rebuild the growth record from inside the system?

He abandoned the value aggregates and rebuilt the path from physical indicators, tonnages, freight, and energy, whose mutual consistency constrains fabrication. Cross checking the material series against one another exposed overstatement that value series, compiled from the same reporting chain, could not reveal.

The reception of Khanin's work is itself evidence in the measurement dispute. Western scholars who had built careers on the reconstruction enterprise received his far lower path with a scepticism that was partly methodological, since physical indicators carry their own biases, overweighting the heavy industries whose output is easiest to count and underweighting quality change and new goods, and partly, one may suspect, institutional, since accepting his figures meant accepting that the established estimates had been generous for decades. Later assessments, informed by archival access, moved the scholarly centre of gravity toward his end of the range for the postwar period without endorsing every element of his construction. The episode is the seventh instance material of this article in miniature, and it generalises: in this field, the estimate that survives is rarely the one produced by the best funded apparatus. It is the one whose method exposes its own weaknesses most completely, because those are the weaknesses the next generation of evidence will test.

A note on sources belongs in this section, since the brief for this article requires it and the reader deserves it here rather than in a footnote. Much of the statistical debate, including the fullest statements of the physical indicator method and the replies it provoked from within Soviet economics, exists only in Russian. What is available in English is substantial but partial: the principal Western reconstructions were published in English with their methods exposed; summaries and discussions of the internal Soviet debate appeared in translation journals during the system's final years and in the scholarly literature after its end; the international projects described next publish in English as a matter of design. A reader confined to English can therefore follow the dispute's structure and its main results, but should know that the archive of the argument, the journal exchanges in which Soviet statisticians defended and qualified their own practice, remains largely untranslated, and that English language certainty about this record should be discounted accordingly. Untranslated is not a synonym for minor. In this field it is close to a synonym for primary.

## The international projects: a third generation of estimates

A third generation of reconstruction followed the first two, differing from both in organisation as much as in method. Where the intelligence estimates had been the work of a state apparatus and Khanin's the work of an individual, the later projects were collaborative and international, assembling scholars from the successor scholarly communities and from Western universities to rebuild the historical statistics of the planned economies on common definitions. The historical statistics enterprise associated with Angus Maddison, which reconstructed output series for the world's economies across very long spans, incorporated the Soviet and East European cases into a single comparative frame, adjusting the bloc's data toward gross domestic product concepts and publishing the adjustments for inspection. Successor projects in economic history, including collaborative volumes on the economic history of the Soviet Union and of Eastern Europe written after the archives opened, refined the picture industry by industry and period by period. These bodies of work continue, and this article describes them as bodies of work rather than as settled authorities, because their estimates remain under revision as archival series are published and methods are contested within them.

### What did the later international reconstruction projects add to the dispute?

A common frame. By rebuilding the bloc's series on shared definitions alongside the series of market economies, and by publishing their adjustments for inspection, they made the comparison problem visible as a problem, rather than leaving each side with a private set of numbers.

The mature scholarly picture that emerges from the three generations, stated with the caution the dispersion imposes, can be summarised in a sentence before the article turns to substance. The official series overstated growth, increasingly over time. The first Western reconstructions corrected the overstatement partially and inherited more of it than their authors knew. The physical indicator reconstruction probably understated some elements of the early transformation while coming closest on the postwar deceleration. The international projects place the record inside a comparative frame in which its shape, rapid early growth from a low base, long deceleration, terminal stagnation, is not in serious dispute, while its magnitudes remain contestable in every period. Shape agreed, magnitudes disputed, verdict still to come: that is the measurement problem's honest residue, and it is smaller, and more useful, than the fog from which the article began.

It is worth pausing on the word shape, because it carries the transition to the next part of the article. A shape can be established by methods that disagree about everything else, in the way that witnesses who dispute distances may agree that a road descends. The descent of the growth path, from the steep early decades through the long shallowing to the flat final years, is confirmed by the official series themselves in their later publications, by every reconstruction, by the physical indicators, and by the testimony of the system's own reformers, who did not invent the deceleration they were trying to reverse. What the shape cannot tell the reader is whether the journey was an achievement or a failure. For that, the levels, the costs, the provisions, and above all the comparisons are required, and the article now turns to what is reasonably established, keeping the measurement basis of each claim in view as it goes.

## What is reasonably established: the industrial transformation

Begin with the transformation itself, because it is the fact that every account, however hostile, must somehow accommodate. Between the late 1920s and the 1950s the Soviet Union, and after the war the states of Eastern Europe on a compressed timetable, converted predominantly agrarian economies into industrial ones. The physical evidence is of a kind that no statistical controversy reaches. Cities that had been provincial towns became centres of metallurgy and machine building. Regions that had never known a railway were crossed by lines built to serve new industrial complexes. A generation that had drawn water and ploughed with horses staffed power stations, steelworks, and aircraft plants. The output of the heavy branches, steel, coal, electricity, machinery, rose across the period by multiples that the physical series of every reconstruction register, whatever they dispute about the aggregates. The pace of the structural change, the movement of population from village to town and from agriculture to industry, stands among the fastest sustained transformations of its kind in the historical record, comparable in speed to the most rapid industrialisations elsewhere and achieved, unlike most of them, without a prior accumulation of commercial wealth and without colonies.

The method of the transformation is as well established as its scale, and it belongs in any honest statement of the record because it prices the achievement. The investment claim on output was held at levels that market economies have rarely sustained outside wartime, and the counterpart of that claim was consumption held down with deliberation: the industrialisation was financed, in the main, out of the current consumption of the population that performed it, and above all out of the countryside, whose produce was extracted at prices set to transfer resources to the towns. Agriculture was collectivised in the Soviet case with a violence whose human cost is part of the record this article is assessing, not an external moral footnote to it, since the famine that accompanied collectivisation in the grain regions belongs to the economic history of the transformation as directly as the steel output does. The transformation's defenders and its critics divide over how that cost should be entered in the ledger, and over whether alternative paths to industrialisation were open. They do not divide, in the serious literature, over whether the transformation occurred.

### What is reasonably established about the industrialisation of the late 1920s to the 1950s?

That the transformation was real, swift, and centred on heavy industry. Tonnages, freight, and power output corroborate it from every side, at a pace that ranks with the fastest sustained industrialisations anywhere, paid for by investment held at extraordinary levels and consumption held down to match.

Two qualifications keep the statement honest, and both come from within the scholarship rather than from polemic. The first concerns the starting point: the Russian Empire of 1913, the conventional base, was not the stagnant backwater of the cruder accounts, but an economy already industrialising at a respectable pace in its final decades, with modern large scale industry in its western regions and a railway network among the world's largest. Measuring the transformation from 1913 therefore credits the Soviet period with a discontinuity that is partly real, the acceleration and the change of structure, and partly an artefact of the base year's position in a longer industrialisation that the revolution interrupted and then resumed on new terms. The second qualification concerns war. The period contains the most destructive invasion in modern history, which annihilated a large share of the capital stock and population of the western Soviet Union, and then a reconstruction that restored output to prewar levels with a speed that itself belongs in the record. Growth measured across a span containing that destruction and recovery is not commensurable with peacetime growth elsewhere, in either direction: the destruction depresses the average, the recovery inflates the postwar segment, and any single figure for the whole span conceals both. Directional statements survive these qualifications. Point estimates do not, which is why this article makes none.

The East European cases replicate the pattern with instructive variations. States that entered the bloc already industrialised, notably the Czech lands and the eastern territories of Germany, experienced the transformation as a reorientation and a change of ownership more than as a creation of industry, and their subsequent records cannot be credited with an industrialisation they did not need. States at the Balkan level of development experienced something closer to the Soviet sequence, compressed into fewer years and executed with the Soviet template applied to economies whose structures differed from the template's assumptions. The variation matters for the comparison sections below, because it warns against treating the bloc as one economy with one record. There were several records, sharing institutions and a measurement system, diverging in starting point and therefore in what the shared institutions can be credited or charged with.

## Extensive growth: the engine and its limit

The growth the system achieved was, in the term the literature settled on, extensive. It was obtained by mobilising more inputs: more labour drawn from the countryside and from women entering industrial employment, more capital installed in new plant, more land brought under cultivation, more raw materials extracted from an expanding territorial frontier of mines and wells. Output rose because the quantities of everything employed rose. What rose far less, and in the later decades barely at all, was the output obtained from a given combination of inputs, the efficiency term that economists call total factor productivity and that lay language calls working smarter rather than harder. The distinction is not a refinement. It is the key to the record's shape, because extensive growth carries its limit inside itself. A country has only so many peasants to move, so many women to draw into the workforce, so much virgin land, and an investment share that cannot rise forever against the resistance of a population whose consumption is the residual. When the reserves are exhausted, extensive growth does not slow gracefully. It approaches a wall.

### What does it mean to call the growth of these economies extensive?

It means output rose by employing more inputs, more labour, more capital, more land and materials, rather than by obtaining more output from given inputs. The method is powerful while reserves of unused inputs last and self exhausting when they run out.

The Soviet economy met its wall in stages that the literature dates with reasonable agreement. The rural labour reserve, swollen at the start by a countryside holding far more people than its techniques required, was drawn down across the postwar decades until the flow to the towns thinned and the remaining agricultural workforce aged. The participation of women reached levels among the highest recorded anywhere, leaving no comparable reserve behind it. The extensive margin in land closed with the steppe campaigns whose ecological costs the companion environmental assessment records in full. Capital deepening continued, but each increment of new plant yielded less additional output than the increment before, a pattern visible in every reconstruction and consistent with an economy installing capital in the branches it already possessed rather than in the branches a changing economy needed. The growth engine did not break. It ran out of fuel of the only kind it could burn, and the system's long failure to build another engine, one driven by efficiency and innovation in civilian production, is the analytical core of the deceleration to which the article now turns.

There is a defence of extensive growth that deserves its paragraph, because the affirmative case at full strength, presented later, builds on it. For an economy at the Soviet starting point, extensive growth was not a primitive substitute for the real thing. It was the historically normal road: the industrialisation of Britain, of Germany, of the United States, and later of Japan and Korea all ran for generations on the mobilisation of labour and capital, with efficiency gains riding on the structural change itself as workers moved from low productivity agriculture to higher productivity industry. Judged against that company, the Soviet method differs in its instruments, coercion where others used wage incentives and enclosures, and in its speed, rather than in its extensive character. The difference that proved fatal was not the choice of extensive growth at the start. It was the failure to accomplish the transition that the successful cases accomplished, from mobilisation to efficiency, when mobilisation reached its limits. That failure, not the starting method, is where the record's critics and its honest defenders can meet, and several of the most careful scholars on each side do meet there.

## The deceleration: the fact every series records

From the 1960s onward, on every series that exists, the growth of the Soviet economy slowed. The official series record the deceleration. The intelligence reconstructions record it more steeply. The physical indicator reconstruction records it earliest and most severely. The international projects confirm its shape. Deceleration, sustained across three decades and ending in the stagnation of the 1980s, is the single most robust finding in the entire field, robust precisely because it does not depend on the level disputes that divide the reconstructions: methods that disagree about how fast the economy grew in any given period agree that it grew more slowly in each successive period than in the one before. The East European economies trace the same arc with national variations, several of them sustaining their figures in the 1970s by borrowing abroad, a expedient that converted a growth problem into a debt problem and thereby dated the reckoning rather than escaping it.

### On what do all the rival series and reconstructions agree about the record?

On the shape. Growth was rapid in the early decades, decelerated steadily from the 1960s on every series including the official one, and stagnated in the 1980s. The methods dispute magnitudes in every period while agreeing on the direction of the path and on its long shallowing.

The causes of the deceleration are debated with an intensity that the fact itself escapes, and this article's scope is the record rather than the collapse, [whose explanations have their own article](/2019/05/01/soviet-collapse-marxist-explanations/) in this series. What belongs here is the connection between the deceleration and the measurement problem, which is tighter than readers usually notice. A system growing extensively can report its slowdown honestly only if its statistics register efficiency, and the material product apparatus, built to count physical production in the branches the plan prioritised, was structurally better at registering mobilisation than at registering its exhaustion. The slowdown therefore appeared in the official figures later and gentler than it appeared in the lived experience of shortage and queue, and the gap between the reported economy and the experienced economy became, in the final decades, a political fact in its own right. Citizens who are told that output rises while the shops empty draw conclusions about more than statistics. The credibility of the entire informational order of the system was among the casualties of the deceleration, and that casualty is part of the record too, though it appears in no table.

A comparative observation closes the section, because it disciplines the temptation to treat deceleration as a uniquely socialist pathology. Growth slowed across the industrial world in the 1970s, in economies of every institutional type, as the postwar reconstruction boom ended and energy prices convulsed. The socialist deceleration began earlier, proceeded further, and ended in stagnation rather than in the slower but positive growth the market economies resumed, and those differences of degree accumulate into a difference of kind. But the honest comparison, which the later sections formalise, is not between a decelerating planned economy and an imaginary market economy growing forever at postwar rates. It is between two decelerations, one of which bottomed out at stagnation and systemic crisis while the other did not. Precision about the counterfactual is the comparison problem arriving early, as it tends to do in this subject, where every apparently factual section turns out to have a comparison concealed inside it.

## Basic provision: the achievement that was not propaganda

Against the deceleration stands a body of achievement in basic provision that the critical literature, at its crudest, treats as a propaganda claim, and that the evidence does not permit any account to treat that way. Literacy, which at the revolution had been the possession of a minority of the population of the Russian Empire and of a small minority of its women and its non Russian peoples, became near universal within a generation, through campaigns of adult education conducted in dozens of languages, several of which received written forms for the first time under the Soviet state's own linguistic programmes. Primary and then secondary schooling became general, free, and compulsory in law and largely in fact. A system of basic health provision, built around clinics, feldsher stations in the countryside, vaccination, and the control of the great infectious killers, reached populations that no previous regime in those territories had attempted to serve. Employment was universal in the strong sense that open unemployment was abolished as a social category: the right to a job was constitutionally proclaimed and administratively delivered, at wages whose purchasing power is a separate question, but with a security against destitution that the population concerned had not previously known and that many of its members, in later recollection, ranked first among the system's features.

### What did the system achieve in basic provision, stated without figures?

Literacy became near universal within a generation, schooling became general and free, basic health care reached populations no earlier regime had served, and open unemployment was abolished, giving households a security against destitution that money measures of the record do not capture.

The quality and the texture of the provision require the same honesty in the other direction. Schooling was universal and also an instrument of political formation, its curricula controlled, its history falsified in particulars that mattered, its access to higher levels rationed by political as well as academic criteria in the system's worse periods. Health provision was broad and also shallow: strong on prevention, immunisation, and the infectious diseases of poverty, weak on the chronic diseases of affluence, on pharmaceuticals, on equipment, and on the dignity of the patient, with outcomes that the life expectancy section below examines in their most troubling form. Employment security coexisted with the direction of labour in the system's coercive periods, with chronic underemployment inside enterprises that hoarded workers against plan contingencies, and with the criminalisation, in some periods and places, of the joblessness the constitution declared impossible. A provision can be real and be all of these things at once, and the mature scholarship increasingly insists on holding the conjunction rather than selecting a side of it. The conjunction is the record.

Housing deserves separate mention because it is where provision and shortage met in daily life. The urban housing stock, devastated by war and overwhelmed by the migration the industrialisation itself generated, was rebuilt in vast standardised estates that housed tens of millions in self contained flats with heat, water, and sanitation, at rents held nominal by policy. The flats were small, the construction often shoddy, the waiting lists long, and the communal apartment, with its shared kitchen and its absence of privacy, persisted for decades in the older cities as the lived reality behind the aggregate achievement. Both the estate and the communal flat are the record. A literature that shows only the estate is apologetics; a literature that shows only the communal flat is polemic; and the reader who wants the truth of the housing question has to hold the two images together and resist the relief of dropping either.

The provision achievement also has a comparative dimension that the article flags here and develops later. The speed with which literacy and basic health were generalised in the Soviet case compares favourably with the historical experience of market economies at similar income levels, several of which tolerated mass illiteracy and absent rural medicine far longer into their development. It compares less favourably with the East Asian cases that later achieved the same generalisation faster and alongside rising consumption. And within the bloc, the provision floor was the feature that survived the deceleration longest: schooling and clinics continued to function, and employment to be guaranteed, through the stagnation decades, so that the system's social contract, provision and security in exchange for political quiescence, was still being honoured on its provision side when its growth side had failed. That asymmetry explains a good deal of the stability the late system enjoyed and of the shock its disappearance administered, and it is a large part of what the affirmative case, presented at full strength below, is entitled to claim.

## The consumer economy: shortage as a permanent condition

If provision was the record's credit side, the consumer economy was its debit, and the debit was not a transitional embarrassment but a structural condition that persisted, with variations of intensity, from the first five year plan to the final year of the system's existence. Consumer goods were chronically short in quantity and dismal in quality. The mechanism is treated in [the article on Soviet planning](/2019/04/15/soviet-planning-how-it-worked/) in this series and is only summarised here: an allocation system that prioritised producer goods and defence treated consumption as the residual, enterprises judged on gross output had no incentive to attend to assortment, durability, or design, and prices fixed below the level that would clear the market converted excess demand into the queue, the empty shelf, and the network of personal connections through which goods actually moved. The Hungarian economist János Kornai gave the resulting order its canonical analysis under the name of the shortage economy, demonstrating that shortage in such a system is not a disequilibrium to be corrected but the normal state generated by soft budget constraints and quantity driven behaviour, reproduced at every level from the ministry to the shop counter.

### Why were consumer goods chronically short and poor in quality across the whole period?

Because consumption was the residual of an allocation system built for other priorities. Enterprises rewarded for gross output neglected assortment and durability, prices held below clearing levels turned excess demand into queues, and no mechanism existed to discipline producers who disappointed their customers.

The lived consequences are part of the economic record in the strictest sense, since they represent real costs that the aggregates omit. Time spent queuing was a tax on every household, paid disproportionately by women, whose double burden of employment and provisioning is one of the record's most consistent findings across the memoir, sociological, and statistical literatures. Money accumulated involuntarily in savings accounts because there was nothing to buy, a monetary overhang that signalled repressed inflation in an economy whose official prices did not move. Quality failure imposed its own costs: goods that broke, clothes that fitted nobody, food whose distribution losses between farm and shelf consumed a share of the harvest that Western systems, with all their waste, did not approach. The second economy, the vast penumbra of barter, favour, moonlighting, and outright illegality through which citizens obtained what the plan did not supply, functioned as an unplanned correction to the plan, and its scale is itself a measurement of the official economy's failure to provision. A record that counts only what the plan produced systematically flatters the system, and the reconstructions that attempted to value the second economy, or to deduct the costs of shortage, moved the welfare assessment further against it.

Yet the consumer story also contains the nuance that saves it from caricature. Consumption did rise across the postwar decades in the Soviet Union and more markedly in parts of Eastern Europe: diets improved in quantity and variety from the famine shadowed baseline, consumer durables spread from rarity to normality across the 1960s and 1970s, and the private plot, the dacha, the cooperative flat, and the tolerated market gave households margins of autonomy the system's theory did not foresee. The failure was relative and dynamic rather than absolute: provision rose, but expectations, fed by awareness of Western consumption through every channel the state failed to seal, rose faster, and the technological character of consumption changed in ways the system could not follow. A record of rising televisions looks different in a decade when the comparison household acquires computing. The consumer economy's deepest failure was thus not that it delivered nothing. It was that it delivered yesterday's goods into a world that had moved on, and could not close a gap that its own partial successes kept making visible.

## Agriculture: the failure that outlasted every reform

Agriculture was the system's most persistent economic failure, and its persistence is the analytically interesting thing about it, since the sector received, across six decades, more reform attempts, more investment campaigns, and more leadership attention than any other branch of the economy, and responded to all of them with a stubborn mediocrity punctuated by catastrophe. The Soviet Union, heir to an empire that had exported grain to the world, became a structural importer of grain on a vast scale, purchasing abroad the feed and food its own vast land endowment should have supplied, and financing the purchases from the energy exports whose revenues thereby masked the agricultural failure in the trade accounts. The causes most consistently identified in the literature form a mutually reinforcing set. Collectivisation destroyed the peasantry's incentives and much of its accumulated skill, and the procurement system that succeeded it paid for produce at prices that treated the countryside as a tributary. Investment, when it came, was poured into machinery and chemistry whose returns were dissipated by an organisation of work that severed effort from reward. The private plot, tolerated within the collective structure, demonstrated the incentive diagnosis in miniature across the entire period, producing a share of output, above all of livestock products, vegetables, and fruit, wildly disproportionate to its share of the land.

### Why did agriculture underperform persistently despite repeated reform and investment?

Because its organisation severed effort from reward. Procurement prices treated the countryside as a tributary, collective work diluted individual return to vanishing point, and investment in machinery could not substitute for the incentives the structure removed, as the private plots demonstrated continuously.

Reform attempts trace the system's learning curve and its limits. Successive leaderships reorganised the machine tractor stations, consolidated the collective farms, raised procurement prices, wrote off debts, launched territorial campaigns to bring new land under cultivation, and experimented at the margins with contract brigades that reattached reward to the work of a small group. Several of these measures produced real if temporary improvements, and procurement price increases in particular were followed by better performance, which is itself evidence for the incentive diagnosis. What no reform attempted, until the system's final years, was the restoration of the household as the unit of production, because that restoration was understood, correctly, to be incompatible with the collective structure on which the regime's rural control rested. Agriculture is therefore the clearest case in the record of a sector where the binding constraint was not resources, knowledge, or effort, all of which were supplied in quantity, but a political choice about the organisation of rural life that the system could not revisit without revisiting itself. The grain ships unloading at Soviet ports in the final decades are the balance sheet of that choice, drawn in the most legible form the record offers.

The East European variations sharpen the diagnosis. Poland, which reversed collectivisation after 1956 and left most of its land in private peasant hands, suffered chronic agricultural problems of its own, fragmentation, underinvestment, and a politically explosive food price question, but not the Soviet pattern of incentive collapse, and its difficulties were those of a poor peasant agriculture rather than of a demoralised collective one. Hungary, which rebuilt its collectives with greater autonomy and better incentives after its revolution, achieved the bloc's most successful food economy, exporting produce while its neighbours imported. The comparison within the bloc thus performs a small controlled experiment of its own: where the collective form was moderated, agriculture moderated its failure; where the form was preserved intact, the failure was preserved with it. Few findings in the record rest on as many independent national confirmations.

## Technology: excellence in the priority sectors, failure in diffusion

The technological record divides sharply, and the division is one of the best established patterns in the field. In the sectors the state prioritised without limit, armaments, rocketry, space flight, nuclear technology, and the fundamental sciences that fed them, the system achieved results at or near the world frontier, sustained across decades against the richest competitor on earth. The first satellite, the first human spaceflight, a thermonuclear arsenal, and a mathematics and physics establishment of genuine distinction are not propaganda artefacts; they are entries in a record that the competitor's own intelligence services tracked with alarm. In the civilian economy, by contrast, the system fell progressively behind, and the falling behind had a characteristic shape. The planned economies could invent, and their research institutes produced discoveries of the first rank. They could not diffuse. An innovation arising in one enterprise conferred no advantage on it under the indicator system, threatened its plan fulfilment during the disruption of adoption, and spread to other enterprises only if a ministry chose to order its spread, which ministries, judged on the gross output of their existing product mix, had every reason not to do. The economy thus accumulated invention at the frontier of its priority sectors and stagnation across the breadth of its civilian production, a combination with no close parallel among the major economies.

### Where did the technological gap between the systems open widest?

In civilian diffusion. The priority sectors reached the world frontier and held it, while innovation across ordinary production stalled, because adoption disrupted plan indicators and conferred no reward, so the computing and consumer technologies of the final decades found an economy organised against their spread.

The computing revolution exposed the structure with unusual clarity, because it was a general purpose technology whose value lay precisely in diffusion. The Soviet Union possessed computer science of high quality and produced machines of respectable design, yet the economy's stock of computing, its software culture, its networking, and above all its integration of information technology into ordinary production and distribution lagged ever further behind the Western economies as the technology's importance compounded. A system that allocates by material balances and administrative instruction has, in principle, an affinity for computation, and theorists inside the system saw the affinity early and proposed networked planning schemes of real sophistication. The proposals died against the interests of the ministries whose information monopolies they threatened, an episode the literature on the system's information pathologies treats as diagnostic. The economy that could orbit a station could not computerise a warehouse network, and the contrast is not a joke at the system's expense but a precise statement about where its capabilities lived: in concentrated mobilisation for a defined goal, and not in the dispersed, incentive driven adoption of a technique whose benefits nobody had been ordered to want.

Eastern Europe's technological record adds the dimension of dependence. The smaller bloc economies, unable to sustain frontier research across the board, specialised within a division of labour coordinated through the bloc's own institutions and leaned increasingly, in the final decades, on imported Western technology purchased with borrowed money. The imports modernised particular plants without transforming the innovation system that had made the imports necessary, and the debt incurred to buy them became one of the mechanisms by which the deceleration of the 1970s converted into the crises of the 1980s. The pattern, borrowing to import the technology that the domestic system could not generate, is the technological face of extensive growth: inputs purchased to postpone the transition to efficiency, on terms that made the postponement progressively more expensive.

## The social indicators reversal: life expectancy turns

Every account of this record must pass through one finding that resists the consolations available on both sides, and this article gives it a section of its own, as the brief for the article requires. Life expectancy in the Soviet Union improved substantially from the revolution's aftermath into the 1960s, an improvement built from the conquest of infectious disease, the generalisation of basic medicine, rising nutrition from a desperate baseline, and the spread of sanitation and literacy, whose effects on survival, above all of infants and children, are among the best documented relationships in social medicine. The improvement was a genuine achievement of provision, achieved at income levels where many societies had failed to achieve it, and it belongs in the affirmative ledger without qualification. Then, from the middle 1960s, the improvement stopped. From the 1970s, on the demographic reconstructions, life expectancy stagnated and then declined, with the decline concentrated among men of working age. The causes identified in the demographic literature are cardiovascular disease untreated by a health system organised for infection, alcohol, above all vodka, consumed at levels the state's own revenues encouraged, industrial and road accidents, and the pathologies of a society whose official optimism forbade honest public discussion of any of them. In accordance with this article's protocol, no figures are stated. The direction, attested by the system's own demographers once publication was permitted and confirmed by every subsequent reconstruction, is sufficient for the argument.

### Why is the life expectancy reversal the most damaging single finding in the record?

Because a peacetime industrial society whose life expectancy falls is a rarity in the modern world. The reversal cannot be charged to war, famine, or the starting point, and it indicts the mature system, its health care, its alcohol regime, and its suppression of honest information, directly.

The reversal matters beyond its human meaning, which is primary, because of what it discriminates between. A critic who wishes to argue that the system's statistics were a facade must confront the early improvement, which the demographic evidence, the hardest in the field to fabricate across a whole population, confirms. A defender who wishes to argue that the system delivered welfare in exchange for freedom must confront the reversal, which shows the welfare delivery itself failing, in its most fundamental indicator, during the decades when the system was richest, most stable, and least violent. Neither confrontation is optional. The reversal also carries a measurement lesson characteristic of this subject: the Soviet state, recognising what the figures showed, ceased publishing the relevant series for a period, so that the indicator's absence became its own datum, and Western demographers reconstructed the trend from fragmentary releases in one of the field's most impressive exercises in inference. A state that suppresses its mortality statistics has already delivered a verdict on them, and the suppression belongs in the record beside the trend it hid.

Comparison disciplines the finding without dissolving it. Mortality from cardiovascular disease rose in several Western societies in the same decades before new treatments and changed habits reversed it, and alcohol has devastated populations under every economic system. The Soviet peculiarity lies in the conjunction: a decline sustained across two decades, in a state that controlled the alcohol trade as a fiscal monopoly and profited from the consumption it deplored, administered health care as a universal service whose budgets and priorities it set absolutely, and suppressed the information that might have forced the question into politics. The system's defenders are entitled to the observation that the post Soviet mortality catastrophe, which followed the system's end, was worse, and that observation is true and belongs in the record of the successor period rather than in mitigation of this one. The reversal happened under the system, at its zenith of stability, and it is the single finding that the affirmative case, to which the article now turns, has never satisfactorily answered.

## The affirmative case at full strength

The affirmative case for the record deserves to be stated as its serious proponents state it, with attribution, at the strength they give it, before any answer is attempted. Its most rigorous scholarly form is associated with the economic historian Robert C. Allen, whose reconstruction of the Soviet experience, published under the title Farm to Factory, argued from rebuilt series that Soviet growth across the middle decades of the century was rapid by the standards of the economies with which comparison is fair, that consumption, after the catastrophes of the transformation period, rose substantially across the postwar decades, and that the system's failure was a failure of its final phase rather than of its whole career. Behind the scholarly form stands the older affirmative tradition of the system's own economists and of sympathetic Western observers, from Maurice Dobb onward, who emphasised the structural achievement: the abolition of unemployment and of the business cycle's mass destitution, the planning of investment at a scale no market economy of the period attempted, and the conversion of a peasant continent into an industrial power within a generation. Stated at full strength, the case runs as follows.

An agrarian country with mass illiteracy became an industrial power with universal education, universal employment, and universal basic health provision within a generation, without unemployment or destitution. It did so starting from devastation, through invasion, without colonies to despoil and without the capital imports that financed other industrialisations, and it sustained, across the same decades, the defeat of the most powerful military machine in Europe and the achievement of strategic parity with the richest state on earth. Its people, who at the start of the period faced the classic insecurities of peasant life, famine, epidemic, ignorance, and the landlord, ended it literate, schooled, doctored, pensioned, and housed, at standards modest by the measure of the richest countries and transformed by the measure of their own grandparents. Comparing such a record with the wealthiest economies rather than with countries at comparable starting points is, the proponents argue, the standard method of understating it: an accounting trick dressed as a judgement, which would convict almost every successful development in history, since almost none began at the frontier. The right comparison, on this view, is with the economies that stood where the Soviet Union stood, in income, in literacy, in industrial structure, and by that comparison the record is one of the outstanding developmental achievements of the century, purchased at a cost its proponents do not deny and argue was imposed substantially by the hostility of the environment and the backwardness of the inheritance.

### What is the strongest form of the affirmative case for this record?

The starting point form. Judge the transformation against the society it began from, a largely illiterate peasantry, and against economies that began at similar levels, not against the richest states on earth, a comparison its proponents call the standard device for making the achievement disappear.

This article accepts the starting point argument. It is correct as a matter of method, and the comparison sections below are built on its logic: a verdict that does not state its base is not a verdict but a selection. The affirmative case is also right about the omissions of the standard critical account, which routinely begins its consumption story after the transformation, treats the provision achievements as though they were natural features of the landscape rather than constructions of the system being judged, and writes as though the alternative to the Soviet path, for the Russia of the period, was the path of Sweden. The early record is a genuine achievement, and critical accounts that omit it forfeit the authority to pronounce on the whole. Those concessions are made here without reservation, because the answer that follows does not need to withhold them. The answer is that the starting point argument, accepted in full, does not settle the question, because the historical record contains two cases in which the starting point is controlled by the experiment itself. Where a single people, with a single culture, a single starting level, and a shared history up to the moment of division, was split between the two systems, the comparison no longer depends on the scholar's choice of base. The divided countries are the closest approach to a laboratory this subject provides, and what they show is unfavourable to the affirmative case in a degree that no measurement dispute reaches. The article turns to the comparison problem in general first, and then to the divided cases that discipline it.

## The comparison problem: the question underneath every verdict

Compared with what? The question is short, and the whole literature on this record can be organised as a set of evasions of it. Every verdict stated in the dispute, triumph, failure, mixed record, crime, achievement, silently presupposes a comparison base: a set of countries, a pair of dates, a starting level, and a measure. Change the base and the verdict changes, not marginally but categorically, while the underlying facts remain exactly where they were. This article's governing discipline, set by its brief, is that every comparative claim must state the countries, the dates, the starting level of development, the measure used, and the accounting concept, and that any claim which cannot carry its basis in the open is to be removed rather than qualified. The sections that follow apply the discipline to the four bases the literature actually uses, in ascending order of the control they exercise over the starting point. The reader should watch the verdict move as the base changes. That movement, not any single resting place, is the article's finding.

A preliminary distinction prevents a confusion that dogs the subject. A comparison base is not chosen well or badly in the abstract. It is chosen well or badly for a question. If the question is what the system did for the population it inherited, the base is the inheritance itself, and the record is measured against the starting point. If the question is whether the system was an efficient way of organising a modern economy, the base is the performance of other modern economies over the same years. If the question concerns achievement relative to what was possible for countries at its level, the base is the cohort of comparable economies. Polemic consists largely of answering one of these questions with the base appropriate to another, and then denouncing the mismatch as dishonesty when the other side commits it. Scholarship consists of keeping the questions separate and reporting all three verdicts, which is what this article attempts. The theoretical form of the efficiency question, whether allocation by plan can outperform allocation by market, is taken up in [can planning beat the market](/2019/10/15/can-planning-beat-the-market/). The divided cases earn their special place because they answer all three questions with a single base, which is why both sides of the dispute treat them with a respect, or an avoidance, that they grant no other evidence.

### Why does the choice of comparison base move the verdict more than any measurement dispute?

Because measurement corrections move a growth path within a range, while the base selects the standard the path is judged against. Against the starting point the record is transformative; against the frontier economies it is a failure to converge; the facts are the same in both sentences.

## The first base: the starting point, the empire and the inheritance

Take the base the affirmative case prefers, and take it seriously. The comparison is between the Soviet Union at the end of its career and the Russian Empire of 1913, the last prewar year and the conventional benchmark of the literature, with Eastern Europe measured against its own interwar inheritance. On this base the record is transformative, and the word is not too strong. An empire of peasants, in which the majority could not read, in which industrial production was concentrated in a few western enclaves, in which life was governed by the harvest and the epidemic, became a society of universal literacy, urban majorities, electrification, heavy industry across its territory, and provision systems that reached the Central Asian village and the Arctic settlement. Measured against 1913, on gross domestic product reconstructions for output and on the social indicators for welfare, the system's career is one of the largest improvements in material life ever recorded for a population of its size. The affirmative case rests here, and here it is impregnable.

The qualifications that honest scholarship attaches to this base are real but do not overturn it. The empire was already developing, and a counterfactual in which some other regime industrialised Russia across the same decades cannot be excluded; the two world wars and the civil war sit inside the comparison span and depress any path measured across them; the transformation's costs, collectivisation and famine above all, are part of what the base comparison records if it is conducted honestly, since the population of the endpoint is a population diminished by the process. What the qualifications establish is that the starting point verdict, transformative, is a verdict about the distance travelled and not about the necessity of the route or the acceptability of its price. Travellers can cover a great distance by a road that should never have been taken. The base answers the question it was built for, how far, and is silent on the questions it was not, at what cost and against what alternative. Polemic on the affirmative side consists of presenting the distance as though it answered the other questions. Polemic on the critical side consists of refusing to record the distance at all.

## The second base: Western Europe and the frontier

Take now the base the critical case prefers. The comparison is between the socialist economies and the market economies of Western Europe, with the United States as the frontier case, across the postwar decades when both systems ran in peacetime conditions. On this base the record is one of failure to converge, and again the directional statement is robust across the reconstructions. The socialist economies began the postwar period behind the Western European level of output per head, on any reconstruction, and ended it, on the international projects' estimates, no closer in relative terms, with the consumption gap wider than the production gap throughout. The promise held out by the system's own ideology, to overtake and surpass the advanced capitalist economies in per head production within a stated historical span, was quietly retired by its own leadership as the arithmetic became undeniable. Measured against the frontier, the system's career is a long failure to close a gap it had defined as the test of its legitimacy.

### What does comparison with Western Europe over the postwar decades show?

Failure to converge. Beginning behind the Western European level, the socialist economies ended the period no closer in relative terms on the international reconstructions, and households' distance from Western living standards exceeded the factories' distance from Western output, while the overtaking pledge was quietly dropped by the leadership that had made it.

This base also has its honest qualifications, and they are not trivial. The frontier economies enjoyed advantages the comparison silently includes: undamaged or rapidly rebuilt capital stocks, access to the world market and to the dollar system, the productivity spillovers of the frontier itself, and in the American case a continental resource endowment and two oceans. Convergence is, in the growth literature, the exception rather than the rule among nations, and most market economies also failed to converge on the frontier across the same decades. A verdict of failure to converge therefore places the socialist economies in large company, and the critical literature that presents non convergence as a unique indictment overstates what the base can show. What the base does show, and what survives the qualifications, is narrower and still damaging: the system claimed, as a matter of doctrine, that its organisation of production was superior to the market's and would demonstrate the superiority in the one currency its rivals recognised, output per head. Against its own stated test, on its own chosen base of the advanced economies, it failed. A system is entitled to be judged by standards other than its own boasts. It is not entitled to have its boasts excluded from the evidence.

## The third base: countries at comparable starting points

The third base is the one the growth literature regards as methodologically correct for assessing a development strategy: the cohort of economies that stood at comparable levels of income and structure when the socialist path began, followed across the same decades. The cohort for the Soviet Union of the late 1920s includes the poorer economies of Southern and Eastern Europe, parts of Latin America, and, at the Asian edge of the comparison, Japan at an earlier stage and the economies that would later form the East Asian growth cases. On this base the verdict is mixed, and its mixedness is informative in a way the cleaner verdicts of the other bases are not. The Soviet record stands well against the Southern European and Latin American members of its cohort across the middle decades: industrialisation was faster, provision more general, and the transformation of structure more complete than in economies that left the process to the market and to foreign capital. It stands less well as the decades extend, and against the East Asian cases, once those begin their ascent, it deteriorates sharply, since the East Asian economies accomplished the structural transformation and then accomplished the efficiency transition the Soviet economy never made, reaching income levels and consumption standards that left the socialist economies behind from a comparable or poorer start.

### What does comparison with countries at similar starting levels show about the record?

A mixed verdict that changes with the decades. The record stands well against Southern European and Latin American peers across the middle decades, on industrialisation and provision, and deteriorates against the East Asian cases, which completed the efficiency transition the socialist economies never made.

The mixedness is the finding. A development strategy that outperforms its cohort for three decades and is then overtaken by the cohort's best performers is neither the triumph of the affirmative literature nor the unbroken failure of the critical one. It is a strategy with a phase structure: exceptionally effective at the mobilisation stage of development, where its instruments, forced saving, directed investment, suppressed consumption, mass education, are precisely the instruments the stage rewards, and progressively ineffective thereafter, when growth depends on the efficiency, innovation, and responsiveness its instruments suppress. The cohort base thus converts the argument from a verdict into a periodisation, and periodisation is where the serious literature on both sides has increasingly come to rest. Allen's affirmative reconstruction and the critical syntheses of scholars such as Alec Nove, who insisted on holding the achievement and the failure in a single account, converge on this shape from opposite directions, which is the strongest sign the field offers that the shape is real. What the cohort base cannot do is remove the scholar's discretion entirely, since the cohort's membership is itself a choice, and the inclusion or exclusion of the East Asian cases moves the verdict again. For that removal, the subject offers only the divided countries, where history, not the scholar, chose the comparison.

## The divided countries: the controls history supplied

Twice in the twentieth century, a single nation was divided between the two systems, and the divisions were drawn by the fortunes of war rather than by any difference in the populations divided. Germany was split along the line where the armies met, Korea along a parallel chosen in haste at a war's end. In each case the two successor states began with the same language, the same culture, the same stock of skills and habits, and starting levels of development that were close, in the German case arguably favourable to the eastern part, which inherited a concentration of industry and skilled labour, in the Korean case favourable to the northern part, which inherited the peninsula's heavy industry and hydroelectric capacity built under colonial rule. The experiments then ran for four decades under the gaze of the entire world, with each side's performance a matter of state doctrine. No social scientist could have designed the comparison, and no scholar assessing the record is entitled to ignore it. It is the closest approach to a controlled experiment that economic history provides on the question this article exists to answer.

### Why do the divided country cases matter more than aggregate comparisons?

Because they control for the starting point. A single people with a shared culture, skills, and history was divided by the fortunes of war, so the divergence that followed cannot be assigned to the inheritance the affirmative case rightly insists on elsewhere.

The German case is the cleaner of the two for the European record, and this article states its result plainly, as its brief requires, without figures and without burial. The eastern state began, on the reconstructions, at a level of output per head not far below the western one, and by most reconstructions the industrial heartlands it inherited placed it among the most advanced economies of the socialist bloc, the showcase whose performance the bloc itself cited. Across the four decades of division, the western economy pulled away. The gap in consumption widened beyond the gap in production, as the eastern system reproduced in miniature the bloc's general pattern of investment and defence priority. Eastern productivity in comparable branches settled at a fraction of the western level that the post unification exposure of the eastern industrial stock made impossible to dispute, since the capital stock, valued at world prices in the unification settlement, proved in large part to be worth less than the cost of the inputs it consumed. The final evidence was demographic and unambiguous in direction: across the whole period of division, migration flowed overwhelmingly from east to west, in such volume that the eastern state walled its capital city to stop it, and the flow resumed the moment the wall fell. Populations voting with their feet are not an economic statistic, and this article does not present them as one. They are evidence about revealed welfare that no reconstruction can argue away, and they point the same way as every reconstruction does.

### What does the divided Germany case show about the two systems?

That with starting points controlled, the planned economy fell behind. The eastern state inherited advanced industrial regions yet saw the consumption gap widen across four decades, its capital stock exposed at unification as worth less than its inputs, and its population flow westward whenever movement was permitted.

The qualifications the affirmative case enters against the German comparison are known, and this article records them at their real strength. The eastern state paid reparations from current production in the division's first years, including the dismantling of plant, while the western state received Marshall aid and integration into the Western trading system. The eastern state was denied access to Western technology markets by embargo in strategic categories and tied into a trading bloc whose dominant member extracted favourable terms. Its population loss to the west before the wall was concentrated among the young and the skilled, damaging the labour force it retained. Each qualification is genuine, and together they explain a portion of the divergence. What they do not explain, as the post unification scholarship has established in detail, is the divergence's scale and its persistence across the decades after reparations ended, aid equalised in memory, and the eastern economy had every opportunity to demonstrate the convergence its doctrine predicted. The controls are imperfect, as every control in history is imperfect. The direction of the result is not in reasonable dispute, and the dispute that continues concerns its size, which this article's protocol forbids it to state and does not need.

## The Korean division: the second control

The Korean case replicates the German one under harder conditions and with an even sharper reversal, and its inclusion matters because it removes the suspicion that the German result is a European peculiarity. The northern state inherited the peninsula's industrial base: the colonial power had concentrated heavy industry, mining, and hydroelectric generation in the north, near the raw materials, leaving the south predominantly agricultural, with light industry and the capital's commerce. For the first decades after division, the northern economy, rebuilt with bloc assistance after the devastation of the Korean War, outperformed the southern one on the indicators the period tracked, a fact the critical literature once found inconvenient and the affirmative literature cited with justice. The southern takeoff, when it came, was the East Asian efficiency transition executed under an authoritarian developmental state, and across the following decades the southern economy industrialised, enriched, and eventually democratised, while the northern economy stagnated, contracted, and descended in the 1990s into a famine that its southern counterpart, at the same starting culture and a worse starting endowment, had rendered unthinkable. The divergence between the two Koreas is visible from orbit, in the literal illumination of the southern night against the northern dark, an image that has become the record's most reproduced exhibit because it compresses the comparison into a single datum that requires no accounting convention to read.

### What does the Korean division add to the evidence of the German case?

A replication with the initial advantage on the planned side. The north began with the peninsula's furnaces, mines, and dams and outpaced the south for a generation, yet the south made the efficiency transition its neighbour never made, and the north ended in stagnation and famine.

The Korean case also contributes a discipline of its own, because its early decades warn against reading the divided comparisons as a simple story of one system's uniform superiority. The northern economy's early performance was real, and it fits the phase structure the cohort comparison produced: mobilisation works, for a time, at the stage where mobilisation is what development requires. The southern economy's own early decades were authoritarian, protectionist, and marked by failures of their own, and its later success was not the unfolding of a market essence but a constructed transition that many market economies failed to make. What the divided cases jointly establish is therefore not that one system was better at everything at every stage. It is that across the full span, with the starting point controlled, the planned economies could not accomplish the transition from mobilisation to efficiency on which sustained development came to depend, and the market economies of the divided pairs, whatever their own costs and crises, could. That is a narrower claim than the polemics on either side prefer, and it is the claim the evidence supports. The famine at the Korean experiment's end, and the wall at the German experiment's heart, are its two most legible symbols, and neither is a statistic.

## The comparison base decides: the claim stated and defended

The article's namable claim can now be stated in its full form. The comparison base decides: the verdict on the economic record of the socialist states depends more on which countries and which dates are compared than on any dispute about measurement, because measurement corrections move the estimates within a range while the choice of base moves the verdict across its whole span, from transformative achievement against the starting point, through a mixed and phase structured record against the comparable cohort, to failure to converge against the frontier. The two divided country cases are the closest available controls, and they are unfavourable, which is why accounts on both sides of this dispute select their comparison rather than defending it: the affirmative literature dwells on the starting point and passes over the divided countries in a paragraph, the critical literature dwells on the frontier and the divided countries and passes over the starting point in a sentence. Each side's table of contents is its verdict, announced in advance.

### Why must a path and a level never be stated as one claim?

A growth claim describes a path between two dates on a stated measure. A starting point claim describes the level from which the path began. Confusing them lets a rapid path from a low base be read as a high level reached, or a modest path from a high base as stagnation.

The claim is a finding about the literature as much as about the economies, and it should be defended as such. If measurement were the heart of the dispute, the convergence of the reconstructions on the record's shape would have settled the argument, since the shape is agreed. The argument continues because the shape underdetermines the verdict, and the verdict is supplied by the base. This is not a counsel of despair about assessment. It is an instruction for conducting it: state the base, state the question the base answers, report the verdicts on the other bases beside it, and treat any account that reports only one verdict as an exhibit in the dispute rather than as a contribution to settling it. The reader who applies that instruction to the literature will find that the temperature of the dispute falls rapidly, because most of its heat is generated by bases colliding without being named. What remains, once the bases are on the table, is a smaller and genuine disagreement about costs, counterfactuals, and the weight to give the divided cases, and that disagreement is the one worth having.

## Did living standards rise? The welfare question separated

A question that the growth dispute persistently muddles deserves separation before the article proceeds to its verdict. Did living standards rise under state socialism? The answer, on the evidence reviewed here, is yes, across most of the period and for most of the population, with the catastrophic exceptions of the transformation's famine years and the war, and with the reversal in the health indicator standing as the great qualification of the final decades. Consumption per head rose on every reconstruction across the postwar decades. Diets improved from the baseline of the 1940s, housing space increased, durables spread, education and health access became universal, and the security of employment and pension removed a class of anxieties that market societies distribute unequally. To deny the rise is to deny the testimony of the generations who lived it, whose retrospective assessments, in survey after survey conducted after the system's end, distinguished sharply between the system's political character, which they condemned, and its provision of security, whose loss many of them mourned. The critical case does not need the denial, and weakens itself whenever it makes it.

### How did living standards change across the period, stated directionally?

They rose across most of it. Consumption, diet, housing space, and access to education and health all improved from the baselines of the 1940s on every reconstruction, with famine and war as catastrophic exceptions and the health reversal qualifying the final decades.

The rise, however, is compatible with the failure, and the compatibility is the point that both polemics resist. Living standards rose absolutely while falling relative to the frontier, rose while the queue and the shortage taxed them in ways the consumption aggregates omit, and rose in the dimensions the system chose to supply, housing, schooling, basic medicine, staple food, while stagnating in the dimensions it neglected, variety, quality, choice, and the consumer technologies of the final decades. A household can be better fed, better housed, and better schooled than its grandparents, and simultaneously further behind the households it watches on a smuggled television broadcast than its parents were, and both facts belong to its standard of living as lived. The welfare question, separated from the growth question, thus yields the record in miniature: a real rise, achieved and felt, overtaken by a relative decline that was also achieved and felt, with the system's tragedy located precisely in the conjunction. It had taught its population to read, and the population read the comparison for itself.

## The defence burden: output that was not welfare

No assessment of the record is complete without the allocation that its aggregates conceal most successfully: the claim of defence. The socialist states, the Soviet Union above all, sustained a military establishment sized for confrontation with the richest coalition on earth, on an economic base that every reconstruction places well below that coalition's. The burden, however it is estimated, and the estimates were among the most contested products of the reconstruction enterprise, absorbed resources of a quality the aggregates do not distinguish: not merely materials and labour, but the scarcest inputs the economy possessed, its best engineers, its most advanced machine tools, its priority access to components and energy. The opportunity cost of the defence claim is therefore understated by any share of output, because the claim fell disproportionately on the margin where the civilian economy was weakest, the generation and diffusion of advanced technology. A tank and a tractor may enter the accounts at comparable values. They do not leave comparable residues in the economy that produced them, one consuming its inputs to stand in a depot, the other multiplying the productivity of every harvest it serves.

The affirmative case is entitled to its reply, and the reply has force. The Soviet Union had been invaded twice in a generation, the second time by an enemy conducting a war of annihilation, and the determination never to be defenceless again was not a paranoia manufactured by its rulers but a conclusion its population shared and its history taught. The Western coalition's military spending, on a far larger base, posed the threat to which the burden responded, and a full accounting would enter the arms race as a cost imposed on both systems, falling with crushing relative weight on the poorer. All of that is true, and none of it dissolves the economic finding, which is about the record and not about blame: a substantial share of what the system produced was neither consumed nor invested in future civilian production, and the share's weight on a smaller base is among the structural reasons the consumption gap exceeded the production gap on every comparison base this article has examined. Blame belongs to the history of the Cold War. The burden belongs to the record, and the record is this article's subject.

## Energy, borrowing, and the postponement of reckoning

The deceleration's timing in the Soviet case was masked, for roughly a decade, by geology. The development of the west Siberian oil and gas fields, among the largest hydrocarbon provinces on earth, delivered the economy a flood of export earnings precisely when its manufacturing growth faltered, and the earnings financed the grain imports agriculture required, the technology imports industry wanted, and the consumer imports with which the leadership of the 1970s attempted to purchase social peace. The masking matters to the record because it distorts the periodisation on which verdicts depend. Read from the aggregates of the 1970s alone, the system appears to sustain a moderate performance; read with the energy windfall separated, the underlying industrial deceleration is steeper and earlier, and the 1980s collapse of energy prices appears not as an external misfortune that befell a healthy economy but as the removal of the prop from an economy whose weakness the prop had concealed. The distinction matters for the collapse literature, which has its own article in this series, and it matters here because it is a measurement lesson in political dress: a windfall is not a growth rate, and a record that cannot tell the difference will misdate the turning point of the whole story.

Eastern Europe's version of the postponement was financial rather than geological. The bloc's more consumer minded governments, Poland and Hungary prominently, borrowed in Western capital markets across the 1970s to finance imported technology and sustained consumption simultaneously, on the theory that the technology would generate the exports to service the debt. The theory failed where the innovation system failed: the imported plant produced goods the Western market largely did not want at the quality offered, the debts matured into the interest rate shock of the early 1980s, and the reckoning arrived as austerity, shortage intensified to crisis, and in the Polish case a mass movement that fused economic grievance with national and political revolt. The borrowing decade is thus part of the record and not an epilogue to it. It demonstrates, in the bloc's own laboratory, that the constraint on these economies was never primarily finance or access to equipment. Given both, in quantities their earlier history would have envied, the systems could not convert them into competitive production, because the constraint sat in the incentive and information structure that borrowing left untouched. The lesson generalises uncomfortably to every development strategy that treats capital import as a substitute for institutional capability, which is one reason this record remains required reading far beyond the history of the bloc itself.

## Quality: the dimension the aggregates cannot see

Running beneath every measurement dispute in this article is a dimension that none of the rival methods captures and that may, for welfare, outweigh them all: quality. Two economies can register identical physical output of shoes while one population walks in footwear that lasts and fits and the other in footwear that does neither, and the difference, multiplied across the entire assortment of consumer goods and a good part of the producer goods as well, constitutes a divergence in real output that the tonnage and the value aggregate alike record as parity. The reconstructions handled quality asymmetrically and knew it: Western output was conventionally credited with quality improvements embodied in new models and rising specifications, while Soviet output, whose nominal model changes were the vehicle of hidden inflation, was discounted, and the argument over the size of the respective adjustments is one of the genuine technical disputes of the field. What is not in dispute is the direction of the underlying reality. Visitors, migrants, and the post 1991 exposure of the consumer stock all testify in one direction, and the eastern capital stock's valuation at unification, when machinery that had entered the accounts at full value was written down toward scrap, supplied the producer goods counterpart of the consumer evidence.

Quality failure had a systemic source that this article has met in other forms. An enterprise judged on quantity indicators treats quality as a cost with no corresponding reward, since a better product counts the same as a worse one in the gross output by which success is measured, while the effort and risk of improvement count against the plan's fulfilment. Shortage then protects the failure from correction, because customers who cannot obtain the bad product at all do not discipline its maker by refusal. The result is an economy that can be, simultaneously, statistically respectable and experientially shoddy, and the gap between those two descriptions is precisely the gap between the record as the aggregates state it and the record as the population lived it. Any verdict that rests on the aggregates alone will miss the shoddiness, and any verdict that rests on the shoddiness alone will miss the literacy, the clinics, and the steel. The record is the conjunction, and this article has tried to keep both halves of it in view at all times, because dropping either is the founding error of the two literatures it seeks to replace.

## Were the official statistics fabricated?

The question is asked constantly and usually answered with a slogan, and it deserves the slower treatment the evidence supports. Were the official statistics fabricated? In the main, no, if fabrication means the wholesale invention of figures by the central statistical organs. The central apparatus worked within a coherent accounting system, applied its concepts with professional consistency, and published series whose internal structure specialists could and did analyse for decades, an exercise that wholesale invention would have rendered impossible. The system's own economists used the figures critically, its planners complained about their quality in internal documents that the archives later confirmed, and the most damaging reconstruction of the growth path, Khanin's, was built substantially from the state's own published physical data, which is not a foundation fabrication provides. The charge of invention, applied to the centre, does not survive the evidence.

### Were the official statistics simply invented by the state that published them?

No, in the main. The central apparatus applied a coherent accounting system consistently, and the sharpest internal reconstruction was built from the state's own physical data. The distortions entered through concepts, prices, hidden inflation, and pressured reporting at the edges, not wholesale invention.

What the evidence does support is a more precise indictment with three counts. The first is conceptual: the aggregates measured a narrower thing than readers assumed, and the state did nothing to discourage the assumption when the comparison flattered it. The second is the price and index structure, whose flattering effects were understood by specialists inside the system and left uncorrected in public presentation. The third is reporting distortion at the base of the pyramid, where enterprise and regional officials, whose careers hung on plan fulfilment, inflated returns, concealed losses, and reported capacity with strategic dishonesty, a practice the Russian language named and the archives documented at every level below the centre. Add the suppression of series that turned unfavourable, the mortality statistics above all, and the picture is complete: not a fabricated economy, but a real economy reported through a system of concepts that narrowed it, prices that flattered it, incentives that corrupted its base data, and censorship that hid its worst indicators. The distinction matters because the remedies differ. Fabrication would license discarding the data entirely. The actual diagnosis requires the harder work this article has described, reconstruction, cross checking, and the naming of bases, and it is why the field's serious literature, however critical of the system, continues to work with its statistics rather than merely denouncing them.

## The measurement dominance pattern: the seventh instance

Readers of this series will have met the structure of this article's problem before, and the brief for the article requires the pattern to be named. This is the seventh instance of the measurement dominance pattern: the recurring finding, across the quantitative disputes of Marxist economics, that the verdict on a contested magnitude is determined less by the evidence than by the measurement construction through which the evidence is gathered, so that the argument about the number is really an argument about the instrument. The pattern appeared in the measurement of value, where the choice of labour embodied conventions governs the result; in the measurement of exploitation, where the treatment of productive and unproductive labour moves the rate; in the measurement of profit rates, where the valuation of the capital stock decides the trend before the data are consulted; in the ecological decoupling dispute, where the choice between territorial and consumption based accounting reverses the conclusion; and it appears here, in its most consequential historical form, in the measurement of socialist growth, where the accounting concept, the price basis, and above all the comparison base decide the verdict on the largest economic experiment of the twentieth century. A pattern that spans value theory, exploitation, profit rates, decoupling, and socialist growth is no longer a peculiarity of any one dispute. It is a general finding about quantitative claims in this field, and the finding has a practical corollary that this series has stated in each instance and states again: in any quantitative controversy in Marxist economics, interrogate the instrument before the number, and treat the scholar who specifies the instrument, the basis, and the defeater as the scholar to trust, whatever verdict that scholar reaches.

The corollary cuts against the field's friends as readily as against its enemies, which is the mark of a methodological finding rather than a partisan one. The affirmative literature on this record has its own measurement conveniences, its preferred base years, its consumption series unadjusted for shortage, its silence on the mortality reversal, and they fail the corollary's test as completely as the critical literature's frontier comparisons and inherited intelligence estimates do. What the pattern finally teaches is a habit of reading. Every figure in this field arrives with a construction inside it, and the construction is usually where the argument actually happened, upstream, in decisions about concepts and comparisons that the finished figure conceals. The historian of the record and the student of the theory are, at that point, doing the same work: refusing the finished number's invitation to stop thinking, and reopening the decisions the number was built to close.

## Misreadings to correct

Five misreadings recur so reliably in coverage of this record that correcting them is a service in itself, and each correction has been argued in the body of the article. The first is that official and Western figures measured the same thing, so that one side must have been lying: they measured different aggregates on different price bases, and the dispersion between them is methodological before it is anything else. The second is that the Western intelligence estimates understated the adversary's economy, whether from incompetence or design: the post archival assessment runs the other way: the estimates proved generous, because the returns beneath them had been inflated before any Western analyst touched them. The third is that growth was uniformly poor across the whole record, a reading the early decades refute on every series: the transformation was real, rapid, and corroborated by the physical evidence of the system's harshest internal critic. The fourth is that social provision was a propaganda facade: the literacy campaigns, the clinics, and the employment guarantee were constructions of the system, attested by the populations who used them and mourned their loss, and a criticism that cannot accommodate them is a criticism of a caricature, not of the record. The fifth is that some single comparison, the empire, the frontier, the cohort, settles the question: each base answers a different question, and the verdict that does not carry its base is an artefact of selection. A reader who carries the five corrections into the literature will find most popular writing on the subject disqualifying itself within a page, in one direction or the other, which is perhaps the most useful skill this article can teach.

## What would settle the argument?

It is worth asking, in the spirit this series applies to every controversy, what evidence would settle the argument about this record, because the answer explains why the argument continues. The measurement disputes are, in principle, closable, and archival publication has been closing them: as the internal series, the planners' own working estimates, and the suppressed demographic data have entered the public record, the range of defensible magnitudes has narrowed, and the remaining dispersion is increasingly a matter of stated method rather than of rival facts. A reader should expect the magnitudes to keep converging, slowly, in the hands of the reconstruction projects described in this article as bodies of work. The comparison dispute is not closable in the same way, because it is not, at bottom, a dispute about evidence. It is a dispute about which question the record is required to answer: what it did for its inheritance, what it proved about economic organisation, or what it achieved against the possible. Evidence cannot choose between questions. It can only discipline the answers, and the discipline consists of exactly what this article has attempted: every verdict labelled with its base, every base's verdict reported beside the others, and the divided countries given the weight their control earns them. An argument conducted on those terms would be shorter, duller, and nearly over. The literature's preference for the other kind is a fact about the literature, and about the political uses of the record, rather than about the record itself, which has been sitting in the archives, patient and increasingly legible, while the argument about it went on without it.

## The record decade by decade: a narrative the bases share

The bases dispute the verdict, but they share a chronology, and a reader who holds the chronology firmly can see where each base fastens on the story. The first act runs from the late 1920s to the German invasion. Collectivisation remade the countryside at a human cost that included a famine in the grain belt whose scale the demographic literature establishes directionally beyond argument, while the first plans raised the industrial skeleton: steel cities in the Urals and Ukraine, tractor and machine plants, the electrification network, and a technical education system built from almost nothing to staff them. Output of the heavy branches multiplied. Consumption fell, recovered partially in the middle 1930s, and remained austere, and the population paid for the skeleton in rations, in housing compression, and in the famine's dead. Every base records the same act. They differ in whether they call it foundation or crime, and the honest account calls it both, in the same sentence, because the steel and the famine were products of a single process and separating them is the original sin of both literatures.

The second act is the war and the reconstruction. The invasion destroyed the western third of the economy the first act had built, killed a generation's worth of its people, and was defeated by a mobilisation that drew on the industrial base east of the front, much of it evacuated or newly built in the first act's geography of dispersal. Reconstruction then restored the prewar output level within a span that surprised foreign observers, at consumption levels held down again, and under a political tightening that treated the population's wartime sacrifices as no claim on relaxation. The reconstruction's speed belongs in the record beside the industrialisation's: twice in two decades the system demonstrated a capacity for concentrated material achievement under adversity that no fair account can omit, and twice the achievement was extracted by methods, and at prices, that no fair account can excuse. The pattern of the whole record is present in embryo in those two demonstrations. The system was formidable at exactly the tasks it set itself, and the tasks it set itself were never the welfare of the population as the population would have chosen it.

The third act, the 1950s, is the record's most genuinely ambiguous decade and therefore its most instructive. Growth on all series remained rapid, now broadening from the heavy base into chemicals, machinery of greater sophistication, and the beginnings of a consumer sector. The political thaw after the dictator's death released prisoners, relaxed the countryside's burdens, and raised procurement prices, and living standards improved from the austerity of the reconstruction years in ways the population registered as a new world: housing construction on a mass scale moved families from barracks and communal corners into flats of their own, wages and pensions rose, and the leadership committed itself publicly to overtaking the leading Western economy in per head production within a historical span its own arithmetic soon made embarrassing. Eastern Europe, after the explosions of the decade's middle years in Poland and Hungary had been contained, settled into its own version of the same bargain, provision and rising consumption in exchange for political submission. A scholar assessing the system at the act's close, on the evidence then available, would have described an economy that was poor, harsh, and closing ground. The description would have been reasonable. It would also have been wrong about the future, because the act's growth still ran on the extensive fuel whose exhaustion the next acts record, and the system's capacity to change its fuel was the variable nobody, inside or out, could yet measure.

The fourth act, the 1960s and 1970s, is the long shallowing this article has described as deceleration, and in narrative terms it is the story of reform attempted and absorbed. The Soviet leadership of the middle 1960s introduced profit and sales indicators, greater enterprise autonomy, and price revisions intended to make the plan respond to efficiency; the reforms were diluted by the ministries, contradicted by the indicator system's deeper logic, and effectively abandoned within a few years, a sequence repeated in variations across the bloc, most seriously in Czechoslovakia, where economic reform fused with political liberalisation and was ended by invasion, and most durably in Hungary, where a market socialist hybrid survived in moderated form and delivered the bloc's best consumer economy at the price of the debt described earlier. The 1970s added the energy windfall in the Soviet case and the borrowing spree in the East European one, twin postponements that kept consumption rising gently while the underlying engine weakened. The population experienced the act as stability, even as improvement, and the experience was not false consciousness: the shops were fuller than in the 1950s, the flats were built, the televisions spread. It was stability purchased by consuming the future's resources, geological and financial, and the act's tragedy is that the purchase was invisible in every statistic the system published about itself.

The fifth act, the 1980s, is stagnation acknowledged. The growth path flattened on every series, the technological gap in the new industries widened visibly, the mortality reversal deepened, and the leadership that finally named the condition inherited an economy whose reform, attempted in earnest at last, released political forces the economic reform could not satisfy. The endpoint belongs to the collapse literature and its own article in this series. For the record, what matters is the shape the five acts compose: a curve that rises steeply through catastrophe and reconstruction, broadens into genuine improvement, shallows across two decades of postponement, and flattens into the stagnation from which the system did not recover. Every base in this article is a way of reading that curve. The starting point base reads its total ascent. The frontier base reads its failure to keep rising. The cohort base reads its phases. The divided countries read it against the twin curves of their western and southern halves, which shallow and then rise again. The curve is one. The readings are the argument.

## One bloc, several records: the national variations

The bloc is a statistical convenience that conceals as much as it reveals, and a section on the national variations protects the article's general claims from the exceptions that would otherwise be used to discredit them. Czechoslovakia entered the system as one of Europe's industrial economies, with engineering, armaments, and consumer manufactures of established reputation, and its record is therefore the bloc's hardest case for the affirmative argument from starting points: there was little transformation to accomplish, and the system's achievement there reduces to the maintenance, and gradual relative decline, of an inheritance. The Czech lands ended the period having fallen from the company of Europe's advanced economies toward its middle, a trajectory the frontier base records without needing any assistance from measurement disputes, since the comparison economies include its own prewar peers. The German Democratic Republic, treated in the divided countries section, shared the advanced starting point and the relative decline. For the bloc's two most industrialised members, the record is not a development story at all. It is a story of advanced economies administered into relative backwardness, and the affirmative case, which depends on the starting point, has no purchase there.

Hungary and Poland supply the middle cases, and their interest lies in the reforms. Hungary, after its revolution was crushed, built under a chastened leadership the bloc's most pragmatic variant: collective agriculture with real incentives, a tolerated private sector at the margins, prices partially freed, and a consumer provision that earned the country its contemporary nickname as the bloc's happiest barracks. The Hungarian record demonstrates that the system's performance was not invariant to its management, that relaxing the model's rigidities improved outcomes in consumption and agriculture measurably, and that the improvement had limits set by the elements that could not be relaxed without dissolving the model: the party's monopoly, the soft budget constraint in heavy industry, and the bloc's external discipline. Poland demonstrates the obverse. Its uncollectivised peasantry, its powerful church, and its restive working class made it the bloc's least governable economy, its history a cycle of consumer promises, borrowing, price crisis, and revolt, in which the system's economic failures repeatedly detonated as political ones. The Polish cycle is the record's clearest demonstration that the consumer economy was not a secondary matter: a system that legitimated itself by provision stood or fell, in the end, at the meat counter.

Romania and Bulgaria mark the bloc's southern range. Romania under its final leadership pursued an autarkic industrialisation of exceptional harshness, exporting food and energy to extinguish foreign debt while its population queued in unheated flats, a demonstration that the model's instruments, pushed to their limit by a leadership unconstrained by even the bloc's norms, could produce immiseration in the midst of statistical growth. Bulgaria, more orthodox and more dependent, tracked the Soviet pattern at a lower level with fewer convulsions. The Balkan cases matter to the assessment because they isolate the contribution of leadership and national circumstance within a common institutional frame: the same system, administered with Hungarian pragmatism, produced the bloc's best shops; administered with Romanian fanaticism, its worst. Institutions set the range. Governments chose the position within it, and the range itself, bounded above by Hungary's partial success and below by Romania's catastrophe, never included the frontier performance the doctrine promised. That bounded range, visible only when the national records are read together, is one of the strongest summary statements the evidence supports about what the system could and could not do.

## The counterfactual problem: what was the alternative?

Every verdict on the record smuggles in a counterfactual, an answer to the question of what would have happened instead, and intellectual honesty requires the smuggling to stop. Three counterfactuals circulate in the literature, rarely distinguished. The first imagines the starting country continuing under its previous trajectory: the Russian Empire industrialising at its prewar pace, the peasant economies of Eastern Europe evolving under their interwar regimes. Against that counterfactual the socialist record looks strong in its early phases, since the previous trajectories were themselves undistinguished, marked by foreign dependence, landlordism, and in several cases authoritarian stagnation, and the honest critic must concede that the alternative on offer in 1928 was not Scandinavian social democracy but a set of regimes several of which collapsed into dictatorship and war without any assistance from Marxism. The second counterfactual imagines a reformed socialism, the market socialist or democratically planned variant that internal critics proposed from inside the system across its whole life. Against that counterfactual the record is an indictment of the specific institutional choices made, since the Hungarian experiment and the internal critics' programmes suggest that better outcomes were available within a broadly socialist frame and were refused for political reasons. The third counterfactual imagines the East Asian path: an authoritarian developmental capitalism executing land reform, mass education, export discipline, and the efficiency transition in sequence. Against that counterfactual, available only in hindsight and dependent on conditions, American sponsorship, land reform imposed by occupation or invasion fears, labour repression, that the comparison usually omits, the record fails on the transition it never made.

A verdict is only as good as its counterfactual, and the three yield three different verdicts from the same facts, which is the comparison problem wearing its philosophical dress. This article's position is that all three counterfactuals are legitimate questions and none is the question, and that the scholarly task is to keep them separate and answer each. Against continuation, the record is a formidable, if bloodily purchased, acceleration. Against its own internal alternatives, it is a story of refused reforms and a political monopoly that valued control above performance whenever the two conflicted, which was whenever performance required information the monopoly could not tolerate. Against the East Asian path, it is a failure of transition, the more pointed because the divided Korean case runs that very counterfactual on a single peninsula. The divided countries retain their privilege among the evidence precisely because they replace the imagined counterfactual with an observed one: not what might have happened, but what did happen, to the other half of the same people, in the same decades, under the other system. Counterfactual reasoning can qualify that observation at the margins, reparations, aid, embargoes, and this article has recorded the qualifications. It cannot replace it, and the literature's recurring attempt to argue the divided cases away, rather than to weigh them, is the surest sign of their evidentiary force.

## The household economy: queues, savings, and the second economy

The aggregates describe an economy of production. The population lived in an economy of acquisition, and the distance between the two is a measurable part of the record that the production statistics omit. Obtaining goods in the shortage economy required a second, unpaid labour of search, queueing, connection, and improvisation, performed overwhelmingly by women, whose working day in consequence stretched across the factory shift and the provisioning shift in a double burden that the system's proclaimed emancipation of women never relieved. The time tax was real output forgone and real welfare lost, and it appears in no one's national accounts, East or West, which is a reminder that the measurement problem is not exhausted by the bloc's peculiarities. What the bloc's peculiarities added was scale: where shortage is the normal state rather than the exception, the acquisition economy grows until it rivals the official one in the hours it commands, and the citizen's practical economic knowledge becomes a knowledge of queues, delivery days, and the geography of favour rather than of prices.

Money itself behaved differently in such an economy, and the difference corrupted every monetary magnitude a naive comparison might use. Because goods were unavailable at official prices, households accumulated savings they did not choose to hold, deposits that represented not deferred consumption plans but frustrated ones, and the stock of unspendable money grew across the later decades into the overhang whose existence the system's economists acknowledged in their internal debates. Wages, in consequence, overstated welfare in a specific way: the wage was paid in a currency whose marginal unit bought, not a good, but a place in a queue and a probability. Income comparisons between the systems that convert wages at any exchange rate, official or purchasing power, import this distortion whole, which is why this article's protocol excludes income comparisons entirely and why the reader should treat every rouble or forint figure converted into dollars, in any publication, as an artefact of the conversion convention rather than a measurement of a life.

The second economy grew in the space the first economy failed to fill, and its scale is the shortage's most honest index. Private repairmen, tutors, builders, and traders; collective farmers selling plot produce in urban markets at prices the state tolerated because the alternative was empty stalls; enterprise managers trading materials through networks of reciprocal favour; citizens of every station converting access, a position in a shop, a signature, a hospital connection, into a currency more reliable than the official one. The second economy fed, clothed, housed, and healed a substantial share of the population's real consumption, and it did so by violating, daily and universally, the legal and ideological order of the system it kept alive. Its existence complicates every verdict. For the critical account, it demonstrates that the official economy could not provision its citizens without an illegal supplement it officially denied. For the affirmative account, it demonstrates a popular resourcefulness that kept living standards above the official economy's delivery. For the record, it demonstrates something colder: that the economy the statistics measured and the economy the population inhabited were two different systems, overlapping and interdependent, and that any assessment of the first as though it were the second is a category error, whichever side commits it.

## Women, work, and the emancipation that stopped halfway

The record has a gendered structure that aggregate accounts flatten, and a section on it is owed to the evidence. The system drew women into paid employment at rates that stood, for decades, among the highest in the world, and it educated them to levels that transformed the professions: medicine, teaching, engineering, and the technical occupations filled with women to a degree the Western economies of the same decades did not approach. These are entries in the achievement ledger of exactly the kind the affirmative case cites, and they are real. The same system then assigned the employed woman the whole second shift of provisioning and care described in the household section, staffed its feminised professions at wages and statuses below the male industrial trades, reserved political and managerial authority overwhelmingly to men, and addressed women in its official culture as workers and mothers in a combination that left no recognised space for the woman as citizen. The emancipation was genuine in its educational and employment dimensions and arrested in its domestic and political ones, and the arrest was not an oversight but a settlement: the system needed women's labour in the factory and their labour in the home, and it organised its ideology to obtain both.

The demographic consequences run through the record's later chapters. The burden of the double shift expressed itself in family size decisions taken in cramped housing, in health outcomes that form part of the mortality picture, and in a quiet, massive non compliance with the system's demographic hopes that no exhortation reversed. When the life expectancy reversal is disaggregated, and when the alcohol catastrophe is assigned its gender, the picture that emerges is of a male population dying early under burdens the statistics summarise and a female population surviving it while carrying the economy of care on which survival depended. An assessment of the record that does not see this structure has not seen the society, only its accounts, and the accounts, as this article has argued from its first page, are exactly where the seeing is hardest.

## How to read a figure in this field: a provenance guide

The article's argument can be compressed into a practice, and the practice is worth stating as guidance for any reader who will meet these figures elsewhere, which is to say any reader of modern history. When a growth figure for a socialist economy is presented, ask five questions of it, in order. What does it count: the material product or the whole product, and if a conversion between them has been performed, by whom and on what assumptions. At what prices is it valued: an early base year's, with the Gerschenkron flattery that implies, or a later year's, with the reverse effect, or a foreign price structure imported wholesale. Who produced it: the statistical office of the state concerned, a foreign intelligence service with its own history of generosity, an independent scholar working against his own state's interest, or an international project publishing its adjustments. What has been done about hidden inflation and quality: purged by physical cross checking, adjusted by assumption, or ignored. And finally, the question that outweighs the other four: compared with what. A figure that survives the five questions with its answers stated is a contribution to knowledge. A figure that cannot answer them is a rhetorical object, and the reader is entitled to treat its author's verdict as unevidenced, however distinguished the author and however confident the presentation.

The practice applies symmetrically, and its symmetry is its credential. The affirmative literature's favourite figures fail it as regularly as the critical literature's: the official growth path fails the price and hidden inflation questions; the consumption claims fail the shortage adjustment; the frontier comparisons of the critical side fail the comparison question by declining to defend their base; the intelligence estimates fail the provenance question in the direction nobody expected. A reader armed with the five questions will notice, in addition, how rarely they are all answerable from the text in which the figure appears, and that rarity is the field's true scandal. Not that scholars disagree, which is their function, but that the conventions of presentation allow the disagreement's grounds to be concealed inside a decimal point. The decimal point is the enemy. Directional claims with named bases are weaker in appearance and stronger in fact, and a literature that adopted them would lose its most dramatic sentences and gain the one thing it has lacked: conclusions that survive the next archive opening.

There is a final provenance that deserves naming, because it is the one the reader of this article is using. Synthetic accounts, textbooks, encyclopaedias, and articles such as this one stand at the end of the chain of reconstructions, and their figures, where they give them, are borrowings at third hand. This article's refusal to give them is its method of breaking the chain: it reports the shape the reconstructions agree on, the directions the evidence supports, and the bases on which verdicts depend, and it leaves the decimals in the primary literature where their assumptions can be inspected. A reader who finds that abstention frustrating has understood it correctly. The frustration is the accurate emotional register for this evidence, and the fluency of accounts that never provoke it is purchased by suppressing exactly what this article exists to display.

## The environmental ledger: a pointer

One dimension of the record is assigned by this series to its own article, and a paragraph here marks the boundary and the connection. The material product system counted production and did not deduct destruction: an economy could raise its measured product by exhausting a fishery, draining a sea, or contaminating a region, and record the whole of the activity as gain, since the accounting concept contained no line for the capital consumed in nature. The environmental record of the socialist states, the desiccation of the Aral Sea as the type case, is therefore not an accidental blemish on the economic record but one of its accounting consequences, compounded by the information suppression that prevented the costs from entering politics. That ledger is kept in full in [the companion assessment of the environmental record](/2017/11/01/socialist-states-environmental-record/). Here it is enough to register that any verdict on the growth this article has examined is a verdict on growth measured gross of its natural costs, and that correcting the omission, were the data to permit it, would darken the later decades' record in particular, since the extensive method treated nature as it treated labour, as a reserve to be mobilised until it was gone.

## Urbanisation and education: the transformation of the population itself

Beneath the output record lies a demographic transformation that outlasts it, and that any complete assessment must enter, because it changed the population whose welfare the output was supposed to serve. At the revolution, the Soviet population was overwhelmingly rural, its life governed by the village, the season, and the commune. Within two generations it was majority urban, housed in the new industrial cities and the expanded capitals, working to the clock rather than the harvest, and educated to levels that made it, by the system's final decades, one of the most schooled populations on earth, with a technical intelligentsia numbering in the millions and a scientific establishment that published across every discipline. Whatever is said of the economy's efficiency, the population it produced was not the population it inherited, and the difference is the transformation's most durable residue: the successor societies, whatever their subsequent trajectories, began from a human capital stock, literate, urban, technically trained, that the starting point did not possess and that no subsequent failure has dissipated.

The educational achievement deserves its detail, because it is the provision success least vulnerable to the measurement disputes. Enrolments can be counted in heads rather than in prices, and the heads are corroborated by the census record, by the testimony of every observer, and by the occupational structure the economy actually displayed. A state that could not stock a shop reliably could and did teach a continent to read, staff universities across eleven time zones, and produce engineers in numbers that the frontier economy watched with professional anxiety. The achievement's shadow side is recorded elsewhere in this article: curricula under political control, the humanities and social sciences deformed by doctrine, whole disciplines, genetics most notoriously, destroyed for a generation by ideological intervention. The shadow is real and it is not the whole picture. The mathematics that Western journals translated cover to cover, the physics that armed the state and illuminated its science, the engineering that built the dams and the stations, were taught in those universities to the children of peasants, and the social mobility thereby effected, for all its political rationing, was among the largest in the century's history. A record that omits it is not a record.

Urbanisation carried its own double entry. The new cities were built at speeds that guaranteed their shoddiness, and the housing shortage they embodied has been entered in this article's debit column. But the movement itself, from the village world of the starting point to an urban society with urban services, urban medicine, and urban expectations, is the social form of the industrialisation the growth figures struggle to measure, and it proceeded on a scale that the physical evidence of the built environment renders undeniable. The visitor to the successor states walks through the record: the metro systems, the district heating, the institute campuses, the standardised housing districts that shelter the population still. The built environment is the one dataset that cannot be revised, and its testimony is that something enormous was constructed, at enormous cost, by methods that wasted much of what they built and built much that no alternative on offer was building. Holding those clauses together is, once again, the whole discipline of this subject.

## Reform attempts as evidence: what the system could not change

The history of economic reform inside the system is usually told as politics, and it belongs in this article as evidence, because each attempt functioned as an experiment testing which elements of the model could be altered and which were load bearing. The Soviet reform of the middle 1960s, associated with Premier Alexei Kosygin and drawing on the economist Evsei Liberman's proposals, sought to judge enterprises by sales and profit rather than gross output, to free a margin of enterprise decision, and to make prices carry more information. Within a few years the ministries had reabsorbed the autonomy, the indicators had multiplied back into a thicket in which gross output again dominated, and the experiment closed. The Hungarian New Economic Mechanism, launched at the decade's end, went further and lasted longer, freeing many prices, tolerating a private margin, and exposing enterprises to a partial market discipline, and it delivered the consumer performance recorded earlier in this article. The Yugoslav experiment, outside the bloc after the break with Moscow, replaced state ownership in form with social ownership and workers' self management, marketised much of the economy, and produced a record of its own, growth with inflation and unemployment and regional divergence, that demonstrated both the gains available from relaxing the model and the instabilities of the halfway house. The Prague Spring's economic programme, the most intellectually ambitious of the reforms, was terminated by invasion before its evidence could accumulate, which is itself the datum: the point at which economic reform threatened the political monopoly, the monopoly answered with tanks, in a fraternal capital, and every subsequent reformer in the bloc worked inside the boundary that answer had drawn.

The pattern across the experiments is the finding. Reforms that adjusted indicators within the system produced marginal gains and were absorbed. Reforms that introduced genuine market information and enterprise autonomy produced real gains and were contained, diluted, or destroyed at the point where their logic implicated the allocation of resources by political authority, which is to say at the point where they began to work as intended. The system's economic problem, on this evidence, was not that its managers chose bad policies from a menu that included good ones. It was that the policies capable of accomplishing the efficiency transition were the same policies that dissolved the system's defining institution, the party's direction of the economy, and the institution defended itself with the consistency of an organism. That is why the deceleration could be diagnosed inside the system, by its own economists, in print, for two decades, without being treated: the diagnosis was available and the treatment was inadmissible. A record of growth that ends in stagnation despite universal recognition of the stagnation's causes is a record about political economy in the deepest sense, and it is the strongest single argument the critical literature possesses, stronger than any growth figure, because it does not depend on one.

## Trade within the bloc: integration without a market

The bloc was also an experiment in international economic organisation, and its record belongs in a complete assessment, because the Council for Mutual Economic Assistance attempted for four decades what the Western economies accomplished through the market and the treaty: the integration of national economies into a division of labour. The attempt had real achievements to its name. Energy grids were interconnected across the bloc, pipelines bound the Soviet fields to the East European refineries in a physical integration that survives in the successor states' infrastructure, and specialisation agreements assigned branches among the members in a planned counterpart of the multinational supply chain. The system's trade prices, however, reproduced at the international level the pathologies of the domestic price system. Prices within the bloc were set by formula from world market averages of earlier years, revised at intervals, so that they lagged every movement of the world market and converted the energy price shocks of the 1970s into a slow motion transfer from the Soviet exporter to the East European importers, a subsidy whose size the literature has debated ever since and whose existence no one disputes. Trade was balanced bilaterally in inconvertible units, which meant that a surplus with one partner could not be spent with another, and the incentive structure of domestic planning reproduced itself between states: each member hoarded the goods others wanted and pressed the goods nobody wanted on its partners, in the diplomatic form of the shortage economy.

The integration's deepest failure mirrors the domestic record with instructive fidelity. A division of labour imposed by negotiation among ministries inherits the information problems of the ministries, multiplied by sovereignty. The specialisation agreements were honoured in the breach as each state pursued its own completeness in steel, in machinery, in chemicals, duplicating capacities across a bloc whose rationale was their concentration. When the system ended, the trade structure collapsed with a speed that revealed how little of it had rested on economic advantage as the market measures advantage, and the successor economies reoriented their trade westward within years, at severe transitional cost. The bloc's trade record thus supplies a coda to the measurement argument of this article: here too, the volumes were real and the values were artefacts, and here too the verdict depends on whether the base is the physical integration achieved, pipelines and grids that still function, or the economic integration claimed, which the first contact with world prices dissolved.

## The numbers as weapons: the record in Cold War politics

No account of this record is complete that ignores what the numbers were for, in the decades when they were produced, because the political use of the figures shaped the figures themselves and certainly shaped their reception. The Soviet leadership made growth a weapon of statecraft, announcing overtaking targets, publishing plan fulfilments as victories in a world contest, and staging the system's achievements, the exhibitions, the space spectaculars, the model farms shown to delegations, as arguments addressed to the uncommitted world. The Western response built the reconstruction enterprise this article has described, housed substantially inside the intelligence services, and its estimates circulated in a political atmosphere in which a high estimate could be attacked as alarmism and a low one as complacency, so that the estimators worked under pressures symmetrical to those on the statisticians they studied, if gentler in consequence. The developing world, whose allegiance both camps courted, received the dispute as a choice of models, and several of its governments adopted planning apparatuses and material product accounting on the strength of growth claims that neither side's specialists would have defended unqualified. The record's numbers thus had a career independent of the record, and the career left deposits in the literature, textbook certainties, polemical classics, and inherited suspicions, that a reader of the subject still has to dig through to reach the evidence.

The digging is easier now than it was, and the reason deserves a sentence of gratitude in an article that has had little occasion for any. The opening of the archives after the system's end, incomplete, uneven across the successor states, and in places since restricted again, nevertheless transformed the field's evidentiary base within a decade, confirming the broad shape the best reconstructions had drawn, correcting the levels in the direction the physical indicator method had indicated, and replacing a literature of estimation under ignorance with a literature of documentation. The scholars who had worked in the dark, on both sides of the old divide, were vindicated and refuted in proportions that tracked the quality of their methods rather than the colour of their politics, which is as it should be and is the closest thing to a happy ending that the history of a statistic affords. The reader who takes one lesson from this section should take that correlation. In a field saturated with allegiance, method was destiny, and the figures that survived the archives were the figures whose makers had shown their working.

## Science and the research establishment: strength without transmission

The scientific record deserves separate treatment because it is the case where the system's defenders and critics can exchange their usual positions, and the exchange is illuminating. Soviet fundamental science, in mathematics, theoretical physics, and several branches of chemistry and earth science, stood at the world level across the whole period, produced laureates and schools whose work the frontier literature absorbed, and did so on resources far below the frontier's. The organisation that achieved this was the academy system: research concentrated in institutes separated from the universities and from production, generously funded in the favoured disciplines, insulated in its internal life by the prestige of its leading figures, and connected to the economy by the weakest of transmission belts. The insulation explains both halves of the record. Within the institutes, a culture of genuine inquiry survived political weather that destroyed other disciplines, because the state needed the results and learned, after the catastrophes of its interventions in genetics and the near catastrophe of its threatened intervention in physics, to purchase results with a measure of autonomy. Between the institutes and the factories, the results travelled badly, for the reasons the technology section gave: no enterprise gained by adopting them, no ministry gained by ordering their adoption against its output indicators, and the research establishment's excellence therefore accumulated in journals and in weapons rather than in the productivity of the civilian economy.

The human sciences and the disciplines adjacent to ideology present the record's inverse. Economics itself, the science this article practises at second hand, was deformed at its centre: the political economy of socialism, as an official discipline, existed to systematise the current doctrine, and the genuine economic analysis the system produced came from its margins, from mathematical economists whose linear programming and optimal planning work, associated with Leonid Kantorovich, who received the Nobel recognition his own system had delayed for decades, proceeded in a technical idiom that partially shielded it, and from heterodox figures such as Khanin whose relationship to the official discipline was adversarial. History was conscripted, sociology abolished and refounded, genetics destroyed and rebuilt. The pattern is consistent with everything else in this record: capability concentrated where the state defined a goal and protected the work, sterility where the work's conclusions might judge the state, and a transmission failure between knowledge and application wherever application required the initiative of those the indicators governed. A system is entitled to be proud of its mathematics. It is not entitled to cite the mathematics as evidence about its economy, when the economy's own science was the one it would not permit itself.

## The welfare settlement: pensions, prices, and security

Between the provision achievements and the consumer failures stands the welfare settlement proper, the set of guarantees that defined the system's social contract and whose terms the population understood precisely. Retirement pensions, generalised across the urban workforce and extended, later and more thinly, to the collective farm population, paid at ages low by international comparison and at replacement levels modest but real. Prices of the staples, bread, milk, rent, transport, utilities, held nominal by subsidy across decades, so that the cost of subsistence was insulated from the economy's failures in a way no market system attempts. Child provision through subsidised nurseries and kindergartens, incomplete in coverage and variable in quality, supporting the female employment the system required. Sickness and disability provision through the workplace and the clinic network. The settlement's character can be stated in a contrast: it was comprehensive in coverage and shallow in level, universal in entitlement and modest in amount, and above all it was secure, in the specific sense that its terms did not vary with the business cycle, with the employer's fortunes, or with the individual's market value, because there was no cycle, no employer's fortune, and no market to vary them. Security of that kind is a real good, priced at nothing in the growth accounts, and its presence explains the retrospective judgements this article noted earlier, in which populations that rejected the system entire continued to rank its guarantees among the things they had lost.

The settlement's costs were the obverse of its virtues and belong in the same paragraph of the ledger. Prices held nominal for decades ceased to carry information or to clear markets, and the subsidies that held them consumed a share of the budget that grew as the pretence was maintained, crowding out the investment in the service sectors the population increasingly wanted. Provision through the workplace tied welfare to the enterprise and thereby to the state's employment structure, immuring workers in jobs the economy needed to shed and making the enterprise a welfare ministry with a factory attached. The universality was shallower at the edges than at the centre: the rural population's pensions arrived late and low, the unregistered and the marginal stood outside the settlement's protections, and the privileged strata drew their provision through separate channels, the closed shops, the special clinics, the allocated housing, whose existence the ideology denied and the population catalogued in detail. A settlement can be genuinely universal in its floor and genuinely stratified above it, and this one was, and the stratification's hypocrisy, equality proclaimed over a structure of privilege, corroded the legitimacy the settlement was built to purchase. The corrosion is part of the record too, and it helps explain why a population that valued the guarantees did not, in the end, defend the system that provided them.

## A worked example: one claim carried through every base

The article's method can be shown, rather than merely described, by carrying a single familiar claim through the bases in sequence. Take the claim that the Soviet economy succeeded. Against the starting point base, measured from the empire of 1913 to the system's final decades, on the international reconstructions for output and the demographic record for welfare, the claim is true in the directional sense this article has defended: output, industry, literacy, urbanisation, and provision all stand transformed beyond recognition from the base, and no reconstruction, however hostile, returns a verdict of failure on that span. Against the frontier base, measured against Western Europe and the United States across the postwar decades, the claim is false in the same directional sense: relative output per head did not converge, consumption diverged further than production, and the system's own overtaking pledge was abandoned by its authors. Against the cohort base, the claim is true for the middle decades and false thereafter, a phase verdict that the East Asian transition converts from mixed to adverse as the span extends. Against the divided country control, the claim fails in both theatres, with the early Korean decades entered as the exception that confirms the phase structure. The claim, carried honestly, thus dissolves into four claims, of which two are true, one is divided by period, and one is false, and the original sentence, the Soviet economy succeeded, is revealed as a device for selecting among the four without announcing the selection. Every famous sentence in this dispute dissolves the same way under the same treatment, and the reader who performs the dissolution habitually will find that the dispute's literature divides, with very few exceptions, into works that announce their base and works that hope the reader will not ask for it.

The worked example also shows why the divided cases carry the argumentative weight this article assigns them. Each of the other bases leaves a residue of discretion: the starting point's counterfactual, the frontier's unequal endowments, the cohort's membership. The divided cases minimise the residue, not to zero, reparations and aid and embargoes stand in the margin of both, but below the threshold at which discretion can reverse the direction. That is the precise sense in which the comparison base decides. It is not that bases are arbitrary, or that all verdicts are equal. It is that the verdict is a function of the base, that the function is steep, and that the one base where the function's inputs were chosen by history rather than by the disputants returns the clearest reading. Scholarship consists of reporting the whole function. Advocacy consists of reporting one point on it. This article has tried to report the function, and its verdict section now gathers the readings into the only form they can honestly take: a set of judgements, each with its base attached.

## The verdict: judgements with their bases attached

The verdict, stated as this article's discipline requires, is a set of judgements rather than a sentence. Against the inheritance, the record is a transformation of the first magnitude, achieved at a human cost that belongs inside the assessment and not in a footnote to it, and financed by the consumption of the generation that performed it. Against the frontier, the record is a failure to converge that falsified the system's central economic promise in the terms the system itself had chosen. Against the comparable cohort, the record is phase structured: a mobilisation strategy of exceptional effectiveness at the stage where mobilisation is development, followed by an incapacity, rooted in the political monopoly's defence of itself, to accomplish the transition to efficiency on which further development depended. Against the divided country controls, the record is adverse in both theatres, with the adverse reading strengthening as the spans lengthen and the starting advantages of the socialist halves are taken into account. On provision, the record is a genuine and generalisable achievement in literacy, basic health, employment security, and the welfare floor, shadowed by the mortality reversal that stands as the mature system's most damaging single indicator. On consumption, quality, agriculture, and civilian innovation, the record is a persistent, structural failure, mitigated in the Hungarian variant, unmitigated in the Romanian, and never overcome anywhere in the bloc. On measurement, the record's statistics were neither the truth nor a fabrication but the output of a coherent accounting system whose concepts narrowed the economy, whose prices flattered it, whose base reporting was corrupted by the incentives of fulfilment, and whose worst series were suppressed when they turned.

One further judgement belongs to the populations rather than the systems. The people who inhabited these economies were their instruments, their beneficiaries, and their casualties in proportions that shifted by decade, by republic, by village and city, and by the accident of birth into a favoured or a suspect category. Their testimony, gathered in the successor period's oral histories, refuses the aggregate as firmly as the statisticians' critics do: the same witness praises the school and curses the shop, mourns the pension and remembers the queue, and declines to surrender either memory to the other's verdict. An assessment that aspires to completeness should end where they stand, inside the conjunction, because the conjunction is not a failure of their reasoning. It is the contour of the life being described, and the evidence, properly handled, points toward it from every standard of comparison surveyed in these pages.

No single sentence summarises that set without selecting from it, and the refusal to select is the article's conclusion as much as its method. What can be said in one sentence is the claim with which the article began, now earned rather than asserted. The comparison base decides, the divided countries are the controls that discipline the choice, and the record, read whole, is of a system that could mobilise anything except the information it needed to become something else. The states are gone. The question their record poses, what an economy organised against its own information can and cannot do, and at what price in both directions, has not gone with them, and it is why this record remains, decades after the last plan was fulfilled on paper, among the most instructive documents in economic history.

## Transport and infrastructure: the arteries of the extensive method

The physical infrastructure of the bloc is the record rendered in concrete, and it displays the extensive method's virtues and limits in their purest form. The railway network, inherited as one of the world's largest and extended across the whole territory, carried a freight burden per kilometre that no Western system approached, moving the coal, ore, steel, and grain of the material economy with an intensity that wore the permanent way as fast as it was renewed. Electrification of the trunk lines, the metro systems of the capitals and then of the provincial cities, the intercontinental pipelines, the unified power grid synchronised across eleven time zones, the river fleets and the icebreaker route along the Arctic coast: the inventory is of a civilisation that built its arteries first and its amenities later, and the arteries, judged as engineering, were achievements of the first order, several of them without parallel anywhere. The passenger side tells the familiar obverse. Air travel was cheap, extensive, and uncomfortable; the road network for private motoring barely existed outside the capitals, since the private car was a rationed privilege rather than a consumer good for most of the period; and the freight system's very intensity reflected an economy that moved bulk because it could not move information, hauling materials across continental distances between specialised plants in a pattern that a price system would have reorganised within a decade.

Infrastructure also supplies the record's most melancholy exhibit, the unfinished and the unmaintainable. A system rewarded for new construction starts and under rewarded for maintenance accumulates a capital stock whose recorded value assumes an upkeep that was never performed, and the successor states inherited power stations, housing blocks, and industrial plant whose real condition diverged from their book condition by the accumulated arrears of decades. The divergence is another of the measurement problem's faces, appearing this time inside the capital stock rather than the flow accounts: depreciation in the material product system was an accounting convention, not a fund, and the convention's optimism is embedded in every level estimate the reconstructions produced. When the eastern German capital stock was valued at unification, the write downs recorded in that settlement were, in part, the arrears made visible. The lesson generalises beyond the bloc. Any economy whose accounts reward the new over the maintained will, in time, be found to own less than it reports, and the finding always arrives as a surprise to those who read the reports.

## Development within the union: the republics and the levelling claim

The Soviet state claimed, among its economic achievements, the levelling of its constituent peoples: the raising of the Central Asian, Caucasian, and Siberian territories from colonial backwardness toward the level of the European core, through directed investment, the transfer of industry and specialists, and the construction of the provision systems, schools, clinics, universities, in republics where the starting point had been, in several cases, a population largely without literacy in any language. The claim, assessed directionally, has substance. The republics' indicators of literacy, schooling, health access, and urbanisation converged toward the union's averages across the period to a degree that the colonial comparisons of the starting point make remarkable, and the affirmative literature is entitled to the convergence as it is entitled to the literacy campaigns that drove it. The qualifications are equally part of the record. The development was directed toward the centre's purposes, cotton monoculture imposed on Central Asia with ecological consequences that the environmental assessment records, industry implanted without local linkages, and the demographic balance of several republics altered by migration encouraged from the core. The levelling was real and it was instrumental, a combination the record displays at every level, and the successor states' divergence after the union's end, some building on the human capital the levelling left, others collapsing into war or personalised rule, belongs to their own histories rather than to the verdict on this one. What the union's internal record adds to the article's argument is a further comparison base, the internal one, on which the system's performance appears in its most favourable light, since the base is the colonial starting point and the span is the whole career. That the affirmative literature rarely exploits its best base, preferring the contest with the frontier it cannot win, is a curiosity of the dispute that the comparison base thesis predicts: each side selects the base that suits its polemic, and the polemic of pride demanded the frontier.

## Health care in depth: the system behind the indicator

The mortality reversal has been stated, and the health system behind it deserves the same two sided treatment as every other element of the record, because its structure explains how a service can be simultaneously a genuine achievement and the site of a historic failure. The system's design was, on paper and largely in fact, the most rationally organised of its era: a unified state service, free at the point of use, built on prevention, with a hierarchy from the rural feldsher post through the district polyclinic to the specialised institute, a sanitary inspectorate with real powers, and a doctrine that treated health as a condition of production to be engineered like output. Against the infectious diseases that were the starting point's great killers, the design was devastatingly effective, and the campaigns against typhus, malaria in the southern republics, tuberculosis, and the childhood infections rank among the public health achievements of the century. The system's medical workforce grew to a density among the highest recorded, its hospital bed stock likewise, and a reader of the input statistics alone would conclude that no population was ever so doctored.

The failure entered through the same door as the economy's general failure: the indicators. A service judged on beds, visits, and staffing will produce beds, visits, and staffing, while the outcomes those inputs exist to purchase, survival, recovery, the management of chronic disease, go unmeasured and therefore unmanaged. The mature system's disease burden had shifted, as every maturing society's does, from infection to the cardiovascular and oncological conditions whose treatment depends on pharmaceuticals, on technology, on continuity of care, and on a candour about risk factors, alcohol above all, that the fiscal interest in vodka revenue and the ideological interest in optimism jointly forbade. The result was a health system with the inputs of a rich country and, increasingly, the outcomes of a poor one, staffed by a feminised and low paid medical profession whose dedication the memoir literature records with respect and whose equipment the same literature records with despair. The reversal in the headline indicator was the summary of that structure, and it is why this article ranks it as it does: not as an anomaly in an otherwise successful provision record, but as the provision record's own internal verdict, delivered in the one currency, survival, that no accounting convention can redenominate.

## The indicator society: gross output and the corruption of measure

It is worth gathering, in one section, the thread that has run through every part of this article, because it converts a set of observations about statistics into a finding about the society. The planned economy governed by indicators, and the indicators, once they became the targets of reward and punishment, ceased to measure the activity they named. Output indicators produced assortment distortion, the famous production of oversized and unusable goods where the measure was weight, of fragile goods where the measure was units, of whatever the indicator counted in whatever form counted most easily. Quality indicators, introduced to correct the distortion, were gamed in their turn. Plan fulfilment indicators produced the storming at the period's end, the falsified return, the hidden reserve, and the negotiation for a lower target that the planning literature documents as the system's normal metabolism. The phenomenon is not socialist in essence; it is the general law of indicator governance, known in every bureaucracy and corporation, and the market economies exhibit it wherever a metric becomes a target. The planned economy's distinction was that no measurement existed outside the indicator system. A market economy's distorted internal metrics coexist with prices, profits, bankruptcies, and a press, which, however imperfectly, register the distortion's consequences from outside. The planned economy had abolished the outside. Its statistics were its only mirror, and the mirror was made of the same material as the face.

This is the deepest stratum of the measurement problem, and it reframes the fabrication question with which the article dealt earlier. The central statisticians did not need to invent an economy. The economy's own reporting metabolism, from the enterprise return upward, generated a representation systematically more flattering than the reality, through a million rational acts of self protection, and the centre's concepts and prices then processed the flattery into aggregates whose relationship to the lived economy loosened decade by decade. The system's tragedy, stated informationally, is that it could not know itself. Its rulers governed a representation, its reformers argued with a representation, and the population, which inhabited the reality, drew the conclusion that the representation was a lie even where it was merely a distortion, with consequences for the legitimacy of everything else the state asserted. A reader who grasps this section has grasped why this article began with accounting and why the accounting was never a technicality. The measurement problem was the system, viewed from the angle of its knowledge of itself, and its failure to solve that problem is the thread that connects the growth deceleration, the consumer shortage, the mortality suppression, and the final collapse of belief into one story.

## Food and diet: the most intimate indicator

Of all the dimensions of the record, diet is the one the population experienced three times a day, and its history condenses the whole assessment into a single sense. The starting point's diet was a peasant subsistence, adequate in good years, lethal in bad ones, narrow in composition, and governed by the harvest. The transformation's first act made it worse before it made it better: collectivisation and the procurement system stripped the countryside, the cities were rationed, and the famine years stand at the record's moral nadir, the point at which the economic assessment and the human catastrophe are the same event described in two vocabularies. Recovery from that nadir was real. Across the postwar decades the diet's quantity rose on every reconstruction, its composition broadened from bread and potatoes toward meat, dairy, sugar, and vegetables, and the urban population of the final decades ate, by the standards of its own grandparents, abundantly. The failures persisted inside the improvement: supply was erratic, quality poor, regional variation extreme, the meat counter a standing political grievance, and the system's final decades saw provincial cities where the staple assortment of the capital was a rumour. The dietary record thus replicates the master pattern exactly: transformation from the base, inadequacy against the frontier, phase dependence in between, and a population whose judgement was formed not by the aggregate but by the queue for the particular.

The food economy also displays, in its purest form, the interaction of the system's priorities with its information failure. Agriculture's underperformance has been entered in this article as an incentive failure, and it was; but the food chain's losses continued after the harvest, in transport, storage, and distribution, where produce rotted between the field and the shelf in quantities that the system's own press, in its franker periods, reported with astonishment. A chain in which no link owns the outcome will lose the harvest link by link, and the losses were the more galling because the system had demonstrated, in its priority sectors, an organisational capacity of the highest order. It could supply an army at the front across a continent. It could not supply a provincial city with vegetables across a province, because the army's supply was a priority with a commander and the vegetables were a residual with a statistic. Priority, in this record, is destiny: what the system prioritised, it achieved at world level; what it treated as residual, it failed at levels that shamed its own statistics; and the population's welfare lived almost entirely in the residual categories. That sentence, generalised, is as close as this article comes to a theory of the whole record, and the reader will notice that it explains the space programme and the sausage queue with a single premise, which is what theories are for.

## The scholars of the record: a guide to the authorities

A reader who wishes to go further deserves a map of the authorities, since the field's literature is large, unevenly available, and divided by the very disputes this article has described. The reconstruction enterprise begins with Abram Bergson, whose methodological writings remain the model of how to rebuild an alien accounting system with the assumptions in the open, and continues through the intelligence estimates and their retrospective assessments, in which the estimators themselves, to their credit, conducted much of the correction. The internal critique is represented by Grigory Khanin, whose physical indicator reconstructions, available in English mainly through summaries and the secondary literature, state the lowest defensible path, and by the Soviet statistical profession's own debates, accessible in translation collections from the system's final years. The international projects are represented by the historical statistics associated with Angus Maddison and by the collaborative economic histories of the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe produced after the archives opened, among which the works of Mark Harrison on the wartime and postwar economy, of Paul Gregory on the system's political economy, and of Stephen Wheatcroft on the demographic and agricultural catastrophes set standards of archival practice. The affirmative reconstruction is Robert Allen's, and every reader should meet it in the original, since it is the strongest form of the case this article answered. The analytical literature on the system's functioning is headed by János Kornai, whose shortage economy framework organises more of the record than any rival, and by Alec Nove, whose economic history of the Soviet Union remains, decades on, the single volume in which the achievement and the failure are held in one hand with the least flinching. A reader who works through those authorities in that order will possess the field, and will notice that their disagreements, fierce as they are, concern magnitudes and bases rather than the shape this article has reported, which all of them, from their opposite ends of the dispute, confirm.

## What the record tests: implications for the theory

This article belongs to a series on Marxism, and its bearing on the theory should be stated with the same care as its bearing on the states, since the two are persistently confused in both directions. The record does not test Marx's analysis of capitalism, which concerns a different object, and the habit of treating Soviet growth figures as a referendum on the labour theory of value or the analysis of crisis is a category error that [the series' article on testing Marxist economics](/2016/08/01/testing-marxist-economics/) examines in full. What the record does test is a set of propositions that the twentieth century's Marxist movements actually held and acted on: that the abolition of private ownership of the means of production would release the productive forces from the fetters of the market; that planning could replace the price mechanism as an allocation system at the scale of a modern economy; that the suppression of commodity production in the commanding sectors was compatible with rising welfare; and that a state organised on these principles would demonstrate its superiority in output per head within a historical generation. Those propositions were stated by the movements themselves, in their programmes and their leaders' pledges, and the record is their test. On the evidence assembled here, the mobilisation propositions survive in qualified form: the system did release a formidable development of the productive forces from a low base, by methods its theory had not foreseen and at costs its theory had not admitted. The allocation propositions fail: planning as practised could not replace the information and incentive functions of prices, and the failure was structural rather than incidental, demonstrated by four decades of reform attempts that foundered on the same rock. The welfare propositions divide: provision of the floor was achieved beyond what comparable economies managed, and the dynamic welfare of rising consumption, quality, and choice was not. The demonstration proposition, the overtaking pledge, failed on its own terms and was abandoned by its makers.

That distribution of verdicts, some confirmed, some divided, some refuted, is what a test of a research programme looks like when it is conducted honestly, and it is more damaging to the programme's orthodox form than any wholesale dismissal, because it cannot be waved away as prejudice. It is equally more favourable than the wholesale dismissal, because the confirmed elements, the mobilisation capacity, the provision floor, the educational transformation, are real findings about what collective organisation can do, and a successor theory that cannot explain them is as incomplete as the orthodoxy that cannot explain the failures. The record's final service to the theory is thus disciplinary: it converts a faith and a phobia, the two popular relations to this history, into a set of findings with bases attached, and it leaves the argument where arguments belong, on the findings. The prior definitional question, whether a system carrying this record counts as socialist at all, is argued on its own terms in [was the USSR socialist](/2019/05/15/was-the-ussr-socialist/).

## Housing in depth: the construction achievement and its arithmetic

Housing has appeared in this article in fragments, and it earns a consolidated treatment because it is the dimension where the record's two faces meet in a single artefact that the reader can still inspect. The starting point's urban housing was a catastrophe of overcrowding compounded by war: families in corners of communal rooms, in barracks, in dugouts in the liberated cities, at densities that the sanitary statistics of the period render in disease. The response, from the middle 1950s, was the largest housebuilding programme in the world's history to that date, standardised estates of prefabricated blocks rising in every city of the bloc, moving tens of millions into self contained flats with central heating, running water, and a kitchen of one's own. The programme's social effect was transformative in a register the growth figures do not capture: privacy, the precondition of a private life, was delivered to a population that had been denied it for two generations, and the delivery was remembered, in the retrospective literature, as among the system's unambiguous gifts. The blocks were ugly, the flats small, the construction quality poor and deteriorating with the decades, the estates underprovided with the shops and services their plans promised, and the waiting lists for improvement stretched across working lifetimes. All of that is true, and the population that queued for the flats and cursed their walls also defended the programme that built them, because the alternative in living memory was the communal corner. Housing is therefore the record in miniature and in concrete: an achievement that was real, enormous, and insufficient, delivered by the mobilisation method at the moment the method still worked, and decaying thereafter into the maintenance arrears and quality failure of the extensive economy's old age. The estates still stand across the successor states, housing the grandchildren of the families they first sheltered, which makes them the one part of the record that its subjects continue, literally, to inhabit.

## The scope of the record: which economies, which years

Precision about scope protects every claim in this article, and a section stating it is owed before the personas and questions that close the piece. The record assessed here is, in its core, the record of the Soviet Union from the late 1920s to 1991 and of the East European states from the later 1940s to 1989, the economies that operated the material product system under the classical planning model for spans long enough to constitute a test. Mongolia followed the model as the bloc's Asian member and its record tracks the East European pattern at a lower starting level. The Asian and Caribbean cases that adopted Marxist governments under different conditions, China above all, Vietnam, and Cuba, possess records of their own that this series treats in the regional clusters devoted to them, and they enter this article only where the comparison requires, as in the Korean control, where the divided peninsula belongs to the European story's logic because the division replicated its structure. Yugoslavia, socialist in its own self description and outside the bloc's discipline for most of its life, functions in this article as a variant and a control rather than as a member, its market socialist hybrid illuminating by contrast what the classical model's institutions caused. The temporal scope matters as much as the geographical. The record's phases, mobilisation, broadening, deceleration, stagnation, are dated to the Soviet core, and the East European states run the same phases on a compressed and offset calendar, entering at the mobilisation phase in the later 1940s and reaching stagnation within four decades. Claims in the literature that silently extend the record to economies outside this scope, or compress its phases into a single verdict on a single decade, fail the provenance practice this article has taught, and the reader now has the equipment to detect the failure unassisted.

## The service sectors the accounts excluded: growth that did not count

Return, near the article's end, to the accounting exclusion with which it began, because the exclusion conceals a final irony that completes the measurement story. The services that net material product refused to count, health, education, administration, passenger transport, personal services, were among the fastest growing activities of the mature socialist economies, as they are of every maturing economy. The teacher and the surgeon multiplied across the decades exactly as the material branches' growth slowed, so that the official aggregate, by construction, progressively understated the economy's real expansion of activity in precisely the decades when the understatement did the system's reputation the most damage in Western comparisons, which were typically run between the bloc's narrow aggregate and the West's broad one. The reconstructions corrected the exclusion, and the correction is one reason the Western series, for all their faults, stand above the official ones as instruments. But the irony deserves its moment: the system's own accounting doctrine, devised from its theory of productive labour, hid from the world the growth of the very provision sectors that constituted the system's best claim on the world's respect. The schools and clinics that the affirmative case rightly celebrates were, in the official numbers, literally worthless, counted as a charge on the product rather than as part of it. A doctrine that cannot see its own achievements is a fitting emblem for the record's informational tragedy, and the emblem is not a construction of this article. It is in the accounts, where it always was, waiting for a reader patient enough to notice what the aggregate excluded and what the exclusion cost the excluders.

## The final measurement irony: the collapse of the counters

The record has a coda that belongs to it, although the events fall outside the record's own span. When the systems ended, the successor economies underwent a contraction of measured output so severe that, in several cases, the recorded fall exceeded the recorded achievements of decades, and a new measurement dispute opened instantly over the corpse of the old one. The contraction was real: industries closed, supply chains dissolved, and the populations endured a shock whose welfare costs, in the mortality crisis of the principal successor state, exceeded the reversal this article recorded for the mature system. But the measured contraction overstated the real one by mechanisms this article's reader will recognise at sight. The old aggregates had counted output the market valued at nothing, so its disappearance registered as loss where the welfare loss was smaller or nil; the new economies' activity migrated into services and informality that the inherited statistical apparatus, built for the material product, could not see; and the price liberalisation converted hidden inflation into open inflation, wrecking the real series at the moment of transition. The counters collapsed with the counted. The episode is the measurement dominance pattern's eighth instance in embryo, and it carries the pattern's moral into the present tense of the successor societies: the first casualty of a systemic change is the statistics by which the change will be judged, and every verdict on the transition, like every verdict on the record that preceded it, must carry its basis in the open or be read as advocacy. This article ends its substantive argument there, in the conviction that the habit of asking for the basis is the most useful thing a reader can take from the whole melancholy, magnificent, and instructive record.

## Taut planning and the shape of the working year

The record's texture includes a rhythm that no annual aggregate displays: the rhythm of the plan period itself. Targets were set taut, in the planners' own term, meaning at or beyond the edge of the achievable, on the theory that strain elicits effort. The consequence was a working year with a distinctive pulse, slack at the period's opening while materials were awaited and negotiated, frenzy at its close as enterprises stormed to fulfil the figures on which bonuses, premiums, and careers depended. Output bunched at the reporting dates, quality collapsed in the storming weeks, machinery was run without maintenance and repaired in the slack, and the assortment delivered bore the marks of the calendar in which it was made. The aggregates average the pulse away, but the pulse was the economy as its participants knew it, and it imposed costs that the annual figures omit: the spoilage of rushed work, the accidents of exhausted shifts, the capital worn by abuse and idled by disrepair in alternation. A growth figure for such an economy is a photograph of a runner taken at midnight, accurate as far as it goes, and the distance it does not go is measured in the difference between the economy's statistical year and its lived one. The planning article in this series treats the mechanism in full. Here the point is bookkeeping: when the reconstructions disagree about the level of output, part of what they disagree about, without knowing it, is how much of the stormed output should count as output at all, a question with no technical answer, which is one more reason this article reports directions and declines the decimals that pretend otherwise.

## Privilege and the economy of access

Every economy distributes advantage. This one distributed it through access, and the distribution belongs in the record because it determined what the aggregate provision figures meant for actual households. Above the universal floor stood a graduated structure of privilege attached to position: closed distribution networks where scarce goods circulated at official prices for the entitled, special clinics and hospitals, allocated housing in the preferred districts, official cars, dachas, and the hard currency shops where the goods the ordinary citizen queued for, or never saw, were available to those with the certificates. The structure was not a corruption of the system from outside. It was the system's own mechanism for rewarding the stratum that administered it, in a society that had abolished the monetary forms of reward its ideology condemned and therefore paid its elite in kind, in access, in the currency of the shortage economy itself. The economic significance is double. First, the aggregates overstate the equality of the provision they record: the floor was universal, but the distance between the floor and the entitled standard widened with every scarcity, so that shortage itself was a regressive tax, paid by the unconnected and rebated to the connected. Second, the privilege structure corroded the indicator system from the top, since the stratum best placed to report the economy's failures was the stratum best insulated from them, travelling in cars it did not queue for to shops that were never empty. A ruling group that does not experience its economy's failures will learn of them late, and the lateness is written across the record's final decades, in which the leadership's picture of the country, drawn from the representation rather than the queue, diverged ever further from the country's picture of itself. The divergence is not measurable in the aggregates. It is, in the end, why the aggregates ceased to matter.

## Population: the demographic record beneath the economic one

The population itself is a record, and its broad movements corroborate the economic narrative in the manner of an independent witness. The starting point's population was growing rapidly, its growth checked by the catastrophes this article has recorded, war, famine, and terror, each of which carved a notch into the age structure that demographers can still read in the successor populations, generations thinned at the points where the century's violence passed through them. Across the postwar decades the population completed the classic transition: mortality fell first, on the provision achievements, then fertility fell, on urbanisation, education, female employment, and the housing constraint, until the European republics approached or reached the replacement boundary and the state found itself exhorting births it had spent decades making impractical in flats of the size it built. The Central Asian republics ran the transition later and faster, their populations multiplying across the period and shifting the union's demographic centre of gravity southward and eastward in a movement whose political consequences the system's final years were beginning to register. Ageing arrived in the European territories by the record's end, with the pension settlement's costs rising against a labour force whose growth the extensive method had required and could no longer command. The demographic record thus closes the extensive story from the population side: an economy built to mobilise abundant labour ended with labour scarce, ageing, and increasingly unwilling to be mobilised, and the transition from abundance to scarcity, in people as in land and capital, is the record's deepest rhythm. The demographers, working with the hardest data the system produced, told that story earliest and most accurately, which is why this article has leaned on their findings at the points where the economic statistics could not be trusted to tell it themselves.

## Energy: the late success that concealed the failure

The energy sector deserves a section of its own in the debit and credit accounting, because it was at once the mature system's greatest material success and the instrument of its self deception. The development of the west Siberian fields, in conditions of Arctic difficulty that the engineering record honours, made the Soviet Union the world's largest producer of oil and gas in its final decades, a superpower in hydrocarbons as in arms. The achievement was real, and it was extensive growth in its purest form: new reserves, new fields, new pipelines, more of everything, extracted from a frontier that geology had endowed and that the mobilisation method was perfectly designed to exploit. The revenues then performed the concealment described earlier in this article, financing the imports that agriculture and industry required and thereby relieving the pressure that might have forced the efficiency question a decade earlier. When the world price collapsed in the middle 1980s, the concealment ended in a single budgetary moment, and the system's final leadership inherited simultaneously the stagnation, the debt, and the knowledge that the prop was gone. The energy story is therefore the record's parable of itself: a genuine triumph of the mobilisation method, in the sector where mobilisation works, whose very success postponed the reckoning with the method's limits until the postponement itself became a cause of the end. Development economists who study resource windfalls in market economies will recognise the structure, and the recognition is the point: the pathologies of the windfall are general, and the socialist record is their largest documented case, which is a further reason the record repays study by readers with no stake in the old dispute at all.

## Lessons for development: why this record is still assigned

The record's afterlife in the development literature is the final evidence of its importance, and a section on the lessons drawn from it, by scholars with no allegiance to either camp of the old dispute, gathers the article's findings into their usable form. The first lesson concerns mobilisation: a determined state, commanding the surplus and indifferent to the consumption of the present generation, can industrialise a poor country at extraordinary speed, and the record is the proof of concept at continental scale. The second lesson concerns the bill: the speed was purchased with the countryside, with the consumer, and with the information system, and the purchases were not incidental cruelties of particular rulers but the method's own accounting, since forced saving, suppressed consumption, and controlled information are what the method is. The third lesson concerns sequence: the instruments that succeed at mobilisation fail at efficiency, and the failure is not a matter of trying harder but of the instruments themselves, so that a development strategy must plan its own supersession, building the price system, the enterprise autonomy, and the information freedoms it will need at the next stage while it still commands the power to build them. The socialist states never planned their supersession, because their doctrine held the mobilisation instruments to be the destination rather than the road. The fourth lesson concerns measurement, and it is this article's own: a development effort that cannot measure itself honestly cannot correct itself, and the corruption of the indicators is therefore not a statistical footnote to the failure but one of its mechanisms. Those four lessons, mobilisation's power, its price, its expiry, and its blindness, constitute the record's bequest to every government that has since attempted to develop by command, and they are cited, in those terms, in the development literature of countries that never heard the old dispute's slogans. The record outlives its politics because the questions it answers are permanent.

## Three questions the record answers, and one it cannot

Gather the findings once more, in the form of the questions a reader brings to the subject. Could a poor country industrialise by command? The record answers yes, at a price, for a phase, and the answer is among the best attested in economic history, confirmed by the physical evidence and by the internal critics who had every reason to minimise it. Could the command economy then mature into an efficient, innovative, consumer satisfying system? The record answers no, across four decades, six national variants, and every reform that stopped short of dissolving the command principle, and the no is attested by the divided countries with a control no other question in the field enjoys. Could such a system guarantee the floor of a decent life, literacy, medicine, employment, security in age, to a whole population? The record answers yes, and the yes survived the system in the memory of the populations who lived under the floor and measured its loss. The question the record cannot answer is the one the disputants most want answered: whether the price was necessary, whether the transformation could have been had without the famine, the terror, and the queue. That question is counterfactual in its bones, depending on judgements about the starting country's alternatives that the evidence constrains but cannot settle, and the honest literature, at its best, marks it as open and argues it as such. A reader who leaves this article knowing which questions the record closes and which it leaves open possesses the subject. The rest is commentary, and the commentary, as this article has shown at length, is mostly bases in disguise.

## The consumption gradient: capitals, provinces, and the geography of shortage

Shortage had a geography, and the geography is part of the record because it distributed the system's failure as unequally as its privileges did. The capitals and the showcase cities were provisioned first, by design: the supply system allocated to Moscow and the republican capitals a standard the provinces saw in broadcasts and in the luggage of travellers, and the resulting gradient, capital, large city, small town, village, was the lived map of the economy for every citizen who shopped. Provincial residents travelled to the capitals on provisioning expeditions, the sausage trains of the folklore, carrying home the goods their own shops lacked, in a internal trade that reversed the direction of the official distribution system and testified, in the most concrete form available, to its failure to provision its own territory evenly. The villages stood at the gradient's end, supplied last and worst, their shops stocked with the assortment nobody else would take, their inhabitants dependent on the plot, the forest, and the town relatives for the margin between subsistence and comfort. The gradient matters to the assessment because aggregate consumption figures average it away: a mean that combines the capital's provision with the village's describes a place where nobody lived. It matters also to the political history, since the grievance of the provinces against the capital's privilege was among the solvents of the system's final years, when the regions and republics began to hoard their own produce against the centre's distribution, and the union's economy disintegrated along the gradient's lines before the union itself did. The record's geography is thus not a detail of distribution. It is a preview of the ending, legible in the shops decades before it arrived in the parliaments.

## Money, prices, and the meaning of a wage: a methodological postscript

A postscript on money completes the measurement argument, because the wage is the figure the general reader most often meets and most completely misunderstands in this field. A wage in the shortage economy was a claim ticket in a rationing system whose rationing was informal. Its purchasing power depended on the holder's city, connections, workplace distribution channels, and luck, so that two identical wages commanded different lives, and the average wage, the statistic most often converted and compared, summarised a distribution of access that the average erased. Exchange rates compounded the fiction: the official rate was an accounting convention for state trade, the black market rate a measure of desperation at the margin, and purchasing power conversions, though methodologically serious, inherited the shortage problem whole, since they price a basket the holder often could not buy at the prices recorded. The conclusion this article draws is the one its protocol enacted from the start: income comparisons between the systems are the least reliable figures in the entire field, less reliable than the growth rates, because they stack the accounting problem, the price problem, and the availability problem in a single number. The reader who encounters one should ask which of the three problems its author has solved, and will find, in the overwhelming majority of cases, that the answer is none. Welfare in this record is real, and this article has insisted on its reality against the caricatures. It is simply not denominated in money, and the attempts to denominate it have produced the field's most confident and least founded numbers.

## The military economy in depth: the state within the state

The defence sector has been entered as a burden, and it deserves a closer look as an economy in itself, because its internal organisation was the system's most successful and its success is diagnostic. Within the defence industrial complex, the pathologies of the civilian economy were substantially suspended. Priorities were absolute and enforced, resources arrived, quality was inspected by a customer, the military representative at the plant, with the power to reject, and the feedback loop between producer and user, abolished in the civilian economy, functioned. The result was a sector that met a demanding specification at the world frontier across decades, staffed by the system's best talent and consuming its best materials. The contrast with the civilian economy next door is the cleanest demonstration in the whole record that the system's failures were not failures of its people, its science, or its organisational energy. Given a customer who could refuse, a specification that could be tested, and a priority that overrode the indicator games, the same factories and the same engineers performed at the frontier. The civilian economy's tragedy is precisely that its customers could not refuse, its specifications could not be tested against welfare, and its priorities were set by the indicators rather than by the users. Reformers inside the system saw the contrast and drew the conclusion the system could not act on: that the defence sector worked because it had recreated, inside the plan, the discipline the market supplies outside it, a demanding buyer. The lesson generalises to every assessment of the record. The system was not incapable. It was capable wherever it built a feedback loop and incapable wherever it abolished one, and the map of its achievements and failures is, with remarkable fidelity, the map of where feedback survived.

## Culture, sport, and the prestige economy

A complete record includes the prestige economy, the sectors in which the system competed for glory rather than for output, because the resources and the achievements there were real and because the population's own accounting included them. Classical music, ballet, chess, mathematics competitions, and Olympic sport were funded as state priorities and produced excellence that the world acknowledged without qualification: conservatoires that set the century's performance standard, dancers and pianists who defined their arts, chess supremacy sustained across generations, and Olympic teams whose medal standings the frontier economies contested at full stretch. The achievements belong in the record beside the space programme as demonstrations of the mobilisation method in its favoured terrain: a defined goal, a selected cadre, unlimited priority, and a measurable result. The population valued them, and the retrospective literature records a pride in them that survived the system's other failures, a pride the historian should register as data about what the system meant to its citizens and not dismiss as manufactured, since manufactured pride does not usually survive the manufacturer by decades. The debit side is the familiar one: the prestige sectors consumed talent and resources the residual economy needed, their excellence was conscripted as evidence for the whole, and the arts that could not be conscripted, the literature and cinema that judged the system, were censored, exiled, or driven into the drawer and the samizdat typescript. Even here the pattern holds. What could be prioritised without feedback from below flourished. What required the free judgement of its audience was treated as a threat, and the record of the culture is therefore double, like every other part of this history, and for the same reason.

## The record in the successor states: memory as evidence

The populations who lived the record have since delivered their own assessment of it, in the successor states' surveys, elections, and literatures, and their testimony is a source the historian must handle with the same provenance discipline as the statistics. Retrospective approval of the old system, in several successor states, has at times commanded majority support in survey responses, concentrated among the old, the rural, and those whose post transition lives were hardest. The affirmative literature cites these responses as the people's verdict. The critical literature dismisses them as nostalgia for youth. The provenance practice gives a better reading: the surveys measure, with reasonable accuracy, the value the respondents place on the settlement's security, the floor, the pension, the job, the predictable price of bread, against the insecurities that followed, and they measure it in populations for whom the comparison base is their own biography. A respondent who approves the old system in a survey is performing the starting point comparison with herself as the base, and her answer is evidence about the floor's reality, not about the frontier comparison she is not being asked to make. Handled so, the memory evidence corroborates this article's structure exactly: the floor was real and is mourned, the stagnation was real and is not denied, and the same respondents who praise the security condemn, in adjacent answers, the shortage, the privilege, and the lies. Memory, like the statistics, does not yield a single verdict. It yields the set of verdicts, each with its base, and the convergence between the testimony of the archives and the testimony of the survivors, on the shape if not the meaning of the record, is the closest this subject comes to confirmation.

## Why the argument continues: the record as a political inheritance

A final analytical section asks why a record whose shape is agreed continues to generate dispute, because the answer completes the article's account of its subject. The record is not merely history. It is the founding evidence in the largest argument of modern politics, the argument about whether an economy can be organised without the market, and every participant in that argument, including those who have never opened a statistical yearbook, holds a position that the record is summoned to support. The stakes explain the selection of bases: a verdict on the record is treated, by a shorthand nobody examines, as a verdict on every proposal for collective provision, every industrial policy, every expansion of the state's economic role, and the shorthand loads a historical assessment with a weight no assessment can bear. This article has tried to unload it. The record condemns the comprehensive command economy, on the divided country evidence and the reform evidence, beyond reasonable rehabilitation. It does not condemn, and its provision chapters affirmatively support, the capacity of collective organisation to guarantee a floor of education, health, and security at low income levels, a capacity the frontier economies themselves later exercised through their welfare states on the basis of a wealth the socialist economies never reached. Readers who need the record to be a verdict on everything will find this article unsatisfying in both directions, and the dissatisfaction is the sign that the bases have been stated honestly. History's evidence is specific. It is the polemicist, not the historian, who finds in it a general licence, and the record of the socialist states has suffered more from its licensers, on both sides, than from any of its statisticians.

## The record of the smaller branches: light industry, food processing, and services of daily life

Between the heroic branches and the household stood the industries of daily life, textiles, footwear, food processing, furniture, household goods, and their record is the one the population would have written if the population had kept the accounts. Light industry occupied a permanent last place in the allocation queue, receiving the machinery the heavy branches had finished with, the materials that remained after the priority claims, and a workforce drawn disproportionately from women at the system's lower wage scales. Its output, measured in the physical series, grew across the period, and its products, measured against the bodies they were meant to clothe and the rooms they were meant to furnish, lagged in assortment, sizing, durability, and design by margins that no aggregate records. The food processing industry lost and spoiled between the farm gate and the shelf a share of the harvest that the system's own investigations, in the franker periods, reported with disbelief, through failures of transport, refrigeration, packaging, and timing that were nobody's indicator and therefore nobody's responsibility. The services of daily life, repair, tailoring, laundering, hairdressing, existed in a penumbra of official inadequacy and private improvisation, the state ateliers with their queues and their indifference, the private craftsman with his tools and his illegality, dividing the population's custom according to its connections. A reader who wants the record's centre of gravity should look here rather than at the steel tonnages: in the branches where the citizen met the economy daily, the economy was at its weakest, and the weakness was not a phase. It was the allocation system's permanent expression of its values, in which the citizen as consumer ranked last among the claimants the plan recognised, below the investor, below the soldier, below the exporter, below the plan's own prestige. The ranking, sustained across six decades and six national variants, is as much a finding as any growth path, and it explains, more completely than any aggregate, why a population whose literacy, health, and housing the system had transformed could withdraw its belief from the system while standing in a queue for shoes that did not fit.

## Construction and the capital stock: building as a way of life

Construction was the extensive economy's characteristic activity, and its record epitomises the method's strengths and pathologies in a single industry. The volume of building was enormous and continuous: whole cities raised, industrial complexes, dams among the largest on earth, canals, railways, and the housing programme already described. The industry's organisation reproduced the system's signature: ministries and trusts judged on completed volume and commissioning dates, a chronic incentive to start and a weak one to finish, so that the landscape accumulated unfinished projects, the long construction sites that stood for decades as monuments to the indicator that rewarded beginnings. Capital invested in the unfinished was capital recorded and unproductive, and the stock of incomplete construction grew across the later decades into a significant share of the investment effort, a frozen margin that the aggregates counted as accumulation and the economy experienced as absence. Maintenance, as this article noted in the infrastructure section, was the industry's orphan, and the built stock aged faster than its book life, so that the capital stock the level estimates capitalised was, in physical truth, younger on paper than in fact. The construction record thus supplies the level dispute with its material substrate: the reconstructions that valued the socialist economies' capital generously were valuing, in part, unfinished sites and unmaintained plant at the conventions of the system that built them, and the write downs that followed the system's end, in the east German settlement and in the successor states' revaluations, were the market's audit of the conventions. The dams remain, and they generate. The unfinished cooling towers remain too, and they testify. The record is both structures, and a verdict that cites only one of them has selected its base in concrete.

## Innovation from below: the rationalisers and the tinkerers

One corrective remains to be entered against the article's own account of civilian innovation, and fairness requires it, because the record contains a stratum of ingenuity that the diffusion failure has obscured. The planned economy's workers and engineers, confronted daily with shortages of parts, tools, and materials, developed a culture of improvisation that the system itself celebrated under the banner of the rationaliser movement: the worker who redesigned a jig, substituted a material, repaired the irreparable, and kept a line running that the supply system had abandoned. The culture was real, skilled, and in its own terms heroic, and the memoir and oral history literatures record it with a pride that rings true. Its limits define the system's innovation problem with final precision. Improvisation of this kind is defensive: it restores a planned output against entropy, it does not create a new product, a new process at scale, or a new industry, and it could not accumulate into technical progress because its fruits were local, unrewarded beyond the certificate and the small premium, and untransmitted by any mechanism that could carry a workshop's trick into an industry's practice. The economy thus consumed the ingenuity of its people in the maintenance of its own failures, an expenditure of human capability that the innovation statistics, counting patents and rationalisation proposals in their millions, recorded as dynamism. Here, at the end of the substantive argument, is the measurement problem in its most human form: a statistic that counted the proposals and could not count what they were for. The record's people were not the record's problem. The uses to which the system put them, and the uses it forbade, are the problem entire, and any verdict that blames the population of these economies for their economy's performance has the record exactly upside down.

## A note on the famine and the terror in an economic assessment

The article has entered the transformation's human cost inside the economic ledger at several points, and a closing note on method is owed, because the placement is a decision and the decision should be defended. An economic record can be compiled in abstraction from the violence that accompanied it, and much of the technical literature so compiles it, treating the famine of collectivisation and the terror's camps as belonging to political history. This article has declined the abstraction, for a reason that is economic and not only moral: the violence was an input. The grain that financed the machinery imports was extracted by the coercion that produced the famine; a portion of the construction and extraction of the first plans was performed by forced labour whose output enters the physical series; the consumption suppression that financed the investment was enforced, in the decisive years, by a terror that made refusal a mortal act. A growth accounting that credits the output and debits none of the coercion is not neutral. It is an accounting with a base selected to exclude the price, which is the manoeuvre this entire article exists to expose. The converse manoeuvre, in which the violence is made to annul the output, the steel unbuilt because the builders were coerced, commits the same selection in reverse. The record holds both, the tonnage and the terror, in a single set of books, and the reader who has followed the argument this far will recognise the discipline required: state the basis, carry the costs in the open, and refuse every summary, including the summaries that flatter one's own side, that arrives with a column of the ledger missing.

## The record of prices paid by the countryside: the internal colony question

The financing of industrialisation from the countryside has been stated as a mechanism, and its distributional record deserves a section, because the village was the system's internal colony in the precise economic sense: a sector whose terms of trade were set against it by policy, whose surplus was extracted for investment elsewhere, and whose population's welfare lagged the towns' by margins the aggregates conceal. Procurement prices for farm produce were held for long periods at levels that returned to the collective a fraction of the value its labour created, while the machinery, fuel, and manufactures the farm purchased were priced at the industrial branches' convenience, the scissors between the two price sets opening and closing with policy but rarely favouring the village. The collective farmer's income, a residual after the state's deliveries and the farm's obligations, stood below the industrial wage across the whole period, and the village's provision, its schools, clinics, roads, and shops, stood below the towns' in the gradient this article has mapped. The countryside's reply was the private plot and the quiet withdrawal of effort from the collective fields, the dual economy of the village in which the family's labour flowed to the fraction of land that rewarded it. The internal colony question matters to the verdict because the starting point base, the affirmative case's fortress, aggregates the village and the town into a single transformation: the literacy and the clinics reached the village, genuinely, and the extraction that financed the towns' industry left it, also genuinely, and the record of the countryside is the conjunction, an uplift and a tribute collected from the same households across the same decades. Assessments that report the national mean report neither. The village is where the record's two faces lived at the closest quarters, in the same street, and its testimony, in the oral histories that the successor scholarship has gathered, is correspondingly the least reconcilable and the most believed.

## Foreign trade with the West: the hard currency mirror

The bloc's trade with the market economies supplied an external check on the record that its participants could not manipulate, because the Western customer paid in hard currency only for what it wanted. The pattern of that trade is therefore evidence of a special kind. The socialist economies exported, overwhelmingly, fuels, raw materials, and semi processed goods, the products of their geology and their heavy industry, and imported machinery, technology, grain, and, at the margin, the consumer goods their own industries could not supply at acceptable quality. The structure is the structure of an underdeveloped trading partner, and its persistence across four decades, in economies that had industrialised two generations earlier, is among the record's most quietly damning exhibits: industrialisation had been achieved in volume without being achieved in the composition of what the world would buy. Attempts to export manufactures westward foundered on quality, design, delivery reliability, and the absence of the marketing and service networks that manufactured trade requires, all of them feedback functions the system had abolished at home. The hard currency mirror thus reflects the domestic record without distortion by accounting convention, since the prices are the world's and the refusals are the customers'. Defenders reply, with justice, that the Western embargoes in strategic goods and the discrimination of the trading system raised the barriers the exporters faced, and the reply explains the technology trade's difficulties. It does not explain the consumer manufactures, which faced no embargo and found no market. The mirror's verdict stands beside the divided countries' as evidence that does not depend on anyone's statistics, and it points the same way: an economy formidable in the branches where the state was the customer, and uncompetitive in every branch where the customer could choose.

## Standards of living between the blocs: the East European comparison with its neighbours

Eastern Europe supplies a comparison the article has used only in passing, and it deserves a paragraph of its own because it operates at the region's internal scale. The East European states began the postwar period interleaved with their Western neighbours in income and structure: Austria and Czechoslovakia had stood as comparable industrial economies in the interwar years, the eastern territories of Germany as the Reich's industrial heartland, Finland and the Baltic states as comparable northern peripheries. Across the four decades of division of the continent, the interleaving dissolved. The Western neighbours, including poor relations such as Greece, Portugal, and Ireland at the period's start and Finland at its edge, converged toward the European core, while the eastern states marked time relative to it, so that pairs that had begun as peers ended as exhibits. The comparison is the divided country logic at regional scale, with more pairs and looser controls, and its direction is uniform in a way that chance and endowment cannot explain: no eastern state converged on the western core, and several western laggards converged past every eastern state. The affirmative case's replies, the war damage differential, the reparations, the integration into the richer trading system, are the same replies as in the German case and carry the same weight, explaining a portion of the early divergence and progressively less of the later. What the regional comparison adds is breadth: the German result is not a single experiment that might be anomalous, but the sharpest instance of a continental pattern in which the system boundary, wherever it was drawn, became over four decades a welfare boundary visible in every pair it separated. The pattern is the record's most general empirical shape after the deceleration itself, and it is the reason the comparison sections of this article, and not its measurement sections, carry the verdict.

## The price of bread: staples, subsidies, and the politics of the nominal

One price in these economies was never merely a price. The cost of a loaf, held unchanged across decades by deliberate policy, functioned as a clause of the social contract written in the only language every citizen read daily: whatever else the system failed to deliver, the staples would not move. Rents stood in the same clause, frozen at levels set in an earlier era until they became a token payment, a ritual acknowledgement rather than an economic magnitude, and transport fares, utilities, and the basic medicines stood near them in the hierarchy of the untouchable. The fiscal consequence has been noted in the welfare section: the subsidy bill swelled as the gap between the frozen price and the real cost widened, until the pretence consumed resources that the service sectors and the farms were starved of. The political consequence was sharper, and it explains why the pretence was maintained long after its cost was understood inside the government itself. A state that had abolished the market's fluctuations had also abolished its own alibi. When bread costs more in a market economy, the harvest, the world price, and the currency can be blamed in turn. When bread costs more in an economy where a committee sets the price, the committee has raised it, and the population reads the act accordingly, as a decision taken against them by nameable men.

The system's history contains the demonstration, and it is among the most instructive episodes in the whole record. In 1962 the Soviet leadership, pressed by agricultural failure, raised the retail prices of meat and butter while a large locomotive works in Novocherkassk simultaneously cut its piece rates, and the town's workers, facing dearer food on smaller wages in a state that had promised them the opposite trajectory, marched on the party headquarters. Troops fired on the crowd, demonstrators were killed, and the episode was erased from public record for a generation, surviving in the successor period's archives as the leadership's own secret tuition in the politics of the nominal. No Soviet government again raised a staple price across the board. The lesson generalised across the bloc, where food price increases detonated the Polish crises of 1970, 1976, and 1980 with a regularity that made the meat price the most sensitive instrument in East European politics, read in the chancelleries of both alliances the way other governments read exchange rates. An economic record that omits the price of bread omits the mechanism by which the consumer failure, documented at length in this article, converted itself into political history. The aggregates cannot show it, since a price that never moves generates no series. The crowd in Novocherkassk is the datum, and the decades of frozen prices that followed it are the system's own assessment of what the datum meant: that the floor's credibility rested on the loaf, and that a government which had taken responsibility for every price would be held responsible for every price, in the street if necessary. The shortage economy's citizens forgave the queue for a generation. The record suggests they would not have forgiven the price rise, and the men who ruled them, having tested the proposition once, never tested it again.

## The statistical service: the professionals inside the numbers

Behind every series this article has handled stood an institution of working statisticians, and their professional history is the human substrate of the measurement problem. The central statistical administration of the Soviet Union, and its counterparts in the bloc, employed tens of thousands of trained personnel, ran the censuses whose demographic results this article has treated as the hardest evidence in the field, maintained the inter branch balances on which planning operated, and published the handbooks from which the reconstructions were built. The profession's internal life was more complicated than its political masters intended. Its journals carried genuine methodological argument within the permitted boundaries. Its senior figures understood the defects of the price structure and the index numbers as well as any Western critic, and said so in the coded vocabulary that the environment required. Its history also contains the profession's martyrs: the census of the late 1930s, whose results contradicted the leadership's announced population expectations, was suppressed and its organisers destroyed, and the replacement count was conducted under instructions that guaranteed a more acceptable answer. That episode is the extreme point of the pressure curve, and it should be read as such, the limiting case that reveals the force acting on every lesser decision, rather than as the profession's norm. The norm was quieter: a competent service working a flawed conceptual scheme under intermittent political weather, producing data that were usable exactly to the extent that the user understood the scheme and the weather. The archives have since vindicated that reading in detail, recovering internal series that track the published ones with deviations concentrated where the pressure model predicts. The statisticians were neither the heroes of the reconstruction story nor its villains. They were its medium, and the record they transmitted bears the marks of their competence and their constraints in proportions that the specialist literature is still, productively, disputing.

The afterlife of the service completes the story. When the systems ended, the statistical administrations were among the few institutions to pass into the successor states substantially intact, because every successor government needed to count itself before it could do anything else, and the same offices that had compiled the material product balances spent the following decade rebuilding their national accounts on the Western framework, republishing the historical series on the new basis, and thereby performing, as an administrative routine, the conversion exercise whose difficulty this article has laboured. The republication is a gift to scholarship: for the overlap years, the same economies exist in both accounting languages, translated by the professionals who had compiled the original. A reader who wants to feel the measurement problem rather than merely assent to it could do worse than to set the two versions of a single year's economy side by side and watch the country change size, structure, and trajectory as the concepts change beneath it. Nothing in the underlying territory has moved. Only the counting has, and the counting, as this entire article has argued, is where the verdicts live.

## The Baltic and Caucasus variations: the record at the union's edges

The union's internal geography supplies a final set of comparisons, quieter than the divided countries and in some ways as instructive. The Baltic republics entered the Soviet Union in 1940 as independent states whose income levels, literacy, and commercial development stood at or above the union's European average, in the Estonian case comparable to Finland's, with which it shared language kinship and an intertwined history. Their record inside the union is therefore a small frontier comparison conducted within a single state: the same institutions, the same planning system, the same provision floor, applied to populations that began ahead. Across the five decades of incorporation, the Baltic economies industrialised further, urbanised, and shared the union's provision achievements, while their relative position against the Nordic neighbours they had once matched deteriorated along the same trajectory the German comparison traces at state scale. Finland is the standing control in this internal experiment, a country that began the period beside Estonia in income and culture of administration, fought its own wars, paid its own reparations to the same victor, and ended the period among the richest societies on earth, while Estonia ended it queueing in the same shops as the rest of the union. The comparison lacks the German case's formality, since the two were never one state. It compensates in intimacy: the populations watched each other across a narrow sea for fifty years, and the direction of the small boat traffic, when it moved at all, was the German direction in miniature.

The Caucasus and the union's south present the opposite starting configuration and therefore test the opposite claim. Armenia, Georgia, and Azerbaijan entered the Soviet period as borderlands of two empires, and the Central Asian republics as colonial possessions in all but name, and for these territories the starting point base is the honest one and the record on it is the affirmative case's best internal evidence: industrial plant where none had existed, universities in capitals that had been caravan towns, medicine and literacy generalised, and a material transformation visible in a single lifetime. The costs were the system's usual ones, with regional inflections: cotton monoculture imposed on the Central Asian irrigation economy with the ecological consequences the companion assessment records, the suppression of national cultures alongside the genuine construction of national institutions, and a development whose direction was set in the distant capital for the capital's purposes. The internal verdict thus replicates the article's whole structure within one state's borders: transformation against the inheritance in the south, relative decline against the peers in the north west, and no single summary that does not select its republics as carelessly as the polemics select their countries. A union of fifteen records was never going to yield one verdict, and the scholar who reports the spread, rather than the average, has understood the federation as well as the figures.

## For students and exam candidates

This record is examined wherever twentieth century economic history is taught, and the candidate's advantage lies in method rather than in figures. State your comparison base in the opening of any answer, name the accounting concept behind any series you cite, and prefer the directional claims the evidence supports to the decimals it cannot. The divided country cases are the highest value evidence available, because they answer the starting point objection before it is raised, and the life expectancy reversal is the strongest single exhibit on provision, cutting against whichever side deploys provision carelessly. Candidates preparing revision notes on the measurement problem will find the VaultBook study notes at [the Marxism study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) a useful way to keep the measurement and comparison table in active recall, and those revising the wider Indian civil service syllabus alongside this material can organise their previous year questions through [the UPSC previous year question explorer](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html). The answer that scores is the answer that shows the marker a basis, a method, and a defeater, in that order.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The record teaches comparison better than any other case in the economic history syllabus, because its bases are so cleanly separable and its controls so unusually strong. Build the lesson on the table in this article: give the seminar one verdict, triumph or failure, and require the room to identify the base that generates it, then to regenerate the opposite verdict from the same facts by changing only the base. The exercise lands in a way lectures on bias do not, because the students perform the selection themselves and feel its power. The divided countries then serve as the lesson's discipline, the point at which the class discovers that not all bases are equal and that the historian's craft lies in ranking them by the control they exercise. Khanin's story carries the second lesson, that the most damaging evidence against a system may be produced by its own servants working honestly inside it, and a seminar that reads the reconstruction controversy alongside the planning mechanism will understand both the record and the reason the record was so hard to know.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Three injunctions govern work on this record. First, provenance before publication: no figure from this field should appear in print without its accounting concept, its price basis, and its producing institution attached, and editors should treat the unattributed growth rate as they treat the unattributed quotation. Second, distrust the aggregate and triangulate: the physical series, the demographic record, the trade mirror, and the testimony of the populations constrain one another, and a claim that survives all four is worth more than a precise figure that rests on one. Third, remember that the reconstruction literature is a living body of work, revising its estimates as the archives yield their series, so that any figure in a manuscript should be checked against the state of the reconstruction literature at the time of writing rather than inherited from the secondary canon. The journalist's version of the same discipline is the five questions of the provenance guide: count, prices, producer, adjustment, and comparison. A newsroom that asked them habitually would retire, within a year, most of the figures about this record that its own archive contains.

## The record and the arts of peace: leisure, tourism, and the good life the plan did not plan

A last dimension of welfare deserves entry before the questions that close this article, because it is the dimension on which the system's promise was most intimate and its delivery most revealing. The socialist states proclaimed the enrichment of free time as among their purposes, and they built for it: houses of culture in every district, subsidised theatres and concert halls whose ticket prices were set at levels the poorest citizen could pay, libraries, sports facilities, trade union holiday homes on the coasts and in the mountains, and a network of children's camps that gave the urban child a summer in the country as a matter of entitlement. The provision was real, its prices were nominal, and the cultural participation it supported, measured in attendances the statistics did record, stood high by international comparison. Against it stands the familiar obverse: the content was supervised, the repertoire policed, foreign travel rationed to the trusted and organised in groups, domestic tourism confined within the bloc's borders for most citizens, and the spontaneous, commercial, various leisure of a consumer society, the restaurant trade, the private holiday, the unplanned journey, stunted by the same shortage economy that governed goods. The citizen of the record could hear a great orchestra for the price of a loaf and could not choose to spend a month in a country whose politics differed from her own state's. Both halves of that sentence are the record of leisure, and together they state the system's welfare philosophy in its purest form: provision generous in the dimensions the state valued, absent in the dimensions that required the citizen's unscripted choice. It is the same philosophy the article found in consumption, in health, and in information, and its recurrence across every dimension of welfare is the deepest unity the record possesses. The system did not fail to provide. It provided, abundantly in places, exactly what it had decided its citizens should want, and it failed, comprehensively, at everything that required finding out.

## The argument restated for the reader who arrived with a verdict

Readers arrive at this record with verdicts already formed, and the article closes its substantive case by addressing the two arrivals directly. To the reader who arrived certain of failure: the record you must accommodate includes the industrial transformation of a peasant continent inside a single generation, the generalisation of literacy and basic medicine to populations no earlier regime had reached, the abolition of unemployment and of destitution's classic forms, and a security of floor that the populations concerned, free to speak after the end, remembered as the system's gift even while condemning the system. An account that cannot hold those facts is not a criticism of the record but a refusal of it, and it will persuade only those who arrived with you. To the reader who came convinced of achievement: the record you must accommodate includes two divided nations whose controlled comparison runs against your verdict in both theatres, a mortality reversal in peacetime that no comparable society matched, a consumer economy that failed its citizens at the counter for six decades, an agriculture that could not feed the country that collectivised it, and a reform history in which every remedy was refused at the point where it threatened the monopoly that administered it. A defence that leaves those facts out is a selection from the record, of exactly the kind this article was written to expose. Between the two refusals stands the record itself, larger than either, and the discipline of the bases, which is the only road into it that does not end where it began.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What was the economic record of socialist states?

A transformation followed by a long shallowing. From the late 1920s the planned economies industrialised at exceptional speed, generalised schooling, basic medicine, and guaranteed work, and raised consumption from wartime depths. From the 1960s every series shows deceleration; the 1980s brought stagnation. Consumer goods, farm output, product quality, and civilian technology lagged throughout. No single adjective covers a trajectory of that shape, which is why the assessment has to be given phase by phase and base by base, with the costs of the first phase carried inside the account rather than footnoted beneath it.

### Q: Why do the growth figures for these economies differ so much?

The rival series were constructed on unlike principles. Official statisticians counted a narrow aggregate that omitted most services, valued output at prices fixed by administrative decision, and let price increases slip into the growth figures disguised as novel products. Analysts in the West rebuilt the accounts on their own concepts and obtained markedly lower paths. A Soviet scholar who distrusted values altogether and worked from tonnages and freight obtained lower paths again. Later collaborative projects harmonised definitions across countries. Divergence on that scale reflects method, not mendacity, and it is why magnitudes in this field should always travel with their construction attached.

### Q: What is net material product?

The aggregate the bloc's statisticians published. It summed the output of the material branches, farming, industry, building, goods haulage, and the productive slice of distribution, net of the material inputs those branches consumed. Activities classed as services sat outside it: medicine, schooling, homes, passenger carriage, banking, government, and the armed forces counted as living off the product rather than adding to it. Western practice folds all of those into gross domestic product, so the two totals measure different economies on paper even when the underlying economy is identical, and naive comparisons between them mislead in both directions.

### Q: What did independent reconstructions show?

They replaced a slogan with a shape. The rebuilding undertaken by Western scholars, and institutionalised in the intelligence estimates, cut the official growth path substantially while confirming that the early industrial surge had been genuinely fast. Archival hindsight then judged even those reduced paths generous, because the underlying returns had been padded at the source. The independent Soviet reconstruction built from physical series cut deeper for the postwar decades. Collaborative projects after the archives opened broadly ratified the reduced picture: steep ascent, progressive shallowing, eventual standstill, with argument continuing over magnitudes in every period rather than over the trajectory itself.

### Q: What is reasonably established about the record?

Several findings stand on evidence of more than one kind. Heavy industry was created at remarkable speed from the first plans through the 1950s, paid for by squeezing household consumption and the countryside, at a human price that belongs inside the assessment. Deceleration thereafter appears in official, Western, and physical reconstructions alike, ending in the stagnation of the final decade. Schooling reached effectively everyone, clinics reached most settlements, and a job was assured. Set against that: shops stocked poorly and shoddily, housing in short supply for generations, farms that never performed, and a widening civilian technology lag behind the frontier economies.

### Q: What happened to life expectancy?

Survival improved dramatically during the first half of the period, as infection was conquered, sanitation spread, nutrition recovered, and basic medicine reached the villages, gains won at income levels where comparable societies often failed to win them. The improvement halted around the middle 1960s. Thereafter the trend turned down, concentrated among men in the working ages, driven by heart disease, drink, and accidents, inside a medical system designed for epidemics rather than for chronic illness. Falling survival in an industrial country at peace is almost without parallel in the period, and the downturn remains the gravest single count against the mature system.

### Q: What is the right comparison for these economies?

None, taken alone, because each standard interrogates a different aspect of the performance. Set beside the pre revolutionary inheritance, the achievement looks enormous. Set beside the rich Western economies of the same decades, it looks like ground never made up. Set beside economies that began at similar levels, it looks strong for three decades and then fades, as the East Asian performers completed a transition the planned economies could not. The two partitioned nations furnish the sternest test, since their halves began as one people, and both tests go against the planned side. Honesty consists in reporting the set, not in anointing one member of it.

### Q: What does the divided Germany case show?

It is the nearest thing the subject has to a controlled experiment, and its outcome is adverse to the planned model. The eastern half began with formidable industrial assets, Saxon engineering among them, and with living standards not far behind the western half. Over forty years the western half drew steadily ahead, the shortfall in what households could buy exceeding the shortfall in what factories produced, while movement between the halves ran overwhelmingly in one direction until a wall was built to halt it. When the two halves merged, eastern plant was revalued at world prices and much of it proved worthless. Reparations and embargoes explain part of the gap, not its persistence.

### Q: Did living standards rise under state socialism?

They did, for the majority and for most of the span, the famine and war years always excepted. What households ate grew more plentiful and more varied after the 1940s, floor space per person expanded, appliances spread from luxuries to norms, and schooling, treatment, work, and old age pensions were placed beyond the reach of market misfortune. Yet the improvement trailed the advance of the rich economies, so relative position worsened while absolute comfort grew, and the official consumption totals leave out the hours spent hunting and queueing and the shoddiness of what was finally obtained. The deterioration in survival late in the period qualifies the story further.

### Q: Were the official statistics fabricated?

Wholesale invention by the central statisticians is not what the evidence shows. Their office worked a consistent, if peculiar, accounting scheme, argued about its weaknesses internally, and published physical series solid enough to build the most damaging rival reconstruction upon. The flattery entered by other doors: a concept that counted less than readers assumed, price weights that systematically favoured the fast growing branches, novelty pricing that dressed price rises as extra output, local returns padded by officials whose careers rode on fulfilment, and the quiet withdrawal of series, mortality above all, once they embarrassed. That is a subtler indictment than forgery, and a harder one to answer.

### Q: How does the comparison base grade the verdict on the record?

It determines the grade almost before the evidence is heard. A verdict is a comparison wearing a conclusion's clothes: measure the distance from the starting point and the record earns its highest marks; measure the distance remaining to the frontier and it earns its lowest; measure it against its starting cohort and the marks divide by period. Measurement quarrels shift a grade within a band. The choice of base moves the record between bands altogether. Grading an assessment of this record therefore starts with an audit of its standard, and an assessment that conceals its standard has, in effect, chosen the grade first and the evidence afterwards.

### Q: What should a student separate between a measurement dispute and a choice of what to compare?

One is an argument about instruments, the other about yardsticks. Whether a series was honestly and competently constructed, what it counted, how it was priced, what corrections were applied, is a technical question that better archives and better method can progressively settle, and largely have. Which countries and which years the result ought to be judged against is a question about the purpose of the judgement, and no archive can settle it, because reasonable purposes differ. A student who keeps the two apart will neither expect a statistic to deliver a verdict nor treat a verdict as though it were a statistic, and will state the instrument and the yardstick separately in every answer.

### Q: Why do the divided-country cases matter more than aggregate comparisons?

Because they strip out the variable every other comparison leaves free. When a scholar picks a benchmark country and a start date, the conclusion is partly chosen with the benchmark, and opponents can reply in kind forever. Partition removed the choice: the two halves of Germany, and of Korea, inherited the same language, schooling, work habits, and in the Korean instance the heavier industrial endowment sat in the planned half. Whatever then diverged over forty years diverged under the two systems' management, subject to outside shocks that can be itemised and argued over but not plausibly stretched to cover the whole gap. That is why the partitions anchor the debate.

### Q: How does the measurement and comparison table order the choices?

It ranks the decisions a verdict rests on by how much each one moves the result. First comes the accounting concept, which fixes the territory the figures cover. Next the price basis, which fixes the values in which the territory is measured and smuggles in the index number effects. Then the treatment of disguised price increases, which fixes how much recorded growth was real. Then the family of reconstructions, each repairing some defects and importing others. Last and heaviest comes the benchmark itself, the countries and dates held up for comparison, which can overturn every conclusion the earlier rows suggested. The table's order is the argument's order: technique first, judgement last and largest.

### Q: What separates a claim about growth from a claim about the starting point?

One statement reports motion, the other reports position. To say how fast an economy travelled between two years, on a named measure, is to describe a journey. To say where it stood when the journey began, how poor, how illiterate, how agrarian, is to describe the point of departure. Polemic thrives on swapping them: a swift journey from a poor departure is exhibited as though a rich destination had been reached, or a slow journey by a rich economy as though it were poor. The record examined in this article demands both statements at once, a remarkable journey and a destination short of the frontier, and most of its quarrels dissolve once the two are written in separate sentences.
