Ideology critique is among the most invoked and least taught methods in the Marxist tradition. Students meet it first as a finished performance: a celebrated reading of a novel, a newspaper, a speech, in which the critic moves with apparent effortlessness from surface detail to hidden function, and the text stands exposed. What the performance conceals is the procedure. The beginner who tries to imitate the result discovers that knowing what ideology means does not tell her what to do on Monday morning with a policy document open on the desk, an advertisement to analyse, or an essay deadline approaching. Definitions circulate freely. Instructions do not. This article supplies the missing instruction set: a five-step procedure that can be followed in order, a typology of the operations ideology performs on representation, one fully worked example carried through every step, and an honest account of the two limits that decide whether the exercise counts as analysis or degenerates into denunciation with footnotes.

![Ideology critique as a method, the five steps, immanent critique and the limits of the method - Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-100.webp)

A claim about status frames everything that follows. Ideology critique is a method, and a method earns its standing the way any research procedure does: by specifying its operations in advance, by stating what evidence each operation requires, and by declaring what result would count against its own conclusions. Where those conditions are met, the critique of a text is a piece of research that another worker can check, repeat, and dispute at identified points. Where they are not met, the vocabulary of ideology becomes a licence to redescribe disagreement as delusion, and the critic's opponents are refuted by definition rather than by argument. The difference between the two practices is not a difference of political commitment. Sympathetic and hostile readers alike produce both kinds. The difference is procedural, and it can be taught, which is the premise on which this guide is built.

One boundary should be drawn at the outset. The article teaches a procedure for analysing representations: texts, images, speeches, institutional statements, advertisements, policy documents, and the commonplaces that travel between them. It does not attempt to settle what ideology is in every sense the word has carried, because the word has carried several, and the series treats the concept itself in its own place. Readers who want the conceptual map before the method should begin with the [marxist theory of ideology guide](/2012/02/01/marxist-theory-of-ideology-guide/), which distinguishes the senses and traces their history. What follows assumes only a working definition, stated in the next section, and concentrates on the sequence of moves by which a representation is examined, the evidence those moves demand, and the points at which a responsible critique stops.

## Ideology critique as a procedure, not a verdict

A verdict announces an outcome. A procedure earns one. The confusion between the two explains most of what goes wrong when the method is taught by example alone, because the celebrated examples are remembered by their conclusions, and conclusions copied without procedures become prejudices with a technical vocabulary. A student who has absorbed the conclusion that advertisements naturalise consumption, without absorbing the steps by which a particular advertisement was shown to do so, possesses not a method but a reflex. Presented with a new text, the reflex fires, the verdict is delivered, and nothing has been analysed. The text's actual claims go unrecorded, the counter-evidence goes unexamined, and the reader who disagrees has been given nothing to argue with except the critic's confidence.

Procedural discipline reverses the order. The representation is recorded before it is judged, in terms its own author would recognise. Its operations are demonstrated from its features, not inferred from its authorship. The interests it serves are established by evidence about institutions and effects, not by intuition about motives. Acceptance, the hardest phenomenon, is explained rather than assumed away. And the critique declares, before it concludes, whether it has shown the representation to be partial or claims to have shown it false, since those are different achievements requiring different evidence. Each of those requirements corresponds to a step in the template set out later, and each exists because a characteristic failure of published critiques demanded it.

There is a second reason for insisting on procedure, and it concerns the reader rather than the writer. A critique written procedurally can be used by someone who rejects its politics. The schoolteacher who needs to show a class how a recruiting pamphlet works, the journalist checking a ministry press release against the document it summarises, the historian weighing a memoir's silences: all of them need moves they can perform and defend in rooms where the word Marxist carries no authority. A method that only persuades the persuaded is a ritual. A method that exposes its steps to inspection travels, and the history of the tradition suggests that its analytical tools have always travelled further than its conclusions.

## The concept the method inherits

Every method inherits a concept, and the inherited concept here is unusually contested, so the working definition must be fixed with some care. In the critical sense that Marx and Engels gave the term in The German Ideology, written in the middle of the eighteen forties and unpublished in their lifetimes, ideology names representation that systematically misdescribes the social world in ways connected to the position of those who produce and circulate it. Their governing image was the camera obscura, the optical device that projects an inverted image: in ideology, they argued, social relations appear upside down, with ideas presented as the movers of history and material life as their shadow. The famous sentence of the same manuscript states that "the ideas of the ruling class are in every epoch the ruling ideas", a claim about which representations achieve dominance and why, rather than a claim that every belief held by anyone is false.

Two features of that inheritance matter for practice. The first is that the critical concept is not a synonym for falsehood. A representation can be ideological by being selective, by treating a partial standpoint as the whole, by presenting a historical arrangement as natural or eternal, while every individual sentence in it remains true. The second is that the critical concept is not a synonym for political belief in general. A neutral usage, in which ideology means any organised system of political ideas, exists alongside the critical one and generates endless confusion when the two are traded mid-argument. John B. Thompson, whose typology this article uses later, proposed reserving the critical sense for meaning in the service of power: representations are ideological insofar as they establish and sustain relations of domination. The working definition adopted here follows that proposal. Ideology, for the purposes of the method, is representation that serves to establish or sustain asymmetrical relations of power, and ideology critique is the disciplined examination of how a given representation does that work, whether it does it at all, and what follows if it does.

### What makes a representation ideological rather than merely mistaken?

A representation is ideological, on the working definition used here, when its distortions are systematic, patterned rather than random, and when the pattern serves to establish or sustain relations of domination. Mere error is unsystematic and harms its holder as readily as anyone. Ideological distortion runs in one direction, flattering an arrangement, and that direction is the evidence.

The distinction in that answer deserves expansion, because it protects the method against its most common abuse, which is treating every false or disliked claim as ideology. A weather forecast that errs is mistaken, not ideological, because its errors scatter in all directions and embarrass its makers. A land registry that consistently records customary holdings as vacant, by contrast, errs in one direction only, and the direction coincides with the interests of those who acquire the land. The critic's task is to demonstrate the pattern and its direction from evidence, not to pronounce on the moral character of the registry's clerks. Where no pattern can be shown, the honest finding is error, confusion, or ordinary persuasion, and a critique that cannot return that finding is not a method but a labelling machine.

It is equally important that the working definition leaves room for a representation to be ideological in function and accurate in content. An employer's safety notice may state the accident figures correctly and still function ideologically if its framing converts a structural question about staffing levels into a story about individual carelessness. Partiality of framing, not falsity of statement, is the more common finding of serious critiques, and the fork between the two findings, partiality and falsity, is the single most important distinction in this article. It is named and developed in its own section later, because nearly every failure of the genre can be traced to crossing it without noticing.

## The sources behind the procedure

Template O discipline applies here: a method article must show its sources the way a research guide shows editions, because a procedure assembled from unnamed authorities cannot be checked. The five steps synthesised in this article are not the invention of any single author; they consolidate a practice distributed across a literature, and the honest course is to name the contributors and let the reader verify the synthesis. From Marx and Engels comes the founding suspicion that representation is socially produced and that dominant representation tracks dominant power, together with the practice, above all in Capital, of criticising a doctrine from inside its own categories. From Karl Mannheim's Ideology and Utopia, published in German in 1929, comes the reflexivity problem in its sharpest form and the demand that the sociology of knowledge apply to itself. From Antonio Gramsci's prison notebooks, written between 1929 and 1935, comes the analysis of consent and common sense that the fifth step depends on. From Louis Althusser's essay on ideological state apparatuses, published in 1970, comes the insistence that ideology lives in practices and institutions, not only in propositions, and therefore that circulation and ritual belong inside the analysis rather than outside it.

From Raymond Geuss's The Idea of a Critical Theory, published in 1981, comes the cleanest philosophical sorting of the concept's senses and a precise statement of the genetic problem. From Thompson's Ideology and Modern Culture, published in 1990, comes the typology of modes of operation used in the template's second step and the definition of ideology as meaning in the service of power. From Terry Eagleton's Ideology: An Introduction, published in 1991, comes a survey of competing definitions wide enough to inoculate any student against the belief that the term has one settled meaning. Later sections return to each of these at the point where the debt becomes specific. The point of naming them here is procedural honesty: the template is a synthesis, syntheses involve choices, and a reader who knows the sources can locate the choices and contest them.

A warning about translations and editions belongs in any research-facing treatment. The German Ideology exists in English in several forms, and the wording of its most quoted sentences varies between them; a critique that hangs an argument on a single English phrase should check the phrase against the standard scholarly edition or flag its dependence on one translation. Mannheim wrote in German and revised for English readers, and the two versions of his argument are not identical. Gramsci's notebooks reach English readers through selections whose editorial arrangement shapes what the theory of hegemony appears to be. None of this paralyses work. It does mean that citation in ideology critique should carry the same apparatus of edition and translator that any textual scholarship carries, and that a critic who quotes a thinker on ideology while ignoring how the quotation was transmitted has failed the method's first standard on the method's own authorities.

## Step one: record the representation and its claim

The first step is deliberately unglamorous. Before any judgement, the critic records what the representation is, who issued it, in what genre, to what audience, and what it claims, in terms the issuer would accept as fair. A ministerial white paper, a recruitment advertisement, a textbook chapter, a chief executive's letter to shareholders: each has conventions, and a claim that looks like evasion under one genre's conventions may be ordinary compression under another's. Recording the genre is not pedantry. Genre determines what may legitimately be omitted, what the audience has been primed to supply, and which features of the text carry argumentative weight. The critic who skips the recording stage will spend the remaining steps analysing a text of his own construction, assembled from the passages that struck him, and the resulting critique, however brilliant, answers a document that does not exist.

Recording has three components. The first is the claim itself, stated as a proposition or a small set of propositions, including claims made by framing rather than assertion. A headline that calls a spending decision a reform has made a claim, as has a photograph that shows a factory's chimneys at dawn without its effluent channel. The second component is the intended audience and the action the representation invites: belief, purchase, vote, application, acquiescence. Representations that invite no action and assert no proposition, pure decoration, exist, and finding that a specimen belongs to that class is a legitimate outcome of step one. The third component is provenance: the issuing institution, its position, and the channel through which the representation reaches its audience, recorded neutrally at this stage. Provenance is evidence for later steps. It is not, at step one, a verdict.

A discipline borrowed from textual scholarship governs the whole step: quote or describe the specimen at sufficient length that a reader can test the description. Selective quotation is the original sin of the genre, because every later step inherits the selection. Where the specimen is long, the critic should record the principle of selection used and apply it consistently, for example by analysing every paragraph of a short document or every occurrence of a key term in a long one. Corpus tools have made exhaustive treatment of defined terms cheap, and a critique of a policy document that examines only the passages the critic happened to notice will not survive a competent reply. The reply will quote the omitted passages, and the critique's authority, which rests entirely on having read more carefully than the text expected, collapses.

## Step two: establish partiality from the text's own features

Partiality is the load-bearing finding of most serious critiques, and step two is where it is earned. A representation is partial when it selects, frames, or orders its material in ways that systematically favour one standpoint while presenting itself as complete, neutral, or natural. Four operations recur so reliably that the critic should test for each by name. Omission is the simplest: material fact, standardly relevant to the claim, is absent, and its absence is patterned. Universalisation presents the experience or interest of a part as the interest of the whole, so that what benefits employers becomes what the economy needs, or what suits one region becomes the national interest. Naturalisation presents a historical, changeable arrangement as a fact of nature or of human nature, removing it from the field of things that could be otherwise. Eternalisation, its close relative, presents an arrangement as timeless, stripping it of a history and therefore of a possible end.

Because the brief for this method requires the two operations students most often merge to be kept apart, the distinction deserves a paragraph of its own. Universalisation is a move between part and whole: a standpoint that belongs to some is asserted of all. Naturalisation is a move between history and nature: an arrangement that was made is presented as given. A sentence can perform either without the other. Claiming that everyone aspires to own a business universalises a particular ambition without saying anything about nature. Claiming that hierarchy is simply how human beings are wired naturalises one arrangement without universalising any group's standpoint. The repairs differ too. Against universalisation, the critic exhibits the standpoints the claim absorbs and silences. Against naturalisation, the critic exhibits the arrangement's history and its variations across societies, since what has been otherwise can be otherwise again.

Evidence at this step is comparative and internal. Internally, the critic shows what the text does with the material it includes: which agents appear as actors and which as scenery, which processes get verbs and which get nominalisations, whose costs are itemised and whose are rounded into phrases about tough choices. Comparatively, the critic sets the specimen against the range of what could have been said: the statistics the agency itself publishes, the earlier drafts, the parallel document issued to a different audience, the treatment of the same event by a source with a different standpoint. Partiality is a relation between a representation and its alternatives, and it cannot be demonstrated from the specimen alone. A critique that never leaves the specimen's own pages has shown, at most, that the text is emphatic.

## Thompson's modes of operation

John B. Thompson's Ideology and Modern Culture, published in 1990, supplies the most usable classification of the strategies by which meaning serves power, and any researcher intending to practise the method should know its five modes as a checklist rather than as a theory. The first mode is legitimation, by which relations of domination are represented as worthy of support. Its characteristic strategies are rationalisation, the construction of a chain of reasons defending an arrangement; universalisation, already met in the previous section; and narrativisation, the embedding of claims in stories that treat the present as the outcome of a valued tradition. The second mode is dissimulation, by which domination is concealed or denied. Its strategies include displacement, where a term proper to one thing is used of another to transfer its aura; euphemisation, where actions are redescribed in language that strips their negative charge; and the family of tropes, metaphor and metonymy doing quiet argumentative work.

The third mode is unification, by which a collective identity is constructed that embraces individuals across their divisions. Its strategies are standardisation, the promotion of one form as the shared norm, and the symbolisation of unity, the building of emblems, flags, anthems, and founding stories under which differences are subsumed. The fourth mode is fragmentation, the mirror of the third: potential opposition is divided, by differentiation, which emphasises the differences between groups that might otherwise combine, and by expurgation of the other, which constructs an enemy, internal or external, against whom unity is demanded. The fifth mode is reification, by which a historical and transitory state of affairs is represented as permanent and natural. Its strategies are naturalisation and eternalisation, already met as operations, together with nominalisation and passivisation, grammatical habits that delete agents: decisions are taken, mistakes were made, markets react, and nobody in the sentence does anything to anybody.

### What does Thompson mean by reification?

Reification, in Thompson's typology, is the mode of operation by which a historical, humanly made arrangement is represented as though it were permanent, natural, and outside human making. Its strategies include naturalisation, eternalisation, and the grammatical deletion of agents through nominalisation and passivisation, so that processes appear as things and decisions appear as events nobody took.

Two cautions govern the use of the typology. The modes are analytic categories, not accusations, and finding a strategy in a text is the beginning of an argument, not the end of one: narrativisation, for example, is how all institutions explain themselves, and the critic must show what a given narrative excludes and whose position the exclusion serves before the finding carries critical weight. Nor are the modes a taxonomy of bad writing. They describe relations between meaning and power, and a text can deploy several in a single paragraph while remaining, sentence by sentence, accurate. That compatibility between ideological operation and factual accuracy is precisely why the typology belongs to a method of partiality analysis rather than to fact checking, and why the fork between partiality and falsity, treated later, must be declared before the typology's findings are announced as conclusions.

## The five-step critique template

The template consolidates the procedure. Its five steps are: identify the representation and its claim; establish its partiality by showing what it omits, universalises, naturalises, or presents as eternal; identify whose position it favours, without assuming that favouring implies intention; explain the institutional mechanisms that produce and circulate it; and explain why it is accepted by those it disadvantages. The worked example carried through the table is the De Beers diamond campaign, analysed in full in the next section: a slogan coined in 1948 by the copywriter Frances Gerety for the agency N. W. Ayer, which taught generations of buyers that a diamond engagement ring, priced by a guideline of months of salary, was the necessary form of a marriage proposal.

The five-step critique template is a discipline before it is a format. Steps may not be skipped because their answers seem obvious, since the steps that seem obvious are where published critiques most often fail: step three collapses into motive guessing, step five is replaced by a gesture at manipulation. Nor may the order be reversed. A critique that begins at step three, with the beneficiary, and reads the text backwards from the interest it has already decided the text serves, has built its conclusion into its evidence selection. The order is the method's main protection against itself, and it is cheap: it costs only the patience to record before judging.

## A worked example: the diamond campaign through all five steps

Worked examples in method writing usually fail by being too easy, so this one is chosen for its resistance. The representation at step one is minimal: four words, "A Diamond Is Forever", written in 1948 by Frances Gerety, a copywriter at the Philadelphia agency N. W. Ayer, for De Beers, the company that then controlled the greater part of the world's rough diamond supply. The genre is the slogan, the most compressed form of advertising claim. The invited action is purchase, but not purchase of a stone in the abstract: the campaign's wider materials taught that the purchase should take a specific form, a diamond ring, given at engagement, sized by a guideline expressed in months of the giver's salary. Recording the specimen fairly means conceding its craft. The slogan asserts nothing that could be read to a trading standards officer. It is a metaphor, and its truth or falsity as metaphor is not the critique's business. What the critique examines is what the metaphor did.

Step two establishes partiality, and the history does the work. Diamond engagement rings were not an immemorial custom awaiting a slogan. Rings with stones had existed for centuries among the wealthy, but the mass practice, in which a proposal without a diamond became socially incomplete, was largely a twentieth century construction, built by sustained advertising in the decades around the slogan's coinage and extended market by market. The representation eternalises that construction: forever is the operative word, projecting a recent commercial custom backwards into the nature of love and forwards into permanence. It naturalises as well, because the diamond's geological durability is transferred to the institution of marriage and to the obligation to buy, so that a market arrangement borrows the authority of mineral fact. And it omits, systematically, the supply side of the story: the stones' abundance in the ground, the controlled release that maintained their price, and the near absence of a resale market, which meant the emblem of permanence lost much of its price the moment it left the shop. Each of those omissions is demonstrable from the historical and commercial record, which is what distinguishes the finding from an insinuation.

Step three identifies the favoured position, with the template's caution in force. The pattern served De Beers and the retail jewellers who lived by the guideline, and it served them whether or not any individual copywriter, editor, or jeweller intended anything beyond competent work. Gerety's own surviving accounts describe a professional solving a brief: since a diamond, unlike other luxuries, could not be advertised by novelty, it had to be attached to permanence. Nothing in the critique requires her to have been a propagandist, and the analysis is stronger, not weaker, for treating her as a skilled worker inside a mechanism. The favouring is structural. A custom that makes a controlled commodity the obligatory token of a universal life event, and prices the token as a fraction of income, transfers wealth in one direction with remarkable reliability. That is a claim about effects and institutions, evidenced by prices, sales patterns, and the guideline's history, and it stands even if every participant believed sincerely in the romance.

Step four asks how the representation was produced and repeated until it read as folklore. The mechanisms were ordinary and therefore instructive. An agency on retainer developed the theme across decades. Magazine advertising placed the lesson before the audiences whose marriages set the pattern for others. Film storylines, encouraged by the industry's lending of stones and advice, showed proposals performed in the prescribed form. Jewellers, whose training materials and counter talk repeated the salary guideline, delivered the norm at the exact point of hesitation. Etiquette writing absorbed the practice and returned it to readers as manners, the most authoritative genre of all, since manners are experienced as one's own judgement rather than as instruction. No step in that chain required coordination beyond contracts, professional routine, and shared commercial interest, which is the general lesson step four exists to teach: circulation is usually a sociology of institutions doing their ordinary work, not a plot.

Step five, the explanation of acceptance, is where the critique either becomes analysis or curdles into contempt for the buyers, and the buyers' own reasons repay attention. The ring solved real problems for the people who bought it. A proposal is a public act performed under uncertainty, and a legible, costly token reduces that uncertainty for both parties: the giver demonstrates commitment in a currency everyone can read, the receiver holds evidence that third parties, family and friends, will recognise without explanation. Refusing the norm was possible but expensive in a specific currency, the suspicion, never quite voiced, that reluctance about the ring signalled reluctance about the marriage. Each couple's compliance raised the cost of the next couple's refusal, because the measure by which sincerity was publicly judged became more standard as it spread. Acceptance, in other words, was produced by the interaction of a genuine expressive need, a token whose meaning the campaign had standardised, and a ratchet of social expectation, and none of that requires the buyers to have been fooled about anything except, perhaps, the custom's age. The critique that ends here has explained its object. The critique that ends at step three, with the cartel, has merely located a villain, and villain location is a beginning that mistakes itself for an end.

## Step three and the discipline of benefit without intention

The third step is the one the genre is famous for and the one it performs worst, because the question it asks, cui bono, who benefits, is fatally easy to ask rhetorically and surprisingly demanding to answer evidentially. Benefit comes in kinds that must be distinguished. A representation may benefit a group materially, by shifting income, custom, or law in its favour, as the diamond custom shifted income towards a cartel and its retailers. It may benefit a group positionally, by confirming its members' standing, as a curriculum that treats one nation's literature as the universal standard confirms the cultural authority of that nation's educated class. It may benefit an institution functionally, by supplying the beliefs its routines require, as a firm's internal literature about flexibility may supply the consent its scheduling practices need. The critic should say which kind of benefit is claimed, because the evidence differs: material benefit shows in flows of money and rules, positional benefit in patterns of deference and credential value, functional benefit in the fit between a belief and an institution's operating requirements.

Intention is a separate question, to be investigated separately and often to be left unanswered. The template's instruction, to identify whose position a representation favours without assuming that favouring implies intention, is not a courtesy extended to the powerful. It is a methodological requirement, for two reasons. The first is evidential: intentions are hidden, contested, and usually mixed, and a critique that depends on them can be destroyed by one honest memo or one sincere memoir, even when its structural analysis was sound. Building the conclusion on effects and mechanisms makes it robust against discoveries about what anybody meant. The second reason is analytical. The most effective ideological representations are typically produced by people who believe them, since sincerity is a better transmitter than cynicism, and institutions select for believers in their own legitimation stories with the reliability of any selection process. A method that requires villains will miss the normal case, in which the work is done by the sincere.

Jon Elster pressed the sharpest version of this discipline from within social theory, in his criticisms of functional explanation in Marxist writing. To say that a belief exists because it serves capital, Elster argued, is not yet an explanation: a genuine functional explanation must specify the feedback mechanism by which the benefit maintains the belief, the loop through which the beneficiaries, or the selection pressures of the environment, keep the useful representation in circulation and let useless or harmful ones die. G. A. Cohen defended a more permissive account of functional explanation, arguing that consequence laws can be legitimate before their mechanisms are known, as they were in biology before genetics. The working critic need not settle that philosophical dispute, but the dispute sets the standard for step four: the stronger the mechanism the critique can exhibit, the stronger its step three claim becomes, and a critique that can show neither a mechanism nor a selection process should present its benefit claim as a hypothesis about effects, not as an explanation of origins.

## Step four: institutions produce and circulate

Circulation is where ideology critique meets the sociology of media and education, and the meeting disciplines both sides. The critic must show the channels: who owns the outlet, who funds the institute, which professional routines govern the genre, what gets syndicated, translated, anthologised, set on syllabuses, and repeated until repetition itself becomes evidence of truth. Claims about news framing, for example, belong to the detailed treatment in the series article on [ideology and news media](/2012/04/15/ideology-and-news-media/), where the mechanisms of agency copy, deadline, and source dependence are examined at length; the method point here is general. Every step four account should be able to answer three questions. What selects this representation for repetition over its rivals? What does repetition cost, and who pays? What happens to versions that deviate, the editor who spikes the story, the teacher who drops the text, the advertiser who breaks the guideline?

Selection is usually mundane, and its mundanity is the finding. Advertising finance selects for content that does not disturb the buying mood and for audiences worth selling. Curriculum committees select for the teachable and the uncontroversial, and against whatever generates complaints from organised constituencies. Professional training selects for the categories the profession can bill, promote, and defend. Syndication selects for the version that travels without local knowledge. None of these mechanisms requires an instruction from a ruling class, and a critique that implies one has failed step four even if its conclusion happens to be right, because it has replaced a checkable mechanism with an uncheckable committee. Conversely, documented instruction exists in the record often enough, in proprietors' interventions, state censorship, and public relations contracts, and where documents exist the critique should quote the mechanism rather than theorise it. The step is empirical all the way down.

Althusser's contribution belongs here, because his essay on ideological state apparatuses relocated ideology from propositions to practices: schools, churches, families, legal and cultural institutions, operating through rituals and routines in which beliefs are enacted before they are examined. His formula, that ideology represents "the imaginary relationship of individuals to their real conditions of existence", is quoted often and used less often than it should be, since its methodological consequence is severe. If ideology is carried in practice, then a critique confined to a text's propositions will miss the specimen's main mode of existence, and step one must record the practice the text belongs to: the ceremony it scripts, the form it makes citizens fill in, the interview ritual it normalises. The diamond example obeyed that consequence without announcing it. The slogan's power was never in its four words alone but in the proposal ritual the words came to script, a practice performed by millions who had never read the advertisement.

## Step five: explaining acceptance without contempt

The fifth step separates analysis from accusation, and it is the step most critiques skip. The skip is rational in one narrow sense: explaining why people accept a representation that disadvantages them is genuinely hard, and failing at it in print is embarrassing. But a critique that cannot explain acceptance has explained nothing about the representation's power, since power over belief that cannot account for the believers is a label for the critic's puzzlement. Worse, the skipped step leaves a vacancy that contempt rushes to fill. The audience becomes dupes, the believers become sheep, and the analysis has quietly adopted the least defensible theory in the whole tradition, the theory that millions of people are simply stupid in the precise pattern of their rulers' convenience.

The respectable explanations available to step five share a refusal of that shortcut. Gramsci's account of hegemony and common sense, developed in the prison notebooks, treats acceptance as consent organised through the institutions of civil society and sedimented into the taken for granted, and it has the merit of treating common sense as a real philosophy, incoherent and composite, rather than as an absence where philosophy should be. The wider comparison of vocabularies, hegemony against discourse against ideology proper, is treated in the series article on [ideology hegemony discourse compared](/2012/04/01/ideology-hegemony-discourse-compared/), and the researcher should keep the terms distinct, since each implies a different mechanism of acceptance. Beyond Gramsci, the step five toolkit includes partial truth: most durable representations contain enough accuracy to live in, and their distortions ride on verified experience. It includes material concession: arrangements that deliver real goods, security, wages, status, purchase consent honestly rather than extracting it by trick. It includes the cost of dissent, counted in careers and marriages as much as in punishments. And it includes the absence of an alternative vocabulary: a grievance that cannot be said cannot easily be thought in common, which is why control of the available categories matters more than control of any single message.

### Why is the explanation of acceptance the hardest step?

Acceptance is the hardest step because it requires evidence about other people's reasons, gathered from testimony, practice, and the costs they face, rather than from the text under analysis. The critic must reconstruct why a representation is plausible from inside the lives of those who hold it, which forbids the easy answer that they have been fooled.

Two tests discipline step five writing. The recognition test asks whether the people described would recognise their own reasoning in the critic's account of it, not agree with the critique, but recognise the reasons as the ones they actually have. An account of diamond buyers that none of them would recognise as their experience has failed empirically, whatever its elegance. The symmetry test asks whether the critic's explanation of other people's acceptance could be turned, without alteration, on the critic's own convictions, and if the answer is no, the asymmetry needs a justification stronger than confidence. Step five done well is the most humane part of the method. It treats belief as something people do for reasons inside lives that make sense, and it locates the critique's target in the arrangement that supplies the reasons rather than in the people who reason with them.

## Immanent critique: judging a text by its own standards

Ideology critique has two routes to its conclusions, and confusing them produces much bad writing. External critique judges a representation against a standard imported from outside: the critic's theory, the critic's values, the record as the critic's tradition reads it. Immanent critique takes the standard from the object itself. It grants the text its own premises, its own professed values, its own criteria of success, and then demonstrates, from evidence the text's defenders must accept because it is theirs, that the object fails by the measures it set itself. The form descends from Hegel's procedure of testing a shape of consciousness against its own criterion, runs through Marx's critique of political economy, which convicts the economists' categories using the economists' own definitions more often than by denouncing them, and survives in Adorno as the insistence that criticism measure society against the concepts society itself professes.

The methodological advantages are concrete. Immanent critique cannot be dismissed as the application of an alien yardstick, because the yardstick is the object's own. It locates contradiction, the gap between a profession and a practice, a constitution's promise and a statute's effect, a company's stated values and its scheduling system, and contradiction is evidence of a kind that survives changes in the political weather. It also teaches better than denunciation does. A reader led through the distance between a document's promises and its own figures has been shown something she can reperform on the next document, whereas a reader handed a verdict has been shown only the critic's allegiances. For the working researcher, the practical rule is to attempt the immanent route first, exhaust the object's internal tensions, and import external standards only for what remains, with the import declared.

### What standard does immanent critique judge a text by?

Immanent critique judges a text by the standards the text itself professes: its stated purposes, its own definitions, its promises, and the criteria of success it announces. The critic grants those standards provisionally and demonstrates, from the object's own record, the distance between profession and performance, so the conclusion rests on premises the object's defenders already accept.

Immanent critique has limits that honesty requires naming. Some objects are internally consistent and wicked, and against them the internal route finds little purchase; a candidly supremacist pamphlet may fulfil its own stated aims without contradiction, and criticising it requires external moral and empirical argument, openly owned. Some objects are consistent and trivial, and immanent attention flatters them. And the route demands a patience that polemic rarely rewards, since the critic must master the object's self understanding before being licensed to convict it, which is slow. Those limits explain why the genre keeps sliding towards denunciation, but they do not excuse the slide. The slide's cost is measurable in the genre's reputation: readers who have watched critics denounce texts they plainly had not finished reading discount the next critique, including the sound ones, and the method's credit falls with its worst practitioners.

## The partiality-or-falsity fork

Every ideology critique arrives, whether its author notices or not, at a fork, and the quality of the work depends on choosing the branch knowingly. Down one branch lies the claim of partiality: the representation selects, frames, universalises, naturalises, omits, and these operations can be demonstrated by the analysis itself, from the text's features and their comparison with alternatives. Down the other lies the claim of falsity: the representation asserts what is not so, and demonstrating that requires an independent argument about the world, built from evidence that is not generated by the ideology analysis at all. The partiality-or-falsity fork names the obligation to declare which claim a critique is making, because the first is established by the method's own steps while the second borrows authority the method has not earned. Conflating them is the commonest failure in the genre, and it has a characteristic signature: a critique demonstrates selection and framing beautifully for six pages, then concludes, in its final paragraph, that the belief in question is false, as if falsity had been among the things demonstrated.

The conflation is tempting because partiality feels like a lesser prize. A critic who has shown that a textbook's account of industrialisation omits the enclosures, universalises the factory owner's standpoint, and naturalises the wage relation wants the stronger word. But the stronger word must be bought with different currency: demographic records for the enclosure claim, wage series for the living standards claim, the whole evidentiary apparatus of economic history. Where that apparatus has been assembled, the falsity claim may well succeed, and several celebrated critiques succeed precisely because their authors did the second, unglamorous investigation after the first. Where it has not, the honest conclusion is partiality, and partiality is not a consolation prize. A representation shown to be systematically partial has been shown something real and damaging about it: that it cannot be trusted as a complete account, that its silences have a direction, and that its direction serves a position. Most of what the tradition's best work established was established at exactly that strength.

### Does showing a belief serves someone's interests prove it false?

No. That is the genetic fallacy. A belief can serve powerful interests and still be true. Ideology critique can establish that a representation is partial, selective, or naturalising, but demonstrating falsity requires a separate argument about the world, not about the belief's origins.

The fork also disciplines the critic's treatment of opponents. A representation may be partial in the service of a cause the critic supports, and the fork obliges her to say so with the same vocabulary she uses on adversaries, since partiality is a property of representations and not of sides. Symmetrically, a representation serving an opposed interest may be accurate in every checkable particular, and the fork obliges her to record that too, and to confine the critique to the framing. Critics who apply the method in one direction only are not practising a method. They are operating a court in which the verdict precedes the hearing and the procedure decorates it, and readers learn, correctly, to discount the court's findings in both directions.

## The genetic problem: origin is not refutation

The fork's first branch rests on a logical point old enough to have a Latin flavoured name in the textbooks: the genetic fallacy, the error of treating an account of a belief's origin as a judgement of its truth. Beliefs have origins, all of them, and the origins are always social in part: nobody's convictions about property, gender, or the nation fell from the sky unmediated by family, school, and interest. If social origin as such invalidated belief, every belief in the room would be invalidated together, including the belief that social origin invalidates belief. The critic's legitimate claim is narrower and more interesting. Origin becomes evidence when the origin is of a kind that makes the belief's truth irrelevant to its survival: when a representation would be produced and believed whether or not it were true, because the mechanisms producing it are insensitive to its accuracy. Demonstrating that insensitivity is real work. It requires showing that the channel selects for serviceability rather than correctness, as advertising selects for persuasion, as a ministry's communications office selects for reassurance, as a profession's training selects for the categories the profession can act on.

Examples clarify the boundary in both directions. Consider a manufacturer's statement that its boiler meets the national safety standard. The statement serves the manufacturer plainly, and its origin lies in a marketing department, and it may nevertheless be true, checkable against the standard and the test certificate; the ideology critique of the manufacturer's literature belongs to its framing of safety as a property of the product rather than of the maintenance regime, and any claim beyond framing must go and look at boilers. Consider, in the other direction, a belief with a disreputable origin and a true content: handwashing in hospitals was promoted by Ignaz Semmelweis in the eighteen forties with statistics his colleagues found insulting, and the belief's later triumph owed as much to institutional change as to its evidence, yet none of that biography bears on whether handwashing prevents infection. Origin explains careers of beliefs. Truth is settled elsewhere. A critique that keeps the two apart can use origins aggressively, as evidence about selection mechanisms, without ever pretending that an origin is a refutation.

Geuss's philosophical treatment remains the clearest guide through this territory, because he distinguishes the senses in which a belief system might be called ideological: by its content, by its function, or by its genesis, its origin in interests. The three senses can come apart in every combination, and much confusion in the literature is the sound of them being treated as one. A belief might be ideological in genesis and true; ideological in function and false; ideological in neither and merely unpopular with the critic. The written critique should state, for each of its targets, in which sense the charge is laid. That single habit, cheap to adopt, would retire a large fraction of the genre's published disagreements, which turn out on inspection to be one author charging genesis and another defending content, each victorious against a claim the other never made.

## Reflexivity: Mannheim's paradox and the situated critic

The second limit is deeper, because it concerns not the critique's logic but its location. Karl Mannheim, in Ideology and Utopia at the end of the nineteen twenties, generalised the concept of ideology into a sociology of knowledge and immediately faced the consequence he was honest enough to state. If all thought is socially situated, bound to the standpoint of its thinkers and serviceable to their positions, then the sociology of knowledge is situated too, and its findings are one more standpoint's product. The particular conception of ideology, which unmasks individual opponents' claims as interested, gives way to the total conception, which attributes an entire epoch's or class's thought structure to its social being, and the total conception, applied consistently, applies to the analyst. This is Mannheim's paradox, and a century of method writing is partly a record of attempts to escape it without cheating.

Mannheim's own answer was twofold. He proposed relationism against relativism: situated knowledge is not thereby arbitrary knowledge, since standpoints can be compared for the range and coherence of what they let their holders see, and a synthesis that incorporates more standpoints may claim, provisionally, to see further. And he assigned the synthesising role to the intelligentsia, a stratum recruited from many classes and educated into a relative detachment from each, free floating in Alfred Weber's phrase, whose social position made it the plausible bearer of the least partial account available. The answers deserve respect as the first serious attempt to build reflexivity into the method rather than pretend it away. They also exhibit the problem they solve. The intelligentsia's detachment is asserted from its recruitment pattern, yet intellectuals have interests of their own, in status, in credentials, in the expansion of the very institutions that employ them, and the history of expert consensus includes episodes no defender of the stratum would exhibit proudly. A solution that exempts the analyst's own group by describing its virtues has the shape of the disease in professional dress.

## Three escapes from the paradox, assessed

The first escape is Mannheim's own, the free floating intelligentsia, assessed in the previous section: it names a real phenomenon, the partial loosening of thought by education and mixed recruitment, and inflates it into a warrant it cannot carry. Intellectuals are less bound to a single class position than most groups and more bound to the interests of the knowledge professions than the theory admits. Treat the intelligentsia as a standpoint among others, somewhat wider in its materials, and the claim becomes usable. Treat it as a balcony above ideology and it becomes an exemption, and exemptions are what the method exists to refuse.

The second escape is the standpoint claim, given its classical form by Georg Lukács in History and Class Consciousness, published in 1923. The proletariat, Lukács argued, occupies the position in the production process from which the social totality can be seen as it is, because its own liberation requires understanding the whole, while the bourgeoisie's class position requires it to misrecognise the whole as nature. The claim was later taken up, transformed, and criticised within feminist standpoint theory, where Nancy Hartsock and Sandra Harding argued that socially marginal positions can afford epistemic advantage about the relations that produce the margin, and where critics inside the same tradition objected that standpoints are achieved through analysis and struggle, not conferred automatically by location. That internal correction is the version a careful method should adopt. A standpoint is not a birthright and not a guarantee; it is a resource. Occupying a position where an arrangement's costs are felt gives a researcher access to evidence and questions that comfort conceals, and the advantage is real, checkable in the record of what inquiry from below has repeatedly noticed first. But the advantage must be earned in each inquiry by the ordinary virtues, evidence, comparison, declared defeaters, and it lapses the moment it is treated as a title deed. So treated, it becomes the exemption again, with a different group on the balcony.

The third escape is the science and ideology break, associated with Althusser and with the epistemology of Gaston Bachelard from which he borrowed the figure of the epistemological break. On this account, Marx's mature work broke with ideology as such and founded a science of history, and the critic speaks from the science's side of the line. The attraction is obvious: if the line exists and the critic stands on the right side of it, reflexivity is answered by jurisdiction. The difficulties are equally plain. The line's location is itself a theoretical judgement made by situated thinkers; the history of the claim's use includes its hardening into an orthodoxy that licensed dismissing any inconvenient finding as ideology; and the sciences themselves, on inspection, turn out to have institutions, selections, and common senses of their own, which the sociology of knowledge has documented without thereby dissolving their findings into mere interest. The working verdict of this guide is that the break is a useful description of a real event in Marx's development and an unusable answer to Mannheim. No jurisdiction exempts the critic. What remains is the slower discipline: declare your standpoint, state your defeaters, apply the method to your own text, and let the claim rest at the strength the evidence buys.

## The unfalsifiability objection at full strength

The strongest objection to the method must now be stated as its opponents would state it, at full strength, before any reply is attempted. The objection holds that ideology critique is unfalsifiable in practice, whatever its practitioners say in principle. Show the critic a representation that appears innocent, and the innocence is itself the ideology, the concealment working perfectly. Produce testimony from the supposed victims that they endorse the arrangement, and the endorsement is false consciousness, the ideology speaking through them. Produce evidence that a criticised institution has reformed, and the reform is legitimation, a deeper move in the same game. Every possible observation has been pre assigned to the confirming column, and a method that cannot lose cannot win either, since winning and losing are the only ways a claim about the world announces that it is about the world. On this objection, the critique's resemblance to conspiracy theory is not a smear but a structural description: both systems convert counter evidence into evidence of the plot's depth, and both flatter their holders with the feeling of seeing through a world that deceives everyone else.

The objection gains force from the tradition's own history, and honesty requires the concession to be specific. Doctrines of false consciousness were used, in more than one state that claimed the Marxist inheritance, to discount the expressed preferences of actual workers whenever those preferences embarrassed the doctrine, and the discounting was not an aberration in the application but a predictable output of a theory that grants the analyst authority over other people's real interests. Popper's broader attack on historicism, whatever its own excesses, identified a genuine degeneration pattern in Marxist argument: predictions that fail are protected by auxiliary explanations, and the theory's empirical content drains away as the protections accumulate. A method guide that cannot look at that record steadily has no business teaching the method.

The reply does not deny the pattern. It locates the pattern in a specific corruption of the method and states the conditions under which the corruption is excluded. Unfalsifiability enters when a critique refuses to specify, in advance, what the world would look like if its reading were wrong. The discipline that excludes it has several components, all cheap and all routinely skipped. The critic states what evidence would count against the reading: passages in the specimen that resist it, beneficiaries who oppose the representation, acceptance explained better by the representation's truth. The critic separates the claims by strength, partiality findings resting on demonstrated selection, falsity findings on independent investigation, origin findings on documented mechanisms, so that the failure of one does not take refuge in another. The critic applies the template to a representation she supports, in the same document if possible, since a method demonstrated on friendly material cannot be dismissed as a weapon. And the critic treats acceptance testimony as evidence requiring explanation rather than as a symptom requiring diagnosis, which removes the objection's central example, the conversion of disagreement into pathology.

### What would count against an ideology critique?

Specified counter evidence: features of the specimen the reading cannot accommodate, beneficiaries who resist the representation, circulation explained better by accuracy than by serviceability, and acceptance explained better by the representation being true. A critique naming its defeaters in advance can be wrong, the precondition of ever being right; one naming none has exempted itself from its own method.

The reply must end with the concession the brief for this article requires, stated plainly. Narrowed to partiality claims, documented findings about selection, framing, and circulation, ideology critique is self applicable without paradox and falsifiable without strain: a partiality claim about the critic's own text is as checkable as any other, and this guide's claims about the genre are themselves partial, resting on a selection of the literature that another scholar could contest by exhibiting what the selection omitted. The stronger version of the critique, the version that claims to generate falsity verdicts from functional analysis alone and to know other classes' interests better than they do, has no satisfactory answer to Mannheim and no defence against the unfalsifiability charge. The method survives its limits by respecting them. It does not survive by denying them, and the literature's least persuasive pages are those in which the denial is attempted.

## Neighbours: discourse analysis, bias, and propaganda study

A method is partly defined by what it is not, and three neighbouring practices are confused with ideology critique often enough to require demarcation. Critical discourse analysis, associated with Norman Fairclough and with the linguists who built it, is a method for examining language in use: lexical choice, grammar, genre, and the social practices discourse constitutes. It shares the critical ambition and much of the politics, and it supplies tools, notably the analysis of nominalisation and transitivity, that step two of the template borrows gratefully. It is not the same method. Its primary evidence is linguistic form, its unit is the text or corpus, and it does not by itself supply the institutional and acceptance analyses of steps four and five. A discourse analysis can be complete without asking who benefits; an ideology critique cannot. The two combine well, and the researcher will often run the linguistic analysis inside the larger procedure, but she should know which claims belong to which method when the examiners, or the reviewers, ask.

### How does ideology critique differ from critical discourse analysis?

Ideology critique is the wider procedure: beyond textual analysis it requires an account of whose position the representation serves, the institutions circulating it, and the reasons its audience accepts it. Critical discourse analysis is a linguistic method centred on form and genre. The first can employ the second as its textual instrument, but their standards of completeness differ.

Bias is the second neighbour, and the demarcation matters for journalism. Bias names a deviation from a standard of balance or accuracy within a practice that has such a standard, as news reporting does. Ideology names something prior: the framework that decides what counts as balance, which that framework presents as neutral. A bulletin can be scrupulously unbiased between two parties while its categories, what counts as the economy, whose deaths lead, which experts are standing by, enact a partiality that bias hunting will never find, because bias hunting uses the framework to check the content. The forum question, what separates bias from ideology, therefore has a clean answer: bias is skew within a frame, ideology is the frame's own service, and the methods for detecting them differ accordingly. Propaganda study, the third neighbour, concerns deliberate, organised persuasion by identifiable agents, and it keeps intention at its centre, where ideology critique deliberately does not. A state campaign may be both propaganda and ideology carrier, but the analyses answer different questions, and the propaganda finding, being about intention, requires the documents the ideology finding can survive without.

## Applying the template to a policy document

Policy documents are the specimen type on which the method is most often examined and most often practised badly, because they invite the critic to argue with the policy when the assignment is to analyse the document. The procedure imposes its order. Record first: the document's issuer, its statutory or consultative status, its announced audience, and its claims, distinguishing the claims made in its summary and press handling from those made in its chapters, since the divergence between a document and its official summary is itself primary evidence. Then partiality: test the document against its own data annexes, which is immanent critique in its most practical form, because governments publish, in the small print of their own evidence volumes, much of what their summaries omit. A summary that reports an average improvement while its annex shows the improvement concentrated in one decile and deterioration in another has handed the critic a step two finding that no amount of rhetorical analysis could match for solidity.

The characteristic operations of policy prose reward attention. Nominalisation flourishes there, because decisions must appear to happen without deciders: reforms are introduced, efficiencies are delivered, challenges are faced. Conditional generosity does quiet work, in which benefits are framed as rewards for conduct and their withdrawal as the natural consequence of choices, converting a distributional decision into a moral biography of the recipient. The specimen phrase of the genre, in many countries' documents, is the hardworking family, a unification device that divides the population into those whose industry the document honours and those whose absence from the phrase marks them, without naming anyone at all. Critiquing such a phrase by the template means showing its work: whose standpoint it universalises, which institutional mechanisms repeat it until it becomes the vocabulary in which journalists must ask questions to be answered, and why those it excludes often repeat it themselves, since claiming membership of the honoured category is the only defence the vocabulary offers them. Acceptance, once again, is explained by the structure of the language available, not by the gullibility of the speakers.

Step four for policy has a documentary concreteness the critic should exploit. Consultation processes select which interests are heard in form and which are heard as noise. Impact assessments model the effects their commissioners specify and leave unmodelled the effects nobody commissioned. Drafting passes between departments leave a recoverable trail in released versions. The point of insisting on these mechanisms is not bureaucratic antiquarianism. It is that a critique able to show where, in an identifiable process, a partial framing was selected over a fuller one has answered the unfalsifiability objection in the only coin that matters, a checkable claim about the world, and has given the document's defenders a specific finding to dispute rather than a worldview to deplore.

## A second worked example: the company that calls itself a family

A shorter application, to an institution rather than a campaign, shows the template working at ordinary scale. The specimen is a form of words found in staff handbooks and induction talks across many countries and sectors, in which the company describes itself as a family and its employees as members of it. Step one records the claim fairly: the words assert belonging, mutual obligation, and permanence, and they are uttered by an employing institution to people it may dismiss. Step two finds the operations. The metaphor universalises one party's interest in loyalty as the whole group's sentiment, and it dissimulates, in Thompson's sense, because the family's defining obligations, that its members are not made redundant, are not selected out at appraisal, are carried through unproductive years, are precisely the obligations the institution does not assume. What the metaphor omits is the contract, which is the actual relation, terminable at notice by the stronger party.

Step three asks whose position the figure serves, without imputing cynicism to the managers who repeat it, many of whom feel the sentiment they voice. The function is legible in its timing and its direction of obligation. The family's claims are invoked downwards, when flexibility, unpaid availability, or forbearance about pay is requested, and are rarely invoked upwards, when the institution's obligations to permanence would cost it anything. Step four locates circulation in induction rituals, internal communications, and the appraisal vocabulary of commitment, which converts the metaphor into criteria individuals are assessed against. Step five, acceptance, is again the instructive part. Employees repeat the language because workplaces are real communities and the metaphor names something genuinely present, colleagues' mutual aid and loyalty to one another, while quietly redirecting that horizontal loyalty upwards into obligation to the firm. The critique's finding, note, is a partiality finding. Nothing false has been asserted by anyone, a metaphor cannot be false in the way a statistic can, and the analysis loses nothing by the restraint. What it has shown is a patterned transfer: real solidarity among workers, harvested as loyalty to an institution whose obligations run the other way only in the metaphor. That is ideology critique at its most characteristic, and its most defensible.

## What to read: the literature mapped by position

Reading lists in this field mislead when they are organised alphabetically, because the concept's literature is a quarrel and the order of reading should follow the quarrel's logic. Begin with the primary provocation: the relevant parts of The German Ideology, read in a scholarly edition with its posthumous publication history in view, so that the founding text is met as the fragmentary polemic it is rather than as a finished system. Move to Mannheim's Ideology and Utopia for the generalisation of the concept and the statement of its paradox, preferably with its critics alongside. Gramsci's selections on hegemony and common sense supply the acceptance problem's classical treatment, with the warning, recorded earlier, that editorial selection shapes the Gramsci a reader receives. Althusser's essay on ideological state apparatuses then relocates the whole discussion from beliefs to institutions and rituals, and should be read against the criticisms it attracted for functionalism, the assumption that whatever exists in the apparatus exists because the system needs it, which is step three's characteristic error dressed as theory.

The philosophical sorting comes next. Geuss's The Idea of a Critical Theory distinguishes the senses of ideology with a clarity that repays slow reading, and his treatment of the genetic question should be considered required equipment for anyone intending to publish a critique. Thompson's Ideology and Modern Culture supplies the typology and a worked approach to interpretation in the study of symbolic forms. Eagleton's Ideology: An Introduction surveys the definitions in competition and is the best single protection against imagining the term settled. Jorge Larrain's studies trace the concept's career through Marxist thought with a historian's patience. Žižek's The Sublime Object of Ideology, published in 1989, represents the turn to ideology as fantasy and practice rather than belief, drawing on the account of cynical reason in which people act as if they did not know, and knowing does not free them; the researcher should meet that turn because it changes what step five must explain, from why people believe what is false to why people enact what they do not believe. The reading advice that governs all of it is the method's own: read each of these as a situated intervention, note what each one's selection of the field omits, and the bibliography becomes the first exercise.

## Failure modes: how critiques go wrong in practice

The genre's failures cluster, and naming the cluster is a service to practitioners, because most failed critiques fail in one of five familiar ways. The first is the denunciation that skips the steps, delivering step three as a verdict on a text it has not recorded, and recognisable by its quotations, which are always the specimen's most foolish sentences, selected to make the analysis unnecessary. The second is intention mongering, the biographical turn in which the critique becomes an investigation of the author's soul, patrons, and school fees, as if an argument about a text could be settled by a curriculum vitae. The third is conspiracy drift, in which step four's mechanisms, too mundane to satisfy the writer, are replaced by coordination for which no documents exist, and the absence of documents is read as the conspiracy's competence. The fourth is selective application, in which the template is run only over opponents' texts, so that the method's findings correlate perfectly with the critic's prior allegiances, a correlation readers notice and price accordingly. The fifth is vocabulary inflation, in which every framing becomes ideology, every omission a dissimulation, and every metaphor a reification, until the terms describe everything and discriminate nothing.

Each failure corresponds to a skipped discipline, which is why the template insists on its order and its declarations. Recording before judging blocks the first. The intention rule blocks the second. The mechanism requirement blocks the third. Symmetric application blocks the fourth. The fork, together with the recognition that most texts are mostly not ideological in most respects, blocks the fifth, since a critic who must declare the strength of every claim learns to reserve the vocabulary for the findings that earn it. There is a professional version of the same list for journalists, treated in the series article on [marxism for journalists](/2022/05/15/marxism-for-journalists/): the habits of frame checking, source position, and omission testing that a newsroom can adopt without adopting a politics, and the researcher teaching the method will find the classroom design in the article on [teaching ideology in the classroom](/2012/06/15/teaching-ideology-in-the-classroom/), to which this article defers on pedagogy in order to stay on procedure.

## The research protocol: what to record before judging

Practice, consolidated, is a record keeping discipline, and it can be stated as a sequence of entries the researcher makes before any verdict is drafted. Record the specimen exactly, with its source, date of issue, genre, and channel. Record its claims as propositions, including the framed and photographed ones. Record the alternatives: what the same issuer said elsewhere, what the data annexes contain, what rival standpoints say, so that partiality has a comparison set. Record the benefit claim and its kind, material, positional, or functional, with the evidence type each requires and the intention question explicitly bracketed. Record the circulation mechanism and its documents. Record the acceptance evidence, testimony first, and the recognition test's result. Record the fork declaration, which claims are partiality claims and which are falsity claims with their independent investigations attached. Record the defeaters, the evidence that would change the finding, before the finding hardens into print.

That record is the critique's real substance, and keeping it in durable, revisitable form is what separates a research programme from a series of performances. A structured notebook for the purpose, in which each specimen keeps its claims, comparison set, and defeater list attached to its sources rather than scattered across drafts, is the working habit this article most recommends; readers can [keep the critique record and build a citation-linked reading list on VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html), whose study notes tool is designed for exactly that attachment of claim to source. The same record serves the examined versions of the method: students preparing answers on ideology and method can [practise with previous-year questions on ReportMedic](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html), where the recurring examination pattern, define the concept, apply it to a specimen, state its limits, maps directly onto the template's steps and the fork. The wider craft of finding and verifying sources for this field is treated in the article on [researching marxism sources](/2021/10/01/researching-marxism-sources/), which covers editions, archives, and the standard finding aids in the depth a sources article owes them.

## Before Marx: where the suspicion of ideas came from

The method did not begin with Marx, and knowing its prehistory prevents two errors: crediting him with inventing suspicion, and missing how radically he redirected it. The word ideology itself was coined in the seventeen nineties by Antoine Destutt de Tracy for a proposed science of ideas, an Enlightenment project for tracing every concept back to sensation so that error could be audited at its source. Napoleon turned the word into an insult for the project's practitioners, the ideologues, whose abstract principles he blamed for political disaster, and the pejorative sense, ideology as impractical doctrine blind to reality, entered ordinary language from the mouth of a head of state with an interest in the charge. Before either of them, the critique of religion had built the template of suspicion: Enlightenment writers argued that priestly doctrine was not merely false but functional, maintained by institutions whose position it secured, and that its hold over believers was explained by education, habit, and the monopoly of consolation in a world of suffering. Feuerbach's account of religion as projection, published in 1841, gave the suspicion a mechanism: human beings alienate their own powers into a being who then rules them, so that the critique of the belief becomes a recovery of what the believers themselves made.

Marx's redirection, accomplished across the eighteen forties, was to move the entire structure from the heavens of religion and the salon of philosophy onto the ground of production. Ideology's inversions were no longer primarily errors of priests or philosophers to be corrected by better ideas. They were products of a social order that generated them as reliably as it generated commodities, and their persistence was explained by the order's persistence, not by the cleverness of their authors. The methodological consequences still govern practice. First, the target shifted from propositions to the conditions producing them, which is why steps four and five of the template concern institutions and acceptance rather than refutation. Second, the criticism of ideas alone was demoted: a representation anchored in material practice survives its refutation in print, a lesson every generation of critics relearns when a demolished argument returns the following season in a new vocabulary. Third, the critic's own ideas were placed under the same law of production, planting the seed that Mannheim would grow into the paradox. A researcher who knows the prehistory reads the classical texts differently: not as the discovery that ideas can serve power, which was old, but as the discovery that they do so systematically, through mechanisms that a method can enumerate.

## The exemplar: Marx's own practice in Capital

Methods are best audited against the practice of their founders, and the founder's mature practice is more disciplined than much of what later bore his name. Capital's critique of political economy is overwhelmingly immanent in structure. Marx takes the economists' own categories, value, labour, exchange between equals, and shows that the wage relation, examined by the standards those categories themselves set, produces a result the categories cannot acknowledge: the working day divides into the hours that replace the wage and the hours that do not, and surplus value arises inside an exchange of equivalents rather than by cheating across it. The famous turn of the argument, in which the sphere of circulation is described as a noisy Eden of freedom and equality that the analysis leaves for the hidden abode of production, is a demonstration staged on the opponent's premises. Whatever a reader concludes about the labour theory of value, the procedure is the template's ancestor: record the doctrine fairly, grant its standards, exhibit the contradiction its own record generates.

The section on commodity fetishism performs a complete ideology critique in a few pages, and it rewards study as a specimen of the steps compressed. The representation is the commodity form itself, in which the social character of labour appears as a property of things. The partiality is not falsity: prices are real, exchange works, and the appearance is generated by the practice rather than imposed on it by a deceiver, which is why Marx's analysis has no villain and needs none. The mechanism is the practice of exchange itself, repeated until the form becomes second nature. Acceptance requires no theory of duping, because the appearance is how the world genuinely presents itself to participants whose cooperation reproduces it daily. Later ideology critique degraded when it forgot that exemplar and reverted to a hunt for liars. The researcher should return to the fetishism section whenever the genre's cruder versions tempt her, because it proves that the method's most powerful form explains a misrecognition that nobody authored and everybody enacts.

## Grammar as evidence: the textual operations in detail

Step two's findings must be cashable in features a reader can point to, and grammar supplies the hardest currency. Transitivity analysis, borrowed from linguistics and discourse study, asks of every clause who does what to whom, and then asks where the doers went. The passive without an agent, the nominalisation that converts a process into a thing, and the abstract subject that acts in place of persons form a family: workers were dismissed, restructuring was implemented, the market demanded. Each is a legitimate construction in isolation. A pattern, in which one class of actors reliably loses its verbs while another keeps them, is evidence, because grammar distributes responsibility and the distribution has a direction. Modality carries similar weight. The modal verbs of necessity, must, cannot, no alternative, mark the boundary a text draws between the negotiable and the given, and mapping which arrangements a document treats as open to choice and which it places beyond choice is among the most reproducible findings a critique can offer, since another analyst given the same document will draw nearly the same map.

Lexical evidence is softer and must be handled with more ceremony. Word choice matters, the selection of reform against cut, of flexibility against insecurity, but single words prove little, because writers inherit vocabularies and editors normalise them. What carries weight is patterned contrast within a corpus: the same agency describing its own staff reductions as modernisation and a rival's as devastation, the same newspaper calling one country's forces troops and another's fighters across a year of coverage. Corpus methods have changed the evidential standard here. Frequency, collocation, and comparison against a reference corpus let the critic show that a selection is systematic rather than anecdotal, and a step two finding supported by counts across a declared corpus will survive replies that a handful of quotations cannot. The discipline cuts both ways, honourably: corpus checks also acquit texts, by showing that a suspicious phrase is the genre's universal habit rather than this issuer's pointed choice, and the critic who runs the check before publishing the charge has performed the method on her own suspicion first.

## Working with images and other non verbal specimens

Representations are not all prose, and the method's steps translate to visual and material specimens with adjustments the researcher should make explicit. Step one for an image records its elements, its commissioning and placement, and the claim it makes in the loose but real sense in which a recruitment photograph claims that service is glamorous or a bank's headquarters claims that finance is solid. Step two examines selection in the visual register: what the frame excludes, which standpoint the camera occupies, literally in the case of the executive portrait taken from below or the protest photographed from the police line, and whose faces are individuated while others form a crowd. Naturalisation in images often works through the unposed effect, the documentary style that borrows the authority of the found scene for an arranged one, and eternalisation through the monumental, stone and bronze proposing that the arrangement they honour shares their permanence.

Circulation and acceptance translate as well. The image's mechanisms include syndication libraries, stock categories that pre sort the visual world into searchable standpoints, and the economics of the front page, where the picture editor's choice among agency frames does more ideological work in an afternoon than a leader column does in a month. Acceptance of visual ideology rarely takes the form of belief at all; nobody assents to a photograph the way one assents to a claim, which is precisely its advantage as a carrier, since representations that are not experienced as assertions are not examined as assertions either. The critic's corresponding discipline is restraint: because images underdetermine their claims, the range of defensible readings is wider, and the written critique should carry its uncertainty openly, distinguishing what the composition demonstrates from what the critic's tradition expects it to mean. A critique of an image that could have been written without seeing it has failed step one in the visual register.

## A third worked example: the history textbook chapter

The textbook is the specimen where the method meets its most consequential audience, and a brief application shows the steps working on prose of the most ordinary kind. Take the standard chapter, found in many national traditions, that narrates industrialisation. Step one records its claims: that factories arose, that inventors invented, that populations moved, that living standards in time rose. None of these is false. Step two tests the chapter against the comparison set, its own tradition's scholarship, and finds the operations. Agency is distributed upward: entrepreneurs and inventors act in the active voice, while the enclosed, the displaced, and the factory children appear as the objects of processes, moved, drawn, absorbed. Omission runs in pattern: the legal violence of enclosure and combination law is compressed into background, the empire's markets and materials arrive as geography rather than as conquest, and the chapter's periodisation, beginning with inventions rather than with the reorganisation of land and labour, selects a story in which technology drives and people follow. Universalisation appears in the chapter's we, the national subject whose industry this was, absorbing the standpoints of those for whom the same decades were catastrophe into a single narrative of progress at different speeds.

Steps three to five complete the analysis without a villain in sight. The favoured position is that of the national settlement the textbook serves, a citizenry reconciled to an order presented as the outcome of ingenuity rather than of contested decisions, and no ministry needs to have dictated a line for curriculum selection, examination syllabuses, and publishers' risk aversion to produce the chapter reliably across decades. Acceptance is the easiest step here and the most instructive: the chapter is believed because it is half true, because its numbers on output and eventual wages are real, because its story flatters the reader's membership, and because the counter narrative, where it exists, is fragmented across specialist works no fourteen year old will meet. The fork declaration is partiality throughout, with one exception: where a chapter asserts specific falsehoods, a date, an attribution, a suppressed statute's nonexistence, those are falsity findings, and they are established by the archive, not by the critique. The example's lesson generalises. The method earns its keep on the vast middle territory of true but arranged material, and it discredits itself when, impatient with that territory, it inflates arrangement into invention.

## The critic's own text: applying the template reflexively

Reflexivity stops being a paradox and becomes a practice when it is converted into a checklist performed on one's own draft before publication. Run the template over the critique itself. Step one: has the critique's own claim been recorded as precisely as the specimen's, including what it invites readers to do, which is usually to distrust a named institution and trust the critic? Step two: what does the critique omit? The standard omissions are the specimen's strongest passages, the evidence for the opposing reading, and the cases where the same operations appear in texts the critic admires. A critique that exhibits its own comparison set, including the passages that resist it, has answered the charge of selection in the only way available, by showing the selection. Step three: whose position does the critique favour? The honest answer always includes the critic's own profession, since ideology critique is cultural capital in the academy and in journalism, a performance of vigilance that advances careers independently of its accuracy, and naming that interest costs the critic nothing but the appearance of purity she should not want.

Steps four and five apply with equal force. The critique circulates through mechanisms, journals, syllabuses, and feeds, whose selection pressures reward the striking reading over the accurate one and the total verdict over the forked one, and the writer who knows this can discount her own incentives accordingly, as a surveyor corrects for a known instrument bias. Acceptance, finally: readers accept critiques for reasons, including the pleasure of feeling undeceived, which is a pleasure the genre manufactures and sells, and a readership's applause is therefore not evidence of a critique's truth, any more than a congregation's applause settles a theology. None of this self application paralyses the work. It prices it. A critique that has passed its own template carries the one credential the genre cannot fake, the demonstration that its author submits to the discipline she applies, and hostile readers, who are the readers who matter, extend credit to exactly that demonstration.

## Other objections the method must answer

Unfalsifiability and reflexivity are the deepest objections, but three others recur in the literature and deserve answers of the same candour. The first is reductionism: that the method treats ideas as mere reflections of interests and so cannot explain why representations differ among societies with similar economies, or why a critique is ever worth writing if belief is only a shadow of position. The answer is built into the procedure. Steps two and five attend to the specific content and internal force of representations, which a reflection theory cannot do, and the tradition's own best work, Gramsci above all, treated ideas as a material force in their own right, organising consent and action. The method assumes connection, not identity, between position and representation, and the space between the two is where the analysis lives.

The second objection is relativism: that by situating all thought the method destroys the ground for preferring any account, including its own findings, to their rivals. The reply distinguishes situatedness from arbitrariness, as Mannheim's relationism attempted. Accounts are compared by their handling of evidence, the range of phenomena they accommodate, their survival under the defeaters they declare, and their performance when applied to their authors. A partiality finding about a textbook does not become optional because the critic also has a standpoint; it becomes a claim to be checked against the textbook, which sits there indifferent to both parties' standpoints and settles the matter by being read. The world's resistance to being misdescribed is the researcher's oldest ally, and ideology critique, at its disciplined best, is a technique for arranging collisions between representations and the records they claim to describe.

The third objection is political asymmetry of suspicion: that the method's practitioners direct it overwhelmingly at arrangements they already oppose, so that its findings, even when locally sound, compose into a literature whose selection of targets is itself ideological by the method's own definition. The objection is largely just as a description of the literature, and the remedy is procedural rather than defensive. Symmetric application, pre committed specimen selection, and the publication of acquittals, the critiques that find a suspected text innocent of the charged operation, would repair the literature's selection problem at its root. Researchers building a programme in this field should treat the acquittal as a publishable result with the same status as the conviction, since a method that only convicts is, by the argument of this entire article, a court and not an inquiry.

## Building the specimen dossier: sampling and provenance in practice

Step one sounds elementary until a long or diffuse specimen forces choices, and the choices deserve a protocol of their own. For a bounded text, a speech, an advertisement, a chapter, the dossier is the whole text, and the only discipline is completeness. For an unbounded specimen, a newspaper's coverage, a ministry's output, a platform's messaging across a season, the critic must define the corpus before analysing it: the dates, the sections, the search terms by which items were gathered, and the treatment of items the search missed. A corpus defined after the findings are known is not a corpus but a selection wearing a corpus's clothes. The protocol is borrowed from content analysis, and borrowing it costs the critic nothing but an afternoon, while buying the one thing the genre chronically lacks, a sampling story a sceptic can inspect.

Provenance work belongs to the dossier as well. Documents have versions, and the differences between a draft, a released text, and a later corrected edition are evidence of the selection process at work, which is why archives and freedom of information releases have become the method's growth area: the redaction, the softened paragraph, the statistic present in the briefing and absent from the speech are findings that no amount of close reading of the final text could supply. The researcher should record, for every specimen, its chain of custody in miniature: who issued it, through what process, with what opportunity for revision, and what earlier states survive. Where the specimen is an utterance, a minister's interview answer, the dossier records the question asked, since answers inherit their framing from questions more often than speakers admit. Where the specimen is a translation, the dossier records the translator and the edition, for the reasons the sources section gave about the tradition's own texts, which apply with double force to the texts of governments and movements publishing in several languages at once.

## Comparison sets: how partiality is actually measured

Partiality is a relation, and relations need two terms. The comparison set is the second term, and its construction is the least discussed and most decisive act in the whole procedure. Four comparison sets recur, each answering a different charge. The internal set compares the specimen with itself: its annexes against its summary, its treatment of one group against its treatment of another, its past editions against its present one. Internal comparison powers immanent critique and needs no external standard at all. The horizontal set compares the specimen with contemporaries of different standpoint covering the same matter: rival newspapers, competing ministries, the union's account against the employer's. Horizontal comparison shows selection by exhibiting the road not taken, in the words of writers who took it. The vertical set compares the specimen with the record beneath it: the statistics it summarises, the archive it cites, the testimony it edits. Vertical comparison is where falsity findings, when they exist, are usually born. The counterfactual set, the most demanding, asks what a representation produced from the standpoint the specimen silences would look like, and is constructed from the silenced parties' own documents where they exist, as they usually do, in pamphlets, proceedings, and literatures the canon ignored.

Each set disciplines a characteristic vice. Internal comparison disciplines the critic who denounces a text for failing standards it never set itself. Horizontal comparison disciplines the critic who treats the available alternatives as imaginary. Vertical comparison disciplines the critic who prefers rhetoric to records. Counterfactual comparison disciplines the whole enterprise against the subtlest vice of all, which is assuming that the selection the specimen made was the only selection the material allowed. A critique should declare which sets it used, and a reader should be able to infer, from the declaration, exactly how far the findings can travel. Findings from internal comparison convict the specimen of incoherence. Findings from vertical comparison can convict it of misdescription. Findings from the counterfactual set alone convict it only of being written from somewhere, which everything is, and the writer who presents the fourth as though it were the second has committed the genre's standard fraud in its most respectable costume.

## Ideology in official categories: law, statistics, and the census

Some of the most consequential representations a society produces are not arguments at all but categories: the boxes on the census form, the classifications of unemployment, the legal definitions that decide whose work counts as work and whose household counts as a family. Ideology critique applies to them with unusual power, because categories share the method's central property, they select and arrange while presenting themselves as mere description, and they add a property texts lack, they compel. A citizen may ignore a speech; she must answer the census in its own terms or be counted wrong. The critique of a category proceeds by the template with the steps adjusted. The claim a category makes is that the world divides this way for the purposes at hand. Its partiality shows in its history, since every official classification has a genealogy of revisions in which earlier arrangements, and the interests they served, remain legible, and in its residuals, the categories of other, mixed, and not stated where the scheme's failures accumulate.

The favoured position of a statistical category is often an administrative one before it is a class one, which is a useful corrective to critics who reach for the largest beneficiary first. A definition of unemployment that excludes those who have stopped searching serves a government's presentational interest, certainly, but it also serves the statistical office's interest in a measurable, stable series, and the two interests can conflict, as they do when offices defend a series against ministers who want it improved. The method's benefit discipline, with its separation of kinds of benefit and its ban on inferred intention, was designed for exactly such cases. Acceptance of categories, finally, is the deepest acceptance of all, because categories are accepted by being used, and their users include the classified themselves, who organise claims, identities, and solidarities inside the boxes provided, sometimes turning a category imposed from above into a banner from below. That reversibility is a standing warning against the critic's condescension: the objects of classification are also its authors in the next round, and a method that cannot see their agency in the material will misread the material's politics entirely.

## The method in the archive: memoirs, official histories, and testimony

Historians practise a version of the method whenever they weigh a source, and making the kinship explicit improves both crafts. The memoir is a representation whose step one facts, author, date of writing, distance from the events, intended audience, anticipated litigation, shape every later step. Its partiality has a signature the template names easily: the memoirist universalises his own vantage into the meaning of the episode, naturalises the outcome as the only one the situation allowed, and omits, with a regularity that experienced readers price in advance, the meetings at which he was absent and the advice of his that was ignored. The official history adds institutional mechanisms: commissioned authors, controlled access, publication timed to settlements, and the quiet selection of which archive was complete when the historian was admitted to it. None of this makes such sources unusable. It makes them usable in the way ideology critique teaches, as evidence of two things at once, the events they describe and the self understanding of the power that produced the description, with the second often the more reliable yield.

Testimony from below requires the method's symmetric application, and here the discipline bites the critic's own sympathies. Subaltern testimony is evidence of the first order about experience, and it is also representation, selected, framed, and circulated by mechanisms, including sympathetic ones, with their own selections: the testimony that fits the campaign is quoted, the testimony that complicates it waits in the box. A researcher who runs the template over friendly testimony and finds the expected operations, compression, emblematic selection, the occasional polished anecdote, has not betrayed the witnesses. She has treated them as adults making representations under pressure, which is what they were, and her history, built on testimony whose manufacture she understands, will stand up in rooms where a history built on innocence would not. The acceptance step, in archival work, becomes the history of a text's career: which institutions reprinted it, which syllabuses carried it, which anniversaries revived it, and a memoir's ideology is often less legible in its pages than in the pattern of its revivals.

## Specimens that resist: science, mathematics, and the limits of suspicion

A method's maturity shows in its treatment of the specimens that resist it. Claims in the mature natural sciences are the standing test. Their representations are produced by institutions with interests, careers, and funding streams, and the sociology of science has documented the resulting selections in detail: which questions get asked, which results get published, which anomalies get shelved. Ideology critique has real work to do at that level, the level of agendas and institutions. It has almost none to do at the level of the individual established result, because the results are anchored by a discipline of replication and prediction whose indifference to the result's serviceability is precisely what step two's insensitivity test measures. A channel that publishes findings embarrassing to its funders, as scientific channels demonstrably and repeatedly do, fails the ideological selection test at the point that matters, and the critic who presses the charge anyway has mistaken the sociology of an institution for the refutation of its findings, the genetic fallacy in a laboratory coat.

The honest treatment states the boundary as a finding about the method rather than an embarrassment to it. Ideology critique is a method for representations whose production is governed by persuasion, legitimation, and administration. Where production is governed instead by a working error correction machinery, the method's proper object shrinks to the machinery's margins: the framing of results for publics, the selection of research programmes, the rhetoric of popularisation, all of which remain rich specimens. Knowing where a method stops is part of knowing the method, and the critic who claims everything as territory ends, as the unfalsifiability section argued, able to explain nothing in particular. The same restraint applies to mathematics and logic, whose institutional histories are human and critiqueable and whose proofs are not, and to the critic's own favoured sciences, where the temptation to exempt is the reflexivity problem returning in a white coat.

## Writing the critique: the finished piece and its order

The finished critique is a piece of writing with its own rhetoric, and its order should mirror the template's, because the reader's trust is built in the same sequence as the analysis. Open with the specimen, recorded fairly and at a length that lets the reader test the record; a critique that opens with its verdict teaches the reader to stop reading at the verdict. Proceed to the operations, each exhibited in the specimen's features and measured against the declared comparison set, and resist the anthology effect, the stringing of damning quotations without the pattern that makes them evidence. State the benefit finding in its exact kind and strength, with the intention question answered or explicitly left open. Document the mechanism. Give the acceptance account the space its difficulty deserves, written to pass the recognition test. Close with the fork declaration and the defeaters, in that order, so the piece ends by stating what it has and has not shown and what would change its author's mind.

Two stylistic rules protect the substance. The first is that the critic's prose should be plainer than the specimen's, since a critique written in denser jargon than its object invites the suspicion, usually justified, that obscurity is doing the work of demonstration. The second is that adjectives are rationed: the operations have names, and a passage that calls a text dishonest, sinister, or Orwellian has spent its credibility on temperature. Reviewers and examiners, who read the genre in bulk, develop an infallible sense for the ratio of demonstration to denunciation in the first two pages, and they price the whole piece by it. The writer who wants the findings believed should therefore perform the discipline conspicuously at the start, where it is most expensive, quoting the specimen's best passage against herself early, conceding the true parts before indicting the arrangement, and letting the pattern accumulate until the conclusion feels less like a verdict delivered than like a sum the reader has watched being added.

## A fourth worked example: the museum exhibition

The exhibition is a specimen in three dimensions, and curators are among the most self conscious producers of representation working anywhere, which makes their product the fairest test of the method's manners. Step one records the exhibition's declared subject, its funders and lenders, its sequence of rooms, and the wall texts, which are the exhibition's propositions and should be quoted with the same fairness as a pamphlet's. Step two examines the hang as an argument: which objects open and close the story, what the lighting honours, the ratio of masterpieces to context, the point at which the empire, the slavery, the expropriation that financed the collection is present as a label, as a room, or as an absence. Omissions in exhibitions have a physical quality that textual omissions lack. The object not borrowed, the gallery not built, the provenance line that ends one owner too early: these can be photographed, counted, and compared against the collection's own database, which the institution usually publishes, handing the critic an internal comparison set of unusual completeness.

The remaining steps follow the pattern the earlier examples established, with one variation worth noting. The favoured position in exhibition critique is frequently that of the lending and funding structure rather than of any doctrine, and the mechanism is the loan system itself, which rewards narratives that honour lenders and punishes, by quiet non renewal, narratives that interrogate them; no instruction from a sponsor is required when the renewal calendar does the instructing. Acceptance, finally, is performed by the visitor's feet: the route taken, the rooms lingered in, the shop at the exit where the exhibition's argument is sold back as merchandise, and the critic who wants to explain the exhibition's power must account for the real pleasures it gives, beauty, craft, the company of remarkable things, since an account in which millions attend only because they are deceived fails the recognition test as surely here as anywhere. The fork, declared as usual: partiality, demonstrated from hang, label, and loan record; falsity only where a label asserts what the provenance file contradicts, established by the file.

## How critiques are judged: referees, examiners, and hostile readers

A method exists inside institutions that judge its products, and the working researcher should know the tribunal's habits. Journal referees in the relevant fields test a submitted critique at predictable points: the fairness of the specimen record, the declaration of the comparison set, the separation of benefit from intention, and the fork. A critique vague at any of those points draws the same report in different handwriting, that the piece asserts where it should demonstrate. Examiners test for the same joints in compressed form, which is why the student layer of this article teaches the steps as a sequence rather than as a vocabulary. Hostile readers, the subject's defenders, test at the point of maximum danger to themselves, which is step five: a critique whose acceptance account insults the audience will be answered, successfully, by the audience's representatives exhibiting their own reasons, and the original finding, however sound its textual work, is buried under the wreckage of its fifth step.

The tribunal's habits suggest a pre submission ritual. Give the draft to the reader least sympathetic to its conclusion who will still read it carefully, and ask only where the demonstration thins. Check every quotation against the specimen at full length, once, with the surrounding paragraph restored. Read the benefit section aloud and strike every sentence that would require the subject to have intended what the evidence only shows to have resulted. And reread the defeaters paragraph with the suspicion that it has gone soft, that the defeaters named are ones the author privately knows cannot occur, because a defeater that cannot occur is a decoration, and referees can tell. The ritual is unglamorous, and it is the difference between the genre's durable work and its season's noise.

## Encoding, decoding, and the audience fights back

The acceptance step acquired its most influential modern instrument from media research, and the researcher should know it because it changed what counts as evidence at step five. Stuart Hall's encoding and decoding model, drafted in 1973 and published in revised form in 1980, proposed that producers encode meaning within a framework of assumptions, while audiences decode from their own positions, producing readings that may align with the encoded one, negotiate with it, or oppose it outright. The model's importance for the method is that it converted acceptance from a speculation into a researchable distribution. A critique can no longer assert that a programme teaches its viewers a lesson; it must ask which viewers, in what proportions, take the lesson as offered, which haggle, and which invert it, and the asking requires audience evidence, interviews, ethnography, letters, the record of what audiences actually did with the material.

The model also corrected the tradition from inside, and the correction deserves emphasis because it answers a taunt the unfalsifiability section took seriously. If audiences routinely produce negotiated and oppositional readings, then the image of ideology as a message injected into passive minds is empirically false for the most studied medium of all, and any critique still written in that image is refuted by its own field's evidence. What survives the correction is the more precise claim the mature method always intended: power operates less by dictating conclusions than by setting the agenda of decodable meanings, supplying the preferred reading with institutional weight, and arranging the costs of the oppositional one. Hall's own later work on the authoritarian populism of the nineteen eighties showed the two levels working together, consent actively constructed through real grievances addressed in a particular vocabulary. The student of step five should treat the encoding model as the floor of respectability: an acceptance account that cannot say where its audience sits in the distribution of readings has not yet begun.

## A specimen phrase: no alternative

Small specimens repay the template as reliably as large ones, and the career of a single phrase shows why. The assertion that there is no alternative, attached in public memory to Margaret Thatcher's governments and thereafter to a whole style of economic argument, is a claim compressed into a refusal of claims. Step one records it fairly: as uttered in context it usually meant that a specific policy's rivals had been weighed and found unworkable, a judgement open to evidence. Step two finds the operation in the phrase's grammar and career rather than in any single utterance. The phrase performs eternalisation by moving a contestable ranking of options outside the field of contest, and it does so without asserting anything that could be refuted, since no alternative is named and therefore none can be examined. Its partiality is measurable in its asymmetry of application: the phrase is deployed against departures from an existing settlement and rarely against the settlement's own continuations, which are also choices among alternatives in the plain sense.

Steps three to five run cleanly. The favoured position is whoever administers the existing settlement, and the favouring requires no intention beyond the wish to end an argument one is winning. Circulation runs through the phrase's portability: it fits headlines, ends interviews, and relieves editors of the duty to commission the rival case, a mechanism of professional convenience rather than of command. Acceptance is the instructive step, as usual. Opponents repeat the phrase because it names a real experience, the exhaustion of alternatives that have genuinely been tried and have genuinely failed, and because constructing a credible alternative is expensive collective work while conceding the phrase costs an individual nothing at the moment of concession. The critique's finding is partiality, precisely declared: the phrase arranges a real debate into a false closure. It does not show, and does not pretend to show, that any particular policy defended with the phrase was wrong, which would require the independent investigation the fork demands, conducted on budgets and outcomes rather than on slogans.

## Drills: learning the steps by exercise

Procedures are learned by repetition against specimens of graduated difficulty, and the researcher training herself, or the teacher building the skill in others, can use a sequence of drills that isolate the steps before combining them. The first drill is pure recording: take a speech or editorial and produce a statement of its claims that its author would sign, with no critical vocabulary permitted, and continue until the fairness stops feeling like surrender. The second drill is grammatical: take one page and mark every clause for agency, noting who acts, who is acted upon, and which processes arrive without actors, then compare the pattern against the same outlet's treatment of a different subject. The third drill is comparative: write the step two analysis of one event from two opposed sources' coverage, then write the paragraph that neither source could have published, which is the comparison set made visible. The fourth drill is the fork: take a published critique, including celebrated ones, and mark every conclusion as a partiality claim or a falsity claim, then check what evidence the falsity claims actually rest on; the exercise is chastening in a way no lecture achieves. The fifth drill is reflexive: run the finished template over a text of one's own side, a campaign document, a favoured columnist, one's own earlier essay, and publish nothing of the result until it is as exact as the work done on opponents. A practitioner who has completed the sequence possesses the method in her hands. A practitioner who has only read about it possesses, as the opening section warned, a set of conclusions in search of specimens to attach themselves to, which is the condition the whole procedure exists to cure.

## Analysing absence: the disciplined study of silence

Omission is the operation critics allege most and demonstrate least, because an absence can be alleged against any text whatever, and the allegation is cheap until the critic establishes that the missing material belonged there. Three tests convert silence from an insinuation into a finding. The relevance test asks whether the omitted matter bears directly on the specimen's own claims, by the specimen's own standards: a company report that celebrates safety while omitting the accident figures it is legally required to compile elsewhere fails this test cleanly, while a sonnet that omits the accident figures fails nothing. The availability test asks whether the material was within the producer's reach at the time, in its own files, its own earlier publications, or the public record it routinely uses; an omission of what could not have been known is a misfortune, not an operation. The pattern test asks whether the silence is selective, falling consistently on one side of the account while comparable material on the other side is included in detail, since a text that omits everything equally is merely short.

Silence has levels that the written critique should distinguish, because they carry different weights. There is the silence of the sentence, the agent deleted by a passive. There is the silence of the document, the topic the report never raises. There is the silence of the archive, the records never created or never kept, which is the historian's special problem, since power decides in advance what the future will be able to know, and whole categories of experience, the informal economy, the unwaged household, the unrecorded meeting, reach the researcher only through the traces they left in other people's paperwork. And there is the silence of the field, the questions a discipline does not ask for a generation, visible only when a later generation asks them and finds the cupboard bare. A critique should name the level it is working at, because the evidence differs: the sentence is shown by grammar, the document by comparison, the archive by the records of record keeping, and the field by bibliography. Confusing the levels produces the characteristic overclaim in which a writer moves from a deleted agent in a press release to the silences of an epoch in three sentences, and the reader who was persuaded at the first level is lost, correctly, by the third.

## How findings age: the critique re-read after its moment

Ideology critiques are unusually perishable goods, because their specimens decay and their contexts evaporate, and the researcher consulting the classics of the genre needs a protocol for reading work whose moment has passed. The first question is what the critique took its readers to know. The celebrated mid century readings of advertisements assumed a visual culture their readers shared at the level of the street hoarding; detached from that culture, the readings look like ingenuity exercised on trivia, when in fact they were precise instruments aimed at the most repeated representations of their societies. The second question is which of the critique's findings were structural and which were conjunctural. A finding about how advertising finance selects content outlives the campaigns it was drawn from. A finding about one campaign's meaning dies with the campaign, and reviving it as a general truth about advertising is the error of treating a specimen as a theory. The third question is what later evidence did to the findings: audience research overturned some confident mid century claims about passive absorption, the encoding and decoding literature being the famous correction, and the honest modern reader keeps a small ledger of which classics were confirmed, which were qualified, and which were quietly abandoned by their own schools.

The protocol runs in the other direction as well, as a discipline for the writer who wants her own critique to age well. Date the specimen precisely and archive a copy, because links rot and editions change, and a critique whose specimen cannot be retrieved becomes unverifiable in a decade. Separate the structural claims from the conjunctural ones in the text itself, and mark the conjunctural claims as such, so that later readers can salvage the structure without defending the moment. Prefer mechanisms to meanings where a choice must be made, since mechanisms, ownership, selection routines, cost structures, persist across the turnover of messages, while meanings are renegotiated by every audience. And write the defeaters paragraph as though its first reader were the future: the findings that survive longest in this genre are those whose authors specified, in advance, the evidence that would retire them, and whose retirement, when it came, could therefore be recognised as knowledge gained rather than as defeat concealed.

## A fifth worked example: the job advertisement

The job advertisement is the specimen most readers have personally been processed by, and running the template over it teaches the method's central surprises in miniature: that the ideological work is done by genre conventions rather than by villains, and that acceptance is engineered through the applicant's own hopes. Take the standard advertisement for a junior post in a competitive industry, assembled from the phrases the genre repeats everywhere. Step one records its claims: the post offers a fast paced environment, a dynamic team, exposure to senior figures, a competitive package, and the opportunity to grow, and it requires a self starter with passion for the field, resilience, and flexibility. Recorded fairly, none of this is invention; the genre's phrases are the small change of recruitment, and many are written by staff copying the last advertisement the firm placed.

Step two finds the operations, and they are systematic in a direction. The advertisement's nouns name everything the firm receives, passion, resilience, flexibility, self starting, in the vocabulary of character, so that requirements which are in fact about unpaid availability and tolerance of understaffing are redescribed as virtues the applicant either possesses or lacks. Its account of what the applicant receives is conducted, by contrast, in the vocabulary of atmosphere and possibility: environment, exposure, opportunity, growth, words that commit the firm to nothing measurable, while the one measurable item, pay, is concealed inside the genre's most accomplished euphemism, competitive, a word that asserts a comparison it never performs. Agency follows the template's familiar grammar. The firm offers, seeks, and invests, active throughout, while the duties of the post arrive nominalised, responsibilities include, the role involves, and the possibility that the workload of two posts has been merged into one appears nowhere, because the genre has no sentence for it. Naturalisation completes the arrangement: the pace is fast as weather is fast, a property of the environment the applicant must suit, not a consequence of a staffing decision that could have been taken otherwise and priced.

Steps three to five show why the specimen matters beyond its comedy. The favoured position is the employer's, in the specific form of a screening device: the advertisement selects for applicants who will experience the post's demands as tests of character rather than as terms to be negotiated, and it does so before any interview, at the cost of a page of copy. The mechanism is pure genre reproduction, agencies reusing templates, managers approving familiar wording under time pressure, job boards structuring the fields, which is step four's standing lesson that circulation usually needs no one's ideology but everyone's routine. Acceptance is the masterclass. Applicants do not merely tolerate the vocabulary; they reproduce it against themselves, writing cover letters that promise passion and resilience, because the advertisement has set the currency in which applications are denominated, and declining the currency means not being considered at all. The applicant who privately translates every phrase does so and applies anyway, enacting the arrangement while seeing through it, which is the condition the later theorists of ideology as practice described: the knowledge is present, the critique is fluent, and the behaviour is unchanged, because the mechanism never depended on belief. The fork, declared: partiality throughout, since nearly every phrase is defensible in isolation and the finding rests on the patterned asymmetry between what is specified and what is left atmospheric. A critique of a single advertisement would be a squib. A critique of the genre, run across a corpus of a thousand advertisements with the asymmetries counted, is research, and the distance between those two objects is the distance this whole method exists to teach.

## Standards of proof: matching the claim to the evidence

The method's claims come in grades, and much refereeing trouble is avoided by grading them explicitly in the text. The weakest grade is the observation: this specimen, on this occasion, deploys this operation. An observation needs one fairly recorded text and survives almost any dispute about the wider world, and it is correspondingly modest in what it buys. The next grade is the pattern: across a declared corpus, the operation recurs with a direction. A pattern needs the corpus, the counts or the systematic survey, and the comparison set, and it buys the right to speak of a genre, an outlet, or an institution rather than of a document. The third grade is the mechanism claim: the pattern is produced and maintained by these institutions through these routines. Mechanisms need documents, testimony, or process evidence, and they buy explanation rather than description. The fourth grade is the acceptance claim: the audience holds the representation for these reasons. Acceptance claims need audience evidence of the kind the encoding and decoding literature modelled, and they buy the right to speak about belief rather than about texts. The fifth and highest grade is the system claim: the pattern, mechanisms, and acceptance together form part of how a social order reproduces itself. System claims are legitimate ambitions and terrible defaults, since each lower grade must be in place before the one above can be asserted, and most published critiques assert the fifth grade on evidence sufficient for the second.

Grading disciplines the fork as well. Partiality is usually established at the pattern grade, and a critic who has graded honestly will find that many of her drafts claim falsity at observation strength, the single harsh editorial standing in for a demonstrated falsehood about the world. The correction is mechanical: either fund the claim with the independent investigation its grade requires or demote the sentence to the grade its evidence supports, and demotion, in this genre, is not defeat. A demoted claim is a true claim, and a literature composed of true claims at declared grades would be more damaging to its targets than the present literature of magnificent overclaims, because its targets would lose the standard rebuttal, that the critique exaggerates, which at present is so often available. Editors and examiners reward the grading instinct visibly when they meet it, since it is the instinct their own standards are built from, and the researcher who acquires it early will find her critiques surviving referees who return her rivals' work with the margins full of the single word: evidence?

One practical instrument enforces grading at the drafting stage. Assign every substantive sentence in the analysis a grade in the margin during revision, and require the final text to contain no sentence whose language asserts a higher grade than its margin allows: pattern language, typically, systematically, the genre's habit, requires pattern evidence in the dossier, while mechanism language, produces, maintains, selects for, requires the process record. Sentences that cannot be graded are usually the rhetorical ones, and they are cut or converted with a loss the draft rarely misses. The instrument feels bureaucratic for exactly one draft. Thereafter it is the difference between writing critiques and writing about them.

## A note on tone: writing about believers

The last discipline is tonal, and it is not decoration. A critique's findings are about representations, but its sentences land on people, the believers whose acceptance it explains and the producers whose work it anatomises, and the genre's habitual tone, a knowing weariness at the spectacle of other people's illusions, does measurable damage to its own findings. It licences the reader to enjoy superiority instead of following evidence. It guarantees the hostile response the acceptance section warned against, since people who recognise themselves described as dupes stop reading at the description and never reach the demonstration. And it falsifies the analysis, because the sneer is itself a rhetorical operation of exactly the kind the template catalogues: a unification device, gathering the critic and her readers into the company of the undeceived, against a fragmented them, defined by their deception. A critique that needs that device to hold its audience has confessed something about its evidence.

The alternative tone is the one the best practitioners share across their other differences: a surface calm, an almost legal patience, in which the specimen is quoted fully, the concessions are made early and without irony, the producers are credited with their intelligence and often with their sincerity, and the conclusion is allowed to arrive at the speed the demonstration sets. That tone is not politeness as strategy, although it is also that. It is the sound of the fork being respected in public, of claims priced at their evidence, of believers treated as the analysis's subjects rather than its butts. Writers who adopt it discover that it is also the more damaging tone, since an institution can survive being denounced by its enemies indefinitely, and denunciation even certifies its importance, but it survives less easily being described, accurately and without heat, in a prose its own members cannot fault for fairness. The method's founders understood this in their practice if not always in their polemics: the most quoted pages of the tradition are its most patient ones, and patience, in the end, is the whole technique.

## For students and exam candidates

Your examinable asset is the procedure itself, because method questions reward the candidate who can do something rather than merely define something. Learn the five steps as a sequence you can state cold, and attach to each its evidence requirement and its characteristic failure, since questions about limits are answered from the failures. Learn the partiality-or-falsity fork as your evaluative engine: it converts any specimen question into a judgement about what the critique has established and what it has merely asserted, which is the distinction marking schemes describe as analysis against evaluation. Learn Thompson's five modes as a checklist, with one strategy under each, and Mannheim's paradox as a named problem with three assessed escapes, the intelligentsia, the standpoint, and the break, none accepted whole. When a question hands you a text, spend your first minutes recording its claims before reaching for the vocabulary; scripts that begin with the specimen and end with the fork outscore scripts that begin with denunciation, whatever the candidate's politics, because they demonstrate the method instead of announcing its conclusions.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

Teach the method as a practical, with specimens short enough to be read whole in the room, because the recording discipline of step one cannot be learned from summaries of texts. Set the same specimen twice, once for a partiality critique and once for an attempted falsity critique, and let the class feel the difference in the evidence the second attempt demands; the fork is understood in the hands before it is understood in the head. Insist on the intention rule early, since seminars drift towards motive speculation within minutes if the drift is not blocked by a norm, and use the diamond campaign as the demonstration that a critique can be complete, and more damaging, with every participant's sincerity granted. The fuller classroom design, sequencing, misconception handling, and assessment, is set out in the teaching article this method article cross references, and the present layer confines itself to the one warning experience insists on: assess the procedure, not the verdict, or students will learn to guess the verdict you reward and call the guessing critique.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

The method in usable form is a sequence of separations, and your craft is keeping them separate under deadline. Separate the specimen from your paraphrase of it, and quote at a length your subject would call fair. Separate benefit from intention, and file the intention question as unanswered unless documents answer it. Separate the partiality finding, which your textual and comparative evidence can carry, from any falsity finding, which needs an investigation of the world and not of the text. Separate the mechanism you can document from the coordination you suspect, and print only the documented one. Journalists, in particular, can use the frame questions without the vocabulary: what does this release omit that its own agency publishes, whose standpoint does its summary universalise, what would the same event look like described from the other end of the relation? Those questions survive translation into any newsroom's idiom, and they catch more than bias spotting does, because they examine the frame rather than the tilt within it. Historians will find the acceptance step their natural home, since the archive of testimony is where explanations of consent are tested, and the recognition test is only the historian's ordinary standard applied to critics.

## The method, within its limits

The closing summary is a restatement of discipline rather than of doctrine. Ideology critique, practised as this article specifies, is a five-step examination of representations that declares its claims' strength, documents its mechanisms, explains acceptance without contempt, and names the evidence that would defeat it. Its two limits are not embarrassments to be managed but the conditions of its credibility: origin does not refute, so falsity must be earned by other means, and the critic is situated, so no exemption, professional, political, or scientific, replaces the slow work of declared standpoints and symmetric application. A method that respects those limits produces findings a hostile reader can verify and a sympathetic reader can build on. A method that ignores them produces a literature of verdicts, impressive in the season of its confidence and unreadable after it. The tradition's best work has always known the difference. The procedure exists so that knowing it does not depend on talent.

For the researcher leaving this article with work to do, the order of operations is the whole bequest. Choose a specimen small enough to record whole. Build its dossier before judging it, and its comparison set before indicting it. Run the steps in order, grade every claim to the evidence that funds it, and write the defeaters paragraph while the finding is still young enough to be changed by it. Declare the fork in the finished piece, apply the template once to a text you admire for every three you apply it to texts you oppose, and keep the record in a form your future self, or your sharpest critic, can audit. Nothing in that discipline requires agreement with the tradition that produced it. Everything in it is what makes the tradition's central suspicion, that representations serve power in patterned and demonstrable ways, into a finding another scholar can test rather than a faith another believer can recite. The method is the message, in this field above all, because the method is the only part of the message that a reader is entitled to take on trust, and it earns that trust one declared, checkable, defeatable claim at a time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How do you actually do an ideology critique?

Follow the five steps in order. Record the representation and its claims fairly, in terms its issuer would accept. Establish partiality from the text's features, testing for omission, universalisation, naturalisation, and eternalisation against a comparison set. Identify whose position the pattern favours, without treating benefit as proof of intention. Document the institutional mechanisms that produce and circulate the representation. Explain why those it disadvantages accept it, using their own reasons. Finish by declaring whether the critique claims partiality or falsity, and what evidence would count against the reading.

### Q: What are the modes of operation of ideology?

Thompson's typology names five. Legitimation represents domination as worthy of support, through rationalisation, universalisation, and narrativisation. Dissimulation conceals it, through displacement and euphemisation. Unification builds a collective identity across divisions, through standardisation and symbols of unity. Fragmentation divides potential opposition, through differentiation and the construction of an enemy. Reification presents a historical arrangement as natural and permanent, through naturalisation, eternalisation, and the grammatical deletion of agents. The modes are a checklist for analysis, not a set of accusations: finding a strategy begins an argument about what it excludes and whose position the exclusion serves.

### Q: Does showing a belief serves interests prove it false?

No, and the reason has a name: the genetic fallacy. A belief's origin and function are facts about its career, not about its truth. A manufacturer's claim that its product meets a safety standard serves the manufacturer and may be entirely true, checkable against the standard itself. Ideology critique can establish that a representation is partial, selective, naturalising, or produced by mechanisms insensitive to accuracy. Demonstrating falsity requires a separate, independent argument about the world the representation describes, conducted with the evidence appropriate to that world rather than with evidence about the belief's usefulness to anyone.

### Q: What is Mannheim's paradox?

The paradox arises when the sociology of knowledge is applied to itself. Mannheim generalised ideology into the claim that all thought is socially situated, bound to the standpoint and position of the thinker. Applied consistently, that claim covers the analyst's own thought, so the critique of ideology appears to be one more situated product with no privilege over its targets. Mannheim answered with relationism, the view that situated accounts can still be compared and synthesised, and with the relatively detached intelligentsia as the synthesising stratum. Later answers, the standpoint claim and the science and ideology break, are assessed in this article; none grants a complete exemption.

### Q: How do you stop ideology critique becoming name calling?

By keeping the procedure and dropping the shortcuts that produce the degeneration. Record the specimen fairly and quote it at testable length. Demonstrate operations from its features rather than inferring them from its authorship. Keep benefit and intention in separate columns. Document mechanisms instead of imagining coordination. Explain acceptance through the believers' own reasons. Declare whether the claim is partiality or falsity, and name the evidence that would defeat the reading. Name calling begins exactly where those disciplines end, when a label is asked to do the work of a demonstration, so the remedy is procedural rather than tonal.

### Q: What should you read on the concept of ideology?

Read in the order of the quarrel. Start with The German Ideology in a scholarly edition, then Mannheim's Ideology and Utopia for the paradox, Gramsci's selections for hegemony and common sense, and Althusser's essay on ideological state apparatuses for the move from beliefs to institutions. Geuss's The Idea of a Critical Theory sorts the concept's senses philosophically. Thompson's Ideology and Modern Culture supplies the typology of modes, and Eagleton's Ideology: An Introduction surveys the competing definitions. Each should be read as a situated intervention, with attention to what its selection of the field leaves out.

### Q: Is there a standpoint from which ideology can be judged?

Three answers have been defended. Mannheim proposed the relatively unattached intelligentsia, whose mixed recruitment loosens class standpoint. Lukács proposed the standpoint of the proletariat, whose position was said to require knowledge of the social whole; feminist standpoint theory later revised the claim, treating standpoint as an achievement of analysis rather than a gift of location, and its own critics pressed the same point. Althusser proposed the science and ideology break. This article's verdict is that no standpoint exempts its holder. A position can supply evidence and questions that comfort hides, but the advantage must be earned in each inquiry by ordinary evidential virtues.

### Q: How do you write an ideology critique of a policy document?

Analyse the document before arguing with the policy. Record its issuer, status, audiences, and claims, separating the summary and press handling from the chapters, since divergence between them is primary evidence. Test the summary against the document's own data annexes, which is immanent critique in practical form. Track the genre's operations: nominalisations that delete deciders, conditional generosity that converts distribution into moral biography, unifying phrases that honour one part of the population by implication against another. Document the circulation mechanisms, consultation design, commissioned modelling, and drafting history, wherever records allow. Declare the fork before concluding.

### Q: What are the limits of ideology critique?

Two limits are structural. The genetic limit: showing that a representation serves an interest, or arises from a position, does not show it false, so the method's own steps establish partiality and only an independent investigation can establish falsity. The reflexivity limit: the critic is socially situated like everyone else, and no proposed exemption, the detached intelligentsia, the privileged standpoint, or the science and ideology break, survives inspection as a general warrant. A third practical limit follows: the method examines representations, and where the decisive facts are material rather than representational, it must hand over to other kinds of inquiry.

### Q: Is ideology critique still used in the academy?

Yes, though the vocabulary has dispersed into neighbouring fields. Media studies, political communication, education research, and the sociology of knowledge all run versions of the procedure, often under the names of framing analysis, discourse studies, or critical policy analysis, and often without the Marxist framework that produced the method. The dispersion has costs: versions that keep the textual steps and drop the acceptance step reproduce the genre's weakest form. University teaching in sociology and politics continues to examine the concept directly, and the method questions set there reward the procedural version, steps, evidence, fork, and limits, over the purely definitional answer.

### Q: What should a researcher record before judging a text ideological?

Record the specimen exactly, with issuer, genre, channel, and date of issue. Record its claims as propositions, including claims made by framing and imagery. Record the comparison set: the issuer's other documents, the underlying data, and rival accounts, since partiality is a relation between a text and its alternatives. Record the benefit claim and its kind, with the intention question explicitly bracketed. Record circulation mechanisms and the evidence for acceptance, testimony first. Record the fork declaration and the defeaters, the evidence that would change the finding. Judgement drafted after that record exists is research; judgement drafted before it is posture.

### Q: How does immanent critique differ from denouncing a text from outside?

Immanent critique judges an object by standards the object itself professes: its stated purposes, its own definitions, its promises, its announced criteria of success. The critic grants those standards and demonstrates the distance between profession and performance from the object's own record, so the conclusion rests on premises its defenders already accept. Denunciation imports the critic's standards and announces the verdict they produce, which persuades only readers who already share them. Immanent critique is slower and demands mastery of the object's self understanding, but its findings cannot be dismissed as an alien yardstick, and they teach the reader a reperformable skill.

### Q: Why must ideology critique state what would count against its own reading?

Because a reading that no evidence could disconfirm is not a claim about the world. The unfalsifiability objection, the strongest objection the method faces, holds that critiques absorb every counter observation as deeper ideology: innocence becomes concealment, endorsement becomes false consciousness, reform becomes legitimation. Stating defeaters in advance, passages the reading cannot accommodate, beneficiaries who resist the representation, acceptance better explained by truth, blocks that absorption at the only point it can be blocked, before the evidence is in. The declaration also tells readers exactly where to aim a reply, which is what makes the exchange scholarship rather than theatre.

### Q: What separates a functional explanation from proof that a belief is false?

A functional explanation accounts for a belief's existence or persistence by the effects it produces, the position it serves, the institutional need it meets. It answers a question about the belief's career. Proof of falsity answers a question about the belief's correspondence to the world, and it requires evidence of the kind appropriate to the subject matter: measurements, records, observations. The two can coexist in any combination, since a belief can be functional and true, functional and false, or useless and false. Confusing them is the genetic fallacy in its explanatory form, and it is the confusion the partiality-or-falsity fork exists to prevent.

### Q: How do you test whether an institution needs the belief it circulates?

Treat need as a hypothesis and look for the feedback loop. Ask what would happen to the institution's routines if the belief were withdrawn: whether recruitment, consent, funding, or discipline would actually falter, and whether substitutes are available that would serve as well. Examine moments of stress, when the belief was challenged internally, and observe whether the institution defended it, replaced it, or shrugged. Compare similar institutions that operate without the belief; if they function equally, the need claim weakens. Elster's discipline governs the whole test: benefit alone is not need, and a mechanism connecting the benefit to the belief's maintenance must be shown, not presumed.
