A reader who has just worked through the transformation problem arrives at an uncomfortable verdict. It is the verdict to which the New Interpretation and its rivals are all replies. Marx set out to show that prices of production are transformed values, that profit is redistributed surplus value, and that the labour embodied in commodities governs the system of prices beneath its surface. The procedure he left in the third volume of Capital was then shown, by a long line of critics and eventually by many of his defenders, to be incomplete in a damaging way: the outputs of production were transformed into prices while the inputs were left standing at their values, and the two aggregate identities Marx asserted could not both be made to hold. The natural next question is whether anyone has repaired the argument. The answer is that several repairs exist, that each of them works on its own terms, and that they are not interchangeable. The New Interpretation, the temporal single-system interpretation, and a macro-monetary family of readings each deliver an internally consistent version of Marx's theory, and they differ chiefly in what they preserve, what they redefine, and what they quietly let go.

That difference in what is preserved is the subject of this article, and it matters more than the technical machinery. One reconstruction keeps Marx's claim that aggregate profit equals aggregate surplus value and rebuilds the definition of labour power's value to do it. Another keeps the letter of the falling rate of profit argument and rebuilds the valuation of inputs to do it. A third argues that Marx never operated with a separate system of values at all, so that nothing needs transforming. Because each school preserves a different part of the inheritance, asking which reconstruction is correct turns out to be several questions wearing one coat: correct as a reading of Marx's text, correct as a piece of economic logic, and correct as an instrument for measuring actual economies are three separate judgements, and the schools rank differently under each. A further complication, and the observation this article is built around, is that the reconstruction claiming the strongest fidelity to Marx's text is not the one that most empirical researchers use. The one they use is the one whose central magnitude can be computed from published statistics. That gap between fidelity and usability is named below as the tractability divide, and it explains more about the shape of the literature than any doctrinal quarrel does.

What follows states each reconstruction in its proponents' own terms, sets out what it changes and what it preserves, gives the criticisms in the critics' own terms, and declines to declare a winner, because the dispute is a genuine one between serious economists and because the criteria of victory are themselves part of it. The transformation problem itself, the failed procedure that all three schools are answering, is treated in full in the series article on the [transformation problem explained](/2016/04/15/transformation-problem-explained/), and readers who have not met it should begin there. The second front of the same war, the dispute over the falling rate of profit and the Okishio theorem, has its own article on the [falling rate of profit debate](/2013/11/15/falling-rate-of-profit-debate/), and the external challenge mounted from Sraffa's system is covered in the article on [sraffa and marxist economics](/2016/05/01/sraffa-and-marxist-economics/). The whole field within which these reconstructions sit is mapped in the [marxist economics complete guide](/2016/04/01/marxist-economics-complete-guide/). This article's narrower task is the comparison: three ways of making Marx consistent, priced in the currency of what each one costs.

## The ancestry: Ricardo's invariable measure

The problem the reconstructions answer is older than Marx, and its ancestry explains its peculiar shape. Ricardo had built his theory of distribution on the labour embodied in commodities and then discovered, with characteristic honesty, that the rule failed at the margin that mattered most to him: when the rate of profit changes, the relative prices of commodities produced with different proportions of labour and fixed capital change too, even though the labour embodied in them has not altered, so that prices move for reasons unconnected with labour. Ricardo spent his final years hunting for an invariable measure of value, a commodity whose own value would be exempt from these movements and against which all others could be stably measured, and he died with the hunt unfinished, conceding in correspondence that no such commodity exists. Marx inherited the difficulty entire and transformed it: where Ricardo had sought a measuring rod outside the system, Marx made the divergence of price from value into the system's own mechanism, the means by which a uniform profit rate is imposed on unequal compositions of capital. The transformation chapters are Ricardo's problem answered by dialectical inversion, the defect promoted to a feature.

### What did Marx inherit from Ricardo's unsolved problem?

The coupling of distribution to relative price. Ricardo had shown that a change in the division between wages and profits alters relative prices independently of any change in production, and Marx's transformation is the attempt to derive that coupling from the labour theory rather than suffer it as the theory's refutation.

The ancestry matters to the reconstructions because each of them can be read as a final disposition of Ricardo's ghost. The simultaneist tradition accepted the coupling as Ricardo had feared it, prices governed jointly by technique and distribution, and drew the conclusion that labour values are at best an intermediate computation. The New Interpretation dissolves the ghost in the aggregate: at the level of the whole net product the coupling is absorbed into the conversion ratio, and relative prices, where the coupling lives, are ceded to other theories. The temporal school exorcises the ghost with time: the movements of price that troubled Ricardo are real events in a sequence, capital is committed before they occur, and a theory that follows the sequence need not find the coupling paradoxical. The ghost, for the macro-monetary reading, is already laid in Marx's monetary form: distribution and price are determined in one monetary process, and the search for a measure outside it was the classical school's founding mistake. A reader who sees the ancestry will also see why the dispute has the emotional register of a family quarrel. These are not three answers to a puzzle set by an examiner. They are three settlements of an estate that has been in probate since 1823, and the heirs' conviction that the settlement is finally theirs is the most traditional thing about each of them.

## The inheritance: an inconsistency and two equalities

Marx's transformation, as he sketched it in the ninth chapter of the third volume of Capital, begins from a table of industries each characterised by a capital advanced, divided into constant capital laid out on means of production and variable capital laid out on labour power, and a mass of surplus value produced. Competition then redistributes the total surplus value so that each capital earns the same rate of profit on its advance, which means that commodities sell at prices of production that diverge systematically from their values. Industries with a high proportion of constant capital sell above value; industries employing much living labour sell below it. The deviations cancel in aggregate, Marx claimed, and he asserted two equalities to mark the conservation: the sum of prices equals the sum of values, and the sum of profits equals the sum of surplus values. The underlying theory, the claim that labour alone creates new value and that profit is unpaid labour in monetary dress, is set out in the series article on the [labour theory of value explained](/2010/07/01/labour-theory-of-value-explained/).

The difficulty, identified within a generation and pressed relentlessly ever since, is that Marx transformed the values of outputs into prices while leaving the values of inputs, the constant and variable capital advanced at the start of the process, untransformed. The capitalist does not buy means of production at their values; he buys them at prices of production formed in the same competitive process. Once the inputs are repriced consistently, the arithmetic changes, and it was shown that the two aggregate equalities cannot in general both survive the repricing. A reconstruction of Marx therefore faces a forced choice among three broad strategies. It can redefine the terms so that the equalities that matter hold by the construction of the definitions. It can revalue the inputs on a different principle, arguing that Marx never intended the simultaneous repricing that generates the contradiction. Or it can deny that Marx ran two systems, a value system and a price system, between which a transformation is needed at all. The three schools of this article correspond, in order, to those three strategies.

### What exactly failed in the transformation procedure?

The failure was local and precise. Marx converted the value of each industry's output into a price of production but carried the cost side of the account, the constant and variable capital consumed, at unconverted values, although those inputs are themselves bought at prices of production in the same economy.

The locality of the failure is worth dwelling on, because it explains why the repairs could be so different in spirit. Nothing in the objection touched Marx's claim that living labour is the source of new value, nor his claim that competition equalises profit rates, nor the qualitative picture of surplus value redistributed among capitals. The objection touched the bookkeeping: a single system was being priced by two different standards on its two sides. Critics from Bortkiewicz onward treated this as fatal because the two aggregate equalities were the proof that the price system was the value system in disguise, and if the disguises cannot both fit, the disguise story fails. Defenders replied, in effect, that a bookkeeping inconsistency invites a bookkeeping repair, and the history of the reconstructions is the history of three different judgements about which ledger entries Marx would himself have corrected. One school corrects the definition of an entry. One corrects the timing of the entries. One denies that there were ever two ledgers.

It is also worth registering what kind of failure this was not. It was not an empirical failure: no measurement of any economy was involved, and no reconstruction that repairs it thereby gains empirical support. It was not a failure of the concept of exploitation, which can be stated, and by some writers is stated, without the transformation apparatus. And it was not a failure that Marx's contemporaries could have caught by closer reading, because the inconsistency emerges only when the procedure is written as a simultaneous system and solved, a step Marx did not take and the mathematics of his circle did not press on him. The reconstructions therefore operate on a text that is genuinely underdetermined at the crucial point: Marx left a procedure, two asserted equalities, and a body of surrounding argument, and each school completes the procedure differently while claiming the surrounding argument as its warrant.

### Why did Marx leave the procedure open to rival completions?

Because the text is an unfinished, posthumously edited draft. The third volume was put together by Engels from manuscripts composed mainly in the middle eighteen sixties, before the first volume had appeared, and Marx never returned to work the transformation sketch up to the standard of finish his published writing observes.

The manuscript state of the book is not a bibliographical curiosity; it is one of the dispute's premises. Marx knew the riddle he had set himself. In correspondence of the early eighteen sixties he announced to Engels that the old puzzle of the average rate of profit, which had wrecked Ricardo's school, was solved in principle by his distinction between value and price of production, and in an 1868 letter to Kugelmann he defended the necessity of the law of value as the regulator of the distribution of social labour with some heat. But the solution he announced existed as a draft chapter, not as a worked system, and Engels, preparing the volume for the press two decades later, said openly in his preface that this part of the manuscript was among the least finished, a sketch whose author had left the difficulties standing where he had encountered them. A sketch invites completion in a way a finished treatise does not. Every reconstruction in this article can present itself, with some justice, as finishing Marx's sentence rather than correcting it, and every critic of a reconstruction can present the same fact as evidence that the school has been writing its own sentences and signing Marx's name. Neither presentation is dishonest. They are the two faces of a genuinely unfinished text, and the reader who keeps the manuscript's condition in view will understand why the interpretive dispute has the structure it does: the less finished the text, the more the criteria of a good completion shift from transcription toward coherence, fruitfulness, and fit with the author's finished work elsewhere.

The critical edition of Marx's manuscripts, published in the complete historical-critical edition of the Marx and Engels writings, has lately given both sides fresh exhibits, since scholars can now compare Engels's printed text with Marx's drafts and with the excerpt notebooks in which he worried at the problem across the eighteen seventies. The comparisons have not closed the question. They have refined it, showing places where Engels smoothed a hesitation into a doctrine and places where the hesitation was Marx's own and survived editing untouched. For the reconstructions, the practical effect is that textual fidelity, the criterion on which the temporal school claims its advantage, is itself a moving target: the text to be faithful to is stratified, and fidelity to the draft of 1865, to the published first volume, and to the intentions Marx announced in letters are three different fidelities that no single completion can simultaneously maximise.

### Why did the two aggregate equalities carry so much weight?

The equalities were Marx's conservation laws, the guarantees that transforming values into prices redistributes a fixed total rather than creating or destroying value in the act of repricing. If total profit can exceed total surplus value, or fall short of it, the claim that profit is surplus value in another form loses its arithmetical spine.

Conservation claims do quiet but decisive work in any theory that distinguishes an underlying magnitude from its surface form. If prices are values transformed, then whatever competition redistributes among industries must sum to what was there before redistribution, in the same way that reshuffling a deck neither adds nor removes cards. Marx's two equalities are the reshuffling guarantees for his system, and critics fastened on them for exactly that reason: break the guarantees and the transformation becomes a loose analogy rather than a derivation. Each reconstruction can be classified by its treatment of the guarantees. The New Interpretation keeps the profit equality in full and keeps a version of the value equality for the net product, surrendering it for gross output. The temporal interpretation claims both equalities entire, for gross output as for net. Both are claimed by the macro-monetary reading as well, on the ground that the inputs were money magnitudes from the start and were never values awaiting conversion. When a reader meets a new contribution to this literature, the fastest diagnostic is to ask what happens to the two equalities: which are kept, which are dropped, and which are redefined until they hold.

Beneath the equalities sits a second stake, less often named. Marx's procedure assumed that the value of a commodity produced and the value of the same kind of commodity purchased as an input are two entries that must be made consistent, and the simultaneist tradition, the tradition running from Bortkiewicz through the Sraffian critics, resolved the consistency by valuing inputs and outputs at the same prices, those ruling when the outputs are sold. That single assumption, simultaneous valuation, is the hinge on which the whole dispute turns. The New Interpretation accepts the assumption and changes the definitions around it. The temporal interpretation rejects the assumption outright and calls its rivals simultaneist as a term of criticism. Sidestepping the assumption, the macro-monetary reading treats input prices as given data of the problem rather than as magnitudes to be solved for. A reader who grasps the hinge can reconstruct most of the literature's positions from first principles, which is why the assumption gets a section of its own in what follows.

## Marx's own numbers: the worked example and its readers

Every school in this dispute eventually repairs to the same few pages: the worked example of the ninth chapter of the third volume, where Marx takes five capitals of equal size and different compositions, computes their values, and derives prices of production by distributing the total surplus value at a uniform rate. The example is the only place where the transformation procedure is exhibited in numbers by its author, and it has therefore become the battlefield on which the question of Marx's own valuation practice is fought. In the example, the inputs of each capital, its constant and variable components, enter the calculation at their values, and the prices of production are computed for the outputs alone. The simultaneist tradition reads the example as Marx's procedure in miniature: inputs at values because, in the example's fiction, the transformation of inputs has not yet been performed, with the clear implication that performing it is the theorist's next task, the task Bortkiewicz performed with the results described above. The example, on this reading, is an unfinished calculation, and its unfinished state is precisely the inconsistency in embryo.

### Did Marx himself notice the difficulty with the inputs?

He noticed it in terms. In the same chapter he remarks that the cost price of a commodity may stand above or below the value of the means of production consumed in it, since those means are themselves bought at prices of production, and he flags the discrepancy as a possible source of error to be borne in mind in what follows.

The remark has been made to carry enormous weight by both sides, and it can bear more than partisans on either side usually admit. For the temporal school, the remark is evidence that Marx saw the input problem plainly and was untroubled by it, because on his procedure, they argue, the cost price is what it is, a sum actually laid out, and the divergence of that sum from the input's value is a fact of the process rather than a defect of the accounts: the example's use of values for inputs is then a simplifying assumption of the illustration, legitimate where nothing turns on it. For the simultaneists, the remark is evidence of the opposite: Marx saw the problem, deferred it, and never returned, and the deferral is the precise point at which the manuscript's unfinished state becomes a theoretical failure, since a corrected procedure was owed and not delivered. A third reading, available to the macro-monetary school, treats the remark as a sign that cost price in Marx is a monetary magnitude from the start, so that the divergence Marx notes is between two money expressions and no translation between systems is involved at all. The example and its footnote thus function in the literature the way a contested clause functions in constitutional argument: everyone agrees the words are the author's, and the disagreement is over the jurisprudence. What an honest survey can say is that Marx saw the joint in his construction, marked it, and left it, and that the three reconstructions are three ways of deciding what he would have done at the joint had he returned to it with the finished theory in hand. That is not a small thing to have established. It converts the dispute from an accusation of blindness, Marx refuted by his own oversight, into a question of completion, and completion questions, as this article has argued throughout, are settled by criteria, not by quotation alone.

## How to read a reconstruction: the anatomy of a completion

Before the schools multiply further, it is worth fixing what any reconstruction of this kind owes its text and its reader, since the standard fixed here will be applied, in the criticisms sections, to schools and critics alike. A completion of an unfinished argument has three debts. It owes the text its assertions: the claims the author actually staked, which for Marx include the two aggregate equalities, the derivation of profit from surplus value, and the tendency of the rate of profit, must be preserved or their surrender openly declared. It owes the reader its decisions: every point at which the text underdetermines the completion, the definition chosen, the valuation adopted, the magnitudes taken as given, must be visible as a decision, so that the reader can locate the joints and imagine the completion built the other way. And it owes the world its consequences: a completion that renders the theory measurably applicable, or shows why measurement must take a stated form, earns a kind of credit that a completion sealing the theory against all data cannot earn, whatever its textual virtues.

### What does a reconstruction owe the text it completes?

Fidelity to its assertions, candour about its own decisions, and consequences that reach the world. The schools of this article can be graded on the three debts separately, and much confused polemic comes from grading one school on a debt it never undertook to pay while excusing another from a debt it did.

Graded on the three debts, the field arranges itself instructively. The temporal school maximises the first debt: every assertion of Marx's, including the gross equality and the falling rate tendency, is preserved, and its candour about the second debt is high, since the valuation decision is announced in its name. Its exposure is on the third debt, where its magnitudes resist the statistical system. The New Interpretation inverts the profile: the third debt is paid in full, the second is paid with unusual candour by its best expositors, and the first is paid selectively, with the gross equality and the bundle definition of labour power's value declared, by its defenders, to be scaffolding rather than assertion: a grading the critics contest precisely at the word assertion. All three debts are claimed at once by the macro-monetary reading, both equalities, declared givens, aggregate measurability, and pays for the claim in the currency of the second debt's harder form, since its decisions about Marx's givens are decisions about a text's silences, the least auditable decisions a completion can make. The value-form refusal, finally, declines the first debt in its received form, holding that the assertions themselves have been misidentified by a century of substantialist reading. No school defaults. No school pays in full. The reader equipped with the three debts can now follow the detailed criticisms without needing this article's verdict, which is as it should be, since the article has none to give.

## The charge before the equations: the first generation of the dispute

The inconsistency charge is older than the mathematics that made it precise, and its first form shaped everything that followed. When Engels published the second volume of Capital in 1885, he used its preface to issue a public challenge: economists who believed the law of value incompatible with an equal general rate of profit were invited to solve the riddle in advance, on the law's own basis, before the third volume appeared with Marx's answer. The challenge was a deliberate piece of stage management, and it worked: attempts were published, by Wilhelm Lexis among others, and the most serious, by Conrad Schmidt at the end of the decade, proposed in effect that the aggregate identities hold for the economy as a whole even though individual prices diverge from values, a shape of answer the Marx circle received with respect. When the third volume appeared in 1894, the profession's verdict was therefore already prepared, and it fell to Eugen von Böhm-Bawerk, the Austrian school's most formidable controversialist, to deliver it in his 1896 essay on the close of Marx's system.

### What form did the charge of inconsistency take before the equation systems?

A charge of contradiction between volumes. Critics of the eighteen nineties argued that the first volume's doctrine, commodities exchanging in proportion to the labour embodied in them, was flatly contradicted by the third volume's prices of production, and that Marx had escaped the riddle only by abandoning his own law when it became inconvenient.

Böhm-Bawerk's essay deserves its fame, because it fixed the terms in which the problem would be discussed for a century. His Marx, he argued, had promised in the first volume that value governs exchange, and had then, in the third, calmly built a theory of price in which value does not govern the exchange of any individual commodity, taking refuge in aggregates that the individual case refutes. The contradiction was presented not as a slip but as a structural feature: the system, Böhm-Bawerk concluded, closes in self-contradiction, and its author's dialectical manner had concealed the join. The first great reply, Rudolf Hilferding's of 1904, is equally instructive in retrospect, because it did not meet the charge on the arithmetic at all. Hilferding shifted the ground to the social character of value, arguing that value in Marx is not a physical property of commodities but the form of a social relation, and that Böhm-Bawerk had criticised a theory of embodied substance that Marx never held. Whatever the merits of that defence, and they are still argued, its form anticipates the whole subsequent literature: faced with an arithmetical objection, the defence reinterpreted the theory's subject matter. The New Interpretation's monetary reading and the macro-monetary school's insistence on given money magnitudes are, in this respect, Hilferding's grandchildren, while the temporal school's insistence that the arithmetic itself acquits Marx is the refusal of Hilferding's retreat, an insistence on winning on the field where Böhm-Bawerk attacked.

Bortkiewicz's intervention a few years later then changed the charge's logical character, and the change is easy to miss because the conclusion sounded the same. A contradiction between volumes can be answered by an account of levels of abstraction, by the claim that the first volume's law holds at a level the third volume's prices complicate but do not repeal. An inconsistency within a solved system cannot be answered that way: if the procedure, applied consistently to its own inputs, cannot deliver its own asserted identities, the failure is internal and no doctrine of levels repairs it. That is why the simultaneist demonstration, though narrower than Böhm-Bawerk's polemic, proved harder to escape, and why the reconstructions of the nineteen eighties, when they came, were built to answer the narrower charge on its own ground. Each of them accepts that the identities must be made to hold within a single consistent construction. They differ, as this article has been insisting, on the construction.

## A century of simultaneity

The simultaneist tradition began as an act of respect. Ladislaus von Bortkiewicz, a statistician of genuine ability who took Marx's economics seriously when most of the academy did not, recast the transformation as a system of simultaneous equations in papers published in 1907 and showed that, with inputs repriced, one of the two equalities could be preserved by a choice of numeraire but not both together, except in special cases. His construction passed into English-language Marxism through Paul Sweezy's presentation in The Theory of Capitalist Development in 1942, which set the template for decades: values computed from technical conditions, prices computed from values by a simultaneous procedure, and the discrepancy between the equalities noted as a defect to be acknowledged. Postwar writers, Winternitz and Seton among them, generalised the equation systems without questioning the simultaneity that structured them. The assumption felt like rigour itself: of course the input and output prices in a single solved system must be the same prices.

The authority of that feeling was reinforced from outside the Marxist camp. Paul Samuelson's interventions around 1970 treated the transformation as an exercise in which one writes down a value table, erases it, and writes down a price table, a jibe whose force depended on the dual-system picture in which values are computed first and separately. Ian Steedman's Marx after Sraffa, in 1977, then pressed the sharpest version of the external critique: given the physical data of production and the real wage, prices and the profit rate can be computed directly, and value magnitudes are a redundant detour, derivable from prices rather than explanatory of them. Steedman's challenge belonged to the Sraffian front and is treated in the article on that subject, but its effect inside the transformation debate was to raise the stakes of consistency. If Marx's system, repaired in the simultaneist manner, delivered values that explained nothing, then the defence of Marx required either a different repair or a different account of what the value magnitudes are for. The three reconstructions of this article are, in part, three answers to that pressure.

### Why must one of the equalities be surrendered inside a simultaneous system?

Because the system has more claims than freedoms. Once inputs are repriced simultaneously, the equations determine prices and the profit rate from technology and the wage, and the two aggregate equalities become two separate conditions on a solution that has no remaining degree of freedom with which to satisfy both, except by special assumption.

Bortkiewicz's construction made the point concretely by dividing the economy into three departments, producing means of production, wage goods, and luxuries, with gold, the money commodity, located in the third. Repricing all inputs at prices of production and requiring the system to reproduce itself, he showed that the equality between total price and total value could be secured by the choice of a monetary standard, in effect by the valuation of the gold sector, while the equality between total profit and total surplus value then failed in general, or the reverse arrangement could be made with the failure transferred: the two conditions together overdetermined the system. Later generalisations, by Winternitz, by May, and by Seton in the nineteen fifties, put the result beyond reasonable doubt for the simultaneous frame: within that frame, Marx's two invariance claims are jointly available only under special conditions, such as an average organic composition in the money-producing sector. The simultaneist tradition absorbed this result as a correction of Marx, sometimes regretful, sometimes triumphant, and built its subsequent work, including Steedman's redundancy argument, on the corrected system. It is important to the fairness of this article to record that many simultaneists were Marx's defenders working under a self-denying ordinance: they accepted the correction as the price of keeping the theory's defensible core, the correspondence between exploitation and profit, available for the theorem that the next sections discuss. The reconstructions can be understood as refusals of that price, each declining a different item of the correction: the temporal school declines the simultaneity, the New Interpretation declines the definition that made the correction bite, and the macro-monetary school declines the premise that inputs entered the theory as values at all.

### Why did simultaneous valuation become the default assumption?

Simultaneity arrived with the equation systems. Once Bortkiewicz wrote the transformation as simultaneous equations, the price of an input and the price of the same commodity as output had to be one unknown with one value, and the convention hardened from a modelling convenience into a test that any reading of Marx was expected to pass.

The hardening deserves a moment's sympathy before it is criticised, because the assumption was not foolish. In a stationary economy, where the same commodities are produced period after period with unchanged technique, the price at which a machine or a bale of cotton is bought and the price at which it is sold do coincide in the long run, and simultaneous valuation is a fair picture of that world. The simultaneist tradition also prized a genuine virtue: a construction in which every magnitude is determined within the system, with nothing smuggled in as a gift from outside the model. Against that background the temporal proposal, that inputs should be carried at prices formed in an earlier period, can look like an evasion, a refusal to solve the system. The temporalists' reply, developed at length below, is that capitalism is not the stationary world of the assumption, that prices change between purchase and sale as a matter of course, and that a rigour purchased by assuming away price change is rigour applied to a different economy than the one Marx analysed. The New Interpretation's reply is different again: it keeps simultaneity and instead denies that the system's value magnitudes were ever the embodied labour totals of the dual-system picture. Both replies concede the historical point, that simultaneity ruled unchallenged for most of a century, while disputing whether the rule was a discovery about Marx or an artefact of the mathematics that happened to be fashionable when the debate was formalised.

There is a further, quieter reason for the convention's longevity. The simultaneist construction made the transformation problem teachable. A lecturer could write the value system on one side of a board and the price system on the other, derive the discrepancy, and present the whole matter as a solved problem with a known defect, all inside an hour. The reconstructions resist that treatment. The New Interpretation requires students to absorb a definition and follow its consequences through the national accounts. The temporal interpretation requires them to track magnitudes through time, period by period, which is slower and resists the single solved tableau. Working through the macro-monetary construction requires a re-reading of Capital with attention to what Marx takes as given at each step. Pedagogy is not a criterion of truth, but it is a criterion of survival, and part of the tractability divide examined later in this article is pedagogical as well as statistical: the simultaneist frame survived partly because it was the version that could be examined.

## The New Interpretation: labour time given a money measure

The New Interpretation was developed independently, at the turn of the nineteen eighties, by Gérard Duménil in France and Duncan Foley in the United States, with Alain Lipietz arriving at a closely related construction by another route. Its founding insight is that the dual-system picture misreads the kind of theory Marx wrote. In the simultaneist tradition, value is a system of embodied labour coefficients computed from technology, and money prices are a second system derived from the first. The New Interpretation replies that Marx's value was monetary from the outset: value is labour time expressed in money, and the bridge between the two is a single economy-wide ratio. That ratio is the interpretation's engine, and everything else in the reconstruction follows from defining it and from one further definition, of labour power's value, that earlier readers had taken over unexamined from the embodied labour picture.

The first definition fixes the monetary expression of labour time as the ratio of aggregate money value added to aggregate living labour performed in the same period. Value added in money terms is a printed magnitude of the national accounts; total hours of living labour is a printed magnitude of labour statistics; their ratio states how much money value an hour of labour expresses. The second definition then does the reconstruction's real work. In the inherited reading, labour power's value is the labour embodied in the bundle of goods that workers consume, a magnitude computed from the technology of the wage-goods industries. In the New Interpretation, that value is the labour time represented by the money wage: the wage, divided by the monetary expression, converts the worker's pay into the hours of social labour it commands. Necessary labour is then the labour time corresponding to the wage share of value added, and surplus value is the remainder of living labour, which converts back into money as aggregate profit. The two moves together restore the profit equality exactly, and restore a form of the value equality for the net product, at prices that are the economy's actual prices throughout, with simultaneity retained and no separate value tableau anywhere in the construction.

### What is the monetary expression of labour time?

The monetary expression of labour time is the ratio that links the total labour time performed in an economy to the total money value added over the same period. The New Interpretation uses it to convert the money wage into labour time, defining labour power's value through the wage actually paid.

To see the ratio at work, consider what it replaces. In the embodied labour picture, converting between the labour world and the money world required the whole transformation procedure, industry by industry, with its inconsistency waiting at the end. In the New Interpretation the conversion happens once, at the economy's aggregate level, through a magnitude that statisticians in effect publish. If an economy's money value added over a year stands in a ratio of, in words, so many currency units per hour of living labour, then any money magnitude in the system can be restated in labour time by division, and any labour magnitude can be restated in money by multiplication, and the restatement is uniform across industries. The uniformity is the point. Marx's transformation had prices diverging from values industry by industry while the aggregates matched; the New Interpretation keeps the divergence, prices of production still depart from embodied labour proportions commodity by commodity, and relocates the matching to the aggregate conversion, where it holds exactly. Whether that relocation is a legitimate completion of Marx's argument or a change of subject is the dispute taken up in the criticisms below. What is not in dispute is the construction's economy: one ratio does the work that a failed algorithm could not.

The ratio also clarifies what the interpretation takes value to be. Value, on this reading, is not a substance hidden inside commodities and measured in hours by an accountant with the production data; it is the social form that labour takes in a monetary economy, in which an hour of labour counts, in the market's own bookkeeping, as a determinate sum of money. Foley has presented the reading as a recovery of Marx's monetary theory of value from the embodied labour reconstruction imposed on it by the equation tradition, and has pointed to passages in which Marx treats the value of labour power as determined by the wage bargain and the working day rather than by a physical subsistence basket. Critics dispute those passages, as critics dispute every passage in this literature, but the interpretive claim is at least pointed: the New Interpretation presents itself not as an invention but as a removal, the stripping away of a dual-system scaffolding that Bortkiewicz built and Marx never did.

### What changes when the value of labour power is defined through the money wage?

Everything downstream of the wage changes its meaning. Necessary labour becomes the share of the working day that corresponds to the wage share of value added, so the rate of exploitation is fixed by the distribution of the net product between wages and profits rather than by the technology of the wage-goods sector alone.

The older definition tied exploitation to a physical magnitude. Given the bundle of goods workers consume and the labour required to produce that bundle, necessary labour was a technical datum, and surplus labour was the excess of the working day over it. The definition had a certain austere appeal: exploitation appeared as a fact about production, prior to markets and money. Its cost, within the transformation debate, was that repricing the wage goods broke the aggregate equalities. The new definition relocates exploitation from the technology of a sector to the division of the net product. If wages take a larger share of value added, labour power's value rises in labour time terms by exactly that amount, and surplus value falls correspondingly; the rate of exploitation becomes, in labour time dress, the ratio of profits to wages in the net product. Defenders call this a clarification, since it makes the theory of exploitation a theory of distributional conflict, which is how the argument functions in Marx's own discussions of the working day and the wage struggle. Critics call it a dissolution, since a magnitude that moves one for one with the wage share can no longer explain the wage share, and the theory's claim to ground distribution in production drains away. Both descriptions are accurate about the mechanics. They differ about what the theory was for.

A subtler consequence follows for the treatment of unproductive labour and for empirical work, and it helps explain the interpretation's spread. Because the construction runs on value added, wages, profits, and hours, all of which statistical offices publish, the theory of exploitation becomes measurable without any reconstruction of embodied labour coefficients from input-output tables. A researcher can state the rate of exploitation for an economy in a given year, track it across decades, and compare it across countries, using a handful of printed aggregates and stated conventions about which hours count as productive. The older definition promised the same measurements but demanded data that exist only in coarse and infrequent form. The tractability divide, examined in its own section below, opens at exactly this point: one definition of labour power's value plugs into the statistical system of modern economies, and the other does not.

### How does the New Interpretation treat labour that produces no value?

By assigning it to the redistribution side of the accounts. Labour employed in circulation, in much of finance, in advertising and in the state's apparatus is, on the classical distinction this school inherits, unproductive: it creates no new value, and its wages are paid out of the surplus value created elsewhere, so the ratio and the rate of exploitation are computed over the productive sectors and the rest of the economy appears as a set of claims on their product.

The treatment matters more in measurement than in doctrine, and it is where the interpretation meets its hardest practical choices. The boundary between productive and unproductive activity does not coincide with any line in the published accounts, and every empirical application of the interpretation must draw it by argument: transport and storage in, pure circulation out; the labour of supervision divided by function; the vast territory of services apportioned case by case. Different drawings yield different totals of living labour, different monetary expressions, and different rates of exploitation for the same economy in the same year, which is why the measuring section below treats the boundary conventions as co-authors of every published series. The temporal and macro-monetary schools face the same boundary in their own idioms, since no reconstruction escapes the need to say which hours count. The point for the comparison is that the New Interpretation, precisely because it is the school that measures, is the school in which the boundary decisions are most visible and most consequential, one more instance of the general law this article keeps finding: the virtues of a framework and its exposures are the same property viewed from two sides, and computability, the New Interpretation's great virtue, exposes it to a hundred consequential judgements that a less applicable framework never has to make in public.

### Which parts of Marx's account does the New Interpretation decline to reproduce?

The surrender is specific. The interpretation does not preserve the gross equality of total price with total value, only the corresponding identity for the net product, and it abandons the determination of labour power's value by the labour embodied in a worker's consumption bundle.

Gross output includes the value of the means of production consumed, transferred to the product, and it is on that transferred component that the surrendered equality fails. The New Interpretation's defenders have generally treated the concession as minor: the equalities that carry the theory's claims, they argue, are the ones concerning newly created value and its division, since constant capital transfers value it does not create, and a bookkeeping identity over transferred magnitudes was never load-bearing. The critics' rejoinder is that Marx asserted the gross equality in the same breath as the net ones, and that a reconstruction which keeps the assertions it likes and drops the ones it cannot fit is editing Marx under the flag of interpreting him. That rejoinder generalises into the deepest question of this literature, taken up near the end of this article: what separates interpreting a text from defending it by revision. For the New Interpretation the honest statement is that its Marx is a Marx whose theory is about the net product, money, and distribution, and whose gross-output arithmetic is allowed to lapse. Readers who find that Marx recognisable, and many do, will find the reconstruction compelling. Readers who find the lapse fatal will not, and no further argument about equations will move either party, because the disagreement is about which sentences of Capital are the theory and which are scaffolding.

The interpretation also leaves the determination of individual prices of production where it found it. It does not offer a new theory of relative prices, and its defenders are untroubled by this, since on their reading the theory of value was never a device for computing individual prices in the first place; it is an account of what profit is and of how the total of profit relates to the total of unpaid labour. That modesty is a strength in debate and a limitation in scope, and it marks the boundary between the New Interpretation and the temporal school, which claims to restore Marx's system entire, prices, profit rate dynamics, and all.

## Two founders, one ratio: how the New Interpretation was built

The independent invention of the same reconstruction in Paris and in the United States, within a couple of years, is itself evidence about the state of the problem around 1980. The simultaneist literature had reached a settled impasse: the inconsistency was universally conceded, the Sraffian redundancy charge was in the air, and the defensive options within the old frame, accept the defect and plead the theory's other virtues, had begun to look like surrender. A reconstruction that changed the definition of labour power's value dissolved the impasse at a stroke, and it is not surprising that more than one economist, reading Marx's monetary chapters against the dual-system tradition, found the move at the same moment. Duménil's version appeared in his 1980 book on the passage from values to prices of production, written within a French debate still marked by the Althusserian inheritance and by a strong native tradition of monetary macroeconomics. Foley's appeared in a 1982 journal article on the value of money and of labour power, and was developed in his subsequent work on the circuit of capital, where the starting point is not a tableau of industries but the movement of a sum of money through purchase, production, and sale. The routes differed in idiom and in emphasis, Duménil's algebraic and French, Foley's circuit-based and American, and the destination was the same: labour power's value through the wage, a monetary expression to convert, the profit equality restored.

### Why did the same reconstruction appear independently on two continents?

Because the obstacle it removes was visible from both places at once. By the end of the nineteen seventies the dual-system construction had been so thoroughly explored that its failure point, the untransformed value of labour power on the cost side, stood out as the joint where a redefinition could relieve the whole structure, and readers in Paris and Massachusetts reached the joint in the same years.

The near simultaneity had a consequence for the school's later development: the New Interpretation never had a single authorised text, and its versions differ in details that matter at the margins. Lipietz's variant, published in the same years, works with a closely related conversion but states the conservation claims in a form that ties them to the macroeconomic closure of the model. Moseley's early sympathetic critique of the school, which appeared under the label of the new solution, already noted divergences among the founders on the treatment of fixed capital and of the gross equality, divergences the later literature has neither needed nor managed to iron out. The label itself wandered before it settled: new solution and new interpretation were both used for the Duménil and Foley construction in the nineteen eighties, and the second name's victory is a small lesson in the sociology this article keeps encountering, since the winning name was the one attached to the version that empirical researchers adopted. Names in this literature are claims. Dual system, simultaneist, temporal, single system, macro-monetary: every term in the dispute's vocabulary was coined by a participant to make a position sound like a finding, and the reader who notices the coinages will read the polemics at the right discount.

Foley's own later presentations of the interpretation are worth a student's attention for their candour about its method. In his survey of the labour theory of value's development at the end of the nineteen nineties, he presented the interpretation frankly as a reading, one that takes Marx's theory to be a monetary theory of exploitation and accepts the consequences, including the consequence that the theory does not determine individual prices and was, on his account, never meant to. That candour distinguishes the school's best expositions from its slogans. It also locates the school's genuine vulnerability with precision: a reading that announces itself as a reading invites the question whether the text can bear it, and the textual fight over the value of labour power, bundle or wage, is where the New Interpretation must win or lose on grounds its equations cannot supply.

## The wage at the centre: what determines the value of labour power

No magnitude in the dispute carries more weight per word than labour power's value, since it is the hinge between the theory of production and the theory of distribution, and the schools' quarrel over its definition deserves a dossier of its own. Marx's text gives the concept a distinctive double character. On one side, the value of labour power is determined like that of any commodity, by the labour time required for its production, which means, for the worker, the labour embodied in the means of subsistence that maintain and reproduce the worker and the worker's family. On the other side, Marx insists that this value contains a historical and moral element: the subsistence that counts as necessary is set by the habits and expectations of a given country and epoch, by the outcomes of past struggles, so that the magnitude is physical in its floor and social in its level. The nineteenth chapter's analysis of the wage form adds the final twist, that the wage presents labour power's value in a disguised shape, as the price of labour itself, a form that makes the division of the day into paid and unpaid portions invisible on the surface of the economy.

### Where, in Marx's text, is the value of labour power actually determined?

In two places that the schools pry apart. The definition chapters anchor it in the labour embodied in the worker's means of subsistence, historically and morally specified; the chapters on wages, the working day, and accumulation treat the actual wage as the outcome of bargaining and struggle within limits that the definition supplies, and the reconstructions divide over which treatment is the theory and which the application.

The New Interpretation's reading fastens on the second treatment and generalises it: since the wage actually paid is the magnitude that enters every real circuit, and since Marx's own aggregate arguments run on the wage bill and the working day, labour power's value is most faithfully rendered as the labour time the wage represents, with the bundle demoted to a determinant of the wage rather than the definition of the value. The temporal school and the simultaneist tradition fasten on the first treatment: the definition is the definition, they hold, and a reconstruction that replaces it has not interpreted the concept but changed the subject, from a theory in which distribution is constrained by production to one in which distribution is whatever the shares say it is. The synthesis its name promises is what the macro-monetary reading attempts: variable capital is the money wage actually advanced, a given of the circuit, and the labour time it represents is recovered through the monetary expression, so that the bundle operates where Marx placed it, behind the wage as its determinant, while the accounts run on the money sum. What the dossier shows, and what a careful student should carry away, is that the textual evidence is genuinely divided because Marx's concept is genuinely double, built to connect a physical reproduction process with a social bargain, and that each school has resolved the doubleness by amputating one of its terms and calling the amputation fidelity. Recognising the doubleness does not end the argument. It explains why the argument cannot be ended by the text, and why the schools' real contest, once again, is over which of the concept's two faces the theory can less afford to lose.

## The circuit beneath the ratio: Foley's framing

Foley's version of the New Interpretation rests on a framing that deserves separate statement, because it explains the interpretation's characteristic calm about questions that agitate the other schools. His starting point is the circuit of capital, the movement Marx traces from money advanced, through the purchase of labour power and means of production and the production process itself, to the sale of the product and the return of a larger sum of money. In the circuit, everything the capitalist handles is money or is bought and sold for money, and the theory's problem is to understand the increment: where, in a movement that begins and ends in money, the expansion of value occurs and what limits it. Read from the circuit, the labour theory of value is the theory of that increment: living labour is the one input whose use adds more value, expressed in money, than its own purchase price commands, and the monetary expression is simply the rate at which the circuit's money magnitudes and the labour performed within it are exchanged in the aggregate. The transformation problem, in this framing, largely evaporates as a separate puzzle, because there never was a non-monetary tableau to be transformed: there was a circuit, running in money from its first step, and a question about the source of its increment, which the interpretation answers.

The framing has a notable consequence for the theory's scope, which Foley's presentations accept openly. A circuit theory of the increment is not a theory of the price of any particular commodity, and it does not compete with accounts of relative price formation, whether classical, Sraffian, or marginalist; it operates at the level of the aggregate and of the class relation, and its defenders hold that this was always Marx's level in the transformation chapters, whatever the dual-system tradition made of them. The critics' corresponding objection is that a Marx relieved of the obligation to explain relative prices has been relieved of much of what made his economics a rival to the orthodoxy of his day and of ours, and that the circuit framing purchases its calm by declining the contest. Between the calm and the contest the reader must choose with open eyes, and the choice will be influenced, as this article's refrain predicts, by what the reader thinks the theory is for. What can be said neutrally is that the circuit framing is the most philosophically developed form of the New Interpretation, the form in which its redefinition of labour power's value appears not as a patch on a broken algorithm but as the consequence of a rereading of Marx's whole procedure, and that serious engagement with the school means engagement with this form rather than with the ratio alone.

## The temporal single-system interpretation: sequence against simultaneity

The temporal single-system interpretation, known by its initials as the TSSI, was built by Kliman, Freeman, and their associates from the late nineteen eighties, with Kliman and Ted McGlone's papers of that period opening the argument and Freeman and Guglielmo Carchedi's 1996 collection Marx and Non-equilibrium Economics giving it a programmatic home. Its founding claim is diagnostic: the transformation inconsistency is not in Marx but in the simultaneist assumption imposed on him. If inputs and outputs are valued at the same prices, Marx's conclusions fail; but nothing in Marx, the temporalists argue, requires that valuation, and much in his text forbids it. Production takes time. The capitalist advances money for inputs at the prices ruling when he buys them, production proceeds, and the outputs are sold later, at the prices then ruling. Value the inputs at the prices actually paid, as historical fact, and value the outputs at the prices at which they sell, and the contradiction dissolves: both aggregate equalities hold, and Marx's account of the falling rate of profit, pronounced incoherent under simultaneity, becomes coherent again. The interpretation's second adjective marks its other claim, that there is a single system here, not two. Values and prices are not separate tableaus computed independently and then compared; the value of output is built from the prices actually paid for inputs, converted into labour time, plus the living labour added in production, and price is value expressed in money through the monetary expression, which the temporalists employ in a period by period form.

The two-period sequence is the whole argument in miniature, and the brief for this article rightly insists that it be carried in prose. Suppose a spinner buys cotton and spindle wear at the prices of the first period, paying a sum that represents, at that period's monetary expression, a determinate quantity of labour time. Production proceeds, and the spinners add living labour to the materials. By the time the yarn is sold, technical change elsewhere in the economy has cheapened yarn, and the price ruling in the second period is lower than the first period's price would have suggested. Under simultaneous valuation, the inputs are revalued at the second period's prices before the accounts are drawn: the cotton counts at its new, lower replacement cost, the capital advanced shrinks retrospectively, and the profit rate is computed against a capital that no capitalist ever actually advanced. Under temporal valuation, the cotton counts at what was paid for it, the advance stands as the historical sum it was, and the profit is the difference between the second period's revenue and the first period's outlay, a smaller difference, possibly a loss, and one that tracks the experience of actual firms, whose balance sheets are full of inputs bought dear and sold into cheaper markets. Multiply the sequence across an economy undergoing continuous technical change, and the two conventions deliver systematically different profit rates from identical physical events. The temporalists' claim is that Marx's dynamics, above all the tendency of the rate of profit to fall as productivity rises, describe the temporally computed rate, and are contradicted only when the rate is computed in the simultaneist manner that rewrites history at replacement cost.

### What does temporal valuation mean when a production period is followed step by step?

It means that magnitudes are inherited, not solved. The value transferred from inputs is fixed by what was paid for them when they were bought, converted at the monetary expression of that time, and the value of the output is that transferred value plus the living labour of the period, whatever prices have done meanwhile.

Follow the inheritance one more step and its character sharpens. In a simultaneous system, the economy's magnitudes are determined together, in a single act of solution, and the past enters only as the technical coefficients of the present. In the temporal construction, the system has a memory: this period's values depend on last period's prices, which depended on the period before, and the chain runs back through the actual history of the economy rather than being reset at each solution. Freeman has emphasised that this makes the framework a disequilibrium framework in a strong sense, since equilibrium, in which purchase and sale prices coincide, becomes a special and unlikely case rather than the assumed norm. The temporalists regard the memory as realism. Their critics regard it as a solvent, since a magnitude that depends on the whole path of past prices can no longer be computed from the data of the present, and a theory whose magnitudes are path-dependent in this way forfeits the clean determinacy that made the simultaneist constructions attractive to economists trained on equation systems. Both reactions are intelligible, and the choice between them is, once again, a choice about what a theory of value is supposed to deliver: a solvable portrait of an economy at a moment, or an account of an economy as a process unfolding in time.

The inheritance also governs the treatment of fixed capital and of devaluation, where the temporal reading claims some of its most concrete support. When a new machine cheapens a product, the owners of older machines suffer what Marx called moral depreciation: their capital loses value because the social valuation of its output has fallen. In a simultaneous construction, that loss is instantaneous and costless to record, since all magnitudes are revalued together at the new prices. In the temporal construction, the loss is a real event in a sequence, suffered by identifiable capitals whose advances were made at superseded prices, and the theory gains a native account of crises of devaluation, of capital destroyed not by physical wear but by the movement of prices against sunk advances. Whether Marx's remarks on moral depreciation amount to an endorsement of the whole temporal apparatus is contested, as every textual claim in this field is contested, but the fit between the apparatus and the remarks is one of the interpretation's genuine exhibits.

### How does moral depreciation behave under the two valuation conventions?

Under simultaneous valuation it vanishes as an event. When a cheaper technique appears, every magnitude is repriced together, old capital is written down instantly and impersonally, and no one is recorded as losing anything. Under temporal valuation the write-down falls on advances actually made, at dates that matter, and devaluation becomes a mechanism of crisis rather than a bookkeeping adjustment.

The contrast generalises into the deepest temperamental difference between the schools. The simultaneist world is a world photographed: at each moment a complete and consistent set of magnitudes, with the past present only as the coefficients of the photograph. The temporal world is a world filmed: magnitudes arrive with dates attached, commitments made under vanished prices persist into changed ones, and the economy's present is encumbered by its history in ways that can hurt. Marx's own writing, with its circuits, its turnover times, its capitals overtaken by cheaper methods mid-life, reads naturally as film, and the temporal school makes much of that naturalness. The simultaneist reply is equally characteristic: a theory that can only be stated as a film cannot be solved, only narrated, and economics is in the solving business. Neither temperament is a refutation of the other. But a reader choosing between the reconstructions is choosing, among other things, between these two pictures of what an economy is like and what a theory owes it, and the choice will be made on grounds that the equations state but cannot settle.

### How does the temporal reading restore the falling rate of profit argument?

By removing the premise of the theorem that killed it. The Okishio theorem, which shows that viable technical change raises the general rate of profit, is proved under simultaneous valuation with a constant real wage; value inputs temporally, at prices actually paid, and technical change that cheapens outputs can lower the rate computed on the capital actually advanced.

The mechanics were visible in the spinner example. A cost-reducing innovation lowers the prices of outputs. Under simultaneity, the inputs are instantly repriced downward too, costs fall with revenues, and the profit rate on the revalued capital rises, which is Okishio's result. Under temporal valuation, the capital advanced was laid out at the old prices and does not shrink when prices fall; revenue falls against an unshrinking advance, and the rate of profit can fall even though the innovation is viable, even though each adopting capitalist benefits at the moment of adoption. The temporalists present this as the recovery of Marx's own reasoning, in which rising productivity cheapens commodities and thereby squeezes the rate of profit on capitals committed before the cheapening. The dispute then moves to the linked article's territory, since the falling rate debate ranges far beyond valuation, over counteracting tendencies, the composition of capital, and the empirical record. What belongs here is the narrow point: the theorem that was widely taken to have refuted Marx's tendency law refutes it only inside a valuation convention, and the temporal school exists largely to make that conditional visible. Critics reply that a tendency law which appears or disappears with the accounting convention has been rescued at the price of its robustness, a charge examined among the criticisms below.

### Why do the temporalists insist on the phrase single system?

Because the name carries their central accusation. In the dual-system picture they oppose, values are computed from technology alone, prices are computed separately, and the two systems are then compared and found inconsistent; the temporalists hold that Marx operated with one system in which prices paid for inputs are among the determinants of the values of outputs.

The accusation cuts at the self-image of the older literature. Generations of commentators, hostile and friendly alike, presented Marx as the author of a value system standing behind a price system, and the transformation as the bridge between them, and then graded the bridge as failed engineering. If the temporalists are right about the text, the grading was a category error from the start: there was no bridge because there were not two banks. The value of a commodity, on this reading, is determined within the monetary process itself, by the labour added and by the values transferred from inputs whose money prices are data of the determination, so that value and price are two expressions, labour time and money, of a single set of magnitudes. That is also why the interpretation treats the monetary expression as an internal component rather than as the whole bridge: the ratio converts between the two expressions of one system, period by period, instead of spanning two systems at a stroke. The New Interpretation, it should be noted, also describes its own construction in single-system language at times, since it too refuses to compute an independent embodied labour tableau. The schools thus agree in rejecting the dual-system inheritance and disagree about what replaces it, a pattern, agreement on the enemy and divergence on the successor, that recurs throughout this literature and explains much of its polemical heat.

## The consensus the temporal school set out to break

The temporal school's most ambitious claim is not about Marx but about Marx scholarship. In Kliman's formulation, the inconsistency of Marx's value theory is a myth: not a finding wrung from the text by neutral methods, but an artefact manufactured by the simultaneist interpretations through which the text was read for a century, and then attributed to the text itself. On this account, the scholarly consensus that pronounced Marx inconsistent was a consensus about a construction of Bortkiewicz's, and the proper business of scholarship is to withdraw the attribution. The claim explains the school's polemical temperature. Its members take themselves to be correcting the record of a discipline, not merely proposing one more model, and their writing carries the energy, and occasionally the exasperation, of people who believe a century of commentary graded the wrong examination paper.

### What is the temporal school's claim about the scholarly consensus itself?

That the consensus confused an interpretation with its object. Generations of readers, the school argues, encountered Marx exclusively through simultaneist reconstructions, found those reconstructions inconsistent, and recorded the inconsistency as Marx's, so that the famous verdict describes the history of the reading rather than the content of the text.

The claim has force and it has limits, and both should be registered. Its force is that the mechanism it describes is real and documented: textbook presentations did standardly build the dual-system construction, derive the inconsistency, and present the derivation as a property of Capital, often without noting that the construction's key assumption had been supplied by the commentator. A verdict so produced is indeed about the reading in the first instance. Its limit is that the simultaneist century was not a monolith of hostile misreading. Within the simultaneist frame itself, serious defences of Marx were mounted, the most famous being Michio Morishima's fundamental Marxian theorem of the early nineteen seventies, which proved, inside a simultaneist model, that positive profit exists if and only if surplus value is positive: exploitation, in the precise sense, is the necessary and sufficient condition of profit. A tradition that produced that theorem was not simply an engine for refuting Marx, and the temporal portrait of a century of misreading flattens a landscape that contained defenders and critics on both sides of the simultaneity line. The measured conclusion is that the consensus claim is a powerful correction stated, as such claims usually are, one notch too strongly: the inconsistency verdict was interpretation-dependent, which the consensus rarely acknowledged, and the interpretations through which it was reached were neither arbitrary nor worthless, which the correction rarely acknowledges.

There is a methodological moral here that outlasts the dispute. In any literature where a canonical text is known mainly through formal reconstructions, the properties of the reconstructions migrate, in secondary discussion, into properties of the text. The migration is invisible while it happens, because the reconstruction is the only version most readers ever meet. The temporal school's lasting service, whatever becomes of its positive claims, is to have made the migration visible in this case and to have forced every subsequent writer on the transformation problem to declare a valuation convention and defend it as a choice. The New Interpretation performs the same service for definitions. A literature in which the load-bearing choices are declared is a healthier literature than one in which they are inherited as facts of nature, and on that criterion, unusually, all three schools can claim to have improved the field they divide.

The comparison can now be stated compactly, and the table below, the reconstructions compared, is this article's findable artifact: one row for each reconstruction, setting out what it changes, which equalities it preserves, what it gives up, the principal criticism it attracts, and whether empirical work uses it. The table is a map of a dispute, not a league table, and its last column records the tractability divide rather than any verdict on truth.

### What does each column of the comparison isolate?

Each column isolates a different criterion of judgement. The changes column shows where a school intervenes in Marx's procedure, the equalities column shows what it counts as the theory's non-negotiable claims, the surrender column prices the intervention, and the final column records use, which is a fact about researchers rather than about Marx.

Reading the table by criteria rather than by rows is the habit this article most wants to teach. A reader whose criterion is textual fidelity, whose question is which construction Marx himself would have recognised as his procedure continued, will weight the surrender column heavily and will find the temporal row attractive, since its surrenders are methodological rather than doctrinal. A reader whose criterion is operational, who wants magnitudes that can be measured against the statistics of actual economies, will weight the final column and find the New Interpretation's row attractive, since its central ratio drops out of published accounts. A reader whose criterion is systematic, who wants to know whether value magnitudes do explanatory work that prices alone cannot, will find every row wanting in a different way, and will gravitate to the criticisms column, where that reader's real debate lives. The schools persist because the criteria persist, and no row wins on all of them. That is the analytical content of the tractability divide: not that one side is careless about truth, but that the field's practitioners sort themselves by criterion, and the empirical branch of the field, which must publish numbers, sorts itself by computability almost regardless of its members' interpretive sympathies.

The table also exposes a shared feature that the polemics obscure. All three reconstructions are, in the strict sense, completions: each supplies a rule where Marx's text gives a procedure and an assertion but not a proof that the procedure delivers the assertion. None of the three is a mere transcription of Capital, and none of their proponents, pressed, claims otherwise; the claim is always that this completion is the one the text's logic and letter support. Recognising the shared structure lowers the temperature of the dispute without dissolving it, because completions can still be better and worse as readings, as logic, and as instruments, and the argument about which is better on each criterion is the literature's real content.

## The third family: single-system and macro-monetary readings

The third family is less a single construction than a shared refusal, the refusal to grant that Marx's inputs are values awaiting transformation. Its most developed statement is Fred Moseley's macro-monetary interpretation, elaborated from the early nineteen nineties and given book-length form in Money and Totality. On this reading, Marx's theory in Capital is a macro-monetary theory from the first page: the total capital of society, the total surplus value produced, and the general rate of profit are determined for the economy as a whole, in money terms, before the distribution of surplus value among individual capitals, industries, and claimants such as landlords and merchants is considered. The circuit of capital begins with a sum of money actually advanced, and the magnitudes of constant and variable capital that enter the analysis are those actual money sums, taken as given data of each period, not as labour values computed from technical coefficients and then converted. Since the inputs were never values in the dual-system sense, there is nothing to transform on the input side, and the transformation problem, as a problem about inputs, does not arise. What remains is the distribution problem, how a predetermined total of surplus value is parcelled out as industrial profit, commercial profit, interest, and rent, and that problem Marx's procedure handles without inconsistency. A related macro-monetary current, associated with Riccardo Bellofiore and others, reaches a similar destination from the side of Marx's monetary theory, stressing that value for Marx is constituted in the exchange against money rather than embodied beforehand in production.

### What does the macro-monetary reading take Marx's starting magnitudes to be?

Money actually advanced. On this reading the capitalist's outlay on means of production and labour power enters the theory as a sum of money given by the market at the moment of purchase, and the theory's task is to explain the increment of money that emerges, not to translate a prior system of labour values into prices.

The emphasis on givenness is the reading's signature and its provocation. Simultaneist critics prize endogenous determination: a theory should derive its magnitudes, not accept them as data. The macro-monetary reply is that every theory takes something as given, that Marx's circuit of capital plainly begins with money in hand, and that a reading which honours the text's actual order of presentation is not evading determination but locating it where Marx located it, in the prior period's production and in the labour process that the advanced money sets in motion. The total surplus value, on this account, is anything but given: it is determined by the total living labour performed and the hours the total wage bill represents, through a monetary expression of labour time that Moseley adopts in a form close to the New Interpretation's. What is given is the money form of the advances. The reading thereby splits the difference that divides the other two schools: like the New Interpretation it runs on aggregates and a conversion ratio, and like the temporal school it claims both aggregate equalities, since the total price of output and the total value of output are, in money terms, two statements of the same aggregate once the inputs are counted at their actual money cost on both sides.

### How does the macro-monetary reading handle the distribution of surplus value?

As a second, separate stage. The total surplus value is fixed first, in aggregate, by the labour performed and the wage bill's labour equivalent; the theory then follows that predetermined total as competition divides it among industrial capitals by size, and as it is further shared with merchants, lenders, and landlords.

The two-stage architecture is the reading's best textual exhibit, because the third volume of Capital really is organised that way: the transformation of surplus value into profit and of the rate of surplus value into the rate of profit occupies its opening parts, the division among industrial capitals follows, and the revenue forms, commercial profit, interest, and rent, are derived afterwards as claims on a total that precedes them. Moseley's argument is that this architecture is unintelligible on the dual-system reading, where nothing is fixed until everything is solved at once, and natural on his: the book's order of presentation is the theory's order of determination. Critics answer that an order of presentation is not an order of determination, that Marx expounds many subjects in an order he explicitly warns is not the order of their real connection, and that the macro-monetary reading mistakes the pedagogy of the manuscript for its logic. The exchange is characteristic of the third family's position in the dispute: its claims are architectonic, about the shape of the whole theory, where the other schools' claims are local, about a definition or a valuation rule, and architectonic claims are harder to test and easier to feel. Readers tend to be converted by them or not at all.

### Where do the macro-monetary and New Interpretation readings part company?

They part on the status of the inputs and on the reach of the equalities. The New Interpretation keeps a transformation of outputs and surrenders the gross equality; the macro-monetary reading denies that inputs were ever untransformed values and so claims the gross equality as well, while accepting a heavier burden of textual argument about what Marx took as given.

The parting is instructive because the two schools share so much machinery: aggregates, money, a conversion ratio, a redefined or relocated value of labour power. Two research programmes can share an engine and still be different programmes, because what each claims the engine proves differs. For the New Interpretation, the equalities that survive are the theory; the surrendered gross equality is arithmetic that Marx asserted but his argument does not need. For the macro-monetary reading, the survival of both equalities is itself evidence for the reading, a sign that the text's assertions were consistent all along once its monetary character is recognised. The temporal school watches this family quarrel with some detachment, since on the temporal view both schools still work with a simultaneity of the net product that the temporal construction rejects, valuing the period's magnitudes together rather than in sequence. The three-way structure of the dispute, each school holding a position the other two unite against on some axis and divide on others, is one reason the literature resists summary as a two-sided debate, and why this article's comparison table has three rows rather than the customary two.

## The debate beyond the Anglophone journals

The transformation problem was never only an Anglophone or a German affair, and two national traditions of work on it deserve record here, because they complicate the picture of a single simultaneist century succeeded by three reconstructions. In Japan, where Marxian economics held an academic centrality unmatched anywhere else in the postwar decades, the problem was worked over with great sophistication from the nineteen fifties, within and against the framework of Kozo Uno's reconstruction of Marxian theory into levels: a pure theory of capitalism, a theory of its stages, and the analysis of concrete economies. Uno's own treatment separated the theory of value from its applications in ways that made the transformation a question about the relation between levels rather than a defect within one, and his school, carried into English substantially by Thomas Sekine, sustained a parallel literature whose categories the Anglophone debate largely ignored, to its cost: several distinctions the reconstructions later fought over, between the theory's logical structure and its historical material, were commonplaces in Tokyo while they were novelties in London and New York. In Italy, the debate ran through the Sraffian ascendancy and the operaist refusal of it, with Claudio Napoleoni's attempt to rethink value through and against Sraffa marking a road on which the question became whether the labour theory could survive its own critique as a theory of the social relation rather than of price formation, a road that joins, at its end, the value-form territory surveyed below.

The moral of the geography is the same as the moral of the consensus section, one level wider. What presents itself, inside any one national literature, as the state of the question is partly the state of that literature's institutions: its journals, its chairs, its translation flows. The reconstructions this article compares are the products of the French and Anglophone institutions of the nineteen eighties and nineties, formed in debates with Bortkiewicz's heirs and Steedman's challenge, and their categories bear the marks of that formation. A reader who knows that other formations existed, asking differently shaped questions of the same text, is inoculated against the provincialism that mistakes the local agenda for the text's own. Marx's book has now supported a century of such agendas, in more languages than any living scholar commands, and the reconstructions earn their place in that history not by ending it but by having stated, with unusual clarity, what their moment's version of the problem was and what it cost to solve it.

## The correspondence theorem and its shadow

One episode in the simultaneist century deserves separate treatment, because it changed what the transformation dispute was about. In the early nineteen seventies, Michio Morishima proved, inside a rigorous simultaneist model of the von Neumann type, that positive profit is possible if and only if the rate of surplus value is positive: exploitation, defined in embodied labour terms, is the necessary and sufficient condition for profit. The result, known as the fundamental Marxian theorem, was a defence of Marx achieved entirely within the frame the temporal school would later indict, and it was widely received as the theory's defensible core extracted from its defective presentation: whatever the fate of the transformation procedure, the deep claim of Capital, that profit rests on unpaid labour, stood proved in modern dress. John Roemer then generalised the theorem in two directions that together cast a long shadow. He showed that a correspondence between profit and exploitation could be established on definitions of exploitation framed in terms of property relations rather than labour values, and commentators drew the moral that analogous theorems could be proved for any basic input: define steel value by the steel embodied in commodities, and positive profit corresponds to the exploitation of steel. If every input is exploitable in the formal sense, the singling out of labour, the parody concluded, is a moral preference dressed as a theorem.

### Does the correspondence between profit and exploitation single out labour?

The theorem alone does not. Formally parallel correspondences can be constructed for other basic commodities, which is why defenders of labour's special place rest it not on the theorem but on what labour is: the one input that is also the worker's life activity, inseparable from the person who sells it and reproduced outside the market's own circuit.

The shadow matters for the reconstructions because it repriced their task. After Morishima and Roemer, a defender of Marx had to say what the value magnitudes add beyond the correspondence, and the three schools can be read as three answers to that repricing. The New Interpretation answers that the magnitudes add a monetary theory of distribution: the point is not the bare correspondence but the account of the net product's division and its measurement. The temporal school answers that the magnitudes add dynamics: the correspondence is static, while the falling rate and the devaluation of capital are claims about movement that only the temporally determined magnitudes can state. From the macro-monetary side, the answer is that the magnitudes add the theory's architecture, the determination of the total before its distribution. Roemer's own trajectory, away from labour value toward a property-relations account of exploitation and class, marks the road not taken by any of the schools, and its existence disciplines their claims: a reader can accept the correspondence, accept the reconstructions' consistency, and still ask whether the value form of the argument is the one in which its content is best kept. The reconstructions do not answer that reader by existing. They answer by the explanatory work surveyed in the objection section below, and the reader who keeps Roemer's shadow in view will grade that work more strictly, which is all to the good.

## The reconstructions and the first volume

The dispute is usually staged in the third volume, where the problem lives, but each school also has a first volume to explain, and their handlings of it are diagnostic. The opening chapters of Capital present the analysis of the commodity, the development of the value form from the simple expression of value through to money, and the doctrine of fetishism, and they can be read in two registers that correspond to the schools' divisions. In the substantialist register, the opening establishes labour embodied as the substance of value, a magnitude that the rest of the work then tracks through its transformations, and the third volume's difficulties are the difficulties of that tracking. In the monetary register, the opening establishes that value has no existence apart from its form of appearance in exchange against money, that the analysis of the form is the theory's core, and that magnitudes of value are from the outset magnitudes in a monetary economy, so that nothing in the third volume needs to cross from a moneyless realm into money. The New Interpretation reads the first volume in the monetary register and claims its chapters on money and on wages as direct support: the wage, in Marx's presentation, is the price of labour power before it is anything else, and the value of labour power is determined in the analysis of the working day and the wage bargain rather than computed from a basket. Systematised, that register becomes the macro-monetary reading. The temporal school reads the first volume as a theory of magnitudes determined in production and realised in circulation, insists that its value is a real magnitude prior to price in the order of determination though not separate from price in a second system, and treats the value-form chapters as an account of the form that magnitude necessarily takes rather than as a dissolution of magnitude into form. The value-form school claims the first volume entire and regards the other schools' readings of it as relapses.

No verdict is offered here either, but an observation may be permitted about what the first volume does to the dispute's temperature. The third volume's text is a draft, and charity toward completions of it comes easily. The first volume is the book Marx published, revised, and stood behind, and a reconstruction whose reading of the draft requires a reading of the published volume that its opponents find strained pays a price in the dispute's internal economy that no consistency result can refund. This is why so much of the literature's real fighting happens over a handful of early chapters, over what the analysis of the form establishes and what the chapter on the buying and selling of labour power presupposes, and why a student who wants the dispute's centre of gravity should read the first volume's opening with the schools' spectacles alternately on and off. The exercise is the best single training the field offers in what interpretation, as distinct from both transcription and invention, actually is.

## The refusal of the problem: value-form readings

Standing outside the three reconstructions is a fourth position that declines their shared question. The value-form tradition, descending from Isaac Rubin's essays of the nineteen twenties through the West German new reading of Marx associated with Hans-Georg Backhaus and Helmut Reichelt, and stated in a rigorous contemporary form by Michael Heinrich, holds that the transformation problem arises from a misreading of what value is. On the substantialist picture the reconstructions inherit, value is a magnitude produced in production, embodied in commodities before they reach the market, which then has to be converted into money prices by some procedure, with the procedure's failures generating the problem. On the value-form picture, value is a social relation that exists only in exchange against money: labour becomes value-constituting labour in the act of sale, money is the necessary form of value rather than its measuring rod applied afterwards, and there is no pre-monetary stock of value awaiting transformation. The problem, on this account, is not solved but dissolved, and all three reconstructions are elaborate answers to a question generated by reading the first chapter of Capital through Ricardian spectacles.

### Why do value-form theorists say the transformation problem is wrongly posed?

Because it treats value as a thing-like magnitude existing before exchange. If value is constituted in the relation of commodities to money, they argue, then there are not two systems, values in hours and prices in money, standing in need of a bridge; there is one monetary economy whose categories the substantialist reading has doubled.

The refusal deserves a place in this article for two reasons. The first is completeness: a reader who meets the reconstructions without knowing that a significant tradition regards their common premise as the error will mistake the field's map, taking an internal dispute for the whole territory. The second is that the refusal casts a clarifying light backward over the three schools. Each reconstruction, whatever its differences, keeps faith with the idea that the relation between labour time and money totals is the theory's testable core, the place where Marx's claims either cohere or collapse. The value-form reading relocates the core to the analysis of form, to why labour's product takes the shape of a vendible thing with a price at all, and it treats the aggregate equalities as a Ricardian inheritance that Marx's mature theory had already outgrown. Whether that relocation is a deepening or an evasion is, predictably, disputed: the reconstructions' proponents reply that a theory of form which declines all quantitative commitment has insured itself against refutation at the price of content, and that Marx's own aggregate assertions show he did not regard the equalities as dispensable. The value-form writers answer that the assertions belong to the manuscript's unfinished surface. The dispute between the refusal and the reconstructions is thus the dispute within the reconstructions, about which sentences of Marx are the theory, conducted one level up, and it is unlikely to be settled by the means either side accepts.

For the working reader, the practical upshot is a fourth question to add to the equipment this article assembles: when a contribution to the transformation literature is opened, ask whether it accepts the problem as posed. Most do, and proceed to their reconstruction. The minority that refuse are not thereby refuted or vindicated, but they are playing a different game, and grading their work by the equalities it does not attempt is the mirror image of the error the temporal school charges against the simultaneist consensus.

## The example as an instrument: numerical models in this literature

A word about method is owed, because this literature argues through a distinctive instrument and the reader should know its powers and its limits. The instrument is the numerical example: a miniature economy, two or three sectors, a handful of periods, with every magnitude specified, through which a claim is exhibited in arithmetic. Bortkiewicz's three departments were such an example before they were a theorem. The temporal school's case rests substantially on examples run forward in time, in which simultaneist and temporal computations of the same physical story are set side by side and shown to diverge. The New Interpretation's expositions characteristically work through a one-period aggregate example in which the ratio is computed and the equalities verified. Examples in this literature do the work that experiments do in sciences that can run them: they are existence proofs and intuition pumps, demonstrations that a construction behaves as advertised on at least one fully specified case, and training grounds on which a reader's understanding is built by following the numbers.

### What can a numerical example in this literature prove, and what can it not?

It can prove possibility: that a construction is coherent, that an alleged necessity, the failure of an equality, the rise of a profit rate, does not hold in general, since a single coherent counter-case breaks a claim of necessity. It cannot prove actuality: that any real economy behaves as the example's economy does, or that Marx's own reasoning followed the example's path, either of which needs evidence the arithmetic cannot supply.

The asymmetry is the key to reading the literature's exchanges without being misled by their rhetoric. When a temporalist example shows the profit rate falling under temporal valuation while rising under simultaneous valuation of the same events, it refutes, decisively, the claim that viable technical change must raise the profit rate, and it refutes nothing else: not the claim that actual profit rates have risen, nor the claim that Marx valued as the simultaneists say. When a simultaneist example generates negative surplus value under joint production, it refutes the claim that the correspondence of profit and exploitation holds in every simultaneous construction, and it does not refute the correspondence as a tendency or as a theorem under other assumptions. Polemic in this field characteristically inflates an example's verdict by one category, from possibility to actuality or from one construction to the theory itself, and the reader who applies the discount mechanically, asking of every exhibited case which modal claim it supports, will find the literature's disagreements shrinking to their real, still considerable, size. The examples also explain the literature's pedagogical durability: a student can be brought to the heart of the transformation problem in an afternoon with three departments and a pencil, which is more than can be said for most comparably deep disputes in economics, and the schools' survival in teaching owes as much to the instrument as to the doctrines.

## A field guide to the polemical vocabulary

The dispute's vocabulary is an instrument panel on which every dial was fitted by a combatant, and a short guide to the coinages will protect the reader from their settings. Simultaneism is the temporal school's name for the assumption it rejects: it converts a modelling convention into an ism, a doctrine with adherents, where the convention's users had experienced it as simple rigour. Dual system is the same school's name for the picture of Marx it opposes, two tableaus and a bridge, and it carries the suggestion that the picture was a scholarly invention rather than a reading. Physicalism, in this vocabulary, names the deeper error the temporalists diagnose beneath simultaneity: the treatment of value as a property of physical quantities, determined by technical data independently of the monetary process in which commodities are actually bought and sold. Each term does argumentative work before any argument begins, since to accept the vocabulary is to accept that the convention was chosen, that the picture was constructed, and that the physical reading was a metaphysics. The other schools mint in their turn. The New Interpretation's monetary language presents its rivals as prisoners of a barter picture of value, and the macro-monetary school's givenness presents its rivals as manufacturers of unnecessary problems. None of this is dishonesty; it is the normal rhetoric of research programmes, and every mature literature has it. But a reader new to the field should know that the neutral terms in this article, reconstruction, convention, completion, were chosen precisely because they belong to none of the schools, and that any summary of the dispute written in one school's vocabulary has taken that school's side before its first argument.

### What does the temporal school mean by calling a claim interpretive?

It means the claim is about Marx rather than about the economy directly. The school's central contention, that Marx's value theory is not internally inconsistent, is offered as a finding about a text and its correct construction, to be tested against the manuscripts and the logic of the argument, and its proponents insist that simultaneist critics who refute rival economic models have not touched it until they engage the reading as a reading.

The insistence has a defensive use and a legitimate one, and the reader should learn to tell them apart. Legitimately, it enforces the distinction this article has maintained between the three questions a reconstruction must answer: fidelity, logic, and use. A criticism that shows the temporal construction to be a poor instrument for measuring the American economy does not show it to be a poor reading of Capital, and a refutation aimed at the wrong question is a refutation missed. Defensively, the same insistence can function as a moat: every objection is reclassified as addressed to a different claim than the one under attack, and the reading retires behind its interpretive status whenever its economic content is pressed. The simultaneist literature has its own moat of the same construction, the plea that its constructions are mere formalisations whose assumptions no one is obliged to defend as readings. The healthiest writing in the field, on every side, is the writing that crosses its own moat voluntarily, stating what would count against it. The reader's four questions, posed near the end of this article, are designed to demand exactly that crossing.

## The criticisms, in the critics' own terms

Neutrality in this literature does not mean vagueness about the objections; it means stating each objection as its author would wish it stated, at full strength, before any reply is allowed. The criticisms of the New Interpretation come chiefly from two directions. From the textual side, critics charge that the interpretation preserves Marx's conclusions by changing his concepts: labour power's value in Capital is anchored in the labour required to reproduce the worker, and a definition that makes it a share of value added, identical in movement to the wage share, has not interpreted that concept but replaced it, so that the equality of profit and surplus value is restored for a different theory than the one Marx wrote. From the logical side, critics charge that the monetary expression is defined rather than derived: the ratio is constructed from the two aggregates whose correspondence it is then said to establish, so the equalities hold by the manner of the construction, and a consistency secured by definition demonstrates nothing about the economy or about Marx.

The criticisms of the temporal interpretation are mirror images in an instructive way. Where the New Interpretation is accused of changing the concepts, the temporal school is accused of changing nothing but the accounting: its consistency, critics say, is secured by adopting a sequential valuation convention, and a convention can always be found under which a set of asserted identities holds, so the demonstration shows that a convention exists rather than that Marx's theory is sound. The second charge is that temporally determined values are explanatorily idle: since the value transferred from inputs is simply their purchase price converted, and the value of output is built up from prices and labour time in this way, every value magnitude can be computed only after the prices are known, and a magnitude that waits on prices cannot explain prices: the causation of the older theory, in which values were determined by production conditions independently and then explained the pattern of prices, has been quietly reversed. The third charge is textual again: other readers of the same chapters dispute that Marx valued inputs at historical prices, pointing to the worked examples in which input values enter as given magnitudes alongside outputs in a single table, and to Marx's insistence that the value of a commodity is governed by the labour time that reproduction takes under present conditions, which sounds, to simultaneist ears, like replacement-cost valuation written into the text.

### What is the circularity charge against the monetary expression?

The charge is that the ratio is manufactured from the identity it certifies. The monetary expression is computed as money value added divided by living labour, so announcing that money value added equals the ratio times living labour announces an arithmetic truth about a definition, not a discovery about the relation between labour and money.

Stated carefully, as its best critics state it, the charge is not that definitions are illegitimate. Every science defines its conversion factors, and a definition can organise a field fruitfully even though identities built on it are tautologies taken one by one. The charge is about what the New Interpretation claims for its identities. If the equality of aggregate profit and aggregate surplus value holds because surplus value is defined as the labour time corresponding to the profit share of value added, then the equality cannot also be offered as a confirmation that Marx's theory of exploitation is correct, since any division of the net product whatsoever would satisfy it by the same construction. The confirmation, critics say, has been spent in the definition. The defenders' reply has two layers. The first is that the monetary expression is not a free stipulation but an empirical magnitude: it is whatever it is in a given economy and year, it moves with productivity and prices, and the theory's content lies in the claim that this single ratio governs the conversion for every industry and every magnitude, which is a substantive restriction, not a tautology, since the world could have refused it by making the conversion incoherent across uses. The second layer is interpretive: the defenders hold that Marx's own procedure, read without dual-system spectacles, treats labour power's value through the wage in just this way, so that the definition is a recovery rather than an invention. Neither layer has persuaded the other side, and the exchange is a good specimen of the debate's general form: an objection about logic met by a reply about measurement and text, with the parties partly talking past each other because their criteria differ.

### What do critics mean when they call temporally determined values idle?

They mean that the magnitudes do no work. A value that is computed from the price actually paid, plus labour time performed, can be calculated only once the prices are known, so it cannot be what determines or explains those prices; the theory's explanatory arrow, critics say, has been reversed without announcement.

The idleness charge is the temporal school's most serious problem, because it targets the point of having a value theory at all. The older picture, whatever its inconsistencies, had a clear explanatory architecture: production conditions determine values, values regulate prices, and deviations of price from value are intelligible against that background. In the temporal construction, the value of this period's output depends on last period's prices, and last period's prices depended on the values of the period before, so the chain of explanation never grounds out in production conditions alone; it grounds out in history. Critics argue that a magnitude with this structure is a bookkeeping shadow of the price system, adding a labour time translation of facts already fully stated in money, and that the translation, however faithful to Marx's sentences, is theoretically unemployed. The temporalists' reply is that the charge mistakes the theory's purpose. On their reading, Marx's value theory is not a device for predicting prices from technology; it is an account of the source and limits of profit, of why the total of profit is bounded by unpaid labour, and of how the rate of profit moves as production unfolds in time. For those questions, they argue, the temporal magnitudes are anything but idle: the equality of profit and surplus value, which the construction preserves, is precisely the claim that the money total has a labour time bound, and the falling tendency of the temporally computed profit rate is a prediction the simultaneist construction cannot even state. The reply concedes the narrow point, that temporal values do not determine prices in the simultaneist manner, and contests the framing, that such determination was ever the theory's job. A reader following the exchange should notice that both sides are describing the same construction accurately and valuing its features oppositely, which is the signature of a dispute about the purpose of a theory rather than about its content.

### On what grounds do other readers dispute the temporal reading of the text?

On the evidence of Marx's own tables and of his remarks about reproduction. The worked examples carry input values as given magnitudes in the same calculation as outputs, and Marx repeatedly ties a commodity's value to the labour time its reproduction requires under present conditions, which critics read as simultaneous valuation in Marx's own hand.

The textual dispute deserves care because it is the ground on which the temporal school claims its greatest advantage, fidelity, and its critics claim the advantage is illusory. The temporalists marshal their own passages: Marx's treatment of moral depreciation, in which capital loses value when techniques change, presupposes that advances were made at superseded prices and are not frictionlessly repriced; his discussions of the circuit of capital follow money through purchases, production, and sales in sequence; and his profit rate is computed on the capital advanced, a historical sum, rather than on its replacement cost. The critics answer that a given magnitude in a table is exactly what a simultaneous system also uses, that the reproduction remarks state a general dependence of value on current conditions which the simultaneist construction honours by solving for current values, and that the temporalists read a whole accounting system into texts that state, at most, that production takes time, which nobody denied. Between these readings stand the manuscripts and their editorial history, the fact that the third volume was assembled by Engels from drafts of differing dates, and the circumstance that Marx never faced the question in the form Bortkiewicz posed it, since the inconsistency that makes the question urgent was demonstrated after his death. The prudent conclusion, and the one this article adopts, is that the text underdetermines the choice: it supplies strong passages to both sides and a decisive instruction to neither. That conclusion is not a shrug. It relocates the dispute from exegesis to judgement, where the criteria of the comparison table, explanatory power, measurability, systematic coherence, do the work that quotation cannot.

## Money after the money commodity

The classical constructions assumed a money commodity. Bortkiewicz's system has a gold sector whose own value anchors the price level, and Marx's presentation everywhere supposes money to be a produced commodity whose value is determined like any other. The economies the reconstructions now address run on state and credit money that no industry produces. The disappearance of the anchor is a test the three schools meet differently, and the differences are rarely exhibited, because the literature's polemics run on other rails. For the New Interpretation the test is mild: the monetary expression is defined for whatever money the economy uses, since it is a ratio between that money's aggregate value added and the labour performed, and the absence of a money commodity merely removes a special case the ratio never needed. For the macro-monetary reading the test is equally mild and indeed congenial, since a theory that begins from money advanced is at home in a world of credit money, and its proponents have treated the development of Marx's monetary thought as a strength of their reading. For the temporal school the test is absorbed into the period structure: the monetary expression varies from period to period in any case, and a money whose own footing has changed is one more source of variation for the sequence to carry.

### What happens to the reconstructions when money is no longer a produced commodity?

Less than their critics expected and more than their founders discussed. The aggregate constructions survive the change intact, since none of them needs a sector that produces money; what is lost is a classical closure device, the determination of the general price level through the value of the money commodity, and each school must find the price level's determination elsewhere or leave it open.

The loss is worth a paragraph of honest registration, because it marks a place where all three reconstructions are completions in a stronger sense than their polemics admit: they complete Marx not only where his manuscript broke off but where his world ended. A theory of value for a credit money economy is a theory Marx gestured toward in his chapters on credit and banking without ever building, and the schools' shared silence about the price level's determination, their willingness to take the monetary expression as it comes from the accounts or the sequence, is a shared decision to leave that part of the building unbuilt. The value-form tradition, alone among the positions surveyed here, has made the nature of modern money a central theme, arguing that the constitution of value in exchange against money is if anything clearer once money is no longer a commodity, and the reconstructions' proponents have generally treated this as a change of subject rather than an answer. The reader assembling a complete view should register the unbuilt room: it does not invalidate the reconstructions' claims about exploitation, consistency, and the profit rate, but it bounds them, and the bound is one of the places where the next generation of this literature, if there is one, will have to work.

## Hard cases: joint production and the machine that outlives its price

Formal theories are tested by their awkward cases, and this literature has two standard ordeals, which a complete survey should set before the reader even though no school's fate has turned on them alone. The first is joint production, where a single process yields several products together, wool and mutton from the same sheep being the textbook emblem. In simultaneous equation systems with joint production, pathologies appear that Steedman wielded with effect: configurations in which aggregate surplus value comes out negative while profit is positive, an apparent counterexample to the correspondence between exploitation and profit itself. The second ordeal is fixed capital, the machine that serves for many periods, shedding value into the product slice by slice while its own market price lurches with every technical change, so that the theory must say both how its value is transferred and what becomes of the untransferred remainder when the machine is obsolesced mid-life.

### How is a machine's value transferred to its product under the rival conventions?

In the simultaneous construction, by repricing: the machine contributes to each period's output a transfer valued at its current replacement cost, so the depreciation charge breathes with the machine's present price. In the temporal construction, by inheritance: the transfer is governed by what the machine cost when acquired, and a collapse in its replacement price falls on the owner's capital as a loss rather than rewriting the transfers already made.

The schools' performances on the ordeals follow their founding moves so faithfully that the cases function as identity parades. Faced with joint production, the New Interpretation's monetary construction is comparatively untroubled, since its equalities are stated at the level of the net product taken whole, where the allocation puzzles of joint processes do not arise; its critics note, in return, that a construction the ordeal cannot touch may be one that has declined to answer the questions the ordeal asks. The temporal school meets joint production with its sequence: values determined period by period from actual prices and labour performed, its proponents have argued, do not generate the negative magnitudes, which are artefacts of solving for values from a physical system all at once; its critics answer that the escape is purchased, once again, by letting prices lead and values follow. On fixed capital the temporal school claims its clearest home advantage, since moral depreciation, the destruction of capital value by cheaper techniques, is a phenomenon its conventions register natively and the simultaneous conventions must handle by instantaneous repricing that erases the loss's incidence. Both ordeals are treated by the macro-monetary reading at the aggregate level it prefers, where the totals it determines first are insensitive to the allocation difficulties below. A reader grading the schools will find the ordeals less decisive than the polemics suggest, because each school was designed, consciously or not, with the cases it handles best in view; but the cases remain the quickest way to make a school's representative explain, concretely, what its magnitudes are, and seminar teachers in this field reach for the sheep and the machine for exactly that reason.

## The moving yardstick: the monetary expression through time

One further piece of machinery deserves attention before the criticisms resume, because it operates inside two of the three reconstructions and generates a family of difficulties all its own: the behaviour of the monetary expression itself through time. The ratio converts labour time into money, and it is not a constant of nature. It moves whenever prices move against labour time, rising with inflation, falling as productivity growth cheapens commodities, and jumping with monetary disturbances of every kind. In the New Interpretation, which computes the ratio period by period from the accounts, the movement is absorbed without strain, because the interpretation's magnitudes are avowedly period magnitudes: the rate of exploitation in a given year is a fact about that year's division of that year's product, and comparisons across years are comparisons of shares, which are unit-free and survive the yardstick's movement intact. In the temporal construction the movement enters the machinery itself, since what inputs transfer is fixed by converting their purchase prices at the monetary expression of the purchase period, while the output's money value is realised at the expression of the sale period, so that a changing yardstick is one of the channels through which price movements alter value magnitudes between the advance and the return.

### What happens to labour time magnitudes when the monetary expression itself moves?

They become period-bound. A sum of value expressed in labour time through a ratio that has since changed is not directly comparable with the same expression made later, and careful practitioners in both schools therefore state their magnitudes with their periods attached, as surveyors state heights with their datums attached, because the datum moves.

Critics of both schools have pressed the point into a general objection: a theory whose unit of account drifts with the price level has built its measurements on water, and the drift is not an inconvenience but a symptom, the sign that the labour time magnitudes are translations of money magnitudes rather than an independent standard behind them. The objection is the idleness charge in temporal dress, and the replies are the familiar ones in new dress: the defenders answer that the drift is the theory's realism, since the social valuation of labour really does change as productivity and prices change, and a fixed yardstick would be the fiction. There is, however, a quieter technical consequence that both schools' practitioners acknowledge and that the reader of empirical work should hold onto. Long-run comparisons of value magnitudes, a profit rate series across half a century, are comparisons made with a moving yardstick, and their meaning depends on the care with which the movement has been handled at each joint of the series. This is one more reason, added to the valuation of the capital stock, why the framework provenance of a series matters as much as its numbers, and why the measuring branch's conventions, examined below, deserve the prominence this article gives them.

## The schools against each other

The schools criticise the inheritance together and each other separately, and their crossfire is often more informative than their manifestos, because each school knows exactly where its rival's construction is tender. The temporal school's case against the New Interpretation is that it is simultaneity in disguise with the surrender mislabelled as success. The net product whose equality the New Interpretation preserves, the temporalists point out, is still valued simultaneously, the wage goods and the net output priced in one solved moment, so the construction keeps the assumption that produces Okishio's result where it matters and buys its headline equality by redefining labour power's value into a share. A rescue that proceeds by rewriting the rescued text's central definition is, on the temporal verdict, not a rescue but a revision, and the honest label for the New Interpretation is a new theory of distribution wearing Marx's vocabulary. The New Interpretation's defenders reply that the revision charge proves too much: every completion revises something, the temporal school revises the plain simultaneity of Marx's own worked tables, and the question is whose revision the wider text supports, a question on which the monetary chapters of the first volume speak for the wage-based definition more plainly than the temporalists allow.

### What do defenders of the New Interpretation object to in the temporal construction?

Its dependence on history. Because temporal magnitudes are inherited from past prices, two economies with identical techniques, identical wages, and identical outputs can carry different values and different profit rates purely because their price histories differed, and the critics hold that a theory of value which makes the present depend on the path in this way has lost contact with determination by current conditions of production.

The objection is the mirror of the idleness charge and cuts from the opposite side. Where the idleness charge says temporal values wait on prices and so explain nothing, the history charge says they remember prices and so explain the wrong thing, importing into the theory's core magnitudes the accidents of past markets, panics, and policy, none of which a theory of the capitalist mode of production, as distinct from a chronicle of one economy, should contain. The temporal reply is that the dependence is the point: capitals really are encumbered by their histories, a firm that bought its plant dear really is disadvantaged against one that bought cheap, and a theory that launders this away in the name of determinacy has chosen elegance over the phenomenon. Between the charges the reader can watch the schools' deepest difference recur in miniature: whether the theory's magnitudes should be functions of the economy's present state, clean and solvable, or of its trajectory, encumbered and real. Moseley's macro-monetary reading stands partly aside from this exchange and partly inside it. It shares the temporal school's destination, both equalities preserved, and rejects its route, since on the macro-monetary account no temporal machinery is needed once the givenness of the money advances is accepted; and it has pressed the temporalists on their treatment of the value of labour power and on textual claims it regards as overextended, while the temporalists have replied that givenness is simultaneity's quieter form, the acceptance as data of magnitudes formed in the very process under analysis. The three-way crossfire, in which each school's objection to a second is answered by a third's objection to the first, is the normal state of this literature, and a reader who can follow it without a scorecard has achieved the level of understanding this article exists to produce.

## The surplus divided: merchants, bankers, and landlords

The transformation problem concerns the first division of surplus value, its redistribution among industrial capitals as profit of enterprise. Marx's third volume continues to a second division, among the industrial capitalists and the other claimants the system supports: merchant capital, which performs the circulation functions and claims commercial profit; money capital, lent out and claiming interest; and landed property, claiming ground rent. A reconstruction that secures the first division must still face the second, and the schools' treatments of it are uneven in a way that reflects their architectures. Most at home here is the macro-monetary reading, since its whole construction is a determination of the total followed by its distribution, and Moseley's exposition follows Marx's order into the revenue forms with the least strain: the total surplus value is the fund, and commercial profit, interest, and rent are deductions and shares whose forms the theory explains without further transformation problems, because no second system of values stands behind them. The New Interpretation handles the second division within its monetary aggregates: the claims of circulation and finance appear as shares of the net product, and the labour that staffs them is treated, as the earlier section described, on the redistribution side of the accounts, a treatment whose strength is its measurability and whose cost is a certain flattening of Marx's form analysis, his care for why the surplus takes these distinct shapes and what the shapes conceal.

### How do the reconstructions handle the claims of merchants, bankers, and landlords on the surplus?

As shares of a total determined before them. All three schools agree that these revenues are distributions of surplus value rather than additions to it; they differ in how much of Marx's analysis of the forms, why interest presents capital as self-expanding, why rent presents a gift of nature as an income, their frameworks preserve and use.

The temporal school's treatment of the second division is the least developed of the three, a fact its literature does not hide: the school's energies have gone to the foundations, to consistency and the profit rate, and the revenue forms await the extended treatment the founders' programme promised. The gap is worth registering without triumph, since it illustrates a general truth about research programmes that the survey has met repeatedly: a school is an allocation of finite attention, and its map of solved and unsolved problems reflects its founding emergency. The temporal school was founded to answer the inconsistency charge, and its literature is deepest exactly there. The New Interpretation was founded to make the theory measurable, and its literature is deepest in the accounts. Founded to recover the architecture of the whole, the macro-monetary school is deepest in the distribution stages. A reader who grades each school by the whole of Marx's agenda will find each incomplete, and a reader who grades each by its founding emergency will find each formidable, and the difference between those readers is the difference between a survey and a polemic. This article is a survey.

## The redundancy objection renewed against the reconstructions

Steedman's challenge was addressed to the simultaneist construction, but its logic pursues the reconstructions too, and each school's handling of it is a fair test of the school's self-understanding. The challenge, restated: given the physical data of an economy, its techniques and its real wage, the prices of production and the rate of profit can be computed in full, and any set of value magnitudes is then derivable from the prices so computed, standing to them as a translation stands to a text it did not inform. If the translation is always available after the fact and never needed before it, the value theory is an optional commentary on a price theory that runs without it. Against the New Interpretation the challenge takes a particular form, since that school concedes the computation of individual prices to other theories from the start: the redundancy critic asks why, if prices and distribution can be had from the physical data and the wage, the monetary expression and its apparatus should be retained at all, and the school's answer is its founding one, that the apparatus is not a rival computation but an account of what the computed magnitudes mean, of the relation between the profit total and the labour performed, which the physical computation states without interpreting. Against the temporal construction the challenge meets the school's most distinctive reply: the physical data do not suffice, because the magnitudes of the system depend on the prices at which inputs were actually bought, which are not physical data but historical ones, so that the supposedly complete physical description of an economy is incomplete in exactly the place where the value theory's temporal content lives. Against the macro-monetary reading the challenge dissolves into the givenness dispute once more: the physical data, the reading replies, are never given in physical form to any actual theorist or capitalist, but in money, and a computation that begins from money advances is not a rival to the monetary theory but an instance of it.

### Can prices be computed from physical data alone, without any value magnitudes?

Within a simultaneous model, yes, and the reconstructions do not deny it. Their common answer is that computability within a model is not the same as explanation in the world: the model that computes prices from physical data assumes away, in its simultaneity or in its silence about money and time, the features of actual economies on which the value theory's claims fasten.

The exchange has reached the stable form of a philosophical standoff, and it is worth the reader's while to see its shape clearly, because the same shape recurs wherever a theory is accused of redundancy by a formalism that reproduces its results. The formalist's standard is determination: a magnitude earns its keep by being needed to fix the values of other magnitudes. The defender's standard is constitution: a magnitude earns its keep by saying what the other magnitudes are, why profit is a claim on labour, why the wage is the price of a peculiar commodity, why crises of devaluation take the form they do. Neither standard is disreputable, and neither is reducible to the other, and most of the crossfire between the Sraffian tradition and the reconstructions consists of the two standards being applied in alternation without announcement. The reconstructions' collective achievement, in this corner of the dispute, is to have forced the standards apart: after them, a critic who calls value redundant must say whether the redundancy is in determination, which the schools largely concede in their different ways, or in constitution, which is the claim they exist to contest.

## The strongest objection: consistency by convention

The objections so far have been school against school. There is a stronger objection that stands outside the quarrel and addresses every reconstruction at once, and the conventions of this series require it to be stated at full strength. It runs as follows. A reconstruction that secures the consistency of a theory by choosing an accounting convention has demonstrated only that a convention exists under which the arithmetic works. Consistency is a property of bookkeeping systems, and bookkeeping systems can be designed to order. The substantive question about Marx's theory was never whether its identities could be made to balance under some rule for valuing inputs and converting labour into money; it was whether value magnitudes explain anything in the capitalist economy that is not already explained by prices, physical quantities, and the wage. On that question the reconstructions, for all their ingenuity, are silent in exact proportion to their success, because the more completely a school secures consistency by definition or by convention, the more completely it insulates the theory from the possibility of being wrong, and a theory that cannot be wrong about its central identities has traded explanation for immunity. So stated, the objection is not that the reconstructions fail. It is that they succeed at the wrong task.

The answer available to the reconstructions is to identify what each claims to explain beyond consistency, and the claims are real. The New Interpretation claims to explain the existence and the limit of aggregate profit: profit is not a markup conjured in exchange but the money form of the labour performed beyond the labour represented by wages, and its theory of exploitation yields measurements of that division that can be tracked and compared. The temporal interpretation claims to explain the dynamics of profitability: why the rate of profit, computed on capital as it is actually committed, tends to fall as productivity rises, and why capitals are periodically devalued, claims that are false or unstatable in the simultaneist frame and that connect the value theory to the theory of crisis. The macro-monetary reading claims to explain the architecture of Capital itself: why the argument moves from production to the distribution of surplus value among profit, interest, and rent, an order that is natural if the total is determined first and mysterious if every magnitude must wait on a transformation of inputs. These are explanatory claims, and they can be argued with, tested against texts, and set against rival explanations, which is to say they are not immune in the way the objection fears. The objection nonetheless lands, and lands hard, against the weaker versions of the consistency claim that circulate in the schools' popular presentations: the triumphal version in which Marx has been vindicated because a consistent reconstruction exists. Against that version the objection is decisive. The existence of a consistent reconstruction shows that Marx's assertions can be completed into a coherent system, and it shows nothing about whether the system is true of any economy, a distinction the schools' careful proponents observe and their slogans often do not.

### Does internal consistency show that a theory is true?

No. Consistency shows that a theory's claims can hold together under a stated set of rules, which rules out one kind of failure, self-contradiction, and leaves every other kind untouched. A consistent theory may still be false of the world, and an inconsistent draft may still contain a true theory awaiting a better formulation.

The point is elementary and is nonetheless the most frequently lost point in this literature's public exchanges, where headlines of the form Marx vindicated, or Marx refuted, attach themselves to results that are strictly about internal coherence. The refutations were always of the same limited kind. Bortkiewicz showed that a particular completion of Marx's procedure was inconsistent, not that the labour theory of value was false; Steedman showed that value magnitudes were redundant for computing prices from physical data, not that exploitation was a fiction; and the reconstructions show that other completions are consistent, not that exploitation, so measured, governs actual economies. Between coherence and truth stands evidence, and the evidence for these frameworks is of a peculiar kind: the texts of Marx, for the interpretive claims, and the national accounts, for the measurable ones. A reconstruction can win on the first kind completely and still have its second kind of work all before it. That is why the empirical question, which of these constructions the field's measuring branch actually uses and why, is not a sociological footnote to the dispute but one of its substantive fronts, and it is to that question that the article now turns.

## The long-period method and its refusal

Beneath the valuation dispute lies a dispute about method that the literature states less often than it should, because it explains the valuation dispute. The classical tradition in economics, from Smith through Ricardo to Marx and onward to the Sraffian school, worked by the long-period method: the theory determines the persistent centres around which market magnitudes oscillate, abstracting from the accidental and the transitional, and the data of the determination are the economy's durable features, its techniques, its real wage, its outputs. Simultaneity is the long-period method's natural arithmetic, since a centre of gravitation is a position, and positions are computed all at once. The simultaneist transformation is thus not an arbitrary formalism imposed on Marx from outside; it is Marx read by the method Marx inherited, and its practitioners could fairly claim to be applying the master's own method to the master's unsolved problem. The temporal school's refusal of simultaneity is, at bottom, a refusal of the long-period method itself: on its account, the persistent centres are a fiction of the method, the economy's magnitudes are what the sequence of its actual prices and labours makes them, and a theory that begins by abstracting from time has abstracted from the medium in which capital exists. Freeman's non-equilibrium programme generalises the refusal into a methodology for economics at large, in which equilibrium positions are special cases that a temporal theory may visit but never inhabit.

### What is the long-period method, and why do the temporalists reject it?

It is the classical practice of determining the economy's persistent centres, its normal prices and profit rate, from its durable data, treating market fluctuations as oscillations around those centres. The temporalists reject it because its abstraction from sequence erases the phenomena, devaluation, path dependence, the falling rate on committed capital, that their reading of Marx exists to explain.

The methodological depth of the quarrel should temper any reader's expectation of its resolution, since methods are not refuted by results obtained within rival methods: every temporal demonstration presupposes the sequence the long-period method brackets, and every long-period demonstration presupposes the simultaneity the temporalists refuse, so the demonstrations pass each other like trains on separate tracks, each impeccable by its own lights. The New Interpretation and the macro-monetary reading are, methodologically, long-period constructions with monetary data, which is why their quarrel with the simultaneist tradition could be conducted as a family argument about definitions while the temporal quarrel could not: the temporal school is not offering a better long-period position but denying that the position exists. Historians of economics will recognise the pattern from other methodological schisms, in which the parties' inability to lose is the surest sign that the dispute is about the constitution of the science rather than about any theorem within it. The reader's equipment is unaffected: the four questions still sort the contributions, and the comparison table still prices the schools, because even a dispute about method must declare its equalities, its givens, and its measures before it can begin.

## Gravitation: what the reconstructions say about actual prices

Marx's prices of production were never meant as descriptions of the prices struck in any market on any day. They are centres of gravitation, the levels around which market prices oscillate as capital moves between industries in pursuit of higher returns, and a complete account of the reconstructions should ask what becomes of gravitation in each. The question is less often posed than the equality questions, because gravitation is a doctrine about process rather than about consistency, but it separates the schools in an instructive way. The classical simultaneist tradition handles gravitation as an adjustment story told around its solved systems: the long-period prices are computed, and market prices are said to tend toward them, with the mechanics of the tending left informal. The New Interpretation, having declined to theorise individual prices, largely declines gravitation too, or rather hands it to whichever theory of competition the user prefers: its account fixes the aggregate within which the gravitation occurs, the total of surplus value and its division, and is silent about the paths of the individual magnitudes. The macro-monetary reading behaves similarly at one remove, determining totals and treating the formation of individual prices as the work of competition described in Marx's own qualitative terms.

### Do the reconstructions explain why market prices gravitate toward prices of production?

In the main, they inherit Marx's mechanism rather than rebuild it: capital migrates toward higher profit rates, expanding supply and lowering prices where returns are high, contracting where they are low, until the rate equalises. The temporal school's non-equilibrium framing gives the mechanism a distinctive twist, since in a world of perpetual price movement the centre of gravitation is itself moving, and gravitation becomes a tendency toward a target that technical change keeps displacing.

Freeman's work is the locus of that twist, and it is one of the temporal programme's genuinely original contributions to the wider theory, beyond the transformation dispute itself. If prices are formed in sequence and techniques change continuously, then the economy is never at the centre toward which it gravitates, and the long-period constructions of the simultaneist tradition describe a destination the system approaches only in the sense that a river approaches the sea it is always entering. Whether this is a deepening of Marx's picture or a dissolution of the only determinate content it had is, predictably, disputed: the simultaneist tradition replies that a centre which cannot be computed cannot regulate, and that the temporal alternative leaves relative prices as untheorised as the New Interpretation does, only with more machinery. The convergence of the schools on this point is the survey's quiet finding. All three reconstructions are, in the end, theories of the aggregate relations of a capitalist economy, of exploitation, profit, and their measures, and the theory of individual price formation, the oldest ambition of the value tradition, is in each of them either inherited informally, delegated to a companion theory of competition, or displaced by a dynamics in which the centres move. A reader who came to the literature expecting a completed rival to price theory in the marginalist sense will find the expectation disappointed everywhere at once, and should adjust the expectation: what the reconstructions compete to supply is not a price theory but an account of what profit is, whence it comes, and how its total moves, and on that ground their competition is real and unfinished.

## Two rates of profit, or one

A quiet ambiguity runs through the older literature and the reconstructions divide over it: whether there are two rates of profit, a value rate and a price rate, or only one rate wearing two expressions. In the dual-system construction the ambiguity is structural. The rate of profit can be computed in value terms, as the ratio of surplus value to the value of the capital advanced, and in price terms, as the ratio of money profit to the money advance, and the two computations generally give different numbers, since the transformation alters numerator and denominator differently. A literature then grows up around the gap: which rate governs capitalist behaviour, which rate Marx's tendency law describes, whether the value rate is the price rate's hidden governor or a statistic without a referent. Steedman's redundancy charge drew much of its force from this doubleness, since a magnitude with a shadow twin invites the question why the shadow is kept.

### Which rate of profit does the tendency law describe?

The schools answer by dissolving or by choosing. The temporal and macro-monetary readings dissolve the doubleness: in a single system there is one rate of profit, computable in money and expressible in labour time through the conversion ratio, and the tendency law describes that rate's movement as production unfolds. The New Interpretation chooses: the operative rate is the money rate on the money advance, with its labour time counterpart a restatement rather than a rival.

The dissolution deserves a moment's respect as a piece of theory design, whatever one thinks of its textual warrant. Many of the paradoxes that accumulated around the transformation problem across the century, cases in which the value rate fell while the price rate rose, or in which exploitation rose while profitability fell, are artefacts of the doubling, situations in which two magnitudes that the theory's surface grammar treats as one thing go their separate ways and generate spurious mysteries at their parting. A construction with a single rate cannot generate those mysteries, and the energy the older literature spent on them is released for other questions. The price of the dissolution is the price of each school's founding move, paid in its own currency: temporal determination, or given money magnitudes. The New Interpretation's choice is the more modest operation and generates its own characteristic question, since a reader may ask what the labour time restatement adds to a money rate that firms already compute, and the school's answer returns to its core claim, that the restatement is where exploitation lives: the money rate tells the capitalist what he earns on his advance, and the labour time rate tells the theorist what portion of the social working day that earning represents, and the theory exists to hold those two statements together in one pair of hands.

## What happens to exploitation under each reconstruction

Exploitation is the concept the whole apparatus exists to serve, and the reconstructions differ in what becomes of it, a difference their technical presentations sometimes bury. On the inherited definition, exploitation is a ratio of labour times grounded in production: the working day divides into the hours that replace labour power's value, that value being the labour embodied in the worker's consumption, and the hours that accrue to the capitalist. The rate of exploitation is thus fixed by two physical magnitudes, the length of the day and the productivity of the wage-goods industries, and it can rise only if the day lengthens or those industries cheapen the worker's bundle. The definition gives the theory its famous objectivity: exploitation is not a judgement about fairness but a measurable division of the day, present even where wages are high and workers content, provided the division holds.

### Does a rising real wage lower the rate of exploitation on the New Interpretation?

Not by itself. On that reading the rate is set by the way the net product divides, so a real wage rising in step with productivity leaves the wage share, and with it necessary labour and the rate of exploitation, unchanged; only a rise in the share itself lowers the rate.

The illustration exposes the conceptual distance the redefinition travels. A generation of workers whose material standard doubles while productivity doubles with it is, on the embodied bundle definition, a generation whose exploitation has fallen sharply, since the bundle that once took six hours to produce now takes three. On the New Interpretation's definition, the same generation's exploitation is exactly where it was, since wages command the same fraction of the value the generation adds. Both statements are internally coherent; they are statements about different things wearing one name, and much confusion in the secondary literature, and in political argument that borrows the term, comes from sliding between them. The New Interpretation's defenders argue that the share-based concept is Marx's operative one whenever he discusses the wage struggle, the working day, and the reserve army, since those discussions all turn on the division of the day and the product rather than on the physical composition of the bundle. Its critics answer that something has been lost that the theory cannot spare: an anchor outside distribution itself, a sense in which exploitation is grounded in the material process and not merely in the shares that process happens to throw up. The temporal school, for its part, keeps the inherited architecture and temporalises it: labour power's value is determined by the conditions of the period, the surplus is the excess of the day over it, and the rate of exploitation in value terms can move against its money appearance when prices shift between the advance of wages and the sale of output. The macro-monetary reading determines the economy-wide rate first, as the ratio of the total surplus value to the total variable capital expressed through the conversion ratio, and treats industry-level rates as derivative. A student who can state, for each school, what would make its rate of exploitation rise, has understood the schools more deeply than a student who can recite their equalities, because the equalities are where the schools advertise and the exploitation concept is where they live.

## The tractability divide

The tractability divide is this article's namable claim, and it can be stated in one sentence: the interpretation most used in empirical research and the interpretation claiming the strongest textual fidelity are different, and the divergence is explained by what can be computed from available data rather than by any resolution of the interpretive dispute. Empirical work in Marxist economics, the measurement of profit rates, exploitation rates, and distributional shares from the statistics of actual economies, runs overwhelmingly on the New Interpretation's machinery. The reason is not that the measurers have adjudicated the textual quarrel in Foley's favour. The reason is that the monetary expression can be computed from two published aggregates, money value added and total hours worked, while a temporal reconstruction of an economy would require a period by period sequence of prices and values for every input, a dataset that no statistical office assembles and that historical price records cannot supply at the required detail. Research programmes, like armies, move along their supply lines, and the supply line of empirical Marxist economics runs through the national accounts.

The point is easily misunderstood as a sneer, and it is not one. Tractability is a genuine scientific virtue with a long and honourable history: a framework that can be brought to data disciplines speculation, exposes itself to refutation, and accumulates findings that outlast the framework's own controversies. The empirical literature built on the New Interpretation's definitions has produced real knowledge about how the net product is divided and how profitability moves in several economies, knowledge that would not exist had the measurers waited for the textual dispute to settle. Nor is the temporal school's position a mere excuse for inactivity: its leading figures have produced empirical work of their own, most visibly in the study of the United States rate of profit, where the temporal principle enters through the valuation of the capital stock. There the divide shows its practical teeth, because the American statistical authorities publish capital stock estimates at current cost, replacement value in the simultaneist spirit, while the temporal argument requires historical cost, what was actually paid, and the two measures of the same capital stock yield two different histories of American profitability, one in which profitability recovers after the early nineteen eighties and one in which its fall persists. The choice of interpretation, inert in many contexts, is here the whole finding.

### Why can the monetary expression be read off national accounts?

Because its two components are headline statistics. Money value added is the core aggregate of the national income accounts, and total hours of living labour is published by labour statistical offices, so the ratio between them is available for any economy and year that a researcher wishes to study.

Contrast the data demands of the alternatives and the divide stops looking like a preference and starts looking like a constraint of the material. An embodied labour computation of the older kind requires an input-output table, which statistical offices publish infrequently, in coarse sectoral detail, and years in arrears, together with labour coefficients matched to the table's sectors. A temporal computation requires all of that in sequence, period after period, with the prices actually paid for every category of input distinguished from the prices ruling when outputs were sold, a distinction that published statistics almost never record, since the accounts are built on the opposite convention, the valuation of flows at the prices of the period in which they are measured. The New Interpretation's demands, two aggregates and a set of stated conventions about the boundaries of productive labour, can be met from a statistical yearbook in an afternoon. Foley and Thomas Michl's textbook Growth and Distribution built an entire framework for analysing distribution and accumulation on this basis, and the stream of empirical papers measuring exploitation and profitability in the interpretation's terms, for the United States and for other economies, is the predictable result. When a literature's empirical branch converges on one of several rival frameworks, the convergence is evidence about the frameworks' relation to the available instruments at least as much as about their relation to the truth, and intellectual honesty requires saying so in both directions: the convergence does not show the New Interpretation is correct, and it does not show that its users are lazy. It shows what can be computed.

### What would a temporal reconstruction of a whole economy demand from the data?

A price history, not a price level. Every category of input would need the prices actually paid at its purchase dates, capital goods would need their acquisition costs rather than their replacement values, and the sequence would have to run unbroken across the whole period studied, with a monetary expression for each period along the way.

The demand explains a feature of the temporal literature that puzzles newcomers: its reliance on numerical examples, small constructed economies of two or three sectors run forward for a handful of periods, in place of the statistical studies that fill the rival literature. The examples are not a failure of ambition. They are what the framework can honestly operate on, given that its data requirements exceed the historical record for any real economy, and they serve the framework's actual purpose, which is demonstrative: to show that under temporal valuation the results the simultaneists pronounced impossible, both equalities holding, a falling profit rate under rising productivity, follow without contradiction. A demonstration settles a question of logic. It does not settle a question of fact, and the temporal school's critics are within their rights when they observe that the school's claims about actual economies rest on a small number of studies, the historical cost work on the United States prominent among them, rather than on a broad statistical programme. The school's defenders answer that demonstrating the coherence of Marx's dynamics was the task the century set, that the simultaneist consensus had pronounced those dynamics nonsense, and that a literature should be graded first on whether it reopened a closed question. Both the observation and the answer are fair, and a reader who holds them together has understood the divide better than a reader who takes either side's summary of it.

### What happens to a measured profit rate when the valuation convention changes?

It can change its story. Measured against capital valued at current replacement cost, the postwar United States rate of profit falls into the early nineteen eighties and then recovers; measured against capital at historical cost, the sum actually advanced, the recovery largely disappears and the fall persists across the whole period.

This case, developed in Kliman's empirical work and contested by researchers working with the conventional measures, is the cleanest demonstration available that the interpretive dispute is not scholastic. The underlying events are agreed: the same factories, the same outputs, the same money profits, the same published accounts. Everything in the two series is identical except the denominator, the value placed on the capital stock against which profit is reckoned, and that single conventional choice reverses the conclusion that four decades of commentary have been built on, whether American profitability was restored after the nineteen eighties or continued its long decline. Debates about restoration feed directly into explanations of wage stagnation, financialisation, and the crisis of 2008, so the valuation convention reaches, by a short chain, into some of the largest arguments in political economy. The lesson generalises beyond this case. Every empirical magnitude in this field, the rate of exploitation, the share of profits, the rate of profit itself, is the output of a framework as well as of a dataset, and a reader who does not know which framework generated a series does not know what the series says. That readerly moral, more than any verdict on the schools, is what the comparison of the reconstructions is for.

## The founder's second career: Duménil with Lévy

An irony of the field illuminates the divide from an unexpected side. Gérard Duménil, co-founder of the New Interpretation, has conducted his best-known empirical work, the long collaboration with Dominique Lévy on the history of the profit rate and the dynamics of capitalist economies, largely in a different idiom: a classical framework of prices, gravitation, and disequilibrium, in which the operative magnitudes are money profit rates and their movements across the eras of capitalism. The collaboration's histories of profitability, of the managerial and financial transformations of the twentieth century, are among the most cited empirical achievements of modern Marxist economics, and they proceed without leaning on the value of labour power definition that made their senior author's name in the transformation debate. Nothing discreditable follows: a theorist may hold that a question of interpretation is settled one way and that a question of historical measurement is best pursued in another framework, and Duménil's own writings are explicit about the distinction between the levels. But the spectacle is instructive for the reader of this article. If even a founder of the interpretation that conquered empirical work conducts his empirics substantially outside it, then the tractability divide is not a line between schools but a line between kinds of question, running through individual careers as readily as through the literature. What can be computed governs what is computed, at the level of the single research life as at the level of the field, and the interpretations survive alongside the practice they only partly govern, as programmes of textual and theoretical allegiance whose empirical dominion is real but uneven.

## The computer enters: calculation at the scale of an economy

One development has altered the practical balance among the frameworks in a way none of the founders foresaw, and a survey that ignored it would be incomplete. The embodied labour computations that the older tradition demanded, the inversion of an economy's whole input-output structure into labour coefficients, were for most of the twentieth century a practical impossibility beyond toy models, which is one reason the dual-system theory lived as algebra rather than as measurement. Electronic computation removed the impossibility. Working with published input-output tables and modern machines, Paul Cockshott and Allin Cottrell computed the labour contents of the commodities of an actual economy, the British, and compared them with market prices, reporting correlations close enough to argue that labour values track prices directly, with deviations of the size that the transformation literature's theoretical quarrels dwarf. Their conclusion was characteristically direct: with adequate computing, the transformation problem shrinks toward a bookkeeping detail, since the values it agonises over can be calculated and their closeness to prices inspected rather than presumed.

### Has cheap computation changed the practical standing of the rival frameworks?

It has changed what can be checked. Labour contents for whole economies are now computable, so claims about the closeness of values and prices have become empirical rather than rhetorical; but computation cannot choose the conventions, simultaneist or temporal, definitional or embodied, under which the contents are calculated, and the frameworks' quarrel survives the machines intact.

The episode deserves its place for the light it throws backward over the tractability divide. The divide, it turns out, is not a fixed property of the frameworks but a function of the instruments of an era: what was intractable for the simultaneist tradition in the age of the desk calculator, an economy-wide system of labour coefficients, became tractable in the age of the database, while the temporal framework's demands, sequences of actually paid prices, remain intractable in every age, because the past does not retroactively publish its invoices. Instruments giveth and instruments taketh away, and a literature's sense of which of its problems are deep and which are merely hard is quietly set by the machinery on its desks. The reconstructions' founders worked that machinery as they found it. The next turn of the instrument wheel, comprehensive digital records of transactions, would if anything deepen the temporal school's disadvantage in a subtler way, by making the simultaneist and embodied computations trivially exact while leaving the interpretive question, which of the computable magnitudes is Marx's theory about, exactly where it stands. Computation settles arithmetic. The dispute was never only arithmetic, and the reader who has followed this article's argument will not expect the machines to end it.

## The measuring branch at work

The divide is best appreciated by watching the measurers work. The largest single measurement project in this tradition, Anwar Shaikh and Ahmet Tonak's reconstruction of the United States national accounts on Marxian categories in their 1994 book Measuring the Wealth of Nations, shows what the work involves. The published accounts are built on distinctions foreign to Marx's: they do not separate productive from unproductive activity in his sense, they record the output of trade, finance, and government in ways that mingle the production of value with its redistribution, and they value capital at conventions chosen for other purposes. The measurer's first labour is therefore translational: a mapping, defended category by category, from the statistical office's boxes to the theory's. Only after the mapping exists do the famous magnitudes appear: the rate of surplus value, the profit rate, the shares of the product absorbed by circulation and administration. Shaikh and Tonak's project predates the reconstructions' ascendancy and works in a broadly classical frame, but its lesson generalises to all of them: in this field the measurement is the theory, restated as a set of decisions about data, and two researchers who share a school and differ on the mapping will publish different numbers, exactly as two who share a dataset and differ on the school will.

### Which conventions, apart from the interpretation itself, move a measured rate of exploitation?

The boundary of productive labour moves it most. Whether the hours counted as value-producing include only goods production, or also transport and storage, or also the labour of circulation, wholesale, retail, finance, and the state, changes both the total of living labour and the wage bill against which surplus is reckoned, and with them the rate, by margins that dwarf many effects researchers argue about.

Simon Mohun's long series of estimates for the United States made this unavoidable point into a research programme, decomposing the movement of the measured rate of exploitation into the contributions of the wage share, the boundary decisions, and the treatment of supervisory and unproductive employment, and showing that the story told about postwar exploitation changes with the conventions in ways that no amount of additional data can arbitrate, because the conventions are the theory in operational dress. On the price side of the field, Lefteris Tsoulfidis and his collaborators, working on the Greek economy with input-output data, pursued the older simultaneist question of whether labour values and prices of production track market prices closely enough to count as their regulators, and reported deviations small enough to sustain the classical picture of values as centres of gravity: a result that belongs to the simultaneist tradition's empirical wing and that the temporal school regards as measuring, with great precision, magnitudes its framework says are beside the point. The coexistence of these programmes, all competent, all data-based, none refuting the others, is the empirical face of the interpretive pluralism this article has described. The measurements are real. What they measure is fixed by decisions taken before the data are consulted, and the reconstructions are those decisions, systematised. That is the deepest sense of the tractability divide: not that one school is easy and another hard, but that empirical Marxist economics, as actually practised, is the study of economies through whichever reconstruction's decisions the researcher has adopted, and its findings inherit the adoption.

## Misreadings to correct

Five misreadings recur wherever these reconstructions are discussed outside the specialist literature, and each survives because it flatters a side. The first is that the schools are competing solutions to one problem, rival answers marked against a single question. In fact they partly redefine the problem: the New Interpretation relocates the transformation into a question about the net product and the wage, the temporal school relocates it into a question about the valuation of inputs across time, and the macro-monetary reading dissolves it into a question about what Marx took as given. Schools that redefine the problem they solve cannot be ranked by their solutions alone, and much confused commentary consists of exactly that ranking. The second misreading is that the temporal interpretation is a fringe position, the hobby of a sect. It is a substantial literature with monographs, edited volumes, conference streams, and a decades long presence in the field's journals, produced by economists whose competence its critics do not question even while rejecting its conclusions; fringe is a word for work that can be ignored, and this work demonstrably cannot be, since its rivals write replies to it. The third misreading is that consistency settles truth, already answered above: a consistent Marx is a possibility proof, not a vindication, and an inconsistent draft was never a refutation.

The fourth misreading is that the monetary expression is a discovery, a magnitude found in the economy the way a ratio of physical constants is found in nature. It is a definition, fruitful and empirically anchored, but a definition, and its defenders, Foley prominently among them, have generally been candid about this, presenting the ratio as the interpretation's organising stipulation and defending it by its fruits and its textual warrant rather than by pretending it was measured into existence. The fifth misreading is that all Marxist economists accept one of these reconstructions, so that the field can be summarised by naming the winner. A great many accept none. Value-form theorists hold that the whole transformation framing misconceives Marx's theory, which on their reading concerns the social form of value rather than a quantitative derivation of prices. Economists formed by the Sraffian critique often regard the debate as an internal affair of a research programme they have left. Others again, including many who measure profit rates for a living, use the national accounts conventions pragmatically without enlisting in any school. The reconstructions are the field's most developed answers to its hardest internal question; they are not its census.

### Is the monetary expression a discovery or a definition?

A definition, and the distinction matters for how its results may be used. Because the ratio is stipulated as the link between aggregate labour time and aggregate money value added, identities built with it cannot then be cited as independent confirmations of the theory that stipulated it, though the stipulation itself can be defended by its coherence and fruitfulness.

H3 answers in this article keep being useful places to store small clarifications, and this one stores the distinction that the circularity debate turns on. A discovered magnitude constrains a theory from outside: if the ratio of value added to hours had refused to behave as a single conversion factor, if different industries or different years had demanded wildly unrelated ratios with no pattern, the interpretation would have been in trouble. A defined magnitude cannot refuse in that way at the aggregate level, since it is computed from the aggregates it relates. What can fail, and what critics and defenders actually fight over, is the usefulness of the definition: whether the magnitudes it generates illuminate distribution and profitability better than their rivals, and whether the text of Capital supports defining the value of labour power through it. Those are genuine questions with evidence on both sides. The misreading consists in skipping them, in treating the ratio as a fact of nature that settles the argument by being pointed at, a habit found among the interpretation's popularisers rather than its founders, and one the founders' own candour refutes.

A note belongs here on the word interpretation itself, since the schools' names can mislead a newcomer into expecting exegesis and finding construction. Each of these programmes is an interpretation in the strong sense used of musical performance or of constitutional law: a realisation of a text that necessarily adds decisions the text did not dictate, and that is judged by fidelity and by fruitfulness jointly, with the weighting between those virtues itself in dispute. Readers who approach the literature expecting one school to be simply reading Marx and the others to be inventing him will misunderstand every contribution they open, including the ones they end up agreeing with.

## What the dispute is really about

Strip the technicalities away and the dispute is about what kind of theory Marx's value theory is, a question on which Capital itself can be read in more than one register. On one register, the theory is a theory of price formation, a rival to the marginalist account of relative prices, and it is to be judged as such, by its determinacy and its fit. On another register, the theory is a theory of social form and of exploitation, an account of why the product of labour takes the value form, of what profit is, and of how the working day divides, and it is to be judged by the coherence and reach of that account. The simultaneist tradition judged by the first register and found the theory inconsistent and then redundant. The New Interpretation judges by the second register, rebuilds the theory as a monetary account of how the net product is divided, and accepts that relative price determination is not its business. The temporal school refuses to surrender the first register entirely, insisting that the dynamics of the profit rate, a claim about the movement of an actual price magnitude through time, stand or fall with Marx's argument, and rebuilds until they stand. The macro-monetary reading holds that the second register was Marx's own throughout, that the money magnitudes were the theory's native medium, and that the first register's demands were imposed by readers who wanted Marx to be a price theorist of the classical type. The schools cannot all be right about the text, but their disagreement is not confusion. It is the visible form of a real ambiguity in a large, unfinished, posthumously edited book, and it will last as long as the book is read.

This is also the place to say plainly what the article has implied throughout: the disagreement is technical and scholarly, not political in any partisan sense. The proponents of these reconstructions span the ordinary range of views among academic economists who work on Marx, the criticisms are argued in journals with the usual apparatus of models, texts, and data, and no position in the dispute maps onto any position in practical politics. A reader who encounters the schools presented as factions in a political struggle is reading polemic, not scholarship, and should adjust the weight given to everything else in the same source.

For the reader who has followed the argument this far, the working equipment is short. Ask of any contribution to this literature four questions. Which equalities does it preserve, and which does it surrender or redefine. What does it take as given, and what does it claim to determine. Can its magnitudes be computed from the statistics that exist, and has anyone computed them. And what does it claim to explain beyond its own consistency. A contribution that answers all four clearly is a serious one, whatever school it belongs to, and a summary of the literature that cannot answer them for the schools it names is not a summary but an allegiance. The linked articles complete the equipment: this series' article on the transformation problem for the original failure, the article in this series on Sraffa's challenge to Marxist economics for the external pressure that raised the stakes, and the article on [rate of profit empirical evidence](/2013/12/01/rate-of-profit-empirical-evidence/) for the reconstructions at work on actual data.

### Is the dispute finally about Marx or about economics?

About both, in layers that the participants do not always distinguish. The inner layer is about Marx: what an unfinished, stratified text asserts and permits. The outer layer is about economics: what a theory of value should deliver, determinacy, dynamics, or measurement. Most exchanges in the literature are one layer answering the other.

The layering explains the dispute's peculiar longevity and its resistance to the ordinary solvents of academic disagreement. A purely textual dispute could be expected to converge as the manuscripts are edited and the passages catalogued, and this one has not converged, because the textual findings are fed into an economic disagreement that no manuscript can settle: whether determinacy in a solved system is a virtue or a falsification, whether a magnitude that cannot be computed from published statistics is a discovery or a decoration. A purely economic dispute could be expected to converge on fruitfulness, and this one has not, because the textual layer keeps supplying each economic position with a warrant that its rivals must answer on exegetical ground. The two layers brace each other, and the structure stands. For the general reader, the practical wisdom is to identify the layer before joining an argument: nine exchanges in ten in the secondary literature are at cross purposes because one party is defending a reading and the other is attacking an instrument, and both leave persuaded of the other's obtuseness. The reconstructions, held apart by the criteria of the comparison table, do not generate that confusion, which is why this article has been organised by criteria from its first page.

## How the dispute is conducted: journals, volumes, and replies

An interpretive dispute of this longevity develops its own institutions, and a reader entering the literature should know their shapes, since the venue of a contribution is evidence about its function. The temporal school was built substantially in conference sessions and edited volumes before it was built in monographs: the 1996 Freeman and Carchedi collection functioned as its manifesto and recruiting ground, gathering economists around the twin banners of temporality and non-equilibrium, and the school's arguments were honed in a long sequence of exchanges in the field's journals, the Review of Radical Political Economics, Capital and Class, Historical Materialism, Rethinking Marxism among them, where the characteristic genre is the comment and reply, paper answering paper across successive issues with a closeness of engagement that book publication rarely sustains. Kliman's 2007 monograph gathered the school's case into a single brief and drew the predictable response of critical reviews and symposia, in which the reviewers' criteria, textual, logical, empirical, were often more visible than the book's. The New Interpretation travelled the opposite road, from journal articles into textbooks and empirical practice, acquiring the quiet authority of the framework that graduate students absorb as technique rather than as controversy. The macro-monetary reading built its case in a sequence of papers across two decades before its book-length statement, a pattern of accumulation characteristic of a school of one principal author with a widening circle of users. None of these bibliographical facts decides anything. Together they explain the dispute's texture: why the temporal literature reads as a campaign, the New Interpretation's as an establishment, and the macro-monetary as a brief being steadily assembled, and why a newcomer who samples only one genre, only the monographs, or only the textbooks, will form a distorted picture of where the field's weight actually rests.

## The reconstructions and the theory of crisis

The stakes of the dispute become visible to outsiders when the reconstructions are put to work on the largest events. Each school's profit rate is a different instrument, and pointed at a crisis the instruments give different readings, so that the choice among them shapes the explanation of the crash of 2008 and of the long stagnation debated after it. The temporal school's reading, developed in Kliman's study of the Great Recession, holds that the rate of profit in the United States, measured on capital at historical cost, never recovered from its postwar fall, that the decades of apparent prosperity were financed by an accumulating structure of debt erected over a weakening profitability, and that the crisis was the structure's collapse: an explanation in which the valuation convention is not a scholastic preliminary but the difference between an economy that was fundamentally sound and was struck by a financial accident, and an economy whose profitability had been failing for a generation. The measuring traditions that work with current-cost and share-based magnitudes tell other stories: of a profit share restored at the expense of wages, of profitability recovered and then absorbed by financial claims, of crises of realisation and demand rather than of production. Moseley's empirical work on the American rate of profit, conducted within his macro-monetary frame, has emphasised the weight of unproductive expenditures in absorbing the surplus and depressing the measured rate, a third mechanism with its own evidence.

### What does each reconstruction make possible in the explanation of crises?

A different location for the failure. Temporal magnitudes support explanations in which profitability itself deteriorates beneath the financial surface; the share-based magnitudes support explanations in which a restored profitability is misdirected, absorbed by unproductive claims or frustrated by deficient demand; and the choice between those explanations is partly a choice between the instruments that generated the magnitudes.

No position is taken here on the crisis explanations themselves, which belong to the empirical literature and to the linked article on the falling rate debate. The survey's point is narrower and, for the general reader, more useful: the interpretive dispute this article has anatomised is not a dispute about the museum care of a nineteenth-century text. It is a dispute whose terms decide what the data of the twenty-first century say, because the data reach every reader already interpreted by a framework, and the frameworks are these. When a headline announces that profitability has recovered, or that exploitation has risen, or that the rate of profit has fallen for four decades, a reconstruction is speaking, usually unnamed. The reader who can name it, who knows which valuation produced the capital stock in the denominator and which definition produced the surplus in the numerator, reads the headline as an argument rather than as a fact, and that reader is the one this article has been written to produce.

## What would settle the dispute

A natural closing question is what could settle all this, and the honest answer distinguishes the dispute's layers. The logical layer is settleable and settled: it is known, with the certainty proper to arithmetic, which constructions are consistent under which conventions, and no future work will make the dual-system construction consistent or the temporal construction inconsistent on their own stated terms. The textual layer is settleable in principle and unsettled in fact: the manuscripts are all published or publishing, the passages are known, and the disagreement persists because the text genuinely supports more than one completion, so that further scholarship refines the probabilities without reaching a verdict that all competent readers must accept. The empirical layer is the strangest: it is settleable only relative to a framework, since the magnitudes to be measured are defined by the frameworks, so evidence accumulates inside schools faster than it accumulates between them, and the field's centre of gravity shifts by conversion and by retirement more than by refutation. The criterion layer is not settleable at all by the means internal to economics, because it asks what a theory of value is for, and that is a philosophical question wearing technical dress.

None of this makes the dispute empty, and the reader should resist the sophisticated conclusion that an unsettleable dispute is a pointless one. The dispute has already produced the field's clearest understanding of its own foundations: the load-bearing assumptions are now declared where they were once invisible, the measurements now carry their conventions on their faces, and the text of Capital is read, by all parties, with a care for its monetary and temporal texture that the equation tradition's century did not practise. A dispute that disciplines a field in this way is doing the work that settled doctrine does in quieter sciences. What the reader should carry away is therefore not a ranking but a set of instruments: the four questions posed earlier, the comparison table, and the habit of asking, whenever a magnitude in this literature is brandished, under which reconstruction it was minted. With those instruments the whole literature, schools, critics, refusers, and measurers, becomes legible as one long argument about a genuinely hard problem, the relation between the labour human beings perform and the money their product commands. That problem will outlast every school named in this article, and the schools, read with the instruments in hand, are the best available map of its difficulty.

### Could a newly published Marx manuscript settle the textual question?

It could narrow it, and the critical edition has repeatedly done so, but it could not close it. Marx revised his formulations across two decades of drafts, so a further manuscript would add another stratum to be weighed, and the schools would weigh it by the criteria they already hold.

The history of the evidence so far supports the caution. When the critical edition made Marx's own manuscript of the third volume available for comparison with Engels's printed text, the comparison settled real questions: which formulations were the editor's smoothing, which hesitations stood in the draft, how far the transformation chapter was from the finish its author would have required. Each school absorbed the findings without evacuating its position, because each school's position is a weighting of the whole stratified record, and new strata alter weights at the margin rather than overturning them. A hypothetical manuscript in Marx's hand stating a valuation rule in so many words would come closer to decisiveness, and even it would be met by the questions the literature has learned to ask: of what date, in what state of revision, superseded by what later formulation, consistent with which published text. The fantasy of the settling document belongs to the polemic of the dispute rather than to its scholarship. What the evidence can do, and is doing, is raise the cost of the weaker readings year by year, as the record against which they must be defended grows more complete and more exactly known. Disputes of this kind end, when they end, not by verdict but by exhaustion of the positions that can no longer pay the rising cost, and the reconstructions surveyed here are, all three, still paying comfortably. That is the survey's final measurement, and it is offered as a measurement rather than as a judgement: the dispute is alive, its terms are clearer than they have ever been, and the reader who has followed it through this article holds the instruments by which its future instalments can be read as they appear.

## For students and exam candidates

Your examination risk on this material is not ignorance of the schools but blur between them, so study the contrast rather than the catalogue. Be able to state, for each reconstruction, the single move it makes: the New Interpretation redefines labour power's value through the money wage and a conversion ratio; the temporal interpretation revalues inputs at the prices paid for them; the macro-monetary reading treats the money advances as given from the start. Then be able to state what each move buys and what it costs, in the vocabulary of the two aggregate equalities. Questions on this topic reward the candidate who can say which equality a school surrenders and why its defenders accept the loss. ## For teachers and seminar leaders

This material suits postgraduate teaching better than undergraduate survey work, since it presupposes the transformation problem and rewards students who have already handled simultaneous equation treatments. A productive seminar design gives each school a week and a advocate: one group presents the New Interpretation from Foley's own exposition, one presents the temporal case from Kliman's, one presents Moseley's macro-monetary reading, and the fourth week is given to the critics of all three, with the tractability divide as the closing discussion, since it reliably divides a room along lines students did not know they had. Set the two-period valuation exercise as a written problem; it teaches more than a lecture on simultaneity.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Your first discipline in this field is provenance: never cite a magnitude, a profit rate, an exploitation rate, a share, without knowing which reconstruction's conventions generated it, because the same economy yields different series under different conventions and the differences are occasionally the whole story, as the historical cost dispute over American profitability shows. When you report a series, report its framework beside it. The reconstructions in operation on real data can be inspected in the companion article on empirical evidence for the rate of profit, and the underlying difficulty they answer is the transformation problem treated earlier in this series. For the working researcher, this article's comparison table repays keeping in structured form beside the datasets it describes: one card per reconstruction, its definitions, its data demands, and the series built under it, maintained in a tool such as the [VaultBook study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) companion, so that when a number is challenged the convention that produced it can be produced with it. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is the New Interpretation?

The New Interpretation is a reconstruction of Marx's value theory associated chiefly with Gérard Duménil and Duncan Foley, who arrived at it independently around 1980. It reads Marx's value as labour time expressed in money. A single ratio links the economy's total living labour to its total money value added, so that labour power's value becomes the money wage expressed in hours at that ratio rather than through the labour embodied in workers' consumption. On those definitions, aggregate profit corresponds exactly to aggregate surplus value, and the transformation can be stated without the inconsistency that afflicted the older dual-system presentation. Its name marks the contrast its authors intended: not a new theory, but a new reading of what Marx's value magnitudes had meant all along, recovered once the scaffolding of the older presentations is cleared away.

### Q: What is the monetary expression of labour time?

It is the conversion rate between the labour account and the money account of an economy: the total money value added produced in a period, set against the total hours of living labour performed in that period. Dividing a money sum by this rate restates the sum as the labour time it represents; multiplying a labour magnitude by it does the reverse. The New Interpretation builds its whole construction on the rate, using it to express the money wage as a quantity of labour time and thereby to define how much of the working day replaces the wage and how much is surplus. Because both of the rate's components are published for real economies, the rate itself can be tracked across decades and compared across countries, which is the practical foundation of the interpretation built upon it.

### Q: What is the temporal single system interpretation?

It is the reconstruction developed by Andrew Kliman, Alan Freeman, and their collaborators from the late nineteen eighties. Its claim is that Marx's value theory fails only when a foreign assumption, the simultaneous valuation of inputs and outputs at the same prices, is imposed on it. Value the inputs instead at the prices genuinely paid for them before production began, let outputs sell at the prices of their own moment, and, its proponents argue, Marx's asserted equalities hold and his account of the falling rate of profit becomes coherent. The words single system record the further claim that values and prices were never two separate systems in Marx's text. Its literature includes a book-length defence of Marx against the inconsistency charge and an associated programme of non-equilibrium economics, and it remains the most textually ambitious of the reconstructions surveyed in this article.

### Q: What does simultaneous valuation assume?

It assumes that a commodity entering production and the same kind of commodity leaving production carry one price, the price ruling in a single solved system, so that the cost of inputs is reckoned at what it would cost to replace them when the outputs are sold. The assumption entered the literature with the equation systems of the early twentieth century and became the default test of Marx's consistency. Its defenders value the determinacy it brings. Its temporal critics answer that it rewrites the past at replacement cost, crediting capitalists with advances they never made and erasing the losses that price movements inflict on sums already committed. The assumption is invisible in most textbook presentations, which is why the temporal critique, whatever its ultimate fate, permanently changed the teaching of the problem: the convention is now usually stated aloud before it is used.

### Q: Do these interpretations save Marx's consistency?

Each of them produces a version of Marx's theory in which the asserted aggregate relations hold without contradiction, so each saves consistency for the theory as that school reconstructs it. The saving is never free. One school redefines the value of labour power, another revalues inputs across time, another denies that the inputs were values awaiting conversion, and critics dispute whether the theory saved under each repair is still the theory Marx wrote. Consistency, moreover, is a limited prize: it removes the charge of self-contradiction and says nothing about whether the reconstructed theory is true of any actual economy, which is a matter for evidence rather than for algebra. The reader should therefore always ask, when consistency is claimed or denied, consistency of which completion, under which convention; the unqualified version of the claim is the mark of polemic rather than of analysis.

### Q: What are the criticisms of the New Interpretation?

Critics press two charges, one textual and one logical. The textual charge is that the interpretation keeps Marx's conclusions by replacing his concepts, above all the value of labour power, which in Capital is anchored in the labour needed to reproduce the worker and becomes, under the new definition, a magnitude that simply tracks the wage share of the net product. The logical charge is that the central ratio is defined in such a way that the celebrated equalities follow from the definition itself, so that their holding confirms nothing. Defenders answer that the ratio is an empirical magnitude with substantive restrictions built into its use, and that the reading recovers Marx's own monetary treatment of the wage. The school's own literature does not dismiss these charges but builds its expositions around answering them, which is one reason its textbooks read as arguments rather than as manuals.

### Q: What are the criticisms of the temporal interpretation?

Critics answer the temporal school on three fronts. First, they argue that its consistency is purchased by an accounting convention, sequential valuation, chosen because it delivers the desired results, and that a convention can be found to reconcile almost any set of assertions. Second, they argue that values built from prices already paid are explanatorily idle, since a magnitude that must wait on prices cannot be what explains prices. Third, they dispute the school's claim on the text, pointing to Marx's worked examples and to his remarks tying value to the labour required for reproduction under present conditions. The school's proponents reply on every front, and the exchange remains open. Its defenders regard the three charges as a single misunderstanding in three costumes, the refusal to let go of simultaneity, and their replies are correspondingly unified: grant the sequence, they argue, and the idleness, the convention, and the textual verdict all reverse together.

### Q: What is the macro-monetary interpretation?

It is the reading, developed most fully by Fred Moseley, on which Marx's theory is monetary and macroeconomic in its very structure. The capital laid out at the start of the circuit is a sum of money actually advanced, taken as given rather than computed from a prior system of labour values, so there are no untransformed input values and the classic inconsistency does not arise. Total surplus value is determined in aggregate, through the labour performed and the labour time the aggregate wage stands for, and the business of the theory's later stages is the distribution of that total among industrial profit, interest, rent, and other claims. The reading is sometimes grouped with the temporal school under the single-system heading, since both deny a separate value system behind prices, though its mechanism, given money magnitudes rather than temporal valuation, is entirely its own.

### Q: Which interpretation do empirical researchers use?

The measuring branch of the field works predominantly with the New Interpretation's apparatus, and the reason is practical rather than doctrinal. Its central ratio is a quotient of two published statistics, money value added and total hours worked, so exploitation rates and profit shares can be computed for real economies from statistical yearbooks. A temporal reconstruction would require unbroken sequences of purchase prices for inputs and acquisition costs for capital goods, which published accounts do not provide. The exception that proves the rule is the dispute over the American rate of profit, where temporal reasoning enters through the historical cost valuation of the capital stock and produces a markedly different history. Textbooks of Marxist economic measurement accordingly tend to teach the New Interpretation's definitions as the working standard, whatever their authors' sympathies in the interpretive dispute.

### Q: Does the choice of interpretation change any conclusion?

Sometimes it changes the largest conclusions in reach. The postwar history of American profitability is the standing example: reckon the capital stock at what it would cost to replace, and the rate of profit climbs again after the early nineteen eighties; reckon it at what was actually paid, and the recovery mostly vanishes, leaving a long decline that supports very different accounts of the period. In other settings the choice is quieter, affecting the level of a series more than its direction. The general lesson is that a magnitude in this field is never framework-free, and a conclusion quoted without its convention is only half quoted. Researchers who report both conventions side by side, as the better practice in this field does, convert a hidden assumption into a visible range, and their readers are correspondingly harder to mislead.

### Q: How does the tractability divide separate the two reconstructions?

The divide is the gap between the reconstruction that claims the closest fidelity to Marx's text and the reconstruction that empirical researchers actually use, where the second is chosen because its magnitudes can be computed from the statistics that exist. It separates the temporal school, whose defence of Marx is the most complete and whose data demands exceed what any statistical office publishes, from the New Interpretation, whose reading revises more of the letter and whose central ratio falls out of the national accounts. Neither side of the divide is a verdict on truth. It is a fact about the relation between frameworks and instruments, and it shapes what the literature can know. The divide also predicts the literature's geography: fidelity claims are prosecuted in journals of interpretation, measurements in journals of applied work, and the two conversations cite each other less than either admits.

### Q: What should a student separate between a simultaneous and a temporal valuation?

Separate the moment at which an input is priced. Under simultaneous valuation, an input counts at the price ruling when its output is sold, its replacement cost in a single solved system, so cost and revenue are struck in the same price world. Under temporal valuation, the input counts at the price actually paid when it was bought, in an earlier price world, and the difference between the two worlds is allowed to affect profits, losses, and the value transferred. Keep that one separation clear, which price, from which moment, and the rest of the controversy, the equalities, the falling rate, the charge of idleness, arranges itself around it. An examination answer that holds the two pricings apart and states which magnitudes each one fixes has already escaped the confusion from which most weak answers on this topic never recover.

### Q: Why did the monetary expression of labour time become the hinge?

Because the whole difficulty of the transformation can be restated as the absence of a disciplined bridge between the labour account and the money account, and the ratio supplies exactly that bridge in a single magnitude. Once a uniform conversion between hours and money is in place, labour power's value can be defined through the wage, surplus value can be stated as the unpaid portion of the day in either currency, and the aggregate equality between profit and surplus value can be secured. Whoever controls the bridge controls the reconstruction, which is why critics attack the ratio's definitional status and defenders stake the interpretation on it. The hinge metaphor is exact: a small component, easily passed over in an inventory of the machinery, on which the whole door of the reconstruction swings.

### Q: How does the reconstructions compared table order the dispute?

The table orders the dispute by criteria rather than by allegiance. Its rows are the three reconstructions; its columns separate what each one alters in Marx's procedure, which of the aggregate equalities it manages to keep, what it concedes in exchange, the strongest objection it faces, and whether empirical researchers can operate with it. Read across a row and you have a school in miniature. Read down a column and you have one criterion applied impartially to all three, which is the exercise the literature's polemics usually prevent. The table's lesson is that the schools rank differently under different criteria, and that naming your criterion is the beginning of an informed preference. Used in teaching, the table's discipline is to forbid the question which school is best until the supplementary question, best by which column, has been answered aloud.

### Q: What separates an interpretation of Marx's text from a defence of it?

The direction of the labour. An interpretation asks what the text's arguments are and follows them even where they end in difficulty; a defence begins from the conclusion that the text must be sound and adjusts the reading until soundness is achieved. Every reconstruction in this literature sits somewhere on that spectrum, since each supplies decisions the unfinished text left open, and the honest ones say where the supplying happened. The test to apply is candour about cost: a reading that names what it surrenders, as this article's table requires each school to do, is interpreting; a reading that presents its completion as a mere transcription is defending, whatever it calls itself. The distinction is not a moral ranking, since defences can be learned and interpretations can be lazy; it is a warning about where to look for the work that each text is actually doing.
