The critiques of Marxism are usually encountered as a single pile, and the pile is usually brandished rather than examined. Someone announces that the doctrine has been refuted, someone else announces that it has not, and both speakers treat a century and a half of argument as though it were one argument, made once, against one thing, with one result. Almost nothing in that picture survives contact with the literature. The criticisms are several distinct kinds of claim, aimed at different targets, argued by different people with different credentials, and they have fared very differently under examination. Some have been answered so thoroughly that only their slogans circulate. Some have been answered at a price the answerers were reluctant to name. Some stand exactly where their authors left them, unrefuted and largely unmentioned, because the traditions that should be answering them find them uncomfortable. A survey that cannot tell these apart is not a survey but a cheer, and the cheering happens on both sides of the question with perfect symmetry.

![A scholar's desk with a graded scorecard, annotated volumes of Capital and its critics, and index cards sorted into six piles, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

This article does the sorting. It divides the criticisms into six families according to what they target: its economics, its philosophical method, its record of prediction, its politics, its sociology of class, and its moral foundations. It states each family's leading arguments in the form their own proponents gave them, with the proponent named and the work and year identified, because a criticism that circulates without an author is usually a criticism that has been weakened in transmission. It then grades the families on a scorecard, the findable artifact at the centre of the article, which assigns each major critique a verdict of answered, partly answered, or standing, and attaches to every verdict the condition that would reverse it. And it reports the fact that gives the whole subject its peculiar character, the fact this article calls the internal origin: the strongest criticisms of Marxism have overwhelmingly come from Marxists and former Marxists, working from inside the tradition's own texts and standards, rather than by opponents attacking from outside. That fact is verifiable name by name, and it changes what a reader should do with every other page on the subject.

The reader this article assumes has met the claim that Marxism has been refuted and wants to know what the arguments actually are. That reader should be warned at the outset against the two errors that ruin most writing in this territory. The first error is amalgamation: treating an objection to a price theory, an objection to a philosophy of history, and an objection to a police state as though they were a single case that could be won or lost together. The second error is the mirror image, the defensive amalgamation that answers the weakest criticism in the pile and behaves as though the pile has been dealt with. Both errors are avoided by one discipline, applied relentlessly through what follows: before asking whether a criticism succeeds, ask what it is aimed at. The labour theory of value, the framework of historical materialism, the practice of Marxist parties, and the record of states that ruled in Marxism's name are four different targets, and a blow that lands on one may pass through the space where another was supposed to be.

Nothing here requires the reader to settle the large background theories first. The labour theory of value is linked rather than re-derived, historical materialism is summarised only as far as the criticisms require, and the theory of the state is treated where the political family needs it. Each individual critique also has its own article in this series, where it receives the full hearing a survey cannot give, and the routing to those articles is part of this article's work. What the survey adds, and what no single-critique article can supply, is the comparative grading: which arguments a serious reader must concede, which a serious reader may resist, and which have quietly changed sides and now belong to the general equipment of economics, sociology, and political science, used daily by scholars who would never call themselves Marxists.

## Four targets: what a criticism can be aimed at

Every criticism of Marxism is aimed at one of four targets, and the targets are different enough that confusing them voids the discussion. The first target is a specific claim of Marx's own: a proposition in the texts, such as the claim that the value of a commodity is determined by the labour socially necessary for its production, or that profit rates tend to fall as capital's organic composition rises. Criticisms of this kind are settled, in principle, by the texts and by logic and evidence bearing on the proposition itself. The second target is historical materialism as a framework: the general account of how modes of production structure societies and how history moves between them. A framework is not a single proposition, and it cannot be refuted the way a proposition can; it can be shown to explain badly, to predict loosely, to require constant repair, or to illuminate less than a rival framework illuminates, and the standards for those judgements are the subject of the philosophical family below. The third target is Marxist political practice: what parties, movements, and revolutionaries acting in the tradition's name actually did with its ideas, including what they did to the ideas in the using. The fourth target is the record of states governed in Marxism's name: their economic performance, their political character, their treatment of their own citizens. Criticism aimed at the fourth target is the most emotionally powerful and the most logically indirect, because between the record of a state and the truth of a theory there stands an argument that has to be supplied, about how far the theory directed the practice and how far circumstance did.

The discipline of the four targets pays for itself immediately. Consider the commonest sentence in popular writing on this subject, the sentence that says the twentieth century refuted Marx. Unpacked by targets, that sentence dissolves into four sentences of very different strength. Some specific claims of Marx's were indeed damaged by the century's evidence, and the historical family below names them. The framework was strained by some developments and confirmed in others, and grading it requires the slower work of the scorecard. The practice of Marxist parties produced results that their own tradition's internal critics had predicted with uncomfortable accuracy, which is evidence about the practice and about those critics. And the states' record is a fact of the first magnitude that nonetheless reaches the theory only through the connecting argument, which the political family examines and which cannot be skipped by pointing. A reader who keeps the four targets separate can hold all of these judgements at once without contradiction. A reader who merges them can hold only a slogan.

### What does it mean to criticise a framework rather than a claim?

A claim can be tested against a fact or a derivation, and it stands or falls by the result. A framework organises claims, suggests which facts matter, and supplies the vocabulary in which results are described, so it is assessed comparatively, by how much it explains against rivals, and it degrades gradually rather than collapsing at a single blow.

That difference in how the targets fail explains a persistent asymmetry in the debate. Critics who have demolished a specific claim often announce that the framework has fallen, and defenders who have protected the framework often behave as though every specific claim inside it were safe. Both moves are illicit, and both are common enough to have names in the literature of the dispute. The scorecard in this article therefore grades claims and framework components separately, and its verdicts specify which target each verdict concerns. Where a verdict concerns the framework, the defeater attached to it is framed in framework terms: not a single fact but a performance, a reconstruction, or a record that would change the comparative assessment.

### Why does the target of a criticism decide what would answer it?

Because each target has its tribunal. A textual claim is answered in the texts and in the logic of the derivation. A framework is answered by a rival that explains the same material with fewer repairs. A practice is answered by another practice under comparable conditions. A state's record is answered only by the argument connecting record to doctrine.

The four-targets distinction also disciplines the comparison basis this article uses throughout. Where Marxism's record is compared with other traditions, theories are compared with theories and implementations with implementations, the period is stated, and no tradition's practice is set against another tradition's ideals in either direction. That rule sounds elementary and is violated constantly, in both directions: the Soviet record set against the liberal ideal, or the liberal record of colonial violence set against the socialist ideal. The scorecard refuses both trades. A theory is graded against rival theories on the material both claim to explain; a state is graded against other states of its period facing comparable starting conditions; and the connecting arguments between the two levels are stated openly where they are used, so that a reader who rejects the connection can still use the grades on either side of it.

One further consequence follows, and it governs the survey's selection of material. Because the four targets require different evidence, a criticism's force cannot be inferred from the vehemence of its presentation or the eminence of its author. A devastating critique of a state's record, offered without the connecting argument, has great force against the state and undetermined force against the theory. A dry technical objection to a derivation, by contrast, may have decisive force against a claim on which other claims depend, and therefore indirect force far beyond its modest appearance. The survey grades force, not volume. That is why some of the most consequential entries on the scorecard are the driest, and why the internal origin matters so much: the people best placed to know which claims are load-bearing are the people who were trained inside the structure.

## The four targets at work: three confusions dissolved

Abstract distinctions earn their keep on cases, and three confusions that recur through the popular literature show the four targets doing their work. The first confusion concerns the famine and the derivation. A writer assembles the record of a collectivisation famine, the requisitions, the sealed borders, the mortality that the dedicated articles in this series handle under their figures protocols, and concludes that the labour theory of value stands refuted. The record is real and the conclusion does not follow, because nothing in the derivation of value from labour time predicts or forbids a state's agricultural policy, and the route from the record to the theory runs through the political family: through the argument that a doctrine without a theory of constraint licensed the party that ordered the requisitions. That argument exists, it is strong, and it is graded on the scorecard's political row. The famine reaches the theory through it or not at all, and a writer who takes the shortcut has weakened a strong case by presenting it as a stronger one.

The second confusion runs in the opposite direction and is the defence's favourite. A writer demonstrates, correctly, that a critic has misread a passage of the Grundrisse, or attributed to Marx a slogan coined by his epigones, and concludes that the criticism of Marxism has collapsed. What has collapsed is one criticism of one claim, and the scorecard's rows are untouched: Steedman's redundancy argument does not depend on the misread passage, Okishio's theorem does not quote the Grundrisse at all, and the political family's evidence is a pattern of institutions, not a proof text. Textual fidelity is a genuine scholarly virtue and this series practises it, but its victories are local. The defence that wins a footnote and behaves as though it has won the argument is committing the amalgamation error with the polarity reversed.

The third confusion is the subtlest, because both sides commit it in a single gesture: the comparison of one tradition's practice with another's ideals. The critic sets the record of the planned economies beside the liberal theory of the free society and finds the record wanting, as any record is wanting beside any theory. The defender replies by setting the record of colonial conquest, of famine under laissez-faire administration, of the Atlantic slave economy's integration into early capitalism, beside the socialist theory of the classless society, and finds that record wanting in turn. Both gestures are empty, and they are empty for the same reason: a practice must be graded against the practices it actually competed with, under the conditions it actually faced, and a theory against the theories that claim the same explanatory territory. This survey's comparison basis, stated in the targets section and applied throughout, exists to refuse both gestures at once. The planned economies are graded against the developing economies of their period that chose other roads, and against their own stated aims. The theory is graded against liberal, Weberian, and Keynesian rivals on the material all of them claim to explain. A reader who finds a comparison in this article that violates the rule has found a defect in the article, and the defeaters printed on the scorecard are the standing invitation to report it.

One more application of the targets deserves its own paragraph, because it governs the survey's treatment of its most emotional material. The record of the states includes crimes: deportations, engineered scarcity, camps, executions, the suppression of whole peoples' public life. Nothing in the four-targets discipline diminishes that record or defers its judgement. What the discipline governs is the record's logical address. The crimes are charged, in the first instance, to the governments and parties that committed them, and the historical articles in this series keep those charges in their proper place. They are charged to the doctrine only through the connecting argument, and the survey has been careful to build that argument where it can be built, through the missing theory of constraint, through the substitutionist mechanism the tradition's own critics named in advance, and through the normative family's finding about the standing of persons. Built that way, the charge against the doctrine is narrower than the popular version and far harder to answer, because it does not depend on any single atrocity and cannot be dissolved by disputing any single episode. Precision, in this territory, is not a dilution of moral seriousness. It is what moral seriousness looks like when it intends to be believed.

## Six families of critiques of Marxism: the map

The canonical sorting used in this article divides the criticisms by the part of the doctrine they engage. The economic family engages the theory of value, the derivation of profit, and the possibility of rational allocation without markets. The philosophical family engages the method: whether the framework makes testable claims, whether its explanations explain, and what its dialectical machinery adds or obscures. The historical family engages the predictions: the specific things Marx and the tradition said would happen, set against what happened. The political family engages power: what the doctrine licenses, what it fails to constrain, and what its parties built when they won. The sociological family engages the account of class: whether the map of social positions Marx drew still describes the societies that developed after him, and whether class does the explanatory work assigned to it against rivals such as nation and faith. The normative family engages the moral foundations: whether the framework contains a standard of justice, what it permits in the pursuit of its ends, and what it owes to the people its transitions run over. Six families, six different literatures, six different standards of success. The remainder of this section states the survey's headline answer; the sections after it take the families in turn.

### What are the main criticisms of Marxism?

they fall into six families targeting the economic theory, the philosophical method, the historical predictions, the political consequences, the account of class, and the normative foundations. They succeed to different degrees, and the strongest have generally been formulated by Marxists and former Marxists.

Two features of that headline deserve emphasis before the detail begins. The first is the variation in success. Within a single family, one argument may be decisive against the version of the doctrine it addresses while its neighbour fails completely, and the families themselves are not uniformly strong: the historical family's case against specific predictions is far stronger than the philosophical family's case against the framework as a whole, and the political family's central charge has never received an answer of the same order as the charge. The second feature is the provenance. Read the names attached to the arguments in the sections that follow and a pattern accumulates that no honest survey can omit: a Marxist economist pressed the redundancy argument, analytical Marxists dismantled the functional explanations, a leader of the Second International opened the revisionist challenge, a former Marxist philosopher wrote the most comprehensive critical history, and a Marxist political scientist gave the social democratic dilemma its sharpest account. The survey returns to that pattern in a section of its own, since it is the article's centre of gravity.

The map also tells the reader where the weight of this article falls. The critiques receive more space than any defence, because this is a survey of criticism and not an adjudication, and a survey that shortens the prosecution to make room for the defence has failed at its only job. Where a criticism stands, the article says so plainly, in the family's own section and again on the scorecard. Where a criticism has been answered, the answer is stated in its strongest form and the price of the answer is named, because most successful defences in this literature succeed by narrowing what the doctrine claims, and the narrowing is itself a finding. Replies as a whole belong to their own article in this series, and this survey states them only as far as the grading requires.

## Other maps, and why this survey sorts by target

Sorting by target is a choice, and the survey should defend it against the rival sortings a reader will meet elsewhere, because each rival map makes some things visible and hides others. Sorting by century, the nineteenth century's critics against the twentieth's, displays the doctrine's ageing but scrambles the arguments, since Bohm-Bawerk and Steedman belong to one conversation across eighty years while Bernstein and Kornai, near contemporaries in the long view, belong to different ones. Sorting by school, Austrian against analytical against Frankfurt, displays the critics' formations and is the right map for a history of the critics, but it grades nothing, since a school's internal quarrels, Cohen against Elster above all, vanish when the school is treated as a camp. Sorting by verdict, the refuted against the unrefuted, is the map the popular literature uses and the one this survey exists to replace, since it presupposes the gradings it should be arguing for and forces every argument into a binary the evidence rarely fits. Sorting by target, finally, has the defect of all functional maps: it can make the literature look tidier than it is, with arguments assigned to single families when their authors aimed them at several. The survey accepts the defect and manages it by cross-reference, the political family's terror evidence informing the historical family's state rows, the normative family's justice gap completing the political family's missing constraint. What the target map buys, in exchange, is the only thing a survey of criticism is for: the ability to say, of any argument a reader meets, what it is aimed at, what would answer it, and how it has fared, in one motion. The rival maps are recommended for the purposes they serve. For the purpose of grading, this survey keeps its own.

## A note on names: Marx, Marxism, and the schools

One preliminary tangle must be cleared before the families can do their work, because criticisms are often aimed at a name rather than a position. The doctrine this survey grades is not a single object. There is Marx's own work, unfinished, self-revising, and published in part posthumously by an editor who was also its co-author in spirit. There is the Marxism of the Second International, systematised by Kautsky and Plekhanov into a doctrine of stages and laws, which is the version Popper chiefly attacked and Bernstein chiefly doubted. There is the Leninist codification, forged for a party's use and later frozen into the dialectical materialism of the Soviet textbooks, a catechism whose relation to the manuscripts is itself a scholarly battlefield. There is Western Marxism, the tradition Perry Anderson mapped in his Considerations of 1976, formed by defeat in the advanced countries and migrating toward philosophy and aesthetics. And there is the analytical school, which proposed to keep the questions and discard the dialectical machinery, and whose members produced several of this survey's sharpest criticisms. A criticism graded against one of these objects may be worthless against another, and the survey's fidelity rule therefore includes a naming rule: every grade specifies, in its family section, which object the criticism engages. The transformation problem engages Marx's texts directly. The falsifiability charge engages the codified doctrine and, in Lakatos's form, the programme's whole career. The authoritarianism charge engages the Leninist party form and asks what in the earlier objects licensed it. Readers who keep the names separate will find that half the famous quarrels in this literature are cross-purposes quarrels, in which a defence of Marx is offered against a criticism of the catechism, or a criticism of the catechism is celebrated as a refutation of Marx. The scorecard refuses both celebrations, and the refusal is most of its value.

Anderson's mapping deserves a sentence of its own, because it is the internal origin operating at the level of the whole tradition. A Marxist historian, editing the leading journal of the British New Left, surveyed his own tradition in 1976 and found it characterised by esoteric language, by a migration from economics and politics toward philosophy, and by a structural divorce from the mass practice it theorised, a diagnosis offered as a prelude to repair and received, correctly, as one of the severest criticisms the tradition has absorbed. The episode generalises the survey's central observation. Again and again, the audit that mattered was commissioned from inside, by an editor, an executor, a party theoretician, a politburo member, a professor formed in the school he examined. Outsiders supplied indispensable arguments, and the survey has graded them at full strength. But the tradition's conscience, where it has had one, has been internal, and a survey of its critics that missed that fact would misdescribe not only the criticisms but the criticised.

## The economic family: value, profit, and the possibility of planning

The economic family is the oldest, the most technical, and the family in which the internal origin is most visible. Its arguments engage the machinery of Capital: the theory of value set out in the first volume, the theory of prices of production set out in the third, the derivation of profit from surplus value, and the claim that an economy could be coordinated by a plan rather than by prices formed in markets. Five arguments carry the family's weight: the transformation problem, the redundancy of value magnitudes, the attack on the derivation in the opening chapter of Capital, the calculation problem, and the incentive problem. Each has a literature of its own, a roster of named proponents, and a dedicated article elsewhere in this series where the technicalities live. The survey states each argument at the strength its proponent gave it, then records what the replies achieved and what they cost.

The transformation problem arrived first and has never left. Marx's third volume, published by Engels in 1894, derived prices of production from values by redistributing surplus value across sectors according to the capital advanced in each, so that capitals of equal size earn equal profit whatever their composition. The difficulty, formalised by Ladislaus von Bortkiewicz in 1907, is that Marx transformed outputs into prices while leaving inputs in value terms, and when the transformation is performed consistently on both sides, the two aggregate equalities Marx asserted, that total price equals total value and total profit equals total surplus value, cannot in general both hold at once. One equality can be preserved by a choice of numeraire or convention; preserving both simultaneously requires special conditions. The problem matters beyond bookkeeping because the equalities were the bridge between the value analysis of the first volume and the price phenomena of the third: if the bridge carries only one of its two loads, the claim that value analysis explains the price system is weakened in a way no amount of exegesis repairs.

Piero Sraffa's Production of Commodities by Means of Commodities, published in 1960, rebuilt price theory from physical data: given the technical conditions of production and the real wage, prices and the rate of profit can be determined without any reference to labour values. Ian Steedman, a Marxist economist at Manchester, drew the conclusion in Marx after Sraffa in 1977, and it is the conclusion that gives the redundancy argument its force. If prices and profits can be derived directly from the physical scheme of production, then value magnitudes are a detour: everything they were supposed to explain can be obtained without them, and worse, Steedman exhibited cases, joint production among them, in which the value derivation yields results such as positive profit alongside negative surplus value. Paul Samuelson had made a version of the point from outside the tradition in 1957, describing the value analysis as a detour that could be erased and rewritten in price terms, but the argument bit hardest when a scholar formed inside Marxist economics pressed it with the tradition's own standards of rigour.

### Who pressed the redundancy argument, and why did its source matter?

Ian Steedman, a Marxist economist working in the Sraffian tradition, argued in his Marx after Sraffa of 1977 that prices and profits follow from physical production data alone, making labour values redundant as an explanatory detour. Its source mattered because the objection could not be dismissed as a misunderstanding of the doctrine by a hostile outsider.

The replies to the transformation and redundancy arguments are real, and their price is the finding. The New Interpretation, developed by Gérard Duménil in 1980 and Duncan Foley in 1982, redefined the value of labour power and the monetary expression of labour so that both aggregate equalities hold, at the cost of making the equalities hold by the definitions adopted rather than by a derivation from production conditions. The temporal single-system interpretation, defended in Alan Freeman's work and in Andrew Kliman's Reclaiming Marx's Capital of 2007, rejects simultaneous valuation and reads Marx's procedure as sequential, dissolving the inconsistency by changing the valuation convention. Defenders of the value-form tradition, descending from Isaak Rubin's Essays on Marx's Theory of Value of 1928, reply that value was never a price algorithm at all but an account of the social form wealth takes under generalised commodity production, a reply that saves the concept by relocating its work from price determination to social theory. Each reply preserves something genuine. What none of them restores is the original claim in its original strength, that prices are explained by values through a derivation a sceptic must accept. The scorecard grades accordingly, and the full technical hearing belongs to [the article on Bohm-Bawerk's critique of Marx](/2019/09/15/bohm-bawerk-critique-of-marx/), which takes the opening chapter's derivation as its central exhibit.

### What was Bohm-Bawerk's central charge against the third volume of Capital?

Eugen von Bohm-Bawerk argued in Karl Marx and the Close of His System in 1896 that the third volume's prices of production contradict the first volume's law of value, and that Marx escaped the contradiction by assertion rather than proof. The charge named a genuine tension that later formal work refined rather than removed.

Bohm-Bawerk's book of 1896 is the family's founding external document, and its afterlife is instructive. Its author was the Austrian school's leading theorist and a finance minister of the empire, no one's idea of an internal critic, and parts of his case have worn badly: his treatment of the skilled labour reduction, his impatience with the dialectical presentation, and his confidence that the third volume simply abandoned the first. But the structural charge, that the system required a transformation it never consistently performed, survived every reply and was eventually restated in stronger form by the tradition's own economists. The inequality argument associated with his critique, the charge that the derivation of exploitation passes through an unexamined step in which the value of labour power is treated differently from the value of every other commodity, is graded on the scorecard as the part of his case that still bites. The survey's treatment here is deliberately brief: the four arguments of his critique, their replies, and their grades occupy their own article, and duplicating that hearing would weaken both.

The calculation problem changes the family's target from Marx's texts to the socialist project itself. In his essay of 1920, Ludwig von Mises argued that rational economic calculation requires money prices for the means of production, that such prices arise only from exchange between owners, and that a commonwealth which abolished private ownership of producer goods would therefore lack any common denominator by which to compare alternative uses of resources: it could grope, but it could not calculate. Friedrich Hayek widened the objection across the nineteen thirties and in his American Economic Review essay of 1945, shifting its centre from arithmetic to knowledge: the data a planner would need are not given to anyone in totality, being dispersed, contextual, and partly tacit, and the price system is a mechanism for communicating exactly that dispersed knowledge in compressed form. Oskar Lange answered with the market socialist model of 1936 and 1937, in which a planning board sets prices parametrically and adjusts them by trial and error in response to surpluses and shortages, an answer that met Mises on the ground of static allocation and left Hayek's knowledge objection standing in its general form. The shifted objection, from calculation to knowledge, is the debate's real result, and the scorecard accordingly grades the family's planning argument as partly answered rather than answered. The whole debate receives its hearing in [the article on the socialist calculation debate](/2019/08/15/socialist-calculation-debate/).

### Why did the calculation objection survive the market socialist reply?

Because the reply answered the earlier and narrower version. Lange's planning board, adjusting prices by trial and error, met Mises's claim that no common denominator for comparing uses could exist. Hayek's objection was different: the knowledge that prices compress is dispersed among millions of holders and partly inarticulate, so no board can gather what the mechanism was supposed to replace.

The incentive problem completes the family, and it is the argument whose strongest forms were again built by scholars close to the material. János Kornai, working in Budapest and writing from inside a planned economy's daily operation, identified the soft budget constraint in his Economics of Shortage of 1980 and generalised the analysis in The Socialist System of 1992: enterprises that expect rescue when they fail face weakened discipline over costs, investment hunger becomes chronic, and shortage reproduces itself as a system property rather than a policy error. Alec Nove, a sympathetic student of the Soviet economy, argued in The Economics of Feasible Socialism of 1983 that the coordination problems of a large economy defeated the feasible versions of the planning ideal, and that what remained workable conceded much of what the ideal had promised to transcend. The family's verdict, assembled on the scorecard, is therefore mixed in a specific pattern: the textual arguments were met at the price of the doctrine's original claims, and the institutional ones at the price of the project's original design. A reader who wants the single sentence may have this one: the economic family did not destroy Marxist economics, but it redrew its borders, and the territory surrendered was surrendered mostly to critics the tradition had trained itself.

## Calculation's second life: the computational revival and its audit

The calculation debate refused to stay settled, and its revival in the age of cheap computation is the economic family's most instructive late episode, because both sides claimed the new evidence and the audit had to be run again. Paul Cockshott and Allin Cottrell argued in Towards a New Socialism of 1993 that the computational premise of Mises's objection had expired: the arithmetic of balancing millions of products, prohibitive for the planning bureaus of the paper age, falls within the capacity of modern computing, and labour-time accounting could supply the common denominator Mises found missing, with democratic mechanisms setting the plan's priorities. The proposal was the calculation family's most serious modern defence, technically literate in a way the earlier exchanges were not, and its critics, including sympathetic ones, located the surviving objection where Hayek had left it. The knowledge problem, they argued, was never chiefly arithmetic. It concerns the elicitation of knowledge that holders do not know they have, the discovery of possibilities no catalogue lists, and the incentives to report honestly to a centre that allocates by the reports, none of which faster computation supplies. The revival's defenders answered that market processes elicit the same knowledge imperfectly and at the price of the pathologies the historical family records. This survey files the exchange under the calculation row's existing verdict and notes its methodological moral: a criticism aimed at a moving technology must be restated for each generation's machinery, and the restatement, in this case, confirmed the shifted objection's durability, since the argument that survived the computers was the one Hayek had substituted for Mises's in 1945. The full modern hearing belongs to the calculation article and to this cluster's later article on planning and the market, and the survey's restraint here is the disambiguation discipline in action: a guide that tries every case itself becomes a court that hears none of them properly.

## The Cambridge capital controversy: the audit that wounded both houses

The economic family's strangest episode is the one in which the Marxist-adjacent critics damaged the rival house's foundations as gravely as their own, and it earns its place here because the comparison basis requires grading traditions against each other and this is where the grading gets interesting. Joan Robinson opened the controversy in 1953 with her attack on the neoclassical production function, arguing that the aggregate capital of the textbooks could not be measured independently of the rate of profit it was meant to explain, so that the marginal productivity theory of distribution reasoned in a circle. The debate that followed, between Cambridge in England, where Robinson and the Sraffians worked, and Cambridge in Massachusetts, where Samuelson and Robert Solow defended the parable, turned on reswitching: the demonstration, conceded by Samuelson in his summing up of 1966, that a technique of production abandoned as the profit rate rises can become profitable again as it rises further, so that no monotonic relation between capital intensity and the profit rate exists in general. The concession mattered beyond its technicalities. The same logical terrain, the impossibility of valuing capital independently of distribution, underlies both the transformation problem in Marx's system and the aggregate production function in the neoclassical one, and the Cambridge critics, several of them formed in the Marxian and Sraffian stable, pressed the point impartially: the nineteenth century's two great theories of value and distribution both stumbled on the measurement of capital, and the twentieth century's rigorous work consisted largely of mapping the stumble.

The survey draws two conclusions from the episode. The first is comparative comfort of a strictly limited kind: the economic family's verdicts do not record a unique incompetence, since the measurement problem that broke the transformation also broke the rival's favourite parable, and those partly answered grades sit beside a rival literature whose own capital theory survived by retreating into disaggregated models with narrower claims, the same narrowing at the same price. The second conclusion is about provenance once more. The Cambridge critique was powered by Sraffa's reconstruction of the classical surplus approach, the same reconstruction from which Steedman drew the redundancy argument, so that a single research programme, built by scholars formed in or near the Marxian tradition, supplied the century's most damaging audits of both houses' foundations. When the survey claims that the tradition's serious difficulties and its serious contributions are alike internal products, the Cambridge controversy is the exhibit that covers the contributions side at the highest technical level: the auditors of Marxism and the auditors of its rivals were, in this episode, the same people, using the same tools, in the same journal pages.

## The manuscripts and their editors: textual criticism as doctrinal criticism

A survey of criticisms must include the criticism that operates at the level of the text itself, because the doctrine's object turned out to be less stable than its readers assumed. The second and third volumes of Capital were edited by Engels from manuscripts Marx left in varying states of completion, and the scholarly publication of the full manuscript record, in the Marx-Engels collected works project known as the MEGA, allowed a generation of textual scholars to compare what Marx drafted with what Engels printed. The comparisons fed a critical school, the new reading of Marx associated with Hans-Georg Backhaus's work of 1969 and Helmut Reichelt's of 1970 and developed by Michael Heinrich and others, whose charge was double-edged in the survey's favourite pattern. Against the orthodoxy, the new reading argued that the received doctrine, with its worldview of dialectical laws and its engineering model of value, was substantially Engels's construction, a systematisation the manuscripts do not sustain. Against the critics, the same scholarship implied that several famous refutations had been aimed at the edition rather than the drafts, at the catechism's Marx rather than the manuscripts'. The episode matters beyond philology for two reasons. It demonstrates that the target discipline of this survey must extend below the level of doctrines to the level of documents: a grading that does not know which text it grades will convict and acquit the wrong authors. And it exhibits the internal origin in its scholarly purest form, since the new reading was produced by Marxist philologists whose reverence for the founder took the form of dissolving the founder's church. The scorecard's rows were graded against the strongest textual basis available in each case, and the reader who pursues the valuation debates will find the manuscript scholarship waiting there, since the temporal school's case rests partly on readings the MEGA record made possible.

## Crisis theory and its critics: three mechanisms, three audits

The doctrine's account of capitalist crisis attracted a criticism of a distinctive kind, not that crises fail to occur but that the tradition never settled which of its several mechanisms causes them, and the unsettled state is itself the charge. Three mechanisms compete in the literature. Underconsumption, descending from the reproduction schemes and given classic form by Rosa Luxemburg's Accumulation of Capital of 1913, locates the crisis in the system's need for demand outside itself, since workers cannot buy back the full product and capitalists' consumption is limited; Luxemburg's version required an external market of non-capitalist strata, and its critics, including the Bolshevik reviewers and Bukharin's systematic reply, charged that the schemes themselves show expanded reproduction proceeding without the external vent, so the necessity she asserted does not follow. Disproportionality locates the crisis in the anarchic coordination of interdependent sectors, where investment decisions taken separately produce gluts and shortages that generalise; its critics note that disproportionality explains the cycle's occasion better than its necessity, since a price system exists precisely to correct disproportions and usually does. The profit squeeze, given form by Andrew Glyn and Bob Sutcliffe in their study of British capitalism in 1972, locates the crisis in the distributional struggle itself, full employment strengthening labour until profits are squeezed and investment collapses; its critics, from within the same broad tradition, objected that the mechanism makes crisis a product of the workers' success and predicts recovery through their defeat, an implication its authors accepted and its opponents found politically and analytically perverse.

The audit of the three mechanisms illustrates the survey's grading better than any single row. Each mechanism identifies a real pathology, documented in real crises. None has secured the tradition's own assent as the mechanism, and the attempts to combine them, crisis as the form in which all the system's contradictions are violently reconciled, have the unfalsifiable flavour the philosophical family grades: a theory of crisis compatible with crisis arriving through any channel predicts the fact of crisis, which no one disputes, and nothing about its timing, depth, or sector, which is what a theory would be for. The defenders' strongest reply is comparative: rival traditions have their own unsettled crisis literatures, the Keynesian and monetary accounts disagreeing as cheerfully, and the 2008 crisis found the mainstream's models as unprepared as any. The survey accepts the comparison and grades accordingly, within the historical family's profile: the framework's insistence that crisis is endogenous, a property of the system rather than a visitation upon it, has been absorbed into the general understanding so completely that its origin is forgotten, while its specific mechanisms remain, after a century, a contested inheritance among its own heirs. Both halves of that sentence are the criticism's achievement and its limit.

## Exploitation after the theorems: Roemer's reconstruction

The economic family's story has a final chapter that belongs to the internal origin as much as to economics, because it shows an analytical Marxist rebuilding the doctrine's moral core and discovering, in the rebuilding, that the core did not need the doctrine's machinery. John Roemer's A General Theory of Exploitation and Class of 1982 set out to give exploitation a rigorous foundation using game theory and general equilibrium models. His method was to define exploitation through property relations rather than through labour transfers: a group is exploited if it would do better by withdrawing from the economy with its per capita share of society's assets. The reconstruction succeeded on its own terms and then delivered its author's uncomfortable findings. Exploitation so defined does not require the labour theory of value at all, since the withdrawal test operates on assets and endowments directly. It can arise under socialism, where unequal skills and organisational assets generate their own exploitative transfers, a result Roemer stated plainly as socialist exploitation. And its moral force derives not from the labour embodied in commodities but from the prior distribution of assets, which relocates the normative question from the factory floor to the justice of initial endowments, precisely the relocation the normative family performs from its side.

Roemer's trajectory after the reconstruction completes the exhibit. His subsequent work rebuilt egalitarian theory on foundations owing more to Rawls and to mechanism design than to Capital, and he has described his own path as one in which the Marxist questions survived while the Marxist answers were replaced one by one. Within the economic family's existing rows is where this survey files the episode, since it is the redundancy argument's constructive sequel: Steedman showed the value magnitudes could be dispensed with for prices, Roemer showed they could be dispensed with for exploitation itself, and the two results together mark the point at which the doctrine's economic core passed, in its rigorous form, into the general theory of distributive justice, where it is worked on by scholars who file it under political philosophy rather than under Marxism. A defence that celebrates the survival of the exploitation concept should record the address at which it now lives.

## The Keynesian neighbour: criticism from the left's other house

The economic family has a neighbour whose criticisms are easy to overlook because they came from a house that shared many of the doctrine's aims. Joan Robinson's Essay on Marxian Economics of 1942, written by Keynes's most formidable Cambridge disciple, performed an operation no one else had attempted so cleanly: she separated Marx's questions from his apparatus and graded them inversely. The questions, about accumulation, about the realisation of surplus in demand, about the dynamics of a system that produces its own crises, she treated as the work of a genius from whom Keynesians had much to learn, remarking in substance that Marx had seen problems the orthodox economics of his day could not state. The apparatus, the labour theory of value as a system of equations, she treated as scaffolding that obscured the building, a metaphysical detour around problems that could be handled directly in terms of prices, wages, and effective demand. The verdict from the neighbouring house was thus the redundancy argument in an earlier and friendlier dress, delivered fourteen years before Samuelson's eraser and thirty-five before Steedman's proof, by an economist who wanted the doctrine's insights preserved and its machinery retired.

Michal Kalecki supplies the neighbour's second voice and the internal pattern's borderline case. The Polish economist derived the essentials of the theory of effective demand independently of Keynes in papers of 1933 to 1935, working from Marx's reproduction schemes, which he treated as the correct starting point that Marx himself had failed to develop. Kalecki's relation to the doctrine was that of an heir who rebuilt the inheritance: he kept the class analysis of distribution, the reproduction framework, and the insistence that investment drives the system, while discarding the value apparatus entirely and subjecting socialist planning, in his later work on growth and on the political constraints of full employment, to the same unsentimental analysis he applied to capitalism. His Political Aspects of Full Employment of 1943, predicting that sustained full employment would be resisted by business for disciplinary rather than economic reasons, is the kind of specified, risky claim the philosophical family asks for, and it came from the Marxian stable. The neighbour's lesson, for this survey, is that the economic family's verdicts are not a verdict on Marx's economic questions, which the century's best economists kept confiscating for their own use. They are a verdict on the specific machinery of value and transformation, and the confiscations are themselves part of the evidence: a research programme whose questions are stolen by its rivals while its machinery is retired has undergone the naturalisation the objection section describes, and the scorecard's partly answered grades are the paperwork of that process.

## The transformation debate's long century: replies and their price

The transformation problem deserves a second look in this survey, not for its algebra, which lives in the dedicated articles, but for what its century-long career reveals about how criticism and defence actually proceed in this literature. The problem was posed in its modern form by Bortkiewicz in 1907, in a journal few Marxists read, and it waited a generation for its audience. Paul Sweezy carried it into English in The Theory of Capitalist Development of 1942, presenting Bortkiewicz's solution respectfully as the correct completion of Marx's procedure, and thereby, perhaps without intending it, certifying that a problem existed which the completion had to solve. Ronald Meek's Studies in the Labour Theory of Value of 1956 defended the doctrine by distinguishing its qualitative claim, that labour is the substance of value, from its quantitative exercises, a defence that conceded the terrain on which the quantitative grading would occur. The pattern of the whole century is visible in those two moves: the tradition's own scholars transmitted the criticism because suppressing it was impossible, and defended the doctrine by relocating its centre of gravity, because defending the original position entire had become impossible too.

The nineteen seventies brought the debate to its technical climax and produced its most interesting single result. Michio Morishima, a Japanese economist of Marxist formation, working with the von Neumann growth model, proved in Marx's Economics of 1973 the proposition he called the Fundamental Marxian Theorem: in a wide class of models, the rate of profit is positive if and only if the rate of exploitation is positive, so that profit exists exactly when surplus value does. The theorem mattered because it rescued the moral and analytical core of the exploitation claim, profit rests on unpaid labour, from the wreckage of the transformation procedure: even if prices cannot be derived from values in the manner Marx sketched, the correspondence between profit and surplus labour survives rigorous restatement. Defenders celebrated the result, and they were entitled to. But the theorem's price is written into its method. Morishima's proof works in a model where values and prices are computed in parallel from the physical data, which is precisely Steedman's architecture: the correspondence is established by computing both systems independently, not by deriving one from the other. The Fundamental Marxian Theorem thus confirms the exploitation correspondence while quietly accepting the redundancy architecture, a rescue that saves the conclusion by surrendering the derivation. Anwar Shaikh's work of the same decade, arguing that an iterative transformation converges on the correct prices and that the deviations are empirically small, defended the derivation more directly, and the dispute between the parallel-computation school and the iterative school is the form the argument still takes in the journals.

Samuelson's eraser belongs in this history as the external challenge that forced the pace. His 1957 paper invited the reader to write down the value tableau, then take an eraser and rub it out, then write down the price tableau, claiming the value stage contributed nothing the price stage needed. The provocation stung because it dramatised Steedman's later point in a newspaper version, and the replies it drew, that the eraser assumes competitive conditions Marx was analysing rather than presupposing, that the value tableau records social relations the price tableau merely registers, clarified what the defence had become: an argument that the value analysis does different work, not the same work better. The survey's grading of the economic rows rests on this whole history. The replies are not failures; several are permanent contributions to economic theory, and the Fundamental Marxian Theorem would be a credit to any research programme. But a century in which every generation's ablest defenders preserve the doctrine by relocating its claims is a century whose direction the scorecard is obliged to record. The partly answered verdicts on the economic rows are that record, kept without pleasure and without exaggeration.

### What did Morishima's Fundamental Marxian Theorem rescue, and at what price?

It rescued the correspondence at the doctrine's moral core: profit is positive exactly when surplus value is positive, so exploitation accompanies profit in a formal model. The price was architectural. The proof computes values and prices in parallel from physical data, accepting the structure in which value magnitudes appear as parallel accounting rather than the source from which prices derive.

## The philosophical family: testability, explanation, and the dialectic

The philosophical family engages not what the doctrine says but how it claims to know. Its four leading arguments are the falsifiability charge, the critique of historicism, the attack on functional explanation, and the dialectics question. Two of the four were pressed hardest from inside the movement that called itself analytical Marxism, and the family's history therefore repeats the survey's central pattern in its purest form: the most damaging methodological criticisms were not misunderstandings by opponents but audits by practitioners who had decided to hold the tradition to the standards it claimed for itself.

Karl Popper supplied the family's best known external argument. In The Open Society and Its Enemies of 1945 and in The Poverty of Historicism, published as a book in 1957, he charged that historical materialism in its orthodox form was a historicism: a doctrine claiming to discover laws of historical development that license prophecy, and prophecy of a kind that no evidence could embarrass, because apparent refutations were absorbed by reinterpretation. In the lectures collected in Conjectures and Refutations in 1963, Popper sharpened the point against Marxism specifically. The theory, he granted, had begun as science: it made risky predictions, and the predictions failed, whereupon its followers re-interpreted both theory and evidence until the two agreed again, rescuing the doctrine at the cost of its scientific standing. The manoeuvre Popper described, in which a theory is protected by ad hoc adjustments that explain away every failure, is the seed of the strongest objection this article states in full later, the immunisation charge, and the philosophical family's grades cannot be understood apart from it.

Imre Lakatos, who had been a communist activist in Hungary before becoming a philosopher of science in London, gave the charge its most precise form. In his lecture on science and pseudoscience of 1973 and in the methodology of scientific research programmes he built around it, Lakatos replaced Popper's single-theory test with a comparative one: a programme is scientific while its successive reformulations predict novel facts, and degenerating when its reformulations only accommodate facts already known, trailing behind the evidence and explaining it after the fact. He named Marxism as his standing example of degeneration, asking what novel fact it had predicted since the nineteen twenties and listing the auxiliary hypotheses with which its failures had been papered over. The formulation matters because it concedes what Popper's cruder versions did not: reformulation is normal in science, and every living programme does it. The charge is not that Marxists reformulated. The charge is that the reformulations bought consistency at the price of content, each rescue explaining less than the version it replaced. Whether that charge is just is the question the scorecard's philosophical rows grade, and [the article on whether Marxism is a science](/2019/10/01/is-marxism-a-science/) gives the question its fuller hearing.

### What did Popper object to in historicism, beyond Marxism itself?

Historicism, as Popper defined it, is the claim that history unfolds by discoverable laws that permit large-scale prophecy, and his objection was twofold: the growth of knowledge helps drive history and cannot itself be predicted, and a prophecy protected against every outcome has stopped making contact with evidence. Marxism was his chief exhibit, not his only target.

The attack on functional explanation was mounted from within analytical Marxism and is the family's clearest internal episode. G. A. Cohen's Karl Marx's Theory of History: A Defence of 1978 had rebuilt historical materialism as a set of functional explanations: the relations of production obtain because they suit the development of the productive forces, and superstructures obtain because they stabilise the relations. Jon Elster, then working inside the same analytical project, objected in Making Sense of Marx of 1985 that functional explanation in social science is legitimate only where a feedback mechanism sustains the item explained by its consequences, as natural selection does in biology, and that Cohen's constructions named no such mechanism: they inferred a cause from a beneficial effect and stopped. Cohen replied with elaborations and concessions across the following decade, accepting that functional claims invite a demand for the mechanisms beneath them while defending the claims as legitimate promissory structures. The episode's significance for the survey is that the demand Elster articulated, show the mechanism, was absorbed by the tradition itself and is now the standard its own practitioners apply. A criticism that reforms the criticised practice from inside has done something stronger than refutation: it has changed what counts as a defence.

### Why did the functional explanation dispute matter outside Marxist circles?

Because it set a standard for every social theory that explains an institution by the good it does. Elster's demand, that a consequence explanation name the feedback loop sustaining it, travelled into sociology, political science, and evolutionary theorising generally, and it was forged in an argument between two scholars who both described their project as Marxist.

The dialectics question is the family's oldest member and its least settled. Marx's presentation in Capital moves by contradiction and sublation, categories are developed through their opposites, and the Hegelian inheritance is visible on the page. Critics from several directions have asked what this machinery contributes that plainer reasoning cannot supply, and whether its characteristic moves, the unity of opposites, the negation of the negation, the transformation of quantity into quality, have definite content or function as licences to assert and deny in the same breath. Elster's impatience in Making Sense of Marx was representative of the analytical verdict: where a dialectical passage could be translated into ordinary causal or logical claims, the translation lost nothing, and where it could not, the passage was obscure rather than deep. Defenders reply that the dialectic names real features of social processes, feedback, unintended inversion, development through internal tension, that snapshot categories flatten. Partly answered, in a peculiar direction, is the grade this survey assigns the question: the analytical wing of the tradition itself largely abandoned the machinery while keeping the name available for ceremonial use, and a method one's own ablest practitioners set aside has been graded by the severest tribunal available, its users.

Assembled, the philosophical family's findings are narrower than its reputation. The falsifiability charge in Popper's form overshoots, because frameworks are not single predictions and no serious philosophy of science grades them so simply; in Lakatos's form it lands, and the scorecard records the landing. The mechanism demand landed and was absorbed. The dialectics question was settled less by argument than by migration, as the tradition's rigorous wing walked away from the contested ground. What the family does not establish, despite frequent claims, is that the framework is empty: a research programme can be degenerating in Lakatos's sense and still contain explanations that outperform rivals on particular material, which is why the scorecard grades the framework comparatively rather than by the family's verdict alone.

## Progress audited: the Frankfurt School's quarrel with the inheritance

The tradition's most anguished internal criticism took aim not at a claim but at a faith, the faith that history's direction is the ally of emancipation, and it was written by exiles watching the faith's homeland deliver itself to barbarism. Max Horkheimer and Theodor Adorno composed Dialectic of Enlightenment in American exile, published in 1947, and its argument turned the framework's instruments on the Enlightenment inheritance the framework shared: reason, developed as the domination of nature, becomes the domination of human beings, and the administered world of monopoly capitalism and the culture industry is not progress's interruption but one of its products. Walter Benjamin's theses on the philosophy of history, written in 1940 as he fled the Gestapo, had already refused the consolation in its social democratic form: the faith in progress, he argued, had corrupted the workers' movement itself, teaching it to swim with a current that was carrying it toward the catastrophe, and his image of the angel of history, blown backward into the future by the storm called progress while the wreckage piles before him, is the tradition's most famous internal indictment of its own philosophy of history. The survey places the episode carefully. The Frankfurt audit does not refute a claim and is not graded on a row; it transformed what the tradition's serious members could say in their own defence, since after it, the appeal to history's direction, the oldest consolation in the movement's rhetoric, was available only to those who had not read their own side's best writers. Popper's historicism charge attacked the faith from outside as bad science. The Frankfurt School mourned it from inside as a catastrophe of reason. The two criticisms converge on the same target from opposite directions, and their convergence is the philosophical family's deepest result: the doctrine's philosophy of history was audited by its enemies as unfalsifiable and by its heirs as unforgivable, and it survives, where it survives, chastened into the fallibilism the survey has repeatedly found the tradition's best work quietly adopting.

## The class actor problem: who exactly acts in the theory of history?

A quiet criticism runs beneath the philosophical family's louder ones and disables, if it stands, a habit of speech the whole tradition depends on. Historical materialism's narratives assign actions to classes: the bourgeoisie makes its revolution, the proletariat organises, the peasantry wavers. The criticism, pressed by Elster and by the methodological individualists, and felt by every historian who has tried to use the narratives, is that classes do not act. Classes are categories of people sharing a position; action requires organisation, decision, and enforcement, which are supplied by parties, unions, armies, and states, entities with their own interests and dynamics that cannot be read off from the class they claim to represent. Between the class and its supposed deed there always stands an organisation, and the organisation is where the explanatory action is. The point sounds pedantic and is not. It is the analytical form of the political family's substitutionism: the same gap through which the party substituted itself for the class in practice is the gap the theory papers over in its narratives, treating the representative's acts as the represented's own. A framework that cannot distinguish a class from its self-appointed voice will misread both its history and its politics in the same way, and the critics who pressed the point, from Michels on organisations to Elster on explanation, were pressing one point in two vocabularies.

The tradition's replies are instructive in their variety. The Lukácsian reply grants the gap and fills it with the party as the bearer of the class's imputed consciousness, which states the substitution as a theory and thereby exhibits what the critics object to. The Thompsonian reply fills the gap historically: class is not a thing but a relationship and a happening, made in struggle by people who come to handle their experience in class ways, so that the narrative's subject is real but must be shown being made, never assumed. The analytical reply rebuilds the narratives with the organisations inside them, modelling incentives and collective action problems explicitly, at the price of the sweep that made the originals stirring. Absorbed in the strong sense is how this survey grades the criticism: no serious practitioner now writes the unmediated class narrative without embarrassment, the organisations have moved to the centre of the framework's own best work, and the slogan form, in which classes stride across history like persons, survives mainly in the catechism and in the criticisms aimed at the catechism. The episode is the philosophical family's cleanest example of a criticism that won by being conceded, and it pairs with the mechanism demand as the analytical school's joint legacy to every social theory that traffics in collective subjects.

## False consciousness and the evidence problem

One concept in the framework attracts a criticism so compact that the philosophical family gives it a section of its own, because the criticism, if sound, disables a tool the tradition uses daily. The concept is false consciousness: the claim that subordinated groups systematically misperceive their interests, taking the dominant order's categories for their own, so that their expressed beliefs cannot be taken at face value. The criticism is not that the phenomenon is unreal; advertising, deference, and the internalisation of hierarchy are documented by every neighbouring discipline. The criticism is that the concept as used is evidentially rigged. If the workers agree with the analysis, their agreement confirms it; if they disagree, their disagreement confirms it too, as the symptom the concept predicts, and no statement by the people concerned can count against the attribution, since the attribution explains in advance why they would make it. A tool that converts every objection into evidence has the immunising structure in miniature, and it places the analyst in the position the political family found at the level of the state: the vanguard's epistemic privilege, the right to override what people say about themselves in the name of what they would say if they saw clearly.

The tradition's best heirs felt the difficulty and rebuilt the concept with the rigging removed. Lukács's original construction had already located the standard not in the analyst's opinion but in the consciousness appropriate to the class's objective position, imputed rather than polled, which sharpened the theory and worsened the evidence problem in the same gesture. Later work in the sociology of knowledge and in ideology critique proceeded differently, specifying mechanisms, schooling, media ownership, workplace authority, residential segregation, through which beliefs are actually formed, and testing the mechanisms' effects against evidence that could have come out otherwise. Gramsci's concept of hegemony, with its attention to the institutions of civil society and to the contradictory consciousness of subordinated groups, who hold the dominant common sense and their own good sense in unstable combination, supplied the richest version of the rebuilt approach, and its superiority to the slogan form is precisely that it describes a contest with evidence on both sides rather than a diagnosis immune to the patient's testimony. With the philosophical rows is where the concept is graded here: the slogan form stands condemned by the family's own standards, the mechanistic form survives as ordinary ideology critique, and the distance between them is, once more, the price the criticism exacted and the survey's business to record.

## The determination problem: base, superstructure, and the last instance

Beneath the philosophical family's named arguments sits a vagueness its critics regard as the framework's original sin, and the survey gives it a section because the tradition's own history keeps returning to it. Historical materialism's central image is architectural: a base of production relations on which rises a superstructure of law, politics, and ideology, with the base determining the superstructure in some sense the texts never finally specify. The criticisms write themselves in ascending order of severity. If determination means strict causation, the claim is false, since legal and political forms demonstrably react upon and reshape production relations, a fact Marx's own historical writings display on every page. If determination means only that the base sets limits or exerts pressure, the claim is true but weak, compatible with almost any historical outcome and therefore, by the falsifiability standard, nearly empty. If determination operates in the last instance, the question is when the last instance arrives, and Althusser, the doctrine's most sophisticated defender on this point, conceded in For Marx of 1965 that the lonely hour of the last instance never comes, a concession his critics received as the vagueness confessing itself in the defender's own prose.

Engels saw the difficulty coming and attempted the repair himself, in the letters of his last years, most famously to Joseph Bloch in 1890, where he insisted that the economic moment is the ultimately determining one while superstructural elements exercise their own influence and can predominate in form, and protested that he and Marx were partly to blame for the younger writers' mechanical version. The letters are the tradition's first internal criticism of its own vulgarisation, and they set the pattern the survey has tracked throughout: the repair is genuine, the repair is a narrowing, and the narrowed claim, determination as ultimate constraint within reciprocal action, is one that many non-Marxist historians could sign without noticing they had signed anything Marxist. Cohen's functional reconstruction was the analytical school's attempt to give the narrowed claim rigorous content, and Elster's mechanism demand was the audit of that attempt, so the determination problem is the thread on which the family's whole modern history is strung. The survey's grade for the thread is the family's grade in miniature: the strong form indefensible, the weak form absorbed into the common practice of social explanation, and the gap between the two forms measures what the criticism achieved.

### What did Engels's late letters concede to the critics?

They conceded that the mechanical version of the doctrine, in which the economy produces politics and ideas as a stamp produces impressions, was a distortion for which the founders bore some blame, and that superstructures act back upon the base with real force. The concession narrowed determination to an ultimate constraint, which is the form in which the claim survives.

## The dialectics of nature: the house's divided inheritance

The dialectics question has a second front, quieter than the Capital presentation debates and in some ways more revealing, because it divided the tradition against itself for a century. Engels, in Anti-Dühring of 1878 and in the manuscripts published as Dialectics of Nature, extended the dialectic beyond society into nature itself: motion, life, and matter were said to exhibit the same laws of contradiction, negation, and qualitative leap. The Soviet codification canonised the extension as dialectical materialism, the official philosophy of the party-states, taught as a catechism with the three laws memorised like a creed. Western Marxism, from its founding generation, largely repudiated the extension. Lukács, in a celebrated footnote in History and Class Consciousness of 1923, restricted the dialectic to the social and historical world, where subject and object interact, and treated Engels's natural dialectic as a misunderstanding of the method's essence. The repudiation was itself an internal criticism of the founders by their most gifted heir, and it split the inheritance permanently: the Eastern parties canonised what the Western philosophers discarded, and each side regarded the other's Marxism as the heresy.

The critics' reading of the episode is straightforward, and this survey endorses it. A method whose domain its own masters cannot agree on, society only or nature too, and whose natural form functioned in the party-states as an instrument for policing science, most notoriously in the Lysenko affair, where genetics was condemned as bourgeois and Soviet biology was wrecked for a generation by decree, has failed the test the philosophical family applies everywhere: it did not constrain its users, it licensed them. The Lysenko episode belongs also to the political family, since its mechanism was the party's monopoly, but its philosophical lesson is the family's: a dialectic that can certify any result the authorities require has the precise property Popper charged against the whole framework, compatibility with every outcome. Jean-Paul Sartre's Critique of Dialectical Reason of 1960 attempted the rescue from the Western side, rebuilding dialectics as the logic of praxis and scarcity rather than a law of nature, and the rescue's difficulty and obscurity are themselves data. The survey closes the family's account here: on method, the tradition's rigorous heirs kept the questions, accepted the audit standards of mechanism and evidence, and let the dialectical machinery, natural and much of the social, go quiet. Traditions vote with their practice, and the practice has voted.

## Laws, trends, and the prophecy charge

Popper's critique of historicism contains a distinction that outlasted his polemic and that any fair grading of the philosophical family must apply, because the tradition's defenders and critics both profit from blurring it. A law, in Popper's usage, is unconditional and universal; a trend is a summary of what has been happening under conditions that may change. Prophecy mistakes trends for laws, extrapolating a direction of development into a destiny. Applied to Marx, the distinction cuts the corpus unevenly, and the unevenness is the finding. Marx's careful work is full of tendencies explicitly stated with their counteracting influences: the falling rate of profit chapter is titled by its tendencies and counter-tendencies, and the analysis of concentration proceeds by opposed pressures. The movement's catechism, by contrast, converted tendencies into timetables: the collapse was coming, the polarisation was proceeding, the revolution was on history's agenda, and generations of adherents were taught the converted versions. Popper's charge lands with full force on the catechism and with reduced force on the texts, and the survey's grading reflects the split. Where the philosophical family is graded partly answered rather than standing, it is often because the defence consists of returning from the catechism to the texts, a legitimate move that nonetheless concedes how much of the tradition's public doctrine the charge destroys.

The concept of ideology carries a parallel difficulty, pressed most elegantly from a neighbouring tradition. Marxist analysis explains beliefs by the social positions of their holders: ideas are interested, consciousness is conditioned, and the ruling ideas of an age are the ideas of its ruling class. Karl Mannheim's Ideology and Utopia of 1929 turned the instrument around: if all thought is existentially conditioned, the sociology of knowledge is conditioned too, and the analyst's exemption of his own position from the analysis is an unearned privilege. The tradition's replies, that the proletarian standpoint is epistemically privileged because the proletariat has no interest in illusion, or that the party embodies the class's consciousness in corrected form, were developed by Georg Lukács in his History and Class Consciousness into the doctrine of imputed consciousness, and they have persuaded few outside the tradition, since the privilege is asserted by the very theory it is meant to validate. The difficulty is genuine and the survey records it as part of the philosophical family's unfinished business: a theory of ideology that cannot state its own exemption without circularity must either accept a general fallibilism, in which its claims are as revisable as everyone else's, or claim a standpoint whose credentials it cannot exhibit. Much of the best later work in the tradition quietly chose the fallibilism, which is another way of saying the criticism was absorbed by being conceded.

The family's internal history has one more episode the survey should not omit, because it shows the mechanism demand arriving from the historians before the philosophers formalised it. E. P. Thompson's The Poverty of Theory of 1978, a polemic of extraordinary violence and learning, attacked the structural Marxism of Louis Althusser and his school from within Marxist historiography, charging that its theoreticism had evacuated history of agency, evidence, and moral judgement, replacing the discipline's empirical procedures with a machinery of concepts that generated its conclusions from its definitions. Thompson wrote as a Marxist defending a Marxist practice, historical scholarship, against a Marxist philosophy, and his book is the historians' counterpart to Elster's audit: the demand that the framework's abstractions answer to material they did not themselves produce. Between Thompson's polemic and Elster's analysis, the philosophical family's deepest criticisms were an inside job twice over, delivered by the tradition's historian and its philosopher against its reigning orthodoxies. The scorecard's philosophical rows, graded partly answered, record a family whose charges were absorbed as standards: mechanism, evidence, fallibilism. Absorption is the strongest form a criticism can take. It is also, the survey notes without needing to labour the point, indistinguishable at a glance from the reformulation pattern the objection section examines, and the difference between the two, absorption that raises a tradition's standards against absorption that lowers its claims, is a judgement the reader is now equipped to make row by row.

## The historical family: the predictions and the record

The historical family is the most straightforward to grade and the most damaging where it lands, because its material is the public record. Marx and the tradition made predictions: specific, directional claims about where capitalism was going. The family assembles the leading ones, immiseration, class polarisation, revolution arriving in the advanced countries, the tendency of profit rates to fall, and the withering away of the state, and sets each against what happened in the century and a half after it was made. The grading must be careful in both directions. A prediction hedged by its author cannot be graded as though it were absolute, and a prediction that failed in its strong form cannot be rescued by pointing to a weak form its author would have scorned. The family's standard is the one the tradition's own best critics applied: take the prediction as its serious proponents stated it, in the texts and programmes that organised movements, and ask what the record did to it.

Immiseration is the family's founding case, and its founding critic was again an insider. Eduard Bernstein, a leader of German social democracy and an executor of Engels's literary estate, published The Preconditions of Socialism in 1899 after a series of articles in the party's theoretical journal, and argued from the evidence available to him that the catastrophe theory was failing its tests: real wages were rising rather than falling, the middle strata were growing rather than vanishing, share ownership was diffusing rather than concentrating into fewer hands, and crises, while real, were being moderated by credit, cartels, and the world market rather than sharpening toward collapse. Rosa Luxemburg answered in Reform or Revolution in the same year with the movement's classic rebuttal, arguing that Bernstein's adaptations were the system's temporary expedients and that he had mistaken the postponement of the reckoning for its cancellation. The dispute is the template for the family's whole method: a prediction, an internal critic with statistics, a defender with a mechanism, and a record that kept arriving. Graded across the long run, the absolute form of immiseration, the claim that the working class's condition would sink as capitalism developed, failed in the advanced economies: the century's trajectory of real consumption, longevity, and leisure for wage earners in the industrial countries runs the other way. Relative forms, concerning shares of income or security rather than absolute condition, describe a more contested record and are graded separately on the scorecard.

### How should a failed prediction be weighed against a confirmed one?

By the risk each ran when it was made. A prediction earns credit in proportion to what it excluded: a forecast that ruled out the outcome that occurred loses heavily, while a forecast compatible with every outcome earns nothing when one arrives. Weighing by risk is what separates grading a research programme from collecting its greatest hits.

The prediction of revolution in the advanced industrial countries carries the family's heaviest weight, because it was not a marginal corollary but the framework's central historical expectation: the countries where capitalism was most developed, the working class largest and best organised, and the contradictions most mature would be the countries where the transition began. The record delivered nearly the opposite geography. Successful revolutions in the tradition's name occurred in Russia, China, and other societies where industrial capitalism was shallow, carried by peasant majorities and parties organised for seizure rather than by the mature proletarian majorities the theory had cast in the leading role, while the advanced countries, Germany above all, where the largest Marxist party in the world operated, produced reform, catastrophe of a different authorship, and eventually social democratic government within capitalism. Defenders reply that the prediction was conditional, that Marx allowed for uneven development, and that Lenin's theory of imperialism explained the displacement of revolution to the periphery as a development of the framework rather than an evasion of it. The reply is a genuine development and the scorecard credits it as one; it is also, in Lakatos's terms, a reformulation produced after the failure it explains, and the grading records both facts. What the record does not contain, anywhere in the advanced industrial world across a century and a half, is the event the original expectation described, and the scorecard's verdict on that row is standing.

The falling tendency of the rate of profit illustrates a different grading problem, the prediction whose derivation was damaged from inside. Marx's third volume argued that as the organic composition of capital rises, the rate of profit tends to fall unless counteracting tendencies intervene. In 1961 the Japanese Marxist economist Nobuo Okishio proved the theorem that bears his name: given a constant real wage, cost-reducing technical changes that capitalists would individually choose raise, rather than lower, the equilibrium rate of profit, so the tendency cannot be derived from rational technique choice alone. The result did not end the argument; defenders relocated the tendency's source to rising real wages, to the composition of demand, or to the temporal valuation conventions defended by the single-system school. But the relocation is the finding: the prediction in its derived form depended on a step that a Marxist economist showed to be invalid, and its surviving forms are different claims wearing the original's name. The withering of the state, finally, is graded with the political family, since its failure is measured not in economic series but in the apparatuses the revolutionary states built, and the two families share that row's evidence.

One discipline governs the whole family's use, and the survey states it plainly because popular writing violates it in both directions. The failure of a prediction is evidence against the framework that generated it, weighted by the prediction's centrality and by the risk it ran. It is not, by itself, a refutation of every claim the framework contains, any more than a confirmed prediction would validate the whole. Conversely, the collapse of the states that ruled in the tradition's name is not, without the connecting argument, a verdict on the theory: states fall for many reasons, and the argument that connects a state's fall to a doctrine's falsity must specify the mechanism, as the political family's treatment of the authoritarianism charge specifies it. Readers who want the states' record itself, rather than its bearing on predictions, are routed by the political family to the articles that hold that evidence.

## The Asiatic mode: the category the orthodoxy suppressed

The historical family contains one episode in which the criticism aimed not at a prediction but at a category, and the orthodoxy's handling of it belongs to the survey's evidence about how the tradition policed itself. Marx's scheme of modes included, alongside slavery, feudalism, and capitalism, an Asiatic mode: societies organised around irrigated agriculture, a state standing over self-sufficient villages, and the absence of private landed property in the Western form. The category was always unstable in the texts, and it carried an implication the later orthodoxy found intolerable, since a state that owns the land, commands the works, and faces no independent proprietors resembles nothing so much as the destination the Soviet order was accused of reaching. In the Leningrad discussions of 1931 the category was effectively condemned within Soviet scholarship, and the official scheme hardened into the five-stage sequence through which every society must pass, a sequence whose universality the Asiatic exception would have broken. The suppression is the datum: a scholarly category was retired by institutional decision because its implications were dangerous, and the retirement was carried out by the tradition's own academy against the tradition's own founder.

Karl Wittfogel then performed the manoeuvre the orthodoxy had feared. Wittfogel, a German scholar formed in the communist movement and associated in the nineteen twenties with the Frankfurt Institute's circle, broke with communism and spent his exile building the hydraulic thesis: in Oriental Despotism of 1957 he argued that societies organised around large-scale water control generate total power as a structural necessity, and he aimed the category explicitly at the Soviet Union, presenting it as the Asiatic restoration in industrial dress. The book's scholarship was contested, its author's Cold War commitments plain, and its central mechanism, irrigation's administrative demands, has not carried the comparative literature. But the episode's structure is the survey's point. The category the orthodoxy suppressed as dangerous returned as the prosecution's exhibit, wielded by a former insider who knew exactly which shelf it had been removed from. Traditions that retire their own concepts by decree should expect the concepts to be collected by their defectors, and the historical family's ledger records the Asiatic affair as a small, complete model of the internal origin operating in reverse: the criticism the insiders were forbidden to make, made by the insider who left.

### Why was the Asiatic mode of production dangerous to the orthodoxy?

Because it described a social form, state ownership over village communities without independent proprietors, whose resemblance to the Soviet order was available to any reader, and because its existence in Marx's scheme broke the universal five-stage sequence the official doctrine required. The category implied that the destination might be a mode of its own rather than a transition.

## The transition debate: historians auditing the framework's engine room

The historical family's most technical episode is also its most hopeful, because it shows the framework generating first-rate history through its own internal quarrels. The question was the framework's home question: how did feudalism give way to capitalism? Maurice Dobb's Studies in the Development of Capitalism of 1946 located the engine inside feudalism's own contradictions, in the lords' extraction squeezing the peasantry until the relations dissolved. Paul Sweezy, in the exchange that ran through Science and Society in the early nineteen fifties, objected from within the same tradition that Dobb's feudalism was too stable to dissolve itself, and assigned the solvent to trade and the towns, an external shock to a system geared to use rather than exchange. The debate drew in historians across several countries, refined both positions, and left the framework's categories sharper than it found them, which is what a progressive problem shift looks like in Lakatos's sense and the survey credits it as such.

Robert Brenner then reopened the question in 1976 with an internal criticism of both camps and of the demographic school beside them. In his Past and Present article on agrarian class structure, Brenner argued that Dobb, Sweezy, and the neo-Malthusian historians all explained development by factors, trade, population, that operated across Europe with opposite results, while the variable that actually discriminated the outcomes was the class structure of the countryside and the balance of power between lords and peasants: where peasants won property, as in France, development took one road; where lords re-enserfed them, as east of the Elbe, another; where lords lost and peasants were separated from the land, as in England, the capitalist road. The Brenner debate that followed, collected and contested through the following decade, is the historical profession's own verdict on the framework's usefulness: a Marxist historian's refinement of Marxist categories produced the research programme that organised a generation of early modern historiography, including the work of its critics, who had to answer Brenner's questions even when rejecting his answers. The survey enters the episode as the historical family's counterweight exhibit. The framework's predictions about the future failed in their strong forms; its questions about the past, audited and rebuilt by its own practitioners, organised real knowledge. A grading that recorded only the failures would be as false as the catechism that recorded only the triumphs, and the partly answered verdicts are where the sentence's two halves meet.

## The other half of the record: expectations the century bore out

A grading that weighed only failures would commit the mirror image of the sin it corrects, and the historical family's method, weighing by risk, requires the survey to enter the framework's confirmed expectations in the same ledger. Several of Marx's directional claims about capitalism's development were risky when made, were denied by the respectable opinion of their day, and were borne out on a scale their author did not live to see. The concentration and centralisation of capital, the tendency of competition to produce ever larger units and of capitals to absorb one another, describes the trajectory from the workshops of the eighteen forties to the multinational corporation with a fidelity no rival framework of the period matched. The globalisation of production, announced in the Manifesto's account of the bourgeoisie battering down national walls and drawing even the most barbarian nations into civilisation's market, was written when world trade was a fraction of its later extent and reads, against the supply chains of the developed world economy, less like rhetoric than like a prospectus. The recurrence of crisis as a system property rather than a sequence of accidents, the reserve army's pressure on wages, the commodification of domains once outside the market, land, labour, care, attention, each entered the record in forms the framework's categories describe naturally and its rivals' categories had to be extended to reach.

The weighing must be exact, and exactness cuts the credit down to its true size in three ways. First, several confirmed expectations were shared with observers who drew opposite conclusions: concentration was visible to liberal economists too, and credit for a prediction is divided among all who ran its risk. Second, the confirmed expectations concern capitalism's dynamics, the second target's home ground, and they do not offset failures concerning the transition, because the scorecard forbids cross-target arithmetic: a framework can be the best available account of how capitalism develops and a poor guide to what succeeds it, and the record suggests precisely that combination. Third, the tradition's own custodians damaged the credit by the catechism's method, claiming every success as a confirmation of the whole and explaining every failure as a postponement, a procedure that, applied consistently, would make the ledger meaningless. Lakatos's question disciplines both sides: which confirmations were novel, predicted before the fact against a stated alternative, and which were accommodations dressed as trophies? Graded by that standard, concentration and globalisation earn real credit, crisis recurrence earns partial credit shared with underconsumptionist and monetary rivals, and the transition expectations earn none. The historical family's overall profile is therefore not a rout. It is a split decision in which the framework's account of the system it opposed outperformed its account of the future it promised, and a reader who grasps that split has grasped the family's whole lesson.

### Which confirmed expectation earns the framework the most credit?

The internationalisation of production, because it was stated earliest, against the strongest contrary appearance, and in a form specific enough to have been wrong. Concentration of capital runs it close and shares its credit with liberal observers. Both concern capitalism's dynamics rather than the transition, so neither offsets the failures graded on the transition rows.

## The war that broke the International: the first great falsification

The historical family's ledger conventionally opens with Bernstein's statistics, but the framework's first great practical falsification arrived in August 1914, and its critics were, once again, the movement's own. The Second International had resolved at its congresses, Stuttgart in 1907 and Basel in 1912, that its parties would oppose a European war by every means including the general strike, on the framework's reasoning that the proletariat's international solidarity outweighed its national attachments and that the war would be the ruling classes' crime against a class with no fatherland to defend. In the event, the German party's parliamentary fraction voted the war credits, the French socialists entered the union sacrée after Jaurès's assassination, and the International dissolved into its national sections within a week. The internal audit began immediately and produced two of the tradition's classic texts. Luxemburg's Junius Pamphlet of 1915, written in prison, charged the German party with a betrayal that was also a theoretical collapse, the discovery that the organisation built to embody internationalism had become a national institution with internationalist memories. Lenin's writings on the International's collapse drew the organisational conclusion, that the opportunist parties had to be split and a new International built, the conclusion whose institutional career the political family has already graded. The survey's interest is in the falsification's structure. A framework expectation, stated as a resolution and believed as a certainty, failed at the moment of its test, in public, within days. The tradition's responses exhibit the full repertoire the philosophical family catalogued: the expectation was conditionalised, the parties were disowned as unrepresentative of the class, the theory of imperialism was developed to explain the defection by the labour aristocracy's bribery, a genuine theoretical development that also functioned as the failure's accommodation. The grading follows the family's rules. The expectation as stated failed. The development that explained the failure earned partial credit and the standing note that it arrived after the event it explained. And the episode fixed the pattern by which the century's subsequent falsifications, larger in scale, would be processed by defenders and critics alike, which is why the survey gives a week's collapse in 1914 the same analytical care as the decades that followed it.

## Reform's century: the social democratic evidence

Between Bernstein's prediction and the century's end lies a body of evidence neither the revisionists nor their opponents fully anticipated, and the historical family must grade it because both sides claimed it in advance. The social democratic governments of postwar Europe, above all in Scandinavia, delivered much of Bernstein's programme within capitalism: full employment maintained for a generation, welfare states of genuine universality, compressed wage structures, and labour movements integrated into the state's machinery. Luxemburg's counter-prediction, that reforms without the conquest of power would be rolled back when they threatened accumulation, met its test later and partially: the postwar settlement's erosion from the nineteen seventies, under pressure of capital mobility, inflation, and electoral change, displayed exactly the reversibility she insisted on, though at a pace and by mechanisms, democratic elections prominent among them, that her framework had not cast in the leading roles. The Swedish wage-earner funds, the Meidner plan proposed in 1976, are the episode's purest experiment and deserve the survey's attention. Rudolf Meidner, the Swedish labour movement's own economist, designed the funds to convert Bernstein's gradualism into a road across the property line itself: a levy of shares on large firms, paid into collectively held funds, would over decades transfer ownership of Swedish capital to wage earners without expropriation's rupture. The plan was the reformist strategy's most rigorous internal product, and its fate is the strategy's biography in miniature: adopted in principle by the movement, diluted under employer mobilisation and electoral pressure, enacted in 1983 in a form its author disowned, and wound down within the decade. The critics of reform cite the episode as the dilemma Przeworski formalised, operating in the movement's most favourable terrain. The defenders cite the same decades' delivered welfare as the comparison that matters. As evidence for both rows it touches, the episode is graded here: it confirms the reversibility the revolutionaries predicted, and it confirms the deliverability the revisionists promised, and a framework whose two wings each predicted half of the Swedish century has been graded, by that century, as divided against itself in fact as well as in programme.

## The peasantry problem: the class that refused to disappear

One expectation of the framework failed so instructively that the historical family gives it separate treatment, because its failure redirected the tradition's whole practical history. Marx expected the peasantry to dissolve under capitalist development, ground between the millstones of landlord and market, its smallholdings consolidated, its people proletarianised, and he wrote of the French peasantry with a famous impatience as a class formed by mere addition, like potatoes in a sack. The dissolution proceeded in some places and stalled in others, and where it stalled it stalled for centuries: family farming proved adaptive, subsidised, politically defended, and stubbornly persistent across the advanced economies, while in the vast agrarian societies of Asia and Latin America the peasantry remained the majority of humanity deep into the era of the revolutions made in Marxism's name. Karl Kautsky's Agrarian Question of 1899, the tradition's most serious internal engagement with the problem, documented the small farm's unexpected resilience even while predicting its eventual subordination, and the Russian debates added Alexander Chayanov's analysis of the family farm's own logic, an economics of subsistence and drudgery that obeyed neither capitalist nor simple commodity rules, written by a scholar the Soviet state eventually arrested and destroyed.

The political consequences of the failed expectation were the century's largest irony, and the critics have not been slow to state it. The revolutions that succeeded did so by reversing the theory's assignment of roles: Lenin's worker-peasant alliance, Mao's protracted war from the countryside, the Vietnamese and Cuban adaptations, all made the peasantry the revolution's mass base and the industrial working class its junior partner or its symbol. Defenders count the adaptations as the framework's flexibility, its categories applied to conditions their author never faced. Critics count them as the historical family's pattern in its purest form: a central expectation failed, the practice inverted it, and the theory was amended afterward to describe the inversion as its own development. Alongside the immiseration and polarisation rows is where this survey grades the peasantry expectation, as a strong form that failed and a weaker form, the peasantry's subordination to capital through markets and credit where not through dispossession, that describes much of the record. And it notes the human footnote the critics rightly insist on: the states built by the inverted revolutions then turned on the peasantry that had carried them, in collectivisation drives whose costs are held in this series' record articles, so that the class the theory misjudged paid for the misjudgement twice, once in the theory's contempt and once in the practice's gratitude.

## The political family: power, parties, and the missing constraint

The political family engages what the doctrine does with power: what it authorises, what it restrains, and what its organisations built when they held a state. Its central charge is the authoritarianism charge, and its central finding, in the form this series' own treatment gives it, is that the tradition never developed a theory of institutional constraint on power: no doctrine of separated authorities, no account of rights that bind a revolutionary government, no architecture for the transition it proclaimed beyond the Paris Commune's sketches and the soviets' brief season. The charge and its replies receive a full article of their own, [the article on Marxism and authoritarianism](/2019/07/01/marxism-and-authoritarianism/), and the survey's task here is to place the family on the map, state its strongest materials, and grade it without duplicating that hearing.

The family's most striking feature, for this survey's purposes, is when its warnings were issued and by whom. The prediction that a Marxist seizure of power would produce a new despotism was made from the rival wing of the workers' movement by Mikhail Bakunin in Statism and Anarchy in 1873, before any Marxist held power anywhere, on the stated ground that a state wielded by a self-certifying vanguard would become the property of its wielders. The warning was then repeated from inside Marxism itself, earlier and more precisely than any external critic managed. Karl Kautsky, the leading theorist of the Second International, argued in The Dictatorship of the Proletariat in 1918 that the Bolshevik road substituted the rule of a party over the working class for the rule of the working class, and that a socialism built without democracy would reproduce domination in a new form. Rosa Luxemburg, writing in prison in 1918 in her manuscript on the Russian Revolution, supported the revolution while warning that the suppression of public life, of elections, of a free press and assembly, would leave the soviets themselves lifeless and deliver rule to a clique, since freedom reserved for government supporters is no freedom at all. Her sentence on the point has become the family's emblem: "Freedom is always freedom for the one who thinks differently." The warnings were not heeded, and the apparatuses that grew in the revolutionary states, the party's fusion with the state, the political police, the camps, are the record against which the charge is graded, with the figures and the national cases held in the articles devoted to them rather than in this survey.

The replies are of three kinds, and the survey grades them separately because their fortunes differ. The circumstantial reply assigns the outcome to conditions: civil war, invasion, backwardness, encirclement, the inheritance of older despotisms. It explains much about particular episodes and nothing about the pattern, since the outcome recurred across circumstances that shared little except the party form and the doctrine. The mechanism reply, the strongest of the three, concedes the pattern and locates its engine in the Leninist party structure: democratic centralism, the ban on factions, the monopoly of a single party claiming to embody the class, which converted a revolutionary instrument into a self-perpetuating sovereign. That reply has real explanatory power and the support of the counterexamples, the Marxist parties that governed within constitutional orders and left office when voted out, but it purchases its power by treating the doctrine's political theory as separable from the organisational form the doctrine's founder of parties designed, and the separation is contested ground. The third reply, that the doctrine contains democratic resources its victorious parties ignored, in the Commune model, in the early soviets, in Luxemburg herself, is textually true and practically unproven at the scale that matters: no state built in the tradition's name institutionalised those resources durably. The scorecard's political row therefore stands, with its defeater drawn from the brief's own list: the verdict falls if a party of the tradition, governing a whole country, sustains the alternation of power across repeated electoral defeats, leaving office when beaten and returning only by election, as a matter of settled practice rather than episode.

### What connects the collapse of a state to the truth of a theory?

Only an argument, built rather than gestured at. It must show that the doctrine, rather than circumstance or inheritance, directed the practice that produced the outcome, and it must survive the counterexamples of adherents who acted otherwise. Where that argument is built, the record counts; where it is skipped, the record is being used as a poster.

The family's second strand concerns the state that was supposed to wither. Engels's phrase promised that after the transition the state would not be abolished but would die out, its functions of repression shrinking as class antagonisms dissolved. The revolutionary states built the largest repressive and administrative apparatuses in their societies' histories and maintained them across generations, and no mechanism inside the doctrine explained when withering should begin or who would certify that a function had become unnecessary. Internal critics noticed early: the Yugoslav vice-president Milovan Djilas, in The New Class of 1957, argued from inside a ruling party's summit that the party bureaucracy had become a new possessing class, appropriating through its control of the state the privileges ownership confers elsewhere. The book cost him his position and his liberty, and its argument, that the political family's problem was not a deviation from the system but the system's own product, remains the strand's most consequential internal statement. The survey routes the reader who wants the charge's full anatomy to the authoritarianism article and keeps its own grade narrow: on the evidence of the pattern, the warnings, and the unbuilt constraint, the political family's central charge stands.

## The writers' congress: culture as the constraint's test case

The political family's missing constraint can be tested in a small, well documented theatre, the treatment of writers, and the survey uses the theatre because the evidence there is textual and the internal critics are on the record in their own words. The Soviet model organised literature as it organised industry: a single writers' union from 1934, membership as the licence to publish, socialist realism as the prescribed method, and the party's cultural officials, Andrei Zhdanov's name attaches to the postwar enforcement, grading works by their service to the state's pedagogy. The model's premise was the doctrine's own suspicion of autonomous spheres: culture was superstructure, its independence a bourgeois illusion, and its administration therefore a legitimate function of the transition's state. The internal criticism, when it came, came from the writers the system had made. The Fourth Congress of the Czechoslovak Writers' Union in June 1967 heard Milan Kundera and Ludvík Vaculík argue, from inside the union's own rostrum, that a culture administered into unanimity ceases to be a culture, and that a nation whose literature is a department of its politics has been robbed of the organ by which it thinks about itself. The congress is conventionally counted among the Prague Spring's detonators, and the sequel followed the political family's script: the experiment was ended by invasion, the union was purged and refounded under reliable management, and the critics were expelled from print into the samizdat and exile literatures that became the regime's most durable cultural product.

The episode's analytical value is its scale. No civil war, no encirclement, no backwardness explains why a socialist state must license its novelists, and the circumstantial reply, so serviceable elsewhere, has nothing to grip here. What explains it is the doctrine's transmitted suspicion of autonomous institutions, the same absence the scorecard's political row records, operating in the sphere where its costs are most legible: a theory with no account of why a press, a court, or a literature must be beyond the ruling party's reach will license the reach wherever the party rules, in culture as in law. The Western Marxist traditions mostly drew the conclusion and rebuilt their commitments to cultural and legal autonomy, another amendment entered in the ledger as a concession to the critics. The survey closes the theatre with the observation the writers themselves made in several forms: the regimes' most quoted literary products are now the works they suppressed, from the manuscripts circulated in typescript to the novels published abroad, so that the administered culture's final achievement was to certify, by its prohibitions, the very independence it existed to deny.

## The audit from the rostrum: the secret speech and the parties' haemorrhage

In February 1956 the political family acquired an internal document unlike any other, an audit of the terror delivered by the terror's own institution. Nikita Khrushchev's report to the Twentieth Congress of the Soviet party, the secret speech, catalogued from the rostrum the fabricated cases, the torture, the deportations, and the cult's crimes, attributing them to Stalin's personal pathology and the security organs' lawlessness. The speech was an internal criticism of a peculiar and revealing kind: accurate about the crimes it listed, silent about the system that had organised them, and designed to preserve the party's monopoly by sacrificing its dead leader's reputation, the circumstantial reply administered as state policy. Its effects escaped its designers. In Poland and Hungary the speech detonated the year's crises, as the parties' own members drew the conclusion the speech had avoided, that the pathology was the arrangement, and the Hungarian rising was put down by the army of the state whose congress had begun the audit. In the Western parties the speech, and the rising's suppression that followed it, produced the haemorrhage that reshaped the left: thousands of members left the British and other parties, and the departing intellectuals, E. P. Thompson and John Saville among them, founded the journals of the New Left in the conviction that the tradition had to be rebuilt outside the parties that had disgraced it. The survey records the episode as the internal origin's most consequential single event, because it demonstrates the pattern at civilisational scale. The most damaging audit of the Soviet record ever delivered was delivered by the Soviet party's own first secretary, from its own congress rostrum, out of its own archives; and the system's response to the audit, containment at home, tanks abroad, haemorrhage in the fraternal parties, exhibited in one year every element of the political family's analysis: the missing constraint, the substitution of the institution's survival for the doctrine's standards, and the treatment of internal critics as a security problem. A criticism does not need to be believed by its target to be verified by its target's behaviour.

## The Twenty-one Conditions: the party form goes global

The political family's mechanism had a transmission belt, and its design documents survive, so the survey can show the form being standardised rather than merely inferred from outcomes. The Twenty-one Conditions, adopted by the Second Congress of the Communist International in 1920 as the price of affiliation, required member parties to reorganise on democratic centralist lines, to purge reformists and centrists from leading posts, to combine legal with illegal apparatuses, to submit to the International's executive discipline, and to support colonial liberation movements without reserve. The conditions were a deliberate export instrument: the Bolshevik form, designed for illegality and insurrection in a collapsing empire, was made the template for parties operating in functioning democracies, in colonial dependencies, and in peasant societies, with deviation treated as disloyalty. The internal criticism arrived immediately and from the movement's own ranks. The German and French splits the conditions engineered were defended by their engineers as purification and charged by the expelled, who included many of the founding cadres of Western communism, as the substitution of Moscow's tactical needs for the national movements' judgement, the substitutionist mechanism operating internationally before it had finished operating at home. Later historians across the spectrum have treated the conditions as the moment the party form's DNA was fixed for export, and the survey follows them: whatever the doctrine's texts contained about democracy, the organisational genome that actually travelled was the one the conditions specified, and the political family's pattern, replicated across wildly different circumstances, becomes less mysterious when the replication machinery is exhibited. The episode also disciplines the circumstantial reply in its international form. Circumstances differed by continent; the form did not, because the form was specified in a document, adopted at a congress, and enforced by an executive. Where a pattern has a paperwork trail, explanations from circumstance alone have a document to answer.

## The national question in power: the summit's own audit

The political family has one more internal document of the first rank, produced at the summit of a ruling party in its founder's last months, and the survey includes it because it shows the missing constraint being noticed, too late, by the man whose organisation most needed it. The Soviet state was constructed as a federation of national republics, and its nationalities policy combined formal recognition of nations, their languages, and their cadres with a centralised party that made the federation's forms revocable at the centre's pleasure. Lenin's final dictated notes of December 1922, provoked by the Georgian affair, in which Stalin's apparatus had overridden the Georgian communists with a heavy hand and an investigating commission had whitewashed the violence, charged that the Great Russian chauvinism of the apparatus threatened the whole union's legitimacy, and demanded exemplary punishment and structural concessions to the non-Russian nations. The notes were suppressed in his lifetime's aftermath, circulated to a congress in partial form, and published in full only decades later. Their content is the political family's argument stated by the revolution's own leader: an apparatus armed with a universal doctrine and unbound by enforceable rights for its constituent peoples will reproduce the dominations the doctrine exists to abolish, and will call the reproduction unity.

The critics' use of the episode is careful, and the survey follows them. The notes do not show that Lenin had acquired a theory of constraint; his remedy was personnel and exhortation, punishment for the offenders and vigilance from the party, the same instruments whose failure the notes themselves document. They show something almost as valuable to the prosecution: that the mechanism the family describes, the substitution of the apparatus's judgement for the rights of those it governed, was visible from inside the summit within five years of the seizure, was named by the one man whose authority might have checked it, and was checked by nothing, because no institution existed through which the naming could bind the named. The nationalities settlement's later career, the deportations of whole peoples in the nineteen forties, the Russification pressures, the federation's eventual dissolution along the very national seams its constitution had drawn, is held in the record articles. Here the survey records only the audit and its fate, which together make the political family's most economical exhibit: the warning was issued, at the highest level, in writing, and the system contained no device by which a warning, however authoritative, could become a rule.

## The dictatorship of the proletariat: a phrase and its career

The political family's charge often fastens on a phrase, and the phrase's career is a case study in how the four targets interact. Marx used the expression dictatorship of the proletariat rarely and late: in the Critique of the Gotha Programme of 1875 it names the transitional state between capitalism and communism, and in the letter to Weydemeyer of 1852 it is the political form of the class's rule. Its content, in Marx's own usage, was supplied by the Paris Commune: universal suffrage, revocable officials paid workmen's wages, the standing army replaced by the armed people, a government more democratic in structure than the parliamentary republics it opposed, dictatorial only in the Roman sense of emergency power exercised by the majority class against the dispossessed minority. The phrase's twentieth-century career inverted the proportions. In the Leninist codification it named the party-state's monopoly, democracy for the working people defined so that its institutional content, contested elections, independent courts, a free press, could be absent while the definition was satisfied, and the Commune's safeguards, revocability above all, survived as decoration on constitutions whose operative clauses entrenched the leading role. Critics from Kautsky onward charged that the inversion was licensed by the phrase's own vagueness about institutions: a transition defined by its class character and not by its constitutional form can be filled with any form the class's self-appointed trustees choose. Defenders reply that Marx's usage is recoverable and the Commune model explicit, which the survey grants, and that the recovery has been performed repeatedly by democratic Marxists, which the survey also grants. The grading question is the political family's standing one: why the recovery never governed a state. The phrase's career supplies the answer's shape. Where the doctrine's institutional content was optional and its class legitimation was portable, the organisations that held power kept the legitimation and dropped the content, and nothing in the doctrine as their catechism transmitted it made the dropping a heresy they could be charged with by their own rules.

### How often did Marx use the phrase at the charge's centre?

Rarely enough that the passages can be listed: the Gotha critique of 1875, the Weydemeyer letter of 1852, and the Commune writings that supply its content. The rarity matters because the phrase's later career, as the legitimation of party-states, was built by the codifiers on a foundation their founder left almost entirely unelaborated institutionally.

## Substitutionism: the warning the tradition gave itself

The political family's mechanism reply rests on a concept the tradition produced before it produced a state, and the concept deserves its own section because it is the single most prescient piece of internal criticism in the entire survey. In Our Political Tasks of 1904, the young Leon Trotsky, then opposing Lenin's organisational plans, warned that the logic of substitution ran through the design: a party that substitutes itself for the class will generate a central committee that substitutes itself for the party, and a leadership that substitutes itself for the committee, until a single dictator substitutes himself for the leadership, with each substitution defended as the necessary form of the previous one's fidelity. Trotsky later joined the organisation he had warned against, led its army, and was expelled, exiled, and finally murdered by the apparatus the warning described, a biography that gives the text its terrible standing in the literature. The warning's force for the survey is that it identifies the mechanism in advance, from inside, in the vocabulary of the movement itself: not that bad men would corrupt a good design, but that the design contained a ratchet, each level's substitution licensing the next, and no internal stop.

The ratchet's teeth are documentable without rhetoric. Democratic centralism, as practised, made lower bodies elect higher ones while binding lower bodies to higher decisions, a combination whose democratic element operated at congresses and whose centralist element operated every day between them. The ban on factions, adopted at the Tenth Congress in 1921 as an emergency measure amid civil war and economic collapse, converted organised disagreement inside the party from a method into an offence, and the emergency measure was never repealed, because the body empowered to repeal it was the body it protected. The fusion of party and state followed administratively: the party's committees shadowed every state institution, its nomenklatura lists controlled appointments across the whole society, and its monopoly was eventually written into constitutional text as the leading role. Each step was defended in the doctrine's vocabulary, as the dictatorship of the proletariat exercising its rule through its vanguard, and each step is therefore chargeable to the doctrine through the connecting argument the survey requires: not that the texts commanded the steps, but that the texts contained no principle by which a loyal adherent could refuse them, and several principles, the suspicion of formal rights, the unity of theory and practice, the vanguard's epistemic privilege, by which refusal could be condemned. That is the missing constraint stated operationally. A theory of constraint is precisely a set of principles that bind the faithful when fidelity is inconvenient, and the tradition's library, rich in principles that bind its enemies, is the survey's emptiest shelf where principles binding its own power are concerned.

Later internal critics refined the warning into analyses. The Yugoslav Praxis school and the dissident Marxists of Eastern Europe, several of whom appear in the authoritarianism article's ledger, argued that the one-party monopoly was not a Russian accident but the system's load-bearing wall, removable only with the building. Western Marxists of the post-1956 generations, formed by the secret speech and the Hungarian rising, rebuilt democratic commitments into their versions of the doctrine, and the Eurocommunist parties of the nineteen seventies formally renounced the dictatorship of the proletariat and accepted alternation in office, a development the survey counts as the tradition conceding the criticism by amendment. The concession's limits are equally instructive: the amended parties never held national power alone, so the defeater printed on the scorecard's political row, alternation sustained over multiple defeats by a Marxist party governing at national scale, remains unclaimed, and the row stands. The political family's verdict is thus the survey's most carefully bounded: the charge is not that the doctrine commands despotism, a claim the counterexamples refute, but that it equips its adherents with no internal resources for refusing despotism when the party form and sovereign power combine, a claim the record, the warnings, and the amendments together sustain.

### Who named the substitutionist mechanism, and when?

Trotsky named it in Our Political Tasks in 1904, before the revolution, as a warning against the organisational design he later served. His sequence, party for class, committee for party, leader for committee, was stated as the design's internal logic, and the record followed the sequence closely enough to make the text the political family's most cited document.

## The sociological family: class and its rivals

The sociological family engages the doctrine's map of society. Marx's account assigned class, defined by position within the relations of production, the master role in explaining political allegiance, collective action, and historical change, and it carried a structural expectation: capitalist development would simplify the map, dissolving intermediate positions into two great camps. The family's criticisms press three points: the persistence and expansion of intermediate strata, the multi-dimensional character of stratification, and the durability of nation and religion as bases of collective action rivalling or overriding class. Each point has its literature, and each, once again, was developed furthest by scholars working in or near the tradition.

Bernstein's Preconditions of 1899 opened the empirical case, observing that the middle classes were not disappearing: the growth of joint-stock companies was multiplying shareholders, salaried employees and technicians were expanding as a new middle stratum, and the polarisation the theory expected was being complicated by the very development that was supposed to produce it. Ralf Dahrendorf's Class and Class Conflict in Industrial Society of 1959 restated the objection in sociological form: ownership had separated from control, the working class had decomposed into strata with divergent interests, and conflict had been institutionalised rather than sharpened, so that class in Marx's sense explained less of the political behaviour of advanced societies than the theory required. Max Weber's stratification scheme, class, status, and party as independent dimensions of inequality, supplied the standing alternative: a map on which economic position is one axis among several, and on which prestige and organisational power generate allegiances that cross-cut the production axis entirely.

The tradition's most serious answer came from the Marxist sociologist Erik Olin Wright, whose Classes of 1985 rebuilt the map rather than defending the old one. Wright introduced contradictory class locations, positions such as managers and supervisors that are simultaneously exploiters and exploited along different dimensions, and later rebuilt the scheme around multiple mechanisms of exploitation, organisational assets and skill assets alongside property. The reconstruction conceded the criticism's substance, that the two-camp map does not describe advanced societies, while preserving an explanatory programme in which class, more subtly drawn, still structures life chances and politics. The scorecard grades the polarisation row as partly answered on exactly those terms: answered by a successor theory that its own author presented as a rebuilding, which is to say answered at the price of the original map. Readers who want the question pursued into the present structure of work are routed to [the article on whether the working class still exists](/2011/11/01/does-the-working-class-still-exist/).

### Why do intermediate strata embarrass the polarisation thesis?

Because the thesis was directional and the strata moved the wrong way. Salaried managers, technicians, professionals, and small proprietors were predicted to thin out as capital concentrated, squeezed into the two camps. Instead they thickened into the largest layers of advanced societies, and their political behaviour, variable, organised, often decisive, fits no simple reading of the two-camp map.

The family's third point concerns rival loyalties. The framework expected class solidarity to override national attachment as capitalism internationalised, an expectation the workers' movements of 1914 famously failed to meet when the parties of the Second International voted war credits and their members marched under national flags. The Scottish Marxist historian Tom Nairn, in the essay The Modern Janus of 1975 and in The Break-Up of Britain of 1977, gave the failure its canonical internal verdict: "The theory of nationalism represents Marxism's great historical failure." Nairn's point was structural, not merely chronological: nationalism mobilised the same masses, in the same centuries, through the same print and party machinery, and the framework had no theory of why the national bond should prove stronger than the class bond it was supposed to dissolve. Religion's durability poses the parallel problem. The expectation that religious allegiance would fade as its social roots were transformed underestimated both the autonomy of religious institutions and their capacity to organise collective action, including action against states ruling in Marxism's name, where churches supplied the shelter under which opposition gathered. Later Marxist scholarship produced serious work on both phenomena, Gramsci's on hegemony and national culture above all, but the work has the character of repair: it explains the durability after the fact, with concepts the original expectation did not contain.

### What rival loyalties compete with class in the sociological family?

Nation and faith, and behind them status and profession. National attachment organised the masses of Europe into war in 1914 against the International's explicit commitments, and religious institutions outlasted the states that persecuted them, sheltering the oppositions that helped bring those states down. A map of allegiance drawn from production alone predicts neither result.

## The managerial revolution: the new class goes west

The sociological family's new class argument, met already in Djilas's Yugoslav form, had a Western twin whose author's biography is the internal pattern in its most dramatic arc, and the survey completes the strand with it. Bruno Rizzi, an Italian Trotskyist, argued in The Bureaucratisation of the World of 1939 that the Soviet bureaucracy constituted a new ruling class of a new social formation, neither capitalist nor socialist. James Burnham, then a leading American Trotskyist, took the argument up, broke with Trotsky in 1940 in a famous exchange over dialectical materialism and the Soviet Union's class character, and generalised the thesis in The Managerial Revolution of 1941: across the Soviet Union, Nazi Germany, and New Deal America alike, power was passing to the managers, the administrators and production executives, a class ruling through its control of organisations rather than through property. Max Shachtman, Burnham's former comrade, carried the bureaucratic collectivist version back into the Marxist debate as the third camp's foundation. The thesis's Western career then diverged from its Eastern twin's. Applied to the corporations, it merged with the separation of ownership and control that Adolf Berle and Gardiner Means had documented in 1932, and with John Kenneth Galbraith's technostructure of 1967, becoming a standard theme of organisational sociology; applied to the party-states, it remained the internal critics' master key, from Djilas's possessing bureaucracy to the dissidents' analyses of the nomenklatura as a class with hereditary transmission of advantage.

The survey grades the strand as the sociological family's most successful export and its most instructive ambiguity. As criticism of the doctrine, the new class thesis charged that the framework's own categories, applied without piety, described the socialist states better than the doctrine's official self-description did, and the charge was made, in Rizzi, Burnham, Shachtman, and Djilas, by men formed inside the movement the charge indicted. As sociology, the thesis overstated the managers' unity and understated property's resilience in the West, where owners returned to the centre of the analysis in later decades. Both halves belong in the ledger, and the strand's presence in this survey makes a methodological point the reader should carry away: the families interlock. The new class is a sociological description with a political charge and a normative sting, and the survey's sorting by target is a device for grading, not a claim that the arguments themselves respect the borders. The borders are crossed, in the article's design, by cross-reference, and the reader who follows the crossings will find the survey's separate grades assembling into the single connected finding the convergence section states.

## Religion's return: an expectation the critics shared

The sociological family's religion strand carries a grading subtlety the survey must state, because it disciplines the comparative method. That allegiance would wither as its social foundations transformed was an expectation the doctrine held in common with its rivals, not a private property. The secularisation thesis, in forms owing more to Weber and Durkheim than to Marx, was the common sense of postwar sociology across the spectrum, and its failure is therefore a shared failure that cannot be charged to one framework's account. Peter Berger, among the thesis's most distinguished architects in The Sacred Canopy of 1967, performed the recantation himself, editing The Desecularization of the World in 1999 and announcing that the world remained as religious as ever, with the secularised zones, Western Europe and the international intelligentsia, standing as the exceptions requiring explanation. The episode is the critics-of-the-critics pattern in its most honourable form, a leading theorist auditing his own school's central expectation against the record and publishing the correction. Its bearing on the scorecard is precise. The religion strand of the nationalism row stands, because the doctrine's version of the expectation was stronger than the sociologists', tied to a mechanism, the transformation of the base, that should have operated most powerfully where the doctrine ruled, and because in the doctrine's own states religion's survival took the pointed form of sheltering the opposition. But the strand's grade is recorded with the comparative note attached: on this question the framework failed in company, and its rivals' failure is part of the evidence a fair reader weighs. The survey's method, grading comparatively or not at all, exists for exactly such cases, and the reader should distrust any account of this strand, celebratory or prosecutorial, that omits the company.

## The death of class: the sociological family's boldest obituary

Every family in this survey contains one criticism that overshot so far it became evidence for the other side, and the sociological family's is the death of class thesis. Jan Pakulski and Malcolm Waters argued in The Death of Class of 1996 that class had ceased to organise inequality, identity, and politics in the advanced societies, displaced by status, consumption, and lifestyle, and that the concept should be retired from sociology's working vocabulary. The thesis was the embourgeoisement argument at maximum strength, and it arrived just as the income and wealth distributions of the advanced economies began the sustained widening that has since made inequality the central topic of their public economics. The timing was unkind and the survey notes it without triumphalism, because the episode grades both camps. The obituary's authors had correctly registered the decline of class as identity and as electoral badge, the phenomena the family's earlier rows record. They erred in inferring the decline of class as structure, the distribution of assets, risks, and life chances that continued to sort populations whether or not the sorted used the vocabulary. The distinction between class as badge and class as structure is the family's most useful late product, and it was produced by the collision: defenders of the concept, Wright's school prominent among them, were forced to specify which of its senses their evidence supported, and the specification, structure yes, badge declining, structure's political expression organisationally mediated, is the position the scorecard's partly answered grades encode. A criticism that forces its target to become precise has done critical work even when its headline is falsified, and the survey counts the death of class among the family's productive errors, the mirror image of the doctrine's own productive errors, of which the transition debate's history offers several.

## Reproduction and mobility: the class map meets the school

The sociological family's most consequential testing ground after the factory was the school, because the framework's account of how class positions reproduce themselves across generations stands or falls on evidence about education and mobility, and the evidence arrived in bulk. Samuel Bowles and Herbert Gintis, Marxist economists working in the United States, gave the reproduction thesis its strongest modern form in Schooling in Capitalist America of 1976: the correspondence principle, by which schools reproduce the relations of production by training the dispositions each level of the division of labour requires, docility for the routine posts, internalised direction for the trusted ones, with the meritocratic ideology certifying the outcome as fair. The book was the tradition's own sociology of education at its most ambitious, and the criticism it attracted was correspondingly instructive. Empiricists objected that the correspondence was asserted at the level of structure and rarely traced in classrooms; historians objected that school systems predated and outlasted the capitalist forms they were said to correspond to; and the mobility research programmes, above all the Oxford studies directed by Goldthorpe, measured the actual flows between class positions and found a pattern neither the reproduction thesis nor its liberal rival predicted cleanly: high absolute mobility driven by the changing occupational structure, stubbornly unequal relative chances by class of origin, and no secular tendency for the relative inequalities to close.

Pierre Bourdieu's work supplied the strand's most durable concept and its characteristic ambiguity. Cultural capital, the embodied dispositions, credentials, and tastes that families transmit and schools reward as individual gifts, explained reproduction's mechanism more finely than the correspondence principle, showing how advantage passes through institutions formally committed to fairness. Bourdieu's relation to the tradition was that of a critical heir: formed by its questions, armed with its suspicion of meritocratic innocence, and unwilling to reduce the cultural mechanism to an economic reflex, since the fields he mapped, art, academia, taste, obey logics of their own that the base and superstructure image flattens. The survey grades the strand inside the family's profile as another partly answered complex with the pattern by now familiar to the reader: the doctrine's strong claim, that education is the reproduction machinery of class, was narrowed by the evidence into a set of mechanisms, cultural transmission among them, that the general sociology of education now operates without needing the doctrine's name. The mobility data's refusal to show either polarisation or equalisation is the empirical ground on which the family's two-camp row received its grade, and the reader who wants the occupational sequel is routed, as before, to the working class article.

## The feminist audit: the labour the class map left unwaged

The sociological family's map had a blank space its own categories created, and the critics who exposed it were, in the survey's by now familiar pattern, largely women working inside or beside the tradition who found their formation indispensable and insufficient at once. The blank was unwaged labour: the cooking, cleaning, childbearing, and care by which labour power itself is daily and generationally reproduced, performed overwhelmingly by women outside the wage relation, and therefore invisible to a class map drawn from positions within production for the market. The domestic labour debate of the nineteen seventies made the blank impossible to ignore. Mariarosa Dalla Costa and Selma James, in The Power of Women and the Subversion of the Community of 1972, argued that housework produces the commodity labour power itself and is therefore productive labour in the framework's own terms, a claim pressed into the Wages for Housework campaign's demand that the wage be extended to expose the work. Orthodox replies defended the categories: domestic labour produces use values, not value, and the distinction preserves the theory's coherence. The critics answered that the coherence was the problem, since a theory of exploitation that cannot see the labour reproducing the exploited has mistaken its map's edge for the world's.

Heidi Hartmann's essay on the unhappy marriage of Marxism and feminism, first published in 1979, gave the audit its canonical form: the marriage, she argued, had been unhappy because Marxism's categories, class above all, are sex-blind, analysing capital's relations while treating the relations between men and women as a secondary effect, so that feminism supplied the analysis of patriarchy as a system with its own material base in men's control of women's labour. Lise Vogel's Marxism and the Oppression of Women of 1983 attempted the internal reconstruction, rebuilding the account from the reproduction of labour power within the theory of surplus value, and the attempt's quality is the survey's point: the audit produced, from inside, the tradition's most serious work on the subject, while the need for the audit stands as the criticism. Graded by the scorecard's method, the feminist rows would sit inside the sociological family's existing grades as another partly answered complex: the original map's silence is not disputed, the reconstructions are genuine, and the reconstructions concede that the silence was structural rather than accidental, since a map drawn from the wage relation will miss the work that makes wage labour possible wherever that work is unwaged. Later scholarship on social reproduction has carried the reconstruction into the discipline's mainstream, one more component naturalised into the general equipment of social science under a name its originators would recognise.

## The affluent worker: an empirical test both sides failed

The sociological family's abstract disputes about class maps were once subjected to a direct empirical test, and the test's result, which embarrassed both camps in the debate, is the family's most instructive episode. Postwar affluence in Britain generated the embourgeoisement thesis, the claim, popular among commentators and some sociologists, that prosperous manual workers were becoming middle class in income, outlook, and allegiance, dissolving the working class as a political force. The thesis was the mirror image of polarisation: where Marx's map predicted the middle would vanish downward, the embourgeoisement map predicted the bottom would vanish upward. John Goldthorpe, David Lockwood, Frank Bechhofer, and Jennifer Platt tested the claim in their Affluent Worker studies, published in 1968 and 1969, examining highly paid workers in Luton's advanced industries and comparing them with white-collar employees. Their findings refused both maps. The affluent workers had not adopted middle-class norms, sociability, or aspirations in the embourgeoisement sense; they remained collectivist, but instrumentally, treating unions as instruments for wages rather than as expressions of class solidarity, and their lives were privatised around home and family rather than oriented to either class community or status emulation. A new pattern, the privatised instrumental worker, stood where both theories had predicted a familiar figure.

The episode matters to the survey for three reasons. It shows the sociological family's questions are empirical and can be put to evidence, unlike several of the doctrine's disputes that proceed by exegesis. It shows the evidence disciplining the critics as well as the doctrine: the embourgeoisement thesis, the standing alternative of the period, failed its test as clearly as polarisation failed its own, and the scorecard's comparative method, grading the framework against rivals rather than against perfection, is vindicated by the double failure. And it shows what the successor sociology looked like: Lockwood's and Goldthorpe's later work on class structure, mobility, and the service class became foundational for the stratification research that now proceeds with class as one variable among several, measured finely, its effects estimated rather than assumed. That research tradition is the sociological family's largest single legacy to the general equipment of the social sciences, and its founding generation included scholars, Lockwood among them, whose early work was a serious engagement with Marx's questions from outside the doctrine's allegiance. The family's verdict, assembled, is that the doctrine's map was wrong in its directional claims and fertile in its questions, a combination the scorecard records as partly answered rows and a standing confession on nationalism, and which the working class article pursues into the occupational structure of the present century.

One further strand completes the family, the fate of class as an explanation of politics in the democracies. The electoral evidence accumulated by political science across the postwar decades shows class voting declining from its mid-century heights in most advanced democracies, while never vanishing, and shows education, region, age, and cultural attitudes claiming shares of the explanation that the older model assigned to class alone. Przeworski's dilemma, treated in the internal origin section, supplies the strategic mechanism: parties of the working class that chase majorities dilute the appeal that made them class parties, so declining class voting is partly the parties' own work. The critics' strongest form of the point is that a variable whose political expression depends so heavily on the organisations claiming to represent it is doing less independent explanatory work than the framework assigned it. The defenders' strongest reply is that the framework never claimed class acts without organisation, that consciousness and party were always mediations in the serious versions, and that the decline of the mediating organisations is a historical event to be explained, not a refutation of the variable they mediated. This survey files the strand under the family's existing rows and notes it as the point where the sociological family hands its unresolved business to the political scientists, who are still, on the evidence of their journals, profitably arguing about it.

## The normative family: justice, means, and the standing of persons

The normative family is the smallest and, in the judgement of many of its participants, the deepest, because it asks not whether the doctrine is true but whether it is just, and whether it contains the resources to say so. Its opening question divided the tradition's scholars against themselves: does Marx condemn capitalism as unjust? Allen Wood argued in his essay on the Marxian critique of justice in 1972 that he does not, that Marx treated justice as a juridical standard internal to each mode of production, by whose lights capitalist exchange, the purchase of labour power at its value, is perfectly just, and that the critique of capitalism rests instead on non-moral goods such as freedom, community, and self-realisation that capitalism frustrates. Ziyad Husami replied in 1978 that Marx plainly does condemn capitalist distribution by a trans-historical standard, and Norman Geras, in The Controversy About Marx and Justice in 1985, assembled the textual case that Marx both held capitalism to be unjust and believed that he did not, a contradiction Geras charged to Marx himself rather than to his interpreters. Steven Lukes, in Marxism and Morality of 1985, organised the terrain as a paradox: the framework is deeply hostile to morality as ideology, dismissing rights and justice as bourgeois forms, while being driven by moral commitments it cannot avow in its own vocabulary.

The paradox has practical teeth, and the family's second strand pulls them. A doctrine suspicious of rights as bourgeois has difficulty saying what may not be done in its name during the transition it licenses: if justice is internal to modes of production, the revolutionary government's acts are judged by a standard that arrives only later, and the persons the transition runs over have no standing from which to object that the framework is obliged to hear. Critics connect this gap to the political family's findings, arguing that the missing theory of constraint and the missing theory of justice are the same absence seen from two sides, while defenders reply that the framework's silence about justice is a principled refusal to moralise structural questions, and that its positive commitments, to the free development of each as the condition of all, supply the standard the critics claim is absent. The family's third strand concerns the distribution principle of the higher phase itself, from each according to ability, to each according to need, and whether a principle that transcends justice can govern a society that must still allocate scarce goods among strangers. The survey grades the family's central row as partly answered: the scholarly dispute has clarified what the texts contain, the clarification itself reveals the tension Geras named, and the tension's practical form, the standing of persons during transitions, remains the least answered item in the family's ledger.

### Does Marxism contain a theory of justice, or only a critique of one?

The honest answer is that the tradition's scholars have been unable to agree, on strong textual evidence both ways. Wood found a critique of justice as a mode-relative standard and no trans-historical tribunal. Geras found the tribunal in use and disavowed in theory. The disagreement is itself the datum the scorecard grades.

## The missing blueprint: utopia's revenge

The normative family contains one criticism that is easy to state and hard to answer, because it concerns an absence the founder was proud of. Marx refused, on principle, to describe the future society in institutional detail, dismissing the utopian socialists' blueprints and declining to write recipes for the cook-shops of the future, as his phrase ran. The refusal had honourable grounds: blueprints are fantasies untested by the movement that must build them, and a science of the present need not moonlight as architecture. The critics' charge, pressed by liberals and by later Marxists auditing the transitions, is that the refusal left a vacuum at exactly the point where the doctrine's followers would need designs: how a post-capitalist economy coordinates, how its polity constrains, how its law protects, questions on which the texts offer the Commune's sketches, the Gotha critique's two phases, and silence. Into the vacuum stepped improvisation, and the improvisations, the war communism of emergency, the plan as command system, the party as universal administrator, hardened into the institutions the political family grades. The charge is therefore the normative form of the missing constraint: a tradition that would not design its destination in advance cannot complain when the destination is designed by its worst placed members under its worst conditions, and the utopians' revenge is that the despised blueprints, Fourier's phalansteries and Owen's villages aside, contained the one thing the science lacked, an account of Tuesday morning in the good society, who decides, who appeals, who audits the auditors.

The internal heirs divided over the repair. The market socialists and the analytical egalitarians accepted the charge's force and produced the designs, Lange's board, Roemer's coupon economy, Cohen's camping trip writ large, each an attempt to supply the missing institutional imagination within the tradition's values. The defenders of the refusal reply that the designs' fate vindicates the abstention: Lange's model was a debating victory that no ministry could operate, Roemer's coupons a philosopher's instrument, and the transitions that occurred were shaped by war and scarcity, not by the absence of drawings. This survey files the exchange under the normative row and records its own judgement plainly: the refusal was defensible as method and costly as legacy, and the cost is measurable in the political family's ledger, where the unbuilt constraint and the undesigned economy are the same silence heard in two registers. A doctrine may decline to draw its destination. It cannot decline and also direct the journey, and the century's Marxist states are what happened when movements attempted both.

## Equality of what: the question the tradition taught its critics to ask

The normative family's modern form owes as much to a friendly outsider as to any insider, and the survey includes the debt because it marks the point where the family's questions passed into philosophy's mainstream. Amartya Sen's Tanner Lecture of 1979, Equality of What, took the egalitarian tradition, Marxists emphatically included, and asked what its currency was: income, wealth, utility, resources, or the capabilities, the real freedoms to achieve valued functionings, that Sen proposed. The question exposed an underspecification the Marxist tradition shared with its rivals and had less excuse for, since its own critique of distribution by desert and its needs principle of the higher phase both implied a currency neither named. Cohen's subsequent work, the most rigorous normative Marxism of its generation, can be read as the tradition answering Sen's question from inside: his critique of Rawls's incentives argument, his defence of an ethos of equality beyond the basic structure's rules, and his insistence that the tradition's habitual contrast between its own historical science and mere moralising had licensed an evasion, all press the family toward the precision the question demands. The needs principle itself received the audit it had avoided: a principle of distribution according to need requires a public standard of needs, an authority to apply it, and an account of what happens when needs as certified exceed the product, and the critics who posed those questions, several of them egalitarians auditing their own inheritance, found the tradition's texts eloquent on the destination and silent on the administration. The strand is graded within the normative family's existing row and the exercise draws the now familiar moral. The family's questions were sharpened most effectively by scholars who wanted them answered, and the answers, where they exist, were built after the audit and bear the auditors' names.

## The two Marxes: alienation, the break, and the humanist quarrel

The normative family's deepest division is not between the doctrine and its critics but between two versions of the doctrine's founder, and the quarrel over which version is real has structured the criticisms for ninety years. The Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844, written by the twenty-six-year-old Marx and published only in 1932, presented a thinker of alienation: labour under capitalism estranges the worker from the product, from the activity, from other people, and from the species-being, the free conscious life activity that defines the human. The manuscripts' appearance transformed the doctrine's reception, supplying the humanist Marx whom existentialists, theologians, and the New Left could read as a philosopher of human flourishing, and against whom the mature economist of Capital could be set as a colder and narrower figure. The critics of the humanist reading charged it with anachronism, reading the old man's system through the young man's notebooks. The critics of the scientific reading charged it with amputating the doctrine's moral heart, leaving a mechanics of history with no account of why anyone should care which side won.

Althusser's intervention of 1965, the thesis of the epistemological break, attempted to settle the quarrel by decree from inside: the young Marx, he argued, belonged to an ideological problematic of human essence, and the break of 1845 separated that juvenilia from the science founded in Capital, so that the humanist critics and the humanist defenders were alike reading the wrong books. The thesis organised a generation of scholarship and attracted the full battery of the survey's familiar criticisms: that the break's date migrated under pressure, that the mature texts retain the vocabulary of estrangement in the fetishism analysis, that the decree functioned to protect the science from the philosophy's embarrassments, an immunising stratagem in historiographical form. Thompson's Poverty of Theory, already met in the philosophical family, was aimed at the school the break founded. The survey's grading of the quarrel is deliberately modest, because its function here is diagnostic. The two Marxes problem matters to a survey of criticisms because each family grades differently depending on which Marx is its object: the normative family's justice dispute is transformed if the young Marx's standard of flourishing is admitted as the doctrine's own, the philosophical family's verdict on the dialectic shifts with the weight given to the manuscripts, and the political family's defendants reach instinctively for the humanist texts when the record is read against them. A reader who knows which Marx a criticism engages holds one more key, and the survey hands it over with the warning that the tradition's scholars, after ninety years, have not agreed on the answer themselves.

### Why did the early manuscripts change the criticisms' target?

Because they gave the doctrine a moral psychology its published system had kept offstage. Critics could now charge the mature work with abandoning its own standard of human flourishing, and defenders could answer charges against the system by appealing to the notebooks. The target doubled, and every subsequent grading has had to say which Marx it means.

## Means and ends: the moral economy of the transition

The normative family's abstract dispute about justice had a concrete twin, the dispute about means, and its classic exchange states the family's whole problem in miniature. In Their Morals and Ours of 1938, Trotsky, writing from exile against the critics of Bolshevik methods, argued that the morality of means is derived from the end, that the end, the liberation of humanity, justifies means that would otherwise be condemned, subject to the condition that the means genuinely serve the end and that the end itself is not a cover for a caste's interest. John Dewey replied in the same exchange that the interdependence of means and ends cuts the licence: an end is not a fixed star by which means are graded, because the end actually achieved is the means' product, so that methods of suppression produce a suppressed society as their end in the only sense history recognises. The exchange is the family's cleanest exhibit because both parties accept the framework's seriousness: Dewey was no Cold Warrior but a philosopher of democracy auditing a revolutionary's moral logic, and Trotsky's candour about deriving means from ends states, more honestly than his tradition usually managed, the position the critics attribute to it. Graded by the survey, Dewey's point has worn better, and not only philosophically. The political family's record is its empirical footnote: regimes built by methods of monopoly and suppression did not subsequently relax into the free association the end described, and the means' persistence is the strongest evidence that means are not instruments laid down after use but institutions that reproduce their operators.

The family's internal critics pressed the same point from the victim's side of the barricade. Victor Serge, a revolutionary who had served the Bolsheviks and broken with Stalinism, spent his later writing, in Memoirs of a Revolutionary and in his novels, documenting what the derivation of means from ends did to the people who administered it and the people it was administered to, and his testimony belongs to the normative family as much as to the witness shelf, because its argument is moral before it is historical: a movement that treats persons as material will be staffed, in the end, by people good at treating persons as material. The later analytical generation rebuilt the family's questions in a different idiom. Cohen's turn to normative philosophy, culminating in Rescuing Justice and Equality of 2008, argued from inside egalitarian socialism that the tradition had evaded the fundamental questions about what equality requires and what may be traded for it, and that its habitual appeal to historical necessity had functioned as an excuse for not answering. When a tradition's most rigorous living philosopher spends his maturity charging it with moral evasion, the normative family's verdict writes itself in the survey's terms: the questions are real, the tradition's answers are contested by its own, and whether persons retain standing during transitions remains the ledger's least settled item.

### Why does the means and ends dispute belong to the normative family?

Because its question is not what happened but what may be done. Trotsky's derivation of means from the liberating end and Dewey's reply, that the achieved end is the means' own product, are claims about permissible action, and they bind or license practice in advance of any record. The record then grades the rival answers empirically.

## The scorecard: verdicts with defeaters attached

The families have now been stated. Grading them requires an instrument, and the instrument is the scorecard: one row for each major critique, naming its family, its leading proponent, the strongest reply in the literature, the verdict this survey assigns, and the defeater, the stated development that would reverse the verdict. The defeater column is this cluster's innovation and it is not decoration. A verdict without a defeater is a loyalty statement, since nothing could count against it; a verdict with a defeater is a claim about the world, exposed to the future in a specified way. The whole scorecard, in turn, stands under three master defeaters drawn from the cluster's design: its verdicts fall if a reconstruction of value theory preserves both aggregate equalities without adopting a contested valuation convention; if a Marxist party governing at national scale sustains democratic alternation over multiple defeats; or if a specified prediction derived from the framework is confirmed against a stated alternative. Reading the scorecard requires three cautions, each of which is a rule rather than a suggestion. The first is that the verdicts grade arguments, not allegiances: a verdict of standing records that the best reply in the literature does not meet the argument as stated, and a verdict of partly answered records that the reply succeeds by narrowing the claim, changing its terms, or relocating its ground, which is a real success with a real price. The second caution is that the rows are not additive. Fifteen rows do not sum to a verdict on Marxism, because the rows concern different targets, and the four-targets discipline forbids the arithmetic of piling a price-theory result onto a political one. A reader who wants an overall judgement must build it target by target, and the overall judgement will be a profile, not a score. The third caution is that every verdict is provisional in the printed way: the defeater column states what the future, or the archive, or a better argument, would have to produce to change the grade, and the survey commits itself in advance to changing it.

### How is the scorecard's middle verdict, partly answered, assigned?

Partly answered is assigned when the best reply meets the argument by narrowing the original claim, redefining its terms, or shifting its ground, so that something genuine survives and something genuine has been surrendered. It is the scorecard's most populated verdict because it is the commonest real outcome of a century of argument.

The distribution of verdicts is itself a finding worth stating without varnish. No row is graded answered outright. That is not because the replies are worthless; several are major intellectual achievements, and the New Interpretation, the market socialist model, and Wright's rebuilding of class analysis are all permanent contributions to their disciplines. It is because in every case the reply, examined closely, either preserves the doctrine's letter by altering its valuation conventions, meets the objection by conceding the phenomenon and disputing its interpretation, or rebuilds the claim in a form its originator would have had to argue for afresh. A survey that graded any of these as a clean answer would be grading the effort rather than the result. Equally, the standing rows are not all of one kind: the incentive row stands against a project's design, the immiseration row stands against a prediction's strong form, the nationalism row stands against an expectation the tradition itself has confessed, and the political row stands against a theory's completeness. Grouping them under one word is the scorecard's compression, and the family sections above supply the differences the compression hides.

### What would count as a defeater for a scorecard verdict?

A defeater is a specified development, named in advance, whose occurrence would reverse the verdict it is attached to: a reconstruction meeting stated conditions, a political record of a stated kind, a prediction confirmed against a stated alternative. It differs from a general openness to evidence by being concrete enough that both sides can agree when it has occurred.

The defeater discipline also answers, in advance, a suspicion the reader may already feel forming, and which the objection section below states at full strength: that a survey which concludes with some criticisms standing and a framework still alive has arranged its grades so that nothing could disturb them. The defeaters are the survey's exposure. If the value reconstruction is produced, the economic rows change. If the governing party sustains alternation, the political row changes. If the specified prediction is confirmed against its alternative, the philosophical rows change. A grading that publishes its own reversal conditions has not immunised itself; it has done the opposite, and it invites the reader to hold it to the printed terms.

## The master defeaters in detail

The scorecard's three governing defeaters were stated compactly in its introduction, and they deserve a section of their own before the worked example, because they are the survey's largest exposure and a reader should be able to test them for honesty. The first defeater concerns value theory: a reconstruction that preserves both aggregate equalities while resting on no contested valuation convention. Its honesty test is whether the condition excludes the existing claimants, and it does: the New Interpretation's convention is contested by the temporal school, the temporal school's by the simultaneous valuers, and the condition's wording, without a contested convention, was chosen precisely so that no existing school can claim the prize by acclamation. The second defeater concerns the political row: a party of the tradition in national government sustaining the alternation of power across repeated defeats. Its honesty test is whether near misses count, and they do not: parties have governed and lost office once, have shared power in coalitions, have amended their doctrines toward alternation without holding the power the amendment concerned, and the condition's wording, at national scale, over multiple defeats, was chosen so that the event, when it occurs, will be unambiguous and the survey's concession unavoidable. The third defeater concerns the framework's predictive standing: a prediction specified in advance from the framework and borne out against the alternative named for it. Its honesty test is the specification requirement, since the tradition's archive contains many claims that can be read as predictions after their outcomes are known, and the condition excludes them by requiring the prediction and its alternative to be fixed before the evidence is consulted, the standard the historical family applied to the old predictions throughout. A survey that wished to immunise itself would phrase its defeaters vaguely, so that their occurrence could always be disputed. These are phrased to be triggerable by events their author cannot control, in the journals, in the archives, and in the polling stations of countries the survey does not name, and that triggerability is the difference between the scorecard and the catechism it grades.

## A worked row: the transformation verdict unpacked

Abstract method convinces few readers, so this section walks one row of the scorecard through the four rules at the speed of the working. Take the transformation row. Proponent fidelity first: the criticism graded is not the slogan that Marx contradicted himself but Bortkiewicz's formal result, that consistent simultaneous valuation of inputs and outputs cannot in general preserve both aggregate equalities, together with Steedman's extension, that the price system is fully determined without the value magnitudes. That is the strongest form, and weaker forms were set aside. Reply parity next: the replies graded are the strongest available, the New Interpretation's redefinition of the monetary expression of labour and the value of labour power, under which both equalities hold, and the temporal school's sequential valuation, under which the inconsistency does not arise. Price accounting then does the decisive work. The New Interpretation preserves the equalities by adopting definitions under which they hold identically, which critics across the spectrum, including scholars sympathetic to the project, have noted converts an empirical correspondence into a bookkeeping identity; the temporal reading preserves consistency by changing the valuation convention from the simultaneous one the problem assumes, which its critics call answering a different question and its defenders call recovering Marx's own. Either way, something in the original claim, that the derivation as Marx presented it carries both loads at once, has been surrendered, and the surrender is the difference between the verdict given, partly answered, and the verdict the defence's surveys print, answered. Target discipline closes the working: the row's grade touches nothing else, not the exploitation correspondence Morishima proved, not the political rows, not the framework's historical performance, and a reader who accepts the grade has accepted one finding about one derivation. The defeater, finally, is the row's exposure: produce the reconstruction that preserves both equalities without a contested convention, a statement precise enough that the temporal school, the New Interpretation's authors, and their critics can all recognise the event, and the grade changes in print. Fifteen rows were built by the same four operations, and a reader who disputes any of them is invited to name the operation at fault, which is the most a grading instrument can offer.

## Disputed rows: where the specialists push back

The methodology section promised that disagreements could be located precisely, and this section honours the promise by recording the three rows where specialist pushback against the survey's grades is strongest, with the pushback stated fairly. The valuation rows attract the first dispute. Scholars of the temporal single-system school hold that the survey's partly answered grade understates their case: on their reading, the transformation problem is an artefact of simultaneous valuation, Marx's procedure was sequential and consistent, and both equalities hold without redefinition, so the correct grade is answered. The survey's response is printed so the reader can judge: the sequential reading is a genuine interpretation with textual support, and its status as the correct account of Marx's procedure remains contested among scholars of the manuscripts who accept neither school, so the grade records the contest rather than either camp's victory. The class rows attract the second dispute, from the opposite direction: stratification researchers who regard Wright's rebuilding as having conceded so much, multiple assets, contradictory locations, organisational exploitation, that the surviving programme is class analysis in name only, and the row should read standing against the original with the successor filed as a different theory. The survey's response is that the scorecard grades claims and their recognised successors together where the succession is acknowledged by both parties, as Wright acknowledged it, and that filing the successor separately would flatter the original by hiding the succession's price, which the partly answered grade exists to display.

The political row attracts the third and gravest dispute, and it comes from the defence. Historians of the revolutionary states argue that the survey's standing grade, resting on the missing constraint, underrates the circumstantial case in its strongest modern form: not the crude plea of encirclement, but the demonstration, archive by archive, of how specific escalations, the civil war's brutalisation, the famine's pressures, the invasion's approach, selected for the apparatus's worst features at each fork, so that the pattern the survey attributes to the party form is overdetermined by a sequence of emergencies no party form would have survived democratically. The survey's response follows its own rules. The circumstantial case in that form is strong at the level of the episode and has been credited there; the row grades the general explanation, and the general explanation must still account for the pattern's recurrence across cases where the emergencies differed in kind and timing, and for the warnings, issued in advance by the tradition's own, that identified the mechanism the emergencies are said to have created. A reader who finds the defence's version persuasive has the defeater to consult: it specifies what would settle the matter, and it is not another archive. It is a governing record of a kind that has not yet occurred. The survey leaves the row there, graded, disputed, and exposed, which is where every row on an honest scorecard should be left.

## How these verdicts were reached

A scorecard that asks to be trusted should show its working, and this section states the method by which the verdicts were assigned, so that a reader who disputes a grade can locate the exact step at which the disagreement begins. Four rules governed the grading. The first rule is proponent fidelity: each criticism was graded in the form its own leading proponent gave it, in the work the scorecard names, not in the slogan form in which criticisms usually circulate. Bohm-Bawerk graded as a slogan, Marx contradicted himself, is weaker than Bohm-Bawerk graded as an argument about the transformation's consistency, and Popper graded as a slogan, Marxism is unfalsifiable, is weaker than Popper graded through Lakatos's programme standard. The rule systematically strengthens the prosecution, deliberately, because a survey of criticism that grades weak versions has graded nothing.

The second rule is reply parity: each criticism was set against the strongest reply the literature offers, regardless of the reply's provenance, and the reply was graded by the same fidelity standard. The New Interpretation was graded as Duménil and Foley stated it, not as its popularisers inflate it; Wright's rebuilding was graded as a rebuilding, with the concession it contains counted as part of its content. The third rule is price accounting: where a reply succeeds by narrowing, redefining, or relocating the original claim, the verdict records partly answered and the surrendered territory is named in the family sections. This rule does most of the scorecard's work, and it is the rule a partisan survey in either direction would drop: the defence's surveys grade the reply's success and omit the price, the prosecution's surveys grade the original claim's failure and omit the reply. The fourth rule is target discipline: no verdict was allowed to migrate across the four targets, so that the political row's standing grade contributes nothing to the economic rows, and Morishima's theorem contributes nothing to the political one. The overall profile the survey reports, no clean answers, four standing rows, the rest partly answered at named prices, is an output of the four rules, not an editorial posture adopted in advance, and a reader who grants the rules yet disputes a grade has a genuine scholarly disagreement of the kind the defeaters exist to settle.

Two limitations of the method should be stated with the same plainness. The grading weighs literatures, and literatures have geographies: the valuation debate's centre of gravity, the stratification literature's, and the historiography of the states' records sit in different scholarly communities with different standards of closure, and a survey that reads across all of them imports judgements that specialists in each will sometimes resist. The survey accepts the risk because the alternative, fifteen separate verdicts by fifteen separate specialists with no common standard, is not a scorecard but a symposium. The second limitation is temporal. Several literatures are still moving, the valuation debate and the class analysis of the platform economy among them, and verdicts graded on moving literatures carry shorter warranties than verdicts on closed ones. The defeater column is the survey's device for handling both limitations honestly: it converts each grade into a dated, revisable claim and specifies the revision's trigger, so that the scorecard's authority rests not on the pretence of finality but on the clarity of its exposure.

## The internal origin: the sharpest critics were formed inside

Every survey of this subject contains a fact that most surveys bury, and this article is built around bringing it to the surface. The strongest criticisms of Marxism, the ones graded standing or partly answered on the scorecard because the replies could not meet them at full strength, were formulated overwhelmingly by Marxists and former Marxists. The redundancy argument was pressed by a Marxist economist. The demolition of unmechanised functional explanation came from analytical Marxism's own workshop. The revisionist challenge to the predictions was opened by a leading figure of the Second International, an executor of Engels's papers. The fullest critical history of the doctrine came from a philosopher who had been a Marxist of the Polish school. The account of the social democratic dilemma, the sharpest statement of why Marxist parties in democracies are pulled apart by their own strategy, was written by a Marxist political scientist. The theorem that broke the derivation of the falling rate of profit was proved by a Japanese Marxist economist. The warnings about authoritarianism that the century vindicated were issued by the Second International's leading theorist and by a revolutionary who died at the hands of the counter-revolution she was warning her own side about. This section verifies the pattern name by name, because the pattern is the article's namable claim and claims of this kind are worth exactly the names that carry them.

Begin with the economists, since their criticisms are the most technical and their credentials the hardest to wave away. Ian Steedman's Marx after Sraffa was not an attack from the Austrian or neoclassical outside; Steedman worked within the Marxist and Sraffian reconstruction of classical political economy, shared its premise that Marx's questions were the right questions, and concluded, with regret recorded on the page, that the value apparatus could not do the work assigned to it because the physical data of production determined prices and profit without it. Nobuo Okishio's theorem of 1961 was likewise an internal result: a Marxist economist formalising Marx's own argument about technique choice discovered that the argument, formalised, yields the opposite of the tendency it was meant to establish, given the assumption that capitalists adopt techniques that cut their costs. Neither result can be metabolised as a misunderstanding. Each was produced by applying the tradition's own standards, rigour about derivations, honesty about assumptions, to the tradition's own claims, and each forced the surviving forms of those claims into shapes their originators did not give them. When defenders reply that the critics attacked a vulgarised Marx, the biographies answer: these critics were the least vulgar readers the texts have had.

The philosophers repeat the pattern in a different register. The analytical Marxist project of the late nineteen seventies set out to rebuild historical materialism with the tools of analytic philosophy and modern social science, discarding what could not survive clarity. Within a decade its own members had produced the era's most damaging methodological criticisms. Jon Elster's demand for mechanisms beneath functional explanations, and his impatience with dialectical formulations that resisted translation into checkable claims, were not the complaints of a positivist outsider; they were the audit of a participant who had invested in the project's success and concluded that parts of its inheritance were counterfeit. G. A. Cohen, whose defence of the theory of history Elster was auditing, spent his later career migrating from the theory of history to normative political philosophy, where his work on equality and justice became an internal criticism of a different kind: an argument, pressed against his own tradition, that it had neglected the moral questions its opponents had been asking all along. Leszek Kolakowski stands at the pattern's edge and confirms it from the far side. His Main Currents of Marxism, published in Polish between 1976 and 1978 and in English in 1978, remains the most comprehensive critical history of the doctrine ever written, three volumes moving from the founders through the Golden Age to the breakdown, and its authority rests precisely on its author's formation: Kolakowski had been a Marxist philosopher of genuine standing in Poland, revised his position under the pressure of the regime that claimed the doctrine, and wrote the history as a man auditing his own education. Hostile reviewers could call him an apostate. They could not call him uninformed, and the distinction is the whole point.

### What makes a criticism internal rather than merely informed?

An internal criticism is made from within the tradition's standards, texts, and aims, by a critic whose formation was the tradition's, and who accepts enough of its framework for the criticism to register as a failure by its own lights. It cannot be answered by correcting the critic's picture of the doctrine, because the picture is the doctrine's own.

The historians and political scientists extend the roster. Eduard Bernstein's revisionism was not an external assault on German social democracy but a crisis inside its leadership: the party's theoretician, Engels's literary executor, a man whose credentials within the movement were beyond challenge, announced in the party's own journal that the evidence contradicted the programme's expectations. The party condemned his conclusions and kept his facts, and the tension between the two has structured social democracy ever since. Adam Przeworski's Capitalism and Social Democracy of 1985, written by a Polish political scientist formed in the Marxist tradition and working at its analytical frontier, posed the dilemma that still organises the study of left parties: participation in elections forces a Marxist party to appeal beyond the working class, diluting the class identity that was its reason for existing, while refusal to participate abandons the field; and the working class, even at its largest, is never the numerical majority the strategy required. The argument is devastating precisely because it is sympathetic: it assumes the tradition's goals and shows the strategy defeating them. Robert Michels belongs on the list with a note of caution. His Political Parties of 1911 formulated the iron law of oligarchy, the tendency of organisations, including avowedly democratic workers' parties, to generate self-perpetuating leaderships, and Michels had been formed inside the socialist movement he anatomised, close to its syndicalist wing. The law became a standing criticism of the political family's organisational optimism, and its author's trajectory, from socialist circles toward disillusion, is the internal pattern in biographical form.

The revolutionaries who warned complete the pattern and give it its moral weight. Kautsky's 1918 pamphlet against the Bolshevik dictatorship was written by the man the movement called its pope, the guardian of orthodoxy against Bernstein on one side and the ultra-left on the other; when he argued that the suppression of democracy would corrupt the revolution's ends, he spoke with the tradition's highest theoretical authority. Luxemburg's prison manuscript of the same year came from a revolutionary who had spent her life fighting the reformists Kautsky led, and who supported the October seizure while insisting that its emergency measures, generalised, would strangle the public life socialism needed to breathe. Djilas wrote The New Class from the top of a ruling party, as its former vice-president, and paid for the analysis with prison. These were not the criticisms of people who wished the project ill. They were the criticisms of people who had staked their lives on it, and the project's later custodians answered several of them with cells. A tradition's treatment of its internal critics is evidence about the tradition, and the survey records the evidence in both directions: the critics existed, in numbers, at the highest level of formation; and the organisations they warned about repeatedly confirmed the warnings by their handling of the warners.

### Why did the internal critics see the difficulties first?

Because they knew which claims were load-bearing. An outside critic attacks the doctrine as presented, slogans, summaries, the public catechism. An insider knows where the derivation is fragile, which prediction the programme actually rests on, which organisational habit contradicts the stated aim, because the insider was trained to lean on exactly those points.

Why did the pattern arise? Three causes, each instructive. The first is access: the serious difficulties of a doctrine are visible in its machinery, and the machinery is studied by its adherents. The transformation problem could only be pressed by someone who had worked through both volumes with the intention of using them; the functional explanation gap could only be exposed by someone rebuilding the theory of history from inside. The second cause is standards: a tradition that prizes rigour and self-criticism, as this one does in its founding texts, trains the instruments of its own correction. Marx's ruthless criticism of everything existing was quoted by the critics against the movement that canonised him, and the quotation was fair. The third cause is experience: the internal critics of the political family were not speculating about power. Kautsky watched a party he had theorised for become a state; Djilas sat in its politburo; Luxemburg died in the first weeks of the German revolution's failure. Their criticisms carry the authority of witnesses, which is why the replies to them so often take the form of disputing their loyalty rather than their evidence.

The pattern cuts both ways, and the survey states both edges without flinching. In the tradition's favour, the internal origin indicates seriousness: this is a body of thought that produced, trained, and published its own most effective critics, and the comparison with traditions whose difficulties are named only by enemies is not flattering to the others. A doctrine whose ablest students keep finding its faults is, at minimum, a doctrine whose students are honest and whose texts repay the attention that finds faults in them. Against the tradition, the pattern indicates that its difficulties are not inventions of hostile misrepresentation. When the redundancy argument, the mechanism demand, the revisionist statistics, and the oligarchy law all originate inside the house, the defence that the house has been misdescribed by visitors is unavailable. The problems are the tradition's own discoveries about itself. Any survey of the criticisms that draws only on external critics, on the Austrians and the Cold War liberals and the popular refutations, has missed the sharpest arguments and misdescribed the subject; and any defence that treats criticism as a genre of hostility has failed to notice who wrote its best examples.

## Yugoslavia as living criticism: the alternative that audited itself

One state in the record attempted to institutionalise the internal criticism of the Soviet model, and its career is the survey's final exhibit on the political family's border, because it tests the mechanism reply against a real alternative. Yugoslavia's break with Stalin in 1948 forced an ideological reconstruction, and the reconstruction took the form of self-management: the law of 1950 handed factories, in formula, to their workers' collectives, and the system's theorists presented councils, communes, and the withering of the central state as Marxism's democratic answer to Soviet statism, a living criticism built into institutions. For two decades the experiment attracted the sympathetic attention of Western Marxists precisely because it seemed to supply the missing pieces: decentralisation against the plan's command, workers' councils against the ministry, a non-aligned foreign policy against the bloc. The internal audit then arrived from the experiment's own philosophers. The Praxis school, whose journal this survey has already recorded being extinguished, argued that self-management inside a one-party monopoly was structurally falsified: the League of Communists retained the appointments, the banks, and the police, so the councils managed within a cage whose bars were the monopoly itself, and the economists' findings added that marketised self-management generated its own inequalities between firms and regions without generating the political pluralism that might have disciplined them. The system's end, in the federation's violent dissolution after its founder's death and the debt crisis of the nineteen eighties, is documented in the record articles; the survey's point is the audit's provenance and precision. Yugoslavia is the closest the century came to running the mechanism reply's experiment, changing the party form's economic shell while keeping its monopoly, and the result its own Marxist philosophers reported was that the monopoly metabolised the reform. Defenders of the reply answer that the experiment therefore confirms the reply's deeper claim, that nothing within the form suffices and the form itself must go. Critics answer that a reply which every outcome confirms has joined the immunised. The survey leaves the Yugoslav row where the evidence leaves it, as the political family's most instructive near miss, and as proof that the internal critics' standard, judge the institution by the constraint it actually builds, could be applied by the tradition to its own best hope and found the hope wanting.

## The exiles' journals: where the audits were printed

Internal criticism requires infrastructure, and the survey pauses on the journals and schools that supplied it, because the pattern's material basis explains its geography. In the West, the audits appeared where universities and independent journals sheltered them: the New Left Review, founded in 1960, published Anderson's mapping of Western Marxism and a generation of internal audits while remaining a journal of the tradition it audited; Monthly Review, founded in 1949 by Sweezy and Leo Huberman, carried the Dobb debate's American echoes and the dependency arguments to their first audiences; the analytical school worked from Oxford, Cambridge, and the American research universities, whose tenure systems, whatever their other effects, made a Steedman or an Elster professionally survivable. In the East, the infrastructure was thinner and its fate is the political family's evidence in bibliographical form. The Yugoslav journal Praxis, published from 1964 by the Zagreb and Belgrade philosophers whose names the companion article on authoritarianism records in its ledger, was the bloc's one sustained venue for Marxist criticism of Marxist states, and it was extinguished by administrative pressure in the mid nineteen seventies, its editors removed from their university posts by legislative manoeuvre in 1975. The Budapest School around Agnes Heller and her colleagues, formed as Lukács's students, lost its institutional base in the nineteen seventies and continued in exile. The contrast writes the survey's conclusion without assistance. Where the tradition's critics had journals the tradition did not control, the criticisms accumulated into literatures and the literatures into the scorecard. Where the tradition controlled the journals, the criticisms were answered by the ministry. The internal origin is thus not only a fact about gifted individuals; it is a fact about institutions, and it doubles as the strongest practical argument in the whole survey for the missing constraint, since the difference between the audit's two fates was nothing but the presence or absence of institutions the ruling doctrine could not switch off.

## Audits from the periphery: the doctrine judged where it was applied

The internal origin has a geography, and its map is incomplete until the survey records the critics who audited the doctrine from the societies it was exported to, where its universal stages met local structures the stages did not describe. José Carlos Mariátegui, the Peruvian founder whose Seven Interpretive Essays on Peruvian Reality appeared in 1928, performed the founding adaptation and, in the same act, the founding criticism: Indo-American socialism, he insisted, could not be a copy or a tracing of the European sequence, since Peru's reality, the indigenous community's surviving collectivism, the latifundio's semi-feudal relations, the imperial economy's enclaves, required the doctrine to be rebuilt from the material rather than applied to it. The orthodox parties of the continent, aligned with the Communist International's stage theory, which required a bourgeois democratic phase under national bourgeois leadership before any socialist one, received his rebuilding as heresy, and the quarrel between the stagist orthodoxy and the adapters is the periphery's version of the survey's central drama: the ablest local Marxists charging the centre's doctrine with misdescribing their societies, and the centre answering with discipline.

The dependency generation renewed the audit with economics. Andre Gunder Frank's work of the nineteen sixties charged that the Latin American communist parties' feudalism thesis, on which their stage strategy rested, was empirically false: Latin America had been capitalist, on his account, since the conquest, its underdevelopment produced by the same metropolitan connection that produced the centre's development, so that the prescribed alliance with a progressive national bourgeoisie rested on a misdiagnosis of the patient. The ensuing quarrels, with Ernesto Laclau's critique of Frank's circulationist definition of capitalism among the sharpest internal replies, belong to the cluster on Latin America in this series and are graded there. For this survey the episodes matter as provenance. From Mariátegui's essays to the dependency debates, the doctrine's application outside its home societies generated its critics from its own local adherents, who had the same double equipment as Steedman or Bernstein: formation in the texts, and a record in front of them that the texts' custodians preferred not to see. The survey's claim about the internal origin is therefore not a European curiosity. It is the doctrine's general law of criticism, operating on every continent the doctrine reached, and it is the strongest single reason for a reader to distrust any account of the criticisms that presents them as an external siege.

## The Sovietologists' audit: a field that graded its own graders

The record of the states generated a scholarly field, and the field's internal quarrels supply the survey with a useful control experiment, because Sovietology contained its own internal critics whose fate mirrors the larger pattern. The totalitarian model, associated with the early Cold War scholarship and given journalistic force by Robert Conquest's The Great Terror of 1968, read the Soviet system from the summit down: ideology, party, terror, a monolith directed by design. A revisionist generation, of whom J. Arch Getty's Origins of the Great Purges of 1985 is representative, worked the archives and the regional records and complicated the picture: centre and periphery in friction, purges driven partly by chaos and denunciation from below, a state less monolithic and more improvisational than the model allowed. The quarrel was ferocious and genuinely scholarly, and its resolution, insofar as the opened archives after 1991 resolved it, vindicated elements of both: the terror's central direction and mass scale, which the revisionists' emphasis had risked minimising, and the system's internal disorder, which the monolith model had denied. The survey draws two lessons. The first is methodological comfort: grading by proponent fidelity and price accounting works on this literature exactly as on the doctrine's, since each school was partly answered in the survey's precise sense, its strong form untenable, its narrowed form absorbed. The second lesson concerns Isaac Deutscher, the Polish Jewish journalist and historian whose biographies of Stalin and Trotsky remain the period's most readable Marxist scholarship. Deutscher wrote as a Marxist critic of Stalinism who expected the system's eventual democratisation from within, an expectation the record refused, and his failure is the internal pattern's poignant case: the critic whose analysis of the past was superb and whose prediction repeated, in a sophisticated form, the framework's oldest habit of trusting the direction of history. The survey honours the analysis and grades the expectation with the historical family's rows, where it belongs.

## The parties answer back: receptions as evidence

The internal origin has a sequel that belongs in the survey because it is evidence of the same kind the critics themselves used: what the tradition's organisations did with their internal critics when the criticisms arrived. The receptions form a spectrum, and the spectrum's shape is a finding. At one end stands Bernstein's party. The Social Democratic Party of Germany debated revisionism through the late eighteen nineties, condemned Bernstein's conclusions by resolution at its Hanover congress of 1899, and then neither expelled him nor adopted his programme, electing him to the Reichstag and tolerating a permanent internal opposition whose facts it quietly absorbed while denouncing its theory. The episode is the healthiest reception in this section and it occurred in a party that did not hold state power. At the spectrum's other end stand the receptions administered by parties that did. Luxemburg was murdered in January 1919 by counter-revolutionary soldiers in the Berlin rising's suppression, not by her own side, and the survey records the fact exactly; but her warnings were answered by the victorious Bolshevik tradition with decades of official condescension, her name honoured and her argument interred. Djilas was expelled, tried, and imprisoned by the Yugoslav party he had helped to lead, for publishing abroad the analysis his colleagues privately recognised. Kolakowski was expelled from the Polish party in 1966 and driven into exile after 1968, his critical history written from Oxford because Warsaw had made its writing impossible at home.

Between the ends of the spectrum sits the academic reception, and it complicates any simple moral. Steedman, Elster, Cohen, Wright, and Przeworski published their criticisms from university posts in open societies, were answered in journals, and built careers partly on the strength of the auditing. The tradition as an intellectual enterprise, in the settings where it held no police, behaved as the fallibilist method requires: it argued. The tradition as a governing enterprise behaved as its critics predicted: it administered. The survey draws the conclusion the pattern supports and no stronger one. The internal origin of the criticisms is a credit to the doctrine's intellectual culture and a standing problem for its political one, since the organisations that suppressed their critics were staffed by people formed in the same culture that produced the critics. What differed was not the formation but the possession of a state, which returns the survey, by a biographical road, to the political family's finding: the missing constraint is missing precisely where it would have bound the possessors. A reader assembling the article's whole argument should hold the two sections together. The internal origin tells you the criticisms are real. The receptions tell you what the criticisms were up against.

## The testimony shelf: criticism as witness

Alongside the critics who argued stands a shelf of critics who testified, and the survey must place them because their books did more to shape the general reader's verdict than any treatise. The God That Failed, the collection Richard Crossman edited in 1949, gathered six former communists and fellow travellers, Arthur Koestler, Ignazio Silone, Richard Wright, André Gide, Louis Fischer, and Stephen Spender, each narrating an allegiance and its breaking. Koestler's Darkness at Noon of 1940 had already given the Moscow trials their enduring fictional anatomy, written by a man who had been a party member and a prisoner of Franco. The testimony genre has a distinctive evidentiary character. It does not derive, it reports: the texture of belief from inside, the mechanisms by which intelligent people sustained commitments the evidence strained, the moment and manner of the break. Its weakness is the mirror of its strength, since a conversion narrative is organised by its ending and selects its memories accordingly. Graded by this survey's standards, testimony is powerful evidence about the third and fourth targets, the practice of parties and the character of states, and weak evidence about the first and second, the claims and the framework, because a man's disillusion with a party in power settles nothing about a derivation in Capital. The popular literature constantly promotes testimony across that boundary, treating a broken faith as a refuted theory. The scorecard refuses the promotion while honouring the testimony on its own ground, where it is often the best evidence available.

The internal origin pattern governs this shelf too, with a twist the survey should record. The witnesses were insiders by definition, since only an adherent can apostatise, but their formation differs from the scholar-critics of the previous section: they testify from experience of the movement rather than from study of the texts, and their criticisms bite where experience bites, on discipline, on the demand for public assent to what is privately doubted, on the treatment of persons. Kolakowski, who belongs to both shelves, having been formed as a scholar and broken by a regime, is the bridge figure, and the comprehensiveness of Main Currents comes from the combination: the derivations audited by a philosopher, the movement judged by a man who had watched it govern his own country. The survey draws the lesson for its readers plainly. When a criticism of Marxism is quoted, ask first which shelf it comes from: the study or the witness stand. Both are honourable. They prove different things, and confusing them is the four-targets error in biographical dress.

## The refutation industry: how a genre deformed its subject

One environmental factor in the literature deserves naming before the survey closes its analytical work, because it explains the poor quality of the average encounter with this subject. Refuting Marxism became, across the middle of the twentieth century, a genre with a market: the ex-communist memoir, the conversion narrative, the compendium of the doctrine's crimes, the philosophical demolition addressed to readers who would never open the demolished books. Genres have economics, and the refutation genre's economics rewarded the amalgamated target, since a book that refuted everything could be sold to everyone, and punished the distinctions this survey lives by, since a refutation of the transformation problem alone has a small natural audience. The defence developed a mirror industry with the same economics, the compendium of the doctrine's triumphs and the catechism for the already convinced, and the two industries between them occupied the shelf where the general reader shops. The scholarly literatures graded in this survey proceeded elsewhere, in journals and monographs whose print runs the industries would have found embarrassing, and the distance between the two literatures, the industrial and the scholarly, is the single best predictor of the confusion a newcomer brings to the subject. The survey's remedy has been procedural rather than polemical: name the proponent, date the work, state the target, price the reply, print the defeater. A reader equipped with the procedure can walk through either industry's warehouse and sort its stock in an afternoon, which is all the inoculation this article can offer against the next century's versions of the same shelves.

## Critiques of the critics: where the prosecution overreached

Fairness to the record requires a section the prosecution will not enjoy, because the critics have their own scorecard and several of its entries are poor. Bohm-Bawerk's founding broadside, for all its structural insight, misread the skilled labour reduction, treated Marx's dialectical presentation as a conjuring trick rather than engaging its logic, and announced a closure of the system that the subsequent century of productive Marxist economics, whatever its grades, did not exhibit. Popper's portrait of Marx himself was drawn substantially from the movement's catechism and from Engels's systematising, and scholars of the texts have shown that his Marx is more deterministic, more prophetic, and less attentive to counteracting tendencies than the Marx of the manuscripts; the charge survives against the catechism in a form its author did not bother to distinguish. Samuelson's eraser, for its part, assumed the simultaneous valuation framework within which the redundancy conclusion is easiest to reach, and the temporal school's entire case is that the assumption begs the question it purports to settle. The Cold War popularisations went further and worse, attributing to the doctrine positions its founder explicitly attacked, treating every adherent as an agent and every argument as a manoeuvre, and their circulation damaged the critics' cause by associating it with caricature: a generation of students met the caricature first and discounted the genuine articles when they arrived wearing similar colours.

The overreach has a pattern worth naming, because it is the mirror of the defence's reformulation pattern and the survey grades both. Where the defence narrows the doctrine until the criticism misses, the prosecution widens it until the criticism cannot miss: Marx becomes responsible for every claim made in his name, the framework becomes a single proposition that a single fact can kill, the record of the worst state becomes the doctrine's biography. Widening is as much an immunising stratagem as narrowing, since a target defined to include everything can be declared hit by anything, and the verdict costs nothing to reach. The scorecard's method, proponent fidelity on both sides, best replies at full strength, prices counted, exists to refuse both stratagems with the same instrument. A reader who has followed the survey this far can apply the refusal unaided: ask of any criticism whether it grades the proponent's argument or the slogan, and of any defence whether it reports the reply's price or only its conclusion, and the literature will sort itself with very little further help.

## Orwell's Catalonia: the witness who audited the auditors

The testimony shelf holds one volume that belongs also to the survey's argument about criticism itself, because its author testified against the side he had fought for and was punished by the literary marketplace of his own camp. George Orwell went to Spain in December 1936, joined the militia of the POUM, the anti-Stalinist Marxist party, was shot through the throat at the front, and returned to find the party he had served suppressed by the Soviet-aligned authorities in Barcelona, its leaders imprisoned or murdered, and the British left's press repeating the suppression's official story, that the POUM had been fascist agents. Homage to Catalonia of 1938, his account, was refused by his usual publisher, sold poorly, and made its author a marked man in the literary world he depended on. The book's evidentiary character is the survey's point. Orwell's testimony indicted not the doctrine in the abstract but a practice he had witnessed at close range, the conversion of a revolutionary alliance into a police operation against its own left wing, conducted with fabricated charges of the kind the political family's record displays at scale. He drew the conclusion that governed the rest of his writing life, that the totalitarian tendency was a temptation of power rather than a property of one ideology, and his later fiction universalised the warning. Graded by the four targets, Homage is evidence about practice, devastating and precise, and its author was careful, as the slogan versions of him are not, about the limits of what a witness can settle. The survey places him at the head of the testimony shelf because he demonstrates its standard: testimony earns its authority by testifying against the witness's own interest, and Orwell's interest, in career, in comradeship, and in the cause he had been shot for, all ran the other way.

## Where the critics converge

Six families, fifteen scorecard rows, and a hundred and fifty years of argument might be expected to produce chaos, and at the level of verdicts they do. At the level of diagnosis, a surprising convergence runs through the critics who otherwise agree about nothing, and stating it is the survey's last analytical task before the objection that challenges the whole enterprise. Critics as different as Steedman and Popper, Bernstein and Kornai, Kautsky and Dahrendorf converge on three findings. The first is that the doctrine's difficulties concentrate where it meets institutions: the theory of value falters at the passage to prices, the theory of history at the passage to mechanisms, the theory of revolution at the passage to government. Again and again the criticism takes the form of showing that a connection Marx asserted between two levels was never built, and that his successors lived in the gap. The second finding is that the tradition's repairs are real and costly in a consistent currency: each successful defence narrows the doctrine's claims, and the sum of the narrowings, not any single refutation, is the strongest cumulative case the critics possess. The third finding is that the framework's explanatory successes are also real, and concentrate where its founder concentrated: on capitalism as a system with dynamics, crises, and a history, rather than as a distribution of goods at a moment. Even hostile critics write about capitalism in a vocabulary Marx built. The convergence matters because it tells the reader what the scorecard's profile means when assembled: not a doctrine destroyed, and not a doctrine intact, but a doctrine whose asserted connections failed at identifiable joints, whose surviving parts were absorbed into the social sciences' shared equipment, and whose unbuilt joints, above all the theory of power and the theory of justice, remain open problems that no rival tradition has closed either.

## The liberal audit: liberty, pluralism, and the monism charge

One external tradition deserves a section of its own before the objection, because its criticisms are the ones the general reader is likeliest to have absorbed without knowing their source. The liberal audit, in its rigorous form, is associated with Isaiah Berlin, whose Karl Marx of 1939 remains among the most sympathetic biographies written by a non-Marxist, and whose Two Concepts of Liberty of 1958 supplied the audit's analytical core. Berlin distinguished negative liberty, the area within which a person is left unobstructed, from positive liberty, the condition of being one's own master, and argued that the positive concept carries a structural danger: once the true self is identified with a rational or collective self, coercion can be redescribed as liberation, forcing people to be free for the sake of what they would will if they were what they ought to be. The argument was aimed at a family of doctrines, and its application to the Marxist tradition runs through the vanguard's epistemic privilege, the same node this survey has met in the false consciousness audit and the substitutionism section, which is why the liberal charge, though external in provenance, converges with the internal critics' findings at the decisive point. Berlin's second charge was pluralism: human values are many, objective, and sometimes irreconcilable, and a doctrine that promises their final harmony in a classless order has mistaken the moral world's structure, so that its politics will treat genuine conflicts of value as residues to be engineered away, with the engineers holding the instruments.

The tradition's replies to the liberal audit are of the now familiar kinds. The negative liberty of the propertyless, defenders argue, is a formal shell where the material means of action are absent, so that the audit's preferred concept grades the doorway and ignores the room. The pluralism charge is answered by distinguishing the doctrine's eschatology from its analysis: whatever the final harmony's status, the critique of a present order's false harmonies, its presentation of sectional interests as universal ones, survives the pluralist correction and indeed supplies pluralism with some of its best case studies. The survey grades the liberal audit within the political and normative rows it touches and records the convergence as its finding: the strongest external tradition and the strongest internal critics, arriving by opposite roads, identified the same missing furniture in the doctrine's house, an account of why power over people who disagree must be limited even when the powerful are certain they are right. Berlin, who had read the tradition with more sympathy than most of its custodians, would have recognised the sentence as the one his biography had been written to make possible.

## The scorecard's exclusions: what this survey does not grade

An instrument is defined by its exclusions as much as by its rows, and four exclusions should be on the record before the objection section challenges the whole. The survey does not grade Marx's literary and journalistic achievement, the historical writings, the analysis of the Commune or of Bonapartism, which many readers across the spectrum regard as the work of a master and which no row of the scorecard touches, since the rows grade claims and not prose. It does not grade the moral character of individual adherents, whose courage under persecution is a matter of record in every generation and whose failings are biography, not theory. It does not grade the counterfactuals that haunt the subject, what a different Bolshevik leadership, a victorious German revolution, or a surviving Luxemburg might have built, because counterfactuals cannot carry verdicts, only illuminate them. And it does not grade the rival traditions' own scorecards, which exist and are in places as uncomfortable as this one: liberalism's rows on empire and on the compatibility of its rights with its property order, conservatism's on change, social democracy's on the distance between its programmes and its governments. The comparison basis forbids importing those grades here, since a criticism of Marxism is not answered by the observation that its critics' houses also leak. The exclusions are stated so that the survey's severity cannot be mistaken for a verdict that the graded tradition is uniquely flawed among doctrines. It is uniquely documented, which is a different thing, and the documentation exists largely because its own adherents kept the records, wrote the audits, and published the confessions this survey is built from.

## The strongest objection: immunisation rather than defence

Every survey must face the objection to its own existence, and this one is formidable. It was stated in pieces by Popper and Lakatos, given its sharpest form by Kolakowski, and pressed by the critics of the research programme tradition, and it runs as follows. A framework whose defenders respond to every failed claim by reformulating it, whose survey of criticism concludes that some criticisms stand while the framework survives, has been immunised rather than defended, and the honest conclusion from a scorecard on which several central claims are unanswered is that the programme has failed. Consider what the reformulations look like from outside. The prediction fails, and the prediction is discovered to have been conditional. The derivation breaks, and the derivation is discovered to have been a detour to a social form. The party builds a despotism, and the party is discovered to have departed from the doctrine. The class structure refuses to polarise, and the class concept is rebuilt until the refusal becomes a confirmation. At each step the defence is locally reasonable, and at each step the doctrine that emerges claims less than the doctrine that entered. A theory that can survive anything explains correspondingly little, and a survey that grades this process as partial answers all the way down has confused the programme's longevity with its health. Longevity, the objection concludes, is what institutions provide: chairs, journals, parties, and states kept the vocabulary in circulation long after the programme stopped paying its way in novel truth, and the scorecard's charitable middle verdict is the sound of a survey refusing to read its own results.

The objection deserves its full strength because it is the one criticism aimed at the survey itself, and answering it requires distinguishing three things the objection runs together. The first is reformulation as such. Every living research programme reformulates; the question Lakatos taught the philosophy of science to ask is whether the reformulations predict novel facts or merely accommodate old ones. By that test the Marxist programme's record is genuinely mixed, and this survey has graded it so: the imperialism development explained the geography of revolution after the fact, while other developments, the analysis of capitalist crisis dynamics and of commodification's spread into new domains, generated expectations that were subsequently borne out in material their authors had not examined. Mixed is not a euphemism here; it is the grade, and the objection is wrong to treat any survival as proof of immunisation when the standard it invokes, Lakatos's own, delivers a mixed verdict rather than a death certificate.

The second distinction is between the doctrine and its components. The immunisation charge assumes that the framework stands or falls as one thing, so that reformulating a part while retaining the name is a fraud. But the scorecard's whole method denies the premise: it grades claims, mechanisms, and expectations separately, records which were surrendered, and prices the surrenders. What remains in use, and this is the answer's positive half, can be inventoried without reference to the name. Class analysis, rebuilt by Wright and others, is standard equipment in stratification research practised by sociologists of every allegiance. The critique of ideology, the suspicion that ideas serve interests they do not avow, is a working tool of media studies, of the sociology of knowledge, and of ordinary political journalism. The labour process tradition descending from the analysis of the working day informs organisational sociology. World-systems analysis reorganised a generation of historical sociology and development studies. Unequal exchange and dependency mechanisms, whatever their final grades, permanently changed how development economics frames trade between unequal partners. The analysis of commodification frames debates in fields from education to care. A programme whose components are in daily use by disciplines that do not carry its name has not merely been immunised; parts of it have been naturalised, and naturalisation is a fate refuted programmes do not meet. Nobody's research is organised by the surviving components of phlogiston.

The third distinction is the concession, and the survey makes it without varnish, because the objection is partly right and its right part is the part aimed at the replies. The reformulation pattern is real. It is documented across this article's families: predictions conditionalised after failure, derivations relocated to social form, class concepts rebuilt until the counterevidence became data, outcomes disowned as departures from a doctrine whose organisational instruments produced them. The pattern is so regular that this series gives it a name and an article of its own: the reader will find it anatomised in [the article on Marxist replies to the standard critiques](/2019/12/01/marxist-replies-to-critiques/), where the replies are graded by the same standard applied here, and where the narrowing each reply performs is measured rather than excused. This survey's defence against the immunisation charge is therefore not that the pattern is absent, but that the survey has priced it: every partly answered verdict on the scorecard is a receipt for a narrowing, the standing verdicts mark where no narrowing sufficed, and the defeaters state what would change the grades. An immunised doctrine does not publish its reversal conditions. This survey has published fifteen of them, and three more that govern the whole.

## The archival turn: how the opened files regraded the rows

One development after the period of the classic criticisms changed the evidence base under several rows, and the survey should record its effect before assembling the balance sheet, because it is the nearest thing the subject has to a controlled experiment in grading. The opening of the Soviet and East European archives after 1989 and 1991 admitted scholars to the internal records of the party-states: the directives, the quotas, the correspondence between centre and periphery, the statistics the regimes had kept for themselves. The effect on the literatures was discriminating rather than uniform, which is what makes it valuable as a test. On the terror's direction and scale, the archives confirmed the central direction the totalitarian school had asserted and the revisionists had questioned, while complicating its picture of a smoothly obedient machine with evidence of chaos, local initiative, and centre-periphery friction that the revisionists had emphasised. On the economy, the internal records confirmed the shortage and falsified-reporting pathologies Kornai had modelled from outside the files, a vindication of institutional analysis over the official statistics both camps had sometimes used. On the doctrine's texts, by contrast, the archives changed almost nothing, since the derivations and the predictions had always been public, and the rows graded on texts stand where the arguments left them. The pattern is the four targets operating on new evidence: the state rows moved, the practice rows moved less, the claim rows did not move at all.

The archival turn also administered a grading to the graders. The scholars whose accounts survived contact with the files best were, disproportionately, those who had worked by the standards this survey has used throughout: proponent fidelity in reading the regime's own categories, suspicion of both the official presentation and its mirror image, and attention to mechanisms over atmospherics. The popular verdict of the early nineteen nineties, that the collapse had settled every question the survey grades, has itself been graded by the subsequent literature and found to be the amalgamation error in its moment of maximum confidence. The questions that remained open in 1989, the valuation debate, the mechanism problem, the justice dispute, the class map's redrawing, remained open in the journals a generation later, pursued by scholars for whom the Cold War's end was a change of evidence, not a change of subject. A survey written in the archival turn's light can therefore be more precise than its predecessors about which verdicts are final and which are merely old, and the scorecard's defeaters mark the difference in print.

There is a further lesson in the episode for readers of the older polemics. Much of the twentieth century's argument about the states was conducted under conditions of radical ignorance, in which the same closed society could be described as a workers' paradise or a single camp by visitors who had seen the same itinerary, and in which the absence of evidence licensed the confidence of both descriptions. The internal critics were not immune to the ignorance, but their method protected them better than the visitors' impressions protected anyone: Luxemburg reasoning from the party's structure in 1918, Djilas from the politburo's privileges in 1957, and Kornai from the shortage economy's daily operation in 1980 all reached conclusions the files later sustained, because mechanisms, once correctly identified, generate expectations that outlast the secrecy surrounding their instances. The survey's preference for mechanism over atmosphere, visible in row after row of the scorecard, is the archival turn's methodological bequest, and it is the standard by which the next generation's criticisms, of whatever doctrine, should be graded when their archives open, the scorecard's rows being offered in exactly that spirit, as findings awaiting their files.

## The balance sheet: assembling the profile

The survey has forbidden itself cross-target arithmetic, and it keeps the rule here, but a reader is owed the assembled profile stated as a profile, family by family, in the plainest language the evidence allows. The economic theory survives as a set of questions and one correspondence theorem, its derivations replaced by parallel computations and its planning project answered in its strong form by the knowledge objection. The method survives as an audited practice, its functional explanations mechanised or abandoned, its dialectics retired by its own rigorous wing, its philosophy of history chastened by enemies and heirs alike. The predictions survive in split form, the dynamics of capitalism read better than any rival of their century read them, the transition expectations falsified in their strong forms and repaired at the prices the ledger records. The politics stands as the doctrine's open wound, the one family in which the charge was made in advance, from inside, and answered by the record in the charge's favour. The sociology survives as the discipline's own equipment, its map redrawn by its heirs and its concepts naturalised past recognition of their origin. The normative foundations stand as the family's smallest and deepest file, a set of questions about justice, permissible means, and the standing persons retain, which the tradition's own philosophers now press with a rigour its founders reserved for other people's systems. Assembled, the profile is neither the refutation of the posters nor the vindication of the catechism. It is the condition of a research tradition that was strong enough to train its own auditors and unlucky enough, or honest enough, to publish them, and the survey's last analytical word is that the two halves of that sentence cannot be separated, because the auditors are the evidence for both.

## After the verdicts: the framework's afterlives

A survey of criticisms owes its reader a final accounting of what became of the criticised thing, because the afterlives are themselves evidence in the grading. The analytical school's trajectory is the most legible. The group of scholars who gathered around Cohen from the late nineteen seventies, sometimes called the September Group and by its members the No-Bullshit Marxism Group, set out to rebuild the doctrine with analytic rigour, and within two decades its leading figures had largely ceased to describe themselves as Marxists at all: Elster moved to a general theory of rationality and social mechanisms, John Roemer rebuilt egalitarianism on liberal and game-theoretic foundations, Cohen became a philosopher of equality arguing with Rawls's heirs. The school's own verdict on its project, delivered by emigration, was that the questions survived the doctrine: distribution, exploitation, class, and historical explanation remained worth a life's work, while the system that had bundled them did not survive the bundling's audit. The survey counts the trajectory as the internal origin's last exhibit and its most ambiguous. A tradition whose ablest auditors conclude by leaving has been graded by the tribunal it trained. A tradition whose questions colonise the disciplines its auditors leave for has been naturalised past the point where leaving is a refutation.

The other afterlives are briefer to state. In historiography, the social history the Marxist historians built, the history from below associated with Thompson and with Eric Hobsbawm, whose syntheses carried a Marxist framework to the largest readership any historian of his century achieved, permanently changed the profession's sense of whose past counts as history, and the change survived the framework's local defeats. In the universities of the developing world, the dependency and world-systems descendants of the tradition still organise curricula and policy argument, graded by their home literatures with the same mixture the scorecard records. In political practice, the parties that amended the doctrine toward alternation and rights mostly dwindled, while movements that kept the vocabulary and dropped the economics proliferated, a selection effect the political family's analysis predicts and the survey merely notes. And in the general culture, the vocabulary of class, ideology, commodification, and crisis circulates as common property, detached from the system that coined its modern senses, which is either the doctrine's defeat or its victory depending on whether one grades systems or words. The scorecard grades systems, claims, and records. By those grades the article's findings stand, and the afterlives explain why the findings, however severe, have not emptied the library: the questions outlasted the answers, and the critics, internal above all, are the reason anyone can now say precisely which is which.

## Reading a criticism: source, force, and the route onward

The survey closes with the equipment a reader needs to use the literature without this article's help, and the equipment is three questions. The first question is the target question: what is this criticism aimed at, a claim, the framework, a practice, or a state, and does its evidence reach the target it names? The second question is the source question, and it must be asked correctly: not whether the critic is friendly or hostile, since hostility has produced accurate criticisms and friendship has produced evasions, but what the critic's formation entitles the criticism to. An internal criticism cannot be dismissed as misdescription; an external criticism cannot be dismissed as ignorance without checking whether it is Steedman's case or the slogan version. The third question is the force question: what would answer this criticism, and has the answer been given, at what price? A reader who asks the three questions in order will find that most popular writing on the subject fails at the first, most partisan writing at the second, and most scholarly writing, which is better, occasionally at the third, where a reply is celebrated without its price being counted.

### Where does each family receive its full hearing in this series?

Each critique named in this survey has an article of its own in the cluster or its neighbours, where the derivations are worked, the texts quoted at length, and the replies heard in full. The survey grades; the articles try the cases. A reader pursuing one family should start from that family's article and return here for the comparative grade.

The routing, stated once and plainly, is this. The economic family's technical core, the opening derivation and Bohm-Bawerk's four arguments, is tried in the Bohm-Bawerk article linked from that family's section, and the planning controversy in the calculation article linked beside it. The methodological family's question, whether the framework is a science and by what standard, is tried in the science article. The political family's charge is tried in the authoritarianism article, which holds the pattern evidence and the verdict ledger for that charge. The sociological family's master question, the fate of the working class under developed capitalism, is tried in the working class article. The replies, graded as a set and named as a pattern, are tried in the replies article. The historical and normative families are distributed across the cluster's case articles, since their evidence is the record itself. What this survey alone supplies is the map, the grades, and the provenance: the knowledge of where each argument lives, how it has fared, and who, formed where, made it.

A last word on the survey's own limits, since a grading instrument should state its tolerances. The verdicts are judgements about literatures, made by weighing the best replies against the arguments as their proponents stated them, and scholars of standing dispute several of them, particularly where the replies are young and the technical literature is still moving, as in the valuation debates. The defeaters are the survey's protection against its own fallibility: they convert each verdict from an epitaph into a standing offer. If the reconstruction arrives, if the alternation is sustained, if a forecast is borne out against the rival named for it, the grades change, and the change will be recorded in the same table that now records the grades. Until then, the findings stand as stated: six families, no clean answers, several standing charges, and the sharpest of them written, overwhelmingly, by the tradition's own.

## Five misreadings, corrected in one place

The survey's findings can be compressed, for the reader who will meet this subject mainly in other people's summaries, into the correction of five misreadings that between them account for most of the confusion in circulation. The first misreading is that the critiques form a single case. They form six families aimed at four targets, and the case metaphor, with its implication of a verdict that settles everything, is the original error from which the others grow. The second misreading is that all the critiques target the same thing, so that answering one answers the pile: the transformation problem and the terror are not the same argument, and the literature that treats them as one argument, in either direction, has not begun to read. The third misreading is that the collapse of the communist states settles the theoretical questions. The collapse settles a great deal about the states and, through the connecting argument this survey built, a specific and damaging amount about the doctrine's theory of power; it settles nothing about the derivation of prices, the mechanisms of ideology, or the class structure of the surviving societies, which is why those literatures continued after 1991 without noticing an obituary. The fourth misreading is that the critiques come from outside the tradition, the work of enemies who never understood it. The survey has spent a fifth of its length on the correction: the sharpest critiques were written by the tradition's own economists, philosophers, historians, party leaders, and politburo members, and any account that omits them has omitted the subject. The fifth misreading is the defence's mirror, that because no single criticism refutes the whole, the whole stands unrefuted and intact. The scorecard's profile, with its empty answered column, its four standing rows, and its decade of narrowings priced and recorded, is not intactness; it is the specific condition of a framework whose parts have been graded separately because the whole could no longer be graded at once. A reader who can state the five corrections without notes has acquired the survey's essential equipment, and the families and the scorecard exist to give the corrections their evidence.

## The literatures, family by family: a reader's shelf

For the reader who wants to go past the survey into the literatures themselves, this section names the shelf, family by family, in the order a newcomer should climb it. In the economic family, begin with the exchange in its classic documents: the third volume's transformation chapters against Bohm-Bawerk's Close of His System, then Sweezy's presentation of Bortkiewicz, then Steedman's Marx after Sraffa for the modern form, with Morishima's theorem as the defence's finest hour and Kliman's Reclaiming Marx's Capital as the single-system school's fullest statement. In the philosophical family, Popper's Poverty of Historicism gives the charge, Lakatos's methodology gives its refined standard, Cohen's Theory of History gives the framework's best modern form, and Elster's Making Sense of Marx gives the audit, four books that between them contain the family's whole modern argument. In the historical family, Bernstein's Preconditions and Luxemburg's Reform or Revolution remain the founding exchange, readable in an evening and inexhaustible in seminar, with the Brenner debate's collected volumes as the sequel that shows the framework doing history well. In the political family, the shelf is short and severe: Kautsky's Dictatorship of the Proletariat, Luxemburg's Russian Revolution manuscript, Djilas's New Class, and the authoritarianism article in this series for the pattern they belong to. In the sociological family, Dahrendorf's Class and Class Conflict states the alternative map, the Affluent Worker volumes give the empirical episode, Wright's Classes gives the rebuilding, and Nairn's Break-Up of Britain gives the nationalism confession in its author's own anguished prose. In the normative family, Lukes's Marxism and Morality maps the paradox, Geras's controversy article gives the justice dispute its evidence, and Cohen's later work gives the tradition's moral self-audit at its most rigorous. Kolakowski's Main Currents stands behind the whole shelf as the one work that attempts everything, three volumes in which a former believer grades the entire tradition with a philosopher's equipment and a witness's knowledge, and with which every other book on this shelf is, in some chapter, arguing.

Two reading rules accompany the shelf. The first is the survey's own: read the criticism and the best reply as a pair, never either alone, and keep a note of the price each reply pays, since the pair with its price recorded is the smallest unit of honest knowledge in this field. The second rule concerns translations and editions, which matter more here than in most subjects: several of the tradition's central texts reached their readers in forms their authors did not finalise, the later volumes of Capital above all, edited by Engels from manuscripts, and the scholarly arguments about what Marx wrote, what Engels constructed, and what the codifiers simplified are part of the criticisms' substance rather than pedantry around it. The reader who knows which object a book is grading, Marx's manuscripts, Engels's edition, or the movement's catechism, holds the key that the names section of this article supplied, and the shelf will open in the right order.

Your marks on this material are won in evaluation, and evaluation is exactly what this article trains. ## For teachers and seminar leaders

Teach the four-targets distinction before you teach any single criticism, because it prevents the confusion that ruins the topic in the classroom, the slide from a state's record to a theory's truth with no argument in between. Set the scorecard as a seminar instrument: assign each row to a pair of students, one to prosecute the criticism in its proponent's own terms, one to defend with the best reply, and require the pair to agree on the defeater before they disagree about the verdict. The exercise teaches the cluster's central skill, since a class that can state what would change a grade has understood the grade. Use the internal origin as the second lesson. Students arrive expecting the critics to be the doctrine's enemies; showing them Steedman's biography, Bernstein's party standing, and Luxemburg's prison manuscript rearranges their picture of how intellectual traditions actually work, and it models the standard you want in their essays, that the strongest criticism of a position is the one its own ablest adherents could not answer.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Your use for this survey is orientation: which literatures are settled, which are live, and where the evidence for a claim actually sits. Three cautions from the scorecard's construction apply directly to your work. First, the testimony shelf and the scholarly shelf prove different things, and copy that promotes a defector's memoir into a refutation of a derivation commits, in biographical dress, the familiar four-targets error. Second, several verdicts graded partly answered rest on technical literatures, the valuation debate above all, that are still moving; a report that treats the transformation question as closed in either direction is a decade behind one of the two camps. Third, the internal origin is a reporting resource: the tradition's own critics, from Djilas on the new class to Wright on the rebuilt class map, are the sources least vulnerable to the charge of caricature, and the replies article in this series grades their opponents' answers with the same instruments. Where this survey routes a question to a dedicated article, the evidence and the figures, where the series prints them, are held there.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What are the main criticisms of Marxism?

They sort into six groups according to what they attack. Economic arguments dispute the value theory, the passage from values to prices, and the workability of planning. Methodological arguments dispute testability and the style of explanation. Historical arguments measure the predictions against the record. Political arguments concern power and its limits. Sociological arguments concern class and its rivals. Normative arguments concern justice and permissible means. The groups differ enormously in strength, and treating them as one combined case is the error this survey exists to correct.

### Q: What are the six families of criticism?

First, the economic family: transformation, redundancy, calculation, incentives. Second, the philosophical family: falsifiability, historicism, functional explanation, dialectics. Third, the historical family: the fate of the predictions about impoverishment, polarisation, revolution, and profit rates. Fourth, the political family: the authoritarian outcome and the absent theory of constraint. Fifth, the sociological family: intermediate strata, layered stratification, nation and faith as competing allegiances. Sixth, the normative family: whether a standard of justice exists inside the framework and what the transition may permissibly do. Each family has its own literature, its own standards of proof, and its own verdict profile on the scorecard.

### Q: Which criticisms of Marxism have been answered?

None cleanly, and that negative finding is among the survey's most important. The strongest replies succeed by shrinking the claim they defend. The valuation conventions rescue the aggregate equalities by redefining their terms. Lange's model answers the arithmetic form of the calculation objection while the knowledge form persists. Wright's rebuilt class map answers polarisation by replacing the map. Cohen's elaborations answer the mechanism demand by accepting it as an obligation. Each reply is a genuine intellectual achievement, and each is graded partly answered because each preserves something real at the price of something originally asserted. A survey claiming outright answers would be grading effort instead of results.

### Q: Which criticisms still stand?

Four rows carry that grade. The incentive and innovation problem stands against the planned economy's design, since no durable institutional substitute for market discipline over innovation has been exhibited. Absolute immiseration stands refuted as a prediction in its strong form across the advanced economies. The expectation of revolution in the most developed countries stands against a hundred and fifty years of contrary geography. The durability of national and religious allegiance stands against the framework's expectations, confessed by its own historians. And the political charge stands: the theory of constraint on power was never built, and the pattern the internal critics predicted recurred wherever the party form governed unchecked.

### Q: Do the strongest critiques come from Marxists themselves?

Overwhelmingly, yes, and the survey verifies the claim name by name. Steedman pressed redundancy from within Marxist economics. Okishio broke the profit rate derivation from within the same field. Elster dismantled unmechanised functional explanation from within analytical Marxism. Bernstein opened the revisionist challenge from the leadership of the Second International. Kolakowski wrote the great critical history after a formation in the Polish Marxist school. Przeworski framed the social democratic dilemma as a Marxist political scientist. Luxemburg and Kautsky issued the political warnings from the movement's summit. External critics contributed indispensably, Bohm-Bawerk, Mises, Hayek, Popper among them, but the deepest cuts were made by hands the tradition trained.

### Q: What is the difference between criticising Marx and criticising communist states?

They are criticisms of different targets, settled by different evidence. Criticising Marx addresses propositions in texts and a framework built from them, and is answered by derivations, textual scholarship, and comparative explanatory performance. Criticising communist states addresses the performance and conduct of governments, and is answered by the historical record of those governments. Connecting the second to the first requires an argument showing that the doctrine, not circumstance or inheritance, steered the practice, and that argument must withstand the counterexamples of adherents who governed otherwise. Skipping it converts history into a poster. Supplying it, as the political family's best critics did, makes the states' record count with full and legitimate force.

### Q: Which failed predictions matter most?

Weight falls where risk and centrality meet. The expectation of revolution in advanced industrial countries matters most, because it stood near the framework's centre and excluded the geography that actually occurred. Absolute impoveration matters next, since Bernstein's evidence against it opened the revisionist era and the long record in the developed economies ran against its strong form. The derived falling tendency of the profit rate matters in a different way: its failure was technical, shown by Okishio's theorem from inside Marxist economics, and its surviving versions are altered claims. Predictions hedged by their authors, or peripheral to the programme, cost the framework correspondingly less when they fail.

### Q: Is the theory or the practice at fault?

The survey's answer is that the question conceals the four targets and must be divided before it can be answered. Some faults belong to claims: derivations that break, predictions that failed in their strong forms. Some belong to the framework: explanations without mechanisms, a missing theory of constraint, a contested account of justice. Some belong to practice: parties that suppressed the public life their own critics told them was indispensable. And some outcomes belong to circumstance, which the circumstantial reply correctly identifies in episodes while failing as a general explanation. Assigning every fault to practice protects the theory by definition. Assigning every fault to theory ignores the adherents who acted otherwise. The scorecard assigns case by case.

### Q: Has Marxism been refuted?

Not as a whole, because a whole is not the kind of thing this body of thought is. Specific claims have been refuted or badly damaged: the strong immiseration thesis, the derived profit rate tendency, the two-camp polarisation map. Framework components have been graded down: functional explanation without mechanisms, prophecy without risk. Other components remain in working use across the social sciences, in class analysis, ideology critique, and the study of commodification. The political charge stands unanswered at the level of theory. Refutation is the right word for particular rows of the scorecard and the wrong word for the table, which is why the survey prints verdicts row by row, each carrying its stated reversal condition.

### Q: Where should you start with the criticisms?

Start with the four targets, since that distinction costs an hour and saves the rest of the reading from confusion. Then take the families in the order of their evidence: the historical family's record is the most accessible, the economic family's arguments the most technical, and the political family's charge the most consequential. Read at least one internal critic early, Bernstein on the predictions or Luxemburg on the Russian Revolution, to calibrate what criticism from inside sounds like. Keep the scorecard open as you read, checking each argument against its row, its best reply, and its defeater. Finish with this series' article on the replies, where the defence is graded by the same standard.

### Q: How does the internal origin grade the survey's selection of critics?

It acts as a quality filter on the survey itself. A selection drawn from hostile outsiders would systematically miss the strongest material, because the deepest difficulties are visible only in the machinery, which adherents know from working it. The survey therefore weights its selection toward critics whose formation was inside the tradition, not from favouritism but from evidence: those are the criticisms the replies have found hardest. The filter also disciplines fairness. An internal critic cannot be waved away as a caricaturist, so including the internal critics forces the survey to grade the arguments they made at full strength, and to record standing verdicts where the replies, however distinguished, did not meet them.

### Q: What should a student separate between a criticism's source and its force?

Separate biography from validity, in both directions. A criticism's source tells you what it cannot be dismissed as: an insider's objection cannot be dismissed as misunderstanding, a witness's testimony cannot be dismissed as theory. But source never settles force. Force is settled by the argument's fit to its target, the evidence it commands, and whether the best reply meets it or merely narrows the claim it attacked. Students lose marks by grading the person: treating an external critic as refuted by an address, or an internal critic as decisive by a biography. Name the source for the record, then grade the argument as though it had arrived unsigned.

### Q: Why were the sharpest criticisms written by Marxists and former Marxists?

Three causes recur. Access comes first: the fragile joints of a doctrine, the unbuilt transformation, the unmechanised explanation, the organisational habit contradicting the stated aim, are visible to those trained to lean on them. Standards come second: a tradition whose founding texts demand ruthless criticism of everything existing forges the tools of its own auditing, and its ablest students turned those tools inward. Experience comes third: the political critics had watched parties become states from inside the rooms where it happened, and testified with a witness's authority. The combination explains the pattern without mystery. Doctrines are audited most severely by the people who know where the load is carried.

### Q: How does the scorecard order the families of critique?

By target, not by strength, following the order in which a reader meets the doctrine itself. The economic rows come first because the theory of value is the foundation the other claims build on. The philosophical rows follow, grading the method by which all the claims are held. The historical rows grade what the framework said would happen. The sociological rows grade its map of society, the political rows its handling of power, and the normative rows close the table because they ask what the whole enterprise is for and what it may do. Ordering by target keeps unlike verdicts from being summed, and lets a reader enter the table at the family their question belongs to.

### Q: What separates a verdict that carries its defeater from a verdict that cannot be reversed?

A defeater names beforehand a concrete development whose occurrence would change the grade: a value reconstruction that meets the conditions stated for it, a governing record of the specified kind, a forecast borne out against the rival it was tested against. Both sides can recognise the event when it happens, which is what makes the verdict a claim about the world rather than a loyalty. A verdict without one is insulated against the future, since no imaginable evidence has been agreed to count against it. The distinction is the survey's answer to the immunisation charge: fifteen verdicts are printed with their reversal conditions attached, and the whole table stands under three more that govern it as a set.
