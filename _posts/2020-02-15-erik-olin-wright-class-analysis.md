Class analysis in the Marxist tradition begins with a prediction that embarrassed its defenders for a century. Capitalist development, on the classical account, simplifies the structure of society. Small producers are ruined or absorbed, independent crafts disappear into factories, and the population sorts itself into two great camps: those who own productive property and those who own nothing but their capacity to work. Erik Olin Wright built his reputation by refusing to look away from the fact that the sorting never happened. Between the owners and the wage labourers there grew, decade after decade, a vast intermediate population of managers, supervisors, engineers, teachers, technicians, administrators, and credentialed specialists. These people sold their labour power, which placed them on one side of the classical divide, yet they directed the labour of others, held authority inside production, and drew incomes swollen by scarce credentials, which placed them awkwardly on the other. Wright was the most empirically productive Marxist sociologist of his generation because he treated that awkward population not as an embarrassment to be footnoted but as the central problem his theory had to solve, and because he then built survey machinery on an international scale to test whether his solutions survived contact with evidence.

The difficulty deserves to be felt before any framework is introduced, because its force explains why two generations of Marxist writing circled around it. A theory of class that cannot locate the people who run production on a daily basis is a theory with a hole at its centre. The foreman who hires and disciplines, the plant manager who sets the pace of a line she does not own, the software architect whose salary reflects a credential monopoly rather than any property title, the headteacher who manages a budget and a staff while remaining a salaried employee: each of these figures exercises powers the classical vocabulary assigns to capital, while lacking the ownership that vocabulary treats as decisive. Calling them all workers flattens real differences in authority and material interest. Calling them a new middle class abandons the relational logic that distinguishes Marxist analysis from a mere ranking of incomes. Wright's career can be read as a refusal of both evasions, sustained across four decades, two incompatible theoretical frameworks, and the largest comparative survey enterprise Marxist sociology has produced.

## Class analysis and the problem it could not avoid

Start with the prediction itself, stated in its strongest classical form. In the account Marx gave of capitalist development, competition concentrates ownership. The independent artisan cannot match the costs of the mechanised workshop, the small shopkeeper cannot match the chain, the peasant proprietor cannot match the agricultural firm, and each failure swells the ranks of those who must sell their labour power to live. Intermediate strata are transitional on this view, survivals of older modes of production being ground down by a logic that recognises only two stable positions. The political corollary mattered as much as the sociological one. A polarised society would eventually face a confrontation between a shrinking class of owners and an enormous class of the dispossessed, and the second of those classes, organised by production itself, would become the agent of transformation. Every element of that expectation depends on the intermediate population shrinking rather than growing.

What happened instead is the stubborn fact at the origin of Wright's work. Managerial and supervisory employment expanded throughout the twentieth century in every advanced capitalist economy. Professional and technical occupations grew faster than manual ones. The state, itself a huge employer, layered administrators, inspectors, planners, and educators across the economy. Credentialed expertise became a normal route to a comfortable income, and the holders of that expertise were neither owners in any meaningful sense nor proletarians in the classical portrait. A defender of the classical prediction could respond in several ways, and Wright catalogued the responses with some impatience. One could declare the intermediate strata a residual category destined to vanish eventually, which converts a falsified expectation into an unfalsifiable postponement. One could redefine ownership so that control counts as ownership, which dissolves the distinction the theory needs. One could treat income and lifestyle as the real markers of class, which abandons the theory for stratification description. Or one could do what Wright did, which was to ask what class theory would have to look like if the intermediate positions were permanent features of capitalism rather than debris awaiting clearance.

### Why did the polarisation prediction fail in practice?

Because capitalist development generated new intermediate positions faster than it destroyed old ones. Mechanisation eliminated independent producers while simultaneously creating supervisory hierarchies, technical specialisms, and administrative layers inside enlarged organisations, and the expanding state added professional employment of its own, so the middle of the structure was continuously rebuilt even as its older occupants were displaced.

That rebuilding is the phenomenon any serious treatment must explain, and explaining it requires precision about what a class position is. The relational definition, which the tradition's own guide to [the Marxist theory of class](/2011/09/01/marxist-theory-of-class-guide/) sets out in full, treats class not as a rank or an income band but as a position within social relations of production: a location defined by its relation to other locations, above all by ownership or non-ownership of productive assets and by the exploitation that flows from that asymmetry. On a relational definition, a stratum cannot be identified by inspecting its members one at a time. It exists only in its connection to what it dominates, what dominates it, and what it exchanges with. This is why the intermediate population is such a severe test. Its members appear, on inspection, to face in two directions at once, and a relational theory must either find the relation that unifies their position or concede that its map has territories it cannot chart.

Wright's formation helps explain his refusal to concede. Trained as a sociologist in the United States during the nineteen seventies, he worked in an intellectual environment where Marxist theory was being rebuilt with the tools of empirical social science, and where the analytical Marxist circle, whose programme the series treats in its [complete guide to analytical Marxism](/2020/01/01/analytical-marxism-complete-guide/), demanded that inherited concepts either submit to clarification and testing or be discarded. He shared that demand without sharing the suspicion of class that accompanied it in some quarters. Where others concluded that the failure of polarisation discredited the concept of class itself, Wright concluded that the concept had never been specified carefully enough to fail or succeed. A vague theory cannot be refuted by an awkward population, because vagueness absorbs every anomaly. His first task, therefore, was specification: to state the criteria of class location so exactly that a manager's position could be derived rather than intuited, and so exactly that evidence could in principle show the derivation to be wrong.

### What makes the middle positions a theoretical problem rather than a residual category?

A residual category names whatever a scheme cannot place, whereas these positions are occupied by the people who exercise daily authority over production, so a scheme that cannot place them cannot describe how production is organised, who commands within it, or where the interests generated by that command point politically.

The scale of the stakes becomes clearer when the political dimension is brought into view. Intermediate strata vote, organise, strike, and abstain in patterns that neither classical expectation captures. Managers identify with the firms that employ them while resenting the shareholders who own them. Professionals defend credential barriers with a tenacity that resembles property defence, yet many also staff the public services and unions of the labour movement. Teachers occupy staffrooms that are workplaces and hierarchies at once. Any political sociology built on a two-class map will misread these groups repeatedly, attributing their behaviour to false consciousness when the map fails, which is another way of postponing the theoretical work. Wright's insistence was that ambivalence of this kind is data. If a stratum behaves ambivalently in systematic ways, the explanation may be that its location is itself contradictory, and the task is to specify the contradiction rather than to deplore the behaviour it produces. That single move, treating political ambivalence as the signature of a structural position, generated his first framework and much of his influence beyond Marxist circles.

There is also a quieter reason the problem mattered to him, visible across his writing: intellectual honesty inside a tradition he intended to remain within. Wright never treated Marxism as a faith to be protected from evidence. He treated it as a research programme whose core claims about exploitation and class could be sharpened, tested, partly confirmed, and partly revised, and he regarded the middle class problem as the programme's hardest internal test precisely because it arose from the theory's own prediction. A difficulty imported by hostile critics can be parried. A difficulty generated by one's own premises has to be answered. The general question of the intermediate strata, their history, and their composition is treated in the companion article on [the petty bourgeoisie and the middle class](/2011/12/01/petty-bourgeoisie-and-middle-class/), which this profile draws on for the problem while reserving its own space for the man who made the problem his life's work.

### Who was Erik Olin Wright and where did he work?

A sociologist based for most of his career at the University of Wisconsin Madison, where he directed a long running comparative research project on class structure, trained generations of graduate students in empirical class analysis, and wrote the sequence of books in which his successive frameworks were proposed, tested, and revised.

His biography, in outline, is the biography of an academic who chose the unfashionable centre of his tradition and stayed there. Born in nineteen forty seven and educated at Harvard and Berkeley, he arrived at Wisconsin in the mid nineteen seventies and remained for the rest of his career, becoming one of the most cited sociologists in the United States while working on a concept many of his colleagues considered obsolete. He died in twenty nineteen, leaving a body of work unusual in two respects. Its theoretical proposals were stated with a precision that invited refutation, and its empirical programme actually attempted the refutation, through survey instruments administered across many countries under his direction. Sociologists with no Marxist commitments adopted his class schema as a standard tool, which is a fate few Marxist concepts achieve, and the reasons for that adoption are part of the assessment this article eventually reaches. First, though, the problem has to be followed into his initial attempt at a solution, because the shape of that attempt, and the reasons he abandoned it, contain the whole drama of his class analysis in miniature.

## The first solution: contradictory locations within class relations

Wright's initial answer, developed in essays of the mid nineteen seventies and given book form in Class Crisis and the State in nineteen seventy eight, began by refusing the premise that every person must occupy exactly one class position. The classical map assumed a one to one correspondence between individuals and locations. His proposal replaced that assumption with a distinction between class relations and the positions people hold within them. Capitalist production defines a set of fundamental relations, above all the relation between owners of productive property and sellers of labour power. Real individuals, however, can be situated in more than one of those relations simultaneously, because the concrete jobs an economy generates bundle together functions that the abstract model separates. A manager directs production and disciplines labour, which are functions of capital, while also selling labour power and remaining dismissible by owners, which are marks of the worker's position. The manager is therefore not misclassified by a two-class scheme and not a member of some third class either. The manager occupies a contradictory location, pulled by the opposed interests of the two fundamental positions the job combines.

### What are contradictory class locations?

positions that simultaneously occupy capitalist and working-class locations within production. Managers control production and direct labour, which is a capitalist function, while selling their own labour power and owning no means of production, which is a working-class position. The contradiction is objective rather than a matter of perception.

The word objective carries the weight of the proposal and separates it from a family of misunderstandings Wright spent years correcting. A contradictory location is not a state of mind. It does not mean that managers feel torn, although many do, nor that they misunderstand their interests, nor that survey researchers have miscoded them. It means that the position itself, described in terms of the relations that constitute it, contains opposed determinations: the same job that subordinates its holder to capital also deputises its holder as capital's agent over other workers. Interests follow from positions on this account, so a contradictory location generates contradictory interests, and the political ambivalence of managerial strata stops being a puzzle about consciousness and becomes a prediction about structure. Where the location tilts toward the capitalist pole, behaviour tilts with it; where it tilts toward the working class pole, allegiance shifts accordingly. The framework thus promised to explain variation within the intermediate strata, not merely to label it.

Wright worked the idea into a differentiated map rather than a single halfway category. Between the bourgeoisie and the proletariat he placed the contradictory location of managers and supervisors, whose authority over labour draws them toward capital while their lack of ownership holds them within the ranks of employees. Between the proletariat and the old petty bourgeoisie of independent producers he placed semi autonomous employees: workers who retain substantial control over their own labour process, over pace, method, and the organisation of their tasks, without employing others or owning significant property. University lecturers, some craftspeople inside firms, and certain technical specialists illustrated the type. Between the bourgeoisie and the petty bourgeoisie stood small employers, who own and work alongside a handful of employees, sharing the owner's relation to property and something of the worker's relation to the labour process. Each location was defined by its combination of the basic relations, and each carried a hypothesis about the political tendencies its occupants would display.

### What separates a contradictory location from a middle class in the ordinary sense?

An ordinary middle class is defined by standing between rich and poor on some scale of income, status, or lifestyle, whereas a contradictory location is defined by the relations its holder simultaneously occupies, so membership follows from the structure of the job rather than from where its salary falls on a distribution.

That distinction protected the framework from the most common dismissal, which treated it as old stratification language in Marxist dress. A gradational scheme sorts people by how much they have. Wright's first scheme sorted positions by what their holders do to and for one another inside production: who appropriates surplus, who commands, who obeys, who controls the conditions of their own work. Two people with identical incomes could occupy different locations, and two people in the same location could receive very different incomes, without straining the scheme at all. The scheme also handled change over time in a way gradational language cannot. When a firm strips supervisors of hiring authority, the location itself moves toward the proletarian pole, whatever happens to salaries, and the framework predicts a corresponding shift in interests and alliances. Class, on this first account, remained firmly relational: a matter of structured relations between positions, not of possessions stacked against a ruler.

The explanatory ambitions were considerable, and Wright stated them plainly. The framework was meant to illuminate why managerial employees often support collective bargaining for themselves while opposing it for their subordinates, why professionals defend public services they staff while resisting the taxation that funds them, why supervisors can be the most militant defenders of a firm and, after a restructuring that strips their authority, among its bitterest critics. It was meant to explain, in other words, the observed instability of middle strata politics without recourse to manipulation or confusion. Critics inside and outside the Marxist tradition pressed on its weak points from the start. The boundaries between locations were hard to operationalise: how much control over one's labour process makes an employee semi autonomous, and measured how? The scheme multiplied categories without a principle limiting their number, since every bundle of functions could claim its own location. And the underlying dimensions, ownership, control of labour, control of one's own work, sat uneasily together, because the theory never fully explained why these particular dimensions defined class rather than a general sociology of workplace authority.

### Why did semi autonomous employees trouble the first framework from within?

Because their defining feature, control over the immediate labour process, described a property of jobs that varies continuously and cuts across the ownership relation the theory treated as fundamental, so the category threatened to measure autonomy in work rather than class in the Marxist sense, a difficulty Wright himself acknowledged.

Wright's response to such difficulties was characteristic, and it set the pattern for his whole career. He did not defend the framework as a doctrine. He treated the objections as specifications for a better instrument, and he set about building the empirical machinery that could tell him where the scheme worked and where it merely relabelled puzzles. That machinery, the comparative survey project described in later sections, was designed alongside the theory rather than after it, and the interplay between the two is the real subject of his achievement. Before the machinery can be appreciated, though, the framework's fate has to be followed, because within a decade of proposing it Wright dismantled its foundations himself and rebuilt the entire analysis on a different basis. The rebuilding is the most instructive episode in his work, and the one accessible summaries almost always omit.

Understanding why he rebuilt requires a detour through a neighbouring achievement, the reformulation of exploitation by John Roemer, which the series examines separately in its article on [Roemer's theory of exploitation](/2020/03/01/roemer-theory-of-exploitation/). Roemer had shown that exploitation could be modelled with the tools of general equilibrium theory, as a consequence of unequal distribution of productive assets, without relying on the labour theory of value and without describing a transfer of labour inside the production process. Whatever one thinks of that reformulation, and Marxists argued about it ferociously, it offered something Wright's first framework lacked: a single generative mechanism from which class positions could be derived rather than assembled. Feed an unequal distribution of assets into the model, and classes, exploitation, and their correspondence emerge as results. Wright saw in that mechanism the chance to give his map of positions the deductive spine it had been missing. The price, which his critics would name with precision, was that class would instead be derived from the ownership of assets, and ownership is a property of persons and portfolios, not obviously a relation between people in production at all.

### What did the Roemer reformulation offer that the first framework lacked?

A mechanism. Where the contradictory locations scheme assembled positions from observed bundles of functions, the asset based approach derived positions from an initial distribution of productive resources, so the map of classes could be generated from a principle instead of being drawn case by case from the occupational structure.

The transition also reflected a broader migration inside analytical Marxism, away from the vocabulary of structures and functions toward models of individuals, assets, choices, and equilibria. Wright participated in that migration while resisting its destination. He never accepted that class should dissolve into microeconomics, and he insisted throughout the nineteen eighties that exploitation named a real relation of domination and not merely an unequal outcome. Yet the instrument he adopted in nineteen eighty five pulled in the other direction, toward distribution, and the tension between the instrument and the insistence is exactly what his critics seized upon. The second framework must therefore be read in two registers at once: as a technical improvement that answered genuine defects of the first, and as a conceptual wager whose cost only became visible once the framework was stated clearly enough to be attacked. Wright, to his credit, stated it with exemplary clarity.

## The second solution: class from the ownership of assets

The book Classes, published in nineteen eighty five, announced the replacement openly. Wright did not present the new scheme as a refinement of contradictory locations. He presented it as a different foundation. Class positions, on the new argument, are generated by exploitation, and exploitation is generated by unequal ownership of productive assets. Capitalism is defined by one asset asymmetry, ownership of the means of production, but it is not the only asymmetry operating inside capitalist societies, and a class map adequate to those societies must register the others. Two further assets mattered. Some employees control organisation assets: the physical and administrative apparatus of coordinated production, the authority to direct complex divisions of labour, allocate resources among units, and make decisions whose effects bind hundreds of other workers. Others hold skill assets, embodied in credentials and scarce expertise that the labour market rewards with rents, since a certified qualification restricts supply in something like the way a property title restricts access. Managers exploit through organisation assets, on this account, because control of the apparatus lets them appropriate part of the surplus that coordinated labour produces. Experts exploit through skill assets, because the credential barrier lets them capture more than the value their labour would command in an unprotected market.

### What exactly does a manager control when she controls organisation assets?

The coordinated productive apparatus of an enterprise considered as a resource: the plant, schedules, information systems, chains of command, and decision rights through which the labour of many people is combined, control of which allows its holder to direct production and appropriate a share of what coordination yields.

The architecture that followed from these premises had an elegance the first framework never achieved. Cross the ownership dimension with the two employee assets and a matrix of class locations appears, twelve in the canonical version, each cell defined by a combination of asset holdings rather than by occupational labels. At one corner stand owners with organisation and skill advantages, the classical bourgeoisie enriched by every asymmetry. At the opposite corner stand workers with neither asset, the proletariat in its purest form, sellers of labour power without credential protection and without authority. Between them the matrix spreads the familiar intermediate figures across distinct cells: expert managers combine skill and organisational control, non expert managers command without credentials, expert non managers hold credentials without command, and so on down through supervisors and semi credentialed workers. Every location in the matrix corresponds to a determinate exploitation relation, since each asset generates its own mechanism of appropriation, and the messy continuum of real jobs can be assigned to cells by asking three questions about property, authority, and credentials.

Wright drew attention to a consequence that gave the scheme much of its appeal. The matrix is not specific to capitalism in the way the first framework was. Different modes of production, on the asset account, are distinguished by which asset asymmetry dominates: feudalism by organisation and coercive assets bound to land and lordship, capitalism by means of production, and a hypothetical statist socialism by organisation assets alone, where control of the apparatus would make managers the exploiting class even with private property abolished. That generality let the scheme address a question the first framework handled awkwardly, namely the class character of the Soviet type societies, and it let Wright argue that abolishing private ownership while leaving organisational control untouched would replace one exploitation with another rather than ending exploitation as such. Whatever the verdict on the scheme, this was class theory operating at a level of systematic ambition that few contemporaries attempted, and it explains why the book became the reference point for empirical class analysis across several disciplines.

### How does the asset matrix handle a hospital consultant and a ward nurse?

It separates them by asset holdings rather than by workplace or income. The consultant holds scarce credentialed expertise that commands a rent and often exercises organisational authority over teams, placing the position among expert locations, while the nurse's position depends on the specific combination of credentials and supervisory authority the post carries.

The framework also generated testable implications with unusual directness, which was always the point for Wright. If skill assets generate exploitation rents, then credentialed employees should display material interests distinct both from unskilled workers and from owners, and their political behaviour should track the defence of credential barriers. If organisation assets matter independently, then authority within production should predict attitudes and interests even after income and credentials are held constant. The comparative survey project, already running when Classes appeared, could be retooled to measure assets rather than locations in the older sense, and much of Wright's empirical work through the nineteen nineties consisted of exactly that confrontation between the matrix and the data. The results, examined in a later section, were mixed in instructive ways, and Wright reported the mixture without varnish.

Yet even sympathetic readers noticed a change in what the word class was doing. In the first framework, class named relations between people inside production: commanding and obeying, appropriating and producing. In the second, class named positions derived from bundles of assets that individuals own, and an asset is something a person has, like a qualification or a job's decision rights, prior to and independently of any particular other person. The manager's organisation asset can be described without mentioning the workers she directs; the expert's skill asset can be described as a property of her curriculum vitae. Exploitation reattaches these possessions to other people, since a rent requires someone who pays it, but the descriptive order has reversed. Relations are instead inferred from distributions of holdings, whereas the classical theory derived holdings, incomes, and life chances from relations. That reversal, small as it may look in summary, is the hinge on which the strongest criticism of Wright's work turns, and the criticism must be given at full strength before any defence is weighed.

## The criticism at full strength: class dissolved into distribution

The objection, stated as its proponents stated it, runs as follows. Marxist class theory was formulated against the idea that society divides into strata by amount of wealth, income, or status. Its founding gesture was to relocate the division inside production, in the relation between those who own the conditions of work and those who must work under conditions owned by others. Exploitation, domination, and class interest all flow from that relation, and the theory's critical force depends on keeping the relation primary. A framework that defines class by ownership of assets, where the assets include credentials and organisational control, reinstates the distributive picture through the back door. Class becomes a matter of what one possesses, arrayed along dimensions of advantage, and the resulting map differs from Weberian stratification research in vocabulary more than in logic. The Weberian tradition, after all, had always analysed market capacities and the resources individuals bring to market exchange. Skill assets are market capacity under another name. On this objection, the second framework purchases empirical tractability, neat matrices, survey friendly variables, by surrendering the relational core that made the theory Marxist, and the surrender is disguised by retaining the word exploitation for what are in substance rents on scarce resources.

Rosemary Crompton pressed versions of this argument in Class and Stratification, her influential survey of the field, where Wright's asset scheme is treated as evidence of a convergence between Marxist class analysis and the stratification research it once opposed, a convergence she regarded as dissolving the distinctiveness of the Marxist concept. Stephen Edgell, in his study Class, raised the related worry that multiplying assets multiplies classes without a principle of closure, so the scheme risks describing every inequality as a class relation and thereby explaining none of them. Sociologists working in the rival tradition associated with John Goldthorpe offered a different but converging challenge. Goldthorpe's schema grounded class in employment relations, in the contrast between service relationships built on trust and delegated authority and labour contracts built on supervision and payment by effort, which is a relational foundation of a Weberian kind. From that standpoint, Wright's asset matrix could be faulted twice over: it abandoned relations where Goldthorpe kept them, and the survey tests run by Gordon Marshall and his colleagues in Social Class in Modern Britain found Wright's categories performing indifferently against Goldthorpe's when both were set against class identity and consciousness. The empirical and conceptual criticisms thus reinforced one another. The framework that claimed superior rigour appeared, to its critics, neither clearly relational in theory nor clearly superior in measurement.

### What is the criticism of the assets framework in its shortest form?

That it defines class by what people own rather than by their relations to other people in production, so that class becomes a distributional category of the kind Marxist theory was constructed to oppose, and exploitation becomes a name for rents on scarce assets rather than a relation of domination.

Wright's critics were not misreading him, and any honest account must register that he conceded as much about the framework's formulation. The language of assets invites the distributive reading because assets are grammatically possessions. His defence, developed across replies and revisions, fastened on organisation assets as the case where the distributive reading fails. Control of an organisation is not a thing a manager has in the way she has a diploma. It exists only as a relation to the people whose labour the organisation coordinates: strip away the coordinated workers and the asset evaporates, leaving neither rent nor authority behind. To control organisation assets just is to stand in a determinate relation of domination within production, and the exploitation it generates is appropriation through that domination, not a market rent on a possession. Skill assets, he acknowledged, sit less comfortably with the defence, since a credential does resemble a portable possession, and his later treatments increasingly described credentials through the closure they effect, the exclusion of the uncredentialed from positions, which is again a relation between insiders and outsiders rather than a substance owned. The defence therefore runs: read properly, the assets are crystallised relations, and the matrix is a device for mapping relational positions with distributive shorthand.

The verdict this article reaches, following the brief's own discipline, is that the defence succeeds for organisation assets and partly succeeds for credentials, while the framework's manner of statement continues to invite the objection and the invitation is itself a theoretical cost, since a generation of empirical work operationalised the shorthand and forgot the crystallisation. That judgement carries its defeater on its face. The judgement that the second framework is distributional rather than relational would be defeated by a demonstration that organisation assets are constituted by a relation of control over others' labour rather than by possession of a resource, which is the form the defence would have to take and which Wright's own formulations partly support. A reader who finds that demonstration complete in his texts should reverse the verdict: the framework would then stand as relational throughout, with an unfortunate vocabulary. A reader who finds the demonstration gestured at rather than completed will keep the verdict in its qualified form. Either way, the dispute is not a misunderstanding to be cleared up but a genuine ambiguity at the foundation of the most rigorous class scheme the Marxist tradition produced, and locating that ambiguity precisely is worth more than pretending it away.

### Why does the defeater matter for a theoretical verdict of this kind?

Because it states in advance what evidence or argument would reverse the judgement, converting an exchange of impressions into a checkable claim about the texts, so a critic who wishes to overturn the verdict knows exactly which demonstration must be produced and a defender knows exactly where the burden lies.

There is a further criticism, quieter than the distributive charge but damaging in a different register, which concerns the framework's handling of the classical proletariat itself. In the asset matrix, workers without credentials or authority occupy a single cell, yet their situations differ enormously by sector, security, and the organisation of their work, differences the first framework's attention to control of the labour process at least attempted to register. Defenders of the earlier scheme argued that the replacement traded descriptive richness for deductive elegance, and that the trade showed in the survey results, where the matrix's finer cells often failed to differentiate attitudes as sharply as its logic promised. Wright absorbed this criticism too, and his empirical writings of the nineteen nineties are full of local repairs: additional dimensions, interaction effects, qualifications about gender and household position that the matrix in its pure form could not accommodate. The picture that emerges is not of a theorist defending a system but of a theorist using a system hard enough to discover its limits in public, which is rarer and more valuable than consistency.

## The comparative class project: theory submitted to survey evidence

Nothing in Wright's career separates him from the Marxist mainstream more sharply than his decision to test class theory with questionnaires. The tradition's characteristic instruments were the historical monograph, the textual exegesis, and the political pamphlet. Survey research belonged to the sociology departments the tradition distrusted, with their variables, their samples, and their suspicion of grand concepts. Wright's wager was that the distrust ran in the wrong direction. A class concept that cannot survive operationalisation, that dissolves when a researcher tries to decide whether a foreman in Gothenburg or a technician in Toronto occupies this location or that one, has not been protected from vulgar empiricism. It has been protected from knowledge. Beginning in the late nineteen seventies he therefore organised what became known as the Comparative Project on Class Structure and Class Consciousness, an international collaboration that designed common survey instruments, administered them in country after country, and pooled the results so that class structures could be compared across different capitalist societies with the same measuring device.

### How was the comparative class project organised?

A collaborative survey programme directed by Wright from the late nineteen seventies onward, in which research teams in many countries administered shared instruments measuring class location, workplace authority, credentials, and attitudes, producing archived datasets that allowed the structure and consciousness of classes to be compared across national cases.

The project's design embodied the theory it tested, which is what made its results so uncomfortable when they arrived. Respondents were not asked the soft question of which class they felt they belonged to and then sorted by their answers. They were asked concrete questions about their jobs: whether they supervised others, whether they could hire or fire or influence pay, whether their work required certified qualifications, whether they owned a business or employed anyone, how much discretion they exercised over their own tasks. From those answers the researchers constructed class locations according to the framework's rules, first in the contradictory locations version and later in the asset version, and only then examined how the constructed locations related to income, attitudes, union membership, voting, and beliefs about inequality. The sequence mattered. Class was measured independently of the consciousness it was meant to explain, so the association between the two, where it appeared, counted as a finding rather than a definition, and where it failed to appear, the failure counted too.

The scale of the undertaking deserves emphasis because accessible coverage of Wright almost never mentions it. Coordinating comparable surveys across around twenty countries, in the collaboration as it was formally known, meant negotiating translations of concepts that do not travel cleanly, harmonising occupational classifications built for different administrative purposes, and persuading national teams to accept common questions in place of local favourites. The datasets generated by the project have been archived and remain in use in teaching and secondary analysis, long after the theoretical disputes that prompted them cooled. The project's capstone volume, Class Counts, published in nineteen ninety seven, presented the comparative results for the countries with the fullest data and drew the balance sheet of two decades of work. It is the largest empirical undertaking in the history of Marxist sociology, and its near absence from popular accounts of Marxism, which prefer manifestos to datasets, says more about the tradition's self image than about Wright's importance within it.

### Why did the survey results complicate the frameworks they were designed to test?

Because the associations between constructed class locations and measured attitudes, while real, were weaker and less orderly than the theory's logic suggested, with gender, sector, and national context repeatedly cutting across the class pattern, so the instrument built to vindicate the scheme instead mapped the limits of its explanatory reach.

Those complications repay attention, since they distinguish Wright's empiricism from the decorative kind. The surveys did find that class location, measured his way, predicted income and many attitudes better than chance and often better than simple occupational prestige scales. Managers and experts differed from unskilled workers in their views on inequality, on unions, on the legitimacy of managerial authority, in directions the theory anticipated. But the effects were modest beside the variation within locations, and some of the framework's finer distinctions failed to differentiate anything at all. The boundary between supervisors and the workers they supervised, so central to the contradictory locations scheme, turned out to matter less for consciousness than the scheme implied, while gender divided respondents inside every location with a consistency that class categories alone could not absorb. Wright's response, in Class Counts and in the collaborative volume The Debate on Classes that preceded it, was to publish the difficulties as findings about the theory, not as embarrassments about the data. Class structure constrained and biased consciousness, he concluded, without determining it in the strong form the classical tradition had imagined, and any future class analysis would have to build mediation, by households, by gender, by national institutions, into its core rather than appending it as a correction.

There is a methodological legacy here that outlasts the particular results. Wright demonstrated that a Marxist class schema could be specified tightly enough to be coded by researchers who did not share his politics, applied to respondents who had never heard of it, and compared against rival schemas on common evidence. Gordon Marshall's British team could test Wright against Goldthorpe precisely because Wright had made his categories public property in operational form. That openness converted class analysis from a contest of interpretations into something closer to a cumulative research field, with shared instruments, archived data, and disputes that evidence could actually move. Sociologists of stratification who rejected every premise of Marxism adopted the schema and the datasets as tools, because tools that measure authority, credentials, and ownership in a common format are useful to anyone studying inequality, whatever their theory of it. The contradictory locations concept travelled furthest of all, entering the general sociology of work as a way of describing positions that face two ways inside an organisation, often without attribution and sometimes without its author's name attached, which is the sincerest form of conceptual success.

### How well does the class schema perform against survey evidence?

In the limited sense its author eventually claimed for it. Locations constructed by the scheme are associated with income, authority, and patterned differences in attitudes across many national datasets, but the associations are moderate, vary by country and by dimension, and leave much of the variation in consciousness to factors the scheme does not contain.

The empirical career of the schema also clarifies a question students often bring to Wright: whether the two frameworks were successive approximations to a single truth or genuinely different theories sharing a vocabulary. The survey evidence suggests the second description. The contradictory locations scheme and the asset matrix do not merely code the same jobs with different labels. They disagree about which features of a job constitute its class character, control within production in the one case, holdings that generate rents in the other, and they consequently sort borderline occupations differently and predict different patterns of allegiance. A semi autonomous professional is, for the first scheme, defined by retained control over the labour process and pulled toward the petty bourgeois pole; for the second, the same person is an expert whose credential asset generates exploitation, pulled toward the privileged employee locations. Wright's willingness to let the second scheme displace the first in his own empirical practice, rather than harmonising them into a synthesis, is the clearest evidence that he regarded them as rivals. The rivalry is the point the namable claim of this article insists on: the two incompatible solutions are not two chapters of one theory but two theories, and the distance between them measures a real fork in what Marxist class analysis might be.

That fork can be stated cleanly. One path keeps class inside production, as a relation among people organised by the labour process, and accepts that such relations are hard to measure and resistant to elegant derivation. The other path rebuilds class from the distribution of productive assets, gains derivational elegance and measurement convenience, and risks drifting toward the stratification picture the tradition defined itself against. Wright walked the first path in his youth and the second in his maturity, and then, in his later work, did something more interesting than choosing between them. He questioned whether the paths were answers to the same question at all. That late reconsideration, often misreported as a softening or a retreat, is the third act of his class analysis and the one that completes the profile.

## Inside the first framework: control, autonomy, and the texture of jobs

The contradictory locations scheme rewards a slower reading than it usually receives, because its categories encode a theory of the workplace that later class analysis, including Wright's own, largely abandoned. The scheme's underlying intuition is that what makes a position capitalist or proletarian is not exhausted by the legal fact of ownership. Inside production, capital exercises a set of functions: it directs the labour of others, it controls the conditions under which work proceeds, it appropriates the product. Under developed capitalism those functions are delegated. Owners hire people to perform them, and the people hired thereby come to occupy fragments of the capitalist position while remaining employees. Wright's first framework is best understood as a map of that delegation. Each contradictory location marks a particular pattern of delegated and withheld functions, and the map's categories correspond to real organisational forms: the hierarchy of supervision, the enclave of professional autonomy, the small firm where owner and worker share a bench.

Consider supervision, the most numerous of the delegated functions. A front line supervisor in a warehouse or a call centre directs the pace and sequence of other people's work, reports upward on their performance, and may recommend discipline, yet typically cannot set budgets, hire at will, or alter the product. The first framework places such a figure in the contradictory location between bourgeoisie and proletariat, closer to the proletarian pole than a senior manager, and it predicts a characteristic political profile: identification with order and output when the supervisory function is salient, solidarity with fellow employees when pay, staffing, or the supervisor's own subordination comes to the fore. The prediction is subtle enough to be interesting and specific enough to be wrong, and the survey programme spent considerable effort discovering where it held. Wright reported that supervisory authority did structure attitudes, though less powerfully than the scheme's architecture suggested, and that the mere fact of supervising mattered less than the real decision rights bundled with the title, a finding that pushed his later operationalisations toward measuring actual authority rather than formal rank.

### How much authority makes a supervisor a contradictory location rather than a worker?

The framework answers in terms of function rather than title: a position leans toward the capitalist pole insofar as its holder genuinely controls the labour of others, sanctioning, directing, and evaluating them, and remains proletarian insofar as its holder is directed, evaluated, and disposable in turn, with most real supervisory posts mixing both in measurable proportions.

Semi autonomous employees posed the opposite configuration, and in some ways the more original one. Here the delegation runs the other way: capital retains ownership and appropriation but surrenders day to day control of the labour process to the worker, because the work, designing, diagnosing, teaching, researching, cannot be scripted without destroying its value. The university lecturer decides how to construct a course, the field engineer decides how to solve the fault, the journalist decides how to pursue the story, each within budgets and mandates set elsewhere. Wright located such positions between the proletariat and the petty bourgeoisie, since control over one's own labour process was the defining privilege of the independent producer, preserved here inside wage employment. The location explained, he argued, the distinctive politics of the professions: their defence of autonomy as a sacred principle, their ambivalence toward both managerial control and union discipline, their tendency to experience proletarianisation not as falling wages but as the arrival of audit, targets, and standardised procedure. That last insight has worn exceptionally well. The grievances of teachers, doctors, academics, and software specialists under managerial reform are overwhelmingly grievances about lost discretion, and Wright's early category names their structure precisely.

The small employer completed the original map, occupying the seam between bourgeoisie and petty bourgeoisie. Owning productive property and employing labour places the small employer within the capitalist relation, while working alongside employees, exposed to the same market storms and often to longer hours than any of them, preserves a kinship with independent production. Wright treated the category as genuinely contradictory rather than transitional, against the classical expectation that small employers were a vanishing remnant. Their persistence, and their distinctive politics of resentment toward both large capital and organised labour, fitted the framework's logic: a location pulled by opposed interests behaves in opposed ways, courting deregulation that frees the owner and protection that shelters the producer, sometimes within a single speech.

### Why do professionals experience managerial reform as proletarianisation?

Because the reform typically leaves salary and employment intact while stripping the discretion over method, pace, and standards that defined their location, so the loss registers in the dimension the first framework treats as constitutive, control of the labour process, even where pay scales barely move.

Beneath the three locations lay a theoretical commitment that the later Wright would qualify but never quite renounce: that class interests are generated by positions and can be read off them with sufficient care. The first framework's politics are architectural. Give it the floor plan of an organisation, the distribution of ownership, command, and autonomy across its posts, and it will sketch the lines along which alliances and antagonisms should form. The elegance of that promise explains the framework's influence on the sociology of organisations, where the idea of positions facing two ways became a standard descriptive resource. Its weakness was the obverse of its strength. Real consciousness is formed in households, neighbourhoods, nations, and histories as well as in floor plans, and the survey results kept returning that verdict in different national accents. The framework also faced a boundary problem it never solved to general satisfaction. Control and autonomy vary by degree, so every threshold in the scheme, how much supervision, how much discretion, could be accused of arbitrariness, and different thresholds sorted the same workforce into noticeably different class maps. Wright's answer was pragmatic: thresholds are instruments, to be judged by the patterns they reveal. His critics answered that an instrument whose settings change the object measured has told us about itself. The dispute was never resolved, and it formed part of the background pressure toward a framework whose categories could be derived from a mechanism rather than tuned by judgement, which is to say, toward assets.

There is one more feature of the first framework that hindsight makes poignant. It was built to explain the class structure of capitalism from within production, at a moment when the most dynamic sectors of employment were already shifting toward services, care, education, and information, where the labour process is interpersonal and its control is exercised through schedules, metrics, and professional norms rather than through foremen. The framework stretched to cover these worlds, sometimes brilliantly, as with the professions, sometimes awkwardly, as with routine service work that combines total absence of autonomy with none of the classical factory's collective organisation. Wright's later judgement was that the scheme described the industrial core of capitalism better than its expanding periphery. A theory of class for the whole economy needed categories that travelled across sectors without depending on the factory's particular architecture of command. Asset ownership travelled. A credential is a credential in a hospital, a ministry, or a software firm, and organisational authority can be recognised in each. Portability, as much as derivational elegance, recommended the second framework, and portability is also what made the second framework exportable into the survey instruments of many countries, where a scheme keyed to the details of production regimes would have fractured on contact with national difference.

## Inside the second framework: rents, closure, and the matrix examined

The asset framework deserves the same slow reading as its predecessor, because its cells contain arguments that summary treatments flatten. Begin with skill assets, the most controversial of the three. Wright's claim was not that skilled workers earn more, which no theory needs help to say, but that credentialed scarcity generates exploitation in a strict sense: the credential restricts the supply of a kind of labour below what an open market would provide, the restriction raises the price of that labour above the cost of producing it, and the difference is a transfer sustained by exclusion. Medical licensing, legal qualification, chartered status in engineering or accountancy: each operates as a fence with gatekeepers, and those inside the fence capture rents that those outside finance through higher prices and lower access. The mechanism deliberately echoes the analysis of property. Just as ownership of a factory excludes non owners from its use and thereby grounds a transfer, ownership of a credential excludes the uncredentialed from a practice and thereby grounds another. Whether the echo is an insight or an equivocation is precisely what the critics disputed, and Wright's own writing oscillates instructively, sometimes treating the credential as a possession analogous to capital, sometimes retreating to the language of closure, where what matters is the relation between the licensed and the excluded rather than any thing the licensed hold.

Organisation assets raise a parallel set of questions at larger scale. To say that managers exploit through control of organisation is to say that coordination itself produces a surplus and that those who command the coordination appropriate part of it. There is a genuine phenomenon here that any theory of the firm must face. Combining labour multiplies its product: a hundred workers coordinated in a plant produce more than the same hundred working separately, and the increment, the productivity of combination, has to be distributed by someone. In capitalist firms the distribution is settled by the hierarchy that performs the combination, and the hierarchy pays itself from the increment it administers. Wright's framework gives that settlement a class interpretation. Senior managers are not merely well paid employees; they are positioned at the valve through which the surplus of cooperation flows, and their material interest in preserving hierarchical coordination, as against either market anarchy or workers' control, follows from the position. The interpretation illuminates why managerial strata defend hierarchy as such, even in organisations, public services, cooperatives with managerial tiers, where private ownership is absent or remote. It also underwrites the framework's most provocative extension, the claim that a society abolishing private property while retaining command hierarchies would generate a class system on the organisational axis alone, with coordinators as its exploiting stratum.

### Can organisation assets exist without the workers they coordinate?

On Wright's own deeper account, no. The asset is constituted by the relation of command over combined labour, so that dissolving the workforce dissolves the asset with it, which is why defenders argue the framework remains relational at its core despite the distributive grammar of its presentation.

The matrix that results from crossing the assets has twelve locations in its standard presentation, and walking its cells clarifies both its power and its artificiality. Owners divide by scale into a bourgeoisie employing many, small employers employing few, and a petty bourgeoisie employing none. Non owners divide by their holdings on the two employee dimensions. Expert managers sit nearest the owners among employees, armed with credentials and command. Beneath them, expert supervisors and expert non managers hold skill without full organisational authority, while non expert managers hold authority without skill. Semi credentialed supervisors and workers occupy intermediate cells, and at the base stand non expert, non managerial workers, the location closest to the classical proletariat. Wright never claimed the twelve cells were twelve classes in the classical sense of collective actors. They were locations in an exploitation structure, and the empirical question was always which of the boundaries between them carried real differences in interest and behaviour. That modesty is sometimes lost in textbook presentations, which reproduce the matrix as a filing system. In Wright's hands it was a hypothesis generator: each boundary proposed a test, and the survey project existed to run the tests.

Two structural criticisms dogged the matrix from quarters friendlier than the distributive charge. The first concerned gender and the household. The scheme locates individuals by their own jobs, yet much of the population's material situation is mediated by households in which one partner's location subsidises or constrains the other's, and in which unpaid domestic labour underwrites every paid location. Feminist critics argued that an individualistic asset map systematically misdescribes women's class situations, assigning locations by jobs held intermittently or not at all while the real determinants of security and dependence sit in the household's combined position. Wright took the criticism seriously enough to devote sustained work to it, exploring mediated class locations, the class character of housework, and the way spouses' positions combine, without ever fully rebuilding the matrix around households. The episode displays his characteristic pattern: a criticism absorbed as a research problem, pursued empirically, and left as an acknowledged incompleteness rather than a refutation.

The second structural criticism concerned time. A location is a snapshot, yet lives move through locations: the trainee becomes the expert, the supervisor is promoted or demoted, the small employer fails into wage work. Critics asked whether class analysis should map positions or trajectories, and whether the political meaning of a location depends on how its holder arrived and where they expect to go. Wright's framework, like most class schemes, maps positions and treats mobility as a separate topic, but the survey data kept suggesting that expectations and biographies coloured the consciousness his locations were meant to explain. His later multidimensional turn can be read partly as a response to accumulations of this kind: not failures of the matrix on its own terms, but reminders that a map of exploitation locations is one instrument among several, answering one question well while neighbouring questions, about life chances, status, mobility, and identity, require instruments of their own.

### What does the asset framework say about a society that abolishes private ownership but keeps hierarchy?

That exploitation would persist on the organisational axis, with coordinators appropriating the surplus of combined labour through their command of the apparatus, so such a society would possess a class structure of its own rather than standing beyond class, a conclusion Wright drew explicitly for statist socialism.

Set against the first framework, the second's virtues and vices invert neatly. The first was relational to its core and impressionistic at its edges; the second is exact at its edges and contested at its core. The first explained ambivalence beautifully and measured badly; the second measures cleanly and explains rents better than allegiance. Wright knew the inversion as well as anyone, since he had built both, and his refusal in later years to crown either as the finished theory reads less like indecision than like a report from the frontier: each framework captures a stratum of the reality the other misses, and the reality is deep enough to resist both. That refusal matures, in the work of the two thousands, into the position examined next, where the rivalry between his own schemes becomes a special case of a general claim about what class theories are for.

## The rival he argued with first: Poulantzas and the new petty bourgeoisie

Wright's first framework was not written into a vacuum. It was written against the most influential Marxist treatment of intermediate strata then available, the account developed by Nicos Poulantzas in Classes in Contemporary Capitalism. Poulantzas had argued that class determination operates on economic, political, and ideological levels together, and that wage earners performing supervisory, mental, or unproductive labour belong not to the working class but to a new petty bourgeoisie, defined by the political and ideological relations of their work as much as by ownership. On that account, a supervisor and a professor and a commercial clerk share a class location despite utterly different relations to production, because each reproduces, in function or in outlook, the domination of capital. Wright's objection cut to the method. A definition that sorts by ideological and political effects, he argued, builds the conclusions into the classification: people are assigned to a class because of the politics they are then said to exhibit because of their class. The circle flatters the theory and empties it. His alternative, contradictory locations grounded strictly in relations of production, was designed to break the circle by defining locations independently of the consciousness they were meant to explain, so that the connection between the two could be discovered rather than assumed.

### How did Wright's first framework differ from the Poulantzas account of intermediate strata?

Poulantzas assigned supervisors, mental labourers, and similar wage earners to a new petty bourgeoisie on combined economic, political, and ideological criteria, whereas Wright defined locations by relations of production alone and treated the political tendencies of each location as an open question for evidence to settle.

The quarrel matters beyond its period because it fixed the methodological signature of everything Wright later did. Definitions first, stated so that classification does not smuggle in the explanandum; measurement second, with instruments published for rivals to use; conclusions last, revised when the instruments report against them. The survey project is that signature institutionalised. It also explains Wright's impatience with a style of Marxist writing, common in the period, in which class categories expanded or contracted to fit the political moral required. If a stratum behaved badly, it was reclassified as petty bourgeois; if it behaved well, it was reclaimed for the proletariat. Wright regarded such elasticity as the death of theory, since a scheme that can accommodate every outcome predicts none of them. His own schemes were built to be capable of embarrassment, and they were duly embarrassed, which he counted as progress.

There is an irony in the sequel that intellectual honesty requires recording. The asset framework of nineteen eighty five, whatever its other merits, moved Wright's categories away from the relations of production that he had defended against Poulantzas, toward holdings that an individual carries across contexts. A Poulantzasian reading the later work might observe that the wheel had turned: the defender of production relations against ideological classification classified by possessions instead, while the stratification tradition he had opposed looked on with recognition. Wright was aware of the irony and addressed it through the crystallised relations defence examined earlier, arguing that assets are relations in condensed form. Whether that answer satisfies is the live question the verdict of this article leaves open under its stated defeater. What cannot be disputed is that Wright, uniquely among the protagonists of those debates, supplied the evidence base on which his own claims could be tested and the conceptual candour to record where the tests went against him.

## What the surveys actually found: a closer balance sheet

The findings of the comparative project are usually summarised too coarsely, as either vindication or failure, when their real interest lies in their pattern of partial successes. Take income first. Class location in Wright's sense predicted earnings with a strength that surprised sceptics, and the asset version of the scheme performed particularly well: credentials and organisational authority each carried independent income effects, in country after country, roughly as the exploitation interpretation required. A framework that merely relabelled occupations would not have produced such regularities, and Wright was entitled to count the income results as evidence that his locations tracked real mechanisms of appropriation rather than statistical artefacts. The attitudinal results were more equivocal. Locations differed in their distributions of opinion on inequality, redistribution, workplace authority, and union legitimacy, and the ordering of locations across those opinions generally matched the theory's expectations, with expert managers nearest the owners and assetless workers furthest from them. Yet the differences were matters of shifted distributions with wide overlaps, not of distinct class cultures, and the proportion of variation explained remained modest throughout.

National comparison sharpened the lesson. The same location carried different implications in different institutional settings: Swedish workers and managers operated inside encompassing unions, centralised bargaining, and a large public sector that compressed the distances the American data displayed in full. Class structure, Wright concluded, sets the terrain on which consciousness forms, but national institutions decide how much of the terrain's slope survives into attitudes and action. That conclusion, obvious in retrospect, was a significant revision of the classical programme, which had expected class position to speak with roughly the same voice everywhere capitalism existed. It also redirected his later interests toward institutions and their alternatives, the line of work that culminated in the real utopias project, treated in its own article on [real utopias](/2020/04/15/real-utopias-explained/) and linked here only to mark the direction of travel.

### Which finding did Wright treat as the project's most important negative result?

That class location, however carefully measured, left most of the variation in class consciousness unexplained, so the strong classical expectation that position generates outlook had to be replaced by a weaker claim about constraint and bias mediated by institutions, households, and political organisation.

Gender cut across the results with a persistence that changed the project's agenda. Within every location, women and men differed in attitudes and in material trajectories, and the locations themselves were gendered in their occupancy, with organisational authority disproportionately held by men and semi credentialed care and service locations disproportionately held by women. A scheme that sorted individuals by jobs risked treating as class effects what were partly the effects of a gendered division of labour, and Wright's collaborative work of the nineteen nineties worried at the problem from several angles: the class location of married women, the significance of unpaid domestic work for the reproduction of labour power, the way household class composition shapes individual consciousness. No settled solution emerged, and Wright said so. The candour is itself part of the record this profile exists to convey. The most rigorous class analyst in the Marxist tradition ended his empirical career with a list of phenomena his categories illuminated and a second list, honestly maintained, of phenomena they did not.

The question of whether the working class, in anything like its classical form, persists through these transformations is pursued directly in the companion article asking [whether the working class still exists](/2011/11/01/does-the-working-class-still-exist/), which draws on Wright's data without duplicating the biographical and theoretical argument made here. His data support a nuanced answer: wage labour without assets or authority remains an enormous location in every country surveyed, its occupants share distinctive material interests, and their political expression varies with the institutions that organise or fail to organise them. Disappearance is not what the evidence shows. Transformation, mediation, and fragmentation are.

## The later position: different questions, several instruments

By the two thousands, Wright's writing on class had changed key. The programmatic statements of this period, including his contribution to the collaborative volume Approaches to Class Analysis in two thousand five and the synthesis offered in Understanding Class in twenty fifteen, no longer ask which single theory of class is correct. They ask what question each theory answers. The Marxist tradition, on this mature view, interrogates class as exploitation and domination: who appropriates the surplus whose labour produces, and through what relations of command. The Weberian tradition interrogates class as opportunity and market capacity: what resources individuals carry into exchange, and how those resources hoard advantages across generations. The gradational tradition of stratification research interrogates class as sorting: how attributes such as education, occupation, and income cluster into lifestyles and life chances. These are different questions, Wright argued, and the appearance of rivalry among their answers is an artefact of forcing three instruments onto one nail. A society can be described, without contradiction, as exploitative in the Marxist register, hoarding in the Weberian register, and finely graded in the stratification register, because each description answers to a different curiosity.

### In what direction did Wright's position move in his later work?

Substantially, yes, though by development rather than repudiation. The analyst who had sought a single correct map of class locations came to treat the Marxist, Weberian, and gradational approaches as instruments answering different questions, retaining exploitation as the Marxist question while surrendering the claim that it was the only question class analysis could ask.

The multidimensional account that followed from this pluralism kept exploitation at the centre of the Marxist register while ceasing to demand that one matrix exhaust the phenomenon of class. In his own terms, Wright moved from a class structural monism, the search for the location map, toward a framework in which class mechanisms form one family among the processes generating inequality, interacting with gender, race, citizenship, and household organisation in ways no single scheme should pretend to derive. Read ungenerously, this is the dissolution of a research programme into eclecticism. Read as Wright presented it, and as this article judges it, it is the programme's findings applied to itself. Four decades of measurement had shown exactly where the location maps bit and where they slid; the pluralist turn codified the biting and the sliding into a division of labour among theories. The evolution is honestly come by, and it is contested within the tradition, where some readers see the late Wright conceding to stratification research what the young Wright had won from it. Both readings can cite texts. The difference between them is whether one treats the early frameworks' imperial ambition, one map for the whole of class, as the essence of the project or as its youthful overstatement.

What survived the turn intact is worth listing, because summaries that report only the pluralism mislead. Exploitation remained, for the late Wright, a real relation and not a metaphor, and the critique of capitalism built on it remained the centre of his normative work. Class locations remained measurable and consequential, and he continued to defend his schema as the best available instrument for the exploitation question specifically. The survey datasets remained a standing resource, archived for other researchers to interrogate with questions he had not asked. And the methodological signature, define independently, measure openly, revise publicly, governed his writing to the end, visible in the posthumous work on anti capitalism where the same habits are applied to strategy rather than structure. What changed was the metaphysics of the enterprise: the expectation that class analysis would converge on a single correct cartography gave way to a picture of complementary instruments, each honest about its question.

### Is class distributional or relational in Wright's final view?

The question dissolves once the registers are separated. Within the Marxist register, class remains relational, a matter of exploitation and domination between positions; the distributional descriptions belong to the stratification register, which answers a different question and neither confirms nor refutes the relational account.

That dissolution has a consequence for the verdict delivered earlier on the asset framework, and intellectual tidiness requires stating it. If the mature Wright is right that distributional and relational accounts answer different questions, then the asset framework's offence can be redescribed: it was a distributional instrument built to answer a relational question, and its critics were correct about the mismatch while its defenders were correct that the instrument measured something real. The defeater stated earlier still governs the narrower textual judgement about organisation assets. But the later position reframes the whole dispute as a cautionary tale about matching instruments to questions, which is perhaps the most exportable lesson of Wright's career for students of any tradition. Theories fail most instructively not when they are refuted but when they are asked to do work they were never shaped for, and the history of his two frameworks exhibits both shapes and both workloads with unusual clarity.

## The assessment: two incompatible solutions and what they reveal

The namable claim of this profile can be stated in its full form. The two incompatible solutions are the centre of Wright's achievement because the distance between them is diagnostic. A thinker who answers the same question twice, on foundations that cannot both be right, and who publishes the evidence that embarrasses each answer in turn, teaches his readers more than a thinker who defends one answer consistently. The first solution locates class inside production, in relations of control and appropriation among people at work. The second locates it in the distribution of productive assets among individuals. The shift from the first to the second is the point at which his class analysis moved from a relational to a distributional basis, and that movement, enacted by the tradition's most rigorous empiricist under pressure from genuine difficulties, reveals what the tradition's commitments actually cost when they are made precise. Relational purity is expensive: it buys conceptual fidelity at the price of thresholds no one can derive and measurements no two researchers reproduce. Distributional clarity is expensive in the opposite currency: it buys derivation and measurement at the price of the founding distinction between a Marxist account of class and the stratification tradition it defined itself against. Wright paid both prices in sequence, in public, and itemised the receipts.

### What pattern do the two incompatible solutions trace across his career?

As a movement from relational fidelity with weak measurement toward distributional precision with a contested foundation, in which each solution solved the other's most obvious defect while acquiring a defect of its own, so the pair together map the trade off that any future Marxist class analysis must navigate consciously.

His standing within sociology rests on firmer ground than the internal dispute suggests. The class schema, in its asset version, became one of the standard instruments of comparative stratification research, used in national studies by scholars with no Marxist commitments who needed defensible measures of workplace authority, credentials, and ownership within one format. The datasets of the comparative project entered archives and teaching collections. The contradictory locations concept passed into the general vocabulary of organisational sociology. Wright himself accumulated the profession's honours, served as president of the American Sociological Association, and trained a generation of students who carried empirical class analysis into fields from education to health. A Marxist concept that achieves that kind of uptake has passed a test no internal debate can simulate: the test of usefulness to people who do not share its premises. By that test, whatever the verdict on foundations, Wright is the most consequential Marxist sociologist of his period, and the middle class problem he refused to evade remains the obligatory passage point for anyone rebuilding class theory after him.

The tradition's verdict is more divided, and the division is instructive. Admirers credit him with saving class analysis from scholasticism, giving it instruments, evidence, and a standard of candour that shames most theoretical writing. Detractors reply that the saving was a hollowing, that by the end of the trajectory the distinctively Marxist content, exploitation as a relation of domination rooted in production, survives as one register among others in a pluralist toolkit indistinguishable from good stratification sociology. This article's judgement sits between the camps, where the evidence sits. The late pluralism does dilute the early programme's ambition, and the dilution should be named rather than celebrated. But the dilution was purchased with knowledge: the knowledge, earned by measurement, of exactly how much of social inequality a class location map can explain and how much it cannot. A tradition that cannot absorb such knowledge without calling it betrayal has confused loyalty with learning. Wright's career stands as the counterexample, a demonstration that a Marxist can let evidence demote his own theory's claims and remain, in the demotion, recognisably and productively a Marxist analyst of class.

## A workforce walked through both frameworks

Abstract comparison sharpens when a single workplace is classified twice. Take a regional hospital, an employer whose staff list contains nearly every location either scheme distinguishes. At its head sits a chief executive employed by a trust or a corporation, owning no share of the enterprise yet commanding its budgets, appointments, and strategy. The first framework reads her position through delegated functions: she performs capital's work of direction and appropriation on behalf of owners or a state board, while remaining a salaried employee dismissible by that board, a contradictory location pressed close to the capitalist pole. The second framework reads the same woman through holdings: expert credentials from a career in management or medicine, combined with organisational assets of the highest order, place her in the matrix's most privileged employee cell. The descriptions agree in their verdict and differ in their reasons, and the difference matters when the analysis moves from labelling to prediction. The first framework predicts her allegiances from the functions she performs and the board that can remove her. The second predicts them from the rents her combined assets secure and her interest in defending the scarcity and hierarchy that produce them.

Move down the staff list and the schemes begin to separate cases they once agreed on. A consultant surgeon holds credentials of extreme scarcity, supervises juniors, and controls her own clinical practice to a degree few employees anywhere enjoy. For the first framework she is the semi autonomous employee par excellence, her location defined by retained command of her labour process and drawn toward the petty bourgeois pole by that autonomy. For the second she is an expert whose skill asset yields a rent, with organisational holdings that vary by whether she directs a department. A staff nurse with supervisory duties over a ward team sits elsewhere in each map: a contradictory location leaning proletarian in the first, since she directs labour while taking direction in turn; a semi credentialed supervisor in the second, her modest credential asset and limited authority combining into a middling cell. A hospital porter, a cleaner employed by an outside contractor, a clerk processing appointments: all three frameworks, classical, early Wright, and late Wright, converge on the assetless employee location for such posts, though the contractor's clerk raises the question of which organisation's relations define her position, a boundary problem global supply chains have made general.

### What is the first distinction a student should draw between relational and distributional accounts?

The object each takes as primary. A relational account begins from structured connections among positions, command, dependence, appropriation, and derives holdings and incomes from them, while a distributional account begins from what individuals hold and derives their positions from the pattern of holdings, so the two disagree about what explains what.

The hospital exercise exposes the practical stake in choosing between the schemes, which is the choice of what to measure when resources for research are finite. A study built on the first framework will spend its questionnaire on authority, discretion, and the texture of command, and will speak fluently about workplace politics while saying little that connects to earnings distributions. A study built on the second will spend its instrument on credentials, qualifications, and formal decision rights, will integrate smoothly with earnings data and mobility tables, and will speak less fluently about the lived experience of being managed. Wright ran both studies across his career, in sequence, and the comparative project archives contain the instruments of the second phase in their fullest form. Researchers designing class measures after him inherit the choice in a sharpened form, because his work demonstrates that the choice is not between rigour and vagueness but between two rigours aimed at different strata of the same reality.

There is a lesson in the exercise for readers who will never design a survey. When a politician, a manager, or a commentator invokes the middle class, the two frameworks supply two different cross examinations. The relational examination asks what the invoked group does in production: whom it directs, who directs it, what it controls, what it merely operates. The distributional examination asks what the group holds: which credentials fence its occupations, which assets cushion its members, which rents its scarcity extracts. Answers to the first set of questions predict allegiance and conflict. Answers to the second predict security, income, and the capacity to transmit advantage to children. Public argument about class is chronically confused because it mixes the two examinations without noticing, sliding from holdings to allegiance as if the one entailed the other. Wright's career, read as a whole, is the most sustained demonstration available that the entailment fails and that each examination must be conducted on its own terms before their results are combined.

## Exploitation across the frameworks: what the word carried

Because both frameworks claim to map exploitation, the word's changing load explains much of their incompatibility. In the first framework, exploitation names a process inside production: surplus labour performed by workers and appropriated by those who control the conditions of work, with contradictory locations participating in appropriation to the degree that they exercise capital's functions. The concept is processual and relational throughout, and its measurement, had Wright attempted it directly, would have required observing transfers within production itself. In the second framework, exploitation names a counterfactual about distribution: a group is exploited if its members would be better off withdrawing with a per capita share of the relevant asset, the game theoretic criterion Roemer supplied and Wright adapted. The concept becomes a property of asset distributions, testable in principle from data on holdings and incomes, and detached from any observation of the labour process. Critics fastened on the detachment. A rent on credentials, they argued, may be objectionable, may be unfair, may deserve abolition, but calling it exploitation stretches the word across phenomena whose mechanisms differ, and the stretching conceals the specific domination of the workplace that the classical concept existed to expose.

Wright's considered reply, developed over the nineteen nineties, distinguished the moral and causal strands of the concept. Exploitation in his usage joined three elements: the welfare of the appropriator depends on the effort of the appropriated, the appropriated are excluded from the asset that grounds the transfer, and the relation between the parties is one of domination, of power exercised within production or over access to it. Skill rents satisfied the first two elements straightforwardly and the third in the attenuated form of gatekeeping power, the capacity of credentialing bodies to exclude. On that analysis the credential case is exploitation of a weaker species, real but thinner than the factory case, and the framework's error, where it erred, was to present the species as uniform. The reply did not end the dispute, since opponents denied that gatekeeping constitutes domination in the relevant sense, but it relocated the argument onto precise ground: which of the three elements each asset satisfies, and with what strength. Precision of that kind is Wright's gift to his critics as much as to his followers. Arguments against his schemes tend to be better specified than arguments against vaguer theories, because his schemes give disagreement something definite to grip.

### Why did the second framework replace relations with assets as its foundation?

Because relations of control inside production resisted derivation and consistent measurement, whereas asset distributions could generate the class map from a principle and be operationalised in survey instruments, a gain in rigour and portability that Wright judged worth the conceptual risk his critics then identified.

The exploitation thread also connects Wright's class analysis to the ethical debates that analytical Marxism conducted in parallel, about whether exploitation is inherently unjust or unjust only under further conditions, and about the relation between exploitation and unfreedom. Wright's contribution to those debates was characteristically empirical in orientation. Where philosophers disputed the concept's coherence, he asked what followed for the map of a real society under each interpretation, and he treated the choice among interpretations as partly answerable by their fruits: an interpretation that sorts a workforce into locations whose interests and behaviour diverge predictably earns its keep, whatever its metaphysical discomforts. That pragmatism about concepts, scandalous to purists of several schools, is the deepest continuity across his changes of framework. The two incompatible solutions are incompatible as theories. As episodes in a single method, the method of building the sharpest instrument the problem allows and then reporting honestly what the instrument finds, they are perfectly consistent, and it is the method, more than either solution, that his students carried into the discipline.

## Neighbours and foils: Dahrendorf, Braverman, and the deskilling debate

Wright's first framework entered a field already occupied by rival answers to the same anomaly, and its distinctiveness shows best against two of them. Ralf Dahrendorf had proposed, a generation earlier, that class be relocated from ownership to authority: within what he called imperatively coordinated associations, factories, ministries, universities, hospitals, positions divide into those that command and those that obey, and class conflict is the permanent friction between the two. The proposal captures the manager's situation elegantly, since the manager commands below and obeys above, and Dahrendorf's scheme, like Wright's first, treats that doubleness as structural. The difference lies in the foundation. Dahrendorf severed class from property and production, making authority in any organisation the whole story, whereas Wright insisted that the authority of managers is delegated capital, borrowed from ownership and revocable by it, so that the manager's doubleness is a contradiction within the capital relation rather than a generic feature of hierarchy. The distinction predicts different things. Where Dahrendorf expects conflict to fragment by organisation, each association generating its own opposed camps, Wright expects the camps to align across organisations along the lines of ownership and its delegations, and the survey evidence on whether managers in different sectors share interests speaks, however imperfectly, between them.

Harry Braverman's Labor and Monopoly Capital, published in nineteen seventy four, pressed from the opposite direction. Braverman argued that the logic of capitalist management degrades work itself, separating conception from execution, transferring knowledge of the labour process from workers to management, and thereby proletarianising strata that appeared intermediate. The famous deskilling thesis implied that many apparently privileged employees were workers in transit, their autonomy a wasting asset. Wright's engagement with Braverman was respectful and exact. He accepted the tendency Braverman identified, the managerial drive to control, while denying that the tendency abolished the intermediate locations it pressed upon. A stratum being squeezed is not thereby shown to be identical with the stratum it is squeezed toward, and the semi autonomous employee losing discretion year by year still occupies, at each moment, a location whose interests differ from those of the fully scripted worker. The debate refined both positions. Braverman's readers gained a map of the positions his process moved people among; Wright's readers gained a dynamics, an account of how locations themselves are created and destroyed by the strategies of capital, which the static architecture of his schemes tended to omit.

## Wright among the analytical Marxists

The intellectual circle that formed around the September Group, the loose association of scholars who met to rebuild Marxist theory with the instruments of analytical philosophy and social science, gave Wright his closest interlocutors and his sharpest internal critics. Roemer's reformulation of exploitation supplied the second framework's mechanism. Jon Elster's demands for microfoundations, for explanations that pass through the actions and beliefs of individuals, pressed Wright to specify how locations generate interests and interests generate conduct, rather than invoking structural causation as a solvent. The exchanges were productive because the standards were shared: clarity about concepts, explicitness about mechanisms, willingness to abandon a formulation that could not meet them. Wright's distinctiveness within the circle was his empiricism. Where others modelled, he measured, and the comparative project can be read as the group's programme executed with a sociologist's tools, an attempt to discover whether the clarified concepts survived in the wild of survey data. The school's broader trajectory is charted in the series guide to analytical Marxism, linked in this article's opening sections, and the reader who follows it will find Wright's name at every stage, usually attached to the most concrete proposal on the table.

That concreteness had a cost the circle's debates exposed. Models reward closure: a mechanism stated completely, a matrix with every cell filled. Reality, as the surveys kept reporting, is untidy at exactly the points where the models close. Wright's response to the untidiness distinguishes him from both the modellers and the traditional theorists. He neither abandoned the models for narrative nor defended them against the data, but developed a practice of successive approximation in public: propose the sharpest scheme available, build the instrument that can hurt it, publish the wounds, and rebuild. The practice is visible in miniature in the migration from the first framework to the second, and at full scale in the migration from the second to the multidimensional position. It is also, not coincidentally, a fair description of how empirical science proceeds when it is healthy, which is why sociologists outside the Marxist tradition recognised Wright as one of their own even while disputing his premises. The tradition that formed him rarely worked that way. His career is an argument, conducted by example, that it could.

## Class consciousness: the variable the maps were built to explain

Every element of Wright's apparatus existed to serve one explanatory ambition: to connect the structure of class positions to the consciousness and conduct of the people occupying them. The classical tradition had treated that connection as nearly automatic, expecting position to ripen into outlook and outlook into organisation. Wright's generation inherited the wreckage of that expectation, in societies where affluent workers voted for parties of order, where managers staffed the professions of a labour movement's opponents and sometimes of the movement itself, and where the most class conscious electorates were often those of countries whose class structures differed least from their neighbours. His response was to decompose the connection into stages, each separately investigable. Location shapes interests, interests shape the raw material of consciousness, consciousness is formed through organisation, discourse, and struggle, and conduct follows from consciousness only through opportunities and constraints that institutions supply. A failure at any stage breaks the chain without falsifying the stages before it, which is why the survey findings of modest location effects did not, in his reading, abolish class analysis. They located its power correctly: at the start of a chain whose later links belong to politics and history.

This decomposition had a disciplining effect on debate. Arguments that class does not matter, mounted on evidence that workers' votes or attitudes diverge from classical expectation, could be answered precisely: the evidence tests the whole chain, while the theory of locations claims only the first link, and the interesting research concerns the mediations. Arguments from the other side, that class explains everything, faced the same discipline in reverse. Wright's own empirical estimates of the first link's strength, moderate and variable across countries, became the reference point against which both exaggerations could be measured. The concept of class formation, the process by which locations become organised collectivities, grew in his later writing to fill the space the decomposition opened, and it connects his class analysis to his final interests in institutions and alternatives: if consciousness and organisation are made rather than given, then the designs of unions, parties, workplaces, and welfare states are among the causes of what classes become, and redesigning them is a form of class politics itself.

### What mediates between class location and class consciousness in Wright's account?

Organisation, discourse, and institutional context intervene at every stage: interests must be interpreted, interpretations are supplied by parties, unions, media, and movements, and the opportunities to act on an interpretation depend on the surrounding political and industrial institutions, so identical locations can yield divergent consciousness in different settings.

The comparative design of his survey project was built for exactly this insight, since holding the instrument constant across countries converts national difference from noise into evidence about mediation. The Swedish and American results, persistently divergent in the strength of class patterning, taught the same lesson from the data that the decomposition taught from theory. Where encompassing unions and a labour party organised the interpretation of interests for most employees, locations spoke more coherently in attitudes and votes. Where organisation was fragmented and the discourse of class itself was weak, the same locations whispered. Wright drew the conclusion without flinching: the classical theory had mistaken a variable achievement of organisation for a law of structure. Class analysis survives the correction, but as a theory of potentials and constraints rather than of automatic outcomes, and any exam answer or research design that uses his schema while expecting automatic outcomes has missed the mature form of his position.

## Boundaries of the schema: race, citizenship, and the unwaged

A profile that only celebrated the schema's reach would fail its subject's standard of candour, and Wright himself catalogued the phenomena his locations handle worst. Race in the United States structures life chances, exposure to coercion, and political identity with a force that cross cuts every cell of the matrix, and the comparative project's American data registered the fact without the framework explaining it: two respondents in identical locations, divided by race, faced different labour markets, different states, and different histories. Wright treated such findings as marking the boundary of class analysis rather than its failure, consistent with his later pluralism: racial domination is a mechanism of inequality with its own logic, interacting with class mechanisms at every point but reducible to none of them. The same treatment extended to citizenship, where the line between the documented and the undocumented organises exploitation in ways no asset matrix drawn within one country's workforce can capture, since the decisive asset, the right to be present and to claim protection, is allocated by states rather than held in labour markets.

The unwaged posed the oldest boundary problem. Students, pensioners, carers, the unemployed, and those working without pay in households together constitute a large share of every adult population, and a schema keyed to jobs must either assign them mediated locations through households or leave them unclassified. Wright experimented with both solutions and reported the strengths of each with characteristic even handedness: direct location where a job exists, mediated location through a partner or parent where it does not, and an acknowledged residue where neither captures the reality, as with pensioners whose security derives from past locations and public entitlements combined. Textbooks that present the matrix as a complete sorting of society omit this residue, and with it the honesty that distinguishes the original. The schema maps the class structure of employment. Societies are larger than their employment structures, and the later Wright's multidimensionalism is partly the recognition, formalised, that the map's edges are real edges.

### Does the schema classify people outside paid employment?

Only indirectly and admittedly imperfectly, through mediated locations derived from household members in employment, an expedient Wright explored and criticised himself, since pensioners, carers, students, and the unemployed draw their material situations from entitlements, past positions, and household arrangements that no job based category fully captures.

These boundaries matter for the article's verdict because they locate the frameworks' failures correctly. The two incompatible solutions fail, where they fail, at different places: the first at measurement and derivation, the second at foundation and scope. Neither fails at the centre of its home ground. Contradictory locations still describe the doubleness of supervisory and professional work better than any rival vocabulary, and the asset matrix still predicts the income architecture of credentialed societies with an economy no rival has matched. A reader who understands why each succeeds at home and strains abroad possesses the thing Wright spent his career trying to transmit: not a doctrine of class, but a disciplined sense of what class concepts can and cannot do, earned from the record of the most ambitious attempt ever made to find out.

## The books in sequence: a reader's path through the frameworks

Wright's bibliography is unusually legible as an intellectual autobiography, because each major book marks a station in the argument this article has traced. Class Crisis and the State, his book of nineteen seventy eight, is the young theorist's book: polemical, architectural, confident that the contradictory locations scheme has solved the middle class problem by dissolving it into relations. Its chapters move from the critique of rival class theories through the construction of the location map to applications in the analysis of the state and of class struggle, and its energy is the energy of a framework that believes itself final. Reading it after its successor is a poignant exercise, since the confidence is everywhere shadowed by the operational questions the text raises and cannot answer: how much control, measured how, sorts this nurse from that one. Wright would spend the next decade building the machinery those questions demanded, and the machinery changed the theory it was built to serve.

Classes, in nineteen eighty five, is the mature theorist's book and the centre of the whole corpus. Its opening chapters dismantle the earlier framework's foundations with a frankness few authors direct at their own celebrated work, explaining why a concept of class built on bundles of control could not generate its categories from a principle and why exploitation, reformulated through Roemer's work, offered the missing generator. The asset theory is then constructed, applied to the comparison of modes of production, and turned on the statist societies whose class character the first framework had handled only by analogy. Whatever a reader's verdict on the construction, the book remains the most rigorous single statement of a Marxist class theory in the postwar period, and the criticism it attracted, examined in this article at full strength, is itself a tribute to the statement's clarity. Vague books are not refuted. Classes was refuted in detail, repeatedly, because it could be.

### Which of Wright's books should a new reader open first?

With Classes for the theory at its most rigorous and Class Counts for the evidence at its most complete, reading the earlier Class Crisis and the State afterward for the first framework in its original form, and Understanding Class for the late synthesis in which the rival approaches are assigned their separate questions.

Class Counts, in nineteen ninety seven, completes the trilogy of the schema with the balance sheet of the comparative project, and it is the book that most distinguishes Wright from every other theorist in this series. Its chapters report, country by country and dimension by dimension, what two decades of surveys found: the income effects that vindicated the asset locations, the attitudinal effects that vindicated them weakly, the gender and national complications that vindicated the critics' insistence on mediation. The book's tone is neither triumphant nor elegiac. It reads like the final report of an experiment whose director has decided that the results, all of them, are the property of the field. Interrogating Inequality, published in nineteen ninety four, gathers essays from the project's middle years, including his engagements with feminist criticism of the schema and with the problem of class and race, and it shows the rebuilding in progress. The Debate on Classes, the collaborative volume of nineteen eighty nine that Wright edited, stages the confrontation between his operationalisations and their critics, including the British tests, inside a single set of covers, and remains the best single entry point for a reader who wants the dispute rather than the doctrine.

The late work broadens the frame. Approaches to Class Analysis, the two thousand five volume he edited, places his neo Marxist scheme alongside Weberian, Durkheimian, and Bourdieusian alternatives and argues, in his own contribution, for the different questions settlement that Understanding Class would later present as his settled view. Envisioning Real Utopias, in twenty ten, turns from mapping class structure to designing institutions that might erode the dominations the map records, and belongs to the real utopias article linked earlier rather than to this profile's core. His posthumous short book on anti capitalism, published in twenty nineteen, compresses the strategic conclusions of a lifetime into a short valedictory whose clarity about class, about the limits of location maps for politics, and about the plurality of strategies a movement requires, confirms the trajectory this article has described: from the search for the one correct map toward a disciplined pluralism about instruments, joined to an undiluted conviction that exploitation is real and that naming it accurately is the beginning of opposing it.

## Teaching and using the schema: what the instruments are good for

The schema's afterlife in research and teaching follows the pattern its author predicted for good instruments: adoption by users who discard the packaging. In stratification research, Wright's operational questions, about supervisory authority, decision rights, credentials, and ownership, became standard items in national surveys, because they measure the anatomy of jobs more informatively than occupational titles alone. In the sociology of work, contradictory location survives as the standard description of first line management and of the professions under audit. In health research, class locations in his sense have been used to pattern exposure, autonomy, and mortality, since control over one's labour process, the variable his first framework made central, turns out to predict wellbeing as reliably as income. Each adoption vindicates a fragment of the programme while discarding its totalising ambition, which is exactly the fate the late Wright's pluralism describes and accepts.

For teachers, the material offers a rare pedagogical gift: a controversy in which every party's position can be stated in a paragraph and tested against a table. The middle class problem can be posed in the first ten minutes of a seminar from the students' own families, since most classrooms contain the children of managers, teachers, nurses, and small employers whose locations the classical map cannot place. The two frameworks can then be introduced as successive answers, the criticisms as the seminar's own work, and the survey findings as the verdict that complicates every side. Wright taught this way himself, by most accounts, treating his own theories as the seminar's specimens rather than its conclusions, and the textbooks that reproduce his matrix without its controversies betray the pedagogy even while spreading the vocabulary. The artifact table in this article is designed for that classroom use: five rows, three columns, and an argument in every cell.

## Class interests: the counterfactual machinery beneath the maps

Beneath both frameworks sits a piece of machinery that Wright refined across his career and that exam answers and research designs alike depend on: his account of class interests. A location, in his usage, does not mechanically secrete opinions. It defines a set of material interests, understood counterfactually: the occupants of a location have an interest in whatever would improve their material welfare, and the analyst must distinguish interests within the existing order from interests in transforming it. A manager has an interest, within capitalism, in the profitability of her firm and the scarcity of her skills, and simultaneously an interest, against the existing order, in a society where her security does not depend on either. The two interests coexist and conflict, which is why the same person can defend the firm against its workers on Monday and resent its owners on Friday without hypocrisy or confusion. Wright's formulation gave the concept of contradictory location its political precision: the contradiction is not in the person's head but in the interest structure the location generates, where advancement inside the system and abolition of the system point in opposite directions.

### How did Wright define the interests attached to a class location?

Counterfactually and materially, as the outcomes that would improve the welfare of a location's occupants, distinguished into interests pursued within the existing rules and interests realisable only by changing them, so that a single location could harbour opposed interests whose tension explains divided conduct.

The machinery did further work in his account of class compromise, developed in essays around the turn of the century. Where the classical tradition expected the interests of capital and labour to collide until one side prevailed, Wright asked under what conditions the collision settles into a positive compromise, an arrangement in which workers' organisations accept the prerogatives of ownership in exchange for arrangements that make workers better off when capital does well: coordinated bargaining, welfare provision, full employment policy, codetermination. The Swedish settlement of the postwar decades served as his standing example of a compromise that was positive for both sides for a generation, and its later unravelling served as evidence that compromises are historical constructions requiring institutional maintenance, not equilibria that persist by logic. The concept matters for this profile because it shows the class analyst at work on politics without abandoning the location maps: compromise is what happens when the opposed interests his machinery defines are organised, over decades, into institutions that blunt their opposition, and its breakdown is what happens when the organisation decays. The maps tell you where the pressure lies. History tells you what was built on the fault lines.

## The manager question in its longer history: Burnham and the managerial revolution

Wright's problem had a famous earlier claimant. James Burnham's The Managerial Revolution, published in nineteen forty one, argued that control was passing from owners to managers as a class in its own right, destined to rule the new order whether in corporate, fascist, or communist dress. The thesis haunted Marxist debate for decades, because it accepted the manager's importance while denying the ownership relation's primacy, and because the Soviet bureaucracy seemed to exhibit the type in power. Wright's two frameworks can be read as successive refusals of Burnham from opposite directions. The first framework refuses him relationally: managers are not a class but a location inside the capital relation, exercising delegated functions that owners can revoke, as waves of restructuring duly demonstrated whenever firms flattened their hierarchies and dismissed the supposed ruling stratum by the thousand. The second framework refuses him distributionally while conceding his observation a mechanism: organisation assets are real, coordinators do appropriate through command, and a society that abolished ownership while enthroning coordination would indeed risk a managerial class order, which is Burnham's insight restated as a conditional warning rather than a destiny. The statist societies tested the conditional. Wright's judgement, argued across the nineteen eighties, was that their bureaucracies did constitute an exploiting stratum on the organisational axis, a conclusion that placed him against those Marxists who defended those societies as workers' states and against Burnhamites who thought the outcome inevitable everywhere. Managers in capitalism, on his final account, remain employees with contradictory or asset privileged locations, their power borrowed and their rents real, a formula that accommodates both the executive suite and the redundancy notice.

### Why is the chief executive of a large corporation not, for Wright, simply a capitalist?

Because the position typically combines enormous organisation assets and credentials with an absence of ownership sufficient to command the enterprise independently, making its holder the most privileged of employees rather than a member of the bourgeoisie proper, unless shareholding converts the salary relationship into an ownership one.

That boundary, between the very top of the employee locations and the owning class, exposes a limitation of the survey method that Wright acknowledged and that critics of the empirical programme pressed hard. Ownership in its consequential form is concentrated in a stratum too small and too private to appear in general population surveys in analysable numbers. The bourgeoisie of the datasets is therefore composed overwhelmingly of small and middling employers, while the owners of great capital, whose locations the theory most cares about, are statistically invisible. Wright's response was twofold: to treat the survey as an instrument for the structure of employee and small property locations, where it is strong, and to supplement it with other methods, elite studies, wealth data, historical analysis, for the apex it cannot reach. The concession generalises into one of this article's recurring lessons. Every instrument in his career has a declared blind spot, and the declaration is part of the instrument's value. A class analysis that knows where its map fades into blank paper is more trustworthy than one whose cartography is uniformly confident.

## The changing shape of the structure: Wright's historical empirics

The comparative project compared nations; a parallel strand of Wright's work compared decades. Beginning with Class Structure and Income Determination in nineteen seventy nine, his first empirical book, he used American survey and census materials to ask how the location map itself had moved across the postwar period, and the answers disciplined the theory's expectations in both directions. The proletarian location, employees without assets, authority, or credentials, had not shrunk toward the vanishing point the polarisation thesis required, but neither had it grown into the overwhelming majority that thesis promised. It persisted as a large, slowly changing share of the workforce, while the growth concentrated exactly where his frameworks said to look: in the credentialed and supervisory locations, in the contradictory and asset privileged middle. The petty bourgeoisie of independent producers declined as the classical account expected, yet small employers persisted in niches the account had no reason to spare. Wright's historical verdict was characteristically double sided. The classical theory had correctly identified the solvent acting on independent production and incorrectly extrapolated that solvent to the whole structure, missing the countervailing process by which enlarged organisation manufactures intermediate positions as fast as competition dissolves them.

That countervailing process deserves a name in any summary of his thought, because it converts the middle class problem from an anomaly into a mechanism. Organisation itself generates the middle. Every increase in the scale and complexity of production multiplies the tasks of coordination, supervision, expertise, and administration, and each task is a potential location with interests of its own. Capitalism thus produces its intermediate strata by the same development that destroys the old ones, and the net size of the middle at any moment is the balance of the two processes, an empirical quantity rather than a theoretical fate. Wright's data suggested the balance had been roughly stable in the postwar United States, with composition churning beneath the stability: fewer independent producers, more credentialed employees; fewer foremen of the old type, more managers of systems and information. A theory that predicts the balance's direction in advance, he concluded, claims more than the mechanisms warrant, and the honest theory contents itself with identifying the processes and measuring their resultant. Students should note how far that conclusion stands from the classical prediction with which this article opened, and how completely it was earned rather than asserted: the distance between the two is the distance Wright's career travelled, instrument in hand.

### What happened to the size of the proletarian location in Wright's American data?

It persisted as a large share of the workforce without either vanishing or becoming the overwhelming majority, while growth concentrated in credentialed, supervisory, and managerial locations, a pattern that refuted the simple polarisation expectation in both its optimistic and pessimistic forms.

## Wright and Bourdieu: two theories of portable advantage

No comparison clarifies the asset framework's originality and its exposure better than the parallel with Pierre Bourdieu, whose forms of capital, economic, cultural, social, symbolic, offered European sociology a different machinery for portable advantage. The resemblance is real: Bourdieu's cultural capital, embodied in credentials and cultivated dispositions, does work similar to Wright's skill assets, and both theories explain why the children of the credentialed inherit advantage without inheriting property. The differences are equally real and more instructive. Bourdieu's capitals are resources in struggles within fields, valued differently in different arenas, and convertible into one another at rates set by each field's rules; his framework is a theory of practice and distinction, oriented to taste, honour, and the misrecognition that disguises advantage as merit. Wright's assets are defined within a theory of exploitation, valued by the transfers they sustain, and indifferent to taste except as it guards a fence. Where Bourdieu explains the professor's authority by the whole economy of prestige in which the university sits, Wright explains the professor's rent by the credential fence around the lecture hall and treats prestige as the fence's decoration. Wright's contribution to the two thousand five Approaches to Class Analysis volume sets the two side by side with unusual fairness, granting Bourdieu the phenomena of status and cultural reproduction that the asset scheme cannot reach while insisting that exploitation names a mechanism Bourdieu's vocabulary dissolves into the general play of capitals. The exchange is the later pluralism in action: not a surrender of the Marxist question, but a surveyed boundary between it and a neighbouring question that another instrument answers better.

### How do skill assets differ from Bourdieu's cultural capital?

Skill assets are defined by the exploitation transfer they sustain, the rent that credential scarcity extracts, whereas cultural capital operates across fields as a resource for distinction and advantage whose value shifts with each arena, so the concepts overlap on credentials while belonging to different theories with different explanatory targets.

## Method as message: Wright's philosophy of social science

Running beneath the frameworks is a philosophy of inquiry that Wright articulated in essays on analytical Marxism and on the philosophy of social science, and that students absorb from his practice even when they never read the methodological texts. Its elements are few and demanding. Concepts must be defined independently of the claims they will be used to test. Mechanisms must be specified at the level at which they operate, so that a structural claim can be translated, at least in principle, into the actions and constraints of situated people. Evidence must be gathered by instruments whose construction is public, so that rivals can replicate, extend, or break the findings. And conclusions must be revised in the open when measurement turns against them, with the revision treated as the programme's success rather than its disgrace. Wright called the stance, in various places, a commitment to Marxism as a social science rather than as a doctrine or a worldview, and he accepted the consequences: that some inherited claims would fail their tests, that the tradition's centre of gravity would shift toward the claims that survived, and that class analysis in particular would emerge smaller in its pretensions and larger in its credibility.

The stance explains the otherwise puzzling serenity with which he reported his own frameworks' difficulties. A doctrinal writer experiences a failed prediction as a wound. Wright's writings treat failed predictions as the purchase price of knowledge, and Class Counts, which reports more qualifications than triumphs, may be for that reason his most characteristic book. It also explains his influence on students far beyond his own department and tradition. The Wisconsin programme in class analysis trained researchers who dispersed across the discipline carrying the method with them, and the comparative project's national teams formed a second diaspora, so that the practice of operationalising class, publishing the instrument, and reporting the wounds became a recognisable school in stratification research. When this article's verdict weighs the two incompatible solutions and finds each partly successful at home and strained abroad, it is applying Wright's own standard to Wright, which is the only tribute he consistently asked of his readers.

## The state as employer: bureaucrats, teachers, and public locations

A large share of the intermediate population works for the state, and Wright's frameworks had to decide whether public employment changes a location's character. The question is harder than it looks. A state school teacher and a private school teacher perform similar labour under different relations to capital: neither school's surplus, in the public case, is appropriated by owners at all, since the service is funded from taxation and delivered outside the market. The first framework handles the case through function: the teacher's control over her classroom labour process places her among semi autonomous employees whatever the employer's legal form, while the ministry official directing a directorate occupies a contradictory location defined by delegated command. Wright supplemented the functional answer with an argument about the state's own class character, developed in his early work on state theory: public locations are shaped by the fiscal and political dependence of the state on the accumulation it does not itself organise, so the interests attached to them bend toward the health of the tax base and the legitimacy of the order that funds them. The asset framework handles the same cases through holdings, and in some ways more cleanly: the teacher's credential asset yields its rent in the public sector as in the private, and the official's organisation assets are, if anything, purer specimens, command over an apparatus whose product is administration itself.

The public sector cases also generated one of the empirical programme's most cited patterns. Public employees in the credentialed locations displayed, across several countries, attitudes measurably more favourable to collective provision and redistribution than their private sector counterparts in identical locations, a sector effect the location maps alone did not predict. Wright read the pattern as evidence for the mediation thesis that runs through his mature work: the institutional setting in which a location is embedded, the source of its funding, the mission of its organisation, the composition of its clientele, colours the consciousness the location makes available. A nurse in a public hospital and a nurse in a commercial clinic share a cell in the matrix and inhabit different moral economies, and the surveys registered the difference faithfully. Class analysis that stops at the cell boundary will miss it; class analysis that begins there, as Wright's did, at least knows what it is missing and can measure the size of the omission.

### Do public sector professionals occupy the same locations as their private sector counterparts?

In the asset framework, yes in structure and no in consequence: the credential and authority holdings that define the location are the same, while the institutional setting systematically colours the attitudes and interests the location generates, a sector effect Wright's comparative data documented and his mediation thesis explains.

## Professions and closure: the literature Wright absorbed

The skill asset concept did not emerge from Marxist sources alone. Wright built it in dialogue with the sociology of the professions, where Magali Sarfatti Larson's The Rise of Professionalism had analysed professionalisation as a collective mobility project: an occupation organises, standardises its training, wins state licensed monopoly over its practice, and converts scarcity into income and status. Eliot Freidson's work on professional dominance traced the same closure inside medicine, where the licensed profession secured not only a fence around practice but authority over the adjacent occupations inside it. Wright's contribution was to translate closure into the language of exploitation and class: the professional project, on his reading, is the acquisition of a skill asset, and its success explains why the most privileged employees in capitalist societies are often not managers at all but autonomous experts whose rents rival those of small capital. The translation cut both ways. It gave the professions literature a mechanism connecting closure to the distribution of the social surplus, and it gave Wright's framework its most empirically tractable asset, since licensure is a public fact that surveys can measure without subtlety.

Closure also supplied the framework with a dynamics it otherwise lacked, in the phenomenon of credential inflation. An asset whose value rests on scarcity invites its own erosion: as more people acquire the credential, the fence encloses a larger population and the rent per holder falls, unless the gatekeepers raise the fence by lengthening training or inventing higher tiers. Wright noted the parallel with the devaluation of any overproduced asset and drew the consequence for class structure: the boundary of the expert locations is not fixed by the matrix but contested continuously by aspirants below it and gatekeepers within, and the politics of admissions, accreditation, and professional examinations is, on his account, a form of class struggle conducted by other means. Teachers striking over class sizes, doctors resisting the dilution of medical training, engineers defending chartered status: the asset framework reads such episodes as defences of a class location's material basis, which illuminates their intensity without reducing their stated motives, care, standards, safety, to disguises. A location's occupants can sincerely believe the public justifications for closure and simultaneously benefit from its rents; Wright's machinery was built to hold both truths at once, and its capacity to do so is a large part of why researchers outside his tradition found it usable.

## Structure, formation, struggle: the triad behind the maps

Readers who meet Wright only through the location matrices miss the scaffolding his first book erected around them, a triad of levels that organised his thinking about class for the remainder of his career. Class structure names the map itself: the distribution of locations and the relations among them, existing whether or not anyone notices. Class formation names the process by which occupants of locations become organised collectivities with identities, associations, and capacities for action, a process that may happen thoroughly, partially, or not at all. Class struggle names the practices through which formed classes contend, whose outcomes feed back into the structure by altering laws, organisations, and the locations themselves. The triad's discipline is that no level can be read directly from another. A structure does not guarantee a formation, as the surveys demonstrated across countries; a formation does not guarantee success in struggle, as labour history demonstrates everywhere; and struggle, successful or not, rewrites the structure within which the next round begins. Much confusion in popular Marxist argument comes from collapsing the triad, inferring struggle from structure or structure from the noise of struggle, and Wright's earliest theoretical work is best read as a sustained refusal of the collapse.

The triad also clarifies what the survey project could and could not test. Questionnaires measure locations and, through attitude items, the raw materials of formation; they cannot observe struggle, and they observe formation itself only through proxies such as union membership and organisational affiliation. Wright's empirical claims were correspondingly modest in form: locations constrain the interests around which formation occurs, and the strength of that constraint varies with the institutional environment. Critics who charged the project with failing to find revolutionary consciousness in its data were demanding that a map of structure deliver the third level of the triad unaided, a demand Wright's own framework had ruled illegitimate in nineteen seventy eight. The episode is a small parable of his career: his earliest scaffolding contained the resources to answer his later critics, and the answers had to be excavated from his first book to be used in defence of his last dataset.

### What distinguishes class formation from class structure in Wright's triad?

Structure is the map of locations and their relations, existing independently of anyone's awareness, while formation is the contingent historical process by which a location's occupants acquire organisation, identity, and collective capacity, so the first can be surveyed while the second must be explained country by country and period by period.

## Successors and the field after Wright

The field Wright left behind did not converge on his schema, and the directions it took measure both his influence and the openness of the problems he worked on. John Goldthorpe's employment relations schema remained the workhorse of European mobility research, and the long Wright Goldthorpe comparison became a fixture of methods training: two rigorous schemes, one built from exploitation and assets, one from the governance of employment contracts, delivering overlapping but distinguishable maps whose empirical contest no single dataset has settled to everyone's satisfaction. David Grusky and his collaborators pressed from a different flank with the micro class critique, arguing that the action in stratification lies at the level of detailed occupations, where closure, licensing, and association actually operate, and that big class schemes, Wright's included, aggregate away the mechanisms they claim to capture. The micro class challenge is in one respect Wright's own credential argument radicalised against his matrix: if rents are generated occupation by occupation, perhaps the cells should be drawn at that grain. Wright's response, consistent with his pluralism, was to treat the grain question as empirical and the levels as complementary, big classes for the architecture of interests, micro classes for the machinery of closure.

The Great British Class Survey of twenty thirteen, associated with Mike Savage and colleagues, carried the Bourdieusian alternative to a mass audience, sorting the population by volumes of economic, cultural, and social capital into a seven fold map whose labels entered public discourse. Its popularity demonstrated the public appetite for class cartography that Wright's work had always assumed, while its method, clustering survey responses into types, demonstrated by contrast what his method refused: a map derived from the data's patterns rather than from a theory of the mechanisms generating them. Wright's maps begin from a claim about how exploitation or authority works and accept the risk of being wrong about the world; cluster maps begin from the world and risk having nothing to be wrong about. The contrast is not a dismissal of the Bourdieusian enterprise, whose questions about culture and taste are real, but a marker of the tradition Wright worked in and the standard he set for it. Theory first, stated sharply enough to fail, is the whole of his method in one clause, and the field's continuing argument among his schema, Goldthorpe's, Grusky's, and Savage's is the argument he would have wanted: about mechanisms, with evidence admitted.

## The schema abroad: informal work and the limits of the employment map

The comparative project surveyed the advanced capitalist world, and the schema it carried bears the marks of that world: formal employment, legible credentials, organisations with charts and titles. Much of the world's labour happens elsewhere, in informal economies where work is unwaged or casually waged, where credentials are absent or unenforced, where the employer may be a household, a contractor two links down a chain, or a platform that denies being an employer at all. Wright was aware that his instruments travelled imperfectly into such terrain, and the question of their reach is a fair test of the frameworks rather than an embarrassment to be concealed. The first framework travels by function and therefore copes surprisingly well with informality: a contractor who directs the labour of a gang while remaining dependent on a merchant's credit occupies a contradictory location recognisable in any century, and the street vendor with a pitch and a stock resembles the petty bourgeoisie more than any employee. The asset framework travels by holdings and copes where holdings are enforceable: a licence, a pitch right, a taxi medallion, a rationed permit can each function as a skill or organisation asset in miniature, generating rents through exclusion exactly as the theory describes. Where neither functions nor holdings are stable, where work is allocated by kinship, caste, or coercion, the schema's silence is honest: other mechanisms of domination are doing the work, and the later Wright's pluralism instructs the analyst to reach for other instruments rather than force the matrix onto material it cannot organise.

Platform mediated work offers a compact modern test that can be described structurally without statistics. A driver or courier engaged through an application owns some tools of the trade, typically a vehicle, yet does not control the organisation through which customers, prices, and assignments flow; the platform's coordinating apparatus, its dispatch logic, its ratings, its power to disconnect, belongs entirely to the firm. The first framework reads the position as a contradictory location with a distinctive twist: the worker retains the petty bourgeois shell of owning equipment and choosing hours while the labour process is directed, paced, and disciplined by an organisation asset held elsewhere, so the location leans proletarian in its relations while wearing the costume of independence. The asset framework reaches a similar verdict by its own route: vehicle ownership is too small and too depreciating to constitute a meaningful holding of productive assets against the platform's organisational monopoly, and the worker's lack of credentials or authority leaves the location near the matrix's base. That two incompatible frameworks converge on the case is itself informative. Where they converge, the analyst can be comparatively confident; where they diverge, as with the autonomous professional, the divergence marks the spot where theory choice decides the answer and where evidence about interests and behaviour must be summoned to arbitrate.

### What does the convergence of both frameworks on platform work show?

That the frameworks, for all their incompatibility at the foundation, share a common core judgement about positions stripped of assets, authority, and autonomy, so their disagreements are concentrated in the intermediate locations where the middle class problem lives rather than spread evenly across the structure.

## Unions across the locations: organisation as the test of the maps

Trade unions supplied Wright with a standing natural experiment, since union membership and militancy are behaviours that location theories predict and that surveys can count. The classical expectation was simple: the proletarian location organises, the intermediate locations vacillate, the privileged locations oppose. His data returned a more interesting pattern. Union density was often highest not at the matrix's base but among credentialed public employees, teachers, nurses, health technicians, whose locations the asset framework counts as privileged and whose organisations are among the most durable in the labour movement. Wright's interpretation ran through interests and capacity rather than through deprivation. Credentialed employees possess resources, organisational skills, stable employment, and a defensible collective asset, the credential itself, whose value union action can protect; their unionism is the defence of a location's rents conducted collectively, no less class based for being defensive. Assetless private sector workers, by contrast, face the highest costs of organisation, the most replaceable labour, and the most hostile employers, so their lower density records the difficulty of formation, in the triad's terms, rather than any absence of interest. The distinction between interest and formation, drawn in his first book, thus rescues the location maps from their most obvious empirical embarrassment and converts the embarrassment into a research programme about the conditions under which interests become organisations.

Managerial unionism posed the sharper puzzle, and Wright's treatment of it shows the contradictory locations concept earning its keep decades after its formal replacement. Supervisors and junior managers who join staff associations or unions are behaving, on a one dimensional map, against type. On the contradictory reading, they are acting on the employee side of their location, against the organisation that employs them, while continuing to act on its capital side against their own subordinates, and the doubleness is visible in the distinctive demands such organisations raise: protection against arbitrary treatment from above, coupled with insistence on the status distinctions that separate them from below. Wright's surveys found the attitudinal signature of that doubleness repeatedly, and his writings on class compromise extended it upward: the great settlements of the postwar period were possible partly because intermediate locations could be organised into them, their contradictory interests giving negotiators something to trade in both directions. A labour movement that understands the middle locations as terrain to be organised, rather than as enemies to be denounced or dupes to be pitied, is applying Wright whether it knows his name or not.

## Domination alongside exploitation: the late refinement

One refinement of the final period deserves separate notice, because it quietly repairs the asset framework's most criticised weakness from within. In his mature statements, Wright increasingly paired exploitation with domination as the twin mechanisms of the Marxist register. Exploitation names the transfer: the appropriation of surplus made possible by exclusion from assets. Domination names the power exercised inside the relation: the capacity of managers to command, of gatekeepers to exclude, of owners to close the plant. The pairing matters because it reintroduces, at the centre of the late theory, the relational content the asset language had seemed to shed. A rent extracted without any power over persons, as by a patent holder licensing a design to strangers, registers on the exploitation axis while barely registering on the domination axis. A supervisor's authority registers heavily on domination even where the transfer attributable to it is small. Class locations in the mature scheme are therefore read on both axes at once, and the reading recovers much of the first framework's texture, its attention to command inside production, without surrendering the second framework's derivational clarity about transfers. Wright never presented the pairing as a third framework, and this article follows him in treating it as a refinement, but a reader tracking the two incompatible solutions should register how much of the first solution the refinement smuggles back into the second's house.

### What does the domination axis add that exploitation alone misses?

The dimension of power exercised over people within the relation, as distinct from the transfer of surplus the relation sustains, so that positions can be compared not only by what they extract but by the command they wield and the subjection they impose in the daily conduct of work.

The pairing also sharpened Wright's handling of the statist societies in retrospect. A bureaucracy that commands without owning scores low on private exploitation through property and high on domination, with exploitation reappearing through the organisational asset once the pairing is applied. The mature vocabulary thus describes, in one register, what his two frameworks had described in two, and the convergence suggests the shape a unified account might have taken had he chosen to build it. He chose instead to leave the instruments plural and the questions distinct, a decision this article has treated as the honest codification of his findings. Readers who regret the choice have his own earlier books as the standing invitation to attempt the unification themselves, with the survey archive available to test whatever they build. Few theorists leave their successors so well equipped to prove them incomplete.

## Why the tradition needed an empiricist: Wright's example weighed whole

Step back from the frameworks and the career presents a single shape. A young theorist inherits a tradition whose central prediction about the class structure has failed in plain sight, and whose characteristic response to failure is reinterpretation. He refuses reinterpretation. He specifies the theory's core concept until it can be coded, builds an international instrument to code it, publishes the results that wound it, rebuilds the concept on a rival foundation when the wounds demand it, submits the rebuild to the same instrument, and ends by redrawing the map of what the concept can claim, in full view of the colleagues who watched each stage. At no point does he protect the tradition from the evidence, and at no point does he conclude from the evidence that the tradition's central intuition, that exploitation structures the interests of modern societies, is empty. The intuition survives his career reduced in scope and increased in credibility, which is what survival means for a research programme as opposed to a creed.

That shape explains the otherwise odd breadth of his readership. Marxists read him because he stayed inside the tradition's questions when leaving would have been easier and more rewarded. Sociologists read him because his instruments worked in their hands. Students read him because his texts state their claims where the claims can be found and their evidence where it can be checked. Critics read him because refuting him is possible, which is rarer praise than it sounds. The middle class problem that organised his life has not been solved by his two solutions, and this article's verdict has declined to pretend otherwise: the first solution is relational and underivable, the second derivable and arguably distributional, and the late pluralism is a principled armistice rather than a victory. But the problem has been transformed by his work from an embarrassment the tradition managed into a research field the discipline shares, with instruments held in common, data preserved in archives, and named points of disagreement that evidence can move. Transformations of that kind are what major figures in a science actually accomplish, and by that standard Erik Olin Wright stands among the major sociologists of his time, the Marxist tradition's own most demanding examiner.

## Class as location against class as happening: the Thompson contrast

The deepest methodological challenge to Wright's entire enterprise came not from stratification research but from within Marxist historiography, in the legacy of E. P. Thompson. Thompson had insisted that class is not a thing or a structure but a historical phenomenon, something that happens when people, out of shared experience, come to identify their interests against others; a class, on that view, cannot be read off a map of positions because it exists only in the making. The charge against any location scheme follows directly: to freeze the flow of history into cells is to mistake the analyst's filing system for the reality, and to treat as a structure what lives only as a process. Wright's triad of structure, formation, and struggle was his standing answer, since it grants Thompson the phenomenon, formation and struggle are indeed historical happenings, while denying that the happening floats free of the map. People make their classes, in Wright's paraphrase of the tradition's founding formula, but they make them from locations they did not choose, and the locations explain why the making takes the forms it does in one country and fails to occur in another. The survey project can then be read as an attempt to measure the unchosen half of the formula with the same seriousness Thompson brought to the chosen half.

### How does Wright answer the claim that class happens rather than exists as a structure?

By separating the levels: structure supplies the locations and interests from which class formation proceeds, formation and struggle are the historical happenings Thompson described, and the error lies in collapsing either level into the other, whether by reading struggle off the map or by denying the map constrains the struggle.

The contrast illuminates Wright's place in the tradition's geography. Thompson's Marxism is a historian's, suspicious of abstraction, loyal to experience and agency. Wright's is a sociologist's, loyal to abstraction precisely because abstraction, honestly handled, tells agency where it stands. The tradition is large enough to need both, and the mature Wright, with his insistence that locations constrain without determining, stands closer to a reconciliation than either camp's polemics allowed. His data even supply the reconciliation with its emblematic finding: the same locations, surveyed with the same instrument, yielded strong class patterning where history and organisation had done Thompson's work of making, and weak patterning where they had not. Structure proposed; history disposed; the instrument recorded both.

## The American case: exceptionalism measured

Wright's home case posed the tradition's oldest puzzle in its sharpest form. The United States combined the most developed capitalism with, by comparative survey evidence, among the weakest patterns of class consciousness and class based politics in the advanced world, the anomaly Werner Sombart had named at the start of the twentieth century. Wright's comparative design turned the puzzle from a rhetorical trope into a measurable quantity: American locations did differ from Swedish or Canadian ones in their attitudinal patterning, but the difference was one of degree along dimensions the instrument could specify, in the strength of association between location and outlook, in the polarisation between the most and least privileged cells, in the mediation supplied by unions and parties. The explanations his work supported were institutional and historical rather than cultural in any simple sense: the weakness of labour based political organisation, the racial division of the working population, the winner take all electoral system, and the sheer scale of the credentialed middle locations all registered in the data as mediations, not as mysteries. Exceptionalism, measured, became a set of coefficients requiring explanation rather than a national essence requiring deference, and that transformation is a fair specimen of what his method did to every grand claim it touched, including the claims of his own tradition and his own younger self.

His American empirics also documented a structural feature with consequences for the frameworks' rivalry. The United States exhibited, in his measurements, an unusually large small employer and petty bourgeois sector by the standards of comparable economies, alongside its famous managerial and professional strata, so the American middle was double: an old middle of property persisting beside the new middle of credentials and command. The first framework, with its locations defined around small property, described the old middle's doubleness well. The second, with its asset matrix, described the new middle's gradations better. The coexistence of the two middles in his home case may help explain why Wright, alone among the period's theorists, built both frameworks with equal conviction: his data kept showing him both structures at once, the nineteenth century's and the twentieth's, sharing a workforce and a politics. A theory of the American class structure that cannot hold both middles in view will misread its elections, its small business politics, and its professional strata in a single motion, and the paired frameworks, whatever their foundational quarrel, hold both in view between them.

## Exploitation without the labour theory of value: the quiet revolution inside the second framework

A change inside the second framework attracted less attention than the asset language and may matter more. Classical Marxist exploitation is defined through labour values: the worker sells labour power at its value and produces more value than that wage embodies, the difference constituting surplus value. Roemer's reformulation, which Wright adopted, severed exploitation from that apparatus entirely. On the property relations definition, a group is exploited if it would do better by withdrawing from the economy with its per capita share of society's productive assets, and exploiters are those who would do worse by such a withdrawal. Exploitation becomes a judgement about the distribution of assets and its consequences for welfare, requiring no theory of value, no measurement of embodied labour, and no claim that a transfer of labour time occurs inside production. Wright's endorsement of the severance was deliberate and argued: he held that the moral and explanatory force of the exploitation concept never depended on the labour theory's accounting, and that detaching the two freed class analysis from a doctrine its defenders could not sustain and its critics could not stop attacking.

### What changes when exploitation is detached from the labour theory of value?

The concept's foundation shifts from an accounting of labour time transferred in production to a counterfactual about asset distribution, so exploitation can be identified wherever unequal holdings generate opposed material welfare, at the cost of the production centred specificity the classical definition carried.

Traditionalists heard the severance as surrender, and their objection belongs in any full strength account. If exploitation means no more than disadvantage under an unequal distribution, then the word adds rhetoric to a judgement egalitarians of many schools already make, and the specific Marxist claim, that the wage relation itself, fairly contracted and fully paid, contains the transfer, evaporates. Wright's reply distinguished the concept's core from its classical packaging. The core, he argued, is the triad examined earlier in this article: opposed material welfare, exclusion from productive assets, and appropriation sustained by domination within an ongoing relation. The labour theory was one attempt to measure the transfer the triad describes, and a failed measurement does not acquit the phenomenon. Whether the reply succeeds is entangled with the verdict on the asset framework as a whole, since both turn on whether the relational triad or the distributive criterion is taken as the concept's essence. The defeater discipline applies here as well: a demonstration that the withdrawal criterion and the triad always coincide would dissolve the objection, while a case where they diverge, a group disadvantaged by distribution yet dominating no one and dominated by no one, would force the choice the critics demand. Wright's texts contain materials for both demonstrations, which is why the argument about his legacy continues among people who agree on his importance.

## Can class be surveyed at all: the operationalisation dispute

A methodological objection shadowed the comparative project from its first questionnaire, pressed by scholars who held that class, being a relation and a process, cannot be captured by asking individuals about their jobs and attitudes. A relation, the objection runs, exists between people, not inside a respondent; a process unfolds over time, not in a cross section; and the survey's method, aggregating individual attributes, therefore manufactures a simulacrum of class from the very atomism the theory rejects. Wright's answer was pragmatic and, characteristically, partly concessive. A survey does not photograph a relation; it samples the occupants of locations defined by relations, and infers the relation's effects from the patterned differences among the samples. Cross sections freeze processes, which is why the project repeated its instruments across years and countries, building the comparisons from which process could be inferred. The objection correctly identifies what the instrument cannot do, and the project's publications are careful, at their best, to claim only what survives the identification. Where the care lapsed, in secondary uses of the datasets by researchers who treated location scores as class itself, the objection found its target, and Wright's own cautions in the methodological appendices of Class Counts read as attempts to recall his instrument from its enthusiasts.

There is a broader lesson here for readers of this series, since the same dispute recurs wherever a theoretical concept meets a measuring device. The choice is never between pure theory and pure measurement. It is between concepts that have survived an attempt at measurement, with the scars itemised, and concepts that have been kept safe from the attempt. Wright's career is the standing brief for the first choice, and the comparative project's archive, instruments, codebooks, and datasets preserved for other hands, is its material legacy. A researcher who doubts his locations can use his questions to build better ones, which is precisely the cumulative structure his tradition had lacked and his method supplied.

## The new class question: Djilas and the statist test of the asset framework

The asset framework's most dramatic application was to societies its author opposed and its tradition had often defended. Milovan Djilas, the Yugoslav dissident, had argued in The New Class in nineteen fifty seven that the communist bureaucracy constituted a new owning class in substance, since its collective control over nationalised property delivered the privileges of ownership without its legal form. Defenders of those societies replied that control without title is administration, not class, and that a stratum which cannot bequeath its position or sell its assets lacks the defining marks of a propertied class. Wright's second framework cut through the exchange by relocating the criterion. If organisation assets generate exploitation wherever they are monopolised, then a bureaucracy monopolising the entire organisational apparatus of an economy stands in an exploiting relation to the workforce it coordinates, regardless of the property law in force, and the absence of titles and inheritance marks a difference in the form of the class relation rather than its absence. The conclusion aligned Wright with Djilas's substance while rejecting his imprecision, and it cost Wright standing among Marxists who regarded the conclusion as a concession to cold war polemic. He published it anyway, with the reasoning exposed, which is the pattern this profile has had repeated occasion to note.

### How does the asset framework classify the bureaucracy of a nationalised economy?

As a stratum exploiting through the organisational axis, since its monopoly of the apparatus through which all production is coordinated lets it appropriate a share of the social surplus and command the labour of the workforce, a class relation in Wright's sense despite the absence of private titles.

The statist application also displays the framework's generality as a double edged property. A scheme that finds class in every coordinated hierarchy risks finding it everywhere and therefore nowhere in particular, the closure objection Edgell raised in the capitalist context returning at civilisational scale. Wright's guard against the slide was the exploitation triad: a hierarchy counts as a class relation only where command is joined to appropriation and exclusion, where the coordinators' welfare depends inversely on the coordinated and the coordinated are barred from the coordinating asset. Mere administration, a railway timetable office with no power to appropriate or exclude, fails the test. Whether the guard holds in every application is disputable, and was disputed, but its presence shows the framework resisting its own imperialism from inside, a quality rarer in class theories than the polemics of the period suggest.

## Women in the class structure: the feminist engagement

Wright's most searching internal critics included the feminist sociologists who asked, beginning in the late nineteen seventies, whose locations his maps were mapping. The convention of the period assigned married women the class of their husbands, a practice that made women's own employment analytically invisible and treated the household as a single class unit with a male head. Wright's essay on women in the class structure, published in nineteen eighty nine, subjected the convention to his characteristic treatment: he distinguished the questions it might answer, tested the alternatives against survey data on women's attitudes and interests, and concluded that women's own locations carried independent explanatory weight that the convention discarded, while household position mediated those locations in ways a purely individual scheme would miss. The conclusion satisfied neither the convention's defenders nor the critics who wanted the household itself theorised as a site of exploitation between spouses, a position Wright explored through the concept of mediated and direct locations without fully adopting. His treatment of housework followed the same pattern: unpaid domestic labour reproduces labour power and thereby enters the circuit of class relations, yet assigning it a location in a matrix built for employment strained the instrument past its design.

The engagement matters to this profile for two reasons beyond its content. It demonstrates the range of phenomena Wright allowed to count as tests of his frameworks, including phenomena his tradition had marginalised and his instruments handled badly. And it supplied some of the strongest pressure toward his eventual pluralism, since the feminist critique persuaded him, over two decades of exchange, that class mechanisms interlock with gender mechanisms without either reducing to the other, a sentence that reads as obvious in his late work and was hard won in his middle work. Readers tracing the two incompatible solutions should therefore resist treating the later multidimensional position as a merely taxonomic preference. It was the sediment of specific defeats, on gender above all, where the single map's inadequacy was demonstrated to its author's satisfaction by critics he respected and data he had gathered himself.

## Are teachers working class: the question both frameworks were built to answer

No occupational case tests Wright's schemes in public argument more often than the teacher, and the case deserves a full working because it shows the frameworks operating as instruments rather than slogans. The classical map falters immediately: the teacher owns no productive property and sells labour power, which says proletarian, yet exercises authority over a room of the young, controls the conduct of her own lessons to a degree few factory workers approach, and holds a credential that fences her occupation, which says something else. The first framework resolves the case through the labour process. Insofar as the teacher retains genuine control over curriculum, method, and assessment, she occupies the semi autonomous location between proletariat and petty bourgeoisie, and her characteristic politics, defence of professional discretion against both managerial audit and market discipline, follows from the location. Insofar as scripted curricula, standardised testing regimes, and performance management strip that control away, the location slides toward the proletarian pole, and the framework predicts the observed transformation of teacher unionism from professional association toward industrial militancy as the slide proceeds. The prediction is the framework's home ground: a change in control registering as a change in class character and conduct.

The asset framework resolves the same case through holdings and reaches a partially different emphasis. The teacher's credential is a skill asset yielding a modest rent, visible in the differential between her pay and that of uncredentialed care or service workers with similar hours and burdens. Her organisational assets are slight, confined to the classroom, unless she ascends into management, whereupon the matrix moves her toward the expert manager cell and predicts the familiar estrangement between school leadership and staffroom that the first framework would describe in the language of delegated functions. Where the frameworks agree is in refusing both easy answers to the title question. The teacher is not straightforwardly working class in the classical sense, because credential and autonomy are real material facts with real interest consequences. The teacher is not straightforwardly middle class in the gradational sense either, because her security depends on a salary, her asset cannot be sold or bequeathed, and her location's privileges are revocable by policy in a way that property is not. Wright's schemes replace the forced choice with a specification, and the specification, not any label, is what a student should carry into an examination and a journalist into an interview.

### What happens to a credential asset when governments expand the profession rapidly?

Its scarcity erodes and the premium each holder commands falls unless gatekeepers raise entry requirements or stratify the credential into tiers, which is why expansions of teacher or nurse training are so often accompanied by new grades and titles defending the differential the expansion threatens.

## The death of class debate: Wright against the obituarists

Late in the century a fashionable literature announced that class itself was dying: that affluence, education, and the decline of heavy industry had dissolved the old locations and their politics, leaving a society of individuals whose votes and identities owed nothing to production. Terry Nichols Clark and Seymour Martin Lipset gave the thesis its canonical statement, arguing that the class based stratification of industrial society was giving way to more fluid and fragmented patterns. Wright's response, mounted with the comparative data at his back, was a model of his method. He granted the phenomena: class voting had declined in several countries, the manual working class had shrunk as a share of electorates, and the crude correlations of the postwar decades had weakened. He denied the inference. Declining correlation between location and vote can reflect the transformation of class structure, the growth of the intermediate locations whose politics are cross pressured by design, the weakening of the organisations that once converted location into allegiance, or the strategies of parties that stopped addressing class interests, and each alternative is a claim about class operating differently, not about class ceasing to operate. The obituary, he argued, confused the death of a particular class formation, the industrial formation of the postwar settlement, with the death of class structure itself, collapsing the triad his first book had separated. Subsequent research has largely sustained the distinction: class locations continue to pattern income, security, health, and much of politics, while the translation of location into party allegiance varies with the mediations his framework always insisted on. The episode earns its place because it shows the empirical programme doing defensive work for the tradition at the moment of its greatest intellectual vulnerability, armed with exactly the evidence the obituarists lacked.

## Mobility through the locations: what the maps say about movement

Class schemes are often judged by the mobility stories they permit, and Wright's locations frame a distinctive account of movement between generations and across careers. In a gradational picture, mobility is a journey up or down a ladder of advantage, and its fairness is measured by how open the ladder is. In Wright's picture, movement is a change of location, a crossing of boundaries that differ in kind: the boundary between employee and owner, guarded by capital; the boundary between the uncredentialed and the credentialed, guarded by educational gatekeeping; the boundary between the directed and those who direct, guarded by organisational promotion. Each boundary has its own permeability and its own politics. A society can open the credential boundary widely, through mass higher education, while the ownership boundary stays nearly sealed, producing the characteristic modern pattern in which movement is real, common, and confined within the employee locations, a pattern both frameworks describe and the asset framework quantifies through the income effects of each crossing. Wright's analyses of mobility data pressed the further point that the experience of a location depends partly on the trajectory through it: the manager promoted from the bench and the manager recruited from an elite programme occupy the same cell with different allegiances, a finding his later work absorbed into the general doctrine that locations constrain biographies without exhausting them.

### Which boundary in Wright's map is hardest to cross, and why?

The ownership boundary, because crossing it requires command of capital rather than of credentials or favour, and capital, unlike a qualification, can be accumulated only from a surplus the employee locations rarely generate, so mobility concentrates within the employee structure while the line between employees and owners stays comparatively sealed.

## Delayering and the fate of the middle manager

The corporate restructurings of the nineteen eighties and nineties, which stripped out layers of supervision and middle management across the advanced economies, supplied an unintended test of the first framework's central claim about delegation. If managers were a ruling class in embryo, as the managerial revolution thesis held, their mass dismissal by owners would be inexplicable. If they were occupants of contradictory locations exercising revocable delegated functions, the dismissals were the theory's prediction made visible: when owners judge the delegation too costly or too slow, they withdraw it, and the location's occupants discover which side of their contradiction holds their livelihood. Wright's framework also explained the survivors' experience with uncomfortable precision. Delayering widened spans of control, pushing remaining managers toward longer hours and more direct discipline of larger teams, while simultaneously subjecting those managers to tighter direction from above through targets and information systems: the location moved, in his terms, toward both poles at once, more capitalist in its functions over others, more proletarian in its subjection. The attitudinal signature of that double movement, identification with the firm joined to resentment of its demands, is the contradictory location's fingerprint, and its reappearance in the restructuring literature, often without citation, marks another quiet victory of the first framework's vocabulary over the second's in the sociology of organisations.

The episode carries a general moral about the two solutions that this article's assessment has circled. The asset framework describes the middle manager's situation in the currency of holdings and predicts income and security tolerably well. The contradictory locations framework describes the same person's situation in the currency of functions and predicts experience and allegiance, the texture of being squeezed from both directions, better than any rival. Which description a researcher needs depends on the question being asked, and the dependence is not a defect in either framework but the mature Wright's whole point about instruments and questions, arrived at here from the evidence of a single occupation's bad decades.

## Carchedi and the parallel invention of contradictory functions

Wright was not the only theorist working the seam between capital and labour in the nineteen seventies, and his generous acknowledgements of Guglielmo Carchedi mark a parallel worth recording. Carchedi, an Italian economist working in Britain, developed an account of the collective worker and of positions that perform the function of capital while remaining wage labour, arriving independently at a structure of thought close to contradictory locations. The parallel invention matters for assessing the first framework's originality. The idea was, in the period's sense, in the air: wherever Marxists confronted managerial and technical labour with the classical categories, the notion of positions performing opposed functions forced itself forward. Wright's distinctiveness lay not in patenting the notion but in what he did with it: systematising it into a complete location map, defending its methodological basis against Poulantzas, and above all building the survey machinery to test whether the locations it defined corresponded to anything in the distribution of interests and attitudes. Ideas in social theory are cheap at the moment of first statement and expensive at the moment of measurement. Wright paid the expensive part, for his own ideas and, through the comparative project's open instruments, for everyone else's as well.

## Two national portraits: what comparison did to the theory

The comparative volumes reward reading as national portraits, and two of them state the project's findings in their purest form. The Swedish data showed a class structure recognisably similar in outline to the American, the same spread of locations in roughly comparable proportions, inhabited by a politics of an entirely different temperature: stronger unions, more coherent class patterning of attitudes, and a settlement between the classes institutionalised over generations. The American data showed the same architecture with the mediations stripped away or weakened, locations predicting less, allegiances fragmenting along race and region and religion. Placed side by side, the portraits refute two opposite simplicities at once. Against the claim that class structure determines politics, Sweden and the United States share too much structure and differ too much in politics. Against the claim that class is an illusion dispelled by affluence or individualism, the locations in both countries pattern income, security, and outlook with a regularity no illusion could sustain. What survives the comparison is Wright's mature formula: structure constrains, institutions mediate, organisation decides how much of the constraint becomes visible as collective action. A formula of that kind cannot be printed on a banner, and Wright never tried. It can be printed in a textbook, and it has been, which is the appropriate afterlife for the largest empirical programme Marxist sociology has produced.

### What did the Swedish American comparison establish about class and politics?

That near similar distributions of class locations can sustain very different levels of class consciousness and class politics, with the difference tracing to unions, parties, and welfare institutions, so structure sets limits within which organisation and history determine the outcome.

Canada and Norway, the other full cases in Class Counts, filled in the gradient between the poles: Canadian patterning close to the American with somewhat stronger organisation, Norwegian patterning approaching the Swedish under the shelter of encompassing institutions and oil cushioned public employment. The gradient itself became the finding. Class consciousness in Wright's data behaves less like a property of locations and more like a product of locations multiplied by institutions, and the multiplication sign is his career's most consequential contribution to the theory of class, worth more than either framework's matrix because it tells every future researcher where the unexplained variance lives: not inside the cells, but in the space between the map and the polity.

## Wright as a reader of Marx: reconstruction rather than exegesis

A peculiarity of Wright's work that students should register early is its distance from Marx's texts. The tradition's scholarship often proceeds by exegesis, establishing what Marx wrote and letting the authority of the text arbitrate disputes. Wright proceeds by reconstruction. He takes the tradition's central concepts, class, exploitation, the state, and asks what they would have to mean to function in a contemporary social science, revising or discarding textual commitments, the labour theory of value most conspicuously, when they obstruct that functioning. The method scandalised exegetes, who charged that the reconstruction kept the vocabulary while changing the subject. Wright's defence was that a living theory is identified by its problems and its explanatory ambitions, not by the letter of its founding documents, and that Marx himself, confronting the survey archive or the credential economy, would have revised with the same freedom. The defence cannot be settled by citation, which is its point. It can be assessed by fruits, and the fruits are the subject of this article: two frameworks of genuine rigour, an archive of evidence, and a set of named, arguable verdicts where the tradition previously kept pieties. Readers who need Marx's own account of class should begin with the series class guide linked in the opening sections of this profile, and come to Wright for what exegesis cannot supply: the spectacle of the concepts at work under modern conditions, failing informatively and succeeding in part.

## The old petty bourgeoisie in Wright's hands

Between the two middles of his American data stood the oldest intermediate stratum of all, and Wright's treatment of it repays attention because it shows both frameworks handling a case the classical theory thought it understood. The petty bourgeoisie of shopkeepers, independent tradespeople, small farmers, and solo practitioners owns its means of production and employs no one, or almost no one, working its own property with its own labour. The classical expectation was disappearance, ground between the millstones of big capital and organised labour. The first framework registered the stratum as a non contradictory location of its own, neither fish nor fowl in the delegation structure, and predicted its distinctive politics from its material situation: fierce defence of property and independence, resentment of both the large firms that squeeze its margins and the unions that raise its costs, susceptibility to movements promising to protect the small producer against both. The asset framework registered the same stratum as owners of means of production without organisation or credential assets of consequence, and predicted its vulnerability from the thinness of its holdings: a single asset, exposed to every market weather, without the rents or authority that cushion the employee locations. The stratum's persistence, in niches from food retail to personal services to the trades, embarrassed the classical expectation and vindicated the frameworks' shared refusal of it: locations persist where the functions or holdings that define them continue to be reproduced, and small property continues to be reproduced wherever entry costs are low, demand is local, and the work resists standardisation. Wright's analyses of the American data gave the persistence its most careful documentation, and the companion article linked in the opening sections develops the general question this profile touches only where Wright's schemes illuminate it.

## Uptake beyond sociology: health, education, and the uses of a location

The adjacent discipline uptake promised in the brief for this profile materialised most concretely where researchers needed a measure of workplace power. In social epidemiology, studies of how work affects health found in Wright's categories a ready instrument: the distinction between employees who control their own labour process and those who do not, and between those who direct others and those who are only directed, patterns exposure to strain, autonomy, and insecurity in ways that income bands blur. The semi autonomous professional and the scripted operative may earn alike and sicken differently, and Wright's first framework names the variable responsible. In education research, his credential analysis supplied a framework for studying how qualifications sort and reward, and how the expansion of higher education interacts with the rents of the already credentialed, questions that the sociology of education had pursued through other vocabularies and could pursue through his with the advantage of an explicit mechanism. In political science, the location maps gave the study of class voting a defensible set of categories at the moment the death of class literature was declaring the topic closed. Each uptake took a fragment: a variable, a mechanism, a map. None took the whole Marxist programme, and Wright's writings register no resentment at the fragmentation. Instruments that travel in pieces are still instruments, and a tradition confident in its questions can afford to lend its tools.

## The open problems list: what Wright left unfinished

Wright's late writings contain something few theorists provide: an explicit inventory of what his programme had failed to solve. The inventory deserves reproduction in spirit, because it converts this profile's assessment from a eulogy into an agenda. The relation between class mechanisms and racial domination remained, in his judgement, undertheorised, with the interaction documented and the joint theory unwritten. The household as a unit of class analysis remained unresolved between individual and mediated locations. The class structure of the global economy, in which the locations of one country's workforce are defined partly by relations to workers in other countries through supply chains and migration, exceeded the national frames his surveys inherited. The dynamics of locations over a life, and of structures over decades, lacked the longitudinal instruments his cross sections could not supply. And the normative bridge from class analysis to institutional design, the work of his final decade, remained a beginning rather than a system. Each item on the list is stated with the same precision he brought to the frameworks, and each has attracted researchers who treat the list as a commission. A thinker's unfinished business, itemised by the thinker, is a form of generosity the tradition rarely extends to its successors, and it may in the end be the most Wright like thing about him.

## The university as workplace: a second worked example

The university compresses every difficulty of Wright's subject into one employer, which is why his own profession recurs in his examples and why it serves here as a final test of the instruments. The vice chancellor or president directs thousands, controls budgets that rival firms, and remains an employee of a board or a state. The professor holds a credential fenced by a doctorate and a hiring market of extreme narrowness, controls her teaching and research to a degree that survives, embattled, into the audit era, and supervises doctoral students whose labour contributes to her output. The lecturer on a short contract holds the same credential with none of the security, teaching set syllabuses under observation. The administrator manages the systems through which the others are measured, commanding no classroom and much of the institution's real daily power. The cleaner, the caterer, and the security guard work on the same site under contractors whose relation to the university is a purchase order. The first framework sorts this population by functions with ease and with bite: the professor's semi autonomy, the administrator's contradictory location, the contract lecturer's slide toward the proletarian pole as scripting and surveillance advance, each placement generating a prediction about where each group's grievances will aim. The asset framework sorts the same population by holdings: the doctorate as the site's master asset, organisational assets concentrated in administration, the contract lecturer as the case that proves a credential pays its rent only where posts are scarce relative to qualified aspirants, not by virtue of the parchment alone, since a fenced occupation with more qualified aspirants than positions pays its marginal members like the assetless. Both sortings are recognisably true, and their non identity is the article's whole argument in one workplace: the frameworks are instruments tuned to different properties of the same reality, and mastery consists in knowing which property one's question concerns.

### Why does the same doctorate yield a rent for one lecturer and none for another?

Because a credential's rent depends on the scarcity of the posts it fences relative to the supply of its holders, so where qualified aspirants outnumber positions the marginal holder's asset commands no premium, a dynamic the asset framework predicts and hiring markets in oversupplied disciplines confirm.

## Controversy conducted properly: the lesson of the exchanges

The published exchanges around Wright's work, above all The Debate on Classes, in which critics including the British empirical team and theorists of several schools confronted his operationalisations and he replied to each in turn, deserve study as a model independent of their content. The pattern of the exchanges is invariant. A critic identifies a precise point: a boundary in the matrix, a coding decision in the survey, a slide in the definition of an asset. Wright replies by conceding what the evidence supports, distinguishing what it does not, and restating the claim at whatever strength survives. Positions move. The map that emerges from the volume differs from the map that entered it, and the difference is credited to the critics in print. Set against the ordinary conduct of theoretical controversy, in which positions are defended to the last comma and concessions are disguised as clarifications, the exchanges explain the loyalty Wright inspired among people who disagreed with him fundamentally. He treated disagreement as the mechanism by which the field's knowledge grows, and he built institutions, the project, the edited volumes, the archived data, that made disagreement productive rather than merely polemical. The verdict defeater attached to this article's central judgement is a small homage to that practice: a claim that states what would overturn it invites the only kind of reading Wright ever asked for.

## The lessons of Class Counts, stated as findings

The balance sheet of the comparative project can be compressed into a set of findings that any subsequent class analysis must accommodate, and stating them plainly is the best service this profile can render a researcher approaching the archive. Locations defined by ownership, authority, and credentials pattern income strongly and consistently across countries, so the material skeleton of the theory is sound. Locations pattern attitudes and political dispositions genuinely but modestly, with wide variation inside every cell, so the theory of consciousness must be a theory of mediation and not of reflection. Gender divides every location and reorganises several, so no future version of the schema can treat the workforce as ungendered individuals without repeating a documented error. National institutions multiply or muffle every structural effect, so comparison is not a luxury of the research design but its engine. Sector matters within location, public employment colouring the consciousness of identical cells. Households mediate the locations of their members, most sharply for those outside employment. And the apex of ownership escapes survey measurement altogether, so the method must be supplemented wherever the theory's most consequential relation is at stake. Wright stated every one of these findings himself, in the volumes that reported the project, which is why the archive remains usable by his critics: the caveats travel with the data, in the author's own hand.

## The problem restated: what a successor inherits

A successor taking up the middle class problem after Wright inherits a transformed landscape. The problem is no longer whether intermediate positions exist or whether they matter; the surveys settled their existence and their material patterning beyond serious dispute. The problem is which of the two incompatible solutions, or which successor instrument, should organise the next round of inquiry, and the inheritance includes the criteria for choosing: derivation from a mechanism rather than assembly by judgement, where the second framework set the standard; fidelity to relations of command and appropriation inside production, where the first framework set it; openness to measurement by public instruments, where the comparative project set it; and honesty about scope, where the late pluralism set it, assigning each instrument the questions it can answer and forbidding the imperialism that ruined simpler theories. No existing scheme meets all four criteria at once, and Wright's career can be read as a demonstration that the criteria conflict in pairs: derivation pulls toward distribution, fidelity pulls toward texture, and the tension between them is not a scandal to be resolved but the field's permanent working condition. His two incompatible solutions stand as the twin monuments of that condition, one on each side of the divide, built by the same hands.

For the Marxist tradition specifically, the inheritance carries a sharper edge. Wright showed that the tradition's central concept could be made to survive empirical discipline, at the price of its grandest expectations. Class locations do not generate consciousness as the classical texts hoped; exploitation does not require the labour theory that classical texts used to define it; the middle does not vanish as the classical texts predicted. What survives the discipline is a leaner theory: exploitation and domination as real mechanisms, locations as real constraints, organisation and institutions as the variable machinery between structure and politics. Whether that leaner theory still deserves the tradition's name is the question his critics and defenders will go on disputing, and the dispute itself, conducted with shared evidence and named defeaters, is his doing. The articles surrounding this profile take up the neighbouring questions in their own terms: the general theory of class in the guide linked throughout, the middle strata in their own article, the exploitation reformulation in Roemer's, and the institutional alternatives Wright pursued at the end in the real utopias article, with the VaultBook companion to this series offering a structured card of the two frameworks for readers who keep their notes in a personal vault: [Marxism study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html).

## Before class analysis: the prison book and the making of an empiricist

Wright's first book is a surprise to readers who know only the class analyst. The Politics of Punishment, published in nineteen seventy three while he was still a graduate student, is a critical study of American prisons, written from research and activism inside the California prison system, where he had taught courses to incarcerated students. The book matters to this profile for what it reveals about the formation of his method. Its argument is empirical before it is theoretical: an institution justified by rehabilitation is examined against the evidence of what it does, and the justification fails the examination. The pattern of the mature work is already present, a legitimating claim taken seriously enough to be tested and reported honestly when the test destroys it. Wright's path from the prison study to Berkeley sociology, and from a doctorate that combined fieldwork with theory to the Wisconsin chair, also explains the texture of his class analysis, its impatience with purely textual Marxism and its insistence that concepts earn their place by organising evidence about real institutions. The young man who had watched a system's self description collapse against its practice became the theorist who built instruments to perform the same comparison on his own tradition's self description, and who published the results when the comparison went against the tradition. Consistency of that kind, across a whole career and against his own interests in reputation, is the rarest of scholarly virtues, and it is the deepest reason this series gives him a profile of this length.

## Misreadings to correct

Five misreadings recur in summaries of Wright, and each is worth correcting in the terms his own work supplies. The first holds that contradictory locations describe people who are confused about their class, as though the contradiction were a failure of self knowledge to be cured by education. The contradiction is in the position, not the person: a manager who understands her situation perfectly still occupies a location whose interests point in opposed directions, and her clarity changes nothing about the structure that generates the opposition. The second misreading treats the asset framework as a development or refinement of the first, a later edition of the same theory. The foundations differ in kind, relations of production in the one case and distributions of holdings in the other, and Wright presented the second as a replacement whose incompatibility with the first he stated himself; readers who merge them lose the very phenomenon, the fork in the theory's road, that makes his career instructive. The third misreading concludes that he abandoned Marxism, usually on the evidence of the late pluralism. The late work retains exploitation and domination as genuine mechanisms and class locations as their map, while surrendering the claim that one map exhausts inequality; a surrender of monopoly is not a surrender of the territory. The fourth misreading reports that the survey project confirmed his frameworks, when its published results complicated both, weakened the strong theory of consciousness, and redirected his programme toward mediation, a record he printed himself. The fifth, subtlest misreading treats his later multidimensional turn as a rejection of class analysis, when it is a demarcation of it: the Marxist instrument is assigned the exploitation question with undiminished confidence, and the neighbouring instruments are assigned the questions they answer better, which is the opposite of abandoning the instrument.

### That Wright abandoned Marxism in his later work: what does the record show?

A retention of the core: exploitation and domination as real mechanisms, class locations as measurable and consequential, and the critique of capitalism as the centre of his normative writing to the final book, alongside a surrender of the claim that a single class map could exhaust the analysis of inequality.

## Three short cases: the franchise, the nurse manager, the permanent secretary

Compressed cases discipline theory the way counterexamples discipline logic, and Wright's schemes can be sharpened against three figures who recur in argument. The franchise holder owns a business, hires staff, and bears the risk of failure, yet the product, the prices, the suppliers, the décor, and the opening hours are dictated by a corporation that can terminate the arrangement. The first framework places the figure on the contradictory seam separating owners from independent producers and adds a twist the classical map cannot express: the functions of capital that matter most, strategy and appropriation of the brand's rent, have been delegated upward to the franchisor, leaving the owner with the functions of a manager over her own property. The asset framework reaches the same doubleness through holdings: real ownership of premises and equipment, hollowed by the absence of the organisational assets that govern them. Both readings explain the franchise holder's characteristic politics, defence of property joined to grievance against the corporation, better than either the label small business owner or the label manager can do alone.

The nurse manager, promoted from the ward to coordinate a unit's staffing, budgets, and standards, is the contradictory location in its most legible modern form. She directs the labour of former peers, enforces targets set by administrators she does not control, and remains a salaried clinician whose own work is audited by the same systems she operates. Her interests, on Wright's machinery, are genuinely divided: in staffing levels and clinical autonomy she stands with the ward; in budget discipline and the defence of hierarchy she stands with the administration; and her conduct in a dispute will track whichever side of the location the dispute engages. The asset framework adds the observation that her promotion moved her across a cell boundary without any change in her credentials, isolating the organisational asset as the variable that changed, which is why such promotions are natural experiments in his sense: they vary one asset while holding the person constant, and the surveys' finding that authority shifts attitudes independently of income is the aggregate of ten thousand such promotions.

The permanent secretary, the senior civil servant who directs a ministry while ministers rotate above, tests the frameworks against the state. Ownership is absent; the apparatus directed is the largest in the society; the credential, whether of examination, profession, or career, is formidable. The first framework reads that position as delegated command at its most developed, a contradictory location pressed against the boundary of a state stratum. The second reads it as the purest available specimen of the expert manager, organisation and skill assets at maximum with property at zero, and it predicts from the holdings the position's material conservatism: a stratum so endowed by hierarchy and credential has everything to conserve in any order that keeps both valuable. Wright's statist analysis extends the reading: where such a stratum monopolises coordination across a whole economy, the location hardens toward a class. The three cases share a moral. Wright's schemes earn their keep not by relabelling familiar figures but by specifying, for each, which relations or holdings generate the figure's interests, and therefore which events, a restructuring, a promotion, a change of regime, will move the figure and in which direction.

## Class analysis and the feasibility question

Wright's class work was never only descriptive, and its connection to the question of socialism's feasibility runs through the statist analysis like a spine. If abolishing private ownership while retaining command hierarchies merely relocates exploitation onto the organisational axis, then a socialism worthy of the tradition's own values must democratise coordination itself, dispersing or rendering accountable the organisation assets whose monopoly generates a stratum. That conclusion, drawn in the nineteen eighties from the asset framework, shaped everything Wright later built: his interest in workplace democracy, in associational governance, in the institutional designs gathered under the real utopias heading, and in forms of market coordination that socialise ownership without enthroning a bureaucracy. The class analysis and the utopian engineering are one project at two ends. The frameworks map the mechanisms by which domination reproduces itself inside formally egalitarian arrangements; the designs attempt arrangements in which those mechanisms are blocked. Readers who meet only the later, constructive Wright miss the diagnostic machinery that tells him what a design must block, and readers who meet only the analyst miss the purpose for which the diagnosis was made. This profile has concentrated on the analyst because its brief assigns the designs to their own article, but the connection belongs on the record: the two incompatible solutions were built by a man who intended to use the true one, whichever it proved to be, to change the structure both described.

### What follows for socialist design if organisation assets generate exploitation?

That socialising ownership is insufficient on its own: the coordination of production must itself be democratised or dispersed, since a monopoly of organisation assets by a coordinating stratum would reproduce class exploitation under public ownership, the lesson Wright drew from the statist societies.

## The programme named: neo Marxist class analysis and its standards

Wright eventually gave his approach a label, neo Marxist class analysis, and the label's modesty is instructive. Neo signalled reconstruction rather than orthodoxy: the tradition's questions retained, its answers rebuilt under modern evidential standards. The programme that travelled under the label, in his department and far beyond it, can be recognised by four commitments that this article has encountered repeatedly and can finally be listed together. Class concepts must be tied to mechanisms of exploitation and domination, not to income bands or prestige scales. Locations must be specified without reference to the consciousness and conduct they are used to explain. Claims must be exposed to measurement by instruments other researchers can inspect and reuse. And results must be published whole, including the results that demote the programme's own expectations. A research school is a set of commitments about how to work, more than a set of conclusions about the world, and by that definition Wright founded one: the researchers who operationalise class through authority, credentials, and ownership, who archive their instruments, and who report their null findings in the main text rather than the footnotes are working in his school whether or not they cite him. The frameworks will be revised and perhaps replaced. The standards are the durable contribution, and they are the reason a series on Marxist thought gives its longest sociological profile to a man whose two most famous theories cannot both be true.

## The shadow of exploitation in Weber: Wright reads his rival

Wright's most instructive single essay on a rival is his reading of Weber, in which he argues that Weber's apparently distributive concept of class, defined by market situation and life chances, carries a shadow of the exploitation relation it officially excludes. Weber's classes are constituted in markets, by the assets and skills individuals bring to exchange, and Weber explicitly resisted grounding class in production relations of domination. Wright's counter reading observes that the market capacities Weber catalogues are not natural endowments but products of exclusion enforced within production and property relations: the credential's market value presupposes the fence, the manager's market value presupposes the hierarchy that makes coordination scarce and saleable, and the owner's market position presupposes the exclusion of the propertyless from the means of work. On this reading, the Weberian map is accurate as a chart of consequences and silent about the mechanisms that load the dice before the market opens, and the Marxist register's question, who appropriates whose surplus under what domination, remains standing behind the Weberian answer like the shadow behind the figure. The essay earns its place here because it shows the later pluralism from its strong side. Wright does not dismiss Weber's question; he grants its legitimacy, maps its territory, and then demonstrates that the territory has a basement the Weberian instrument cannot enter. Different questions, yes, but not equal in depth on the question of where market advantage comes from, and the demonstration is conducted with a fairness toward Weber that makes the conclusion harder, not easier, to evade.

### What is the shadow Wright finds inside Weber's concept of class?

The unacknowledged dependence of market capacities on relations of exclusion and domination in production, since the value of what individuals bring to market, credentials, authority, property, is created by fences and hierarchies that the market centred account treats as given rather than as its object.

## The precariat proposal tested on Wright's instruments

A generation after Wright's frameworks, Guy Standing proposed that global capitalism was generating a new class, the precariat, defined by insecure labour, curtailed rights, and the absence of an occupational identity, and the proposal's career offers a convenient test of the standards this article has described. Wright's instruments ask of any proposed class three questions. Is the location defined by a mechanism, exploitation, domination, closure, rather than by a list of disadvantages? Is it defined independently of the consciousness it is meant to explain? And can it be measured with public instruments that rivals may apply? The precariat, as proposed, gathers its members by insecurity, which is a condition that cuts across Wright's locations: the contract lecturer, the platform courier, the seasonal farmworker, and the freelance designer share precarity while differing in credentials, authority, and assets, and therefore, on his machinery, in interests. His likely verdict can be reconstructed from his principles without putting words in his mouth: precarity names a real and growing dimension of employment relations, a degradation of the security that once attached to many locations, and it operates as a modifier across the class map rather than as a location on it. The reconstruction illustrates the frameworks' continuing use as a court of appeal for new class coinages. A proposed class that cannot state its mechanism, its boundaries, and its measurement has not yet earned a cell, and the discipline of demanding those three things from every claimant is Wright's method surviving his matrices.

## What the income gradient showed: the material skeleton in detail

The strongest single body of evidence for the asset framework is the income gradient the comparative surveys traced across its locations, and its shape deserves a closer description than summary usually allows, in the qualitative terms the figures protocol for this article prescribes. Moving across the matrix from the assetless employee locations toward the expert manager cell, incomes rise in a stepwise pattern that tracks the accumulation of assets: each additional holding, a credential, a span of authority, a supervisory tier, carries its own increment, and the increments compound rather than substitute for one another. Ownership, where the surveys could capture it among small and middling employers, sits atop the gradient with the widest dispersion, its occupants ranging from the marginal shopkeeper below the expert employees to the prosperous proprietor above them. Two features of the pattern impressed Wright particularly. The authority increments survive statistical controls for education and occupation, which is what the organisation asset thesis requires and what a pure human capital account, in which pay rewards only skill, cannot explain. And the credential increments survive controls for authority, which is what the skill asset thesis requires against a pure command account. Each asset, in other words, pays separately, in country after country, and a theory that recognised only one of them would leave a documented regularity unexplained. Whatever the fate of the exploitation interpretation placed on the gradient, the gradient itself, the stepwise material architecture of the employee structure, is a permanent acquisition of the surveys, reproduced by researchers who reject the interpretation while relying on the architecture.

The attitudinal gradient, by contrast, refused to hold the same clean shape, and the refusal is equally instructive. On questions of redistribution and the legitimacy of inequality, the locations order themselves roughly as interests would predict. On questions of workplace authority, union legitimacy, and the desirable organisation of the economy, the ordering frays: expert employees divide by sector, supervisors divide by the reality of their authority, and the assetless locations divide by nation and by race. Wright's published reading of the fraying is the mediation thesis in its empirical form. Material interests, carried by locations, speak clearly where the question touches the transfer itself, in pay and redistribution, and speak through interpreters, parties, unions, professions, national settlements, where the question touches the organisation of life. A class theory that expected one gradient to govern both territories was asking structure to do formation's work, and the surveys, by granting the first territory and refusing the second, drew the boundary of structural explanation exactly where his triad had predicted it would be found in nineteen seventy eight. The career's circle closes there: the first book's scaffolding vindicated by the last dataset's pattern of success and failure, with the two frameworks, the instruments built along the way, standing between them as the most thoroughly tested class schemes the tradition has produced.

## Analytical sociology and the demand for mechanisms

Wright's later reception included an audience he had not originally addressed: the analytical sociology movement, which organised itself around the demand that social explanations specify the mechanisms linking structure to action. The movement's canonical complaint against grand theory was Wright's complaint against his own tradition, that structural vocabularies invoke effects without the machinery that produces them, and his class analysis supplied the movement with a worked example of the alternative. A location, in his mature usage, is not a label but a bundle of mechanisms: exclusion from assets, subjection to command, entitlement to rents, exposure to insecurity, each with identifiable consequences for the interests and opportunities of occupants. The comparative project's instruments then appear, in this reception, as mechanism measures rather than class measures, and their findings as evidence about which mechanisms operate where. Wright welcomed the reading while resisting its occasional imperialism, the suggestion that mechanism talk dissolves the need for the tradition's questions. Mechanisms answer how; the Marxist register also asks who benefits and by what right, and a sociology that loses the second question in admiration of the first has traded Wright's programme for a technique. The exchange, courteous on both sides, marks the furthest reach of his influence: into a movement that shares his standards while bracketing his politics, and that therefore tests, in the purest available form, his lifelong wager that rigour is a common currency across traditions.

## The seminar room legacy: how Wright is taught

In sociology curricula, Wright occupies a distinctive pedagogical position: he is the Marxist whom methods courses can assign. A theory syllabus can present the two frameworks as the tradition's most examinable claims, since each can be stated in a lecture, drawn on a board, and disputed with evidence. A methods syllabus can present the comparative project as a case study in operationalisation, following a concept from definition through questionnaire item to dataset to revised definition. A stratification syllabus can set his matrix beside Goldthorpe's schema and Bourdieu's capitals and ask students to classify the same workforce three ways, which is the later pluralism converted into an exercise. The pedagogical afterlife matters because it determines what the next generation of researchers takes class analysis to be: not a catechism of positions but a set of instruments with known properties, open problems itemised by their maker, and a standard of candour about failure that students absorb as professional ethics. Teachers interviewed in the literature on his teaching describe a seminar practice consistent with the writings: his own theories presented as specimens for dissection, the strongest student objections treated as contributions, and grades, one gathers, entirely safe for the student who dismantled the week's reading, including when the reading was his. The persona sections that close this article translate that pedagogy into guidance for the three audiences the series serves, and readers who have followed the argument this far will recognise the pattern: the teacher in Wright and the theorist in Wright are the same person, and the profile's judgements have been formed by watching both.

## The service class: a neighbouring label and what Wright did with it

European sociology possessed its own label for the population Wright studied, the service class, a term descending from Karl Renner and developed by Goldthorpe for the salaried professionals, administrators, and managers whose employment relation rests on trust, delegated authority, and prospective rewards rather than on the discrete exchange of effort for wages. The label and Wright's locations cover much of the same human territory, and the comparison between them is a standard exercise in the field because the two schemes explain the territory's coherence differently. For Goldthorpe, the service class coheres because its members share an employment relationship: employers purchase their loyalty and judgement with careers, increments, and security, since their work cannot be monitored piece by piece. For Wright, the same population is divided against itself by design: expert managers, expert non managers, and supervisors occupy different cells with different assets and therefore different interests, and their apparent coherence as a stratum is an artefact of similar incomes concealing divergent relations to exploitation and command. The empirical contest between the schemes turned on exactly this difference. Where the service class reading predicts uniform behaviour from a shared employment relation, Wright's reading predicts splits along the authority and credential lines inside the stratum, and his data found the splits, modest but real, in attitudes toward unions, redistribution, and workplace authority. The contest remains unresolved in the literature's aggregate, since the schemes also share enough predictions to make many datasets ambiguous between them, and Wright's own conclusion was characteristic: the rivalry is productive, the instruments are public, and the next dataset is invited to do its worst.

### How does Wright's map divide the population Goldthorpe calls the service class?

By assets and authority rather than by employment relationship, separating expert managers from expert non managers and both from supervisors, so that a stratum unified on the rival account appears on Wright's map as several locations whose interests diverge along the lines of command and credential.

## Reading election coverage with Wright's instruments

Journalists reach for class language at election time with more confidence than the language can bear, and Wright's work supplies a corrective kit that the researcher persona section below develops for professional use. The kit has three rules. Treat every invocation of the middle class as a claim about locations that must be specified: which occupations, holding which assets, exercising which authority, since the electoral behaviour of expert public employees and of small employers diverges too reliably for the shared label to predict anything. Distinguish the structure story from the formation story: a result explained by class is usually a result explained by organisation, turnout machinery, union density, and party strategy operating on a structure, and the analyst who credits the structure alone will be surprised by the next election in the same place. And distrust any account in which a class votes as a bloc: Wright's distributions overlap everywhere, his locations shift probabilities rather than deliver verdicts, and honest reporting of class and politics is reporting about shifted probabilities among specified groups. Election coverage that observed the three rules would read less dramatically and err less often, which is perhaps why the rules remain more honoured in the research literature than in the news pages his datasets were partly built to inform.

## Exploitation in ordinary moral argument: what Wright's distinctions contribute

Outside the academy, the word exploitation does heavy and sloppy work, applied to low wages, long hours, sharp bargains, and unpopular profits without distinction. Wright's conceptual labour contributes three distinctions that ordinary argument can use without any commitment to his politics. The first separates exploitation from mere low pay: a badly paid worker in a failing firm may be exploited little in the transfer sense if there is scarcely a surplus to appropriate, while a highly paid expert may be implicated in exploitation as a beneficiary of closure, so pay level alone settles nothing. The second separates exploitation from domination: a worker may be dominated without much transfer, as in a public bureaucracy where command is petty and budgets are thin, and transfers may occur with little personal domination, as through impersonal rents, and the remedies for the two differ, one requiring the redistribution of assets and the other the democratisation of command. The third separates exploitation from unfairness in general: Wright's concept is tied to a mechanism, exclusion from productive assets sustaining a transfer, and arrangements can be unfair, in distribution, in recognition, in procedure, without that mechanism, just as the mechanism can operate inside arrangements every participant regards as fair. Public argument equipped with the three distinctions argues better about everything from executive pay to professional licensing, and the distinctions are available in Wright's texts in forms a careful reader can extract without swallowing the matrix whole. A tradition's concepts earn their keep in the arguments of people who reject the tradition's conclusions, and by that measure, once again, Wright's concepts are among the hardest working the Marxist tradition has issued.

## Modes of production in the asset mirror: feudalism and hybrid forms

The asset framework's claim to generality rests on its redescription of other modes of production, and the redescription repays scrutiny because it shows the scheme thinking at its most ambitious. Feudalism, on Wright's account, is the order in which the decisive asset is neither private capital nor credential but a fusion of landholding with organisational and coercive command: the lord's demesne, his court, his armed retainers, and his jurisdiction together constitute the apparatus through which the peasant surplus is extracted, and the peasant's own holding of a plot, the means of subsistence production, marks the limit of the extraction and the basis of a different class logic from capitalism's. The wage worker owns no means of production and must sell labour power entire; the serf owns enough to reproduce independently and surrenders a portion of labour or product under direct coercion. Wright's scheme registers the difference as a difference in asset portfolios and in the mechanism of appropriation they sustain, labour rent under coercion against surplus value under contract, and thereby explains, in one vocabulary, why the politics of the two orders differ: resistance under feudalism aims at the coercive apparatus and the customary limits of extraction, while resistance under capitalism aims at the wage relation and the organisation of the labour process. Hybrid forms, orders in which several asset asymmetries operate at comparable strength, receive the scheme's most interesting treatment. A society of independent peasants dominated by a bureaucratic state, or a capitalist economy with a vast credentialed public sector, appears in the mirror as a genuine mixture of class logics, whose strata cannot be assigned to a single mode's map without remainder. Wright regarded the capacity to describe mixtures without embarrassment as a mark in the framework's favour, since real societies are mixtures and theories that demand purity describe only themselves. Critics replied that a scheme which redescribes everything explains correspondingly little, and the exchange returns the reader, by a long road, to the closure objection and to the exploitation triad that Wright stationed as its guard.

## Preferences formed by locations: the Elster pressure

Analytical Marxism's demand for microfoundations pressed Wright on a point his location maps initially assumed rather than explained: the passage from interests to preferences. People do not always want what their location's interests prescribe. They adapt their desires to their prospects, discounting goods they cannot reach and consecrating the goods within reach, the sour grapes mechanism Elster made famous. A worker may oppose redistribution from which she would benefit because she expects to be a supervisor within five years, or support it bitterly because she expects never to be. Wright absorbed the pressure by treating preference formation as one of the mediations his mature theory required, and by designing survey items that measured expectations and trajectories alongside locations, so that the data could register the forward looking distortions Elster described. The concession has a consequence worth stating for students: a location's interests, in Wright's final usage, are an analyst's construction, the welfare the location's occupants would enjoy under specified changes, and the construction remains valid as an explanatory tool even when the occupants' stated preferences diverge from it, provided the divergence is itself explained by mechanisms, adaptation, expectation, misinformation, rather than merely asserted. The line between that position and the false consciousness doctrine the young Wright rejected is fine but real. False consciousness names a deviation and stops; the mature machinery names the deviation and owes the reader its mechanism, and the debt is what distinguishes a research programme from a rhetoric.

## The underclass debate: locations outside the map

William Julius Wilson's The Truly Disadvantaged directed American attention to populations concentrated in deindustrialised neighbourhoods, detached from stable employment across generations, and the ensuing debate about an underclass tested every class scheme's treatment of people whose defining feature is exclusion from the locations themselves. Wright's engagement with the debate was characteristically taxonomic before it was polemical. Joblessness, he argued, is not a location but an absence where locations should be, and its analysis requires the concepts his maps presuppose: the structure of demand for labour, the spatial organisation of cities, the racial mechanisms that sort populations into abandonment. Where the underclass label implied a stratum defined by culture or behaviour, Wright's machinery insisted on defining the population by its relation to the opportunity structure, as the residue produced when the employee locations contract in a place and the exits, credentials, mobility, migration, are blocked by race and by policy. The insistence connects the underclass discussion to his boundary inventory: the unwaged and the excluded mark the map's edge, and the edge is produced by the same economy that produces the map's interior, so a complete class analysis must theorise the production of the edge rather than treating it as an external misfortune. His comparative data contributed one sharp observation to the debate: countries with similar deindustrialisation produced smaller and less concentrated residues where welfare institutions and active labour market policies kept the excluded attached to income and services, which is the mediation thesis operating at the map's boundary, and a reminder that the size of the unmapped population is a political outcome, not a natural constant.

## Class structures compared: the similarity that surprised

One of the comparative project's quietest findings may be its most consequential for theory. When the national datasets were coded into Wright's locations, the class structures of the advanced capitalist countries proved broadly similar in their proportions: the spread of locations, the relative sizes of the managerial, credentialed, supervisory, and assetless strata, differed by less than the rhetoric of national exceptionalism in every country had assumed. The structures were cousins. What differed dramatically was everything built on top of them: the strength of unions, the coherence of class voting, the generosity of welfare, the temperature of class language in public life. Wright drew the inference his triad had prepared decades earlier. Class structure, at the level his instruments measure, is a broadly common product of advanced capitalist development, while class formation is a national and historical achievement that varies independently. The inference reorganised the research agenda of everyone who accepted it. Instead of asking why countries have different class structures, the interesting question became why similar structures support such different politics, and that question belongs to the study of institutions, parties, and movements, the mediating machinery. It also delivered a final, ironic verdict on the polarisation debate with which this article began. The classical theory was wrong about the structure's direction and wrong about the structure's political transparency, yet the comparative evidence shows a structure that is recognisably one structure across the capitalist world, patterned by ownership, authority, and credentials in proportions no national story explains. Marxism's structural intuition survived its eschatology, in the work of the man who tested both.

## The judgement assembled

The strands of this profile can be gathered into a small number of judgements, each stated with its standing in the evidence. Wright addressed the hardest empirical problem in Marxist class theory, the location of managers and professionals in a theory predicting polarisation, and he addressed it when most of the tradition preferred to look away: that judgement is a matter of record. He solved the problem twice, with a relational scheme of contradictory locations and with a distributional scheme of assets, and the solutions are incompatible in their foundations, as his own presentation states and as the critics confirmed by attacking the second from the first's premises. The second solution, this article has argued, is distributional in its manner of statement and partly relational in its substance, with organisation assets constituting the strong case for the defence and credentials the weak one: that judgement carries the defeater stated in the criticism section, and a reader who completes the specified demonstration from his texts should reverse it. He built the largest empirical programme in Marxist sociology to test his schemes, and the programme's results complicated both schemes while vindicating their material core: that judgement is the published balance sheet of Class Counts. And he ended by assigning the rival theories of class to different questions, an evolution this article has judged honest and costly in equal measure, honest because the evidence drove it and costly because the tradition's unified ambition was its price: that judgement would be defeated by a showing that the pluralism was adopted before, rather than after, the evidence that supports it, and the chronology of the books and datasets is the tribunal. A profile that has demanded defeaters of its subject's theories owes its subject no less for its own, and the five judgements assembled here are offered under that discipline, to be reversed by any reader who brings the specified evidence to bear.

## Control in the labour process: Edwards, Friedman, and the texture Wright mapped

The first framework's attention to control placed Wright inside the labour process debate that Braverman's book had ignited, and his exchanges with that debate's other protagonists sharpened the framework's categories. Richard Edwards, in Contested Terrain, had distinguished three historical systems of workplace control: the simple control of the foreman's personal authority, the technical control embedded in machinery and line speed, and the bureaucratic control exercised through rules, grades, and internal labour markets. Andrew Friedman, in Industry and Labour, counterposed two managerial strategies, direct control and responsible autonomy, arguing that management alternates between coercing workers and cultivating their discretion according to the resistance each strategy provokes. Wright's location map can be read as the class theoretic deposit of these debates. Bureaucratic control is precisely the environment that manufactures contradictory locations in bulk, since every grade in a rule governed hierarchy delegates a fragment of capital's authority to an employee who remains subject to the grades above. Responsible autonomy is the strategy that creates and recreates the semi autonomous location, granting discretion because the work requires it and withdrawing it when the balance of forces shifts. Where Edwards and Friedman wrote as analysts of management, Wright asked the question their work implied and did not pose: what class interests correspond to each control position, and what politics should be expected from the strata the control systems manufacture? The question converted a literature about technique into a literature about structure, and the conversion is a large part of why the first framework, for all its measurement troubles, remains the reference point for sociologists of work who have never opened Classes.

The debate also exposed, from a friendly direction, the first framework's most persistent ambiguity: whether control is a matter of the job's design or of the incumbent's experience. Two workers on identical lines, under identical rules, may experience radically different autonomy depending on a supervisor's habits, a union's vigilance, or a labour market's tightness, and a location defined by design will classify them together while their interests, formed partly in experience, diverge. Wright's survey instruments opted for design, asking about formal decision rights and sanctioning powers rather than felt autonomy, on the methodological ground that design is comparable across respondents and countries. The choice was defensible and consequential. It biased the framework toward the architecture of organisations and away from the lived texture of work, a bias the asset framework inherited and amplified, since holdings are even more architectural than functions. Critics from the ethnographic tradition, whose method is to inhabit workplaces rather than questionnaire them, pressed the point throughout Wright's career: that a class analysis built from survey items will systematically underrate the informal, resistant, improvisational life through which workers and supervisors actually negotiate the relations the items name. Wright's reply conceded the underrating and defended the trade, on the ground that comparison across nations and decades requires the architecture, while ethnography, whose findings he read and cited with respect, supplies the texture case by case. The division of labour between the methods mirrors his division among theories, and students should recognise the signature: an instrument's known blindness, declared in advance, treated as the price of its sight.

### What did bureaucratic control contribute to the manufacture of contradictory locations?

A graded hierarchy of delegated authority, in which each tier directs the tiers below while obeying the tiers above under impersonal rules, so that whole strata of employees come to exercise capital's functions as a routine of office while remaining dismissible employees within the same rule book.

## Associational power: the late concept that completes the triad

One concept from Wright's final period deserves extraction from the real utopias context where it usually sits, because it completes the machinery of his class analysis proper. Associational power names the capacity for collective action that arises from organisation itself: the unions, parties, associations, and movements through which the occupants of locations convert shared interests into force. Wright distinguished it from the structural power conferred by location, the leverage a group holds by virtue of where it sits in production, as when transport workers can halt a network by withdrawing. The distinction organises a great deal of labour history. Structurally powerful groups with weak association dissipate their leverage in sectional skirmishes; structurally weak groups with strong association, the credentialed public employees of his surveys among them, punch far beyond their location's weight. Class formation, in the triad's middle term, is precisely the building of associational power on structural foundations, and its variation across countries, the great finding of the comparative project, becomes a variation in the associational term rather than in the structural one. Sweden's locations did not differ greatly from America's; Sweden's associations differed enormously, and the politics followed the associations.

The concept also reframes the middle locations' political significance in a way both frameworks prepare. Intermediate strata are not merely cross pressured in their interests; they are disproportionately rich in the raw materials of association, in organisational skill, in credentials that certify competence, in the discretionary time and institutional footholds that organisation requires. A labour movement that writes off the middle locations as hopeless thereby abandons the workforce's most associable strata to rival projects, a strategic error Wright's data make visible in country after country, where the allegiance of credentialed and supervisory employees tracks the presence or absence of organisations bidding for it. His class compromise writings draw the conclusion for strategy: durable settlements are built by organising the contradictory locations into the compromise, not by awaiting their disappearance. The young theorist who began by refusing to footnote the middle of the class structure ended by identifying it as the terrain on which the structure's politics are decided, and the distance between those two moments is the measure of the career this profile has traced.

## Welfare regimes and class: the Esping Andersen encounter

The comparative study of welfare states posed Wright's questions at the level of whole societies, and his encounter with Gøsta Esping Andersen's regime typology clarified both research programmes. Esping Andersen had sorted the advanced capitalist democracies into liberal, conservative corporatist, and social democratic regimes according to how far their welfare arrangements free workers from dependence on the market, a property he named decommodification. The typology explained welfare generosity elegantly while treating class mainly as the political coalition behind each regime. Wright's class analysis supplied the missing micro structure: the regimes rest on different configurations of his locations and different compromises among them. The social democratic regime, in his reading, institutionalised a compromise in which the assetless locations received security and services while the credentialed locations received a vast public sector in which their assets commanded protected rents, a coalition of the map's bottom and middle against the insecurities of the market. The liberal regime left both groups exposed, splitting the middle toward private insurance and market credentials. The encounter mattered because it demonstrated the location map doing explanatory work at the macro level, specifying which strata gain what from each settlement, while the regime literature returned the favour by specifying the institutional machinery through which locations become coalitions. Wright's later collaborative work on class and welfare pursued the synthesis explicitly, and it stands as the most successful application of his schema to comparative political economy: the frameworks, built to locate individuals, turned out to illuminate the architecture of states.

### How does a welfare regime appear when read through Wright's location map?

As a settlement among locations with specifiable gains: security and services for the assetless, protected employment and credential rents for the qualified middle, and limits on appropriation for owners, with each regime type corresponding to a different balance of those gains and a different coalition sustaining it.

## The reception beyond the North Atlantic

The comparative project was centred on the wealthy capitalist democracies, and the schema's travels beyond that centre form a coda worth recording in the approximate terms the evidence allows. Stratification researchers in Latin America, working on societies with large informal sectors and sharp racial hierarchies, adopted and adapted Wright's operational questions because the anatomy of jobs, who supervises, who decides, who holds credentials, could be asked anywhere a wage is paid, and because his categories gave their debates about new middle strata a common measure. The adaptations were not transplants. Where informality dominates, the employee locations compress and the petty bourgeois forms multiply beyond anything the North Atlantic datasets displayed, and researchers in those settings used the map the way Wright's pluralism prescribes, as one instrument among several, supplemented by analyses of informality, clientelism, and race that his framework does not contain. In the rapidly industrialising economies of East Asia, the schema's credential axis proved the most portable element, since educational expansion and examination based recruitment made skill assets and their rents a central fact of the class structure early in development. The pattern of the reception repeats the pattern of the North Atlantic uptake at larger scale: fragments travel, mechanisms translate, the total programme stays home. Wright, who had designed the instruments for comparison and published them for strangers, would have recognised the outcome as the one his method intended. A class analysis that can be disassembled for parts by researchers who share none of its author's commitments has achieved the only kind of universality empirical science recognises.

## Micro locations and macro structures: the two senses of class structure

Wright's writings use the phrase class structure in two senses that readers must keep apart, because his findings differ at the two levels. In the micro sense, a class structure is the set of locations, and of the relations connecting them, within a society at a moment: the menu of positions a workforce is sorted into. In the macro sense, it is the distribution of a population across those locations together with the institutional linkages, the property law, the credential systems, the organisational forms, that reproduce the distribution over time. His comparative finding of similar proportions across countries is a macro finding, and its proper statement is that advanced capitalist societies converge on similar menus and similar distributions while diverging in the linkages, the unions, parties, and welfare arrangements, that connect locations to politics. The distinction disciplines a common error in commentary on his work, which cites the similarity finding against the importance of class, as though similar structures with different politics showed structure to be irrelevant. The finding shows the opposite once the senses are separated: structure, held roughly constant across the comparison, cannot explain the political variation, and the variation therefore indicts theories that read politics directly off structure, which is to say it indicts the classical expectation and vindicates the triad. Wright's whole career is contained in that piece of logic. The concept he inherited promised that one level would explain the others. His life's work consisted in separating the levels, measuring the first with unprecedented care, and demonstrating, with the measurements, exactly how much explaining the other levels have to do for themselves.

There is a further refinement in his macro usage that exam answers rarely capture and researchers ignore at their cost. Wright treated class structures as varying not only in their distributions but in their coherence: the degree to which the boundaries between locations coincide across dimensions, so that the same people are privileged by ownership, authority, and credentials at once, or whether advantage in one dimension is offset by disadvantage in another. Highly coherent structures, where every asset accumulates in the same hands, generate sharper interests and harder politics than incoherent ones, where the credential rich are authority poor and the small owners are credential poor, cross cutting the map. His American analyses suggested a structure of middling coherence, with the expert manager cell as the point of maximum accumulation and a broad middle in which advantages offset one another, and he speculated that coherence, more than the size of any stratum, governs the temperature of class conflict a society should expect. The speculation was never developed into a full research programme, and it sits in his work as one of the promissory notes a successor could still redeem with the archived data: compute the coherence of each national structure in the archive and test whether it predicts the class patterning of politics better than the distributions alone. That the note remains redeemable, decades after the data were gathered, is a fair index of how far ahead of his field's agenda the programme was running when its author set it down.

## Wright and the problem of the capitalist class itself

A final boundary deserves notice, because it concerns the location his theory most cares about and his method least reaches. The capitalist class proper, the owners of large scale productive property whose decisions close plants, move production across continents, and discipline governments, appears in Wright's writings more often as a theoretical pole than as a surveyed population. His datasets capture the small employer stratum in abundance and the great owners scarcely at all, and his conceptual writings acknowledge the asymmetry: the bourgeoisie of the matrix is defined by the same asset logic as every other location, yet the mechanisms that matter most at the apex, the pooling of ownership through funds and boards, the separation of legal title from effective control in the modern corporation, the political organisation of capital as a class, require instruments his programme did not build. Wright's treatments of the apex therefore lean on the first framework's functional language, speaking of the positions from which capital's decisions are made and of the executives who occupy them as employees at the boundary, and on the work of other scholars for the substance. The honesty of the acknowledgement should not obscure its weight. A class analysis whose best evidence concerns employees and small property, and whose account of great capital is comparatively schematic, has mapped the structure from its middle outward and left the summit in relative shadow. Wright's defenders reply that the shadow is the survey method's, not the theory's, and that elite research by other means can be bolted onto the location map without disturbing it. The reply is reasonable, and the bolted on research remains thinner than the map's other regions, a standing invitation that the open problems inventory in this article has already recorded. The summit matters because the theory says it matters most, and a reader who finishes Wright with a clear sense of the middle and a schematic sense of the top has reproduced, in miniature, the state of the field he left.

## Two more workplaces: the warehouse and the software firm

The logistics warehouse tests the frameworks at the structure's base, where their convergence is expected and their small divergences still instruct. The pickers and packers who move goods under the direction of scanners and supervisors occupy the assetless location on any reading: no property, no credentials the market fences, no authority over others, and a labour process scripted to a degree the classical factory rarely achieved. The team leader who allocates tasks and reports faults holds a sliver of delegated authority and nothing else, the contradictory location in its thinnest modern form, proletarian in every dimension except the function of directing fellow workers for a shift at a time. The site manager commands the whole apparatus of schedules, systems, and contracts while owning none of it, an expert or non expert manager depending on the credentials the post demands. Wright's machinery draws two lessons from the site. The first is that the distance between the base location and the first rung of authority has narrowed to a single function, so the boundary the first framework drew with such care is crossed by a promotion that changes almost nothing else in a worker's life, which helps explain why attitudes shift less across that boundary than the classical map of supervision implied. The second is that the scripting of the base location's labour process, the elimination of discretion by design, represents capital's answer to the semi autonomous problem from the other side: where discretion cannot be eliminated, it is fenced and rented as a credential; where it can, it is engineered away, and the location structure simplifies toward the classical pole. The warehouse is therefore not a refutation of Wright's middle but a demonstration of the process that decides, sector by sector, how much middle there will be.

The software firm tests the frameworks where credentials dominate, and their divergence is widest. The senior engineer who owns no share of the company yet cannot be replaced without months of search, who sets technical standards juniors must follow, and who negotiates her conditions from a position of scarcity, is the asset framework's exemplary expert: her skill asset yields a rent visible in every dimension of her package, and her lack of direct reports places her outside the managerial cells altogether. The first framework reads the same figure through autonomy: her command of her own labour process, her freedom to choose methods and refuse tasks, places her among the semi autonomous, and her politics, defence of that autonomy against product management and metric surveillance, follow the location's logic. The engineering manager, who has traded the keyboard for the coordination of others, crosses in the first framework toward the contradictory location and in the second into the expert manager cell, and the two descriptions capture the two halves of the familiar grief of that promotion: the loss of autonomy the first framework names, and the gain in organisational rent the second one counts. A theory of the modern workforce that cannot describe both halves of that grief, in a sector whose weight in employment grows decade by decade, is missing the experience of the very strata whose allegiance contemporary politics contests. Wright built one framework for each half, in sequence, and the software firm is as good a place as any to watch them divide the truth between them.

## Class and democracy: the political stake of the location map

Wright's class analysis was always, at one remove, an argument about democracy, and making the connection explicit completes the profile's account of why the frameworks mattered to their author. A political order can be formally democratic, one person one vote, while its class structure concentrates the resources that make votes effective: the wealth that funds campaigns and media, the organisational capacity that sustains parties and movements between elections, the discretionary time and certified competence that let some citizens draft the options others merely choose among. Wright's locations map the distribution of exactly those resources. Organisation assets are, among other things, training in the exercise of collective power. Credential assets certify the competence that public argument rewards. Ownership commands the surplus from which political organisation is financed. The assetless location, by the same accounting, is the position of maximum formal equality and minimum effective resource, and the gap between the two is the class analysis of why democracies drift toward the preferences of their privileged locations even when majorities oppose them. Wright's democratic commitments, visible from the prison book to the posthumous essay on anti capitalism, ran in the opposite direction to the drift: toward arrangements that equalise the resources of citizenship, decommodify the essentials of security, and democratise the workplaces where most adults spend most of their waking lives under government by others. The class analysis supplies the diagnosis that tells such a programme where the resources are hoarded and which locations must be organised, compensated, or disarmed for the equalisation to proceed. Theory and commitment meet in the map, which is why Wright guarded the map's accuracy with a jealousy that purely academic motives cannot explain. Getting the locations wrong meant organising the wrong coalition, defending the wrong rents, and mistaking the terrain of the democracy he wanted for the terrain of the one he had.

## The durability of the problem: automation and the next middle

Wright's problem has outlived every obituary written for it, because the process that generates intermediate positions did not retire with the industrial economy. Each wave of technical change since his frameworks were published has repeated the founding pattern in a fresh register: a technology dissolves an old stratum of skilled or independent work while manufacturing, in the same motion, new positions of supervision, expertise, maintenance, and coordination that the classical map cannot place. Mechanised production created the foreman and the works engineer. Computerisation created the systems analyst, the network administrator, and the project manager, strata whose members sell their labour while directing the labour embodied in machines and in other people. The automation of routine cognitive work, proceeding sector by sector through clerical, legal, medical, and educational employment, is dissolving some credentialed positions while generating others concerned with designing, auditing, training, and taking responsibility for automated systems, positions that combine scarce certified expertise with subjection to the organisations that own the systems. A Wrightian reading of the process refuses both available slogans. Against the claim that automation finally delivers the polarisation the classical theory promised, it observes that coordination and responsibility are being manufactured as fast as routine is being destroyed, and that the new positions display the familiar signatures, divided interests, credential fences, delegated command. Against the claim that the new positions prove class obsolete, it observes that their occupants' advantages are systematically generated by exclusion and appropriation, measurable in income and security, and therefore class phenomena by the only definition that ever mattered to the research programme.

The frameworks divide the new terrain between them in the established pattern. The asset scheme prices the new positions efficiently: it predicts that the rents will gather where credentials fence the automated systems' design and audit, that organisational assets will concentrate in the strata coordinating human and machine labour jointly, and that displaced experts whose skills the systems absorb will suffer the distinctive fate of an asset devalued by technical change, the credential inflation mechanism operating at the speed of software. The relational scheme reads the same terrain for its politics: it predicts that the auditors and coordinators will occupy contradictory locations, enforcing standards they did not set upon workers they increasingly resemble, and that their allegiance will split along the line between their function and their subjection, as it split for the foremen and supervisors of the industrial case. Neither prediction requires the other's vocabulary, and a researcher equipped with both can say, of any newly automated occupation, what it holds and what it does, what it will earn and where it will stand. That double capacity is the practical inheritance of the two incompatible solutions, and it is available to any reader of this profile who has followed the argument to this point, which is the sense in which Wright's unfinished programme continues in hands other than his own.

## The presidential address and the vocation of the analyst

In twenty twelve Wright served as president of the American Sociological Association, the profession's highest elective honour in his country, and the address he gave in that office can stand as the public summary of the trajectory this profile has traced. Its subject was the transformation of capitalism through institutional alternatives, the real utopias theme that the series treats in its own article, but its method was the class analyst's method carried into a new domain: specify the mechanisms by which the existing order reproduces its harms, identify the points where alternative arrangements interrupt those mechanisms, and submit the alternatives to the same unsparing scrutiny of evidence and feasibility that he had applied to his own frameworks. A discipline that had spent decades regarding Marxist class analysis as a sectarian survival elected its most rigorous practitioner to its presidency on the strength of that method, which is the profession's own verdict on the question this article opened with: whether the middle class problem and its analyst belonged to the centre of sociology or to its margins. The honour also clarifies the sense in which Wright's Marxism and his empiricism were one commitment rather than two. The address asked sociologists to treat the design of a better society as an empirical problem, with mechanisms, evidence, and defeaters, exactly as the comparative project had treated the structure of the existing one. Readers who finish this profile and proceed to the real utopias article will find the same habits at work on new material: the refusal of slogans, the insistence on mechanisms, the published list of difficulties. The class analysis came first, and it remains the foundation, because a programme for transforming a structure must begin from the most honest available map of where everyone in that structure stands.

## The commons of the instruments: a closing observation on scholarly practice

One practice of Wright's deserves a final word because it quietly distinguishes his programme from every rival discussed in this profile. He treated his research instruments as a commons. The questionnaires, the coding manuals, the decisions about thresholds and boundaries, and eventually the datasets themselves were published and deposited for other researchers to inspect, reuse, and attack, including researchers committed to proving his categories worthless. The British team that tested his schema against Goldthorpe's could do so because the instruments were public property in a way that theoretical schools rarely permit their tools to become. The consequence is visible in the shape of the literature his work generated: an unusual proportion of it is cumulative, with later studies reusing earlier measures, disputing earlier codings on shared evidence, and extending the archive into countries and decades the original project never reached. Theoretical traditions ordinarily reproduce by commentary, each generation glossing the founders' texts. Wright's school reproduced by instrumentation, each generation recalibrating the measures and rerunning the comparisons, and the difference explains why his concepts remain live equipment in stratification research while the vocabularies of his more exegetical contemporaries survive mainly in histories of the period. A reader who takes one lesson from this long profile into other subjects should take that one: build the instrument, publish the instrument, and let the instrument's failures, faithfully recorded, become the field's knowledge.

## For students and exam candidates

Wright rewards the student who learns his material as an argument rather than as a diagram. Examiners on this topic are testing whether you can do three things: state the middle class problem as a difficulty generated by the theory's own prediction, present the two frameworks as rival answers rather than as stages of one answer, and reproduce the standard objection to the assets framework, with its attribution to the critics who made it. The table of the two frameworks compared is your revision instrument: learn to reconstruct it from an empty grid, and you possess the essay's skeleton. When a question asks whether managers are working class, answer with a specification instead of a label, distinguishing what each framework says and why they differ, and close by stating what evidence about interests or behaviour would decide between them. That defeater habit, naming the consideration that would change your verdict, is the single most portable technique in this article. Begin further reading with the series class guide linked in the opening sections, for the relational definition Wright inherited, and keep your notes on the two frameworks in a structured form you can revise from, such as the series companion card described in the assessment section.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The most productive seminar on this material begins from the students' own households. Ask the room to place a nurse manager, a franchise holder, a contract lecturer, and a small employer on the classical two class map, and let the failures accumulate before any theory is introduced; the middle class problem then arrives as the students' problem rather than as the syllabus's. Introduce contradictory locations next, and set the boundary disputes as group work, since the thresholds the scheme cannot derive are more instructive than the placements it can. The assets framework should be taught against its critics from the start, with Crompton's convergence charge and the British empirical tests assigned alongside Classes rather than after it, so the replacement is never mistaken for a refinement. The comparative project's findings make the strongest closing session: distribute the findings collected in Class Counts and require each group to defend one finding against the framework it embarrasses. Students who have watched a theorist publish his own difficulties argue differently afterward, with less reverence and more precision, and that change in argumentative manners is the deepest learning outcome this material offers a classroom.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Researchers inherit from Wright an archive and a standard. The archive is the comparative project's: its questionnaires, codebooks, and datasets, preserved for secondary analysis and usable for questions their designer never asked, from the class patterning of health to the mobility of the credentialed. Cite the datasets as archived resources and verify their conditions of access through the holding institutions before promising a design around them. The standard is the discipline of his operationalisations: define locations independently of outcomes, publish the coding rules, and report where the categories failed. Historians will find his three level scheme of structure, formation, and struggle the most portable element, since it disciplines the inference from a period's occupational structure to its politics and back. Journalists should take the three rules set out in the section on election coverage: specify the locations hidden inside every use of the phrase middle class, separate the structure story from the organisation story, and report class effects as shifted probabilities among named groups rather than as bloc behaviour. Wright's own prose is a model for all three audiences in one respect that costs nothing to imitate: he states his claims where they can be found and his evidence where a reader can verify it, and he treats his critics as collaborators in the enterprise of being less wrong.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What are contradictory class locations?

Positions within production that combine opposed class relations in a single job. A manager directs the work of others and helps appropriate the surplus, which belongs to the capitalist side of the structure, while remaining a salaried employee who can be dismissed and who owns no productive property, which belongs to the working class side. Wright argued that the contradiction is a property of the position rather than of the occupant's awareness, and that it explains why such strata display divided interests and unstable political allegiances across many settings.

### Q: Where do managers fit in Marxist class theory?

Wright's career offers two placements. His first framework locates managers in a contradictory location between owners and wage labourers, defined by control delegated from owners combined with the employee's lack of ownership. His second framework locates them among privileged employees, as holders of organisation assets whose command of coordinated labour lets them share in the appropriation of surplus. Both placements deny that managers form a separate ruling class inside capitalism, since their authority is delegated by owners and revocable by them, and both treat seniority, credentials, and real decision rights as the variables that fix any individual manager's exact position.

### Q: Why did Wright replace his first framework?

Because the contradictory locations scheme could describe positions but could not derive them. Its boundaries between locations rested on judgements about how much control or autonomy a job contains, and different judgements produced different maps of the same workforce. Wright wanted a principle from which the map could be generated, and the reformulation of exploitation through asset distribution, associated with Roemer, supplied one: specify who holds which productive assets and the class locations follow. He also judged that an asset based scheme could handle societies without private ownership, where the first framework's reliance on the capital relation left it describing bureaucracies only by analogy.

### Q: What are organisation assets?

The resources embodied in the organised structure of production itself: the division of labour, the chains of command, the information and scheduling systems, and the decision rights through which many people's work is coordinated into a single process. Whoever controls those resources controls the labour of everyone inside the structure, and Wright argued that this control supports exploitation in its own right, since coordination produces a surplus beyond what the same workers would produce separately and the controllers of coordination appropriate part of that surplus. Senior managers are the characteristic holders, and a bureaucracy monopolising an entire economy's organisation assets is the limiting case the concept was built to describe.

### Q: What is the criticism of the assets framework?

The standard objection, pressed by Crompton and others, is that the framework changes the meaning of class. Marxist theory defined class by relations between people in production, by domination and appropriation, whereas the asset scheme defines it by what individuals possess, credentials, authority, property, which makes class a distributional category resembling the stratification research the theory opposed. Skill assets draw the sharpest version of the charge, since a credential looks like a personal possession yielding a market rent. Wright's defenders answer that organisation assets at least are relations in condensed form, since command exists only over other people, and Wright himself conceded that the framework's language invited the reading its critics gave it.

### Q: What was the comparative class project?

The international survey programme Wright directed from the late nineteen seventies, a collaborative programme whose formal title joins class structure and class consciousness as its twin objects. Teams of researchers in the participating countries administered a common questionnaire that measured respondents' ownership, authority, credentials, and autonomy at work, together with their attitudes and political behaviour, allowing class locations to be constructed by common rules and compared across societies. Its results appeared most fully in Class Counts in nineteen ninety seven. The datasets the project generated have been archived and continue to serve teaching and secondary analysis, making the programme the most extensive empirical enterprise in its field and the chief source of evidence about whether Wright's locations pattern real interests.

### Q: Did Wright change his position later?

Yes, by development rather than by renunciation. The theorist who had twice sought one definitive map of class came, by the two thousands, to argue that Marxist, Weberian, and gradational approaches answer different questions: exploitation and domination, opportunity and market capacity, and the sorting of life chances respectively. His mature work therefore treats his own schema as the right instrument for the first question rather than as a complete theory of inequality, and adds the mechanisms of gender and of race, together with citizenship and the organisation of households, as forces interacting with class without reducing to it. He continued to defend exploitation as a real relation and his locations as its best available measure until his final writings.

### Q: Does the class schema work empirically?

Partly, and the parts are specifiable. Locations built by the scheme predict income strongly across many countries, with credentials and authority each contributing separately, as the asset interpretation requires. They predict attitudes, union behaviour, and political dispositions really but weakly, with broad dispersion inside every location and strong modification by gender, sector, and national institutions. Several fine distinctions in the matrix fail to differentiate anything measurable. Wright's own summary was that class structure constrains and biases consciousness without determining it, and that the schema succeeds as an account of material interests while requiring a separate theory of organisation and institutions to explain what people collectively do about those interests.

### Q: Is class distributional or relational?

Wright's career contains both answers, which is why the question organises his profile in this series. His first framework is relational throughout: class positions are defined by control and appropriation between people inside production. His second framework is distributional in its grammar, defining positions by holdings of assets, though its defenders argue the assets condense relations of command and exclusion. His late position dissolves the rivalry by assigning the two descriptions to different questions, keeping the relational account for exploitation and the distributional account for the sorting of advantages. A careful answer therefore names the framework and the question before choosing a side, which is the habit his work exists to teach.

### Q: Where should you start with Wright?

Start with Classes from nineteen eighty five for the theory in its most rigorous form, since the book dismantles his first framework, builds the asset framework, and applies it to capitalist and statist societies in one argument. Follow it with Class Counts from nineteen ninety seven for the evidence, the comparative survey results that confirmed parts of the theory and complicated the rest. Read Class Crisis and the State from nineteen seventy eight afterward, to recover the contradictory locations framework in its original polemical strength, and finish with Understanding Class from twenty fifteen for the late synthesis, where each rival approach is given the question it answers best and the whole career becomes intelligible as one trajectory.

### Q: How do the two incompatible solutions grade the class framework's development?

They grade it as a trade rather than as a march. The first solution scores highest on fidelity to the tradition's relational core and lowest on derivability and measurement; the second reverses the scores, generating its map from a principle and fitting survey instruments elegantly while exposing its foundation to the distributional charge. Judged together, the pair show a research programme learning the price of each virtue by purchasing it, and they set the standard any successor scheme must meet on both axes at once: derivation without desertion of relations, measurement without loss of the production process. No later scheme in the literature meets that double standard completely, which is the precise sense in which Wright's development remains unfinished business rather than a closed chapter.

### Q: What should a student separate between a relational account of class and a distributional one?

Separate the direction of explanation. In a relational account, positions come first and possessions follow: because a manager stands in a relation of command within production, she receives the income, security, and deference that attach to commanding. In a distributional account, possessions come first and positions follow: because an expert holds a scarce credential, she occupies a privileged place in the structure. The separation matters because the two accounts predict different changes. Abolish the command relation and the relational account expects the manager's privilege to collapse with it; merely redistribute incomes and the distributional account expects the structure to regenerate its pattern from the holdings left untouched. Examination answers that keep the directions distinct can compare Wright's frameworks instead of confusing them.

### Q: Why did the second framework replace relations with assets?

For the sake of a generator. Relations of control inside production are real but resistant: they vary by degree, resist common measurement across workplaces and countries, and offer no principle from which a complete map of locations can be deduced. Assets promised all three deficiencies remedied at once. An unequal distribution of productive resources could be specified exactly, the locations derived from it mechanically, and the holdings measured by survey questions about ownership, credentials, and decision rights that travel across contexts. Wright judged the gain in rigour and portability worth the conceptual exposure the move created, and the subsequent criticism, which charged the framework with abandoning relations altogether, was the price arriving as invoiced. His later work reads, in part, as an effort to recover the relational content inside the asset language.

### Q: How does the two frameworks compared table order the concepts?

The table in this article sets the two schemes side by side under five rows, with a third column given to the position of his later years. The rows ask, in sequence, what problem each scheme addresses, how each accounts for managers, on what basis each assigns class position, what each explains best, and which criticism each principally attracts. Reading down a column reconstructs one phase of Wright's thought as a coherent whole. Reading across a row isolates one issue where the phases part company, which is the faster route to the article's central claim: that the first two columns are incompatible solutions to one problem, and that the third column records their author's eventual verdict on the quarrel between them.

### Q: What separates a contradictory location from a middle class?

The criterion of membership. A middle class, in ordinary and gradational usage, is a band in a distribution, the people between the prosperous and the poor by income, wealth, or status, and its boundaries move with the statistician's choice of cut points. A contradictory location is a position defined by the relations it combines, so belonging is fixed by the structure of jobs: whoever directs others' labour while selling their own and owning nothing belongs to the location, whatever their salary, and whoever owns productive property does not, however modest their income. The two notions can overlap in membership while differing completely in logic, and confusing them converts Wright's structural argument into a claim about earnings that he never made and his data never tested.
