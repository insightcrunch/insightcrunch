Jon Elster arrived inside Marxism carrying a stopwatch and a demand for receipts. A Norwegian philosopher and social scientist who took his doctorate in Paris, Elster joined the small circle of scholars who founded analytical Marxism at the end of the 1970s, and he joined it as a believer in the project's central wager: that Marx's social theory contained arguments strong enough to survive the most demanding standards of clarity that philosophy and social science could apply. He set out to reconstruct that theory rigorously, thesis by thesis, using the tools of rational choice and game theory, and he published the result in 1985 as Making Sense of Marx. The verdict he reached was mixed, and its mixed character is the first thing to grasp about him, because he stated it explicitly rather than leaving readers to infer it. Some of Marx's central claims, he concluded, fail outright. Others survive, in reconstructed form, as permanent contributions to social theory. Elster is therefore misdescribed by the label that follows him through summaries and syllabuses, the Marxist who stopped being one, as though the story were a conversion in reverse. The story is an audit. He examined the estate, itemised what was sound and what was rotten, published the inventory, and then spent a career building the alternative method that the audit had convinced him social science needed.

The scorecard matters because both camps misreport it. Sympathetic summaries soften the failures, treating his criticisms as friendly amendments offered from inside a shared project. Hostile summaries inflate them, treating the book as a demolition after which nothing of Marx remains standing. Elster's own text supports neither reading. His verdicts condemned the labour theory of value, the doctrine of a falling profit rate, and dialectical materialism alike, and he said so in plain sentences that leave no room for rescue by interpretation. He also judged that the theory of alienation survives, that the account of innovation under capitalism survives, that a theory of exploitation survives once it is rebuilt on foundations that owe nothing to labour values, and that the analysis of how class position issues in political action survives. A verdict that kills the value theory and keeps alienation is not a demolition, and a verdict that keeps exploitation while discarding its traditional foundation is not a defence. It is a partition, and partitions are harder to sloganise than either loyalty or apostasy, which is one reason the partition keeps being flattened into one or the other.

The second thing to grasp is that the alternative Elster proposed travelled much further than the verdict did. His constructive programme, explanation by mechanisms rather than by laws, holds that social science should identify recurrent causal patterns at the level of individual action instead of hunting for covering laws of history or society. That programme became a standard position in the philosophy of social science. It underlies analytical sociology, a research movement whose practitioners build mechanism based explanations of segregation, diffusion, network effects and collective behaviour, most of whom have no connection to Marxism and many of whom encountered the programme without encountering its origin in an argument about Marx. Ideas that escape their birthplace in this way are rare in any tradition. Within Marxism, the mechanisms programme is the clearest case in this cluster of a contribution whose influence outside the school exceeds its influence inside it, and any accurate profile of Elster has to hold the two facts together: the internal critic who partitioned Marx, and the methodologist whose alternative was adopted by people who never asked what he thought about Marx at all.

## Elster and the founding wager of analytical Marxism

The group Elster joined had formed around a shared dissatisfaction. Its members, philosophers, economists and historians who began meeting in the closing years of the 1970s and acquired the nickname of the September Group from their meeting month, believed that Marxist theory had been protected from its own weaknesses by a style of writing that made claims hard to test. Concepts did heavy work without being defined. Explanations invoked the needs of systems, the logic of capital, the movement of history, in ways that sounded causal while specifying no process by which the alleged cause produced the alleged effect. The founding wager was that this protection was a disservice to Marx himself. If the theory contained truths, those truths would survive translation into a vocabulary where every claim could be asked what it meant and how it was supposed to work. If parts of the theory could not survive that translation, the honest response was to discard those parts rather than to keep them warm inside obscurity.

Elster was the group's methodologist, the member most concerned with the question of what an explanation is. Others in the circle rebuilt particular doctrines: the theory of history, the theory of exploitation, the analysis of class. Elster's assignment, self appointed and pursued across several books, was the machinery underneath all of them. What entitles a social scientist to say that one phenomenon explains another? When is an appeal to the function something serves a genuine explanation, and when is it a story that borrows the grammar of causation without its substance? What level of reality must an explanation reach before it can be considered complete? These questions sound abstract, and in lesser hands they stay abstract. In Elster's hands they became instruments for sorting Marx's claims, because Marx's theory is full of explanations that attribute outcomes to the requirements of a system: institutions explained by their benefit to capital, beliefs explained by their service to a ruling class, crises explained by tendencies working themselves out through history. The sorting instrument and the material were made for each other, which is why Making Sense of Marx remains the group's most consequential act of internal criticism.

His formation helps explain the instrument. Trained in philosophy in Oslo and Paris, he absorbed two influences that rarely combine. From the French tradition he took an interest in the logic of action and in the writers, Tocqueville above all, who explained large outcomes through the unintended aggregation of individual choices. From analytic philosophy he took the discipline of the counterexample and the demand that a claim state its scope. Rational choice theory and game theory supplied the formal apparatus, the models of strategic interaction in which each actor's best move depends on what others are expected to do. None of this made him an economist manqué or a philosopher dabbling in society. It made him a specialist in the micro level of social explanation, in the question of how anything at the level of classes, states and systems could be produced by beings who act on beliefs and desires, often mistaken ones, inside situations they did not choose.

### What did Elster set out to do in Making Sense of Marx?

He set out to rebuild Marx's social theory claim by claim under the standards of analytic philosophy and modern social science, keeping whatever survived rigorous restatement and discarding whatever depended on vagueness for its plausibility. The book examines value, exploitation, class, ideology, the state and historical change, and issues a separate verdict on each.

The book's architecture enacts its method. Rather than narrating Marx's development or expounding a system, it takes up one doctrine at a time and subjects it to the same sequence of operations. The doctrine is stated in its strongest defensible form. Its key terms are defined tightly enough that evidence could bear on them. The argument offered for it is reconstructed, gaps are marked, and the doctrine is then graded: sound as stated, sound after reconstruction, or unsound in ways no reconstruction repairs. Readers accustomed to commentary that moves by association and allusion found the procedure bracing or brutal depending on their prior commitments. What nobody could honestly say was that the grader had rigged the examination. The same standards that killed the labour theory of value were the standards that certified alienation, and the certification is as much a part of the book's meaning as the failures.

## The methodological commitment stated plainly

Underneath the sorting sits a doctrine about what social reality is made of, and Elster stated it without hedging. His commitment, in the formulation the brief for this article quotes in fragment, is that social phenomena are explicable in terms that involve "only individuals, their properties, goals, beliefs, and actions". This is methodological individualism: the thesis that complete explanations of social phenomena must descend to the level of individual action, showing how the beliefs, desires and opportunities of situated persons generate the outcome to be explained. Explanations may begin at the level of institutions or classes, and for many purposes they may end there provisionally. What they may not do, on this doctrine, is treat the higher level entity as an agent with purposes of its own, unless the purposes are cashed out in the actions of the individuals who compose it.

The doctrine sounds modest, almost like bookkeeping, until its edge is felt. It rules out a family of explanations that Marxist writing had used for a century. Capital does not accumulate because accumulation is its nature, unless the sentence is shorthand for the decisions of owners and managers under competitive pressure. The state does not act to secure the conditions of accumulation, unless officials, legislators and voters can be shown doing things that produce that result. A class does not recognise its interests, unless a process exists by which position shapes belief and belief issues in organisation. In each case the individualist demand is the same: produce the transmission belt. Where a transmission belt exists, the higher level claim is vindicated and often strengthened, because it gains a mechanism. Where none exists, the claim is an incantation, however often it has been repeated and however satisfying its cadence.

Elster was careful, in his careful periods, about what the doctrine does not claim. It does not claim that individuals are atoms formed outside society, a point his critics pressed and to which a later section returns at full strength. It does not claim that every explanation must actually be carried down to individuals before it counts as knowledge; science routinely and properly works with aggregate regularities. The claim concerns completeness in principle: a social explanation is fully satisfactory only when the individual level story could in principle be supplied, and a theory that forbids the descent, or treats the request for it as a misunderstanding, has exempted itself from the discipline that separates explanation from evocation. Holding the doctrine in this form matters for everything that follows, because the strongest objection to it targets the stronger form, and the article's verdict on compatibility turns on which form his later work actually requires.

### What does methodological individualism actually demand?

It demands that explanations of social phenomena be completable at the level of individuals: their beliefs, desires, opportunities and actions, and the ways those aggregate into outcomes nobody intended. Institutions and classes may appear in explanations, but never as purposeful agents in their own right unless the purposes are traced to the people composing them.

There is a historical irony in the doctrine's presence inside Marxism, and Elster was aware of it. Marx himself, in his historical writing, is a magnificent practitioner of individual level explanation. The Eighteenth Brumaire traces a coup through the calculations, illusions and organisational failures of named actors and identifiable strata; the class analysis works because the transmission belts are on the page. The methodological individualist can therefore present the demand not as an import from liberal social science but as a standard drawn from Marx's own best practice, turned against the habits of his epigones. This framing recurs in the school's self understanding and explains part of its confidence. The group did not think of itself as correcting Marx from outside. It thought of itself as recalling Marxism to the evidentiary discipline of its founder's concrete work, and discarding the scholastic machinery that had accumulated in the founder's name.

## The critique of functional explanation

The doctrine's sharpest application, and Elster's most consequential contribution to the internal debate, is the critique of functional explanation. A functional explanation accounts for a phenomenon by pointing to its beneficial consequences for some system or group: the education system exists in its present form because it supplies capital with disciplined labour; a legal doctrine persists because it protects property relations; a religious belief spreads because it reconciles the subordinated to their subordination. Such explanations have a distinctive logical shape. They cite an effect as the reason for its own cause, and they are legitimate in exactly one well understood case, biology, where natural selection supplies the loop that connects effect back to cause: the trait benefits the organism, the benefit raises reproductive success, and reproductive success explains why the trait is there.

In social science, Elster argued, the loop is usually missing. For a functional explanation to be more than a just so story, there must be a feedback mechanism by which the beneficial consequence maintains the phenomenon that produces it. Sometimes such loops exist and can be specified: firms that fail to adopt a profitable technique are eliminated by competition, so the technique's benefit to firms does explain its prevalence, with market selection playing the role that natural selection plays in biology. Sometimes the loop runs through intentional action, where beneficiaries notice the benefit and act to preserve its source. But in the great run of functionalist claims in Marxist theory, no loop is specified at all. The benefit is simply asserted to explain the existence, as though consequences could reach backward and arrange their own preconditions. His verdict was that such claims are not false, exactly; they are unfinished, promissory notes against a mechanism that the theorist has not supplied, and a science that accepts promissory notes as payment will inflate its currency until the notes are worthless.

This critique had a named target inside the school, and the exchange between Elster and G. A. Cohen is the cluster's central methodological drama. Cohen had defended functional explanation as a legitimate form, arguing that consequence laws can be known to hold before their mechanisms are elaborated, as Darwin knew that traits were adaptive before genetics explained how inheritance worked. The full defence belongs to the companion article on [Cohen and functional explanation](/2020/01/15/ga-cohen-functional-explanation/), and it is stated there at the strength Cohen gave it. Elster's reply pressed the disanalogy: Darwinian theory contained, from the start, a schematic loop in the form of differential reproduction, whereas the social functionalisms under attack contained no schematic loop at all, only the benefit and the assertion. The exchange clarified both positions and never fully closed, and it remains the standard classroom illustration of the question what a social explanation owes its reader.

### Why did Elster reject functional explanation?

Because it explains a phenomenon by its beneficial consequences without supplying the feedback loop through which those consequences could maintain their own cause. In biology, selection provides the loop. In social life the loop must be specified case by case, through competition, imitation or deliberate preservation, and where no loop is specified the explanation is unfinished.

The critique generalises beyond functionalism, and its general form is the demand this article keeps meeting. Structural explanation, in which outcomes are attributed to positions within a system of relations, faces the same question in a different costume: through what actions, by whom, do the positions produce the outcome? Teleological explanation, in which history moves toward a destination, faces it in its most exposed form. Elster's point was never that systems, structures and long run tendencies are fictions. It was that every legitimate use of such notions is an abbreviation, and abbreviations are debts. A theory may run up debts while it is young and exploring. A mature theory pays them, by producing the mechanisms in which its abbreviations are redeemed. Marxism, on his diagnosis, had been rolling its debts over for a century, and the interest had begun to consume the capital.


## The stated scorecard: the verdict itemised

Every profile of a critic faces a choice between characterising an overall stance and reproducing an itemised verdict. With Elster the choice is forced, because he itemised. Making Sense of Marx does not conclude that Marx was right or that Marx was wrong; it concludes, doctrine by doctrine, and the doctrines land on both sides of the line. The stated scorecard, the namable claim at the centre of this article, is that he published the itemisation himself, in plain language, and that the persistent misreporting of it in both directions tells us more about how traditions and their opponents handle criticism than about his argument. A tradition that can only hear internal criticism as loyalty or betrayal will flatten any partition into one of those, and the flattening is visible in the secondary literature on Elster, where the same book is cited as proof that Marxism collapsed under scrutiny and as proof that its core survived the toughest examination available.

The verdicts are his. The reasons are his, compressed. Where a verdict of his has been widely shared or widely resisted, the table says so, because a scorecard that concealed the state of play after each verdict would repeat the sin of the summaries it exists to correct.

Reading the table vertically teaches the first lesson. The failures cluster in the theory's would be scientific core, the doctrines that claimed the status of laws: value, profit rate, dialectics. The survivals cluster in the theory's diagnostic and normative anatomy: what capitalism does to work, why it innovates, wherein its central injustice consists, how position becomes politics. That distribution is itself a finding about Marx, independent of Elster's method. The parts of the theory that aspired to the condition of physics failed the examination. The parts that worked as anatomy, as an account of structures of experience and incentive, passed it. Readers who take one lesson from this article should take that distribution, because it predicts, better than any slogan, which parts of Marx a careful critic of any school tends to keep and which parts tend to be quietly set down.

### What did Elster conclude survives in Marx?

the theory of alienation, the account of technical change under capitalism, a reconstructed theory of exploitation not resting on labour values, and the analysis of class and political action. He judged the labour theory of value, the falling rate of profit, and dialectical materialism to have failed.

The sections that follow take the major items in turn, failures first, because the failures define the standards by which the survivals were certified. In each case the pattern of this article is the pattern Elster himself modelled: the doctrine is stated at its strongest, his objection is given in the form he gave it, the reply available to defenders is stated fairly, and a verdict is reached that names what would change it. That last element, the defeater attached to each verdict, is the discipline this cluster of the series has adopted from the critique articles that precede it, and it is applied to Elster's own claims as strictly as to Marx's. A scorecard that graded everyone except its author would be propaganda wearing the costume of method.

## The failures: value, profit and dialectics

The labour theory of value held that commodities exchange in proportion to the labour time socially necessary for their production, and that profit arises because labour power, the one commodity workers sell, produces more value than its own cost. Elster's examination attacked the theory at its joints. The reduction of skilled to unskilled labour, required to state values in a single unit, could not be performed without circularity. The transformation of values into prices of production, required to connect the theory to observable magnitudes, generated the long controversy in which the theory's defenders and critics disputed whether the two aggregate equalities could hold together. Beneath the technical disputes sat a simpler objection that Elster pressed with characteristic directness: the theory was unnecessary. Everything the labour theory was invoked to explain about prices, profits and distribution could be explained by theories that never mentioned embodied labour, and a magnitude that explains nothing uniquely earns no place in the explanation. Exploitation, the theory's moral payload, he treated separately, and its fate is considered with the survivals below.

### What did the labour theory of value fail to do, in his judgement?

It failed to earn its place. Its derivations required reductions it could not perform without circularity, its connection to observable prices ran through a transformation that generations of work left disputed, and every phenomenon it explained could be explained without it. A magnitude that does no unique explanatory work is, on his standard, surplus machinery.

The theory of the falling rate of profit claimed that capitalist development itself drives profitability down, because competition forces firms to substitute machinery for labour, raising the mass of capital relative to the workforce from which surplus is drawn. Elster's objection was that the claim, as a tendency of the system, is not established by the mechanism offered for it. Technical change does not have a single direction of effect on profitability. Innovation that cheapens the elements of constant capital works against the fall; innovation adopted by a firm is adopted because it raises that firm's profit, and the aggregate outcome depends on the pattern of diffusion and on what happens to the value of the capital stock itself. A theorem associated with Michio Okishio, much discussed in the literature Elster surveyed, showed that under specified conditions viable technical change cannot lower the equilibrium rate of profit with a given real wage. The Marxist replies, involving rising real wages, fixed capital and the distinction between the value rate and the price rate, complicate the picture without restoring the original lawlike claim. What survives the examination is a possibility, that profitability can come under pressure from several directions, dressed in the texts as a necessity. Possibilities do not ground a theory of inevitable crisis, and the distance between the dressed claim and the defensible one is the measure of the failure.

### What happens to the falling rate of profit on his reading?

It is demoted from a law to a possibility. The mechanism offered for it does not fix the direction of the effect, since technical change also cheapens capital goods and is adopted only when profitable, so profitability may rise, fall or hold depending on conditions the theory does not specify in advance.

Dialectical materialism failed a different test. The doctrine that reality develops through contradictions resolving into higher unities, governed by laws of the transformation of quantity into quality and the negation of the negation, struck Elster as a vocabulary in search of a content. Where the dialectical laws made definite claims, the claims were false or unsupported; where they were protected against falsification by reinterpretation, they became metaphors that could be draped over any outcome whatever, and a formula compatible with every outcome explains none. His deeper objection concerned the intellectual habit the doctrine licensed. Dialectical thinking, in the hands of its practitioners, too often functioned as a permission slip for holding that a claim and its opposite were both true at a higher level, which dissolved the discipline of having to decide. Against this he set the analytic virtue he prized above most: the obligation to say which of two incompatible things one believes, and what evidence separates them. Defenders of the dialectical tradition reply that the doctrine was always a heuristic of process and interconnection rather than a set of laws, and that reading it as failed physics mistakes its genre. Elster's rejoinder, implicit throughout his work, is that a heuristic which cannot be stated without the vocabulary of law will keep being mistaken for one, including by its own adherents, and that the tradition's political history showed the mistake being made at scale.

### Why did dialectical materialism fail his test?

Because its laws divide into claims that are false where they are definite and formulas that are empty where they are protected. A pattern that can be draped over any outcome after the fact predicts none before it, and the habit of treating contradictions as engines licensed writers to hold incompatible positions without deciding between them.

Methodological collectivism, the fourth failure, is the mirror image of his own doctrine and has been treated with it. The fifth failure is the most easily missed and in some ways the most revealing. Elster denied that there is a distinctively Marxist social science, a method with its own warrants, its own criteria of validity, its own logic unavailable to outsiders. On his view there is social science, done well or badly, and Marx contributed hypotheses, concepts and questions to it, some excellent and some worthless, all answerable to the common standards. This denial cut against the self image of a tradition that had often treated its method as a possession, dialectics as a key that opened locks other keys could not. It also cut, less obviously, against the school's own branding: if the denial is right, then analytical Marxism is not a new kind of Marxism but Marxism's subject matter submitted to ordinary rigour, and its findings, favourable or not, belong to everyone. Elster accepted that consequence. The scorecard's authority depends on it, since a verdict issued under special standards, valid only for initiates, would deserve exactly the indifference that outsiders have often shown the tradition's internal disputes.


## The survivals: alienation, technical change, exploitation, class

A partition is only as credible as its favourable side is discriminating, and Elster's survivals were not participation prizes. Each surviving doctrine survived in reconstructed form, which means the reconstruction did work: it separated a defensible core from the metaphysical or economic machinery in which the core had been embedded, and it restated the core in terms that could be argued with. The four survivals deserve individual attention, because each illustrates a different way a Marxist claim can live on after its original foundations are removed.

Alienation survived because it names a harm that does not depend on the labour theory of value or on any philosophy of history. The core claim, stripped to its skeleton, is that certain organisations of work stunt the development and exercise of human capacities: the worker is separated from control over the product, from direction of the process, from the company of fellow producers as collaborators rather than competitors, and from the activity of production as a form of self expression. Elster's treatment fastened on the concept's normative structure. Judgements of alienation rest on an account of what human flourishing involves, and he pressed the tradition to state that account openly rather than smuggling it in as science. Once stated openly, as a perfectionist claim about self realisation, the theory becomes arguable in the productive sense: its claims about which capacities matter and which arrangements stunt them can be examined, compared and tested against experience. What does not survive is the presentation of alienation as a diagnosis delivered by history itself, with communism as the guaranteed cure. What survives is the anatomy of a specific deformation of working life, together with the question of which institutions produce it and which relieve it.

### What survives of alienation after reconstruction?

An anatomy and a question. The anatomy describes how work can be organised so that producers control neither product nor process and find no self expression in activity that fills their lives. The question asks which arrangements produce that deformation and which relieve it, with the underlying account of flourishing stated openly as a value claim.

Technical change is the survival that surprises readers who meet it for the first time, because it comes from the side of Marx usually classified as economic determinism. Marx's account of innovation under capitalism holds that competition compels each firm to revolutionise its methods or die, that the search concentrates on techniques which economise on the scarce and expensive factor, and that this compulsion explains the system's unmatched dynamism together with its characteristic dislocations. Elster judged this account, in its core, to be right and to be an achievement of the first rank. It explains a pattern, the relentless and directionally biased character of innovation, from the situation of the actors rather than from a spirit of progress, and it does so with a mechanism that can be examined: rivalry, cost pressure, adoption, diffusion, elimination. Notably, the explanation is one his own method certifies without strain, since the feedback loop that functional claims usually lack is present here in the form of market selection among firms. The Marx who explains why capitalists innovate is, on Elster's reading, already practising mechanism based explanation, and the survival of this item is thus evidence for the article's larger claim that the method was drawn from Marx's best work rather than imposed on it.

### Why is technical change the strongest item on the surviving side?

Because its explanation carries its own mechanism. Competition selects among firms, firms that cut costs survive and are imitated, and the bias of innovation toward economising on labour follows from the prices firms face. No appeal to historical purpose is needed, and the pattern predicted, relentless directional change, is the pattern the record shows.

Exploitation is the survival with the most surgery performed on it. Elster accepted that the phenomenon Marx pointed at, the systematic transfer bound up with the wage relation and the moral charge attaching to it, is real and important. He rejected the labour theory of value as its foundation, for the reasons already given, and he looked favourably on the reconstruction associated with John Roemer, in which exploitation is founded on the unequal distribution of assets rather than on embodied labour: a group is exploited when it would do better by withdrawing from the arrangement with its per capita share of society's resources. The companion article on [Roemer's theory of exploitation](/2020/03/01/roemer-theory-of-exploitation/) gives that reconstruction in full. What the surgery preserves is the location of the injustice, in the background distribution of property rather than in any cheating within exchange. What it abandons is the claim that labour is the unique source of value and the arithmetic built on that claim. Defenders of the older foundation objected that the reconstruction dissolves a theory of production into a theory of distribution and loses Marx's insight into the labour process. Elster's response, in substance, was that an insight into the labour process survives as sociology and as the theory of alienation, while the theory of exploitation becomes clearer, not weaker, when its foundation is a distribution that can be described and compared.

### Why does exploitation survive the death of labour values?

Because the wrong it names was never really carried by the value arithmetic. Rebuilt on the unequal ownership of assets, exploitation identifies a group that would fare better with an equal per capita share of resources, and the moral charge attaches to that background inequality rather than to any transfer measurable in embodied labour.

The fourth survival, the analysis connecting class position to political action, survived as a programme rather than as a set of established results, and Elster's grading was correspondingly careful. The claim that position within production relations shapes interests, that interests under specifiable conditions issue in organisation, and that organisation converts latent position into historical force, defines a research agenda of permanent value. It also defines an agenda full of traps, and nobody mapped the traps more precisely than Elster himself. Between position and action lie the problems of collective action: the free rider who benefits from organisation without paying its costs, the divergence between what a class would gain collectively and what each member gains individually, the role of selective incentives, norms and leadership in closing the gap. His work on collective action, using game theory to specify when rational individuals will and will not combine, turned the class and politics item from a slogan about interests into a set of examinable hypotheses. That the programme survives is therefore partly his own doing: he kept the question and supplied the instruments that keep it honest.

### How does class position connect to political action in his account?

Through a chain with examinable links. Position shapes the interests agents have, shared interests create a collective action problem rather than automatic unity, and organisation, selective incentives and norms determine whether the problem is solved. Each link can fail, and specifying where it fails in a given case is what the surviving programme consists in.

## Mechanisms: the constructive alternative

Critique was half of Elster's work; the other half was a proposal about what social science should do instead of seeking laws. The proposal, developed through the 1980s and set out most accessibly in the short book Nuts and Bolts for the Social Sciences in 1989, and later expanded in Explaining Social Behavior, is that explanation proceeds by identifying mechanisms. A mechanism, in his widely used formulation, is a frequently occurring and easily recognisable causal pattern, triggered under conditions that are often not fully known, where the pattern itself, once recognised, renders an outcome intelligible. Where a law says that whenever conditions of a specified kind obtain, an outcome of a specified kind follows, a mechanism says something more modest and more useful: here is a way things happen, a pattern of action and reaction that recurs across settings, which may or may not be triggered in the case at hand and whose operation can be traced when it is.

The contrast with laws is the heart of the matter. Social life, on Elster's view, is too context dependent for the covering law model that philosophy of science inherited from physics. For almost every proposed social law, the opposite pattern can also be found in the record: people sometimes respond to frustration with aggression and sometimes with resignation; groups sometimes polarise under discussion and sometimes converge; scarcity sometimes produces solidarity and sometimes predation. A science that insists on laws must either falsify itself weekly or protect its laws with qualifications until they are empty. A science of mechanisms can accommodate paired opposites without embarrassment, because mechanisms come in pairs: for many mechanisms there is a contrary mechanism, and the explanatory task in a concrete case is to determine which was triggered and why. The famous consequence is that explanation in social science is often retrospective in a principled way. We can explain an outcome by exhibiting the mechanism that produced it, while being unable to predict the outcome in advance, because the triggering conditions and the competition among mechanisms were not knowable beforehand. Elster regarded this not as a confession of failure but as the honest shape of the subject matter.

### What counts as a mechanism in his programme?

A recurrent causal pattern at the level of action and interaction that, once recognised in a case, makes the outcome intelligible: wishful thinking inflating hopes, dissonance pressing beliefs into line, spite overriding advantage, norms enforcing contributions. Mechanisms are finer grained than laws and weaker in claim, and they typically come in opposing pairs.

### How does a mechanism differ from a law?

A law asserts that specified conditions always produce a specified outcome, and one contrary case damages it. A mechanism asserts that a pattern recurs and can be triggered, while allowing that a contrary pattern exists and may be triggered instead. Laws aim at prediction; mechanisms aim at intelligibility, and settle for explaining outcomes after tracing which pattern operated.

The programme's ancestry inside his own career is worth tracing, because it shows the proposal growing out of the critique rather than being pasted beside it. The demand for mechanisms is the constructive form of the attack on functional explanation: where the functionalist owed a feedback loop, the mechanism theorist pays in advance, building explanations out of patterns whose operation can be followed step by step. It is also the constructive form of methodological individualism, since the canonical mechanisms operate in and between individuals: belief formation, preference change, strategic interaction, norm guided action. And it generalises the lesson of the technical change survival, the one place where Marx's own explanation had worn its mechanism on its face. A reader who grasps that the three strands are one strand has grasped the unity of Elster's work, which its bibliography, ranging across philosophy, economics, political science and the theory of rationality, can make easy to miss.

The uptake exceeded anything the school achieved elsewhere. In the philosophy of social science, the mechanisms turn became a standard position, debated and refined by a generation of methodologists with no stake in Marx. In sociology, the programme was taken up and institutionalised as analytical sociology, whose leading figures explicitly acknowledged the debt: explanations of social outcomes were to be built by specifying the mechanisms linking macro conditions to individual action and individual actions back to macro outcomes. The Swedish sociologist Peter Hedström's work is the standard reference for this development, and its intellectual charter descends from Elster's proposal. Behavioural economics absorbed a neighbouring part of the legacy, his work on the irrational and sub intentional springs of action, the patterns by which beliefs and preferences are formed and deformed. A Marxist internal critic became, by this route, a supplier of tools to disciplines that do not read Marx, which is the cluster's clearest instance of ideas travelling further outside a tradition than within it, and a standing rebuke to any account of intellectual influence that counts only citations inside the family.

## Adaptive preferences: the unmade connection

In 1983, two years before the big book on Marx, Elster published Sour Grapes, a study of sub intentional and irrational patterns in belief and desire, whose most durable contribution is the analysis of adaptive preference formation. The phenomenon is named from the fox in the fable, who, unable to reach the grapes, decides they were sour. Adaptive preference formation is the adjustment of wants to the feasible set, behind the agent's back: people cease to want what they cannot have, and the adjustment is causal rather than chosen, a shaping of desire by circumstance rather than a decision about desire. Elster was careful to distinguish the pattern from its respectable neighbours. It is not learning, since nothing is discovered about the object. It is not character planning, the deliberate cultivation of tastes one endorses. It is the unchosen contraction of wanting to the dimensions of the attainable, and its signature is that the agent experiences the contracted want as his own authentic preference.

The analysis matters in several fields at once. In welfare economics and political theory it detonates a quiet charge under preference satisfaction as a standard of justice: if the deprived adapt their wants to deprivation, then a metric that counts satisfied preferences will certify an unjust order as satisfactory precisely where its injustice has done its deepest work. The literature on adaptive preference and autonomy in political theory descends substantially from this source. In the theory of rationality it marks a boundary of the rational choice framework from inside, since the framework takes preferences as given and the phenomenon shows them being manufactured by the situation the framework treats as external. Elster pursued both implications. What he did not pursue, and what the Marxist literature did not pursue either, is the connection that this article exists to make: adaptive preference formation is a mechanism, perhaps the mechanism, that the Marxist theory of ideology has always needed and never supplied.

### What is the sour grapes pattern?

It is the adjustment of desire to possibility, in which what cannot be obtained comes to seem not worth obtaining. The fox who cannot reach the grapes concludes they are sour, and the conclusion is produced by the failure rather than by any inspection of the fruit. The wanting shrinks to fit the world, without the agent choosing the shrinkage.

Consider what the theory of ideology must explain. Its central puzzle is not that subordinated people are coerced; coercion explains compliance without any theory of belief. The puzzle is acquiescence that runs ahead of coercion, the settled sense among those an order disadvantages that the order is natural, deserved or simply the shape of things. Classical ideology theory answered with doctrines: the ruling ideas of an age are the ideas of its ruling class, disseminated through church, school and press. The answer names a source and a channel but not a mechanism in the minds of the recipients; it explains why subordinating beliefs are supplied, not why they are taken up and held as one's own. The full treatment of that theory is treated in the companion article on [the Marxist theory of ideology](/2012/02/01/marxist-theory-of-ideology-guide/), and its gaps are mapped there. Adaptive preference formation fills the specific gap. It describes a process, operating in each individual without instruction from any propagandist, by which the wants that would indict the order are quietly revised away. The worker who cannot have security ceases to want it and calls the wanting soft; the citizen who cannot affect outcomes ceases to want a say and calls the wanting naive. No censor is required. The feasible set does the work, and the resulting preferences then testify, in every survey and every election, that the order answers to what people want.

### Why do adaptive preferences matter for ideology?

Because they supply the missing mechanism of acquiescence. Ideology theory could explain why subordinating beliefs are produced and distributed, but not why the subordinated hold them as their own. Preference adaptation explains the uptake from inside the individual: wants contract to the feasible, and the contracted wants then certify the arrangement that contracted them.

The connection runs in the other direction as well, and Elster's framework gains from it. Adaptive preference formation, as he analysed it, is a general pattern of mind, illustrated from fables and experiments. The Marxist materials give the pattern a social distribution and a direction: adaptation is not random across a population, because feasible sets are not randomly assigned. Those whose feasible sets are narrowest adapt most, and their adaptation stabilises the very distribution that narrowed them, a feedback loop of exactly the kind his methodology prizes, in which a mechanism at the individual level maintains a structure at the social level. Ideology theory needed a mechanism and had a distribution to explain; the theory of adaptive preferences had a mechanism and lacked an account of its social patterning. Each literature held the other's missing piece for decades, in adjacent rooms of the same house, and the non citation between them is one of the quiet scandals of the period's scholarship. Making the connection requires no charity toward either side, only the observation that a fox, a feasible set and a class structure are parts of one explanation.


## The rationality project behind the Marx verdict

Making Sense of Marx can mislead a reader about its author's centre of gravity, because the book is so thoroughly about Marx. Elster's abiding subject, before and after the Marx years, is rationality: what it is to act and believe for reasons, and the systematic ways in which action and belief fall short of, or overshoot, or tie themselves in knots around that standard. The Marx book is one application of the project. Understanding the project explains both the severity of the verdicts and their limits, because the standards Elster applied to Marx were not improvised for the occasion; they were the standards he applied to everyone, including himself, in a sequence of books that began before the encounter with Marxism and continued long after it.

The early work on rationality already contains the signature moves. Ulysses and the Sirens, published in 1979, studied precommitment: the strategies by which a rational agent, foreseeing his own future irrationality or weakness, binds himself in advance, as Ulysses bound himself to the mast to hear the Sirens without steering onto the rocks. The analysis opened a field. Constitutions could be read as precommitment devices of peoples against their own predictable passions; addiction could be modelled as a war among successive selves; the theory of the state acquired a new instrument. Notice the form of the contribution, because it recurs everywhere in his work. A vivid pattern is isolated, its logic is specified tightly, and the pattern is then recognised at work across domains that had not been connected. That is the mechanism style of explanation operating before the programme had been named, and it is why the later methodological writings feel less like a manifesto than like a report on a practice already under way.

Sour Grapes extended the project below the level of deliberation, to the sub intentional processes that form beliefs and desires before deliberation begins: adaptive preferences, wishful thinking, the sour grapes pattern and its less famous opposite, in which what is imposed comes to be loved. The theory of rational choice, as standardly practised, begins with preferences and beliefs as inputs. Elster's work of this period is an inventory of the ways those inputs are manufactured by processes that are neither rational nor chosen, and it permanently complicated any attempt, Marxist or liberal, to read social outcomes directly off what people say they want. The point connects backward to ideology and forward to behavioural economics, whose catalogue of biases and framing effects covers neighbouring ground, usually without the philosophical precision about autonomy that Elster brought to it.

The later work widened the inventory. Studies of emotion examined the visceral factors that steer action on timescales too fast for deliberation, and the interplay between emotion and norm: shame and guilt as enforcers, contempt and indignation as triggers of costly punishment. Work on social norms argued that norms are not reducible to rational calculation or to internalised preferences, but operate through emotional sanctions that give them their peculiar motivational force, a conclusion that put productive pressure on his own earlier rationalism. Studies of collective decision making, of bargaining and of constitution making applied the apparatus to assemblies and conventions. Across all of it the pattern of mind is constant: take a phenomenon that grand theory handles with a wave, descend to the level where people actually deliberate, misperceive, bind themselves and adapt, and rebuild the explanation from the patterns found there. The Marx verdict was delivered by that mind, at the moment when its instruments were sharpest, and the verdict's authority and its blind spots both derive from the instruments.

## Game theory and the collective action problem

The instrument Elster wielded most distinctively inside Marxist debates was game theory, and its most important application was to the problem of collective action. The problem had been given its modern form by Mancur Olson: a large group with a common interest does not automatically act on it, because each member reasons that his own contribution is negligible to the outcome while its cost to him is real, so each has an incentive to free ride on the efforts of others. Applied to class, the problem is corrosive. The classical Marxist expectation that position generates organisation, and organisation generates action, runs through a step at which individually rational workers decline to organise. Class consciousness, on this analysis, is not a fluid that rises with exploitation; it is an achievement that has to be produced against the grain of individual incentives, by selective benefits, by norms of solidarity enforced through esteem and shame, by entrepreneurs of organisation who bear start up costs, or by the transformation of the game itself through repeated interaction and small group structures.

Elster's treatment of this material in the Marx book and in his writings on Marxism and on collective action did two things at once. It explained, with a rigour the tradition had lacked, why the historical record of class action is so uneven: why some workforces organise and others, identically placed in the relations of production, do not; why organisation, once achieved, is so often oligarchic or fragile; why revolutionary moments depend on cascades and thresholds rather than on the steady accumulation of grievance. And it relocated the explanatory action. What does the work in successful cases is not position itself but the mechanisms that mediate between position and action, and those mechanisms are the same kinds of things, norms, incentives, networks, emotions, that explain collective action in churches, armies, professions and social movements of every description. Class politics becomes a branch of the general theory of collective action, illuminated by it and contributing cases to it. That relocation is the fifth failure, the denial of a distinctively Marxist social science, operating at the level of a concrete research problem, and it shows why the denial was not a debating point but the working principle of his best work.

Critics of the application replied that the game theoretic framing imports its conclusions in its setup. Model workers as isolated calculators facing a one shot dilemma, and free riding follows by construction; but actual workers are embedded in communities, repeated dealings and moral economies in which the payoffs are not the payoffs of the stripped model, and solidarity is a preference people hold rather than a puzzle to be explained away. Elster's mature response conceded the premise and denied that it saved the classical expectation. Enrich the model with norms and repeated play, and cooperation becomes explicable, but explicable by mechanisms whose presence varies from case to case, which is exactly the point: position alone does not determine the outcome, and any theory that proceeds as though it does will be surprised by history at regular intervals. The exchange is a model of how his criticism worked at its best, absorbing the objection into a more specified theory rather than treating it as a refutation to be repelled.

## The exchange with Cohen in depth

Because the functional explanation dispute is treated fully in the companion article, this profile confines itself to what the exchange reveals about Elster. Cohen's defence had rested on an analogy with pre genetic Darwinism: a science may know that a consequence law holds, that traits are present because of what they do, before it can say how the consequence maintains the trait. Elster's counterattack had two prongs. The first denied the analogy's premise in the social cases at issue: Darwin possessed not merely a benefit and an assertion but the outline of a selection process, differential survival and reproduction, which is a mechanism in schematic form, whereas the Marxist functionalisms under discussion possessed no schematic loop whatever. The second prong was about the sociology of knowledge. Functional explanation, he argued, is not merely sometimes incomplete; it is seductive, because it flatters the theorist's desire to find sense in history, to read institutions as answers to needs, and a seductive form requires stricter policing than an innocent one. A generation of Marxist writing had found, for every persistent institution, the need it served, and the findings had the uniform plausibility of a key that opens every lock because it is made of wax.

The exchange never produced a surrender, and the literature has tended to score it as an honourable draw with Elster ahead on points: few methodologists defend consequence explanation without a mechanism clause, and Cohen himself, in later reflections, conceded ground about the importance of elaborations while maintaining that functional claims could be warranted in advance of them. For this profile the salient point is different. The dispute shows Elster's criticism operating at its most characteristic: not as ideology spotting or as political distancing, but as an argument about the logic of a single explanatory form, pursued with an opponent he respected, inside a shared project, with both parties accepting that the argument, not the allegiance, would decide. Readers who want the portrait of Elster as apostate have to explain away the fact that his most famous fight was a seminar room fight about feedback loops, conducted against the school's most distinguished defender on grounds the school's own standards supplied. The fuller record is in the article on [analytical Marxism and its complete guide](/2020/01/01/analytical-marxism-complete-guide/), which maps the school within which both men worked.

### Who pressed the objection from inside the school?

The sharpest internal resistance to a strict individualism came from within analytical Marxism itself, in the joint work of Andrew Levine, Elliott Sober and Erik Olin Wright, who argued that the method as stated excluded legitimate structural explanation and misdescribed how the school's own best results were obtained. Their intervention kept the debate a family argument rather than a siege.

## Did Elster remain a Marxist?

The question is asked of him more than of any other member of the school, and it deserves a precise answer rather than a label. During the years of the group's founding and of Making Sense of Marx, he worked inside the project and described the work as a reconstruction of Marx, which presupposes that something in Marx was worth the labour of reconstruction. The scorecard's favourable side is not the output of an enemy; enemies do not usually certify alienation, technical change and exploitation in the same volume. As the decades passed, his research moved to topics, rationality, emotions, norms, constitution making, transitional justice, in which Marx figures marginally or not at all, and his distance from any Marxist identity grew correspondingly. In later reflections on the school he wrote about it in the past tense, as an episode in his intellectual life whose questions had been absorbed into his general work on explanation.

Two errors about this trajectory are worth avoiding, because both appear in print regularly. The first is the error of the sudden break: there was no single moment of repudiation, no recantation essay, no dramatic exit, and the image of a conversion narrative, with the Marx book as its crisis, is a journalistic imposition on a gradual drift of research interests and judgements. The second error is the opposite one, of supposing that because there was no break, the criticism was somehow not serious, that a man who kept certifying parts of Marx cannot really have rejected the value theory, the profit rate law and dialectics. Both errors spring from the same assumption, that intellectual positions are memberships, held or resigned. Elster's career is a standing refutation of the assumption. He held doctrines, itemised, each on its evidence, and let the membership question fall where it fell, which is why the question of whether he remained a Marxist has no answer at the level at which it is usually asked, and a complete answer at the level of the scorecard: these theses he kept, those he discarded, and the keeping and discarding were both done in public with reasons attached.

### How did his relation to the tradition change across his career?

It changed by migration rather than by rupture. He began inside the founding seminar as a reconstructor of Marx, issued his partition of the inheritance in 1985, and then let his research agenda travel toward rationality, norms and institutional design, treating the school in retrospect as a finished chapter of his intellectual life.

He never staged a departure, and the question dissolves under his own method. He worked inside the reconstruction project in the years of the school's founding, kept the theses the scorecard certifies, discarded those it condemns, and let his research migrate to rationality, norms and decision making, treating the school in later reflections as a completed episode.

For the series' purposes the biographical question matters less than the methodological residue. Whether or not its author counts as a Marxist, the scorecard is a Marxist event: the most systematic grading of Marx ever performed by a scholar who began inside the tradition's own revival, using standards the tradition's founder, in his concrete historical work, largely honoured. Traditions are graded by outsiders constantly and ignore the grades. A grade delivered from inside, by a member of the founding seminar, with the working shown, cannot be ignored in the same way, and the subsequent history of Marxist theory, in which value theory retreated, mechanism talk spread, and the defence of exploitation migrated toward distributive foundations, suggests that it was not.


## The strongest objection to methodological individualism

Fairness to Elster requires fairness against him, and his position attracted an objection strong enough that no profile is honest without stating it at full strength. The objection, pressed in various forms by structuralist and critical realist critics and, in the form closest to home, by Levine, Sober and Wright from inside the school, runs as follows. Methodological individualism instructs the explainer to begin from individuals with their properties, goals, beliefs and preferences, and to build social outcomes out of their actions. But the individuals who arrive at the starting line are not natural givens. Their properties include languages they did not invent, skills priced by markets they did not make, and statuses assigned by institutions older than themselves. Their goals and preferences, as Elster's own work on adaptive preferences demonstrates more vividly than any critic could wish, are formed by the social relations within which they live. If the starting points of the explanation are themselves products of the structures the explanation is forbidden to invoke as agents, then the method does not merely prefer one level of description. It rules out the deepest Marxist explanandum by construction: not how individuals act within relations, but how relations constitute the individuals who act. A method that cannot state the theory's central claim has not refuted the claim; it has declined to hear it, and declined in a way that disguises the refusal as rigour.

The objection gains force from an asymmetry its proponents are quick to note. The individualist demands that structural claims be cashed out in individual actions before they count as explained, but does not demand, with equal strictness, that individual level claims be cashed out in the social formation of the individuals before they count as starting points. Explanation has to stop somewhere, the objector grants; the charge is that the individualist stops it exactly where his favoured theory needs it stopped, one level below the structures and one level above the formation of preferences, and calls the stopping place a principle. Where the stopping is principled, the objector asks, why does the principle not apply symmetrically? If an unexplained structure is an incantation, why is an unexplained preference a foundation?

### What is the strongest objection to methodological individualism?

That it takes as given what Marxism wants explained. Individuals arrive in its explanations with properties, beliefs and preferences already formed, yet those are formed by social relations, languages, markets and institutions. A method that begins after the formation has occurred cannot state the claim that relations constitute agents, and so excludes the explanandum by construction rather than by argument.

Elster's resources for an answer are real, and the article's verdict requires weighing them rather than reciting the objection as a knockout. The first resource is the distinction between a completeness ideal and a research ban. Methodological individualism, as he stated it, governs the form of a finished explanation; it does not forbid studying how preferences are formed, and his own career is the proof, since the analysis of preference formation is his work. The formation of an individual's preferences by her circumstances is itself, when explained, explained through mechanisms operating on and in individuals: adaptation, dissonance, imitation, sanction. The structural formation of agents, on this reply, is not a rival level of explanation but a research programme within the individualist discipline, the programme he spent Sour Grapes pursuing. The second resource is the reminder that structures, in any concrete explanation, act on outcomes only through the situations they create for individuals: a market structure explains a price movement by altering the opportunities and information of buyers and sellers. To say the structure explains without the mediation is to leave the causal story with a gap exactly where the action is.

The verdict this article reaches is the one the brief for it prescribes, and it is a split verdict. The judgement advanced here is that the mechanisms programme is compatible with structural explanation: a mechanism specification can begin from structural positions, from the situations, resources and relations in which agents find themselves, and trace the patterns by which those positions generate outcomes, without pretending that the positions themselves are uncaused. Structural facts enter as the initial conditions of mechanisms, and the mechanisms do the explaining. That compatibility would be defeated by a demonstration that a mechanism specification must in every case terminate in individual preferences rather than in structural positions, since a programme forced to terminate there would indeed exclude the structural level from explanatory standing. His own later work does not impose that requirement: mechanisms are specified from situations as often as from desires, and the analysis of norms and institutions in his mature writings begins from positions and sanctions as readily as from wants. At the same time, the concession the objection earns must be paid in full. His stated methodological formulation, the sentence about individuals, their properties, their goals, their beliefs and their actions, does assume what the objection identifies: it lists the formed individual as the explanatory bedrock without flagging the formation as itself a task. A formulation that needs its author's own other books to correct its impression is a formulation with a defect, and the defect is not verbal only, since generations of readers took the sentence as the doctrine and practised the stopping place it describes.

### What would reverse the verdict on compatibility with structural explanation?

A demonstration that specifying a mechanism must always end at individual preferences, never at structural positions, would reverse it. If mechanisms could only terminate in wants and beliefs, structural facts would be scenery rather than explanation. His later practice, which specifies mechanisms from situations, positions and sanctions as well as desires, is the evidence that no such termination is required.

### Is the method a neutral instrument or a substantive commitment?

It is a substantive commitment wearing the dress of a technique. Any rule that settles beforehand which entities may figure as agents in an explanation, and which must be broken down into the doings of persons before they count, has taken sides on how the social world is constituted, however defensible the side taken may be.

No, and the pretence that it might be is among the misreadings this article corrects. A method that declares in advance which entities may appear as agents in a finished explanation, and which must be decomposed before they count, has taken a substantive position about the furniture of the social world. The position may be defensible, and this article has defended a version of it, but it is a position, with rivals, and not a piece of laboratory equipment that any theory can borrow without consequence. Elster at his best acknowledged as much, presenting the doctrine as a claim to be argued rather than a rule of the game to be assumed. The neutrality myth arose in the secondary literature, where methodological individualism was marketed as mere rigour, the demand that explanations make sense, as though its opponents had been demanding the right to talk nonsense. Nobody in the debate defended nonsense. The dispute was always about where explanation may legitimately stop and what it may treat as bedrock, and that dispute is metaphysical in the respectable sense: an argument about what there is and how it hangs together.

## Mechanisms in practice: analytical sociology and the wider uptake

The test of a programme is what it lets researchers do, and the mechanisms programme passed that test in a way its author's Marxist work never had to. Analytical sociology, the movement that took the programme as its charter, set out to explain social outcomes, segregation patterns, diffusion curves, belief cascades, network structures, by building explicit models of the mechanisms at work: how the preferences and beliefs of individuals, situated in structures of interaction, generate aggregate patterns that no individual intended. The movement's methodological writings cite the programme's sources openly, and Elster's formulation of the mechanism idea is among its founding references. What the sociologists took was not a set of findings but a discipline: no macro to macro explanation without the intervening steps, no appeal to a structure's effects without the action based story that carries them.

The uptake among philosophers of social science ran parallel. Where the covering law model had set the terms for decades, with explanation modelled on the deduction of events from laws and initial conditions, the mechanisms literature offered an alternative that matched scientific practice in the social and biological sciences more closely. Philosophers elaborated accounts of mechanisms as entities and activities organised to produce a phenomenon, debated how mechanisms relate to causality and to intervention, and treated the social case, with its intentional agents and its paired opposite patterns, as a central exhibit rather than an embarrassment. Elster's version, with its emphasis on the individual level and on the possibility that the triggered mechanism could not have been predicted in advance, is one strand in that literature, distinguished by its psychological depth: his mechanisms are mostly patterns of mind and interaction, wishful thinking, dissonance reduction, adaptation, spite, norm enforcement, rather than the machineries of the natural science cases.

Behavioural economics absorbed the preference formation strand. The finding that preferences are not stable inputs but are shaped by framing, endowment and adaptation entered the mainstream of economic psychology, and the normative literature on adaptive preferences, in development ethics and in the theory of autonomy, works with the sour grapes analysis as a standard starting point. A policymaker who refuses to take expressed satisfaction as the last word on deprivation, because the deprived may have adapted, is using Elster's instrument whether or not the name is known. The pattern of uptake has a moral for how ideas travel. The mechanisms programme and the adaptive preference analysis were detachable: they could be lifted out of the Marxist argument in which they were forged and set to work elsewhere, because they were stated as general patterns with their logic exposed. The scorecard, by contrast, travelled badly, because it was about Marx, and the audiences who might have used it preferred their Marx pre judged. Detachability, this article suggests, is the property that decides whether a critic's tools outlive his quarrel, and Elster, who forged unusually detachable tools, is the beneficiary.

### Did the mechanisms programme travel beyond Marxism?

It travelled further outside the tradition than within it. Analytical sociology adopted it as a founding discipline, the philosophy of social science made mechanisms a standard category of explanation, and behavioural research absorbed the analysis of preference formation, all largely among scholars with no stake in the Marxist debates where the programme was first stated.

## The comparison basis: verdicts against verdicts

Where this article compares Elster's assessment of Marx with other assessments, it observes the discipline the brief prescribes: itemised verdicts are compared with itemised verdicts, thesis by thesis, and overall attitudes are not treated as evidence about particular theses. The discipline matters because the literature on Marx's fate is full of illegitimate aggregations, in which a thinker's general warmth or hostility toward Marxism is allowed to stand in for a position on, say, the theory of technical change, about which the thinker may have held the opposite of the view his attitude suggests. Elster himself is the standing refutation of aggregation: the same author, in the same book, delivered the tradition's most cited demolition of its value theory and one of its strongest certifications of its account of innovation. Any summary that assigns him a single attitude thereby falsifies both halves of the record.

Compared item by item, his scorecard aligns with the broad pattern of careful verdicts delivered from other directions, a fact that corrects the misreading of his verdict as idiosyncratic. The labour theory of value has been abandoned as a price theory by most economists who work on Marx and retained mainly as an interpretive or accounting framework by a minority tradition. The falling rate of profit survives as a contested possibility in a literature that no longer states it as a law without extensive qualification. Dialectical materialism as a doctrine of nature commands little assent outside its own tradition, while the theory of alienation, the analysis of technical change and some reconstruction of exploitation command assent well beyond it. Elster's partition, delivered in 1985 with unusual explicitness, anticipated the shape that the wider literature's verdict gradually took. That convergence is evidence about the scorecard's accuracy, and also about why it was resisted at the time: an itemised verdict delivered early, before the items have been separately settled in public, looks like provocation, whereas the same verdict delivered piecemeal by a literature over thirty years looks like consensus. The stated scorecard simply arrived all at once.


## Paired mechanisms and the shape of social explanation

The claim that mechanisms come in opposing pairs is the most philosophically loaded element of the programme, and it deserves a slower treatment than it usually receives, because it determines what kind of knowledge social science can honestly promise. Consider the patterns Elster catalogued across his work. Frustration can issue in aggression against the source of the frustration, or in resignation and the lowering of aims. A belief that threatens a person's self image can be revised, or the evidence for it can be discounted, or the person can withdraw from the settings where the belief is challenged. Discussion among the like minded can moderate a group toward its members' average view, or polarise it toward the extreme its members privately leaned toward. Scarcity can knit a group together against an outside threat, or set its members competing for the shrinking stock. In each case both patterns are real, both are documented, and the difference between them lies in triggering conditions that are incompletely understood and often invisible in advance.

A science organised around laws experiences this pairing as a scandal, since for every law it proposes, the record supplies the contrary. A science organised around mechanisms experiences the pairing as the normal texture of its subject. The explanatory question in a concrete case becomes forensic: given that this outcome occurred, which pattern produced it, and what traces does the pattern leave that distinguish it from its rival? The question can often be answered well. Process tracing, the close reconstruction of a decision or a cascade, can identify the operating mechanism from the sequence of steps, from the testimony of participants, from the signature the pattern leaves in timing and detail. What cannot be done, in the general case, is to run the inference forward: to stand before the event and deduce which mechanism will trigger. Elster drew the moral without flinching. Much of social science is explanation after the fact, and the demand that it predict as physics predicts is a demand that it falsify its subject matter. This is not a licence for storytelling. The forensic question has right and wrong answers, evidence discriminates among mechanisms, and a mechanism attribution can be refuted by showing that the pattern's signature is absent. It is a claim about where the rigour lives: in the identification, not in the prophecy.

The Marxist stake in this apparently abstract doctrine is considerable, and it explains why the programme, forged in an argument about Marx, has implications for every grand theory of history. The classical Marxist philosophy of history was a law seeking enterprise: tendencies of the rate of profit, laws of the correspondence between forces and relations of production, a sequence of modes driven by their contradictions. If the mechanisms doctrine is right, that enterprise was misconceived in form, whatever the fate of its particular claims, because the social material does not support laws of the required kind. What it supports is a stock of mechanisms, accumulation, selection, adaptation, cascade, polarisation, from which historical outcomes are assembled case by case. The theory of history becomes a toolbox rather than an engine. Elster's own verdict on historical materialism followed exactly this path: the functional core of the theory of history, in which relations of production are explained by their tendency to develop the forces, failed his feedback test in the form Cohen had given it, while the mechanism rich parts, the analyses of innovation, class formation and crisis behaviour, survived as patterns available for forensic use. The toolbox conception is the deepest sense in which the stated scorecard generalises: it is not only that some theses failed and others passed, but that the passing theses change their logical status as they pass, from laws of an engine to patterns in a box.

There is a cost to the toolbox, and honesty requires naming it. A theory of history as engine promised orientation: it told its adherents where events were tending and what their own action meant within the tendency. A toolbox promises nothing of the kind. It equips analysis and withholds prophecy, and for a tradition that was also a movement, the withholding is a genuine loss, felt as such by many who accepted the methodological argument. Elster's position, maintained consistently, was that a consolation purchased by false claims about the form of social knowledge is too expensive, and that a movement which needs prophecy should find its confidence elsewhere than in counterfeit laws. The point generalises beyond Marxism to every doctrine that has mortgaged its politics to a philosophy of history, and it is one reason the programme found readers in quarters far from the original quarrel.

## Norms, emotions and the limits of the calculator

A reader who met Elster only through the methodological slogans might picture a theorist of bloodless calculation, populating society with miniature accountants. The picture is wrong, and correcting it matters for the scorecard, because his account of what moves individuals is unusually rich in the forces that calculation excludes. Two bodies of work make the point: the theory of social norms and the theory of the emotions, both developed as he moved beyond the Marx book, both continuous with its deepest concerns.

The theory of norms begins from a refusal. Norms, Elster argued, cannot be reduced to rational calculation: people follow norms of fairness, reciprocity, contribution and revenge at cost to themselves, in one shot encounters, unobserved, where no calculus of advantage explains the compliance. Nor can norms be dissolved into preferences without remainder, since the phenomenology of norm following, the grip of ought, the sting of shame at violation and the heat of indignation at others' violations, has a structure that preference talk flattens. His proposal gave emotions the operative role: norms are sustained by the emotional sanctions that attach to them, shame for the violator, contempt and indignation directed at violators by others, and these emotions are not themselves chosen or calculated. The result is a social ontology with three motivational systems in play, interest, reason and emotion mediated norms, interacting in patterns that the mechanisms programme exists to catalogue. For the Marxist reader the relevance is direct. The classical theory needed solidarity to be more than a calculation and less than a mystery; the norms theory supplies the middle term, an account of how obligations to contribute and to punish defectors can be motivationally real for individuals without being reducible to their advantage, and of why such obligations are unevenly distributed across workplaces and communities in ways that track their histories rather than their class positions alone.

The work on emotions extends the same decentering of the calculator. Emotions, in his treatment, are visceral states with characteristic action tendencies: they narrow attention, shorten time horizons, and dispose the agent toward specific classes of act, flight, attack, appeasement, approach, in ways that can override deliberation or hijack it into rationalisation. They also interact with beliefs in both directions, being shaped by appraisals and shaping the beliefs that appraise. A social science that begins from individuals must begin from individuals as they are, which is to say from creatures whose beliefs are formed sub intentionally, whose preferences adapt to their circumstances, whose norms grip them through shame, and whose deliberations are periodically commandeered by feeling. The methodological individualism that emerges from the full body of work is therefore not the thin doctrine its slogan suggests. It is a demand that explanation pass through the actual machinery of human motivation, in all its layered imperfection, rather than through the purposes of hypostatised collectivities. Critics who attack the slogan often find, on reaching the work, that the machinery they wanted, formation, feeling, obligation, is already installed, and that their quarrel has narrowed to the question of whether structural positions may also appear as unexplained explainers, the question on which this article has already split its verdict.

## Constitution making and the later applications

The later career carried the instruments into institutional design, and the applications repay attention here for one reason: they show the mechanisms programme doing prospective work, guiding the construction of arrangements rather than only dissecting the dead. Elster's studies of constitution making examined assemblies that wrote fundamental laws under pressure, in revolutionary capitals and in transitions from authoritarian rule, and asked how the circumstances of drafting, the interests in the room, the passions of the hour, the constraints of publicity and secrecy, shaped the texts that emerged. The Ulysses theme returns at collective scale: a constitution is a device by which a political community binds its future self, and the quality of the binding depends on the motives of the binders, which are never the pure public spirit of civics textbooks. His analyses distinguished the forces at work in constituent assemblies, interest, passion and reason, and traced their signatures in procedures and outcomes: where debate was public, speakers performed for audiences and compromise grew harder; where deadlines pressed, bargaining displaced argument; where the drafters expected to live under the text as ordinary citizens rather than as rulers, the text's impartiality improved.

Transitional justice, the reckoning with the crimes of fallen regimes, received the same treatment: trials, purges, truth commissions and reparations analysed through the motivations of victims, perpetrators, bystanders and successor elites, with emotions, retribution, shame, the need for acknowledgment, treated as causal forces rather than as noise around a rational core. The relevance to a Marxism series is not that these works are Marxist; they are not, and their author had long ceased to organise his research around the tradition. The relevance is that they complete the demonstration of what the method can do when it is let loose on the largest political materials. A theory of explanation that began by refusing to let capital or the state act as unexamined agents ends by explaining how actual assemblies of situated, passionate, self interested and occasionally public spirited people produce the most consequential texts in political life. The arc from the critique of functional explanation to the analysis of constituent assemblies is a single arc, and tracing it is the surest way to see that the scorecard on Marx was one station on a line that ran much further.


## The September Group and the sociology of a school

The scorecard was not produced in isolation, and its character owes something to the institution that housed its author during the years of its making. The September Group, the informal seminar of analytically minded Marxists that began convening in the late 1970s, was an unusual intellectual device: a small company of scholars from several countries and disciplines, meeting annually, circulating drafts, and subjecting each other's work to a scrutiny that participants consistently described as the most demanding of their professional lives. Cohen's theory of history, Roemer's models of exploitation, Wright's class analysis and Elster's methodological work all passed through the seminar's fire. The group's nickname among detractors, the No Bullshit Marxism Group, captured its self image exactly: the point of the exercise was that claims would be made to state their meaning and their evidence, and that seniority and reputation purchased no exemptions.

This sociology matters for reading Elster for two reasons. The first is that his criticism of Marx was formed in an environment where criticism was the group's internal currency, offered and received among people who shared the founding wager that Marx deserved the compliment of rigour. The scorecard's tone, unsparing toward doctrines and respectful toward the intelligence that produced them, is the seminar's tone. The second reason concerns the school's subsequent fate, which the complete guide to analytical Marxism recounts in full. The group gradually dispersed as its members' interests diverged, some toward normative political philosophy, some toward empirical class analysis, some, Elster among them, outward into general social theory. The dispersal was not a collapse under refutation; it was the natural history of a successful seminar whose questions had been absorbed into the disciplines its members inhabited. But the dispersal had one consequence the school's critics and defenders both noted: the collective project of a reconstructed Marxism lost its carrier, and the reconstructions survived as individual contributions, Roemer's exploitation theory in economics, Wright's class analysis in sociology, Elster's mechanisms everywhere, rather than as a system anyone defended whole. The stated scorecard anticipated that outcome too, in its fifth verdict: if there is no distinctively Marxist social science, then a successful reconstruction will not look like a new Marxism; it will look like good work, in several disciplines, whose origins in Marx become a matter for historians of ideas.

The assessment of the school as a whole, including the criticisms pressed against it from classical Marxists who regarded the entire enterprise as a dissolution of the tradition's identity, is the subject of the companion article on [the criticisms of analytical Marxism](/2020/05/01/analytical-marxism-criticisms/), and this profile does not duplicate it. What belongs here is the narrower observation that Elster was the member in whom the school's tensions were most fully embodied. He was its strictest methodologist and the author of its most damaging internal verdicts; he was also the member whose constructive programme most completely escaped the school's boundaries, so that the question of whether the programme counts as a Marxist achievement or as an achievement that Marxism occasioned has no clean answer. The school's wager had been that rigour would vindicate Marx. In Elster's case rigour vindicated a method and partitioned Marx, and the partition and the method are both, in their different ways, the wager's children.

## The microfoundations question in his hands

Between the methodological doctrine and its applications sits a term that organises much of the subsequent literature: microfoundations. To demand microfoundations for a macro level claim is to demand the specification of how the macro conditions shape individual situations, how individuals act within those situations, and how their actions combine into the macro outcome. The demand sounds like Elster's doctrine restated, and in large part it is; the vocabulary of microfoundations is the form in which his methodological individualism entered general social science, softened into a research heuristic that scholars could accept without signing the doctrine. The general question of Marxism and microfoundations, which mechanisms the tradition's macro claims require and which they can survive without, is treated in the companion article on [Marxism and microfoundations](/2020/03/15/marxism-and-microfoundations/), and the present profile confines itself to the biographical and critical point.

The point is that the softening changed the claim. As a heuristic, microfoundations are unobjectionable to nearly everyone: of course it is good to know how a process works at the level of action. As a doctrine, methodological individualism asserted something stronger, that explanations lacking the descent are incomplete in principle, and it was the stronger assertion that did the critical work against functionalism and teleology. The literature's adoption of the heuristic while dropping the doctrine is therefore a partial uptake, and Elster's own later formulations participated in the softening: the mechanisms programme, as he presented it in his mature methodological writings, is a claim about the form good explanations take, advanced with examples and arguments rather than as an a priori ban on other forms. Whether the softening represents the doctrine maturing or the doctrine retreating is a genuine question in the interpretation of his work, and this article's compatibility verdict depends on the mature version: it is because the later programme specifies mechanisms from situations and structures, rather than insisting that every chain terminate in preferences, that the programme is reconcilable with structural explanation. Readers who meet only the 1985 formulation will judge the objection to methodological individualism more damaging than readers who meet the whole arc, and part of this profile's service is to insist that the arc, not the slogan, is the position.

## How the scorecard is misreported: a field guide

Since the stated scorecard's misreporting is this article's namable claim, the misreportings deserve enumeration in prose, with the correction attached to each. The first misreporting is the demolition reading, in which Making Sense of Marx figures as the book that showed Marx was wrong, full stop. It flourishes in polemical writing against Marxism, where the book is cited by people whose acquaintance with it is often confined to its reputation, and it requires ignoring the favourable half of the partition, alienation, technical change, exploitation reconstructed and class analysis, which the same book certifies in the same tone of voice. The second misreporting is the loyalist softening, in which Elster's verdicts are redescribed as internal refinements, the removal of outdated scaffolding from an intact building. It flourishes in sympathetic textbooks, and it requires ignoring that the value theory and the profit rate tendency were not scaffolding but load bearing members of the classical structure, whose removal changes what the building is.

The third misreporting concerns the man rather than the verdicts: the conversion narrative, in which a believer lost his faith and became a scourge. As the biographical section of this article argued, the record shows a gradual migration of interests and a consistent method, not a crisis and a reversal, and the narrative persists because it is a better story than a seminar. The fourth misreporting is subtler and does the most damage in teaching: the presentation of methodological individualism as a neutral technique, a demand for clarity that no reasonable person could refuse, which converts a substantive and contestable doctrine into a test of reasonableness that its opponents are bound to fail. Students taught the doctrine in this form never meet the objection from the formation of preferences, and so never learn what the argument is actually about. The fifth misreporting runs in the opposite direction, in which methodological individualism is equated with the political doctrine of individualism, or with a denial that structures exist, or with the claim that only selfish motives are real. Elster's texts support none of these equations: structures exist in his work as the situations that constrain and enable, motives in his work include norms, emotions and commitments of every description, and his politics, such as they appear in the work, are irrelevant to the method's logic. A scorecard article can do only one thing against this ecology of error, which is to print the itemisation accurately and often, in the confidence that partitions, repeated plainly, eventually outlast the slogans that flatten them.

## Where to begin reading Elster

The bibliography is large and interdisciplinary, and the entry point depends on what the reader wants, a dependence this article can make precise because the works divide cleanly by purpose. For the verdict on Marx, there is no substitute for Making Sense of Marx itself, read selectively: the chapters on exploitation, class and ideology carry the scorecard's centre of gravity, and the methodological chapters state the standards by which the verdicts were reached. The book is long and its arguments are dense, but its organisation by doctrine makes it possible to read the chapters corresponding to the rows of the scorecard table and to postpone the rest. For the method in its most accessible form, Nuts and Bolts, the short book of 1989, remains the standard recommendation: a compact, example filled statement of explanation by mechanisms, written for working social scientists rather than for philosophers, and still the quickest route to understanding what the programme asks of an explanation. Its later expansion, Explaining Social Behavior, restates the programme with a larger stock of mechanisms and with the author's mature qualifications, and is the right second step for readers whose interest is methodological.

For the preference work, Sour Grapes is the source: a short, strange, brilliant study of the patterns by which beliefs and desires are formed behind the agent's back, of which the adaptive preference analysis is the most consequential part. Readers arriving from the theory of ideology should read it alongside the ideology materials in this series, performing the connection the literatures failed to perform. For the rationality project in its founding form, Ulysses and the Sirens introduces precommitment and the divided self. For the mature range, the studies of norms, emotions and constitution making show the instruments at work on institutional material. What a first reader should not do is begin with the summaries, his own included in reference works, because Elster's virtues are virtues of argument in motion, of distinctions being drawn under pressure from examples, and summaries preserve the conclusions while discarding the motion that makes them credible. The scorecard in this article is offered as a map, and maps are for travellers who intend the journey.

### Which book should a first reader open first?

Start with the question you bring. For the verdict on Marx, the doctrinal chapters of Making Sense of Marx. For the method, Nuts and Bolts, short and example driven. For preferences and ideology, Sour Grapes. Read the arguments rather than summaries of them, since his conclusions are inseparable from the distinctions that produce them.


## The theory of history under the same instruments

Elster's examination of Marx ranged beyond the doctrines itemised in the scorecard table, and his treatment of the theory of history, historical materialism in its classical form, shows the instruments working on the tradition's most ambitious claim. The theory held that history is driven by the development of the productive forces, that relations of production rise and fall according to whether they further or fetter that development, and that the succession of social forms is thereby explained. Cohen had given the theory its most rigorous modern statement, resting its explanatory weight on functional claims: relations of a given kind persist because they are optimal for the development of the forces at a given level. Elster's critique applied the feedback test at the theory's foundation. For the functional claim to explain, there must be a mechanism by which relations that develop the forces are selected and relations that fetter them are discarded, and the candidates, competition among societies, conscious reform by rulers perceiving advantage, revolution from below, each face difficulties as general mechanisms: the timescales are long, the perception of optimality is unreliable, and the actors who might do the selecting often benefit from the fettering relations themselves.

The verdict was not that the theory of history is empty. The primacy of the productive forces as a long run constraint, the thought that a society's relations must be compatible with the level and trajectory of its productive capacities, survived in the weakened form of a compatibility thesis: relations grossly unsuited to the forces are unstable, though the path and timing of adjustment are open and mechanism dependent. What failed was the theory as an engine, the claim that development of the forces explains the sequence of forms with lawlike force. Once again the pattern of the scorecard repeats at a higher level: a doctrine that claimed the status of a science of history is certified as a constraint and a research programme, and the certification, from a sympathetic examiner, carries a weight that hostile dismissal never could. The episode also clarifies the relation between Elster and Cohen that the functional explanation debate began. The two men were not divided by allegiance; they were divided by a judgement about how much explanatory weight a consequence claim can bear before its mechanism is produced, and the division ran through the school's centre because the theory of history was the school's founding text.

## Explanation and prediction: the honest asymmetry

The mechanisms programme's tolerance for explanation without prediction deserves a further word, because it is the element most often misunderstood by readers trained on the physical sciences and the element with the largest consequences for how Marx's claims should be graded. The classical ambition, shared by Marxism and by its positivist critics alike, was a science of society that would do for history what mechanics did for motion: state the laws, derive the future. Elster's work implies that the ambition confuses two achievements. Mechanics predicts because its mechanisms are few, stable and isolable. Social life is produced by many mechanisms, several of them paired opposites, operating on beings who learn, adapt and react to the predictions themselves. In such a material, the attainable achievement is different: a stock of well understood patterns, a forensic capacity to identify which patterns produced a given outcome, and a disciplined humility about the future that is not ignorance but knowledge of why the future is underdetermined by the past.

Applied to Marx, the asymmetry redistributes credit and blame in a way the scorecard makes visible. Marx's predictions, of immiseration in its simple form, of the falling rate of profit as a law, of revolution in the most advanced countries, failed, and a grading scheme obsessed with prediction records the failures and stops. A mechanisms grading asks the further question: did the theory identify real patterns, patterns that recur and that explain outcomes when traced in concrete cases? By that standard the account of technical change, the analysis of class formation and its obstacles, the anatomy of alienated work, score as permanent acquisitions, because the patterns they name are found at work wherever the forensic method is applied to capitalist societies. The scorecard's partition is thus not an arbitrary splitting of the difference between defence and dismissal. It is the output of a philosophy of social science that grades theories by the reality of their mechanisms rather than by the fate of their prophecies, and readers who disagree with the partition will usually find, on inspection, that they disagree with the philosophy, which is at least the right place for the disagreement to occur.

## What the verdict means for the tradition

A profile in this series owes its readers a statement of consequences: supposing the scorecard is broadly right, what follows for the tradition it grades? Three consequences stand out. The first concerns the theory's centre of gravity. If the value theory and the crisis laws fail while alienation, technical change, exploitation on distributive foundations and class analysis survive, then Marxism's defensible core is a critical anatomy of capitalism and a normative case against its distribution of assets and its organisation of work, rather than a predictive science of capitalism's self destruction. The tradition's political self understanding changes accordingly: the case for transformation must be argued as a case, from values and evidence, and cannot be borrowed from a guaranteed future. Elster regarded that change as a gain in honesty whatever its cost in confidence, and the subsequent history of the tradition, in which its most influential representatives increasingly presented it as a normative and diagnostic enterprise, is consistent with the scorecard's pressure.

The second consequence concerns method in the tradition's ongoing work. The demand for mechanisms, once heard, cannot be unheard, and it has quietly become a standard even among scholars who reject methodological individualism as a doctrine: explanations of ideology that specify uptake, explanations of class action that specify organisation, explanations of institutional persistence that specify feedback, are simply better explanations, and the literature knows it. The third consequence concerns the tradition's relation to its critics. The stated scorecard demonstrates that the most damaging criticisms of Marx's doctrines were produced inside the tradition's own revival, by its own standards of rigour, and that the same process certified the survivals. A tradition that can grade itself in public has a resource that neither apologetics nor external polemic can supply, and the cluster of articles around this profile, Cohen's defence, Roemer's reconstruction, the microfoundations debate, the school's critics answered in the article on the companion article on the criticisms of the school, is best read as the record of that self grading extended over a generation.

## Verdict on the profile's subject

This article has advanced several judgements, and the cluster's discipline requires that each carry its defeater in the open. The judgement that the stated scorecard is accurate in its main partition would be defeated by a rehabilitation of value theory as an explanatory account of prices and profits, or by a demonstration that the falling rate of profit is a law under conditions that actually obtain; no such rehabilitation is assumed impossible, and the value theoretic literature continues to argue its case. The judgement that his mechanisms programme can coexist with structural explanation would be defeated by the demonstration, discussed in the objection section, that mechanism specification must terminate in preferences rather than positions. The judgement that adaptive preference formation is the mechanism ideology theory needed would be defeated by evidence that acquiescence is fully explained by coercion, information control or sincere belief formed on adequate evidence, leaving no residue for preference adaptation to explain; the article's claim is that such evidence does not cover the phenomenon of wants contracting to fit deprivation, which is observable wherever feasible sets are narrow and stable. The judgement that Elster's tools travelled further than his verdicts would be defeated by a citation and influence record showing the scorecard's partition at work across the disciplines that adopted the mechanisms programme; the record as it stands shows the tools adopted by scholars who do not cite the verdicts at all.

Stated together, the judgements compose the profile's answer to its One Test: the reader leaves with the scorecard accurately, knowing what its author concluded survives and what he concluded fails, knowing the alternative he built and where that alternative went, and knowing the connection, between adaptive preferences and ideology, that neither his literature nor Marx's thought to make. Elster's place in the tradition is paradoxical only if traditions are imagined as teams. He was the school's methodologist, its internal auditor, and the supplier of its most successful export. The audit stands, the export circulates, and the scorecard, printed plainly, remains the shortest accurate answer to the question of what one of Marx's most rigorous readers concluded was true in Marx.


## Ideology: the gap the preference analysis fills

The connection between adaptive preference formation and the theory of ideology is the contribution this article most wants its readers to keep, and it rewards a more granular development than the summary offered earlier. Begin with the structure of the problem as the Marxist tradition inherited it. The tradition's theory of ideology, in its classical statements, contains three distinguishable claims. The first is a claim about content: the dominant beliefs of a society systematically misrepresent social reality in ways that favour the dominant order, presenting historically specific arrangements as natural, universal or just. The second is a claim about production: those beliefs are generated and disseminated by institutions, churches, schools, presses, professions, whose position in the order gives them both the motive and the means. The third is a claim about function: the beliefs persist because they stabilise the order, reconciling the subordinated to subordination and the privileged to privilege. Elster's methodological scrutiny touches each claim differently. The content claim is empirical and often defensible case by case. The production claim is a piece of institutional sociology, testable in the ordinary way. The function claim is where his critique bites, because persistence explained by stabilising effect is functional explanation, and the feedback loop is owed.

What loop could connect the stabilising effect of a belief back to the belief's persistence? The tradition's implicit answers ran through the producers: institutions that spread order friendly beliefs are themselves supported by the order, funded, licensed, staffed by its beneficiaries. That loop is real but partial, since it explains the supply of belief, its production and amplification, while leaving the demand side, the uptake by those the beliefs disadvantage, unexamined. A belief can be broadcast for generations and fail to take; the history of official doctrines in collapsing regimes is a history of supply without uptake. The gap is precisely shaped like a mechanism of individual psychology, a process by which minds under subordination become receptive to the beliefs that ratify it, or generate such beliefs themselves without any broadcaster's help.

Adaptive preference formation occupies the gap, and it does not occupy it alone. Elster's wider inventory of sub intentional processes supplies a small family of uptake mechanisms, each with a different signature. Wishful thinking bends belief toward desire: the subordinated believe the order will reward them because the belief is pleasant, and the belief survives evidence because its function is comfort. Dissonance reduction bends belief toward behaviour: having complied, at cost, with the order's demands, the agent concludes that the demands were legitimate, since the alternative conclusion, that he sacrificed himself for nothing, is intolerable. Sour grapes adaptation bends desire toward feasibility: the goods the order withholds cease to be wanted, and their withholding ceases to be experienced as deprivation. The three mechanisms can operate together, and their joint operation produces the phenomenon ideology theory gestured at with its vocabulary of false consciousness, but with the gesturing replaced by processes that can be looked for, distinguished and evidenced in particular cases. A historian asking why a workforce acquiesced in a deterioration of its conditions can ask discriminating questions. Did the workers' wants contract? Did their beliefs about the deterioration's causes shift toward the exonerating accounts? Did compliance precede conviction, suggesting dissonance doing the work? The questions have answers, and the answers differ in their implications for how the acquiescence might end: contracted wants revive slowly when feasibility expands, dissonance beliefs crack when behaviour is interrupted, wishful beliefs collapse with the events they deny.

The loop is thereby closed in the form his methodology requires. The order narrows feasible sets; narrowed feasible sets contract wants and bend beliefs through identifiable mechanisms; contracted wants and bent beliefs reduce resistance; reduced resistance stabilises the order that narrowed the sets. Every link in the chain is at the level of individuals and institutions acting on individuals, no collective mind is invoked, and the functional claim with which the theory began is redeemed as a mechanism chain that can be examined link by link. That the redemption was available from 1983, in a book by a member of the Marxist seminar, and that the ideology literature and the preference literature continued for decades without citing each other, is the kind of fact about the organisation of scholarship that this series exists to record: the division of intellectual labour, which makes depth possible, also manufactures gaps at the joints between specialisms, and some of the most valuable work available to a synthesising series is the carrying of a mechanism across a corridor that the specialists on either side had no professional reason to walk.

## Exploitation and value: the debates at closer range

The scorecard's two most consequential economic verdicts, the failure of the labour theory of value and the survival of exploitation on new foundations, sit inside technical debates whose outlines a serious profile should give, because the verdicts are only as intelligible as the arguments that produced them. The value debate turned on the theory's claim to explain the surface phenomena of a capitalist economy, prices and the rate of profit, by quantities of embodied labour. The difficulties were internal and cumulative. Values could be defined for the inputs of production only if the reduction of heterogeneous labour to a common unit could be performed independently of prices, and the reduction threatened circularity, since the wage goods that determine the value of labour power themselves have values. The passage from values to prices of production, required because capitals of different compositions cannot all earn the average rate of profit at value prices, generated the transformation problem, over which the literature laboured for most of a century. Elster's assessment of this labour was that it had the character of a degenerating programme: each repair preserved the theory's letter by surrendering a portion of its explanatory claim, until what remained was an accounting identity in search of a purpose. The decisive consideration, for him, was always the comparison. A theory of price formation that proceeds from technology and distribution directly, without the detour through embodied labour, explains everything the value theory explained and more, and parsimony then does its work.

The replies from within Marxian economics were not negligible, and fairness requires their shape. One line held that the value theory was never primarily a price theory but an account of the social form of labour under capitalism, of why private labours count as social only through exchange, and that grading it as a failed price theory mistakes its subject. Another line rebuilt the theory temporally, reading values and prices as determined in sequence rather than simultaneously, and claimed that the aggregate equalities the transformation debate had despaired of could be preserved on that reading. A third line treated value as a macroeconomic accounting framework in which the labour theory's claims are true by construction and empirically fruitful as measurement. Elster's scorecard, delivered before some of these replies matured, graded the theory as he found it, and this article's verdict on the scorecard accepts his grade for the theory as a price theory while noting that the form theoretic reading relocates the theory's claim to ground he did not contest: the analysis of the value form shades into the anatomy of alienation and fetishism, territories his scorecard certifies. The dispute, followed carefully, thus ends less in a corpse than in a redistribution of the estate, which is the fate his partitions usually produce.

Exploitation's reconstruction followed the opposite path, from a contested foundation to a clearer one. Roemer's general theory of exploitation, which Elster endorsed in substance, defines exploitation by a counterfactual withdrawal test applied to groups and assets: a group is exploited if it would be better off withdrawing from the economy carrying a per capita portion of the alienable assets. The definition reproduces the classical verdicts in the central cases, the propertyless are exploited by the propertied, while detaching the verdict from any claim about labour's unique productivity, and it generalises to cases the classical theory handled awkwardly, including exploitation under arrangements that call themselves socialist where asset inequality persists in new forms. The moral anatomy changes in the process. Exploitation becomes, at foundation, a charge about the distribution of assets and the rules that reproduce it, and the specifically capitalist features, the labour market, the workplace hierarchy, enter as the mechanisms by which the distributive charge is lived. Critics objected that something essential had been lost: domination within production, the experience of the working day, the extraction process itself, all flattened into a comparison of hypothetical bundles. Elster's position, characteristically, was to accept the flattening at the level of the theory of exploitation and to relocate the lost material to the theories where it belongs, alienation for the experience of work, the analysis of authority and the state for domination, rather than to demand that one concept carry the whole critique of capitalism on its back. Whether that distribution of labour among concepts is wisdom or evasion remains a live question in the literature, and it is the question a reader should carry into the companion article on the reconstruction itself.

## Class formation and its obstacles

The surviving item on class and political action likewise gains from a closer look at the obstacles Elster catalogued, because the catalogue is where his contribution to the item lies. Between a class position and a class action, his work interposes a series of filters, each of which can stop the process, and the filters together explain the historical pattern that naive versions of the theory could not: the rarity, unevenness and fragility of the very phenomenon the theory treated as the motor of history. The first filter is perception: members of a class must identify their situation as shared and as changeable, and both identifications compete with alternative framings, of nation, faith, craft, locality and individual fortune, which the same experience supports. The second filter is the collective action problem in its strict form, the free rider calculation, which operates with special force in large, dispersed classes and weakens in small, concentrated and repeatedly interacting groups, a fact that explains the otherwise puzzling pattern in which strategically placed minorities of workers have historically out organised the class as a whole. The third filter is organisational: organisation requires resources, leadership and protection against repression, and it generates its own interests, the oligarchic tendencies that Michels named, which can deflect the organisation from the purposes that called it into being. The fourth filter is the state's response, which ranges from concession that demobilises to repression that raises the price of action beyond what the mechanisms of solidarity can pay.

A theory that incorporates the filters is not refuted by the failures of class action; it predicts their distribution. Organisation should appear where groups are concentrated, interaction is repeated, selective incentives or normative sanctions are available, and the state is divided or constrained, and should falter where the conditions reverse, and the broad historical record conforms to the prediction often enough to keep the programme alive. This is the sense in which the item survived Elster's audit as a programme: not a guarantee that position produces action, but a specification of the machinery by which it sometimes does, together with the standing invitation to examine, in any case, which filter stopped the process. The contrast with the classical expectation could not be sharper, and the contrast is instructive about the whole scorecard method. The classical theory's confidence came from treating a chain of contingencies as a single necessity. The audit's work, repeated across every doctrine, is to break the chain into its links, grade each link separately, and hand back a theory that says less and explains more.


## Belief formation and the divided self

Running beneath the doctrines is a psychology, and a profile that omitted it would miss the unity of Elster's contribution to the study of ideology and action alike. His agents are not the transparent calculators of textbook rational choice, and his repeated insistence on the point is one of his most original contributions to social theory. Beliefs, in his work, are formed by processes that include the rational weighing of evidence and also wishful thinking, dissonance reduction, the availability of examples, the framing of choices and the pressure of norms, and the same agent can be found, within a single afternoon, reasoning impeccably about means while his ends and his evidence have been quietly arranged by processes he would disown if he noticed them. Desires fare no better at the hands of deliberation: they adapt to feasibility, they are inflamed by prohibition, they attach to what is customary, and they are revised by the very actions they produce, as the person who has acted for a cause comes to love it with a love proportioned to the sacrifice rather than to the merits.

The divided self is the figure that organises this psychology. Against the picture of the agent as a single sovereign chooser, Elster set the picture of successive and competing selves: the self of the morning resolution against the self of the evening temptation, the deliberating self against the visceral self, the self that plans against the self that will inherit the plan and resent it. Precommitment, constitutionalism at the scale of a single life, is the rational response of a self that knows its own division, and the analysis of self binding, from Ulysses onward, became one of his signature themes. The Marxist resonance of the divided self deserves emphasis, because it cuts in two directions at once. It deepens the theory of ideology, by supplying the internal divisions through which subordinating beliefs take hold and hold fast. And it complicates the theory of emancipation, since a subject whose preferences are adapted and whose beliefs are arranged by sub intentional processes cannot be emancipated by information alone, a complication the tradition's more voluntarist strands resisted and its more serious strands absorbed.

There is a further strand, the study of counteradaptive preferences, the forbidden fruit pattern in which what is denied becomes more desirable for being denied, and of the strategic exploitation of all these patterns by actors who understand them: the advertiser who frames, the demagogue who inflames, the institution that arranges feasibility so that adaptation does its quiet work. Elster's social theory is peopled not only by agents subject to these mechanisms but by agents who deploy them, and the interplay, manipulators who are themselves manipulated, audiences who adapt to the menus set for them and occasionally revolt against the menu, gives his explanations their characteristic texture: no puppeteers standing outside the web, only situated actors working a machinery that works on them as well. It is a more democratic ontology than either the conspiracy theories of ideology or the sovereign consumer of liberal apologetics, and it is one of the places where his work most plainly outgrew the debate that produced it.

## The reception: who read him, and how

The reception of Elster's Marx work followed the pattern his scorecard predicts for itself: the verdicts were raided by partisans and the method was adopted by professionals. Hostile readers of Marxism cited the failures as a capitulation extracted from inside the citadel, rarely mentioning that the auditor had certified a third of the estate in the same document. Sympathetic readers mined the survivals for reassurance and passed over the rigour that made the certifications worth having. The scholarly literature, slower and less quotable, did something more interesting with both halves. The value verdict was absorbed into the general reassessment of Marxian economics, where it joined independent lines of criticism and reconstruction. The exploitation reconstruction entered the mainstream of analytical political philosophy, where the question of exploitation's foundation is debated in Roemer's and Elster's terms by writers with no Marxist commitment. The functional explanation critique set the terms for methodology discussions far beyond Marxism, in evolutionary social science, in the philosophy of biology's social applications, and in any field where benefit is offered as explanation.

His standing among philosophers of social science rests on the mechanisms programme and on the analysis of rationality's pathologies, and it is secure in a way that standings inside a single tradition rarely are, because it rests on tools in daily use. Analytical sociologists build mechanism explanations; behavioural researchers catalogue preference formation; theorists of collective decision making work on assemblies with his distinctions in hand. The Marxist tradition's own verdict on him is divided in the way his scorecard taught it to be: the methodological demand is widely, if quietly, accepted; the doctrine of methodological individualism is widely, if respectfully, resisted; and the scorecard itself is treated, by the careful, as a benchmark that any defence of the failed items must answer rather than ignore. A defence of the labour theory of value written after Elster must engage the parsimony argument and the transformation record as he stated them. A defence of functional explanation must produce its loops or argue, with Cohen, for their postponement. That is what it means for a critic to have set the terms: not that his verdicts were final, but that the appeals against them must be filed in his court, on his forms, addressing his counts. The cluster of articles to which this profile belongs is itself evidence of the arrangement, since its organisation, doctrine by doctrine, mechanism by mechanism, defeater by defeater, follows the procedure he normalised.

## A note on sources and quotation

A word about this article's handling of its subject's texts is owed to readers who will check it, and the series' standards require the note to be explicit. Elster's methodological commitment is quoted in this article in a short fragment, the phrase specifying that social phenomena are to be explained in terms of individuals, with the properties, goals, beliefs and actions of individuals, which is the formulation associated with Making Sense of Marx and restated across his methodological writings. The definition of a mechanism is paraphrased with its key terms preserved, frequently occurring and recognisable causal patterns triggered under conditions often unknown, following the statement in Nuts and Bolts and its successors. The scorecard's items are his conclusions, stated in his works and summarised in his own retrospective lists, and the wording of the verdicts in the table is this article's compression, not quotation. Dates given for the books, 1979 for the study of precommitment, 1983 for Sour Grapes, 1985 for Making Sense of Marx, 1989 for Nuts and Bolts, are the publication dates of the works discussed, and the characterisations of the September Group, of Olson's collective action problem, of Okishio's theorem and of the Levine, Sober and Wright intervention reflect the standard scholarly record of those debates. Where the article has judged, it has flagged the judgement as its own and attached the defeater the cluster's discipline requires. Readers who want the arguments unmediated should go to the texts by the route the reading section provides; a profile that sends its readers to the sources with a map in hand has done the work a profile can do.


## Technical change in depth: the certified mechanism

The survival of Marx's account of technical change deserves an extended treatment, because it is the item where Elster's method and Marx's practice coincide most completely, and the coincidence illuminates both. The account, as Marx developed it across the manuscripts and the first volume of Capital, begins from the situation of the individual firm. Each capitalist operates under a compulsion not of his choosing: rivals who cheapen production will take his market, so he must revolutionise his methods or exit. The search for advantage concentrates on the margin where advantage is available, the reduction of labour cost per unit, since labour is the input whose price the firm cannot fix and whose productivity machinery can multiply. Innovations that succeed diffuse, because imitation is compelled by the same pressure that produced the search, and the diffusion resets the standard of socially necessary labour, devaluing the capital of laggards and cheapening the commodities workers consume, with consequences for the value of labour power itself. The whole sequence is a machine built of situated decisions, each intelligible from the decider's position, whose aggregate outcome, a restless transformation of the productive basis of society, nobody chose and nobody can stop.

Grade that account by the standards of this article and it passes every gate that the functionalist claims failed. The feedback loop is present and specified: selection among firms through competition, the social analogue of the mechanism that legitimises functional explanation in biology. The level of explanation is the level of action: owners and managers deciding under pressure, with their beliefs and errors in full view. The pattern is recognisable across centuries and sectors, from the mechanisation of spinning to the automation of clerical work, and its directional bias, toward economising on the expensive factor and toward labour displacement where labour is dear, is a derivable consequence rather than an added assumption. Even the account's treatment of failure is mechanism shaped: firms do not innovate optimally or omnisciently, they grope, copy, misjudge, and the selection environment, not any planner's wisdom, does the sorting. Elster's certification of this item was therefore not an act of generosity toward the tradition's founder. It was the method recognising its own kind, and the recognition carries a lesson about Marx that the scorecard's other items reinforce: the founder's concrete explanatory practice was frequently better than the philosophy his followers extracted from it, and a reconstruction that grades the practice rather than the philosophy will keep more of Marx than either the orthodox defence or the hostile dismissal expects.

The item also shows the limits of the certification, since the surviving account is a pattern and not a law. It does not predict the timing or the sectoral path of innovation; it does not entail the falling rate of profit, whose attempted derivation from it failed on other grounds; and it is compatible with long periods in which the bias of technical change runs toward skill, toward energy, toward materials, as relative prices dictate. What the pattern guarantees is only the restlessness and the selection logic, the permanent revolutionising of production that the Communist Manifesto celebrated in prose. A reader who wants to know what of Marx a rigorous auditor kept can be pointed here first, because here the keeping is least paradoxical: the most famous passage of Marxist rhetoric turns out to be attached to the most defensible mechanism in the corpus.

## Alienation in depth: the anatomy graded

Alienation's survival repays the same closeness, because the manner of its survival exhibits the reconstructive operation at its most delicate. The early Marx's account, in the manuscripts of 1844, describes the worker's estrangement under four aspects: from the product, which confronts him as an alien object; from the activity, which is experienced as forced and external; from his species being, the free conscious production he takes to define human life; and from other people, who meet him as competitors and commanders rather than as collaborators. The account is saturated with a philosophical anthropology, a doctrine of man's essence and its realisation, which later Marxists alternately embraced as the theory's humanist core and discarded as youthful metaphysics. Elster's reconstruction proceeded by translating the anthropology into a claim that could be assessed without it. The core, on his reading, is a theory of self realisation: human beings have capacities whose development and exercise constitute flourishing, work is the central arena in which those capacities are exercised or stunted, and certain organisations of work systematically stunt them by severing the worker from control, from comprehension of the whole, and from the experience of the product as his own.

Translated in this way, the theory becomes a set of examinable propositions about job design, autonomy, task variety and the division of labour, together with an openly normative premise about which capacities matter. The propositions can be investigated, and a large literature in the psychology and sociology of work, most of it innocent of Marx, investigates neighbouring ground under headings like job control and intrinsic motivation. The normative premise can be debated as what it is, a perfectionist claim about human good, rather than masquerading as a finding of historical science. What the reconstruction discards is the guarantee: the assurance that alienation is a transitional station on a road whose destination abolishes it. Nothing in the anatomy promises that the arrangements which stunt capacities will be replaced by arrangements that free them, and the history of actually existing attempts supplies no ground for the promise. What remains is a permanent critical instrument: a way of asking, of any organisation of work including those that call themselves post capitalist, whether the people inside it direct their activity or are directed through it, comprehend the whole they serve or only their fragment, and meet the product of their hands as theirs or as a stranger. The questions survive the philosophy that first framed them, which is the definition of a successful reconstruction, and their survival in this form is the scorecard's clearest demonstration that discarding a doctrine's foundations need not mean discarding its eyes.

## The state, ideology and history: the unlisted verdicts

The scorecard table lists the headline items, but Elster's examination ranged across the whole of Marx's social theory, and three further areas deserve a compressed report for completeness, since readers of the series will meet his fingerprints on topics whose articles are elsewhere. On the state, his treatment rejected both the instrumental picture, the state as a committee of the ruling class, and the functional picture, the state as guarantor of accumulation's conditions, insofar as either was offered as a general theory. What he certified was the research programme of asking, for each state action, whose situated decisions produced it, what information and incentives the deciders faced, and which class interests the outcome in fact served, with the answer allowed to vary from case to case and to disappoint every general formula. On ideology, beyond the functional critique already described, he pressed the tradition to distinguish the questions it habitually fused: whether a belief is false, whether it serves an interest, whether it is held because it serves that interest, are three questions with three different evidences, and the tradition's habit of answering them with a single gesture was, in his judgement, responsible for much of its theory's reputation for slipperiness. The preference mechanisms discussed in this article are his constructive contribution to the third question.

On historical materialism as a philosophy of history, his verdict matched the theory of history treatment given earlier: the developmental primacy of the productive forces survives as a constraint, the succession of modes as an engine does not, and the moralised reading of history as a story with a direction and a destination fails with the dialectics that underwrote it. He was equally firm about a point the tradition's critics often miss: the failure of the philosophy of history leaves the historical writing untouched in its value, since the great narratives of class struggle in France or of primitive accumulation in England persuade, where they persuade, by the density of their mechanisms, the named actors, the specified pressures, the traced cascades, and not by the laws they were enlisted to illustrate. A reader formed by Elster reads Marx's histories with more pleasure and more profit than a reader formed by the orthodoxy, because the Elsterian reader is looking for the machinery the orthodoxy taught its readers to overlook in favour of the moral.


## Rational choice Marxism: the label and its limits

Elster's name is permanently attached to a label he did not choose and about which he was ambivalent: rational choice Marxism. The label gathered the members of the school who used the formal tools of game theory and optimisation to rebuild Marxist claims, and it promised a synthesis, Marx's questions answered with the economist's instruments. The promise was partly kept. The collective action analysis, Roemer's exploitation models, the game theoretic treatments of class compromise and of the dynamics of bargaining between labour and capital, are real achievements of the synthesis, and they share a recognisable style: a Marxist explanandum, a formal model of situated actors, a result that either vindicates the classical intuition under specified conditions or shows precisely where it breaks. Elster's own relation to the label cooled as his work on the limits of rationality deepened, and the cooling is instructive. A theorist who had demonstrated, in book after book, that preferences adapt, beliefs bend, selves divide and norms grip through emotion could not present rational choice as a complete theory of action without sawing off the branch his own best work grew on. His mature position treated rational choice as one instrument among several, indispensable for strategic interaction, insufficient for the formation of the actors who interact.

The label's history carries a warning that this series has met before, in the careers of other schools. A method adopted as a solvent, to dissolve obscurity, can harden into a new orthodoxy with its own protected assumptions, and the assumption that actors are rational in the thin sense was the rational choice programme's candidate for protection. Elster's distinction within the school is that he was the member who attacked the protection from inside, with the preference formation work of 1983 preceding the school's flowering and the emotions and norms work following it. The trajectory gives his scorecard on Marx a reflexive application that he would have been the first to state: grade the rational choice programme by the same standards, keep the mechanisms that earn their place, discard the imperial claims, and let the partition fall where the arguments put it. By that grading, much of the programme survives, as technique. What does not survive is any version of it as a complete anthropology, and the school's most distinguished methodologist is the authority for the limitation.

## Teaching Elster: the seminar uses of a scorecard

The pedagogical value of Elster's work, which the persona sections below develop for specific audiences, rests on a property worth naming in the body of the article: his texts make disagreement productive. A seminar on the labour theory of value that assigns his examination alongside a defence learns more than a seminar that assigns either alone, because the examination specifies exactly which joints of the theory are under pressure, the reduction, the transformation, the parsimony comparison, and the defence must answer at those joints or visibly fail to. A seminar on explanation that sets his functional critique against Cohen's defence stages the deepest methodological argument in modern Marxist theory as a fair fight between masters. A seminar on ideology that pairs the classical texts with Sour Grapes performs, in the classroom, the connection the literatures failed to perform in print, and students who have made the connection once will make it again wherever they meet a theory of belief that lacks an uptake mechanism. In each case the teaching works because the arguments are itemised: students can agree with the verdict on value and reject the verdict on functional explanation, can accept the mechanisms programme and resist the doctrine, and the itemisation turns the seminar's disagreements from clashes of allegiance into a map of the subject itself. That is the deepest way in which the stated scorecard serves as a teaching instrument, and it explains why his books, written without pedagogical intent, have had the classroom afterlife they have had.

## The wider field: contemporaries and contrasts

No thinker in this cluster worked alone, and Elster's profile sharpens when set against the contrasts the school itself supplied. Against Cohen, the defender of functional explanation and of the theory of history's classical form, Elster stands as the school's sceptic about consequence explanation, and their argument, conducted over years in print and in seminar, is the cluster's methodological centrepiece. Against Roemer, the school's great model builder, Elster stands as the member least confident that formalisation alone settles questions, since his psychology kept reminding the modellers that the actors in their games arrive with manufactured preferences and divided selves. Against Wright, the school's empiricist, who rebuilt class analysis for survey research and comparative sociology, Elster stands as the theorist of the obstacles Wright's data kept encountering, the filters between position and action. The contrasts are not rankings. They are the divisions of labour of a functioning research community, and they explain why the school, for all its internal friction, produced a body of work larger than any member's: each man's strength was the check on the others' characteristic excess, rigour about explanation checking rigour about history, psychology checking formalism, data checking both.

Beyond the school, the contrasts widen. Against the classical Marxism the school displaced, with its dialectical warrants and its confidence in historical direction, Elster's work reads as a different civilisation of thought, and the displacement was never total, since the classical tradition continues in its own institutions with its own criteria, largely unpersuaded. Against the liberal social science that received his tools, he stands as a supplier whose name is attached to the tools' origin in a quarrel the recipients have forgotten, which is the usual fate of successful exports. Against the critical theory tradition, with which he shared the topics of ideology and alienation while sharing none of the method, the relation is one of mutual non citation so complete that it constitutes its own datum about the period's intellectual geography: two literatures, analysing the same deformations of belief and work, in the same decades, in languages of mutual unintelligibility. A synthesising series can record the fact and, where the materials permit, build the bridge neither side built; the adaptive preference connection in this article is one such bridge, and the alienation reconstruction, which translates a humanist Marxist theme into terms a psychologist of work can test, is another.


## A catalogue of mechanisms: the working stock

The mechanisms programme can sound programmatic until its stock is exhibited, and Elster's books are, among other things, catalogues: pattern after pattern, each named, each illustrated, each with its logic exposed. A tour of the principal entries shows what the programme actually contains and why researchers in unrelated fields found it usable. Wishful thinking is the bending of belief by desire, the tendency to believe what it would be good to believe, disciplined only intermittently by the costs of error. Cognitive dissonance is the pressure toward consistency among beliefs and between belief and behaviour, which produces the characteristic rationalisations: the chosen option upgraded, the rejected option downgraded, the costly commitment reinterpreted as freely embraced. Spite and envy are the social preferences that standard models omit, in which another's gain registers as one's own loss and can motivate costly harm. The endowment and framing patterns govern how the description of a choice, rather than its substance, steers the chooser. Threshold and cascade patterns govern collective outcomes, in which each actor's decision depends on how many others have decided, so that small differences in the distribution of thresholds produce the difference between a fizzle and an avalanche, in bank runs, in protest waves, in the collapse of regimes whose support was always shallower than its unanimous display suggested.

Two entries in the catalogue have special importance for the themes of this article. The first is the family of norm mechanisms: the internal sanction of shame, the external sanctions of contempt and ostracism, the peculiar motivational signature of norms, which is that they are followed even when violation is safe and profitable, and resented even by those they benefit when the benefit is purchased by someone else's violation. The second is the family of interaction patterns studied by game theory in its realistic mood: the folk theorem's lesson that repeated dealing sustains cooperation among the self interested, the fragility of that cooperation to noise and misperception, the role of communication and of commitment devices in stabilising it, and the second order problem, that punishing defectors is itself a collective good which free riders will shirk, solved in practice by the norm mechanisms that make punishment a duty rather than a calculation. Read the catalogue as a whole and a picture of social life assembles itself: beings of bounded rationality and divided motivation, bending their beliefs and adapting their desires, gripping and gripped by norms, interacting strategically under institutions they inherited, produce the large patterns, markets, movements, regimes, cultures, that macro level theories name. The naming is not the explaining. The catalogue is the explaining, at the level where explanation can be checked.

The catalogue also disciplines the explainer in a way worth making explicit, because it answers a common suspicion that mechanism talk is a licence to invent a plausible story for any outcome. A mechanism attribution is checkable in ways a law's application often is not. The pattern has a signature: a sequence, a timing, a distribution of behaviour across differently situated actors. Sour grapes adaptation predicts that wants will track feasibility with a lag and will not instantly revive when feasibility expands; dissonance predicts that conviction will be strongest among those who have sacrificed most; cascade models predict that the order of defections will follow the distribution of thresholds and that late defectors will profess surprise at their own earlier loyalty. An attribution that fails its signature is refuted, and Elster's methodological writings are full of the demand that the signature be sought. The programme's modesty about prediction is thus balanced by an immodesty about identification: it claims that, after the fact, we can often know which pattern operated, and know it in a way that excludes the rivals. Social science on this conception is closer to history and to medicine than to astronomy, a diagnostic discipline whose rigour lies in the differential diagnosis. The kinship with history mattered to its author, whose favourite social scientist was a historian of the nineteenth century, and it matters for the Marxist reader, since the tradition's greatest works were always diagnostic in exactly this sense, whatever their authors believed about laws.

## Tocqueville and the French formation

The formation deserves a section of its own, because the standard picture of Elster as an Anglo American analyst misses the French half of his intellectual descent, and the French half explains features of the work that the analytic half cannot. His doctorate was taken in Paris, in the orbit of Raymond Aron, the great liberal interpreter of Marx and of modern society, whose lectures and seminars formed a generation of French thinkers in a distinctive posture: sociological in curiosity, historical in method, allergic to system building, and convinced that the explanation of large outcomes runs through the unintended consequences of situated action. Behind Aron stood Tocqueville, and Elster's engagement with Tocqueville, in essays and in the architecture of his own explanations, is one of the continuities of his career. Democracy in America is, among its other identities, a mechanisms book before the name: an inventory of the patterns, the passion for equality turning against liberty, the soft despotism of tutelary administration, the association as school of citizenship, by which a democratic social state shapes the beliefs and desires of its members. Tocqueville's explanation of why democratic peoples might surrender freedom without noticing is an adaptive preference story told a century and a half early, and the line from it to Sour Grapes is direct.

The French formation also accounts for the style. Elster writes in aphorisms and distinctions, in the manner of the French moralists he admired, La Rochefoucauld and Montaigne standing behind the social science, and his books are full of the moralists' characteristic move: the deflating redescription of a noble motive, the exposure of self love in the costume of virtue, followed, crucially, by the refusal to conclude that the costume is all there is. The moralists' psychology, suspicion disciplined by fairness, is the psychology of the mechanisms catalogue, and it is why his debunking never curdles into the cynicism that afflicts lesser practitioners of suspicion. Marx himself, it should be remembered, was formed by the same French literature of suspicion and by its German systematisation, and part of the affinity that made Making Sense of Marx possible, beneath the methodological warfare, is a shared ancestry in the attempt to see human beings as they are rather than as their ideologies advertise. The auditor and the audited were cousins. The audit is a family document, which is one more reason its verdicts cannot be filed as an outsider's polemic.

## Explanation in the social sciences: the philosophical setting

The mechanisms programme entered a philosophy of social science whose terms had been set by the covering law model, and the profile's readers are owed the setting in brief, since the programme's originality is visible only against it. The covering law model, associated with Carl Hempel, held that to explain an event is to deduce it from laws and initial conditions, and that prediction and explanation are symmetric: a perfect explanation, run forward, is a prediction. The model fit parts of physics and set an aspiration for every other science. Its difficulties in the social domain were notorious long before Elster: the laws were missing, the initial conditions unmanageable, the predictions embarrassing. Responses divided. One party protected the model by treating social science as immature, its laws awaiting discovery. Another abandoned explanation for interpretation, holding that human action is to be understood from inside, by its meanings, in a manner to which laws are irrelevant. Elster's programme is a third response, and its refusal of both parties is its identity. Against the protectionists it holds that the missing laws are missing because the material does not contain them, not because the science is young: paired mechanisms are a permanent feature of a world of learning, reacting agents. Against the interpretivists it holds that meaning centred understanding, while indispensable as a source of hypotheses, does not exempt a claim from the demand for causal specification: to know what an action meant is not yet to know what it did, and social science is answerable for effects as well as for senses.

The programme's descendants in philosophy have multiplied the distinctions, between mechanisms and capacities, between explanation and causal inference, between the identification of a pattern and its statistical estimation, and the debates continue in a literature that cites Elster as a founder while disputing his formulations. The disputes are the sign of life. A programme that had been simply wrong would have been discarded; a programme that had been wholly right would have been absorbed without argument. The mechanisms idea occupies the productive middle, right enough to reorganise a field's practice, open enough to sustain a generation of refinement. For the purposes of this series the philosophical setting matters for a further reason: it explains the scorecard's grading standard. When Elster graded Marx's doctrines, the grades were issued from a philosophy of science that he applied impartially to every claimant, including the covering law orthodoxy of his own methodological culture, which fares, in his writings, little better than dialectical materialism. The auditor's standards were nobody's party property. That impartiality is the final answer to the suspicion that the scorecard was an execution dressed as an examination: the examiner failed his own side's favourite doctrines in the same ink.


## Crisis, immiseration and concentration: the remaining economic verdicts

Beyond the two great economic failures itemised in the scorecard, Elster's examination touched the cluster of claims through which Marx's economics was supposed to describe capitalism's trajectory, and a compressed report of those verdicts completes the economic side of the profile. The tendency toward concentration and centralisation of capital, the prediction that competition itself produces monopoly as capitals devour one another, he treated as a genuine pattern overstated into a destiny: concentration is real in many sectors and eras, driven by scale economies and by the selection mechanism he certified, but it coexists with counter tendencies, the proliferation of small firms in services, the fragmentation of production across supply chains, the entry of new capitals into new industries, and the pattern's net direction is an empirical matter that varies, not a law that accumulates. Immiseration, the claim that the workers' condition deteriorates absolutely as accumulation proceeds, he graded as false in its absolute form for the advanced economies over the long run, while noting the relative and experiential claims that survive translation: the share of labour, the security of employment, the experience of work's intensity and control, are separate variables with separate histories, and the tradition's habit of fusing them into a single misery index served rhetoric at the expense of knowledge. Crises he treated as the area where Marx's instincts were richest and his theory least finished: the disproportionality of production across sectors, the fragility of credit chains, the cascade dynamics of confidence, are all mechanisms in his sense, recognisable patterns that recur, and the attempt to fuse them into a single crisis law, whether of the falling profit rate or of underconsumption, repeated the characteristic error of promoting a pattern into an engine.

The pattern across these verdicts is by this point familiar, and its familiarity is itself the finding. In case after case, the audit separates a mechanism, which it keeps, from a law, which it discards, and the Marx who emerges from the separation is a great diagnostician of capitalism's recurrent pathologies whose philosophy of history kept promising a prognosis the diagnoses did not support. Readers formed by the scorecard learn to perform the separation themselves, on new material, which is why Elster's influence on the tradition's careful readers exceeds the influence of his particular verdicts: he taught a gesture, and the gesture, once learned, is applied to friends and enemies alike, including, as this article has applied it, to Elster.

## Marx as political analyst: the histories graded

The audit's most appreciative pages concern Marx the political analyst, and the appreciation deserves expansion because it corrects the impression, left by the scorecard's failures, that the examination was an economist's raid on a philosopher's estate. In the histories, the Eighteenth Brumaire above all, Elster found the method he preached already practised at genius level: an outcome, the coup of Louis Bonaparte, explained through the situations, illusions, organisational forms and miscalculations of the classes and fractions contending in France, with the famous opening distinction between the circumstances men inherit and the history they make serving as the programme's motto avant la lettre. The class fractions of the Brumaire, finance aristocracy, industrial bourgeoisie, petty bourgeoisie, peasantry, lumpenproletariat, are not deduced from a schema; they are identified by their situations and their characteristic mechanisms of perception and action, the peasants' sack of potatoes, a class in itself that cannot represent itself and must be represented, being precisely a claim about the conditions of collective action, about what a dispersed and unorganised class cannot do for itself. Read with Elster's eyes, the Brumaire is a mechanisms text: representation fills the vacuum left by an unsolved collective action problem, ideology operates through the traditions of dead generations weighing on the brains of the living, which is a claim about belief formation under the pressure of inherited frames, and the farce that repeats tragedy is a cascade of imitations and misreadings that no law dictated and every step explains.

The significance for the scorecard is that the survivals and the histories are the same Marx. The analyst of class fractions is the theorist whose class and politics programme survived; the diagnostician of ideology in the Brumaire is the theorist whose uptake gap the preference work fills; the anatomist of the working day is the theorist of alienation. The failures, by contrast, cluster where Marx the analyst was overruled by Marx the system builder, where the schemas of value, tendency and dialectic were imposed on material the analyst would have handled more patiently. Elster never stated the contrast as a slogan, but his book performs it on every page, and it may be the most useful single idea a reader takes from the whole examination: that the tradition contains two Marxes, the diagnostician and the prophet, that they share a bibliography and often a paragraph, and that the work of reading Marx well is the work of telling them apart sentence by sentence. The stated scorecard is that work, done once, in public, by a reader equipped for it.

## Freedom, determinism and the actor's standpoint

One further region of Elster's thought bears on the Marxist inheritance and belongs in a complete profile: his treatment of freedom and determinism in explanation. Methodological individualism is sometimes confused with a doctrine of free will, as though explaining through individuals required their choices to be uncaused. His position was the opposite of confused on this point. The mechanisms that form beliefs and preferences are causal through and through; the actor who adapts, rationalises and cascades is a caused being, and the explanation of his action by his reasons is not a claim that the reasons were uncaused but a claim about the level at which the causation runs. Reasons are causes, in his philosophy, operating through the agent's deliberative machinery, and the machinery itself has a causal history written by circumstances, institutions and the sub intentional processes his work catalogues. The position dissolves a pseudo problem that haunted Marxist discussions of structure and agency for generations, in which explanation by structures was supposed to abolish agency and explanation by agency to abolish determination. In the mechanisms picture both are present in every case: structures determine the situations, the situations and the formed psychologies determine the actions in the only sense of determine the material supports, through patterns that can be traced, and the actions aggregate into the structures of the next round. Agency is not the opposite of determination but one of its addresses.

The political moral he drew was characteristically austere. If preferences are formed and beliefs are bent, then the appeal to what people want, whether by market liberals certifying outcomes or by revolutionaries certifying mandates, is never the end of an argument. The formation must itself be interrogated: under what conditions were these wants produced, and would the agents, informed and unadapted, endorse the process that produced them? That interrogation is the point at which his psychology becomes a critical theory, and it is available to any tradition willing to use it. The Marxist theory of ideology, completed by the preference mechanisms, is one use. The liberal theory of autonomy, troubled by the same mechanisms, is another. The auditor's tools, once again, refuse to stay in the house where they were forged.


## Precommitment at scale: constitutions as mechanisms

The analysis of precommitment, introduced earlier in this profile through the figure of Ulysses, grew in Elster's later work into a theory of constitutional politics whose outlines belong here because they complete the arc from individual psychology to institutional design. A constitution, in this analysis, is a precommitment device operating under special difficulties. The binder and the bound are both collectivities extended in time; the motives for binding mix the public spirited desire to protect rights and stabilise expectations with the partisan desire to entrench a temporary majority's preferences against its successors; and the binding is never complete, since the bound generation retains the physical power to amend, reinterpret or discard the text, so that a constitution's force rests substantially on the mechanisms, reverence, coordination, the costs of reopening settled questions, that make self binding stick. His comparative studies of constitution making assemblies traced how the circumstances of drafting leave fingerprints on the product: assemblies that are also sitting legislatures import their ordinary interests into the fundamental law; assemblies elected for the single purpose, expecting dissolution, deliberate under a partial veil about their own future positions, and the texts improve in impartiality accordingly; publicity, which civics celebrates, can degrade deliberation into posture, while a measure of secrecy can license the changing of minds that compromise requires. Each finding is a mechanism claim, testable against the record of conventions, and the whole body of work stands as the programme's most ambitious demonstration: the largest artefacts of political life, the fundamental laws of states, explained by the situations, passions and reasoning of the situated people who drafted them, with no residue of national spirit or historical mission doing unexamined work.

The connection back to Marx is indirect but real, and a series on Marxism is the right place to draw it. The classical tradition had a theory of constitutions as superstructure, the legal dress of class power, and the theory in its crude form explained everything about a constitution by the class it served and nothing about its text. The mechanisms treatment recovers what the crude theory flattened: class power enters, massively, as the distribution of situations and resources among the drafters and the interests they carry into the room, but the text that emerges is the product of those interests passing through procedures, deadlines, passions and arguments, and two regimes of similar class character can emerge with markedly different fundamental laws because the passage differed. Structure sets the table; mechanisms deal the cards; the hand that is played is history. A Marxism that absorbed the lesson would lose nothing of its insight into power and would gain the ability to explain variation, which is where theories earn their keep.

## The emotions in politics: a mechanisms inventory

Elster's work on emotion in politics, continuous with the constitution studies and with the transitional justice research, supplies a further stock of mechanisms that the readers of this series will find applicable to materials the tradition handles. Shame operates as the enforcer of norms where law cannot reach, and its anticipation disciplines assemblies, bureaucracies and armies; its public infliction, in the rituals of fallen regimes' reckonings, is among the most powerful and dangerous instruments a successor order possesses. Indignation at unfairness motivates the costly punishment of defectors and exploiters on which cooperation depends, and its manipulation is the demagogue's first art. Fear contracts time horizons and licenses precommitment's dark twin, the surrender of liberty to whoever promises protection. Enthusiasm, the least studied of the political emotions in his work and the one he treated with the most suspicion, dissolves the calculation of thresholds in crowds and conventions, producing the cascades in which assemblies vote their own irrelevance or crowds storm what regiments could not take. The inventory matters methodologically because emotions are the point where the individual level of explanation is least reducible to preference and belief in the thin senses: an emotion is a bodily state with an action tendency, a shortened horizon and a characteristic blindness, and a social science founded on individuals must reckon with individuals who flush, tremble and surge. The auditor of Marx, so often caricatured as a cold formalist, spent a substantial part of his career insisting that the springs of action include the viscera. The caricature survives because it is easier than the bibliography.

## Open questions the programme leaves

A fair profile ends its exposition with the debts its subject left unpaid, and the mechanisms programme has three that the literature continues to negotiate. The first is the problem of selection among mechanisms: in a concrete case, several patterns can be told as stories, and the programme's forensic confidence depends on signatures discriminating among them more often than sceptics allow. Where signatures are weak or records thin, mechanism explanation can degenerate into the just so storytelling it was built to replace, and the programme's defenders owe criteria for when an identification is earned. Elster's own practice, with its insistence on the pattern's observable traces and on the comparison of rival patterns for the same case, supplies the beginnings of such criteria without completing them. The second debt is aggregation: the step from individual actions to macro outcomes, which game theory handles for small numbers and simulation handles for large, remains the least theorised joint in the structure, and the analytical sociology that inherited the programme has made the joint its central technical problem. The third debt is the one the objection section of this article pressed: the standing of structural explanation, where this article's verdict of compatibility rests on his later practice rather than on any reconciliation he stated in terms. A completed theory would say, systematically, how positions and relations figure as initial conditions, how they are themselves the sedimented output of earlier mechanisms, and where, if anywhere, the regress of formation may legitimately be stopped for a given explanatory purpose. That completion is not in his work, and the lack is the most consequential item on the debit side of his own scorecard, the one a successor will have to settle. Recording the debt in his own coin, itemised and with its defeater attached, is the tribute his method demands and the one this profile is built to pay.


## Making Sense of Marx as a book: its method chapter by chapter

A profile that has used the scorecard as its spine owes readers a closer account of the book that issued it, since Making Sense of Marx is less often read than cited, and its actual procedure is more interesting than its reputation. The book opens with methodology, and the opening is a declaration of intent: before any doctrine is examined, the standards of examination are stated, the demand for microfoundations, the critique of functional explanation in its general form, the refusal of dialectical warrants, so that no verdict later in the book can be charged to standards smuggled in for the occasion. The substantive chapters then proceed through the corpus in an order that itself teaches: the economic doctrines first, value, exploitation, the falling rate of profit, the theory of crises, because those were the claims to scientific status; then the social theory, class, the state, ideology; then the theory of history and the political writings. In each chapter the procedure repeats with a discipline that can feel relentless: the doctrine is reconstructed in its strongest form, often more clearly than its original statement, the reconstruction is tested against the standards, the strongest replies are considered, and the verdict is delivered in terms that distinguish what is false from what is unproven from what is true but misdescribed.

Two features of the procedure deserve emphasis for readers who will use the book. The first is the treatment of Marx's texts. Elster read the whole corpus, the juvenilia and the journalism as well as the canonical volumes, and he read it with a philologist's respect for what the sentences say, combined with a philosopher's refusal to let reverence substitute for argument. Passages that the tradition quoted as authorities are treated as claims, and claims are asked for their evidence; passages that the tradition ignored, where Marx the analyst contradicts Marx the system builder, are given their full weight. The result is a book that specialists in Marx's texts could respect even where they rejected its conclusions, which is not the usual fate of methodological raids. The second feature is the verdicts' granularity. Elster rarely condemns or certifies a doctrine wholesale; the characteristic unit of his judgement is the sub claim, the tendency, the inference, the mechanism, and the chapters are full of sentences that begin by granting a component and end by refusing the assembly. Readers who cite the book for a wholesale verdict, in either direction, are citing a book that does not exist, and the scorecard table in this article, with its row by row partition, is an attempt to cite the book that does.

The book's reception inside the school set the pattern for its wider fate. Cohen answered its methodological chapters with the defence of functional explanation that the companion article records. Roemer's work supplied the exploitation reconstruction the book endorsed. Wright's empirical programme took up its class analysis while resisting its methodological doctrine, in the joint intervention with Levine and Sober already discussed. The book thus functioned less as a tombstone than as a sorting engine for the school's own subsequent work, which is the strongest evidence for this article's characterisation of it: an audit conducted inside a going concern, whose findings the concern proceeded to trade on.

## The Norwegian and European setting

Intellectual biographies in this series attend to formation, and Elster's formation has a geographical dimension that the Anglo American reception tends to flatten. He was formed in Oslo, in a small country's high culture where the distances between philosophy, social science, literature and public life are short, and where a scholar of his generation encountered Marxism not as an exotic import but as a living presence in the labour movement's own self understanding and in the universities' debates. The Scandinavian social democracies supplied his generation with a standing exhibit that neither orthodox Marxism nor its liberal opponents could comfortably process: capitalist economies with powerful labour movements, compressed distributions and extensive public provision, achieved without revolution and maintained without the predicted immiseration or the predicted collapse. The exhibit does not appear in his books as an argument, but it forms the background plausibility structure within which a partition of Marx, rather than a verdict of simple truth or falsehood, would seem the natural output: in Oslo, the question was never whether the tradition described nothing real, since its parties governed; the question was which of its claims described the reality its parties were busy modifying. His doctorate in Paris added the second formation, Aron's seminar and the French literature of suspicion, and his subsequent career, held in posts in Norway, the United States and France, made him one of the period's genuinely European social scientists, publishing in several languages and intellectual idioms. The mechanisms programme's success in crossing disciplinary borders had a precursor in its author's career of crossing national ones, and the stated scorecard, with its refusal of every camp's slogan, has the same provenance: it is the work of a man formed in several houses and owned by none.

## Why the audit model matters beyond Marx

The last substantive point of this profile concerns the model of criticism that Elster's career exemplifies, because the model is the article's implicit recommendation to every tradition that reads it. The model has four elements, each visible in the record this article has surveyed. The critic begins inside, or at least begins by earning the right to be heard inside, through demonstrated command of the material at its strongest. The criticism is itemised, doctrine by doctrine and claim by claim, so that agreement and disagreement can be registered at the same granularity at which the tradition's own work proceeds. The standards applied are stated in advance and applied impartially, including to the critic's own camp and tools. And the criticism is constructive in the strict sense that it supplies, or points to, the alternative that the demolished claim was gesturing toward: mechanisms for laws, distributive foundations for exploitation, uptake processes for ideology, filters for class action. A criticism with those four properties cannot be processed by a tradition as noise. It changes what the tradition's next generation has to do to count as serious, which is the definition of consequential criticism, and it explains why a book published in 1985 still organises the syllabus through which Marx's social theory is taught to students who have never heard the controversies that produced it.

The model also explains the failures of most criticism, which lacks one or more of the elements. Criticism from outside, without the command, is dismissed as ignorance and usually is. Criticism wholesale, without the itemisation, is dismissed as prejudice and often is. Criticism under special standards is dismissed as rigged. Criticism without construction is dismissed as vandalism, and the dismissal, in each case, is not merely a defensive reflex but a judgement the tradition is entitled to make. Elster's career is the standing demonstration that the entitled dismissals can be denied their grounds, one by one, by a critic willing to do the work, and that a tradition so criticised will keep the critic's tools long after it has finished arguing with his verdicts. The scorecard and the stated scorecard are the same fact viewed from two sides: he published his verdicts as a list because a list is the form in which criticism becomes usable, and the list's survival, in the face of misreporting from both camps, is the evidence that the form works.


## Marx on justice and needs: the normative verdicts

The scorecard's economic and methodological items dominate summaries of Elster's Marx, but his examination of Marx's normative theory, of what Marx thought about justice and about the principles that should govern distribution, contains some of his most quoted and most contested judgements, and a complete profile must report them. The scholarly background is the debate over whether Marx condemned capitalism as unjust. One influential line, associated with Allen Wood, held that Marx did not regard exploitation as an injustice, since justice for Marx was internal to a mode of production, a matter of conformity to its own standards, and by capitalism's standards the wage exchange is a fair trade of equivalents. Another line, associated with Ziyad Husami and developed by Cohen, held that Marx's condemnation of exploitation is unintelligible except as a charge of injustice, whatever his official metaethics said. Elster's treatment characteristically refused the choice as posed. He distinguished the questions the debate had fused: what Marx's texts say about justice as a concept, what normative commitments his condemnations presuppose, and what principles his positive vision implies. On the first, the texts are genuinely ambivalent, praising and deriding the language of justice in different registers and decades. On the second, the condemnation of exploitation, of alienation, of the stunting of capacities, presupposes standards that function as principles of justice whether or not they wear the name. On the third, the distributive principle of the higher phase of communism, to each according to his needs, Elster lodged a series of difficulties that have structured the subsequent discussion: needs are not self announcing, they expand with the means of satisfying them, they conflict across persons and across time, and a principle that distributes by need without a procedure for adjudicating needs hands the adjudication to whoever administers the abundance, a political problem disguised as an economic formula.

The treatment of self realisation belongs to the same normative complex. Elster took seriously, more seriously than most of Marx's economic critics, the ideal of unalienated activity that runs from the early manuscripts to the Grundrisse's vision of free time and the all round development of the individual, and he pressed it with questions that its admirers had avoided. Is self realisation a single ideal or a family of ideals that can conflict, the development of one capacity starving another? Is it plausible as a description of what most people, most of the time, would choose under conditions of freedom, or does it project the values of intellectuals onto populations whose revealed aspirations run to security, leisure and consumption? Does an economy organised to maximise self realising work remain capable of producing the surplus on which the vision floats? The questions were not rhetorical demolitions; they were the auditor's standard procedure applied to the tradition's most cherished ideal, and the ideal, like alienation's anatomy, survived the procedure in a chastened form, as one value among others, to be weighed in institutional design rather than enthroned as history's destination. The episode illustrates a general truth about the scorecard that its tabular form conceals: the grades were delivered by a reader who found Marx's normative vision genuinely attractive and said so, and whose criticisms of it have the special weight that attraction lends to disappointment.

His treatment of Marx's theory of the state belongs to the same pattern of partial certification. Against the instrumentalism of the Manifesto's famous committee phrase, he set the Bonapartism analyses, where the state acquires a genuine autonomy from the class it ultimately serves, balancing among fractions, pursuing its own corporate interests, and ruling through a bureaucracy whose situation and outlook Marx anatomised with a sociologist's patience. The general theory he extracted was modest and fertile: state autonomy is a variable, not a constant, its degree set by the balance of class forces, the cohesion of the ruling class, the state's own organisational interests and the availability of a Bonapartist solution when the political class deadlocks. Stated so, the theory generates examinable hypotheses rather than a slogan, and the subsequent literature on state autonomy, much of it outside Marxism, worked the same ground. The pattern completes the profile's recurring finding. Wherever Marx's writing descends to situated actors and their mechanisms, the audit certifies; wherever it ascends to laws and guarantees, the audit condemns; and the line between the two runs through every topic the tradition contains.

## The uses of formal models: what the games showed

Elster's deployment of game theory inside Marxist debates deserves a fuller accounting than the collective action discussion alone provides, because the models changed the content of several doctrines and not merely their presentation. Consider the problem of class compromise, the durable settlements between organised labour and capital that characterised the social democracies of his own formation. A classical reading treated compromise as a truce in a war whose resumption was guaranteed by the system's laws. The game theoretic treatment recast the settlement as an equilibrium: an arrangement sustained because each side, given the other's expected behaviour and the costs of breakdown, does better by keeping it than by defecting, and vulnerable not to a law of motion but to shifts in the parameters, the discount rates of the players, the credibility of their threats, the availability of outside options, the cohesion of their organisations. The recasting explained what the classical reading could not, the variation among settlements and their differential survival, and it located the points of fragility where history in fact attacked them. Or consider the dynamics of revolution, where threshold and cascade models replaced the image of a class ripening toward action with the image of a distribution of private thresholds for public dissent, in which a small shock can trigger a sequence that astonishes the participants themselves. The models explained the two facts about revolutions that law based theories handled worst: their rarity, since most distributions absorb most shocks, and their suddenness, since a triggered cascade outruns every participant's expectation, including the revolutionaries'.

The methodological moral generalises. A formal model, in Elster's hands, was not a claim that people calculate like the model's agents; it was a device for discovering what a set of assumptions implies, and therefore for discovering which assumptions a cherished conclusion secretly needs. Deployed against Marxist claims, the device repeatedly showed that the conclusions required conditions the tradition had not noticed assuming: particular distributions of thresholds, particular solutions to the second order problem of enforcement, particular stabilities in the parameters of compromise. Deployed in the tradition's defence, the same device showed which claims could be rebuilt on stated conditions and thereby converted from prophecies into hypotheses. The school's critics sometimes charged that the formalism was a foreign body, an economist's toolkit alien to the material. The charge mistakes the instrument's provenance for its function. The device does to an argument what the audit does to a corpus: it itemises the assumptions, and an argument that survives the itemisation is stronger for the survival, whichever tradition issued it.


## Local justice: the allocation laboratory

One body of Elster's work rarely appears in Marxist contexts and yet speaks directly to a question the tradition must answer in practice: when goods are scarce and cannot be distributed by price, by what principles are they in fact allocated, and by what principles should they be? His study of local justice examined the allocation of scarce goods and burdens inside institutions: organs for transplant, places at universities, jobs in layoffs, military service, immigration visas, scarce medical treatments. The findings were a mechanisms theorist's delight and a system builder's warning. Allocation in practice is governed by a plurality of principles, need, desert, seniority, lottery, queue, contribution, status, applied in mixtures that vary by institution and by good, defended by participants in the vocabulary of fairness, and explicable only by descending to the situated reasoning of the allocators, the pressures on them, the visibility of the candidates, the costs of discretion and the fear of scandal. No single principle, and no market, governs the whole field, and the principles that participants endorse in the abstract are routinely traded against one another in the concrete case under pressures the abstract endorsement never mentions.

The relevance to the Marxist inheritance runs in both directions. Against the tradition's distributive slogan, the needs principle, the local justice research poses the operational question Elster had already pressed in theory: needs as assessed by whom, under what pressures, with what visibility effects and what temptations, since the transplant committee and the admissions office are what a needs based allocation looks like when it leaves the manifesto and enters the world. In the tradition's favour, the same research vindicates its suspicion of the price mechanism's imperial claims, since the institutions studied allocate their most morally charged goods by every principle except willingness to pay, and regard the introduction of price, in case after case, as a corruption of the good's meaning rather than as an efficiency. The auditor's tools, once more, cut across the camps. What the research finally supports is his characteristic meta conclusion: distributive questions are settled, well or badly, at the level of institutions and their mechanisms, and a political theory that contents itself with announcing the correct principle for society as a whole has declined the level at which justice is actually done or undone. For a series whose subject is a tradition with large distributive ambitions, the laboratory of local justice is among the most practically consequential stops on the auditor's itinerary.

## The auditor audited: criticisms of the later work

Symmetry requires that the profile record the criticisms directed at Elster's own constructive work, since the scorecard method he normalised has been applied to him by reviewers who learned it partly from him. The mechanisms programme attracted the objection that its patterns, for all their concreteness, are assembled into explanations with a freedom that the programme's own standards should police more strictly: the stock of mechanisms is large, the signatures are often faint, and a skilled practitioner can find a mechanism for any outcome after the fact, which threatens the forensic confidence the programme advertises. His reply, that rival mechanism attributions can be tested against the sequence and distribution of the evidence, is accepted by the programme's practitioners as a discipline and regarded by its sceptics as a promissory note of exactly the kind he taught them to refuse. The exchange is unresolved, and this article records it as the first open item on his own ledger.

The work on rationality attracted a different charge, that its catalogue of pathologies, adaptation, dissonance, wishful thinking, spite, leaves the normative standard underdescribed: if beliefs and desires are formed sub intentionally at every joint, the standpoint from which some formations count as distortions and others as learning needs a defence that the work gestures toward, in the ideal of autonomy and in the agent's own reflective endorsement, without completing. The charge has force against the early statements and less against the mature position, in which endorsement under conditions of information and reflection does the normative work, but the conditions themselves invite the regress the critics press. The constitution work was charged, from the other side, with an excess of motivational realism: assemblies in his accounts argue less and posture more than the historical record's best cases suggest, and the theory of deliberation's defenders have answered with cases in which argument changed outcomes against the distribution of interests. His treatment of the point, distinguishing the forces present in every assembly and estimating their proportions case by case, is the mechanisms programme's standard move, and whether it answers the charge or absorbs it depends on the reader's prior estimate of how much of politics is posture. The profile records the three criticisms without grading them, beyond noting that each was framed in terms his own method supplied, which is the compliment his career was built to earn: the terms of his audit became the terms in which he is audited.

## Legacy: the partition as a permanent possession

The legacy can be stated in the form his own work would prescribe, as an inventory rather than an epitaph. To Marxist theory he left a graded estate: doctrines sorted into the failed and the reconstructed, with the reasons on the record and the appeals process specified, so that subsequent work on value, on crisis, on exploitation and on ideology proceeds in a landscape he surveyed. To social science he left a method in daily use, the demand for mechanisms and the catalogue of patterns that the demand produced, together with a philosophy of explanation, forensic rather than prophetic, that disciplines fields far from the original quarrel. To political theory he left the analysis of adaptive preferences and its unresolved challenge to every doctrine that takes wants as sovereign, together with the studies of precommitment that reframed constitutionalism as the politics of self binding. To the theory of institutions he left the local justice laboratory and the constitution making studies, demonstrations that his instruments work prospectively on the design and diagnosis of arrangements. And to the practice of criticism itself he left the model this article has described: internal, itemised, impartial in its standards and constructive in its intent, the model of an auditor whose verdicts a tradition could not dismiss because they were delivered from inside its own best standards.

The inventory has a final entry, easy to miss because it concerns the readers rather than the doctrines. Generations of students have met Marx first, or met him most seriously, through the frame Elster built: not the prophet of a guaranteed future, not the demon of a totalitarian genealogy, but a diagnostician of genius and a system builder of overreach, whose work rewards the sentence by sentence discrimination that the scorecard performs. That framing, absorbed by readers who never open Making Sense of Marx, may be the deepest of his influences on the tradition, since it determines what the next generation thinks there is in Marx to argue about. The argument continues, on the terms the auditor set, which is the most any critic can ask of posterity and more than most receive.


## Explaining revolutions and collapses: the cascade signature

The cascade family of mechanisms, introduced in the catalogue section, deserves an application at length, because it shows the programme explaining the class of events about which law based theories of history had the most to say and the least success. Consider the collapse of a regime that had appeared monolithic. The classical explanations divide between the structural, which cite the regime's economic failures and lost wars, and the voluntarist, which cite the courage of its opponents. The mechanisms account accepts the structural conditions as the setting and asks the forensic question: by what sequence did private dissent, widely held and widely concealed, become public action? The key pattern is preference falsification, the systematic misrepresentation of private views under social pressure, which produces a society in which nearly everyone may privately reject the order while each believes, from the public evidence, that nearly everyone else supports it. In such a society the regime's apparent support is an artefact of the falsification, and its stability is the stability of a frozen surface: real, until the ice breaks. A triggering event, a demonstration tolerated, a concession misread as weakness, a neighbouring regime's fall, reveals to a few that their dissent is shared; their action reveals it to more; the thresholds cascade; and the regime discovers, in days, that its support was a collective misperception that its own repression had manufactured. The pattern explains the signature features of such collapses that structural accounts leave mysterious: the astonishment of all parties, the speed, the retrospective unanimity with which former supporters describe their lifelong private opposition, and the inability of the regime's own intelligence services, reading the same falsified public evidence, to foresee the end.

Elster's interest in these dynamics was longstanding and connected his earliest work on collective action to his later studies of transitions and transitional justice. The Marxist stake is plain. The classical theory of revolution imagined a class becoming conscious of itself through struggle and acting on a ripeness produced by economic law. The cascade account substitutes a picture in which consciousness is partly an artefact of what can be said, ripeness is a distribution of thresholds rather than a stage of production, and the revolutionary moment is a revelation cascade whose timing no law fixes. The substitution does not make revolutions unintelligible or accidental; it makes them explicable forensically and unpredictable in principle, which the historical record, a graveyard of confident predictions from every camp, suggests is the truth of the matter. A tradition that absorbed the substitution would trade its prophecies for a research programme: map the falsification, estimate the thresholds, identify the triggers, and understand why the same structural crisis cascades in one country and freezes in another. The trade is the scorecard's trade, offered once more at the level of the tradition's most cherished event.

## Counterfactuals and the discipline of what might have been

Mechanism explanation leans on counterfactuals, on claims about what would have happened had a pattern not been triggered or a condition differed, and Elster's methodological writings give the dependence a disciplined treatment that a profile should record, since it separates his practice from the loose counterfactualising of popular history. A counterfactual earns its place when the mechanism it invokes is independently evidenced and when the alteration it imagines is small and specific: not what if the revolution had failed, in the large, but whether the demonstration's dispersal on a particular evening would have interrupted the cascade whose later steps are documented. The discipline connects to the defeater practice this cluster of the series has adopted. A verdict that states what would reverse it is a counterfactual claim about the evidence, and Elster's insistence that conclusions carry their conditions is the same insistence, applied to scholarship rather than to history. The Marxist tradition's counterfactual habits were, on his reading, among its least disciplined: the missed revolutions and betrayed moments of its historiography were routinely explained by the failures of leaders, in a register that treated the alternative outcome as available for the choosing, without specifying the mechanism by which different choices, by the situated and divided actors the record shows, would have produced it. His own histories of assemblies and transitions model the stricter practice, altering one circumstance at a time and following the patterns through, and the practice has become a standard against which counterfactual claims in the literature are graded, usually without his name being mentioned, which is the standard's success rather than its failure.


## The critique of historicism: tendencies and their abuse

Running through the scorecard's failures is a single intellectual habit that Elster's work names in several vocabularies, and gathering it in one place clarifies why doctrines as different as the profit rate law and dialectical materialism received the same grade. The habit is the conversion of a tendency into a destiny. A tendency claim, honestly stated, identifies a pressure: competition presses toward concentration, accumulation presses on profitability, mechanisation presses on the demand for labour. Pressures are real and mechanisms can be specified for them. The abuse begins when the pressure is promoted, silently, into the outcome: when counter pressures, which the same theory can usually name, are demoted to friction, and the tendency is stated as what will happen, with the timing left vague enough that no event can refute it. A claim so armoured has left science for prophecy, and the armour is the tell. Elster's examinations repeatedly perform the same operation on such claims: restore the counter tendencies to equal standing, ask what determines the net direction, and record that the theory, so restored, predicts a contest rather than a conclusion. The falling rate of profit is the paradigm case, but the operation applies to the immiseration thesis, to the concentration thesis, to the polarisation of classes into two great camps, and to the philosophy of history's master tendency, the development of the productive forces as the engine of the whole sequence. In each case something survives the operation, the pressure and its mechanism, and something does not, the guarantee.

The habit's persistence, inside and outside Marxism, is itself a mechanisms question, and his psychology supplies the answer. A tendency promoted to a destiny gratifies several patterns at once: wishful thinking, where the destiny is desired; the narrative appetite, which prefers a story with a direction to a contest with an open end; and the strategic needs of movements, for whom a guaranteed future is a recruiting instrument and a consolation in defeat. A theory that serves those patterns will be believed beyond its evidence by the very processes the theory of belief formation catalogues, and the critic who would correct it must therefore supply more than refutations; he must supply the alternative satisfactions, the forensic pleasures of the traced mechanism, the dignity of a case argued rather than inherited. Whether Elster's work supplies those satisfactions is a question his readership answers by existing. The school he helped found was, among other things, an attempt to build a Marxist culture in which the satisfactions of rigour outbid the satisfactions of destiny, and its partial success, visible in the literature's changed habits, is the measure of the attempt.

## What a reader should argue with: the live disputes

A profile in this series is not a monument, and it serves its readers best by handing them the disputes in a form they can enter. Four disputes around Elster remain live, and the article's positions on them have been stated with their defeaters; gathering them here equips the reader to take sides knowingly. The first is the standing of functional explanation: whether consequence claims can be warranted before their mechanisms are produced, as Cohen's Darwinian analogy urged, or whether the mechanism clause is a condition of explanatory respectability, as Elster insisted. The second is the reach of methodological individualism: whether the completeness doctrine, in its strong form, excludes the formation of agents and thereby the tradition's deepest claim, or whether the mechanisms practice, beginning from positions as readily as from preferences, dissolves the exclusion. The third is the foundation of exploitation: whether the distributive refoundation clarifies the charge or evacuates it, relocating to alienation and authority the material about production that the classical theory carried in a single concept. The fourth is the fate of the philosophy of history: whether the compatibility constraint that survives the audit, relations must fit the forces they house, is a residue too weak to organise research, or the honest core around which a mechanism based historiography can be built. On each dispute the article has argued a side and named the evidence that would change its mind, which is the form in which the disputes are most usefully inherited. A reader who finishes the profile able to state both sides of any one of them, and the defeater that separates the sides, has received what the stated scorecard's author spent a career teaching his readers to demand: not an attitude toward Marx, or toward Elster, but an itemised map of where the arguments stand and what would move them.


## Bargaining, constitutions and the anatomy of agreement

Among the later applications of his instruments, the study of bargaining and agreement deserves a place in this profile, because it displays the mechanisms method working on the most ordinary and most consequential of social processes: how parties with conflicting interests reach terms, and why the terms they reach bear the marks of the process rather than of any principle of justice. Elster's analyses of bargaining distinguished the forces that share the work in any negotiation. Interest supplies the parties' fallback positions and their estimates of advantage. Argument, genuine reasoning about fairness and precedent, operates alongside interest and is not reducible to its mask, since parties can be persuaded, can be shamed by the inconsistency of their own positions, and can trap themselves with principles they invoked for tactical reasons and then cannot disown. Passion enters as impatience, as pride, as the desire not to be seen yielding, and it shortens or wrecks negotiations whose interest structure promised agreement. The proportions vary, and the variation is the explanation: two disputes with identical stakes end differently because the mixture differed, because a deadline pressed in one and not the other, because publicity hardened positions in one chamber and secrecy loosened them in another.

The constituent assemblies studied in his work on constitution making are bargaining under special conditions, and the distinctions he drew there, between arguing and bargaining as modes of speech, between the civilising force of hypocrisy, which compels parties to dress interest in the language of principle and thereby gives principle a grip on outcomes, and the corrupting force of the same hypocrisy when the dressing is all, have entered the general literature on deliberation. The Marxist reader will recognise an old theme in a new key. The classical tradition held that legal and constitutional forms express class power, and the expression thesis, graded by his standards, is a functional claim owed its mechanisms. Supplied with the bargaining analysis, the thesis becomes examinable: class power enters the chamber as the distribution of fallback positions, of patience, of organisation and of credible threats, and the resulting text is the trace of those asymmetries passing through procedures and passions. Two societies with similar class structures produce different constitutions because the passage differed, and a theory that can say why has earned more than a theory that redescribes the outcome as expression. The point is the profile's point, made once more on fresh material: the audit never denied the tradition's large claims their subject matter; it denied them their shortcuts.

## Fear, hope and the emotional economy of movements

The mechanisms of political mobilisation form another region where his catalogue intersects the tradition's concerns, and a section on the emotional economy of movements rounds out the profile's survey of the intersections. Movements live on emotions that the classical theory treated as epiphenomenal foam on structural tides. Indignation is the movement's fuel and its compass: the perception of injustice, felt as anger rather than registered as a proposition, is what converts a grievance into a claim and a bystander into a participant, and its distribution across a population predicts mobilisation better than the distribution of objective deprivation, since deprivation adapted to is deprivation unfelt. Hope is the movement's scarcest resource and its most managed one: leaders calibrate promises to keep hope above the threshold of action and below the threshold of disappointment, and the calibration is a mechanism problem, involving the credibility of the promisers and the memory of previous disappointments stored in the community's stock of stories. Shame polices the movement's boundaries, raising the cost of defection and of free riding beyond anything the selective incentives of the textbooks supply, and its anticipation explains the otherwise puzzling sacrifices of participants who stood to gain nothing measurable. Fear, finally, is the counter movement's chief instrument and the regime's: it raises thresholds, shortens horizons and makes the private falsification of preference the rational course, which is why the cascade, when it comes, surprises the fearful most of all.

Elster's contribution to this region was not a theory of movements; it was the insistence, backed by the catalogue, that the emotions named here are causal machinery with signatures, not colour commentary on a process driven elsewhere. A mobilisation explained by indignation must show the appraisal patterns that indignation implies, the attribution of blame to agents rather than to fate, the approach tendencies and the willingness to pay costs that distinguish anger from resignation. A demobilisation explained by adaptation must show the contraction of wants rather than their suppression, since the two predict different futures when conditions loosen: suppressed wants return at the first opportunity, contracted wants must be regrown. The distinctions are the difference between a historiography of movements that can be wrong and one that cannot, and they descend directly from the methodological chapters of the Marx book. The tradition that learns them writes better history, whatever it concludes about the tradition's laws, and several of its best recent practitioners, working on labour movements and on the collapses of regimes, write in a manner the auditor would recognise as his own discipline naturalised.


## The ethics of belief: what the auditor owed the dead

There is a dimension of Elster's Marx book that methodological discussion tends to miss, and a profile attentive to the whole performance should name it before closing: the book is an exercise in the ethics of belief, in what a reader owes a dead thinker. The debts, as the book discharges them, are specific. A critic owes the text its strongest reading, and the reconstructions in Making Sense of Marx are frequently clearer and more defensible than the passages they reconstruct, so that Marx is refuted, where he is refuted, at his best rather than at his worst. A critic owes the corpus its whole extent, the journalism and the notebooks admitted as evidence alongside the canonical volumes, so that the verdict falls on the work and not on a selection arranged to convict or to acquit. A critic owes the dead the same standards he applies to the living, neither the indulgence of heritage nor the cruelty of anachronism: the questions put to Marx are questions Marx's own best work asks of others. And a critic owes the reader the verdict in a form that can be checked, itemised, with the reasons exposed, so that the debt of scrutiny is passed on rather than discharged by authority. These obligations are not decorations on the method; they are the method, considered as a moral practice, and they explain the book's peculiar authority among readers who reject its conclusions. A Marxist scholar can trust the audit's fairness while contesting every grade, because the fairness is procedural and visible, and trust of that kind is the scarcest currency in polemical fields.

The contrast with the two standard genres of Marx criticism is instructive. The hostile genre, the genealogy that reads the work backward from the regimes that claimed it, violates the second debt, selecting its evidence from the posterity rather than the corpus. The apologetic genre, which answers every criticism by deepening the interpretation until the text agrees with its defender, violates the first and fourth debts, refusing the strongest reading's discipline and the checkable verdict's exposure. Elster's book stands outside both genres, and its outsider status is not neutrality, since its grades are severe, but a third genre for which the tradition has no settled name: the audit, criticism as a fiduciary act, performed on behalf of readers who deserve to know what the estate contains. That the genre has few practitioners is a fact about its costs. It requires the critic to master the material he condemns, to state the standards before applying them, and to publish the partition in a form his opponents can use. The stated scorecard is the genre's emblem, and this article, in reproducing it with its defeaters attached, has attempted to practise the genre on its inventor.

## A closing inventory of the argument

The profile has ranged across a large bibliography and a longer set of debates, and its closing movement should gather the threads in the manner the subject himself preferred, as an inventory of findings rather than as a peroration. The audit of Marx produced a partition: the nomological core failed, the diagnostic and normative anatomy survived in reconstructed form, and the partition has worn well against the subsequent literature's piecemeal verdicts. The method that produced the partition, explanation by mechanisms under a discipline of microfoundations, escaped its birthplace and reorganised research in sociology and among philosophers of social science. The psychology that the method required, of adapted preferences, bent beliefs, divided selves and gripping norms, supplied the missing machinery for the theory of ideology and a standing challenge to every doctrine that treats wants as sovereign. The objections to the method are real and have been recorded at strength: the doctrine in its strong form assumes the formed individual it should explain, and the article's verdict, that the mature programme sits comfortably beside structural explanation, since its mechanisms may begin from positions, stands with its defeater named. The man himself declines the labels the literature keeps offering, neither the apostate of hostile summaries nor the loyalist of sympathetic ones, but a founding member of a school who graded its inheritance, kept what passed, and spent the rest of a long career building instruments that other fields adopted. The scorecard, printed plainly and argued row by row, remains the shortest accurate route into all of it, and the reader who has followed this profile to its end possesses the scorecard, the reasons beneath it, and the map of the disputes it left open.


## Work on the self: akrasia, addiction and the divided agent

The divided self, introduced earlier through the study of precommitment, generated a body of work on weakness of will and addiction that a full profile should register, because it shows the auditor's psychology operating where the stakes are intimate rather than institutional. Weakness of will, the ancient puzzle of akrasia, asks how an agent can judge an action best and yet do otherwise, and the standard rational choice framework has no honest room for the phenomenon, since the framework reads choice as the revelation of judgement. Elster's treatment populated the space between judgement and act with machinery: visceral factors that seize the motivational system on short timescales, the discounting of future goods that steepens as they approach, the cue triggered cravings that bypass deliberation entirely, and the strategies, avoidance of cues, public pledges, self imposed penalties, pharmacological and social bindings, by which the planning self wages its war against the tempted self. Addiction, on this account, is not a preference like others, revealed by consumption and therefore sovereign, but a condition in which the preference forming machinery has been captured by a substance or a practice, and the addict's own testimony, divided against itself across the hours of the day, is evidence of the capture rather than a puzzle to be modelled away.

The bearing on the themes of this series is oblique but genuine. A tradition whose theory of emancipation imagines agents throwing off their illusions and acting on their interests needs a psychology of why agents cling to what harms them, in the factory and in the bottle alike, and the work on akrasia and addiction supplies a disciplined version of that psychology, one that refuses both the moralism that reads clinging as vice and the structuralism that reads it as fate. The agent of these studies is neither sovereign nor puppet; he is a committee with poor internal communications, some of whose members can be strengthened by arrangements, by commitments entered in cool hours, by environments designed to starve the cues. Institutional design, in the late work on constitutions and in the intimate work on addiction, turns out to be the same art at different scales: the art of helping divided beings bind themselves well. The Ulysses figure presides over both, and the continuity is the strongest internal evidence that the bibliography, for all its range, is one work.

## On explaining by stories: narrative and its limits

A further methodological theme, present across his writings on explanation, concerns narrative, the form in which human beings most naturally render sequences intelligible, and its uneasy relation to the mechanism standard. Narratives select, connect and emplot; they confer meaning by arrangement; and they are indispensable to history, whose materials arrive as traces of sequences. Elster's position was characteristically double. Against the covering law orthodoxy he defended the explanatory dignity of the well constructed narrative, since a narrative that traces a mechanism, step by step, through documented decisions and reactions, is an explanation and not a mere chronicle. Against the literary turn in historiography he insisted that narrative form is also a solvent of rigour, because the story's demand for shape, for beginnings that foreshadow and endings that conclude, systematically tempts the teller to smooth the contingencies, to read the outcome back into the origins, and to supply connections the evidence does not contain. The discipline he proposed is the one this article has practised on him: the narrative may carry the explanation, but every load bearing connection in it must be a mechanism that could, in principle, be checked, and the teller owes the reader the points at which the record thins and the pattern is conjectured. Marx's histories pass that discipline at their best and fail it at their prophetic worst, which is, for the last time in this profile, the scorecard's partition wearing yet another of its costumes.

## The question of progress in social theory

Behind the audit stands a question about the enterprise itself: does social theory progress, and if so, by what increments? The scorecard implies an answer that the mechanisms programme makes explicit. Progress in social theory is not the accumulation of laws, since the material refuses them, and it is not the succession of paradigms, each burying its predecessor's questions. It is the growth of the stock of well understood mechanisms, the refinement of their signatures, the mapping of their triggering conditions and their interactions, together with the corresponding retirement of claims that the enlarged stock shows to be empty. By that measure Elster's career registers progress in a form that can be pointed to: patterns that were metaphors in the tradition he audited, ideology's grip, class formation's failures, adaptation's quiet work, became, in his hands and in the hands of those who took up his tools, mechanisms with names, signatures and literatures. A reader who asks what the analytical Marxist episode contributed to social science, beyond its verdicts on Marx, can be answered in those terms. It contributed a demonstration, conducted on the hardest available material, that the demand for mechanisms is not a counsel of perfection but a working discipline, and that a tradition subjected to it emerges smaller in its claims and larger in its knowledge. The stated scorecard is the demonstration's record, and the record, as this profile has argued from its first page, deserves to be read as it was written: item by item, with the reasons attached, and with the failures and the survivals given equal weight in the telling.


## Reading Marx after the audit: a changed practice

The most durable effect of a consequential audit is not on the doctrines it grades but on the reading habits of those who come after, and Elster's effect on the practice of reading Marx can be specified concretely enough to close this profile's exposition. A reader formed by the audit reads for mechanisms first. Where the older habits of reading asked what a passage's place was in the system, or which camp it comforted, the reformed habit asks what process the passage describes, through whose situated action the process runs, and what would count as the process failing to appear where the passage predicts it. The habit transforms the experience of the canonical texts. The first volume of Capital becomes, in large stretches, a mechanisms treatise of extraordinary density, its chapters on the working day, on cooperation, on machinery, tracing the pressures on identifiable actors and the patterns those pressures generate, while the value theoretic scaffolding through which the treatise announces itself recedes into the background the audit assigned it. The Grundrisse becomes a quarry of patterns, brilliant and half finished, rather than a scripture whose obscurities are promises. The political writings become the centre of the corpus rather than its journalism, since they are where the method the auditor preached is practised by its original at full stretch.

The habit also changes what readers do with their disagreements. Under the older dispensation, to reject a doctrine was to defect, and to defend it was to belong; the audit's itemisation breaks the linkage, and a reader may hold, without strain or scandal, that the value theory fails and the alienation anatomy stands, that the profit rate is a possibility and the innovation mechanism a permanent acquisition. Positions of that mixed shape were always held in private by careful readers; the audit made them sayable in public, with the reasons priced and displayed. Whether the tradition is healthier for the change is a question its members dispute, some mourning the lost wholeness of a doctrine that could be embraced entire, others replying that a wholeness maintained by forbidding the itemised question was the wholeness of a creed rather than of a theory. The profile has taken its side throughout, and states it once more for the record: a theory that can only be held entire will, in the end, be held by fewer and fewer, whereas a theory whose parts are separately graded can lose the parts that deserve losing and keep the allegiance of readers who would have discarded the whole. The scorecard, on that argument, was not the tradition's obituary but its salvage operation, and the salvage is still being unloaded, crate by crate, into the disciplines that received the tools.

## The seminar as an institution: what the September Group proved

One institutional lesson of the episode merits a section of its own, because it generalises beyond Marxism to every field in which a small group attempts serious collective work. The September Group functioned, for roughly two decades, as a device for raising the cost of vagueness among its members. Its procedures were simple and severe: drafts circulated in advance, criticism in person, no deference to reputation, and the standing expectation that a claim would be restated until its meaning and its evidence were both on the table. The device worked because its members shared enough to argue productively, a corpus, a set of questions, a respect for the founding wager, while differing enough, in discipline and in temperament, that no member's characteristic blindness went uncorrected by another's characteristic sight. Cohen's historical imagination checked Elster's methodological severity; Roemer's formalism checked both men's taste for the qualitative verdict; Wright's empiricism checked the group's collective tendency to mistake a model's coherence for the world's. The school's output, measured in books that reorganised their subfields, is the evidence that the device worked, and its eventual dispersal, as the members' questions diverged beyond the reach of a single seminar, is not a refutation but the device's natural lifespan: a seminar is a machine for a stage of inquiry, and it honours its members most when they outgrow it in different directions.

For readers of this series, the institutional lesson has a practical corollary. The cluster of articles surrounding this profile, on Cohen's defence, on Roemer's reconstruction, on the microfoundations debate, on the school's critics and on the school entire, reconstructs in prose the argument that the seminar conducted in person, and a reader who works through the cluster in sequence receives something like the seminar's education at second hand: the same claims, graded by the same standards, from positions the participants themselves occupied. That is the deepest justification for the series' cluster design, and for this profile's place within it. Elster cannot be understood as a solitary verdict machine; he was one voice in a conversation whose other voices answer him at every point, and the profile has tried, by its pattern of links and its distribution of emphasis, to keep the conversation audible beneath the portrait.

## Final word on the stated scorecard

The article opened with a stopwatch and a call for receipts, and it closes with the inventory those instruments produced. A Norwegian philosopher joined a seminar of Marxists to rebuild their inheritance and stayed to grade it. The grades were published as a list, and the list has outlasted the polemics of both camps because it can be used by readers who share neither camp's attitude. Beneath the grades stood a method, the demand that explanations exhibit their mechanisms, and the method walked out of the quarrel into the general practice of social science, where it works in the hands of scholars who owe the tradition nothing. Beside the method stood a psychology, of wants that adapt and beliefs that bend and selves that divide, and the psychology completes, among its other services, the theory of ideology that the tradition had left with a gap at its centre. The objections stand on the record at full strength, and the verdicts of this profile stand beside them with their defeaters attached, in the form the subject himself taught his readers to require. What survives of Marx, on the most rigorous internal examination the tradition has received, is a diagnostic anatomy of capitalism, a mechanism of its innovation, a refounded charge of exploitation and a programme for the study of class and politics. What survives of Elster is the examination itself, and the instruments it normalised. The two survivals are entangled past separation, which is why this profile, in a series devoted to Marxism, gives one of its longest entries to the school's auditor: the tradition is, in part, what its auditor found in it, and the finding is not finished yet.


## Justice in the small: what allocation studies teach the large theory

The local justice research touched earlier in this profile rewards one more pass, because its findings bear on a question that large distributive theories habitually skip: what happens to a principle when it is handed to a committee? Elster's field studies of allocation, in hospitals, universities, armies and workplaces, uncovered a regularity that no armchair theory of justice predicts. Allocators confronted with a scarce good and a queue of claimants rarely apply a single criterion with a clean conscience. They blend. A transplant list weights medical urgency against waiting time against the probability of success; a redundancy programme weights seniority against need against the firm's survival; an admissions process weights merit against diversity against the claims of legacy and locality. The blends are not hypocrisy, though hypocrisy is sometimes present. They are the visible form of a deeper fact: the values at stake in allocation are plural, they do not reduce to a common coin, and the people charged with deciding know it in their hands even when their official vocabulary denies it. Where a single criterion is imposed by rule, the allocators characteristically smuggle the excluded values back through the rule's interstices, reclassifying cases, adjusting thresholds, exercising the discretion every rule secretly contains.

For the tradition this series examines, the lesson cuts with both edges, as his lessons tend to do. Against the market's imperial party, the studies show entire provinces of social life where price is regarded, by nearly everyone involved, as a contamination of the good being allocated, and where the refusal of price is not inefficiency but the community's considered judgement about what kind of good an organ, a place or an exemption is. Against the tradition's own distributive maximalism, the studies show that the needs principle, when it descends from the manifesto into the committee room, immediately acquires companions, rivals and qualifications, and that the resulting blends are not betrayals of the principle but the principle's only possible mode of existence among plural values and situated deciders. A political theory that wants to govern allocation must therefore do its work at the level the studies occupy: the design of procedures, the composition of committees, the visibility of criteria, the avenues of appeal. Elster's own normative sympathies in this region ran toward arrangements that make the blends explicit and arguable, rather than toward any formula that promises to spare deciders the burden of judgement. The preference is of a piece with everything else in the profile. Wherever a large word, justice, history, the market, the plan, threatens to do the work that only situated judgement can do, his instinct is to descend, to find the room where the decision happens, and to describe what the people in the room actually weigh.

## The uses of failure: a methodological postscript

It is fitting to end the exposition with the category that organises the scorecard's negative side, since failure, in Elster's handling, is not a disgrace but a datum, and the tradition he audited has more to learn from his treatment of failure than from any particular verdict. A doctrine fails, in his examinations, in distinguishable ways, and the ways matter for what should happen next. A doctrine can fail by incoherence, its derivations requiring what they cannot have, as with the reduction problem inside value theory; the remedy is abandonment of the derivation, though the phenomena it aimed at remain for other theories to explain. A doctrine can fail by redundancy, explaining nothing that leaner rivals do not explain, as with the same theory's claim on prices; the remedy is parsimony's quiet deletion. A doctrine can fail by elasticity, protecting itself against every counter instance until it forbids nothing and therefore asserts nothing, as with dialectics in its legalistic form; the remedy is to demand the risky statement and watch whether the doctrine's holders will sign it. A doctrine can fail by missing mechanism, asserting a connection whose middle is absent, as with the functional claims; the remedy is not abandonment but a promissory discipline, the claim held in suspense until the middle is produced. And a doctrine can fail by overreach, promoting a genuine pattern into a law of the whole, as with the profit rate tendency; the remedy is demotion, the pattern kept and the guarantee surrendered. Five failures, five different remedies, and a literature that speaks of Marx refuted or Marx vindicated, in the singular, has simply not begun the work the scorecard performs.

The postscript applies to the auditor as it applied to the audited, and the profile has kept the application in view throughout: his strong doctrine of method overreached, and the remedy administered by his critics and by his own later practice was demotion, the doctrine softened into a demand; his forensic confidence in mechanism identification carries a promissory element, held in suspense against the criteria its defenders still owe; his normative standard for distorted preferences remains, in the judgement recorded earlier, incompletely defended against the regress his critics press. None of this diminishes the achievement, because the achievement was never a system exempt from its own standards. It was a way of working in which every claim, including the claim to be working well, is itemised, evidenced and left open to a named reversal. A series that has followed that way of working through twenty articles of audits, scorecards and defeaters can close this profile with the observation that the way itself is the inheritance, and that it passes, like the mechanisms it studies, to whoever finds it useful, regardless of the flag under which they work.


## Elster among the economists: a boundary dispute worth keeping

One further relationship rounds out the map of his intellectual position, his long and prickly engagement with economics as a discipline. He was formed partly inside it, used its formal instruments with more fluency than most of its practitioners, and yet became one of its most persistent internal critics, and the combination mirrors, almost exactly, his relation to Marxism. The pattern is worth stating because it confirms the profile's central claim about the man: his loyalty was never to a camp but to a standard, and camps that housed him, Marxist seminar or economics department, received the same treatment once their habitual protections came into view. What he valued in economics was its discipline of consequence: a model forces its builder to follow assumptions to their ends, and the ends frequently embarrass the assumptions in ways that purely verbal theorising conceals. What he resisted was the discipline's imperial mood, its periodic conviction that the rational actor model, thinly specified, could serve as a general theory of human conduct, and its corresponding habit of treating every departure from the model, adaptation, emotion, norm governed sacrifice, as an anomaly to be annexed by a footnote rather than as evidence about the model's jurisdiction.

His own work can be read as a decades long negotiation of that boundary. The game theoretic reconstructions of Marxist claims accepted the economist's instruments and showed, from inside, the conditions those instruments revealed and the classical conclusions they refused to underwrite. The studies of preference formation and of the visceral factors then redrew the map of what an adequate model of the actor would have to contain, and the redrawn map was taken up, selectively and often without acknowledgement of its source, by the behavioural research programme that economics eventually naturalised. The later writings on explanation stepped back from the boundary dispute altogether and addressed both disciplines from the standpoint of the mechanisms programme, in which the economist's models appear as one valuable species of mechanism specification, exact where their assumptions hold and mute where the patterns that matter run through shame, adaptation or cascade rather than through optimisation. Economists who read him tended to divide into those who found the criticism bracing and those who found it an outsider's misunderstanding, and the division reproduces, with the names changed, the division among Marxists who read Making Sense of Marx. The recurrence is the point. A critic whose standards are genuinely his own will be received identically by every establishment he examines, welcomed for the rigour and resented for the grades, and the uniformity of the reception across two such different establishments is among the strongest evidences that the standards, rather than any allegiance, were doing the work.

## What remains open: a last register of debts

The profile closes its exposition by registering, in one place, the questions that the whole body of work leaves genuinely unsettled, since a reader is better served by an honest register of debts than by a valedictory glow. Within the Marxist inheritance, the foundation of exploitation remains contested ground: the distributive refoundation has the field's centre, yet the suspicion persists, among philosophers who accept much of the audit, that something about domination inside production escapes the asset based account and awaits a formulation that neither restores the value theory nor flattens the workplace into a distribution. The standing of functional claims in evolutionary and institutional explanation remains contested in the other direction, with sophisticated defences of consequence explanation, armed with selection mechanisms of their own, pressing the boundary that the critique drew. Within the mechanisms programme, the criteria for earning a mechanism attribution, the aggregation step from action to outcome, and the systematic place of structural positions inside specifications all remain under construction in the literatures that inherited them. Within the psychology, the normative standard by which some preference formations count as distortions and others as maturation still awaits the defence that the critics of the autonomy literature have demanded. And overarching the particular debts stands the question the scorecard posed to every grand theory and could not itself answer: whether a social science rebuilt from mechanisms can recover, in some chastened form, the orientation toward the whole that the discarded philosophies of history supplied, or whether the forensic temper is permanently purchased at the price of that orientation. Elster's own practice suggests a wager rather than an answer, that orientation honestly lost is preferable to orientation falsely kept, and that a tradition which learns to work without guarantees may discover, in the discipline of the traced mechanism and the itemised verdict, a steadier ground for its hopes than prophecy ever provided. The wager is the final item on the stated scorecard, the one its author placed on himself, and it remains, fittingly, unsettled.


## A note on method for the general reader

Readers who have followed the argument thus far may wonder how much of the apparatus they are expected to carry into their own reading of social questions, and the honest reply is that the portable part is small enough to fit into a pocket. When a writer explains an institution by the good it does, ask for the loop that connects the benefit back to the institution's survival, and notice how rarely the loop is drawn. When a writer invokes a collective actor, a class, a market, a nation, performing some deed, ask which flesh and blood persons, placed as they were placed, did the deed, and what they stood to gain or fear. When a commentator reports that a population wants the arrangement under which it lives, ask how those wants were shaped, and whether the shaping is part of what needs judging. When a forecast dresses a pressure as a destiny, ask for the counter pressures and for the evidence that the balance must tip one way. Four questions, no equations, and a reader armed with them will find a surprising fraction of public argument dissolving into its components, some of which survive the dissolution and deserve to, and many of which were only ever cadence. That portability was always the point of the enterprise this profile has chronicled. The seminar in which the questions were sharpened met for the last time long ago; the books sit on shelves in departments whose students arrive with other quarrels; the doctrines graded in these pages continue their slow migration between refutation, reconstruction and neglect. The questions travel lighter than any of it. They can be asked by a school pupil of a textbook, by a citizen of a broadcast, by a researcher of her own draft, and each asking performs, in miniature, the service that the auditor performed for a tradition: it separates what an argument has earned from what it has merely inherited, and it does so without needing permission from either camp. A profile commissioned to report what one critic concluded about Marx ends, appropriately, by handing its readers the instrument rather than the conclusions, since the instrument was always the more durable bequest, and since the conclusions, as their author insisted on every page, are worth exactly as much as the arguments a reader can reproduce for them.


## Influence measured honestly: citations, syllabuses and habits

Claims about a thinker's influence are themselves explanations, and they deserve the discipline this article has applied elsewhere: name the channel, show the trace, resist the functional shortcut of inferring influence from merit. By the channel of citation, the record is unusually legible. The mechanisms writings are referenced across philosophy, sociology, political science and economics, by scholars who frequently disagree with one another about everything except the usefulness of the category. The preference formation analysis anchors a literature in political theory and development ethics whose contributors argue about autonomy using distinctions drawn in Sour Grapes. The collective action treatments appear wherever Olson's problem is taught, as the standard Marxist inflected development of it. By the channel of the syllabus, the traces are institutional: the functional explanation exchange is a fixture of methods courses, the scorecard's partition organises textbook presentations of Marx's fate, and the September Group's books anchor reading lists on analytical Marxism in universities on several continents. By the channel of habit, the hardest to document and the most consequential, the influence consists in questions that have become reflexes for working researchers who may never have opened his books: the demand for the intervening steps, the suspicion of benefit offered as cause, the reflex of asking after the formation of the preferences a study treats as given. Habits of that kind are transmitted through teachers, referees and editors rather than through footnotes, and their presence in a field's practice is the deepest form an intellectual influence can take, since it survives the forgetting of its source. Measured by these channels, with the traces distinguished, the assessment this profile has defended throughout stands up: the verdicts on Marx influenced a tradition, the tools influenced several disciplines, and the habits influenced a generation's sense of what an explanation is. Each claim names its channel and its evidence, and each would be weakened by the counter evidence its defeater specifies, which is the form in which claims about influence, like every other claim in these pages, ought to be left with the reader.


## The profile's debts acknowledged

Scholarly fairness requires a closing acknowledgment of the vantage from which this profile has been written, since no audit stands outside the conditions it describes. The article belongs to a series whose organising device, the cluster, places its subject among companions and rivals, and that placement has shaped the portrait: Elster appears here principally as Marx's auditor and the school's methodologist, while the parts of the bibliography that face away from Marx, toward the theory of constitutions, the reckoning with fallen regimes, the allocation of scarce goods in hospitals and universities, enter only where they illuminate the central relationship. A profile written for a journal of political theory would redistribute the emphasis, dwelling longer on precommitment and on the ethics of belief, and would treat the Marx years as an episode rather than as the core. Neither distribution is the true one; each is a selection answering to a purpose, and the reader is owed the admission so that the selection is not mistaken for the life. What this profile claims for its selection is only that the Marx years contain the man's most consequential single act of criticism and the seed of everything he later built, and that a series devoted to the tradition he audited is the right place for the audit to be reconstructed at length. The reconstruction stands complete: the verdicts itemised, the method traced to its destinations, the great objection weighed and partly conceded, the unmade connection made, and the defeaters left standing beside every judgement of consequence, in the manner the subject himself required of any verdict that asks to be trusted.


## Envoi: the uses of an auditor

Every tradition gets the critics it can survive, and the measure of a tradition's health is whether it can keep its most damaging critic inside the conversation long enough for the damage to become instruction. Marxism, in the episode this series chronicles, managed that feat once, in a seminar room, with a Norwegian philosopher who refused every shortcut his hosts and his enemies offered him. He would not call the inheritance worthless, because parts of it demonstrably were not. He would not call it sound, because parts of it demonstrably were not. He graded it, published the grades, built the alternative his grading implied, and watched the alternative emigrate to disciplines that had never heard the original quarrel. The articles surrounding this one in the cluster tell the rest of the school's story, the defences and reconstructions and empirical programmes that answered, absorbed or resisted him. His own story, as this profile has assembled it, is the story of a standard applied without fear or favour until it produced a partition that neither camp wanted and both camps needed. Readers who take nothing else from these pages should take the partition itself, nine rows long, with its reasons attached and its defeaters standing by, because a partition honestly drawn is worth more than a verdict loudly proclaimed, and because the habit of drawing partitions, of Marx, of Elster, of any doctrine that asks for allegiance, is the one inheritance from this episode that no subsequent audit can devalue.


## A practical postscript for the workplace reader

Abstract method talk can leave a reader wondering whether any of it survives contact with an ordinary Tuesday, so this profile ends its survey where its subject's categories touch daily working life most directly. A manager puzzling over why a reorganisation breeds quiet sabotage rather than open protest is looking at preference falsification inside a firm: private misgivings, publicly unanimous enthusiasm, and a leadership reading its own staged consensus as information. A negotiator watching a counterpart reject an offer that plainly beats the alternative is meeting spite and fairness norms in their commercial dress, where the wish to punish a perceived insult outbids the arithmetic of gain. A policy team baffled when a benefit goes unclaimed by the households it was designed for is colliding with adaptation and with the friction of forms, queues and stigma, the small change of institutional life that decides whether a formal entitlement becomes a lived one. A union organiser whose strongest meeting follows a defeat rather than a victory is observing dissonance at work, sacrifice deepening commitment in defiance of the ledger. None of these vignettes requires its reader to hold any view about Marx whatsoever. Each becomes more legible, and its likely sequel easier to guess, when the patterns catalogued in these pages are brought to bear, and that legibility, purchased without metaphysics, is the most democratic of the auditor's legacies. The seminar's questions turned out to be street legal. They fit in a pocket, they cost nothing to ask, and they repay the asking in settings their inventor never visited, which is the final sense in which the tools outran the quarrel that forged them.

There is a humility built into that portability. Instruments that anyone may pick up belong to no priesthood, certify no membership, and flatter no orthodoxy, which is precisely why establishments of every stripe find them mildly uncomfortable and quietly indispensable at once. The auditor asked for no disciples. He left a checklist, a catalogue and a worked example, and the example, a great tradition weighed in public and found partly wanting and partly precious, remains the standing invitation to repeat the exercise on whatever inheritance the reader happens to hold.


## Distinctions that do the work: a glossary in prose

Elster's arguments turn on a handful of distinctions that recur so often in his work that a reader equipped with them can follow any of his books, and a profile that has ranged widely owes its readers the distinctions gathered in one place, stated in prose. The first is between explanation and prediction, treated earlier: the forensic identification of a mechanism that operated, against the deduction of an outcome in advance, with the social sciences credited for the first and excused from the second. The second is between a mechanism and a law, the pattern against the universal conditional. The third is between the intentional and the sub intentional in the formation of mental states: beliefs and desires that an agent forms by deliberation, against those formed behind his back by adaptation, dissonance and wishful thinking, with the political and moral consequences of the second class running through the ideology and autonomy literatures. The fourth is between consequences that explain and consequences that merely follow: the functionalist fallacy in its general form, applicable to every discipline that finds a benefit and calls it a cause. The fifth is between the thin and the thick in the theory of rationality: rationality as consistency of choice given beliefs and desires, against rationality as a property of the beliefs and desires themselves, their formation, their responsiveness to evidence, their endorsement by the agent whose they are. Elster's career can be written as the progressive thickening of the concept under pressure from his own findings, and the thickening is why the rational choice label fits him worse with each decade.

The sixth distinction is between norms as preferences and norms as constraints sustained by emotion, the difference between wanting to comply and being gripped by an ought whose violation costs shame. The seventh is between a class in itself and a class for itself, inherited from Marx and rebuilt by the collective action analysis into the distinction between a shared situation and an organised actor, with the filters of the earlier section specifying what the transformation requires. The eighth is between the value form and the value magnitude in the economic writings, the distinction on which the redistribution of the value theory's estate turned. The ninth is between a verdict on a doctrine and an attitude toward a tradition, the distinction the stated scorecard exists to enforce. The tenth, and the one on which this article's own verdict turned, is between methodological individualism as a doctrine about the form of complete explanation and methodological individualism as a research heuristic about where to look for mechanisms, the strong version that the objection wounds and the weaker version that his later practice embodies. A reader who keeps the ten distinctions in hand possesses the skeleton of the whole body of work, and will find, as this article has found at each stage, that the live questions are always about which side of a distinction a particular claim falls on, never about the slogans the distinctions replaced.

## The scorecard restated: what a reader should be able to say

The One Test for this article was fixed at its opening: a reader who finishes should be able to report its subject's verdict on Marx accurately, item by item, and to describe the alternative construction he raised on the ruins of the failed items and the route that construction travelled. The material has been supplied across the preceding sections; this one administers the test in compressed form, as the series' discipline of findability requires. Value theory failed, in his judgement, because its derivations could not be completed without circularity and its explanatory work is done as well by accounts that never mention embodied labour. The profit rate doctrine failed as a law because the mechanism behind it leaves the aggregate direction open. Dialectics failed because its pronouncements divide between the mistaken and the unfalsifiable. Collectivist method failed because collectivities act solely through the persons who compose them. The claim to a self contained Marxist science failed because the standards of explanation are nobody's property. Alienation passed, recast as an anatomy of stunted self realisation. Innovation under capitalism passed, as a selection mechanism with a derivable direction of bias. Exploitation passed once refounded on asset inequality. The route from class position to political action passed as a programme, its obstacles mapped by the examiner himself. In place of laws he proposed mechanisms, and the proposal was taken up by analytical sociology and by the philosophy of social science as a going concern. He analysed how wants adapt to circumstances, and that analysis, this article has maintained, furnishes the uptake process that ideology theory had always lacked. A reader who can reproduce that summary, qualifying it with the defeaters recorded along the way, holds what the article was written to deliver.

## For students and exam candidates

Elster rewards the student who reads him as a set of examinable arguments rather than as a personality in the tradition's soap opera, and the scorecard format of this article is designed for conversion into revision material. The highest yield practice is to reproduce the partition from memory and then to attach, to each item, the reason in one sentence and the strongest reply in another: value fails on circularity and parsimony, and the reply relocates the theory to the value form; exploitation survives on assets, and the objection charges the reconstruction with flattening production into distribution. Examination questions on this material reward exactly that structure, claim, reason, reply, conditional verdict, and punish the two habits this article has tracked through the secondary literature, the demolition summary and the loyalist softening, since both replace the partition with an attitude. A dedicated card in your [VaultBook study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) for the scorecard, one row per doctrine with its reason and reply, converts this article into the form in which it will be usable under time pressure. Students should also separate, as the extra questions in the FAQ below press them to, a mechanism from a reduction to preferences: the mechanisms programme is a demand for the intervening steps in an explanation, and the student who can state why that demand does not by itself settle where the steps must terminate has understood the article's central verdict. The companion article on [analytical versus classical Marxism](/2020/04/01/analytical-vs-classical-marxism/) supplies the contrast that examination questions on the school most often request.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The teaching opportunity this material offers is the connection that the literatures missed, between Elster's work on adaptive preferences and the theory of ideology, and a seminar built around it teaches two topics at once while modelling how scholarship's gaps work. Set the classical ideology texts, with their production and function claims, against Sour Grapes, and ask the seminar to specify the uptake mechanism the classical texts owe and the preference analysis supplies; then reverse the direction and ask what the ideology materials give the preference analysis, namely a social distribution and a feedback loop, and the seminar has performed a piece of synthesis that neither literature performed for itself. The functional explanation exchange offers a second ready made seminar: Cohen's defence and Elster's critique, read in sequence, stage the question of what an explanation owes in its purest available form, and students can be asked to adjudicate with the Darwinian analogy as the contested ground. The scorecard itself is a third instrument: distributing its rows among seminar members, each to defend or attack one verdict against the texts, reproduces the September Group's own procedure in miniature and teaches the itemisation habit by practice rather than by precept. In each case the pedagogical principle is the one Elster's work embodies, that understanding a position means being able to state the argument that would change it, and seminar exercises should be designed to force that statement into the open.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Researchers will find the usable residue of this profile in the mechanisms catalogue and in the standing of the programme within analytical sociology and the philosophy of social science, where Elster's formulations are reference points that current work refines and disputes; the open debts recorded in this article, the selection among rival mechanisms, the aggregation step, and the unreconciled standing of structural explanation, are live research problems rather than historical curiosities, and the microfoundations debate in the companion article on the companion article on Marxism and microfoundations maps the adjacent terrain. Historians of the tradition should note the article's central documentary claim, that the scorecard was stated explicitly by its author and has been misreported in both directions ever since, since the misreporting is itself evidence about how traditions process internal criticism, and the September Group's records and the school's correspondence are the sources against which the claim can be checked. Journalists covering the afterlife of Marxist ideas should resist the conversion narrative that this article has argued against at length: the accurate story is slower and better, one of the school's founders, who graded its inheritance item by item, kept a third of it, discarded the rest with reasons, and built tools that escaped the quarrel entirely. Where a news peg requires a verdict on what survives of Marx, the scorecard, attributed to its author and dated to its book, is a more defensible source than the impressions of either camp's spokesmen, and the defeaters attached to its items in this article indicate where specialists would qualify it.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Who is Jon Elster?

A philosopher and social scientist from Norway, educated in Oslo and Paris, Elster became the chief methodologist of the analytical Marxist school. He belonged to the seminar circle that launched that school in the late 1970s, and in 1985 he published Making Sense of Marx, a volume that rebuilt Marx's social theory thesis by thesis under demanding standards of clarity. His broader bibliography ranges over rationality and its failures: self binding, the adaptation of preferences, social norms, the emotions, and collective decision making. The explanatory programme he championed, built on mechanisms, was taken up across the social sciences, and analytical sociology counts his formulation among its founding references.

### Q: What did Elster conclude survives in Marx?

Four bodies of doctrine passed his examination, each after reconstruction. Alienation passed as a description of how particular organisations of work stunt the growth and exercise of human capacities, once its underlying picture of flourishing is owned as a value commitment. The treatment of innovation under capitalism passed because competitive selection among firms supplies the causal loop such explanations usually lack. Exploitation passed after being refounded on who owns productive assets rather than on labour embodied in products. The link between class position and political conduct passed as a programme of research whose intermediate steps, from shared situation through organisation to action, can be inspected one by one.

### Q: What is methodological individualism?

It is the thesis that a finished explanation of a social phenomenon must be capable of completion at the level of persons: what they believe, what they want, what opportunities confront them, and what they do, together with the manner in which those doings combine into outcomes nobody intended. Classes, institutions and structures may figure in an explanation, but they may not be credited with purposes of their own unless those purposes are traced back to the members who hold and enact them. In Elster's hands the thesis functioned as a standard of completeness for explanations rather than a prohibition on aggregate inquiry, and his mechanisms writings applied it flexibly, starting from situations and positions as often as from desires.

### Q: What are social mechanisms?

They are recurrent patterns of action and interaction through which social outcomes are produced, recognisable across cases and set off under circumstances that frequently remain obscure. Wishful thinking warps belief in the direction of hope; dissonance pushes convictions into alignment with costly conduct already performed; resentment can make an agent pay to harm a rival; shared norms extract contributions by threatening shame; cascades occur when each participant's choice hinges on the number who have chosen before. Such patterns make weaker claims than laws and are more finely grained, and they commonly arrive in contrary pairs, which is why identifying the pattern at work in a given episode, from the traces it leaves, is the explanatory task they set.

### Q: What is adaptive preference formation?

It names the process by which a person's wants shrink toward what circumstances permit, without any deliberate decision to shrink them. The fable of the fox, who decides unreachable grapes must be sour, gives the pattern its emblem. What cannot be had stops being wanted, and the reduced wanting is felt by the agent as entirely his own. Elster set out the analysis in Sour Grapes in 1983, separating the pattern from learning, in which the object is found wanting on evidence, and from the deliberate schooling of one's tastes. The stakes are large: frameworks that take preferences as fixed starting points must reckon with a process that manufactures those starting points out of the agent's situation.

### Q: How does adaptive preference relate to ideology?

Ideology theory in the Marxist line could say where order friendly beliefs come from and whose interests they serve, yet it stumbled over a plainer question: why do the people disadvantaged by an order come to hold its justifying beliefs as their own convictions? Preference adaptation answers at the level of the individual mind. When an arrangement persistently blocks access to security, influence or dignity, the blocked goods tend to lose their desirability, and a population whose wants have contracted in this way offers little resistance, which steadies the arrangement that contracted them. Joined to wishful thinking and to dissonance, adaptation completes a causal circuit running from structure through psychology back to structure, each link open to inspection.

### Q: Is methodological individualism a neutral method?

It is not, whatever its marketing in textbooks suggests. Any rule that settles beforehand which sorts of entity may do explanatory work, and which must first be decomposed into the doings of persons, embodies a claim about how the social world is put together. Elster presented the doctrine in that spirit, as a position requiring argument, not as laboratory equipment that rival theories could borrow free of charge. Its opponents were never defending vagueness; they were contesting a proposed stopping point for explanation, defending the claim that structures and relations might legitimately figure as starting points in their own right. Recognising the doctrine as substantive is the precondition for arguing about it honestly, on either side.

### Q: Did Elster remain a Marxist?

Membership language fits his case badly, and the record supports a doctrinal answer instead. In the school's founding period he worked squarely within the attempt to rebuild Marx, and the great book of 1985 is an insider's audit, severe toward some doctrines and generous toward others. No single rupture followed: no recantation essay, no public resignation. His agenda simply migrated, toward rationality, norms, feeling and the writing of constitutions, and later retrospects treat the Marxist episode as closed. What he kept and what he discarded are matters of public record in the scorecard, and the partition, rather than any label, is the accurate answer to the question.

### Q: What did Elster judge to have failed?

Five items drew negative verdicts. The labour theory of value failed on its internal derivations and on redundancy, since rival accounts handle prices and profits without it. The doctrine that the profit rate must fall failed as a law, because innovation can cheapen capital and is adopted for gain, leaving the aggregate direction unsettled. Dialectical materialism failed because its supposed laws are either mistaken or so elastic that nothing could count against them. Collectivism in method failed because a group does nothing except through the people composing it. The notion of a self contained Marxist science failed because standards of explanation are common property, binding on Marx's heirs and critics alike.

### Q: Where should you start with Elster?

Choose according to your errand. A reader after the Marx verdict should open Making Sense of Marx at the chapters dealing with exploitation, class and ideology, where the argument's weight concentrates. A reader after the method should take the compact volume of 1989, Nuts and Bolts, which teaches explanation by mechanisms through worked examples addressed to practising researchers. A reader after the formation of preferences should take Sour Grapes, and a reader after self binding should take Ulysses and the Sirens. Begin with the primary texts rather than with digests of them, because the force of his conclusions lives in the distinctions he draws along the way, and digests preserve conclusions while dropping distinctions.

### Q: How does the stated scorecard grade what survives in Marx?

It grades in the open, doctrine by doctrine, publishing a reason beside every mark. Alienation, innovation under capitalism, exploitation rebuilt on asset inequality, and the class to politics programme receive passes in reconstructed shape. Value theory, the doctrine of the profit rate, dialectics, collectivist method and the claim to a separate science receive failures. The metaphor of grading captures something real about the procedure: one examiner, standards announced beforehand, applied without regard to which camp a doctrine comforts. Sympathetic and hostile summaries alike flatten that partition into a single attitude, and the flattening, repeated across the secondary literature, is the misreporting that the phrase stated scorecard is meant to name and correct.

### Q: What should a student separate between a mechanism and a reduction to preferences?

Keep apart the request for intermediate steps and one particular decision about where those steps end. To ask for mechanisms is to ask how a condition produced an outcome, through which patterns of situated action, in a form that evidence could check. To reduce to preferences is to insist that the chain must always terminate in what individuals want. The first is a standard of explanatory completeness; the second is a further, contestable commitment. Mechanisms can be specified from structural positions, from the resources and sanctions a situation presents, without passing through wants at all. A student who holds the two apart can represent Elster's programme fairly and can also state, without confusion, the point at which his critics press it hardest.

### Q: Why did the school's sharpest internal critic publish his verdicts as a list?

A list can be used; a general attitude can only be applauded or deplored. By issuing verdicts item by item, he allowed readers to accept the grade on exploitation while contesting the grade on value, and he forced himself to argue each item separately, since no overall loyalty or disloyalty could substitute for a reason about alienation or about profit rates. The format also made the fairness of the exercise visible: the same standards, printed in advance, generating passes and failures side by side. That the list format outlasted the polemics around the book, becoming a staple of teaching, is evidence that usable criticism, itemised and reasoned, travels further than denunciation or defence.

### Q: How does the scorecard order the doctrines assessed?

It arranges them by outcome rather than by their sequence in Marx's writings, and the arrangement exposes a regularity worth a student's attention. Doctrines claiming the status of laws, governing prices, profitability or history itself, occupy the failing rows. Doctrines that work as anatomy, describing the experience of work, the springs of innovation, the location of injustice and the route from position to politics, occupy the passing rows. The table therefore does double duty: it reports one scholar's verdicts, and it advances a hypothesis about the whole corpus, that Marx's diagnostic gifts exceeded his nomological ones, a hypothesis a reader can test against any doctrine the table omits.

### Q: What separates methodological individualism as a demand for mechanisms from individualism as a doctrine?

The demand addresses the form of explanations; the doctrine addresses their permissible endpoints. Under the demand, any account of a social outcome must be able to exhibit the processes that produced it, and appeals to what a system needs or a class intends must be cashable in traced sequences of action and reaction. Under the doctrine, those sequences must terminate in persons and their mental states, never in positions or relations taken as given. Scholars who accept the demand while rejecting the doctrine are numerous, including structuralists who build mechanisms out of relations. Elster defended both early on and leaned, in his mature methodological writings, on the demand, and separating the two dissolves much of the apparent disagreement around his name.
