Two theories dominate the opening weeks of almost every sociology course, and they arrive in the classroom dressed as opposites. Functionalism speaks of shared values holding a society together, of parts working for the good of the whole, of order explained by agreement. Marxism speaks of exploitation, of a ruling class bending institutions to its advantage, of order explained by domination. The contrast is tidy, memorable, and easy to examine, which explains its long life in textbooks. It is also, in the form in which it is usually taught, misleading in a way that costs students the most interesting observation the comparison has to offer. Place the two accounts of any single institution side by side and a resemblance appears that the standard table hides. Each explains the institution by pointing to what it accomplishes for a larger arrangement. Each treats the arrangement as having requirements that must be met if it is to persist. Each subordinates the intentions of individuals to the logic of the structure they inhabit. Functionalism, in other words, is not the only perspective in the pair that reasons functionally. Marxism does so as well, wearing different colours, and the recognition of that shared reasoning habit reorganises everything a candidate thought the comparison was about.

![Two facing diagrams of a school, a church, and a courthouse drawn as gears inside different machines, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-63.webp)

The claim deserves stating without ornament before the evidence accumulates. When a functionalist describes schooling as the machinery by which a society sorts its young into occupational roles and binds them to common norms, and when a Marxist describes the same schooling as an apparatus through which a class order renews its workforce and spreads the ideas that legitimate it, the grammatical shape of the two sentences is identical. A practice exists, on both tellings, because of the work it performs for a system that needs that work done. Swap the beneficiary and the sentences trade places. Give the functionalist a dominant class as the party served and the sentence becomes Marxist. Give the Marxist a harmonious whole as the beneficiary named and the sentence turns functionalist. What refuses to change under the swapping is the form of the explanation itself, and form, in questions of method, is not a detail. A student who grasps the shared skeleton can still argue fiercely about which beneficiary is real. That argument is where the genuine disagreement lives, and it is a disagreement about the world, about whose interests institutions in fact serve, rather than a disagreement about how explanations should be built.

Everything that follows in this article hangs from that distinction between skeleton and beneficiary. Roughly a quarter of the discussion examines the common explanatory shape in its own right, tracing where it came from, why it attracts structural thinkers of every allegiance, and what logical burden it carries. A further portion sets out the differences that survive the recognition, and they are substantial differences, touching the source of social cohesion, the meaning of inequality, the engine of historical change, and the moral posture a theory adopts toward its subject. The largest single portion walks through the institutions one by one, education, religion, the family, stratification, crime, and the state, reading each twice, once in each vocabulary, and noting what the paired readings share even where their verdicts collide. Attention then turns to the criticisms that strike each perspective with equal force, because an objection that wounds only one side of a comparison is a debating point, whereas an objection that wounds both is a discovery. The verdict at the end names the evidence that would overturn the article's central claim, since a comparison that cannot specify its own defeat conditions has not finished its work.

A word about what this article declines to do will save the reader from hunting for material housed elsewhere in the series. The philosophical anatomy of functional explanation, its logic, its defenders, and its most rigorous Marxist exponent, receives extended treatment in the companion study of G. A. Cohen and functional explanation, and the present discussion borrows its conclusions without rehearsing its arguments. The application to schooling in depth belongs to the companion treatment of Marxism and education theory, the application to crime and lawbreaking is assigned to the companion treatment of Marxist criminology, and the religion comparison leans on the companion treatment of Marx on religion. Stratification disputes connect outward to the series guide to the Marxist theory of class. What remains here, and what no neighbouring article attempts, is the head-to-head comparison itself, conducted at the level of explanatory form, with the institutions grid as its findable centrepiece and the shared form as its namable claim.

## Functionalism and the question both perspectives ask

Begin with the question each perspective puts to an institution, because questions determine answers more thoroughly than doctrines do. Confronted with a school, a church, a court, or a household, the functionalist asks what the practice contributes to the survival and smooth working of the society containing it. The Marxist, confronted with the identical building and the identical timetable, asks what the practice does to keep a social order divided by class in working repair. The questions rhyme. Both refuse to treat the institution as self-explanatory. Both refuse the answer that the practice exists because people happen to like it, or because it has always existed, or because its participants describe it in flattering terms. Both reach behind the institution's own account of itself toward a beneficiary that the institution may never name. In the functionalist version the beneficiary is the social whole, conceived as something with needs analogous to an organism's needs. In the Marxist version the beneficiary is a class arrangement, conceived as something with requirements analogous to a machine's requirements. The analogy differs. The reaching does not.

That reaching has a history worth a paragraph, because neither perspective invented it in isolation. Nineteenth-century social thought, reacting against explanations that stopped at individual motives, developed a habit of treating societies as systems whose parts could be understood through their interconnections. The habit drew strength from biology, where a structure's presence in an organism is routinely illuminated by the role it plays in the organism's life, and from engineering, where a component's presence in a device is illuminated by the task it performs in the device's operation. Sociology inherited the habit before it inherited any agreement about what societies are. Thinkers who believed societies cohered through shared moral commitments used the habit to trace how rituals renew solidarity. Thinkers who located coherence in the organisation of production turned the same habit upon laws and the property those laws protect. The habit itself was common property, contested by nobody, because it answered a genuine puzzle: institutions persist across generations of participants, outliving every individual who staffs them, and persistence on that scale demands an explanation pitched at a level above biography.

Persistence is the phenomenon, and it is worth dwelling on why persistence puzzles. A custom that harmed everyone it touched and benefited no arrangement that could protect it would face erosion from many directions at once: participants would drift away, rivals would advertise its costs, reformers would find an audience. A custom that endures for centuries has usually found some niche in which its continuation is rewarded, some constituency or structure whose flourishing it supports and whose support it receives in return. Structural theories of every stripe begin by asking after that niche. They differ, and differ profoundly, over the identity of the supporting structure. One tradition locates the support in the moral integration of the whole population, the web of shared sentiments that makes strangers into fellow members. Another locates the support in the distribution of property and the political machinery guarding that distribution. A candidate who sees that both traditions open with the same investigative move, locate the niche, identify the beneficiary, trace the feedback, has acquired the master key to the comparison, and can then spend the remaining marks on the dispute that matters, which concerns the beneficiary's identity.

### Why do structural theories begin with persistence?

because endurance across generations cannot be explained by the wishes of participants who are born into arrangements already running and who leave them still running at death. Persistence points toward some supporting structure that rewards continuation, and identifying that structure is the first investigative move both perspectives share before disagreeing about its identity.

The organism analogy, so prominent in functionalist writing, deserves scrutiny here because it often serves as the supposed line between the perspectives. Functionalists compared societies to bodies, institutions to organs, and social needs to bodily needs, and the comparison is genuinely theirs in its elaborated form. Marxists rarely reached for the body. They reached for the workshop, the market, and the legal code. Yet an analogy is a teaching device rather than an argument, and the argument beneath the body analogy, that parts are intelligible through their contribution to a persisting whole, survives translation into workshop language without loss. A gear in a mill is intelligible through its contribution to the mill's output. A rule in a legal code is intelligible, on the Marxist telling, through its contribution to the security of a property regime. Nobody supposes the mill has feelings or the code has intentions. Contribution talk does not require a conscious beneficiary. It requires only a system whose continued operation depends, in identifiable ways, on the part under examination, and both perspectives traffic in exactly that currency.

There is a further resemblance in temperament that textbooks rarely mention. Both perspectives are impatient with surface description. The functionalist hears that a ceremony exists to honour tradition and asks what the honouring accomplishes for group cohesion. The Marxist hears that a law exists to protect the public and asks whose assets the protection secures in practice. Suspicion of official self-description is common ground, even though the suspicions terminate in different places. One ends at the latent contribution to integration, the contribution nobody avows. The other terminates in the latent service rendered to domination, likewise unavowed. Latency, the idea that an institution's real work differs from its advertised work, belongs to both traditions so thoroughly that the most famous vocabulary for it, the distinction between manifest and latent functions, was coined inside functionalism itself, a point whose full significance for the comparison emerges when the misattributed critics are examined later in this article.

### What do Marxism and functionalism have in common?

both explain institutions by their contribution to maintaining a system, both are structural and macro, and both subordinate individual agency to system requirements. They differ over whose interests the maintenance serves, which means the consensus and conflict contrast describes their conclusions rather than their method.

Hold that answer steady, because the rest of the article is an extended footnote to it. The sentence performs three operations at once. It names the common explanatory currency, contribution to the upkeep of a system. It names the common scale and stance, structural, macro, agency subordinated. And it relocates the celebrated contrast, consensus against conflict, from the column headed method to the column headed conclusion. Consensus and conflict are answers the two perspectives give to a question about what holds societies together. They are not tools the perspectives use, not procedures they follow, not tests they apply. A candidate who writes that functionalism uses consensus as its method and Marxism uses conflict as its method has confused a destination with a road. The road, for both, is the tracing of contributions. The destinations differ because the maps of society differ, one showing a moral community whose members broadly agree, the other showing a terrain of classes whose interests collide beneath a surface calm.

## The shared form, anatomised

Anatomy requires a specimen, so take the simplest available: a claim about schooling that either tradition might produce. Schools exist, in part, because complex divisions of labour require literate, numerate, punctual workers sorted by attainment into occupations of differing demand. Every clause of that sentence is functional in shape. An institution is present. A system, here the occupational order, has a requirement. The institution meets the requirement. The meeting of the requirement helps explain the institution's presence and persistence. Nothing in the shape specifies whether the occupational order is a fair allocation of talent to social need or a hierarchy of exploitation dressed in meritocratic clothing. The Marxist accepts the sentence's shape and disputes its innocence, arguing that the sorting reproduces class position under the appearance of measuring ability, and that the requirement being met is the requirement of employers for a stratified workforce rather than the requirement of society for competence. The dispute is real and important. It is also a dispute conducted entirely inside the shared shape, the way two engineers might dispute which load a beam carries while agreeing completely about how beams work.

Philosophers of social science gave this shape a name and a literature. An explanation that accounts for a practice by citing the beneficial consequences of the practice for some containing system is a functional explanation, and its legitimacy has been argued over for generations. The argument matters here because its terms apply with perfect impartiality. Defenders of the shape point out that biology uses it constantly and respectably: the heart's presence is illuminated by its pumping, feathers by insulation and flight, camouflage by concealment. Critics reply that biology possesses something social explanation usually lacks, a selection mechanism, since variation and inheritance guarantee that traits with beneficial consequences spread and traits without them vanish. Where no comparable mechanism operates, the critic continues, citing a benefit explains nothing, because benefits that nobody selects for are merely happy accidents wearing explanatory dress. That objection, in its classic form, was sharpened most effectively not by functionalism's enemies outside the Marxist camp but by analytical Marxists examining their own tradition's habits, and the later section on the sharpest critics gives that reversal the space it deserves.

For the comparison at hand, the immediate point is simpler and more portable. Whatever verdict a candidate reaches on functional explanation as a logical form, consistency requires reaching the same verdict for both perspectives wherever both use the form. A script that mocks functionalists for explaining institutions by their effects and then, three paragraphs later, explains the state by its effects on class power has applied two standards to one shape. Markers notice. More importantly, the candidate has missed the chance to write the sentence that lifts a comparison out of the table format altogether: the sentence observing that the objection to explanation by consequences is a shared vulnerability, that both traditions must either find mechanisms or moderate their claims, and that the interesting question is which tradition, in its best work, does more of that finding and moderating. That is an evaluative question with genuine discriminating power, unlike the recitation of paired slogans.

### Is explaining by consequences always illegitimate?

not always, since biology explains traits by their effects respectably wherever variation, inheritance, and selection supply a mechanism connecting benefit to prevalence. The shape becomes suspect where no mechanism is specified, where the benefit is asserted without any process by which beneficial arrangements spread and harmful ones disappear from the population of practices.

Mechanisms, then, are the currency in which the shared debt must be paid, and it is instructive to watch each tradition attempt payment. Functionalism's classical attempts invoke adaptation: societies whose institutions meet their needs outlast societies whose institutions fail, so the surviving stock of institutions is biased toward the functional. The attempt struggles, because societies rarely die cleanly of institutional failure, and because the timescale of the alleged selection dwarfs any observable test. Marxism's classical attempts invoke class power as the mechanism: arrangements serving the dominant class are protected by law, funded by surplus, taught in schools, and enforced by courts, while arrangements threatening them meet suppression, starvation of resources, or absorption. That attempt has a concreteness the adaptation story lacks, since the protecting agencies are nameable and their operations observable. Yet concreteness is unevenly distributed across Marxist writing, which often asserts the service an institution renders to capital without tracing the protecting agencies in the particular case. The form's debt is paid in some Marxist work and merely acknowledged in other work, and the same is true, with the currencies reversed, across the functionalist literature. Judging the traditions by their best payments rather than their average ones is a sophistication the verdict section will formalise.

A second anatomical feature deserves naming: both perspectives explain by consequences while officially valuing causes. Functionalism's founding generation inherited from positivism a commitment to causal laws, and Marxism's founding texts insist on material production as the causal ground of social life. Neither tradition set out to build a teleological science, a science in which purposes pull events from the future. The functional shape crept in through the subject matter itself, because institutions, unlike stones, are entangled with the purposes of the systems housing them, and because persistence, the explanandum both traditions care most about, seems to invite consequence talk at every turn. Recognising the creep as a subject-matter effect rather than a partisan vice changes the emotional register of the criticism. The question stops being which camp sinned and becomes how any structural sociology can discuss persistence without sliding into purpose language it cannot cash out. That is a question a candidate can profitably pose to both perspectives in a single paragraph, and posing it demonstrates exactly the analytical altitude the comparison question exists to reward.

## Where the two genuinely part company

Shared skeleton, divergent anatomy: the differences between the perspectives are not illusions manufactured by textbook writers, and establishing the common form would be a cheap trick if it dissolved distinctions that matter. It does not dissolve them. It relocates them, from method to substance, and in their relocated position they stand out more clearly, because a candidate no longer wastes the comparison's opening movement on a contrast that collapses under inspection. Five substantive divides organise the territory. The source of cohesion divides them first: what glues a society together, shared moral commitments absorbed through socialisation, or the organisation of production together with the political and legal machinery that guards it. The beneficiary divides them second, and has already been introduced: the whole population's integration, or a dominant class's security. The reading of inequality divides them third, as necessity or as exploitation. The engine of change divides them fourth, as gradual adaptation to strain or as contradiction maturing into rupture. The moral posture divides them fifth, since one perspective presents itself as detached analysis of how order is possible, while the other announces itself as criticism of an order judged indefensible. Each divide repays slow treatment, because each generates a family of examinable contrasts that the institutions grid later compresses into rows.

Cohesion first. The functionalist tradition, descending from Durkheim, treats shared values as the load-bearing element of social life. Strangers cooperate, contracts are honoured, queues form without guards, because members of a society have absorbed common definitions of right conduct deeply enough to enact them unreflectively. Parsons gave this absorption a systematic form: values are internalised through family and schooling, roles carry expectations that mesh, and deviance is handled by agencies that repair the fabric at its tears. Order, on this account, is a moral achievement renewed in every generation. The Marxist account grants the phenomenon, people do mostly comply, queues do mostly form, and contests the explanation. Compliance, it argues, is overdetermined by less flattering supports: by economic dependence that makes defiance expensive, by legal coercion held in reserve, by habitual resignation, and by ideas that present a contingent arrangement as natural or deserved. Where the functionalist sees values doing the work, the Marxist sees values as one instrument among several, and not the strongest, in a repertoire dominated by the control of livelihoods. The empirical dispute underneath is genuine: how much compliance would survive the removal of economic compulsion is a question neither tradition has answered cleanly, and honest comparison writing says so.

### What holds a society together, on each account?

shared moral commitments, absorbed in childhood and renewed by ritual, carry the explanatory weight for functionalism, whose members comply because they believe. For Marxism the weight falls on economic dependence, legal coercion in reserve, and ideas presenting a contingent arrangement as natural, with belief demoted to one instrument among several.

Beneath the cohesion dispute sits a disagreement about what a society fundamentally is. Functionalism's unit of analysis is the society as a bounded whole, a population sharing institutions and a moral climate, and its explanations terminate in that whole's requirements. Marxism's unit is a mode of production together with the class relations it generates, and its explanations terminate in the reproduction of those relations, which may or may not coincide with the flourishing of the whole population. The difference in units explains a recurring asymmetry in the literatures. Functionalist writing struggles to theorise power that serves a part against the whole, since its explanatory terminus is the whole's welfare, and it tends to redescribe sectional advantage as contribution to general integration. Marxist writing struggles, symmetrically, to theorise goods that genuinely serve everyone, since its terminus is class reproduction, and it tends to redescribe universal benefits as legitimation devices or as concessions extracted by struggle. Each tradition has a characteristic blind spot manufactured by its unit of analysis, and naming both blind spots in one breath is fairer, and more accurate, than the partisan habit of naming only the opponent's.

Inequality, the third divide, receives the fullest steelmanned treatment later in this article through the stratification thesis and its classic critique, so a compressed statement suffices here. Functionalism's characteristic move is to ask what problem stratification solves, and to answer that scarce talent must be coaxed toward demanding roles by differential reward. Marxism's characteristic move is to ask whose labour stratification organises, and to answer that differential reward institutionalises the extraction of surplus from those who produce it by those who own the means of producing. One move treats inequality as a solution looking for acknowledgement. The other treats it as a relation looking for concealment. Candidates should notice that both moves are, once again, functional in grammar: each explains the phenomenon by the work it performs, for the whole in one case and for a class in the other. The grammar is shared even at the point of maximum substantive hostility, which is the strongest evidence the article can offer for its central claim.

Change, the fourth divide, separates an evolutionary imagination from a revolutionary one. Functionalist systems theory pictures societies adjusting incrementally as strains appear, differentiating new institutions the way organisms differentiate new tissues, with change that is real but continuous and largely unplanned. Marxist theory pictures change as the maturation of contradictions internal to a mode of production, periods of stability punctuated by crises in which quantitative pressures convert into qualitative transformation. The contrast is genuine, though each tradition contains the other's accent in minor key: functionalists wrote about strain and dysfunction, concepts that admit serious maladjustment, and Marxists wrote about long stretches of successful reproduction in which nothing revolutionary stirs. The difference lies in the default expectation. Ask a functionalist what tomorrow's institution will look like and the answer extrapolates the present arrangement with modifications. Ask a Marxist and the answer looks for the fault line along which the present arrangement will eventually split. Defaults shape research agendas, teaching examples, and political temperaments far beyond the seminar room.

### How does each perspective picture social change?

as gradual adjustment for functionalism, where strain prompts differentiation and repair inside a continuing order whose members broadly accept its terms. As contradiction maturing toward rupture for Marxism, where pressures accumulate within a mode of production until quantitative strain converts into qualitative transformation of the relations organising production itself.

The fifth divide, posture, is the one students most often sense without articulating. Functionalism aspired to the stance of the physician or the engineer, diagnosing how a system works and where it strains, without arraigning the system before a moral tribunal. Its vocabulary, function, dysfunction, adaptation, integration, borrows the neutrality of the laboratory. Marxism refuses that neutrality as a fiction, holding that a theory of society either criticises domination or, by silence, accommodates it, and its vocabulary, exploitation, alienation, domination, carries its verdicts openly. The posture divide has methodological consequences. A theory that arraigns its subject will hunt for evidence of harm with an energy a neutral theory does not muster, and will correspondingly under-attend to evidence of genuine benefit. A theory that brackets verdicts will map mechanisms with a patience a committed theory rarely sustains, and will under-attend to the question of who pays for the mechanisms it maps. Sophisticated comparison writing treats the postures as trade-offs rather than as virtues and vices, and the verdict section of this article returns to the trade-off when usefulness is weighed.

## Order, values, and the puzzle of compliance

The puzzle of compliance deserves a section of its own because it is the functionalist tradition's home ground, the phenomenon its apparatus was built to explain, and the ground on which Marxism's alternative explanation must prove itself if the comparison is to be more than a slogan exchange. The phenomenon is startling once noticed. Millions of people who will never meet coordinate their conduct daily with minimal supervision. Trains are boarded in sequence. Debts are repaid to institutions borrowers could collectively ruin by simultaneous refusal. Instructions from officials are obeyed by citizens who outnumber the officials by thousands to one. Any theory of society owes an account of this mountain of voluntary-seeming obedience, and the two perspectives pay the debt in different currencies.

Functionalism pays in the currency of internalisation. The child absorbs the norms of the surrounding order before possessing the critical equipment to evaluate them, and absorption at that depth does not feel like obedience from inside. It feels like conscience, like taste, like the obvious shape of a decent life. Parsons described the process as the installation of shared value patterns that then operate as the actor's own wants, so that the system's requirements and the individual's desires arrive pre-aligned. Rituals refresh the installation across the lifespan, collective gatherings recharging the moral sentiments that private life depletes, in the Durkheimian picture that functionalism inherited. Deviance, on this account, is the exception that illuminates the rule: the deviant is processed by agencies whose real work is not punishment but boundary repair, reasserting for the audience where the lines run. The account's strength is its fit with the phenomenology of ordinary life, where compliance mostly feels unforced. Its weakness is a tendency to infer the values from the compliance and then explain the compliance by the values, a circularity its critics inside and outside the tradition have pressed for decades.

Marxism pays in the currency of dependence and hegemony, two denominations that must be distinguished. Dependence is the cruder instrument: the worker complies with the employer's schedule because the alternative is destitution, the tenant complies with the lease because the alternative is homelessness, and no internalised love of schedules or leases is required to produce the observed regularity. Hegemony is the subtler instrument, Gramsci's name for the process by which a dominant class's worldview becomes the common sense of the whole society, so that compliance acquires ideological reinforcement without direct coercion. The Marxist account draws its strength from the distribution of compliance's costs: obedience is most reliable precisely where dependence is most complete, a correlation the values account does not predict. Its weakness is the mirror of the functionalist one: a temptation to treat every professed value as hegemony's product, which renders the theory incapable of registering genuine agreement when it occurs, and which struggles with the compliance of the privileged, whose dependence is slight yet whose allegiance to the order is often the most fervent in the population.

### Why does compliance need explaining at all?

because the sheer volume of unsupervised coordination among strangers exceeds anything coercion could directly enforce, given how heavily the enforcers are outnumbered. Some combination of absorbed norms, material dependence, habit, and belief must be doing the work, and the perspectives divide over which elements carry the greatest load.

A discriminating test between the currencies is occasionally available in historical episodes where the supports fall away at different speeds. When economic dependence loosens, through tight labour markets or generous welfare provision, the dependence account predicts a measurable fraying of workplace discipline, while the values account predicts continuity, since installed values do not uninstall with the business cycle. When moral consensus fractures, through scandal or generational revolt, the values account predicts compliance failures in the fractured domain, while the dependence account predicts continuity wherever livelihoods remain hostage. Episodes supplying both patterns exist, which is why mature versions of each tradition quietly import the other's currency as a supplementary mechanism. Functionalists concede that sanctions matter. Marxists concede that legitimation matters, which is hegemony by another route. The concessions do not erase the divide, because the ordering of the currencies still differs, first cause against auxiliary, but they mark the distance between the traditions' textbook caricatures and their working forms. Comparison writing that reports the caricatures earns the middle marks. Writing that reports the working forms, concessions included, earns the upper ones.

There is also a philosophical dividend in this section's material. The compliance puzzle shows both traditions practising a distinctive explanatory manoeuvre: inferring invisible supports from visible regularity. Values are not directly observable; neither is hegemony. Both are posited entities, defended by the patterns they organise. A candidate alert to the parallel can pose the philosophy-of-science question that examiners reward: by what standards should sociology choose between unobservable supports, and do the two traditions apply those standards symmetrically to their own posits and to each other's? The question has no quick answer, which is precisely its value in an examination, where the candidate's task is to display judgement about evidence rather than to recite a winner.

## The institutions grid

Comparison becomes examinable when it is organised, and the organising device this article offers is the institutions grid: a table with one row per institution, namely education, religion, family, crime, stratification, and the state, with columns for the functionalist reading, the Marxist reading, and what the two share. The grid's third column is its original contribution. Standard tables stop at two columns, readings juxtaposed, and leave the student to infer that juxtaposition is comparison. The third column forces the analytical step, naming in each row the common explanatory grammar that the opposed verdicts share, so that the grid teaches the article's thesis while it organises the material. A candidate who reproduces a two-column table has summarised two theories. A candidate who adds the third column has compared them.

| Institution | Functionalist reading | Marxist reading | What the two share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Education | Allocates roles and transmits shared values | Reproduces labour power and ruling ideas | Schooling explained by the work it performs for a persisting order |
| Religion | Binds members through collective conscience | Consoles sufferers and reconciles them to conditions | Belief explained by its contribution to stability |
| Family | Socialises children and stabilises adults | Renews labour power without charge to employers | Household explained by services rendered to a wider system |
| Crime | Marks boundaries and prompts adaptation | Produced by inequality and defined by property holders | Deviance explained through the order that labels and uses it |
| Stratification | Rewards scarce talent in demanding roles | Institutionalises exploitation between classes | Inequality explained by a requirement it allegedly meets |
| State | Coordinates interests and enforces norms | Guards property relations and class power | Political authority explained by the order it keeps intact |

Read the grid vertically as well as horizontally. The functionalist column, read downward, tells one continuous story: every institution integrates, allocates, stabilises, or repairs. The Marxist column tells its continuous story with equal consistency: every institution reproduces, consoles, renews, or guards. The shared column tells the third story, the one this article exists to tell: in every row, the institution is explained by a contribution to the maintenance of something larger than itself, and the row's disagreement is confined to naming that something. A student who can narrate all three stories, and who can say which story an examiner's question is asking for, commands the topic in a way that memorised contrasts cannot imitate. The sections that follow unpack each row at the length its examination frequency deserves, beginning with the institution both traditions treat as their showcase.

## Education read oppositely

Schooling is the showcase because both traditions regard it as the hinge between generations, the place where a social order quite literally remakes itself in new persons. The functionalist reading assembles three claims. Schools transmit the shared values that cohesion requires, converting a population of family loyalists into citizens with a common moral vocabulary. Schools sort, by attainment, into the occupational structure, matching developed ability to roles of differing demand in a process presented as both efficient and fair. Schools teach the specific skills a complex division of labour presupposes, literacy and numeracy first, then the specialised competences of trades and professions. Durkheim supplied the first claim's classic form, education as the methodical socialisation of the young generation. Parsons supplied the second, the classroom as a bridge between the particularistic standards of the family and the universalistic standards of adult society, where the child first learns to be judged by impersonal criteria applied equally to all. The reading's emotional register is gratitude: schooling is society's gift to its successors, imperfect in delivery, benign in design.

The Marxist reading takes the same three sites and reverses the valence of each. Value transmission becomes ideological transmission: what schools instil, on this account, is less a common morality than the specific dispositions a class order requires, punctuality, obedience to bells and hierarchies, acceptance of external rewards, and the belief that unequal outcomes reflect unequal merit. Sorting becomes reproduction: the attainment schools measure is saturated with advantages purchased outside them, so the sorting machine converts inherited privilege into certified qualification, laundering class origin into individual desert. Skills teaching becomes labour power production: the competences are real, the Marxist need not deny them, but their selection and distribution follow the requirements of employers, and the graduate is delivered to the labour market as a finished commodity whose production costs were borne by families and the public purse rather than by the firms that consume the product. Althusser gave the reading its most systematic form by naming schools the dominant ideological apparatus of capitalist societies, and the correspondence principle associated with Bowles and Gintis specified the mechanism at classroom level, the hidden curriculum of authority relations mirroring the workplace relations awaiting the pupil. The fuller development sits in [marxism and education theory](/2021/05/01/marxism-and-education-theory/), and candidates should follow the link for the empirical literature this summary compresses.

### How do the two readings of schooling differ most sharply?

over the meaning of the attainment schools measure and reward. Functionalism treats attainment as developed ability finding its occupational level, so sorting appears as fair allocation. Marxism treats attainment as saturated with purchased advantage, so sorting appears as inherited position laundered into certified merit through examinations.

What the row shares matters as much as what divides it. Both readings treat schooling's manifest purposes, the ones in prospectuses, as incomplete accounts of what schools do. Both insist the institution's deepest work is systemic: the renewal of an order across generations. Both are therefore committed to explaining the school's persistence by its renewal work, and both face the same demand to specify the feedback that rewards schools performing the work and penalises schools neglecting it. The functionalist feedback runs through societal approval, funding, and the labour market's demand for sorted graduates. The Marxist feedback runs through state policy, employer influence, and the suppression or marginalisation of pedagogies threatening the order. Neither feedback story is absurd. Neither is complete. The comparison's honest verdict on its showcase row is that two functional explanations compete here, differing in beneficiary and in moral sign, and that the empirical contest between them, study by study, remains genuinely open in a way partisans of either column rarely concede.

A nuance from within the Marxist camp sharpens the row further. The ethnographic tradition associated with Willis studied working-class pupils who resisted schooling's dispositions, saw through its promises with considerable clarity, and by their resistance delivered themselves to manual labour with a sense of choice. Resistance inside reproduction complicates any crude version of the Marxist reading, in which pupils figure as passive receptacles, and it complicates the functionalist reading too, since the resisting pupils are neither integrated nor allocated by consent. The nuance is a gift to comparison writing: it shows the Marxist tradition generating internal correctives to its own functionalism, pupils' agency reasserted against the system's requirements, which anticipates the shared determinism criticism examined later. A candidate who knows the nuance can write the sentence examiners prize, the one in which a tradition is shown criticising itself with its own tools.

## Religion read oppositely

Religion divides the perspectives with unusual clarity because their founding texts address it directly and at length, and because the phenomena, ritual, belief, consolation, solidarity, sit close to both traditions' core concerns. The functionalist reading descends from the Durkheimian study of religion's elementary forms, where ritual gatherings generate a collective effervescence that welds participants into a moral community, and where the sacred object, whatever its theology, functions as the community's representation of itself to itself. Religion, on this account, is society worshipping its own unity in disguised form, and its persistence is explained by the irreplaceable integrative work it performs: calendars of shared observance, rites marking birth, maturity, marriage, and death, moral codes carrying supernatural sanctions, and consolation that helps members endure bereavement and disaster without the social fabric tearing. Later functionalists generalised the point beyond churches, treating civic ceremonies and national commemorations as functional equivalents performing the same integrative labour for populations whose theological commitments had thinned.

The Marxist reading begins from suffering rather than solidarity. Religion, in the famous passage whose full context the series restores in [marx on religion explained](/2021/06/01/marx-on-religion-explained/), is the sigh of the oppressed creature, an expression of real suffering and a protest against it, the heart of a heartless world, before it is the opium of the people, an analgesic image drawn from the period's pharmacy rather than from any vocabulary of stupefaction. The reading's analytical core is a claim about function in the strict sense this article has been anatomising: religion persists, in part, because it reconciles sufferers to conditions that would otherwise provoke transformation, offering redress in an imagined order for deprivations in the actual one, and sanctifying the actual order's hierarchies as divinely arranged. Consolation and legitimation are the two services, and they are services rendered, on the Marxist account, to the arrangement that produces the suffering, whatever comfort individual believers genuinely receive. The reading does not require believers to be fools or priests to be conspirators. It requires only that the effects of belief, aggregated across a population, dampen the pressure for structural change.

### Where do the two readings of religion part company?

through the phenomenon each takes as primary. Functionalism starts from solidarity, religion as the ritual machinery by which a community experiences and renews its unity. Marxism starts from suffering, religion as consolation whose aggregate effect reconciles the sufferers to conditions producing their pain and sanctifies those conditions as ordained.

The shared column of this row is unusually instructive. Both readings are resolutely sociological: neither evaluates the truth of religious claims, both treat belief as a phenomenon to be explained by its place in a social system, and both explain its persistence by its consequences, integration in one vocabulary, stabilisation in the other, which are closer cousins than either tradition enjoys admitting. A theologian confronting both readings confronts the same challenge twice: the reduction of faith to function. A sociologist comparing the readings confronts a choice of systems: the moral community whose unity religion renews, or the class order whose tensions religion soothes. Even the celebrated hostility between the traditions softens under this light into a family quarrel about which system's upkeep the rituals principally serve, conducted in a shared grammar of upkeep.

Two complications keep the row honest. The first is that religion's protest capacity, folded into the Marxist founding passage itself, keeps erupting in the historical record: movements of the oppressed have repeatedly organised under religious banners, preached equality from sacred texts, and frightened property holders who expected sedation and got mobilisation instead. A purely consolatory reading cannot digest those episodes without strain, and the tradition's own historians, Engels on the peasant war above all, documented the eruptions with admiration. The second complication mirrors it on the functionalist side: religion divides as readily as it unites, supplying the banners of intercommunal conflict as often as the rituals of integration, so a purely integrative reading faces its own indigestible episodes. Each tradition, in its mature form, absorbs the complication by qualifying the function, religion integrates the faithful partly by marking them against outsiders, religion consoles partly by channelling protest into otherworldly timetables. The qualifications bring the readings closer in structure while leaving their emphases opposed, which is the institutions grid's recurring lesson in miniature.

## The family read oppositely

The household is the smallest institution in the grid and the one where the two readings diverge in tone most dramatically, because the functionalist account is at its most lyrical here and the Marxist account at its most austere. Functionalism's classic statement, associated with Parsons, assigns the family two irreducible functions in industrial societies. The first is the primary socialisation of children, the early installation of the culture's values and the formation of a personality capable of participating in the wider order. The second is the stabilisation of adult personalities, the household as a refuge where the emotional strains of occupational life are absorbed and repaired, spouses restoring in one another the equilibrium the working day depletes. Around these two, subsidiary claims gather: the family regulates sexual conduct, provides the setting for reproduction, and supplies members with ascribed status and unconditional belonging in a world otherwise organised by achievement and contract. The reading's picture is of a small institution performing emotional labour that no larger institution has managed to replicate at comparable depth, which explains, functionally, its persistence through every prediction of its disappearance.

The Marxist reading, whose founding statement is Engels's study of the family's origin in private property, reframes each element. Socialisation becomes the early production of labour power's dispositions and, in the feminist development of the tradition, the site where gender hierarchy is first naturalised. Stabilisation becomes the repair of the worker at no cost to the employer: the household washes, feeds, soothes, and returns the labourer to the workplace each morning, an enormous subsidy to production that appears in no firm's accounts, performed overwhelmingly by women whose work is unwaged precisely because the family form defines it as love rather than labour. Reproduction itself is reframed as the production of the next generation of labour power, again unwaged, again invisible in the wage bargain. The household, on this account, persists not because it uniquely meets universal emotional needs but because it organises the renewal of the workforce and the transmission of property at minimal cost to the classes benefiting from both. Where functionalism sees a refuge from the economic order, Marxism sees one of that order's load-bearing walls.

### Where do the two readings of the family part company?

as refuge or as subsidy. Functionalism presents the household as shelter from occupational strain, socialising children and restoring adults. Marxism presents the same household as an unpaid department of the wider economy, renewing labour power daily and generationally while defining the work as love, which exempts it from wages and accounts.

The shared grammar shows plainly in this row. Both readings explain the family's persistence by services rendered to a containing system, emotional equilibrium for the social order in one telling, workforce renewal for the capitalist order in the other. Both treat the household's self-image, a haven of affection governed by its own laws, as a partial truth concealing systemic work. Both are consequently vulnerable to the same empirical embarrassment: households persist in forms that serve neither master well, chosen by members for reasons, attachments, pleasures, refusals, that neither system's requirements predict. The feminist scholarship that transformed this row drew on Marxist tools while criticising Marxism's own functionalism about gender, pointing out that explaining the household by capital's needs left men's domestic power undertheorised, a benefit accruing to a sex rather than to a class. That internal criticism, like the analytical Marxists' criticism of functional explanation generally, demonstrates the tradition correcting its shared form from inside, and it gives comparison writers a model of how to criticise a perspective without caricaturing it: use its best adherents' own reservations.

Historical variation presses both readings in the same place. Household forms differ dramatically across periods and regions, extended kin networks, nuclear units, communal experiments, single-person households, and a theory explaining the family's persistence by its functions must explain why the functions tolerate such variety of form, or concede that function underdetermines form, which concedes a great deal. Functionalism's concession, made by its careful writers, is that equivalents can perform the stabilising and socialising work, which weakens any claim that the observed form is necessary. Marxism's parallel concession is that the renewal of labour power proceeds under many domestic arrangements, including arrangements the tradition's founders did not anticipate, which weakens claims tying a specific household form to a specific mode of production. The concessions, made symmetrically, leave both readings standing as accounts of pressures rather than of necessities. Pressures are real explanatory currency. Necessities were the stronger claim, and on this row, as on others, the stronger claim is the one the evidence declines to underwrite.

## Crime read oppositely

Crime appears in the grid because both traditions, unusually, treat it as revealing rather than merely pathological, and because their treatments form the comparison's neatest inversion. The functionalist reading, again Durkheimian in descent, holds that crime is normal in the technical sense: present in every known society, impossible to eliminate entirely, and, within limits, useful. Its uses are specific. Punishment rituals dramatise the moral boundaries of the community, gathering members in shared outrage that renews their commitment to the norms infringed. Deviance tests boundaries that would otherwise go unexamined, and the deviant occasionally pioneers adaptations the society later adopts, so that the criminal of one generation may be the reformer of the next. A society with no crime at all would, on this account, be a society whose conformity was enforced at a level of surveillance and repression incompatible with any freedom worth having, since the conduct spectrum would have to be policed down to its mildest deviations. Crime rates that climb too high signal strain, a gap between culturally prescribed goals and institutionally available means, in the elaboration associated with Merton, whose place inside the functionalist house matters to a later section of this article.

The Marxist reading relocates the phenomenon at three points, developed fully in [marxist criminology explained](/2021/06/15/marxist-criminology-explained/). The definition of crime is itself class work: the criminal law protects property with a thoroughness it does not extend to the harms of the powerful, so that the theft of a loaf is prosecuted while the theft of wages, the poisoning of workplaces, and the looting of pension funds are handled, if at all, by gentler jurisdictions. The production of crime follows from inequality: a social order that generates blocked opportunity, humiliation, and surplus populations excluded from stable work manufactures the conduct it then punishes, and the reserve of the excluded serves further functions in disciplining the employed. The administration of punishment falls unevenly by class, so that the prison population describes the class structure more faithfully than it describes the distribution of harmful conduct. Crime, on this reading, is less a threat to the order than one of its products and one of its instruments, and the moral panic surrounding street offending performs the additional service of directing public anger downward toward the dangerous poor rather than upward toward the dangerous arrangements.

### Where do the two explanations of crime part company?

by locating the phenomenon's significance in opposite places. Functionalism finds crime's meaning in the community's reaction, punishment as boundary drama renewing shared norms, deviance as adaptation's scout. Marxism finds the meaning in crime's definition, production, and prosecution, each bent by property relations, so lawbreaking exposes the order rather than threatening it.

The shared column here is subtler than in other rows and therefore more valuable when a candidate articulates it. Both readings refuse the individual-pathology account, the idea that crime is explained by the wickedness or defect of criminals. Both treat crime rates and punishment rituals as data about the containing order rather than about the offenders. Both, in consequence, explain features of the criminal justice system by their contributions to that order's maintenance, solidarity renewed through outrage in one vocabulary, class discipline renewed through selective enforcement in the other. The inversion of verdict, crime as health versus crime as symptom of the order's own disease, sits atop an identity of method so complete that the two literatures cite the same phenomena, the same rituals, the same statistics, and differ in the beneficiary they assign to the pattern. A candidate who writes that sentence has understood the row. A candidate who writes that functionalists approve of crime and Marxists excuse it has understood neither, and the grid exists largely to prevent that particular collapse.

Qualifications accumulate on both sides, as they did for religion. Functionalism's boundary account struggles with harms that provoke no collective outrage because they are invisible or normalised, and with punishments whose severity tracks the offender's poverty more closely than the offence's gravity, a pattern the boundary story does not predict. Marxism's definition account struggles with the genuine popular demand for protection from violence and theft within working-class communities themselves, whose residents are crime's most frequent victims and whose support for policing cannot be wholly written off as manufactured consent without insulting the victims' judgement. The mature literatures absorb these strains by borrowing: functionalists admit power into the boundary story, asking whose norms the drama enforces; Marxists admit protection into the class story, distinguishing the law's class form from the real security populations need. The borrowings, once again, narrow the methodological distance to nothing while leaving the substantive wager, whose interests the pattern serves, exactly where it was.

## The state in both readings

The state completes the grid, and it is the row where the functionalist reading is thinnest and the Marxist reading most elaborated, an asymmetry that itself teaches something about each tradition's centre of gravity. Functionalism treats political authority as the order's coordinating organ: the subsystem charged with goal attainment in Parsons's scheme, setting collective priorities, mobilising resources, and enforcing the norms the value system prescribes. Legitimate authority rests, on this account, on the population's broad endorsement of the values the state implements, and political conflict is processed as competition within a shared framework, parties alternating in office while the framework endures. The reading handles stable democracies gracefully and struggles where the state's violence falls persistently on one section of the population, since patterned coercion sits awkwardly beside the claim that authority expresses a general consensus.

The Marxist reading treats the state as the guarantor of a property order. Its classic compressed formula describes the executive of the modern state as a committee for managing the common affairs of the bourgeoisie, and its developed versions complicate the formula in two directions. One direction, associated with theorists of relative autonomy, grants the state real distance from any particular capitalist's demands, arguing that the order's long-term security requires an arbiter capable of disciplining individual firms, conceding reforms, and organising compromises no single employer would choose. The other direction examines the state's form itself, asking why class power appears as neutral public authority, law and administration standing apparently above the society they organise. Both developments preserve the core claim: the state's persistence and shape are explained by the work it performs for a class-divided order, protecting property, managing crises, and legitimating the arrangement by presenting sectional guarantees as universal services.

### What does each reading make of political authority?

an expression of shared values for functionalism, the coordinating organ of a moral community whose members accept its framework while disputing its occupants. A guarantor of property relations for Marxism, whose neutrality is a form to be explained and whose autonomy from individual capitalists serves the class order's longer horizons.

The shared grammar completes the grid's demonstration. The state, in both columns, is explained through the order whose intactness it secures, and its self-description, servant of the public in one idiom, is treated by both as a claim to be tested against its pattern of action rather than as evidence. The traditions part over the order's nature: a moral community with a coordination problem, or a class structure with a legitimation problem. Every row of the grid has now delivered the same architecture, opposed verdicts on a common frame, which licenses the article's next movement. If the frame is common, then the objections aimed at the frame, teleology, the missing mechanism, determinism, unfalsifiability, cannot be the property of either camp. They are the shared inheritance, and they deserve examination as such, after the two misattributed critics have been restored to their correct positions, since those critics turn out to be the frame's most interesting assailants.

## Stratification and the thesis that must be given at full strength

No section of this comparison is more often botched in student writing, and in Marxist writing generally, than the functionalist account of stratification, because the temptation to present it weakly, refute the weak version, and move on is nearly irresistible. The discipline this article observes, and recommends to every candidate, is the opposite: state the thesis at the strength its authors gave it, with attribution and date, absorb its genuine force, and only then deploy the classic critique, conceding what the critique leaves standing. The thesis belongs to Kingsley Davis and Wilbert Moore, published in 1945 under the title Some Principles of Stratification, and it remains the most influential functionalist argument ever constructed, the point at which the perspective's logic about inequality achieved its purest form.

The argument, at full strength, runs as follows. Every society must somehow distribute its members among positions and motivate them to perform the duties those positions carry. Positions differ in two respects that matter for the distribution problem. Some positions are functionally more important than others, in the sense that the society's survival and effectiveness depend more heavily on their competent performance. Some positions, independently, require scarce talent or lengthy and costly training, so that the pool of people capable of filling them is small. A society that rewarded all positions equally would face a motivational failure at exactly the points of greatest need: nobody would undergo the sacrifices of extended training, or accept the burdens of the most consequential roles, without differential inducement. Unequal rewards, prestige, wealth, leisure, and deference attached differentially to positions, are therefore not an accident or an imposition but a mechanism, the device by which societies solve a universal allocation problem. Stratification, the institutionalised system of such differential rewards, is on this account functionally necessary, and its presence in every known society is offered as evidence: a universal phenomenon points to a universal requirement, not to a conspiracy of the powerful repeated independently across all civilisations.

Feel the argument's real weight before reaching for the reply. It explains a universal by a universal problem, which is methodologically respectable in shape. It does not defend any particular distribution of rewards, only the necessity of some differential system, which makes it immune to the easy refutation that points at an overpaid entertainer or an underpaid nurse: Davis and Moore can reply that particular rankings may be distorted while the principle of ranking stands. It predicts that attempts to abolish differential reward wholesale will either fail or reproduce differentials in disguised currencies, status, privilege, access, which is a prediction with considerable historical support that honest Marxist writing must register rather than wave away. And it poses a question any egalitarian alternative must answer in operational detail: by what mechanism, if not differential reward, will scarce trained talent be allocated to the positions where incompetence is catastrophic? A critique that never engages that question has not engaged the thesis.

### What exactly did Davis and Moore claim about stratification?

the claim, published by Davis and Moore in 1945, that stratification is functionally necessary because positions differ in importance and in the scarcity of talent and training they require, so unequal rewards operate as the mechanism inducing capable people to fill the most demanding roles.

The classic critique, delivered by Melvin Tumin in 1953, attacks the thesis at its joints rather than at its conclusions, which is why it has lasted. Tumin's first cut concerns functional importance: the thesis supplies no independent measure of a position's importance apart from the rewards it already commands, so the reasoning circles, the highly rewarded are declared important because they are highly rewarded, and their rewards are then explained by their importance. A measure that could break the circle, importance gauged by the consequences of a role's withdrawal, yields awkward results for the thesis, since the withdrawal of refuse collectors or nurses disrupts a society faster than the withdrawal of many more lavishly rewarded occupations. The second cut concerns the talent pool: stratification itself restricts the pool it claims to allocate, because children born into disadvantage are denied the training through which talent becomes capability, so the system wastes the majority of the talent it supposedly exists to deploy, and the scarcity it cites is partly its own manufacture. The third cut concerns motivation: differential reward is not the only inducement known to human societies, since duty, craftsmanship, service, and intrinsic interest demonstrably motivate demanding work in many settings, and a theory that treats material and prestige differentials as the sole mechanism has assumed what it needed to prove. The fourth cut concerns power: the thesis describes stratification as society's solution while omitting that those positioned to define importance and to set rewards are the already advantaged, so the mechanism may record their power rather than the society's needs.

### Where does the classic critique cut deepest?

at the measurement of importance, since rewards cannot certify the importance that is supposed to explain them without circularity, and at the talent pool, since inherited disadvantage excludes most ability from the training the allocation story requires, making scarcity partly the system's own product.

Now the concessions, which this article's neutrality discipline requires to be as explicit as the critique. Tumin's cuts damage the thesis as a justification of observed distributions, perhaps fatally, but the universality observation survives the damage: every known large society exhibits institutionalised inequality of reward and standing, and that fact requires explanation from any theory, including Marxism. The Marxist explanation, exploitation grounded in property relations, accounts elegantly for stratification in class societies and grows thin, by the tradition's own admission in its candid moments, when applied to stratification in societies without capitalist property, where differentials of status, bureaucratic rank, and access persisted in forms the property story does not naturally generate. The Davis and Moore framework also survives as a question even where it fails as an answer: the allocation problem is real, scarce competence exists, training is costly, and any proposed order must specify its allocation mechanism or be caught rediscovering differentials under new names. A candidate who writes the thesis strongly, lands Tumin's cuts precisely, and then concedes the universality problem and the allocation question has produced the paragraph that separates first-class scripts from competent ones, because it demonstrates that the writer's allegiance has not been purchased at the price of the evidence. The fuller stratification dispute is pursued in the [marxist theory of class guide](/2011/09/01/marxist-theory-of-class-guide/), whose treatment of class boundaries complements the present focus on the functionalist thesis itself.

There is a final observation about this section's place in the comparison as a whole, and it returns to the shared form. The Davis and Moore thesis is the most uncompromisingly functional explanation in either literature: an institution, stratification, explained entirely by the requirement it meets for the containing system. Its Marxist critics, in replying, frequently construct explanations of identical shape, stratification explained by the requirements of capital accumulation, legitimation, or labour discipline. Tumin himself, notably, was no Marxist, and his critique proceeded by demanding evidence and mechanisms rather than by substituting a rival beneficiary. That distribution of roles, a functionalist thesis, a methodological critique from outside both camps, and functional replies from the Marxist side, is the comparison in microcosm, and it previews the misattribution corrections that follow, where the critics turn out to be standing in rooms the textbooks do not assign them.

## Inequality: necessity, exploitation, and the ground between

Step back from the named thesis to the wider dispute it crystallises, because candidates meet the dispute in forms that never mention Davis, Moore, or Tumin. The functionalist instinct about inequality, across many authors, is to treat it as a by-product of problems any complex order must solve: coordination requires hierarchy, hierarchy requires differential authority, differential authority attracts differential reward, and the resulting gradient, while regrettable in its excesses, is the price of competence at scale. The Marxist instinct is to treat the gradient as the visible surface of a relation: the division between those who own productive assets and those who must sell their capacity to work, with the rewards of the first group measuring, in the last analysis, the surplus extracted from the second. Between the instincts lies a genuinely empirical territory, the study of how much of any observed distribution tracks scarcity and training, how much tracks ownership and bargaining power, and how much tracks neither, persisting through custom, discrimination, and institutional inertia. Both traditions, at their best, have contributed to mapping that territory. Both, at their worst, have announced the map's contents in advance of the survey.

One asymmetry deserves honest statement. Functionalism's inequality story is fundamentally a story about positions, slots in a structure that would exist under many property regimes, while Marxism's is a story about relations between people, owners and non-owners, that a transformation of property could in principle abolish. The asymmetry generates different predictions about reform. If inequality is positional, then changing who owns the factories leaves the gradient largely intact, since the positions and their scarcity relations persist. If inequality is relational, then changing ownership strikes at the gradient's root, though the positional residue, differentials among workers themselves, requires separate handling that Marxist writing has not always supplied with candour. Historical experience with both kinds of change offers partial comfort to each side, which is precisely why the dispute remains a live comparison rather than a settled result, and why examination questions continue to be set on it. Candidates should resist the marking scheme's temptation, and their own, to declare a winner the evidence has not crowned.

### Can inequality be explained without mentioning class?

partly, since scarcity of trained competence, coordination needs, and inherited custom all generate differentials in societies of many kinds. The Marxist claim is not that such factors are fictions but that ownership relations organise the largest differentials and set the terms on which the other factors operate and reward.

The ground between the instincts is where most working sociologists actually labour, combining positional and relational accounts in proportions set by the case at hand, and a mature comparison article should acknowledge that the examination's two-column format is a scaffold for learning rather than a map of the discipline's practice. The scaffold earns its keep by clarifying the pure logics. The logics earn theirs by surviving contact with the mixed cases. What neither logic survives, as the coming sections argue, is the demand for a selection mechanism, and it is time to meet the critics who pressed that demand most sharply, beginning with the one who is persistently quoted from the wrong side of the house.

## The critic inside the house: Merton restored to his own tradition

Every comparison generates its own folklore, and the folklore of this one includes a quotation habit that misplaces a major figure. Robert Merton's distinctions, between manifest and latent functions, between functions and dysfunctions, between the functions an institution performs and the functional alternatives that might perform them, are routinely cited in textbooks and examination answers as criticisms of functionalism, as though Merton stood outside the perspective administering corrections to it. He did not. Merton was a functionalist, arguably the most influential functionalist of his generation, and his distinctions were forged as repairs to his own tradition, attempts to rescue functional analysis from the overstatements of its less careful practitioners by making its claims narrower, testable, and alert to consequences nobody intended. Quoting him as functionalism's critic is not a small bibliographic slip. It reverses the intellectual geography of the debate, and it robs students of the most instructive fact in the whole area: that the perspective's most damaging qualifications were produced by its own leading practitioner, dissatisfied with its textbook form.

Recover what Merton actually objected to, because the objections are specific. Earlier functionalist writing, in his diagnosis, leaned on three postulates that could not survive inspection. The first held that a society's standardised practices are functional for the society as a whole, a postulate of universal functional unity that ignores how a practice may serve one group while injuring another. The second held that every standardised practice performs positive functions, a postulate of universal functionalism that ignores dysfunctions, consequences that lessen a system's adaptation, and non-functions, survivals with no significant effects at all. The third held that the practices performing functions are indispensable, a postulate that ignores functional alternatives, the possibility that different arrangements might meet the same requirement. Against these postulates Merton erected a more disciplined apparatus. Functions are observed consequences contributing to a specified system's adjustment. Dysfunctions are consequences detracting from it. Manifest functions are intended and recognised by participants. Latent functions are neither intended nor recognised. And every functional claim must specify the unit for which the consequence holds, a group, a stratum, an institution, since a practice functional for one unit may be dysfunctional for another in the same society.

### How are manifest functions distinguished from latent ones?

a difference in intention and recognition among participants. Manifest functions are consequences people aim at and acknowledge, the instruction a school openly provides. Latent functions are consequences nobody aims at or acknowledges, the friendships, status sorting, and custodial care a school supplies while officially only teaching.

Linger over the unit specification requirement, because it quietly detonates the textbook contrast this article has been dismantling. Once a functionalist is required to ask functional for whom, the distance between the perspectives narrows to a crack. Merton's own apparatus licenses the question whether schooling is functional for employers and dysfunctional for the pupils it sorts into failure, whether a ritual integrates the dominant group while marking a minority for exclusion, whether stratification serves those it rewards while injuring those it ranks last. Those are questions a Marxist asks in class vocabulary. Merton asked them in functional vocabulary, from inside the tradition, without surrendering the tradition's core commitment to explanation by consequences for specified systems. The sophisticated functionalism that results is not the complacent doctrine of the comparison tables, the one in which everything works for everyone and conflict is a malfunction. It admits conflict between units as a standing possibility, admits that integration for some is domination's other face for others, and still insists that consequence analysis is the way to understand persistence. A candidate who deploys Merton against functionalism has therefore misread the map twice: the man was repairing the house, and the repairs move the house toward the territory the candidate thought belonged to the opposing camp.

Why does the misplacement persist? Partly because teaching needs critics, and a comparison organised as thesis against antithesis will conscript any quotable qualification into the antithesis column. Partly because Merton's tone is genuinely critical, astringent about sloppy functionalist habits, and tone is easier to classify than allegiance. Partly because his strain theory of deviance, goals prescribed culturally while means are distributed unequally, sounds like a conflict account and is taught beside Marxist criminology, where its structural kinship is real. None of these explanations excuses the error, and all of them make it instructive. The correction also carries a strategic lesson for candidates: citing Merton correctly, as functionalism's internal reformer whose unit question opens the door to class analysis, earns more credit than citing him incorrectly as an external critic, because the correct citation demonstrates command of the debate's actual structure. Accuracy, on this topic, is not pedantry laid over the argument. Accuracy is the argument.

### Does functionalism have room for conflict?

the sophisticated ones plainly do not, since Merton's apparatus builds in consequences that injure some units while serving others, and his deviance theory grounds lawbreaking in a structural contradiction between prescribed goals and distributed means. Denial of conflict belongs to the perspective's caricature, not to its strongest published form.

A parallel correction, smaller but of the same species, concerns the assumption that all institutions are functional for the whole society. The assumption is the first of the postulates Merton discarded, which means the most influential functionalist critic of that assumption was himself a functionalist, and the version of functionalism taught in comparison tables, in which every practice harmoniously serves the general good, is a version the tradition's own leading figure had already dismantled before most of the textbooks were written. Students are thus examined, in part, on their ability to criticise a position its holders had abandoned, and the candidates who notice, who write that the naive doctrine is a teaching fossil rather than a living theory, convert a trap into an opportunity. The same pattern, a tradition's internal critics outpacing its external caricature, is about to recur in stronger form, because the sharpest assault on functional explanation as such came from inside the Marxist house, and it was aimed at Marxism's own explanatory habits.

## The sharpest critic wore Marxist colours

The usual account of the functional explanation debate assigns the roles by camp: functionalists explain by consequences, their opponents object, and Marxists watch from the conflict side of the room. The actual history reverses the casting. The most rigorous and consequential criticism of functional explanation within sociology was produced by analytical Marxists, scholars working self-consciously inside the Marxist tradition, applying the tools of analytic philosophy and rational-choice modelling to that tradition's own claims, and finding its characteristic explanations wanting by standards they refused to lower for their own side. G. A. Cohen, the tradition's most distinguished philosophical defender, simultaneously became its most exacting examiner on precisely this point, and Jon Elster pressed the examination further into outright scepticism about whole classes of Marxist functional claims. The companion article on [ga cohen functional explanation](/2020/01/15/ga-cohen-functional-explanation/) develops the philosophical detail; what matters for the comparison is the placement of the critics, and what their placement reveals.

Cohen's position is subtle enough to be misstated in both directions, so it deserves careful summary. He held that Marxist theory does, pervasively and characteristically, explain by consequences: the relations of production are explained by their tendency to develop the productive forces, the legal and political superstructure by its tendency to stabilise those relations. Rather than deny the pattern, Cohen defended its legitimacy in principle, arguing that consequence explanations can be valid forms, familiar from biology, while insisting that their full confirmation requires elaboration, an account of the mechanism, the feedback loop, through which the beneficial consequence maintains the item it benefits. An unexplained consequence claim is, in his terms, a promissory note. Some Marxist notes can be cashed, through class power, state enforcement, market selection among firms, and ideological filtering among ideas. Others, he conceded with a candour that startled admirers and opponents alike, might never be cashed, and the theory's honesty required marking which was which. The defence and the demand arrived in the same hand: functional explanation is legitimate, and most of its unelaborated uses are incomplete.

Elster took the demand and doubted the currency. Surveying Marxist functional claims, he argued that many lacked any plausible feedback loop, that the alleged beneficiaries, classes conceived as collective actors, often could not perform the selecting and maintaining work the explanations silently assigned them, and that the tradition's confidence in beneficial consequences for capital concealed a slide from the true claim that an institution has such consequences to the unsupported claim that it exists because of them. His remedy was methodological individualism in a specific, limited sense: explanations should descend to the level of actors, their beliefs, opportunities, and interactions, from which macro patterns can be rebuilt without teleology. Whether or not a candidate accepts the remedy, the diagnosis's origin is the fact to retain. It issued from a scholar who had written a major sympathetic reconstruction of Marx's thought, who regarded himself as working within its problems, and whose scepticism was the scepticism of an insider auditing the family accounts.

### Who criticised functional explanation most sharply?

analytical Marxists examining their own tradition, Cohen demanding that consequence claims be elaborated with feedback mechanisms, Elster doubting that many such claims possess plausible loops at all. The reversal matters because the standard account casts Marxists as spectators of a debate between functionalists and their external opponents.

Set the two corrections of this and the preceding section side by side and a pattern emerges that reorganises the whole comparison's cast list. Functionalism's most effective internal critic, Merton, qualified the doctrine from within and pushed it toward questions about whose interests a practice serves. Marxism's most effective internal critics, Cohen and Elster, attacked the doctrine's shared explanatory form from inside the house and pressed it toward mechanisms and actors. The traditions' textbook images, functionalism complacent and uncriticised, Marxism methodologically innocent, are both artefacts of teaching compression. The living traditions criticised themselves at exactly the points where the comparison needs criticism, which is why the strongest examination answers increasingly read as dialogues within each perspective rather than as a duel between two monoliths. A candidate who can stage those internal dialogues, Merton against the postulates, Cohen and Elster against unelaborated consequence claims, possesses the comparison at the level its examiners most reward.

There is a deeper moral here about intellectual traditions generally, and the article states it plainly because it governs the verdict still to come. A tradition's maturity is measurable by the quality of the criticism it generates against itself. By that measure both traditions, at their best, are mature: each produced, from its own resources, the objections that textbooks attribute to its enemies. The measure also supplies a fair test for the working forms students actually meet. A functionalism that has absorbed Merton is a different, stronger theory than the fossil of the tables. A Marxism that has absorbed Cohen's demand for elaboration produces a stronger and different theory from one content to name beneficiaries. Comparing the strong forms against each other is harder work than comparing the fossils, and it is the only comparison worth the examination's highest band.

## Teleology: the objection that touches both

Teleology means explaining something by the purpose it serves or the end it realises, letting a future state do causal work in the present, and it is the oldest charge in the file against both perspectives. The functionalist writes that the rain dance exists to promote solidarity, and the sentence's grammar invites the reading that solidarity, an outcome, reached back and produced the dance. The Marxist writes that the ideology exists to legitimate domination, and the same grammar invites the same reading, legitimation reaching back to produce the ideas. In neither case does the careful author believe in backward causation. In both cases the sentence, unelaborated, asserts a connection between an item and its beneficial effect while omitting the machinery that could make the connection explanatory rather than merely suggestive. The charge of teleology is the demand to produce the machinery or withdraw the assertion, and it lands on both desks with identical force because it is addressed to the shared grammatical form, not to the beneficiary named in any particular sentence.

Writers in both traditions developed defences, and the defences converge in instructive ways. The first defence distinguishes explanation by consequences from explanation by purposes: a trait can be explained by what it does without anyone intending it to do that, as the heart's pumping explains the heart without a designer. The defence is sound where a selection process operates, and it relocates the dispute to the existence of the process, which is where the next section's mechanism discussion lives. The second defence pleads shorthand: the functional sentence abbreviates a longer causal story the author could tell on demand, about reinforcement, imitation, funding, and suppression. The plea succeeds case by case, whenever the longer story is actually producible, and fails whenever the demand reveals that no longer story exists. The third defence is candour about levels: some functional claims are offered as heuristics, prompts for research rather than finished explanations, directing attention to consequences a purely causal history might overlook. Heuristic value is real, and both traditions have plainly profited from it, but a heuristic promoted silently into an explanation commits the teleological offence in the act of denying it.

### Which objections strike both perspectives with equal force?

teleology in explaining causes by effects, the frequent absence of a selection mechanism connecting benefit to persistence, determinism that leaves agency unexplained, and a resistance to falsification, since each framework can accommodate surprising observations by redescribing them in its own vocabulary of functions or interests.

Candidates should learn to deploy the teleology charge symmetrically and to recognise its limits. Symmetrically, because the charge's force derives from form, and a script that aims it at functionalism alone while writing functional Marxist sentences unexamined will be caught by any marker who has read Cohen. With limits, because teleology is a charge about unelaborated claims, not a proof that consequence explanation is always void: biology's consequence explanations are the standing demonstration that the form can be legitimate, and the social theorist's task is to show a mechanism of comparable discipline, not to abandon the form in embarrassment. The strongest scripts therefore treat teleology as a standard to meet rather than a weapon to wield, asking of each tradition, claim by claim, whether the machinery has been produced. That treatment converts a stale criticism section into an audit, and audits are what the comparison question, at its most ambitious, is asking for.

## The missing mechanism

The mechanism problem is teleology's constructive twin: it asks not what is wrong with functional sentences but what would make them right. A mechanism, for these purposes, is a process by which an item's beneficial consequences feed back to sustain the item. Biology's paradigm is natural selection, variation generating candidates, consequences sorting them, inheritance preserving the sorted results. Social life offers candidate processes in abundance, and cataloguing them fairly across both traditions is the quickest way to see how much repair work each has done. Reinforcement operates where practices that please powerful audiences get repeated and funded. Imitation operates where successful institutions are copied by neighbours and rivals. Deliberate design operates where legislators and administrators construct arrangements for stated purposes and revise them against results. Suppression operates where arrangements threatening powerful interests are starved, banned, or absorbed. Drift and lock-in operate where early advantages compound, standards entrench, and switching costs preserve arrangements whose original benefits have faded.

Measured against the catalogue, the traditions' repair records differ by institution rather than by camp, which is itself a finding. Marxist accounts of law and the state often specify mechanisms with real precision: property rules persist because courts enforce them, legislatures staffed through expensive politics amend them cautiously, and the costs of overturning them fall on organised defenders while the benefits of overturning fall on diffuse challengers. That is a feedback story with named actors and observable operations. Marxist accounts of ideology are frequently less precise, asserting that ideas serve domination without always tracing who selects the ideas, through which institutions, against which rivals, with what sanctions for deviation. Functionalist accounts of ritual and socialisation can be equally two-sided: the reinforcement of norms through approval and embarrassment is observable in daily life at a granularity few Marxist mechanisms match, while functionalist accounts of whole-system needs, a society requiring integration as a body requires circulation, float at an altitude where no mechanism could be sighted. The honest audit therefore refuses the camp-level verdict. It reports, instead, that mechanism specification is a craft virtue distributed unevenly through both literatures, and that the comparison's most defensible evaluative claim is about particular works rather than about perspectives in the abstract.

### What turns a functional claim into a mechanism claim?

the presence of a feedback process. Function talk names a benefit an arrangement confers on some system. Mechanism talk traces how that benefit sustains the arrangement, through reinforcement, imitation, design, or suppression, closing the loop that teleology leaves open and converting a suggestive pattern into an explanation.

One further refinement protects candidates from a common overcorrection. Demanding mechanisms does not require demanding intentions. A mechanism can run blindly, through differential funding and differential survival, with no actor aiming at the outcome the mechanism produces, and many of the strongest functional explanations in both traditions are blind in exactly that way: schools that alienate employers lose partnerships and resources without any conspirator arranging the loss; rituals that fail to move their participants are quietly dropped from calendars without a committee on solidarity voting them down. Elster's stricter demand, that explanations descend to individual actors and their reasons, goes beyond the mechanism requirement into a separate methodological commitment, and candidates should present it as such, a further step that some analytical Marxists took and others, Cohen among them, declined, holding that consequence laws might stand at the social level without reduction. The debate between those positions is a debate within the Marxist tradition about its own foundations, one more instance of the internal criticism pattern, and it gives the comparison a depth the consensus-conflict table cannot approach.

## Determinism and the missing agent

Both perspectives subordinate the individual's agency to what the system requires, the snippet answer's third clause, and the subordination draws a common criticism from the interpretive and interactionist traditions that arose partly in protest against it. The protest's classic form asks where people went. In functionalist systems writing, actors can appear as occupants of roles, executing scripts the structure wrote, their inner lives reduced to internalised values and role strain. In orthodox Marxist writing, actors can appear as bearers of relations, executing the logic of capital or of class position, their beliefs reduced to ideology and their choices to the menu their location serves. Against both, the interpretive traditions insisted that social life is accomplished moment by moment by persons interpreting situations, negotiating meanings, and improvising conduct that no script fully specifies. The insistence was not a denial that structures exist. It was a denial that structures act, except through the interpretive work of the people supposedly borne by them.

The criticism lands, and both traditions have spent decades absorbing it, with results candidates should know in outline. Functionalism's absorption ran through revisions emphasising that roles are made as well as taken, that incumbents negotiate expectations, bend them, and occasionally rewrite them, so that the system's script is better pictured as a draft under continuous revision by its performers. Marxism's absorption ran through its humanist and cultural wings: Thompson's insistence that class is a relationship and an experience, made by people in struggle rather than delivered by structure; Gramsci's hegemony, which requires the active consent of the governed and therefore their continuous interpretive labour; the ethnographic work on pupils and workers whose resistance and partial insight complicated every reproduction story told about them. The absorptions are genuine, and they share a shape: agency returns to the theory as the medium through which structures operate, rather than as an illusion structures dispense with. A structure that works only through interpretation is still a structure, and its explanatory claims survive in moderated form.

### Where did agency go in structural theories?

into the roles and relations actors were said to bear, until interpretive critics objected that structures act only through persons interpreting situations. Both traditions absorbed the objection by restoring agency as the medium of structural operation, people making roles, consenting, resisting, and improvising inside constraints they did not choose.

Moderation, however, has a price each tradition pays in a different coin, and naming the prices sharpens the comparison. Functionalism pays in determinacy: once actors negotiate and rewrite roles, the system's requirements lose their power to predict conduct, and the theory's explanations soften toward redescription. Marxism pays in revolutionary expectation: once consent must be won continuously and resistance is endemic, the reproduction of an order becomes a contingent achievement rather than a structural guarantee, and the theory's confidence about trajectories softens accordingly. Neither price is a refutation. Both are the normal costs of theoretical maturity, the exchange of sweeping power for accuracy at the joints. Candidates who present determinism as a fatal flaw unique to one perspective reveal that they have read only one perspective's critics. Candidates who present it as a shared pressure, absorbed by both at comparable cost, reveal that they have read the debate.

## Falsification and the accommodating theory

The last shared criticism concerns testability. A theory that can accommodate every possible observation explains, in the philosopher's suspicious sense, too much. Functionalism's accommodating habit is notorious: an institution that integrates is functional, an institution that disrupts is dysfunctional yet thereby functional for change, a practice with no visible effect is latently functional in ways awaiting discovery, so the vocabulary can redescribe any finding without ever predicting one in advance. Marxism's accommodating habit is its mirror: a reform that improves workers' lives is a concession stabilising the system, a reform refused is the system's rigidity, prosperity is exploitation succeeding, crisis is exploitation failing, and the framework's central claims survive every outcome because each outcome was, on inspection, what the framework led one to expect. The symmetry is uncomfortable for partisans of either side and is therefore exactly what an honest comparison must state.

The traditions' best defenders reply in parallel, and the parallel replies deserve equal time. The functionalist reply distinguishes the framework from its claims: functionalism as a perspective generates hypotheses, about integration, about strain, about the consequences of specific arrangements, and the hypotheses are testable even if the perspective itself, like any research programme, is judged by fertility rather than by single experiments. The Marxist reply makes the same distinction with the same structure: the framework directs attention to property, class, and surplus, while its particular claims, about the labour share, about crisis tendencies, about the class composition of state personnel, stand or fall empirically. Both replies invoke, whether knowingly or not, the philosophy of science's verdict that large frameworks are appraised by the progress of the research they inspire. Both replies are respectable. And both concede the criticism's practical core, which is that lazy work inside either framework can drift into unfalsifiable redescription, and that the drift is a standing temptation the framework's vocabulary encourages. A candidate who states the criticism, states both replies, and concludes that vigilance rather than allegiance is the remedy has written the evaluation paragraph in its mature form.

### Why does falsification trouble both frameworks?

because each possesses a vocabulary rich enough to redescribe any outcome as confirmation, integration or legitimation, success or stabilisation, so that no observation is scheduled in advance as counting against the perspective. The defence, shared by both, separates testable particular claims from the framework that generates them.

With the shared criticisms assembled, teleology, mechanism, determinism, falsifiability, the comparison's architecture stands complete enough to support a verdict, but two movements remain before verdict. Each perspective deserves a section stating what it sees that the other systematically misses, because a comparison that only catalogues shared flaws has not yet explained why both perspectives persist, attract serious adherents, and continue to organise research. Persistence, as this article has argued throughout, is itself evidence requiring explanation, and the explanation, in this case, is that each tradition illuminates a region the other leaves dark.

## Origins: two functionalisms and a borrowed grammar

Functionalism did not begin as a single doctrine, and recovering its plural origins dissolves another textbook simplification, the picture of a perspective springing fully formed from one founder's head. One lineage is anthropological. Malinowski, working from fieldwork rather than from the armchair, argued that cultural practices persist because they satisfy needs, biological needs first, food, shelter, reproduction, and derived needs that organised societies generate in meeting the first kind. His functionalism is resolutely empirical in temper: go to the practice, watch what it does for the people practising it, distrust explanations that reach for origins nobody witnessed. Radcliffe-Brown, his contemporary and rival, shifted the beneficiary from the individual's needs to the social structure itself: a practice's function is its contribution to the continuity of the structure, the web of relations among persons and groups. The shift matters enormously for the comparison, because it is Radcliffe-Brown's version, structure as beneficiary, that Marxism's grammar resembles. When a Marxist explains a practice by its contribution to the continuity of class relations, the sentence's skeleton is the anthropologist's, with the structure redescribed in the vocabulary of production.

The second lineage is sociological and older in its roots. Durkheim supplied its founding methodological rule: the cause of a social fact and its function are separate questions, to be investigated separately, and confusing them is the beginner's error. His own practice honoured the rule unevenly, which is itself instructive, but the rule's statement matters because it shows the tradition's founder explicitly warning against the teleological slide his successors are accused of naturalising. Durkheim's substantive studies then modelled functional reasoning at its most powerful: punishment explained not by its effects on offenders but by its service to the collective conscience; religion explained by its service to solidarity; the division of labour explained by the moral integration its interdependence makes necessary and possible. Each study takes a phenomenon whose manifest account is available, deterrence, worship, economic efficiency, and argues that the phenomenon's persistence is unintelligible until its contribution to the moral community is grasped. Replace moral community with class order and the studies' argumentative rhythm survives intact, a fact Durkheim's Marxist readers have always quietly known.

Parsons gathered the lineages into a system. His mature theory organised social life into a hierarchy of systems, behavioural, personal, social, cultural, each with its own problems, and within the social system he distinguished four functional imperatives that any enduring collectivity must meet: adaptation to its environment, attainment of its goals, integration of its parts, and the maintenance of its latent patterns, the values and motivations that keep members committed. The scheme, famous by its initials, is the most ambitious functionalist construction ever attempted, and its ambition is precisely what the comparison should note. Parsons was not describing this or that institution. He was specifying, in advance, the complete list of problems a society must solve to persist, so that any institution could be located by the problem it principally addresses. Education falls under pattern maintenance and integration. The polity falls under goal attainment. The economy falls under adaptation. The scheme's elegance explains its classroom longevity. Its vulnerability is the one this article has anatomised: the imperatives are asserted as requirements, institutions are matched to them by interpretation, and the feedback that would convert the matching into explanation, societies failing the imperatives and thereby disappearing at observable rates, remains elusive at the scale of whole social systems.

### Why does functionalism have two birthplaces?

because anthropologists studying small-scale societies and sociologists studying industrial ones converged on contribution talk from different directions, one through individual needs, the other through the moral community. The structural version, practices explained by continuity of relations, is the one whose grammar Marxist explanation shares.

A third, quieter lineage deserves naming because it complicates the camp map once more: the functionalism of common sense in administration and engineering, the practical habit of asking of any procedure what it is for, what breaks if it lapses, and who notices first. Organisations generate this habit spontaneously, since managers inherit arrangements whose purposes are undocumented and must reverse-engineer them from consequences. Much functionalist sociology reads, at its best, like this practical habit raised to method, and much Marxist institutional analysis reads the same way with the beneficiary question sharpened. The convergence suggests that contribution reasoning is not an ideology imported into social science but a natural response to organised complexity, which any investigator of persistence will reinvent. If that suggestion is right, the shared form is overdetermined: two traditions facing the same phenomenon, durable institutions, with the same cognitive tools, were bound to arrive at the same grammar and then to dispute, with genuine passion, the beneficiary. The dispute is the science. The grammar is the water both fish swim in, invisible until an article like this one names it.

## The Marxist architecture and its functional load-bearing walls

Turn the same genealogical attention on Marxism, because its functional grammar is not a late contamination but a founding feature, visible in the architecture's most famous metaphor. The base and superstructure figure, stated in the preface of 1859, assigns the legal, political, and ideological forms of a society to its economic foundation, and the assignment, in the tradition's standard elaborations, is functional in precisely the anatomised sense: the superstructure's forms are explained by their service to the base, stabilising the relations of production, legitimating them, and managing their conflicts. Cohen's philosophical reconstruction made the functional character explicit and defended it as the theory's distinctive strength, its primacy thesis and development thesis both resting on consequence explanation. The relations of production, on that reconstruction, are as they are because they suit the development of the productive forces. The superstructure is as it is because it suits the stability of the relations. Suitability, tendency to benefit, contribution to persistence: the vocabulary varies, the grammar holds.

Reproduction is the second load-bearing wall, and the concept's career shows the grammar at work across the tradition's development. Early Marxist writing spoke of production's daily and generational renewal almost in passing. Later theory elevated reproduction into the central process a social order must accomplish: labour power must be produced, fed, rested, trained, and replaced; the relations within which production occurs must be renewed in law, in habit, and in belief; the means of production must be maintained and accumulated. Althusser's essay on ideological state apparatuses organised the whole superstructure as a reproduction machine, schools at its centre, and whatever a reader thinks of the essay's politics, its explanatory form is unmistakably the one under examination: institutions inventoried by their contribution to the renewal of a system of relations. Feminist theorists then extended the reproduction concept into the household, as the family section recorded, demonstrating the grammar's fertility inside the tradition that officially opposed functionalism. A concept that keeps generating research across a century, in the hands of a tradition's successive generations, is not an embarrassment to be explained away. It is evidence that the grammar answers a real question, the persistence question, which no theory of society can decline to answer.

### What does the base and superstructure figure commit Marxism to?

an explanatory architecture in which legal, political, and ideological forms are accounted for substantially by their service to an economic foundation, stabilising its relations and managing its conflicts. Cohen's reconstruction states the commitment openly: consequence explanation is not an accident of the theory but its distinctive explanatory wager.

The tradition's internal diversity on this point deserves a paragraph, because candidates often meet a monolithic Marxism that exists only in summaries. One wing, humanist and historicist, distrusts the architecture's mechanical readings and stresses praxis, the making of history by conscious actors, treating base and superstructure as abstractions from a living totality rather than as floors of a building. Another wing, structuralist, embraces the architecture and elaborates it. A third wing, the analytical one already encountered, submits the architecture to logical audit and rebuilds what survives. The wings dispute the functional grammar's scope and status with real ferocity, which is precisely why the grammar cannot be dismissed as an external imputation: it is the tradition's own contested inheritance. Functionalism's internal diversity mirrors the pattern, grand system builders against middle-range empiricists, Merton explicitly rebuking the ambition to construct total theory in favour of theories of the middle range, testable accounts of specified mechanisms in specified domains. The parallel diversities offer candidates a final structural symmetry to exploit: each tradition contains a totalising wing and a modest wing, and the interesting comparisons often run between the modest wings, where mechanisms are specified and claims are sized to evidence, rather than between the totalising manifestos the textbooks prefer to duel.

## The biology parallel and the philosophy of social science

The problem of functional explanation is a standing concern in the philosophy of social science and in evolutionary biology's methodology, and the comparison gains depth when candidates know that sociology's quarrel is a local instance of a general one. Biology faced the teleology charge first and in its sharpest form, since adaptation talk seems to attribute foresight to a mindless process: the eye is for seeing, the wing is for flight, as though purposes preceded the organs. The discipline's resolution is the benchmark against which social functionalism is measured. Natural selection supplies a mechanism in which consequences genuinely explain presence, because heritable variation plus differential reproductive success guarantees that traits contributing to survival and reproduction become prevalent for that reason. The explanation is not teleological in the offending sense, since no future state causes anything; past consequences, iterated across generations, do the causing. Philosophers call the validated form etiological: a trait's function is the effect for which it was selected. The benchmark's lesson for sociology is exact. Consequence explanation is innocent where a selection history exists and suspect where one is merely presumed.

Biology also teaches the complementary lesson, that even with selection available, functional attribution is fallible and must be disciplined by alternatives. Traits persist as by-products of other traits, as developmental constraints, as historical inheritances whose original advantages have lapsed, and the adaptationist habit of inventing a benefit for every feature has drawn famous internal criticism, the spandrels argument, from biologists themselves. The parallel to Merton is striking and worth a candidate's sentence: biology's leading internal critics attacked universal adaptationism the way Merton attacked universal functionalism, by insisting on by-products, survivals, and alternatives, and both corrections came from inside the houses they corrected. Social science adds its own candidate selection processes, market competition among firms, electoral competition among parties, prestige competition among institutions and ideas, differential funding, differential imitation, and the analytical task, in any concrete case, is to establish whether one of these processes actually operated with the strength the explanation requires. Where firms compete and fail at observable rates, functional claims about organisational forms approach the biological benchmark. Where whole societies are the units and failure is rare, ambiguous, and slow, the claims drift toward the presumption the critics target. The gradient, from benchmark to presumption, is the most precise instrument this article can hand a candidate for evaluating functional claims in either tradition.

### How does biology discipline functional claims?

by supplying a selection history that makes consequences genuinely explanatory, and by cultivating internal critics who insist that some traits are by-products, constraints, or survivals rather than adaptations. Social explanation earns the same confidence only where an analogous sorting process demonstrably operated at the required strength.

The philosophy of social science contributes a second instrument, the distinction between explaining an item and explaining a system state. Much confusion in the debate dissolves when the explanandum is pinned down. Explaining why a particular school exists, its founding charter, its benefactors, its site, is a causal-historical task in which functions play at most a supporting role. Explaining why schooling of a recognisable type exists in every complex society is a different task, and there consequence considerations legitimately dominate, because the pattern to be explained is the recurrence of a solution to a recurring problem. Functionalists sometimes slide from the second task's legitimacy to the first task's claims, offering system-level function as though it explained a particular institution's biography. Marxists slide symmetrically, offering class function as the explanation of a particular law's drafting history, when the drafting records show compromise, accident, and personalities the class story must labour to absorb. Pinning the explanandum, item or pattern, biography or recurrence, is a five-second discipline that dissolves a surprising fraction of published disagreement, and seminar leaders build exercises around it in the teacher layer below.

## Conflict inside functionalism: Coser, Dahrendorf, and the safety valve

No section of this article surprises students more than the discovery that functionalism produced a literature on the functions of conflict itself. Lewis Coser, working self-consciously within the functionalist tradition and drawing on Simmel, argued that conflict performs services for groups that a harmony-obsessed reading of the perspective would never predict. Conflict clarifies boundaries between groups, forcing members to define who they are against an opponent. Conflict releases tension through safety-valve institutions, channels for hostility that discharge pressure without rupturing the structure, the sanctioned feud, the ritual insult, the licensed protest. Conflict revitalises norms, since a challenge forces a group to restate and sometimes revise commitments that routine had allowed to go slack. Conflict even creates associations and coalitions, binding previously unrelated parties into new structures. The catalogue is functionalist to its fingertips: a phenomenon is explained and evaluated by its contributions to the persistence of the systems containing it. The phenomenon happens to be conflict, the very substance the textbook contrast assigns exclusively to the other column.

Ralf Dahrendorf pressed from a different direction, building a conflict theory of society that he explicitly framed as a complement rather than a replacement: society has two faces, consensus and coercion, and each theoretical tradition, functionalism and conflict theory, illuminates one face while the other tradition illuminates the remainder. His own model of imperatively coordinated associations, authority positions generating opposed interest groups, is structural and macro, subordinates agency to position, and explains order by the organisation of domination, which makes it, by the standards of this article's anatomy, a near relative of the Marxist account with the class concept replaced by authority. The genealogy matters because Dahrendorf arrived at a conflict structuralism from inside the debate about functionalism, not from the Marxist tradition at all, demonstrating again that the comparison's real divisions cut across the textbook camps. A candidate who can place Coser and Dahrendorf correctly, one functionalising conflict, the other declaring the two faces complementary, possesses evidence for the article's thesis that no amount of definitional argument could match: the traditions' practitioners kept producing each other's signature moves.

### Did a functionalist ever explain conflict itself functionally?

yes, since Coser's study of conflict's functions catalogued its services to groups, boundary clarification, tension release through safety valves, norm revitalisation, and coalition formation. The work dissolves the simple assignment of conflict to one column and consensus to the other from inside functionalism's own literature.

The safety-valve idea rewards a moment's comparison across the columns, because Marxist analysis possesses its exact counterpart under a different name. The sanctioned protest that discharges pressure harmlessly is, in Marxist vocabulary, the contained concession, the reform or ritual of opposition that stabilises an order by venting the grievances that might otherwise accumulate toward rupture. Carnival in one literature, licensed misrule whose inversion of hierarchy renews hierarchy by exhausting inversion's appeal, is read by historians in both vocabularies, sometimes by the same historian in successive works. When two traditions independently develop the same analytical device for the same phenomenon and differ only in the beneficiary they emphasise, the group's cohesion against the class order's security, the shared-form thesis is no longer an interpretation imposed on the material. It is a description of the material's own convergent development, and candidates may say so, with Coser as the citation that proves the convergence is documented rather than conjectured.

## Education again: the empirical contest

The grid's rows were stated as paired readings, and a comparison that stopped there would leave the most important question untouched: what happens when the readings meet evidence? Education supplies the richest empirical record, so the contest is staged there, with studies cited by author and finding in the manner this series prescribes for contested ground, and with the verdict left, honestly, in the divided state the record supports. The functionalist reading predicts that attainment tracks developed ability and effort, that expanding schooling expands opportunity, and that the occupational destinations of graduates reflect the skill requirements of their roles. The Marxist reading predicts that attainment tracks class origin even after measured ability is considered, that expansion inflates credentials rather than equalising chances, and that destinations reproduce the hierarchy of origins beneath a meritocratic surface.

The correspondence research associated with Bowles and Gintis supplied the Marxist reading's most cited evidence: the traits schools reward, punctuality, deference, sustained application to externally set tasks, mirror the traits workplaces require, and the economic returns to schooling were argued to flow substantially through this behavioural socialisation and through credential screening rather than through the cognitive skills the curriculum advertises. Their critics replied that the returns to measured cognitive skill are real, that the correspondence evidence conflates correlation with design, and that no conspiracy of employers is needed to explain why schools and workplaces, both being organised human activities, prize reliability. The exchange is a model of the mechanism audit this article recommends: the Marxist claim gained strength exactly where it specified processes, reward structures in classrooms, screening in hiring, and lost strength where it asserted a systemic fit without tracing the fitting.

Research on class origin and attainment complicated both readings in the productive way good evidence does. Studies in the mobility tradition found persistent origin effects on educational outcomes, children of professional families attaining more at every level of measured ability, which the pure functionalist sorting story does not predict and the reproduction story does. The same literature found genuine mobility as well, substantial minorities of each generation crossing the positions of their parents, which crude reproduction stories, schools as machines stamping origin onto destiny, do not predict either. The mature positions converged toward mechanisms: cultural resources in the home that schools reward without teaching, information and confidence in navigating choices, risk aversion differing by class at identical attainment, each mechanism specified, measurable, and compatible with a moderated version of both grand readings. Candidates should note the moral for the verdict: the empirical frontier of the debate lives at the level of mechanisms, and the perspectives survive there as suppliers of candidate mechanisms rather than as total explanations. That is the shared form's audit operating in the wild, and it vindicates the article's defeater logic, since every mechanism actually delivered moves its row of the grid out of pure functional assertion into causal explanation, whoever delivers it.

### What does the attainment evidence do to both readings?

it disciplines both, since persistent origin effects embarrass the claim that sorting simply matches ability to roles, while substantial mobility embarrasses the claim that schooling simply stamps origin onto destiny. The surviving debate concerns specified mechanisms, home resources, information, risk, through which advantage transmits.

One more empirical theatre deserves a paragraph because it tests functionalism's favourite claim, the value consensus, directly. Surveys of values across class positions find broad agreement on abstract commitments, fairness, effort, the importance of education, alongside sharp disagreement on their application, what fairness requires in taxation, what effort deserves in reward, whose education the state should fund first. Functionalism can absorb the pattern by distinguishing value consensus at the level of generalities from dissensus at the level of policy, and its careful writers do exactly that. Marxism absorbs the same pattern through hegemony's distinction between common sense, the absorbed framework, and good sense, the critical awareness that surfaces in application. The absorptions are, once more, structurally identical moves executed in rival vocabularies, each tradition conceding that the other's headline claim, consensus or domination, is too coarse for the data and refining its own claim until the two refinements describe the same stratified reality from different ends. A candidate who narrates that convergence has written evaluation of a high order, because it is drawn from the evidence's behaviour rather than from a preference declared in advance.

## Religion and the family again: protest, care, and the limits of upkeep

Return to religion with the mechanism audit in hand, because the row's two readings generate distinguishable expectations about when religion stabilises and when it detonates. The consolatory reading expects religious intensity to track suffering and insecurity, and to dampen collective challenges to the orders producing them. The solidarity reading expects religious practice to track the needs of community integration, and to strengthen members' attachment to the collectivity and its norms. The historical record supplies generous confirmations of each and stubborn anomalies for both, and the anomalies are the instructive part. Millenarian movements, in which the oppressed read sacred texts as promises of imminent overturning, display religion intensifying challenge rather than dampening it, and they arise precisely under the suffering conditions where consolation should be deepest. Liberation movements within established churches display clergy and laity turning the tradition's own resources against the order the tradition supposedly legitimates. Established churches in comfortable societies display the consolatory and integrative patterns in their purest form, congregations soothed and bound, hierarchy sanctified, challenge absent.

The pattern across the cases suggests a conditional law neither grand reading states but both can accommodate after the fact: religion's political effect depends on the resources its organisation places in whose hands, the autonomy its institutions possess from the powers they might legitimate, and the interpretive traditions available to its adherents. Where religious organisation is independent, textually rich in egalitarian material, and staffed from the oppressed themselves, protest flourishes. Where it is established, salaried by the order, and interpretively monopolised, consolation and legitimation dominate. Stating the conditional law is the mechanism audit's payoff on this row: the functions are real but conditional, delivered through organisational mechanisms that can be inspected, and the grand readings survive as first approximations whose conditions the research specifies. The full textual foundation, the passage in four clauses and the pharmaceutical context, is laid out in the companion article on Marx and religion in this series, and candidates writing the religion row should build on that restoration rather than on the fragment.

The family's empirical record disciplines its row similarly. The stabilisation claim predicts that household support buffers occupational strain, and the evidence of daily life and of wellbeing research broadly honours the prediction, while adding that the buffering is distributed with gross unevenness, one partner's stabilisation frequently purchased by the other's depletion, a distribution the classic statement's genderless language concealed. The reproduction claim predicts that households deliver renewed labour power at no charge to employers, and time-use evidence honours that prediction's core, the hours of unwaged care and domestic labour being vast and measurable, while complicating its framing, since carers describe their work in vocabularies of love and obligation that a pure subsidy account insults if it dismisses them. The mature literature on this row, much of it feminist work arguing with both traditions simultaneously, treats care as a real good produced under real relations of inequality, refusing the functionalist romance and the austere reduction alike. Candidates can use the row to demonstrate the article's deepest evaluative habit: when a phenomenon is genuinely double, serving a system and mattering to persons in ways no system exhausts, the perspectives' competition should end not in a winner but in a division of descriptive labour, each tradition assigned the aspect its tools handle best.

## Crime and the state again: labelling, legitimacy, and borrowed tools

The crime row deepens when a third tradition is allowed to testify, because the interactionist account of deviance, labelling theory, arose partly as a protest against both structural readings and ended by supplying each of them with tools. Labelling theory's core observation is that deviance is not a quality of acts but a consequence of the application of rules by audiences: the same conduct is criminal, eccentric, or invisible depending on who performs it, who observes it, and what label the observation succeeds in attaching. The tradition is not Marxist, a placement the series is careful to maintain across its crime coverage, since its founders worked from symbolic interactionism rather than from any theory of class or property. Its findings nevertheless entered Marxist criminology as the mechanism connecting class power to criminal statistics: if labels attach unevenly, and if the power to label and to resist labels is distributed by class, then the statistics record the labelling process as much as the conduct. Functionalism borrowed from the same shelf, since labelling's account of public reaction as constitutive of deviance extends Durkheim's insight that the community's response is where crime's social meaning is manufactured. One protest tradition, two structural borrowers: the episode is a compact lesson in how sociological tools migrate across camps while doctrines stay put, and it reinforces the article's standing advice to compare at the level of machinery rather than of banners. The developed treatment remains the companion criminology article in this series, whose account of corporate harm extends the definition argument beyond this summary's scope.

The state's row deepens through the concept of legitimacy, which both traditions need and neither can monopolise. Functionalism requires legitimacy as the felt obligation underwriting authority, the population's belief that the order's commands deserve obedience, and it treats legitimacy as a product of value consensus successfully transmitted. Marxism requires the same phenomenon and redescribes it as hegemony's achievement, consent organised through the institutions of civil society, with coercion in reserve for consent's failures. Weber, standing outside both traditions in the comparison this article is not conducting, supplied the typology of legitimate domination that both quietly use. The empirical study of legitimacy, why populations obey orders they could collectively refuse, why compliance collapses suddenly in some crises and holds astonishingly in others, proceeds in the present era with tools drawn from all three sources, and its findings habitually vindicate a layered account: habit and the absence of imaginable alternatives do much of the work, material dependence does more than value theory expects, sincere normative commitment does more than interest theory expects, and coercion explains the margins rather than the mass. The layered account is nobody's textbook column. It is what the comparison looks like after the evidence has been allowed to vote, and candidates who can sketch it demonstrate the judgement the grid was built to teach.

### How do they explain the state differently in practice?

less than their slogans suggest once legitimacy is examined, since both must explain mass obedience and both assemble the same ingredients, habit, dependence, belief, and reserve coercion, in different proportions. The practical difference lies in proportion and in remedy: transmit values better, or transform the relations dependence rests on.

A final deepening concerns the state's relative autonomy debate inside Marxism, because it is the point where the Marxist reading of the grid's most political row came closest to functionalism's balancing act. The instrumentalist picture, the state as a tool wielded by a unified capitalist class, faced the structuralist rejoinder that the state's separation from any particular capitalist is itself functional for the class as a whole, allowing long-term interests to override short-term greed. The rejoinder's grammar deserves a candidate's full attention: an institutional arrangement, state autonomy, is explained by its beneficial consequences for a system, class domination, without any actor necessarily intending those consequences. It is, by the anatomy of this article, a functional explanation of the purest water, produced at the summit of Marxist state theory, and its critics inside the tradition said so at the time. The episode is the state row's version of the Cohen story, the shared form surfacing inside the tradition's own flagship debate, noticed by the participants themselves. No further proof of the article's thesis should be required after this: wherever the comparison is pursued into the literatures' sophisticated reaches, the same grammar keeps appearing on both sides of the line, because the line was drawn through a shared country.

## The method workshop: how comparisons are actually built

Comparisons are manufactured objects, and a section on their manufacture belongs in an article whose central claim concerns a manufactured contrast. Four decisions determine any theory comparison's output before a single fact is consulted. The first decision fixes the level: are the theories compared as total worldviews, as middle-range claims about specified domains, or as methods? The consensus-conflict table compares worldviews and inherits their vagueness. This article compares methods and finds identity, then compares worldview conclusions and finds opposition, and both findings are true at their levels, which is why level discipline is the comparison builder's first craft. The second decision fixes the versions: founders' texts, mature revisions, or textbook fossils. Comparing fossils yields the sharpest contrasts and the least truth. Comparing mature versions, Mertonised functionalism against mechanism-audited Marxism, yields blurred contrasts and usable knowledge. The third decision fixes the evidence base: programmatic statements, empirical studies, or historical episodes, each base favouring different verdicts. The fourth decision fixes the question the comparison serves, examination display, research design, political orientation, since a comparison built to choose a research programme should weigh fertility, while one built for an examination should weigh discriminating articulation.

Armed with the four decisions, a candidate can reverse-engineer any set comparison, including the ones examiners set, and can state, in an answer's opening, which comparison is being performed: versions mature rather than fossil, level method first and conclusions second, evidence drawn from the institutions grid. That opening move, cheap to write and rare in scripts, signals to the marker that the ensuing material is organised by judgement rather than by recall. It also protects against the comparison's characteristic failure mode, in which a script assembles accurate paragraphs about each theory in sequence and never compares anything, the juxtaposition fallacy the grid's third column was designed to prevent. Comparison is an operation performed on paired material, and every paragraph of a comparison answer should be traceable to a pair: same institution, same phenomenon, same objection, read twice. Paragraphs that cannot name their pair are exposition wearing comparison's clothes, and markers, who read hundreds of scripts, identify the costume quickly.

### How is the institutions grid organised, and why that way?

by institution rather than by theory, one row per institution, the paired readings in adjacent columns and the shared presupposition in a third. Organising by institution forces the comparative operation paragraph by paragraph, where organising by theory invites sequential exposition that never actually compares anything at all.

The workshop's last tool is the steelman protocol this article applied to stratification, generalised. For each theory, state its strongest published form, with attribution, before evaluating. Then state the strongest published criticism, with attribution, noting the critic's address, insider or outsider, since the address changes the criticism's evidential weight. Then state what survives the criticism, conceding explicitly. The protocol is slower than slogan exchange and produces comparisons that resemble scholarship rather than sport. Its examination value is not incidental: markers reward attribution because attribution is checkable, reward insider criticism because it cannot be dismissed as misunderstanding, and reward concession because it demonstrates that the script's judgements are evidence-led. A candidate who internalises the protocol possesses a transferable instrument, applicable to every theory pairing in the discipline, which is the largest single yield this most examined of comparisons has to offer.

## Medicine and the sick role: a seventh institution

The grid fixed six institutions, but the comparison generalises, and medicine is the test case where generalisation earns its keep, because functionalism produced one of its most celebrated analyses there and Marxism one of its most energetic counter-literatures. Parsons defined the sick role as a structured social position with its own rights and obligations. The sick person is exempted from normal responsibilities and is not held responsible for the condition, two rights that protect the ill from blame and from the demand to perform. In exchange, the sick person must want to recover and must seek competent help, two obligations that protect society from malingering and from the indefinite enjoyment of exemption. The physician, in the paired analysis, occupies a role defined by technical competence, affective neutrality, and orientation to the patient's welfare, a professional pattern that functionalism generalised into its account of all professions. The analysis's elegance is undeniable: with four propositions it explains why societies surround illness with ceremony, why the doctor's authority is accepted by strangers at their most vulnerable, and why sickness, unmanaged, threatens the motivational fabric, since exemption without obligation would invite withdrawal from every demanding role.

The Marxist and adjacent critical literatures read the same consulting room through the grid's second column. Illness itself is socially produced in patterned ways: occupational disease tracks class position, the stresses of insecure work write themselves into bodies, and the distribution of sickness across a population describes the distribution of hazards, nutrition, housing, and exhaustion more faithfully than it describes any distribution of individual frailty. Treatment is likewise patterned: access, attention, and the benefit of the doubt flow unevenly, and the medical profession's authority, its control over entry, knowledge, and the definition of legitimate sickness, appears as a monopoly analogous to other monopolies, guarding scarcity and income behind the language of competence. The sick role's obligations, on this reading, discipline the workforce: the requirement to seek help and to recover routes the ill through a certifying authority that polices the boundary between legitimate absence and idleness on behalf of employers and insurers. Where functionalism sees a motivational safeguard, the critical reading sees a checkpoint.

Apply the third column and the row behaves exactly as the grid predicts. Both analyses treat the consulting room as a node in a system, both look past the manifest transaction, diagnosis and cure, to latent systemic work, motivation management in one vocabulary, labour discipline and hazard concealment in the other, and both explain the institution's persistence by that work. The empirical contest between them is, characteristically, mixed and mechanism-shaped: occupational gradients in health are among the most robust findings in social epidemiology, vindicating the production-of-illness claim, while the sick role's description of exemption and obligation continues to organise how patients, employers, and physicians actually negotiate absence, vindicating the functionalist phenomenology. A candidate who adds medicine as a self-built seventh row demonstrates the grid's portability, which is worth more in an examination than a seventh memorised row, since it proves the instrument was understood rather than the table transcribed.

### What does the sick role show about functionalist method?

its characteristic strengths and limits in miniature: a small set of role propositions illuminating a whole institution's ceremonies and authority, purchased by abstracting from who falls ill, whose hazards produce the sickness, and how unevenly the role's rights are honoured across class positions.

The professions extend the same paired reading across law, the clergy, and the academy, and the extension can be sketched in a paragraph because the pattern is now familiar. Functionalism treats professional authority as the reward and instrument of esoteric competence placed at the community's service, its codes of ethics functioning as collective guarantees that the competence will not be exploited. The critical reading treats the same authority as a closure project, a group's successful campaign to monopolise a market for services, control its own recruitment, and convert public trust into income and status, with ethics codes functioning partly as the campaign's public relations. Historical studies of particular professions have found both processes operating in proportions that vary by case, the competence real, the closure also real, and the interesting questions concern their interaction: how genuine expertise legitimises closure, how closure protects incompetence at the margins, how the two readings' mechanisms interlock. That interlocking, rather than either reading alone, is where the research frontier sits, and it sits there for every row of the grid, which is the deepest sense in which the comparison's two columns are complements in practice while remaining rivals in principle.

## Media, sport, and the manufacture of belonging

Mass communication supplies an eighth test, and sport a ninth, and both reward the grid treatment briefly applied. Functionalist analysis of media, in the tradition descending from Lasswell's classic inventory, assigns communication its functions of surveillance, correlation, and cultural transmission: the media watch the environment on the community's behalf, interpret events into shared frameworks, and hand the heritage to the next generation, with entertainment added by later writers as the system's release and reward. The Marxist tradition, from the Frankfurt School's culture industry thesis onward, reads the same output as ideology's most efficient delivery system: consciousness shaped by commercially produced diversion, dissent absorbed as style, audiences delivered to advertisers as the true commodity, and ownership concentration ensuring that the range of frameworks on offer stops short of the arrangements that own the offering. The paired readings were both constructed, note, by asking what the media do for a containing system and then naming the system differently, the community's need for orientation or capital's need for pacified consumers, and the empirical literatures on media effects, agenda setting, and audience activity have spent decades specifying the mechanisms, selective exposure, two-step flows, active decoding, that both grand readings initially skipped.

Sport condenses the same pairing into ninety minutes. The functionalist reading sees ritual integration in its purest surviving form: mass spectatorship generating collective effervescence on schedule, local and national identities rehearsed and renewed, deviance safely dramatised, excellence celebrated under rules whose fairness models the moral order spectators wish the wider world obeyed. The Marxist reading sees the diversion of grievance into harmless channels, the commodification of play, athletes as labour whose bodies are consumed by an entertainment industry, and stadia as cathedrals of consumption anchoring property development. Both readings explain the phenomenon's extraordinary persistence and growth by its systemic services, and both are embarrassed by the same residue: the unscripted joy of play and spectatorship, which participants value in a register neither integration nor ideology fully captures. The residue matters methodologically. Every row of the grid leaves a residue of meaning that participants attach to the institution and that contribution analysis cannot digest, and the honest traditions admit the residue rather than dissolving it. Admission is not surrender: a theory of persistence need not be a theory of everything an institution means. But the admission marks the boundary of the shared form's jurisdiction, and marking jurisdictions is what mature comparison consists in.

### Why do media and sport suit the grid so readily?

because both are mass phenomena whose persistence demands explanation and whose manifest accounts, information, entertainment, play, are obviously incomplete. Each tradition therefore reaches for latent systemic services, orientation or pacification, belonging or diversion, and the reach is the shared form operating in new territory.

A pattern has now repeated across nine institutions, and repetition across cases is the form inductive support takes in comparative theory. Education, religion, family, crime, stratification, state, medicine, media, sport: in every case the two traditions ask the persistence question, posit a containing system, inventory the institution's contributions to that system, and differ over the system's identity and moral sign. The probability that this convergence is accidental, two unrelated methods happening to produce grammatically identical literatures across nine domains, is negligible. The convergence is the finding. What remains genuinely contested, domain by domain, is the beneficiary, and the contest there is empirical, winnable in pieces, by specified mechanisms and discriminating evidence, exactly as the verdict's defeater prescribes. Candidates sometimes worry that the shared-form thesis, by dissolving the methodological contrast, leaves the comparison with nothing to say. The nine rows answer the worry: the comparison's content was never the contrast of methods. Its content is the paired inventory and the beneficiary dispute, and both survive the thesis at full strength, clarified rather than diminished.

## Anomie and alienation: the paired diagnoses of modern discontent

Beneath the institutional comparison runs a deeper pairing that candidates rarely exploit: each tradition carries a signature diagnosis of modern unhappiness, and the diagnoses, anomie and alienation, illuminate each other when set side by side. Anomie, in Durkheim's construction, is the suffering produced by deregulation, the condition in which society's norms lose their grip on desire, leaving appetite without a horizon and the individual exposed to the torment of limitless wanting. Its evidence was statistical and startling: suicide rates varying systematically with the strength of social integration and regulation, rising in prosperity's disruptions as well as in collapse, wherever the collective framework that tells people what they may legitimately hope for is suddenly disturbed. Alienation, in the Marxist construction, is the misery generated by separation: the worker separated from the product, from the activity, from fellow workers, and from the human capacities work should express, under relations that make labour a means of survival rather than a medium of self-realisation. Its evidence is phenomenological and structural at once, the felt emptiness of work whose organisation the worker does not control, producing a product the worker does not own, in competition with workers the process sets against one another.

Set the diagnoses in parallel columns and the family resemblance is immediate. Both locate modern misery in structure rather than in individual failing. Both deny that the misery is the price of progress in any simple sense, treating it instead as a symptom of a specific, describable disorder in the organisation of collective life. Both imply remedies at the level of the disorder: reintegration and reregulation in one case, transformation of the relations of production in the other. And both, crucially, are functional in their logic of symptom: anomie is what a system produces when its integrative function fails; alienation is what a system produces when its productive organisation functions exactly as designed. That last contrast is the sharpest substantive difference in the whole comparison, sharper than consensus against conflict, because it locates the pathology in opposite places. For functionalism, suffering signals malfunction, a need unmet, a mechanism strained. For Marxism, the deepest suffering signals smooth functioning, the extraction proceeding without friction, the worker's separation accomplished daily and on schedule. A candidate who builds a paragraph on that inversion, pathology as breakdown against pathology as business as usual, has found a contrast that survives the shared-form thesis, because it concerns not the grammar of explanation but the location of disorder in the two theories' worlds.

### How do anomie and alienation differ as diagnoses?

in where each locates the disorder producing modern misery. Anomie names a failure of regulation and integration, the system's grip loosening until desire loses its horizon. Alienation names a success of exploitative organisation, the worker's separation from product, activity, and fellows accomplished by relations functioning precisely as arranged.

The diagnoses also generate rival readings of the same statistics, and the rivalry is a gift to evaluative writing. Rising suicide in a period of economic disruption reads, functionally, as anomie's signature, deregulation's harvest. The Marxist reading interrogates the disruption's class distribution, asking whose security dissolved and whose assets the disruption consolidated, treating the deregulation itself as a political product rather than a natural event. Neither reading is embarrassed by the other's data. Each absorbs the shared facts into its own causal depth, functionalism stopping at the regulatory framework's condition, Marxism pressing through to the interests the framework's condition serves. Pressing through is Marxism's characteristic depth move, and stopping at the framework is functionalism's characteristic completeness claim, and a candidate can stage the entire comparison as an argument about how deep an explanation should dig before it is entitled to stop. That staging has the merit of truth: much of the actual debate between the traditions, in journal exchanges and conference corridors, is exactly an argument about legitimate stopping points, conducted by scholars who share more method than either side's manifestos admit.

## The welfare compromise read twice

Historical episodes test theories differently from statistics, by forcing them to narrate the same events, and the construction of welfare states is the episode this comparison cannot avoid, since it sits at the intersection of every grid row: education, health, family support, crime's management, and the state's legitimation, all expanded within a generation across the industrial world. The functionalist narration runs through strain and adaptation. Industrial societies generated needs their inherited institutions could not meet: urban populations without kin networks to absorb sickness and old age, labour markets requiring healthy and literate workers, democratic electorates demanding security as citizenship's substance. Welfare institutions arose as the system's adaptive response, new structures differentiating to meet new requirements, and their persistence since is explained by the requirements' persistence. The narration's strength is its fit with the simultaneity of the development: societies of very different political colour built recognisably similar welfare apparatuses in the same decades, as though a common problem were dictating a common solution.

The Marxist narration runs through struggle and concession. Welfare institutions, on this account, were extracted, not bestowed: organised labour's growing power made the costs of refusal, strikes, electoral defeat, revolutionary example, exceed the costs of concession, and the resulting settlements bear the marks of their extraction in every eligibility rule and funding compromise. Their persistence is explained by a double service: they stabilise the order by insuring its casualties and legitimating its inequalities, while simultaneously embodying real victories that populations defend as rights, which is why attempts to dismantle them meet resistance the pure legitimation story would not predict. The narration's power lies in its fit with the variation: welfare apparatuses differ across societies in generosity and design in ways that track the historical strength of organised labour far more closely than they track any uniform functional requirement, and timing follows political crises and settlements rather than the smooth curve of social need.

### Which narration handles simultaneity and variation better?

each handles one face of the record. Simultaneity across unlike regimes favours the functionalist story of common problems dictating common solutions. Variation in generosity tracking organised labour's strength favours the Marxist story of extraction and concession. The episode therefore grades the comparison as the verdict does, by dimension rather than by coronation.

The episode's deepest lesson concerns the defeater's logic in action. Where the Marxist narration specifies its mechanisms, union density, party competition, strike waves, employer calculations, named and measurable, it exits the shared form and becomes causal history of a high order. Where it rests in the assertion that welfare serves capital's long-term interests, true or not, it remains inside the form, a functional claim awaiting elaboration. The functionalist narration shows the identical split: where it traces the administrative and fiscal feedbacks that entrenched particular programmes, constituencies defending benefits, bureaucracies defending budgets, it achieves causal history; where it rests in societal need, it asserts. The episode thus supplies candidates with a worked example of the audit the verdict prescribes, and seminar leaders build sessions on it for exactly that reason. Two narrations of one episode, each audited paragraph by paragraph for mechanism, teach more about the comparison than a semester of definitions, because the audit's standard is visibly impartial: it praises and faults sentences from both columns by a rule neither column owns.

## The word function and its travels

A short philology of the key term repays the space it occupies, because the word function carries the whole comparison inside its own history. Its oldest relevant sense is mathematical: a function maps values to values according to a rule, expressing dependence without any suggestion of purpose. Its biological sense developed through the study of organs: the function of the heart is pumping, a statement about what the part does within the economy of the whole, initially innocent of design claims and later disciplined by selection theory. Its sociological sense was borrowed from biology by the founders of both lineages examined earlier, and the borrowing imported both the power and the ambiguity of the biological usage: power, because contribution to a whole is a genuinely explanatory notion where wholes have requirements; ambiguity, because social wholes lack the clear survival criterion that disciplines biological attribution, there being no agreed test for a society's death comparable to an organism's.

The ambiguity ramifies into three questions that any functional claim, in either tradition, can be made to answer, and candidates can use the triad as a checklist in the examination room. First, function for what: which system, society, class, institution, is the alleged beneficiary, specified narrowly enough that consequences for it can be traced? Second, function by what feedback: through which process do the consequences sustain the item, reinforcement, selection, design, suppression? Third, function compared to what: which alternative arrangements were available, and what would the world look like if the claim were false? A claim that answers all three questions has exited the territory of the teleology objection entirely, whatever its author's camp. A claim that answers none is a slogan. Most published claims in both traditions answer the first question, gesture at the second, and ignore the third, which is why the audit standard, applied impartially, grades the literatures as mixed rather than grading one as science and the other as rhetoric.

### What three questions discipline any functional claim?

function for what, naming the beneficiary system narrowly enough to trace consequences. Function by what feedback, naming the process sustaining the item through its consequences. Function compared to what, naming the alternatives and the observations that would count against the claim being made.

Systems language added a further twist in the decades when cybernetics and general systems theory lent sociology a vocabulary of inputs, outputs, feedback, and equilibrium. Parsons absorbed the vocabulary eagerly, and its presence in his work explains both its scientific sheen and its vulnerability: feedback in engineered systems is wired and measurable, while feedback in social systems is usually metaphorical until someone specifies the wiring. Marxist theory absorbed less of the cybernetic vocabulary, preferring the language of reproduction and contradiction, but reproduction is itself a systems concept, the output of one cycle becoming the input of the next, and contradiction, in its developed usage, names a feedback that destabilises rather than stabilises, a loop in which a system's successful operation generates the conditions of its transformation. Positive and negative feedback, in the engineer's sense, map onto the two traditions' characteristic dynamics with embarrassing neatness: functionalism theorises the loops that restore equilibrium, Marxism the loops that amplify disturbance toward a new state. An engineer shown both literatures would classify them as studies of the same systems under different loop assumptions, which is, in the vocabulary of another discipline, the shared-form thesis once more.

## Levels, units, and the quiet decisions that decide comparisons

Comparisons are decided, more often than their authors admit, by quiet decisions about levels and units taken before the argument begins. The level decision was introduced in the method workshop; its consequences deserve a fuller airing here because they explain a large fraction of the published disagreement between the traditions' partisans. Functionalism's characteristic level is the whole society and its subsystems, a choice that makes integration the natural explanandum and renders sectional conflict a subordinate phenomenon, visible mainly as strain. Marxism's characteristic level is the mode of production and its classes, a choice that makes reproduction the natural explanandum and renders society-wide goods subordinate, visible mainly as legitimation or concession. Neither level is the world itself. Each is a decision about where explanation should terminate, and the decisions are partly temperamental, partly political, and partly dictated by the phenomena each founder happened to study first, Durkheim the moral community of a nation consolidating its republic, Marx the factories and markets of an industrialising economy. Candidates who state the level decision explicitly, and who note that evidence gathered at one level cannot directly refute claims pitched at another without a bridging argument, write with a methodological self-awareness that markers consistently reward.

The unit decision compounds the level decision. Functionalism's units are roles, institutions, and systems; its actors are occupants, and its explanations improve as the units are specified more narrowly, which is the direction Merton pushed. Marxism's units are classes, fractions, and the organisations that act in their name; its explanations improve as the collective actors are decomposed into the parties, unions, firms, and agencies through which class effects actually travel, which is the direction the analytical critics pushed. The pushes converge on the same methodological destination: specified units, traceable processes, claims sized to evidence. That two traditions so rhetorically opposed should generate, from their own internal criticisms, the same destination is the comparison's final structural irony, and it suggests a prognosis candidates may venture in a conclusion: the traditions' futures lie less in their duel than in their common absorption into a mechanism-conscious structural sociology that neither manifesto foresaw and both made possible.

### Why do levels decide so much of the disagreement?

because each tradition terminates explanation at its chosen level, the whole society for one, the mode of production for the other, and findings collected at one level cannot overturn assertions pitched at another level unless a bridging argument is supplied. Stating the level exposes how much apparent contradiction is actually difference of address.

There is a pedagogical consequence worth stating for the teacher layer to develop. Students taught the comparison as a duel arrive at the examination with two monologues memorised and no operation to perform on them. Learners given the comparison as a shared grammar with a beneficiary dispute arrive with an operation, the grid plus the audit, that generates paragraphs on demand for any institution the question names, including institutions never mentioned in class. The second pedagogy is not merely more accurate to the scholarly record. It is more useful under examination conditions, where the question's phrasing is never quite the phrasing of the notes, and where generative instruments outperform stored text. The article's wager, from its opening paragraphs, has been that accuracy and usefulness coincide on this topic, and the persona layers now collect the wager's proceeds for each audience in turn.

## Grand theory, middle range, and the critic who named the crisis

The comparison's middle decades supply a historical episode that candidates can use to show both traditions being audited by the same external standard at the same moment. By the middle of the twentieth century, functionalism in its Parsonian form had achieved something like official status in American sociology: a general theory, a vocabulary departments taught first, a framework within which research programmes were expected to locate themselves. The reaction, when it came, arrived from several directions at once and is worth disentangling, because only one of its strands is the Marxist one. C. Wright Mills attacked grand theory as such, Parsons named among its exemplars, arguing that abstraction at that altitude had ceased to be a tool for understanding society and become a self-contained verbal edifice, translatable into plain English at a fraction of its length with no loss of content. Merton, from inside, had already counselled the middle range: theories intermediate between working hypotheses and total systems, addressed to specified phenomena, close enough to evidence to be tested and accumulated. Alvin Gouldner then announced a coming crisis of Western sociology, arguing that functionalism's dominance expressed the welfare state's administrative needs and that its harmony bias was an ideology of the social order it served, a critique whose structure, a theory explained by the interests it serves, is recognisably the Marxist move performed on functionalism itself by a sociologist of broadly Marxian sympathies.

The episode teaches three lessons the comparison should harvest. The first is that functionalism's most effective critics were not a camp but a coalition: methodologists demanding testability, humanists demanding agency, Marxists demanding power, and insiders demanding modesty, and the coalition's combined pressure, not any single argument, accounts for the perspective's loss of official status. The second lesson is that Marxism's own grand constructions faced the same coalition in the same decades: structuralist system building drew Mills-style complaints about abstraction, humanist Marxists demanded agency against bearer-of-relations models, and the analytical wing demanded testability with a rigour no external critic had matched. The symmetry of the audits is the historical form of this article's thesis. Two traditions, audited simultaneously by standards neither owned, converged under the pressure toward specified mechanisms, specified units, and claims sized to evidence. The third lesson concerns what survived. Functionalism's total system faded; its middle-range instruments, role theory, reference groups, the manifest-latent distinction, the analysis of deviance through strain, passed into the discipline's common stock, used daily by sociologists who would not call themselves functionalists. Marxism's total system likewise faded as official doctrine in most academies, while its instruments, ideology critique, class analysis of institutions, the reproduction question, passed into the same common stock, used by sociologists who would not call themselves Marxists. The duel of the textbooks continues above a substratum of shared tools that the duel's participants quietly share, and a candidate who describes the substratum has understood the discipline's actual history better than one who reports only the duel.

### What survived each tradition's loss of official status?

the middle-range instruments rather than the total systems: role analysis, the manifest and latent distinction, and strain theory from one side; ideology critique, institutional class analysis, and the reproduction question from the other. Both sets entered the discipline's shared toolkit, used by scholars claiming neither label.

Gouldner's intervention deserves one more paragraph because it performs the comparison's characteristic move in reverse and thereby proves the move's generality. To explain a theory by the interests it serves is the Marxist signature when applied to law, religion, or schooling. Gouldner applied it to functionalism itself, treating the perspective as the ideology of a stratum of welfare administrators and academic managers, its harmony bias functional for their position. Whether the application persuades is less important here than its form: the critic of functionalism reached, spontaneously, for explanation by contribution to a beneficiary's position, which is functional explanation wearing conflict colours, exactly as this article's anatomy predicts. The form is not a party's property. It is the discipline's common temptation and common tool, surfacing wherever persistence needs explaining, including the persistence of theories themselves. A candidate who spots the recursion, functional explanation applied to functionalism, and notes it without triumphalism, has demonstrated the kind of second-order awareness that distinguishes the highest band of scripts on any theory question.

## Feminist and postcolonial audits of both houses

Two later movements audited both traditions from positions neither had anticipated, and their audits belong in a complete comparison because they altered what the grid's rows can responsibly say. Feminist sociology's charge against functionalism was precise: the perspective's account of the family naturalised a historically specific arrangement of gender, presenting male breadwinning and female domesticity as functional necessities of industrial society when they were, in fact, one settlement among others, enforced by law, wage structures, and violence, and experienced by many women as confinement rather than stabilisation. The charge against orthodox Marxism was equally precise: the tradition's account of the household as labour power's reproduction site, while an advance on romance, subordinated gender to class, treating women's oppression as a by-product that the transformation of property would dissolve, when the evidence showed domestic hierarchy flourishing across property regimes and predating capitalism by millennia. The feminist synthesis that emerged, dual-systems and later intersectional work, kept the Marxist insistence on material organisation and the functionalist attention to socialisation while refusing both traditions' habit of treating half the population's experience as a special case. On the grid, the audit's permanent effect is a question appended to every row: functional for whom, at whose cost, disaggregated by gender as Merton taught the discipline to disaggregate by unit, a question both traditions now answer as a matter of routine because the audit made evasion untenable.

Postcolonial and decolonial criticism pressed a parallel audit on the traditions' shared universalism. Functionalism's societies were characteristically bounded national wholes, its comparisons arranged along a traditional-modern axis that placed the theorist's own society at the developed pole, and its functional imperatives were asserted for societies as such on evidence drawn from a narrow band of cases. Marxism's stages of history, its sequence of modes of production generalised from European experience, drew the same objection in a different vocabulary: a universal grammar whose examples were provincial, and whose application to colonised societies repeatedly required embarrassed footnotes about modes that fit nowhere in the sequence. The audits converge, characteristically, on a methodological demand both traditions could accept without surrendering their cores: specify the scope of every claim, name the cases it was built from, and treat the extension to new cases as a hypothesis rather than an inheritance. Scope specification joins mechanism specification in the audit standard this article has been assembling, and the convergence of feminist, postcolonial, analytical, and internal critics on the same standard, from starting points that share almost nothing else, is itself evidence that the standard tracks something real about explanation rather than the fashion of any camp.

### What did the later audits add to the audit standard?

scope and disaggregation. Feminist criticism required every functional claim to specify its beneficiaries and cost-bearers by gender, not merely by class or system. Postcolonial criticism required every universal claim to identify the cases behind it and to treat extension elsewhere as hypothesis rather than inheritance.

There is a reflexive dividend for candidates in this section's material. The examination comparison is itself a product of the traditions' textbook afterlives, and the audits show why the fossil versions persist in classrooms long after the scholarly literatures have absorbed their corrections: curricula change slowly, the duel is teachable, and the corrected versions, conditional, mechanism-specified, scope-limited, are harder to examine with the blunt instruments of timed essays. A candidate who knows this can perform a small act of repair inside the examination itself, answering the set question with the corrected versions while signalling awareness of the fossil the question presupposes. That performance, fidelity to the evidence over fidelity to the course's simplifications, is what the highest mark bands on theory questions are designed, however imperfectly, to detect.

## Mechanisms from an unexpected supplier: choice, games, and exploitation rebuilt

The mechanism audit's most surprising chapter concerns where some of the missing mechanisms were eventually supplied from. Rational-choice modelling and game theory entered Marxist theory through the analytical wing, in the work of scholars such as John Roemer, who rebuilt the concept of exploitation on foundations that owe nothing to the labour theory of value in its classical form: exploitation redescribed as a consequence of unequal asset distribution, testable in models where agents with different endowments interact under specified rules. Whatever verdict a reader reaches on the rebuild, its methodological significance for this comparison is fixed. A central Marxist concept was reconstructed with its mechanisms fully exposed, agents, endowments, rules, equilibria, so that every step from structure to outcome could be inspected and challenged. That is precisely the elaboration Cohen's standard demanded, delivered at the level of the tradition's core rather than at its periphery, and it demonstrates that the shared form's debt can be paid in full by work that remains recognisably within the tradition's questions, who benefits, who is exploited, what would equality of assets change, even as its methods transform.

Functionalism's encounters with the same toolkit were quieter but parallel. Role expectations were remodelled as equilibria of mutual anticipation, norms as conventions sustained by the costs of deviating when others comply, and institutional persistence as path dependence, early arrangements locking in through increasing returns. Each remodel converts a functional assertion into a mechanism claim: the norm persists not because the system needs it but because each actor, expecting others to enforce it, finds compliance prudent, and the enforcers, expecting general compliance, find enforcement cheap. The system's need drops out of the explanation entirely, replaced by interlocking expectations, which is the mechanism audit's ideal outcome and a demonstration that functionalist phenomena, real patterns of integration and persistence, survive the translation into causal language. The patterns were never the problem. The unelaborated grammar was the problem, and translation, where it succeeds, vindicates the phenomena while retiring the grammar, a distinction candidates should preserve carefully, since much polemic on both sides confuses refuting a grammar with refuting the patterns it was used to describe.

### What did the analytical rebuild of exploitation prove methodologically?

that a core concept of the Marxist tradition could be reconstructed with agents, endowments, rules, and equilibria fully specified, so the path from structure to outcome became inspectable step by step. The rebuild paid, for one central claim, exactly the elaboration debt that Cohen's standard demanded of all consequence explanation.

The game-theoretic turn also reframed the traditions' oldest substantive dispute, the cohesion question, in a form where evidence can bite. Cooperation among strangers, the phenomenon functionalism explained by internalised values and Marxism by dependence and hegemony, becomes in the modelling literature a problem of repeated interaction, reputation, and institutional design, with experimental and field evidence distinguishing the conditions under which trust flourishes or collapses. The findings honour neither grand story wholesale: values matter measurably, since populations differ in cooperative dispositions that track their socialisation; dependence matters measurably, since cooperation collapses when exit is costless and sanctions vanish; design matters most measurably of all, since modest changes in monitoring and feedback swing outcomes between the poles. A candidate need not master the modelling literature to profit from its moral for the comparison: the cohesion dispute, the comparison's first substantive divide, turned out to be an empirical question with a conditional answer, and the traditions' role in reaching that answer was to have posed the rival hypotheses clearly enough for better instruments to test. Posing testable rivals is not a small service. It is, arguably, the principal service grand theory renders to the sciences that succeed it.

## The comparison in the world's classrooms

The institution-by-institution comparison is a standard teaching device across sociology curricula worldwide, and its global career is itself a datum worth a section, because the device travels with variations that reveal what different educational cultures want from the duel. In examination systems descended from the British model, the comparison is set as an essay requiring evaluation, and the mark schemes reward the discriminating moves this article has catalogued: shared form, insider critics, steelmanned stratification, conditional verdicts. In systems organised around objective testing, the comparison survives as paired definitions and attribution items, Davis and Moore matched to their thesis, manifest matched against latent, where the fossil versions do the least damage because the items test recognition rather than judgement. In systems where sociology serves professional formation, social work, education, public administration, the comparison is taught as a pair of lenses for practice, the functionalist lens asking what a service holds together, the Marxist lens asking whose interests the holding serves, and practitioners report using both lenses on the same caseload within the same week. The variations converge on the article's practical moral: the comparison's durable value is instrumental, a pair of questions to carry into any institution, and the doctrinal duel is the packaging in which the instrument is shipped to classrooms.

That moral reframes the usefulness question one final time before the verdict. Asked which perspective is more useful, the classroom traditions of several continents answer, in effect, that usefulness is not a property of perspectives but of pairings between questions and tools. The functionalist question, what does this arrangement hold together and what would its removal interrupt, is indispensable to anyone who would reform an institution without wrecking its latent services. The Marxist question, whom does this arrangement serve and whom does it charge, is indispensable to reformers who intend to change an institution while escaping capture by the story it tells about itself. A reformer armed with only the first question will preserve injustices whose functions were real. A reformer carrying only the second will demolish services whose beneficiaries were the very people the reform meant to help. The grid's third column, extended into practice, is the discipline of asking both questions in sequence and refusing to let either answer be given in the other's absence. If the comparison has a claim to be the most valuable single topic in the introductory syllabus, the claim rests there: not in the doctrines it surveys but in the paired interrogative habit it can install, which graduates carry into every institution they will ever work in, vote on, or attempt to change.

### How is the comparison used outside examination systems?

as a paired instrument for practice in the professions that study institutions from inside: the functionalist question asking what an arrangement holds together, the Marxist question asking whom it serves and whom it charges. Practitioners report alternating the lenses on the same cases, treating the duel of doctrines as packaging for an interrogative habit.

The adjacent disciplines confirm the instrument's portability. Political science runs the same pairing as pluralism against elite theory, organisational studies as contingency accounts against critical management studies, and in each field the sophisticated literature discovers the same shared grammar and the same beneficiary dispute underneath the local vocabulary. Evolutionary biology's methodology, already visited, supplies the pairing's most disciplined cousin, adaptation against by-product, with selection as the arbiter both sociological traditions lack in full strength. A candidate who closes an answer by noting the pairing's recurrence across fields performs the final generalisation the topic affords: the comparison is one instance of a permanent structure in the human sciences, the tension between explaining order by its services to the whole and explaining it by its services to a part, and sociology's version is simply the most thoroughly documented case of that permanent tension at work.

## Deviance careers and the audience: a workshop in paired reading

One concrete case, followed patiently through both vocabularies, teaches the paired reading better than another survey of doctrines, so this section follows a single phenomenon, the career of a deviant act from commission to punishment to aftermath, and narrates it twice. A young person takes goods from a shop. In the functionalist narration, the story's centre is the reaction. Detection converts a private act into a public occasion; the courtroom stages the community's values in solemn form; the sentence calibrates the audience's outrage into a measured response that reasserts the boundary the act crossed; and the aftermath, the offender's return or exclusion, tests the system's capacity to reintegrate its repaired members. Details that puzzle a purely deterrent account fall into place under this narration: the publicity of punishment, its ritual excess over any plausible deterrent requirement, the community's appetite for the spectacle, all become intelligible as boundary maintenance, the group reminding itself, through the offender, of what it is. Even the offender's subsequent career is legible: a successful ceremony of degradation can fix a deviant identity that then organises the person's future, a dysfunction the tradition's own labelling borrowers later formalised, while a successful reintegration ceremony, the forgiven debt, the restored standing, performs the opposite repair.

In the Marxist narration, the same events rearrange around different centres. The definition of the act comes first: which takings count as theft is a legal achievement with a history, the enclosure of commons having converted customary rights into crimes within living memory of the generations concerned, and the category's modern boundaries still trace property's outline, severe toward the takings of the poor, procedural toward the takings of the organised. The distribution of the act comes second: shoplifting's incidence tracks the geography of need and exclusion, and the surplus population's exclusion from stable livelihood is the narration's candidate for the act's principal cause, ahead of any individual's moral biography. The reaction comes third and is read as distribution too: detection resources concentrate where the poor shop and live, sentences track the offender's resources and record, and the prison at the corridor's end warehouses a population whose composition describes the class structure with embarrassing fidelity. The aftermath, finally, is read through the mark the punishment leaves on employability, the conviction functioning as a durable sorting device in the labour market, which closes the loop between the criminal process and the reproduction of a marginal stratum.

Now perform the third-column operation on the paired narration, because the case was chosen to make the operation unavoidable. Both narrations refuse to explain the events by the young person's character. Both treat the courtroom as a theatre performing systemic work. Both explain the punishment's scale and publicity by its contribution to an order's maintenance, solidarity renewed or class discipline renewed. Both predict, correctly, that punishment will persist at levels deterrence cannot justify, and each explains the excess by its own beneficiary. The narrations differ in their causal depths and their moral temperatures, and they differ in what they would change: the functionalist narration implies reforms of ceremony and reintegration, the Marxist narration implies transformations of definition, distribution, and the labour market's margins. But a reader who has followed the case can no longer honestly describe the two as opposite methods. They are rival depths of one method, and the rivalry is adjudicable, point by point, by asking which narration's mechanisms are specified, which predictions each risks, and which beneficiary the pattern of evidence, detection maps, sentencing patterns, prison composition, actually supports. That adjudication, conducted on a single case, is the examination answer's highest form in miniature, and it generalises to every row of the grid without alteration of procedure.

### What does a single case followed twice reveal that surveys miss?

the identity of procedure beneath the rivalry of depth. Both narrations refuse character explanations, treat punishment as systemic theatre, and explain its excess over deterrence by a beneficiary. The case makes the shared form visible as a working method rather than as a thesis asserted about two literatures.

The case also exposes, with unusual clarity, where each narration is thinnest. The functionalist narration struggles with the definition question: why this act, among the spectrum of takings, occupies the boundary's centre, and why the boundary's enforcement falls where it falls, are questions its vocabulary of community values answers only by assuming the values whose distribution is in dispute. The Marxist narration struggles with the ceremony's sincerity: the outrage in the courtroom is felt, by magistrates and publics who gain nothing measurable from the particular sentence, and a narration that treats all such feeling as legitimation's theatre must explain why the theatre convinces its own actors, a problem hegemony names more readily than it solves. Naming each narration's thin place, in the presence of the case that exposes it, is the evaluative move this article most wants candidates to carry away, because it is transferable to every theory pairing they will ever meet: find the case, follow it twice, locate the thin places, and let the verdict distribute itself across the dimensions the case reveals rather than crowning a winner the case does not support.

## Consensus measured: what populations actually agree about

The consensus question, the comparison's first substantive divide, can be taken to evidence more directly than the preceding sections have done, and the exercise is worth performing because it shows a headline dispute dissolving into a set of smaller, answerable questions. Populations in class societies agree, measurably, on a stock of abstract propositions: that effort deserves reward, that children should be educated, that violence against the innocent is wrong, that promises should be kept. They disagree, also measurably, on the application of every one of those propositions to contested cases: what reward effort deserves when the effort is a chief executive's or a cleaner's, whose children the state's education spending should privilege, which violence counts as innocent harm and which as order's regrettable necessity, which promises, made by governments to pensioners or by debtors to banks, bind absolutely. The pattern, agreement in the abstract and dissensus in the application, is so regular across survey traditions that it deserves recognition as the empirical shape of the cohesion problem, and both perspectives must be graded by how gracefully they accommodate it.

Functionalism accommodates the pattern through its distinction between values and their institutional interpretation: the consensus is real at the level where the theory needs it, the shared framework of abstract commitments, while dissensus at the level of application is the normal friction of a complex order, processed by its political and legal subsystems without threatening the framework itself. The accommodation is respectable and generates a prediction: systems survive application-level dissensus indefinitely, provided abstract consensus holds, and they enter crisis when the abstract level itself fractures, when populations cease to agree that effort should be rewarded or promises kept at all. Marxism accommodates the same pattern through the distinction between common sense and its hegemonic organisation: the abstract agreements are real but indeterminate, empty vessels that acquire content from the interpretive machinery a class order controls, so that consensus at the abstract level is precisely what hegemony looks like when it is working, and the application disputes are the points where subordinate classes' experience strains against the supplied content. Its prediction is the mirror image: crisis arrives not when abstractions fracture but when the interpretive machinery loses credibility, when the gap between the abstract promise, effort rewarded, and the experienced application becomes too wide for the supplied interpretations to bridge.

Set the predictions side by side and a discriminating observation becomes conceivable, which is the most a comparison can ask of evidence at this altitude. Episodes of systemic crisis can be examined for their sequence: did abstract commitments fracture first, populations ceasing to affirm the framework's generalities, or did interpretive credibility collapse first, the generalities still affirmed while their official applications were rejected as rigged? Historical episodes supply both sequences, which is why the dispute survives, but the exercise has converted a slogan exchange into a researchable question with rival predictions stated in advance. That conversion is the article's whole method applied to the comparison's oldest divide, and a candidate who performs it in an examination, even briefly, demonstrates the conversion skill that the topic, at its best, exists to teach. The remaining divides submit to the same treatment: change's divide becomes a question about sequences in institutional transformation, posture's divide a question about what each stance systematically overlooks, and the beneficiary dispute, row by row, a question about patterns of cost and benefit that records can settle in pieces even where the whole remains contested.

## Change reconsidered: differentiation, contradiction, and the historical record

The change divide, adaptation against contradiction, rewards the same evidential treatment, because the historical record of institutional transformation is rich enough to embarrass both defaults and to reward the candidate who grades them dimension by dimension. Functionalism's evolutionary imagination, differentiation as the master trend, describes a real pattern: as societies grow in scale and complexity, functions once bundled in single institutions separate into specialised structures, the household shedding production, education, welfare, and care to factories, schools, and agencies across the industrial centuries. The pattern is documented at a scale no rival account denies, and the Marxist tradition's own narratives presuppose it, since the separation of the worker from the means of production is itself a differentiation story told in a critical register. Where the functionalist default weakens is in its tempo and its direction: differentiation in the record is neither smooth nor reliably progressive, proceeding by lurches, reversals, and collapses, with new structures as often imposed by conquest, crisis, and class settlement as grown by adaptive pressure, and with differentiation's benefits distributed in patterns the adaptive story does not predict.

Marxism's contradiction imagination describes the lurches better. Its account of change as the maturation of tensions inside a mode of production, forces of production straining against relations that once fostered and later fetter them, generates the expectation of punctuated transformation, long stability broken by concentrated crisis and settlement, which matches the record's tempo in case after case: the welfare compromise examined earlier arrived in a burst after depression and war, not by gradual accretion; the great extensions of education clustered in the decades of state building and labour mobilisation; the legal orders of property were rewritten in revolutionary decades and then conserved for centuries. Where the contradiction default weakens is in its specificity: the tradition is eloquent on why orders change and vague on what they change into, its predictions of direction having repeatedly been overtaken by outcomes, hybrid settlements, restored orders, transformations sideways, that the framework accommodates after the fact with a fluency the falsification section taught candidates to distrust. The balanced grade writes itself: functionalism owns the trend, Marxism owns the tempo, neither owns the destination, and a candidate who distributes the grades in that form has evaluated rather than recited.

### How should the change divide be graded?

by dimension. The long trend of institutional differentiation favours the functionalist imagination, which named the pattern. The tempo of transformation, stability punctuated by crisis and settlement, favours the Marxist imagination of contradiction maturing. The destination of change is owned by neither, both traditions having been repeatedly surprised by outcomes.

A deeper symmetry underlies the graded verdict and is worth stating because it completes the article's anatomy of the pair. Each tradition's theory of change is its theory of order read dynamically. Functionalism explains order by integration, so its change is integration's adjustment, the system re-equilibrating under strain. Marxism explains order by reproduction of relations, so its change is reproduction's failure, the relations ceasing to renew themselves on the old terms. The theories of change therefore inherit the strengths and the debts of the theories of order: both explain transformation by the fate of a system's maintenance, both are functional in the dynamic register as in the static, and both face the mechanism demand in its hardest form, since the mechanisms of system-level transformation, the actors and feedbacks that carry an order across a rupture, are exactly what macro theories specify least well. The historical sociologists who have done most to repair the omission, students of revolution and of state breakdown, worked characteristically with tools from both traditions and from neither, comparing cases, tracing sequences, and treating classes, states, and international pressures as interacting mechanisms. Their literature is the change divide's version of the common stock described earlier: built from the duel's materials, owned by neither duellist.

## The posture divide reconsidered: detachment, commitment, and their costs

Return, finally, to the fifth divide, posture, because it is the one candidates feel in their own writing and the one on which the article's neutrality discipline has been silently taking a position that should now be made explicit and defended. Functionalism's detached posture promised the gains of the laboratory: dispassionate mapping of mechanisms, findings unwelcome to every camp, a science of order that reformers and conservatives alike could use. The promise was partly honoured, in the middle-range instruments that passed into common stock, and partly betrayed by the posture itself, since the choice of the whole society as the level of analysis, and of its persistence as the good to be explained, is not a neutral choice but a conservative one in the strict sense, valuing the order's continuation before asking whether the order deserves to continue. Marxism's committed stance promised the tribunal's rewards: harms named that detachment leaves as externalities, beneficiaries identified that aggregation conceals, and a theory that tells its readers what the world is for changing. That promise was likewise partly honoured, in the research programmes that mapped power's distributions, and partly betrayed by commitment's own gravity, since a theory certain of its verdicts in advance will recruit evidence as counsel rather than as witness, and its practitioners' confidence has repeatedly outrun their mechanisms.

The mature position, which this article has practised and now states, treats posture as a phase of inquiry rather than as an identity. Detachment serves the mapping phase, in which mechanisms are traced and beneficiaries identified by standards any observer can check. Commitment serves the judgement phase, in which the mapped order is arraigned or defended before values the writer states openly. Corruption enters when the phases are confused in either direction: when commitment does the mapping, producing the accommodating redescriptions the falsification section catalogued, or when detachment does the judging, producing the false neutrality that values an order's persistence without examining its costs. Candidates can operationalise the distinction in a single sentence of method, declaring that the answer will first establish what each institution does and for whom, by evidence, and will then judge the pattern by criteria it names. The sentence costs nothing and buys the marker's trust for everything that follows, because it announces that the script knows the difference between finding out and summing up, which is, in the end, the difference this entire comparison exists to teach.

### Is either posture, detached or committed, simply superior?

neither, since each serves a phase of inquiry and corrupts the other phase when it trespasses. Detachment maps mechanisms honestly but smuggles in a valuation of persistence when it judges. Commitment names harms honestly but recruits evidence as counsel when it maps. Maturity sequences the postures rather than choosing between them.

With the five divides now each graded, cohesion by sequence, inequality by dimension, change by trend and tempo, beneficiary by row, posture by phase, the comparison stands fully assembled, and the verdict given earlier in this article can be read as the sum of the gradings rather than as a preference. That is the form a verdict should take in a mature comparison: not a crown awarded to a camp but a distribution of credits and debts across dimensions, with the shared form named as the architecture that makes the distribution intelligible, and with the defeater standing as the verdict's own audit clause, specifying the evidence, mechanism supplied row by row, that would redistribute the credits again. The persona layers that follow translate the assembled comparison for the article's three audiences, and the frequently asked questions consolidate its portable results.

## Knowledge and the university: the grid examines its own home

One institution has been present throughout this article without being examined, the institution that produces comparisons like this one, and turning the grid on the university is more than a reflexive flourish, because both traditions have substantial accounts of knowledge production and the accounts behave exactly as the thesis predicts. The functionalist reading treats the university as the order's organ of certified knowledge: it conserves the cultural heritage, trains the professions, staffs the research frontier, and legitimises expertise by subjecting it to communal standards of evidence, so that a complex society can know things no individual could verify and can trust specialists it cannot personally audit. Knowledge, on this reading, persists in curricula and disciplines because it serves the society's cognitive division of labour, and the university's autonomy is itself functional, a protected space whose insulation from immediate demand allows the long-horizon inquiry the order will eventually need. The Marxist reading treats the same institution as an apparatus of a class order's knowledge: research agendas bend toward funders, disciplines police their boundaries in ways that exclude questions threatening to property and hierarchy, the credential the university issues is the schooling system's sorting function at its apex, and the autonomy the functionalist celebrates is real but relative, enjoyed on condition that the institution's output does not organise itself against the arrangements that endow it.

The third column performs its by now familiar operation. Both readings explain the university's persistence by its services to a containing system, both pass over the manifest account, disinterested inquiry, in favour of latent systemic work, and both must answer the mechanism demand: the functionalist by tracing how societies reward and reproduce the inquiry they need, the Marxist by tracing how funding, hiring, and prestige filter the inquiry that flourishes. The empirical record, as ever, discriminates in pieces. Research with no imaginable application has repeatedly become the foundation of later industries, honouring the autonomy claim's substance. Research threatening to concentrated interests has repeatedly met funding droughts, legal pressure, and manufactured doubt, honouring the filtering claim's substance. And the university's own inhabitants, the professoriate, display a class ambiguity both traditions struggle to place, privileged in culture and security, subordinate in governance and increasingly in employment conditions, a stratum whose existence the stratification section's concession already flagged: the Marxist account of orders without a simple two-class map is thinner than its account of the factory, and the university is one of the places the thinness shows.

### What does the grid reveal when turned on the university?

that the comparison's own home obeys its law. Both traditions explain academic persistence by services to a containing order, integration of certified knowledge or filtration of permissible inquiry, and the evidence honours each in pieces, useless inquiry later proving foundational, threatening inquiry repeatedly meeting organised resistance.

There is a temptation, at this reflexive point, to ask which tradition explains the comparison itself, the durability of the Marxism-functionalism duel in curricula across generations. The functionalist answer writes itself: the duel persists because it performs services for the educational order, organising a syllabus, discriminating among candidates, giving examiners a reliable instrument and students a portable pair of lenses. The Marxist answer writes itself with equal ease: the duel persists because it is safe, a conflict of doctrines that leaves the institutions staging it unexamined, its ritual performance in examinations reproducing the educational order's sorting function under the appearance of intellectual combat. Both answers are functional explanations of the comparison's persistence, differing in beneficiary, and the fact that the article's subject matter generates its own case study, in which the thesis applies to the thesis's own teaching, is either a satisfying confirmation or a warning about the form's elasticity, depending on the reader's judgement of the falsification problem. The article leaves the judgement to the reader, noting only that the elasticity warning was issued, in its sharpest form, by the analytical Marxists about their own tradition, which is where this article came in.

## Law as an institution: form, enforcement, and the appearance of neutrality

Law appeared inside the crime and state rows without receiving a row of its own, and its omission should be repaired, because the legal system is the institution where the two readings' characteristic moves are most easily watched at work on identical material. The functionalist reading treats law as the order's normative skeleton made explicit: it codifies the shared values whose informal enforcement suffices for most conduct, supplies authoritative settlement for the disputes informal mechanisms cannot close, and adapts the normative order to new conditions through legislation and precedent, a controlled evolution that keeps the framework continuous while its contents change. Durkheim's version adds the famous index: the dominant form of law in a society, repressive or restitutive, reads off the type of solidarity beneath it, punishment-centred law betraying a conscience collective that experiences offence as sacrilege, contract-centred law betraying a differentiated order held together by interdependence. Law, on this reading, is the moral community's self-portrait in institutional form, and its authority rests on the portrait's recognisability to the community it governs.

The Marxist reading approaches those codes as the property order's operating system. Its most distinctive contribution is an account of legal form itself: the legal subject, free, equal, and rights-bearing, mirrors the commodity owner, free, equal, and exchange-ready, so that the law's celebrated universality is not hypocrisy laid over class rule but the genuine form class rule takes in a society of exchangers, which is why formal equality and material domination coexist without contradiction and why the law can be, simultaneously, a real constraint on the powerful in particular cases and a structural guarantee of their position in general. Enforcement then supplies the pattern the crime row documented, and legitimation completes the account: the law's neutrality of form is the order's most effective self-presentation, since a guarantee written as everyone's right is defended by everyone, including those whose material position it secures least. The tradition's internal debate, whether law is an instrument wielded by a class or a form generated by relations, replays the state row's autonomy debate in legal dress, and with the same grammar: the form theorists explain law's shape by its service to the relations, the instrumentalists by the personnel who staff it, and the argument between them is an argument about mechanisms conducted inside a shared functional frame.

### What is distinctive in the Marxist reading of law?

its attention to legal form rather than merely to legal content: the free and equal rights-bearer mirrors the commodity owner, so formal universality and material domination coexist without hypocrisy. The functionalist reading, by contrast, treats law's form as the moral community's self-portrait and its authority as recognisability.

The shared column completes the repair. Both readings treat law's self-description, justice blind and equal, as an assertion to be checked against patterned outcomes rather than as evidence. Both explain the legal system's persistence by the order it stabilises. Both predict, correctly, that legal change will lag and lead social change in patterned ways, lagging where the stabilised order is threatened, leading where the order requires new instruments. And both are embarrassed by one shared phenomenon, the law's occasional genuine autonomy, the judgment that wounds the powerful and is enforced, the right that subordinate groups seize and turn into a weapon its drafters never intended them to hold. Functionalism absorbs the embarrassment as the system's self-correction. Marxism absorbs it as contradiction's opening, the universal form harbouring promises its material base cannot honour and therefore cannot fully control. The absorptions differ in vocabulary and converge in structure, each tradition conceding that the institution it explains by service also exceeds its service, and the concession, made on both sides, is the law row's contribution to the verdict: institutions serve systems, and they also, stubbornly, do more, and any comparison that forgets the excess will be corrected by the next judgment, statute, or acquittal that refuses to fit.

## Founding scenes: a lecture hall, a reading room, and two centuries of order

Theories have birthplaces, and the birthplaces explain features of the theories that their propositions alone do not. Marxism's founding scene is the reading room and the factory report: a young philosopher in exile, turning from the criticism of religion and law toward the ledgers of political economy, reading the inspectors' accounts of industrial labour and concluding that the order's secret lay in production, in who owned the instruments of work and who, owning nothing but a capacity to labour, had to sell it to live. The scene bequeathed the theory its characteristic depth instinct, always press through the visible arrangement to the relations of production beneath, and its characteristic moral temperature, since the factory reports documented suffering that no doctrine of harmony could be allowed to describe as the price of order without the description itself becoming an indictment. Functionalism's founding scene is the lecture hall of a consolidating republic: a professor charged with training the teachers of a nation, seeking a science of morality that could give a society emerging from revolution and reaction a shared moral foundation sturdy enough to replace the ones it had lost. The scene bequeathed that theory its characteristic question, how is order possible at all, and its characteristic tenderness toward the rituals and schools that manufacture belonging, since its founder had watched belonging's absence tear his country repeatedly within living memory.

Set the scenes side by side and the substantive divides acquire biographies. The cohesion dispute is a dispute between a man who saw order's underside in the mill and a man who saw chaos's underside in the street. The posture dispute sets a critic whom no university would employ against a professor building a discipline's first chair. The beneficiary dispute opposes an exile reading a nation's wealth as extraction to a citizen reading the same nation's ceremonies as communion. None of this settles any argument, and candidates should say so before a marker says it for them: origins explain a theory's questions, not its answers' truth. But the scenes perform one indispensable service for the comparison. They dissolve the impression that the two perspectives are timeless positions in a logical space, and replace it with the truer picture of two situated responses to the same nineteenth-century catastrophe, the dissolution of the old order under industry and revolution, responses that agreed on the catastrophe's scale and on the need for a science of society adequate to it, and disagreed, from their different stations, on what the emerging order was and what it deserved. Estranged relatives, the verdict called them. The founding scenes supply the family history: one household, the crisis of the old Europe, two children who read the crisis from different windows and never afterwards recognised how much of the house they shared.

### Why do the founding scenes matter if they settle nothing?

because they explain each theory's characteristic questions, instincts, and tendernesses, the depth instinct and moral temperature of the reading room, the cohesion question and ritual tenderness of the lecture hall. Origins govern what a theory notices first, even though they cannot certify what it concludes.

Between the scenes stands a third figure whom the comparison must acknowledge precisely because he belongs fully to neither, the bridge this article mentions only to mark its boundary. Weber's sociology of domination, rationalisation, and the Protestant ethic shares functionalism's attention to legitimacy and Marxism's attention to material interest while refusing both traditions' system grammar, explaining institutions through the meanings actors attach to them and the unintended consequences those meanings accumulate. The comparison with Weber is a separate article's assignment in this series, and it is flagged here only so that candidates do not mistake the present pairing for the whole map of classical theory. The map has at least three great territories, and the two examined here are distinguished, against the third, by exactly the feature this article has anatomised: both explain through contributions toward a system's upkeep, where the third explains by meaning and consequence at the level of action. That the shared form can be used to locate the pair against an outsider confirms its value as an analytical instrument, since a distinction that only redescribes its home comparison is a label, while a distinction that sorts new cases is a tool.

## The long conversation: what a century of mutual criticism actually produced

Step back from the anatomy and survey the yield. A century and more of argument between and within these traditions has produced, first, a set of institutional analyses of permanent value, the grid's rows in their developed forms, which no subsequent sociology has been able to ignore and most has quietly incorporated. It has produced, second, a methodological conscience: the mechanism demand, the unit question, the scope discipline, the steelman protocol, each forged in the duel or in the internal audits the duel provoked, and each now part of the discipline's general standards, applicable to theories neither duellist imagined. It has produced, third, a map of the permanent problems: persistence, cohesion, inequality, change, legitimacy, the short list of questions any theory of society must answer or be judged incomplete for evading, and the list itself is a collective achievement to which both traditions contributed the questions they were best placed to force on the other. Functionalism forced the persistence and cohesion questions on a Marxism tempted to treat order as mere imposition. Marxism forced the beneficiary and power questions on a functionalism tempted to regard order as nothing but achievement. The forcing is the conversation's engine, and it continues wherever a seminar makes each tradition answer the other's best question before stating its own.

The yield can be stated as a single sentence for candidates to carry into the examination hall: the two perspectives are best understood as a standing cross-examination, each tradition the other's most reliable examiner, and the comparison question is an invitation to conduct the cross-examination in public, with the grid as the order of witnesses and the audit as the rules of evidence. Scripts that accept the invitation write themselves into the highest band, because the band's criteria, analysis, evaluation, discrimination, are the cross-examination's own virtues listed in a mark scheme's vocabulary. Scripts that decline it, reciting two doctrines and awarding a crown, fail on criteria that have nothing to do with the candidate's allegiance and everything to do with the operation the question requested. The article's remaining layers translate the cross-examination for its three audiences in practical form, and the questions that follow the layers consolidate the portable results, the thesis, the grid, the critics restored to their addresses, the thesis on stratification at full strength and its critique, and the verdict with the evidence that would defeat it.

## Work, bureaucracy, and the organisation read twice

The workplace is where most adults meet the social order in its least ceremonial form, and both traditions built accounts of organisational life that the grid predicts with its usual fidelity. The functionalist account of bureaucracy, descending from Weber's ideal type but domesticated into systems language, treats the large organisation as a solution to coordination problems that scale creates: hierarchy clarifies responsibility, rules suppress favouritism, files preserve memory beyond any official's tenure, and specialisation concentrates competence. Dysfunctions are admitted in the tradition's own vocabulary, trained incapacity, goal displacement, the means of procedure becoming its ends, and Merton himself supplied the classic analysis of how rule devotion, functional for predictability, turns dysfunctional for the clients the rules exist to serve. The organisation persists, on this reading, because the problems it solves persist, and its pathologies are the shadow prices of its solutions, to be managed by better design rather than abolished by denunciation.

The Marxist account of the same office block begins one floor down, in the labour process. The organisation's hierarchy mirrors and enforces the division between those who direct production and those who are directed, its rules encode the extraction of effort from labour power whose full capacity the wage never purchases, and its celebrated neutrality administers, in practice, the priorities of ownership through budgets, targets, and the quiet discipline of promotion. Bureaucratic dysfunctions receive a different diagnosis in this vocabulary: goal displacement serves those whom the goals would inconvenience, red tape falls heaviest on claimants with the least power to appeal, and the organisation's memory, its files, record what the order needs remembered about its subjects while its own decisions dissolve into minutes nobody can find. Trade unions occupy the paired position the pattern predicts: functionalism reads them as integration machinery, institutionalising conflict into bargaining that stabilises industrial relations; Marxism reads them as organised class power, simultaneously the working class's principal weapon and, in the tradition's self-critical moments, a bureaucracy of their own whose officials acquire interests in the stability of the bargaining order itself. That last observation, a Marxist functional analysis of a Marxist institution, officials explained by their position's requirements, is the shared form operating at maximum reflexivity, and the tradition produced it about its own organisations with the same grammar it applied to churches and courts.

### Where do the rival readings of bureaucracy divide?

over whether the organisation's pathologies are shadow prices or encoded priorities. Functionalism treats goal displacement and red tape as costs of solutions to real coordination problems, manageable by design. Marxism treats the same pathologies as patterned, falling where power directs them and sparing those whom the procedures exist to serve.

The empirical organisation literature, like the attainment literature before it, graded both readings into mechanisms. Studies of actual workplaces found discretion surviving inside the tightest rule systems, workers manufacturing informal orders that no chart records, and managers depending on the informal order they officially prohibit, findings that embarrass any account, functional or Marxist, in which the formal structure simply determines conduct. They found surveillance and effort extraction intensifying with new monitoring technologies in patterns the labour process account predicted in outline. They found professional and public-service organisations whose members' commitments to clients overrode both rule and target in ways neither grand reading budgets for. The pattern of the findings is by now this article's refrain: the phenomena both traditions named are real, the mechanisms are specifiable and specified in the best work, and the residue, the informal, the committed, the unscripted, marks the jurisdiction's boundary for both. A candidate who can narrate the workplace row with its informal residue intact has learned the comparison's deepest habit, which is to let every strong reading keep the evidence that embarrasses it in the same paragraph as the evidence that supports it.

## Cities, generations, and the subcultural bridge

Urban life supplied both traditions with a laboratory, and the paired readings of the city extend the grid into territory the examination occasionally visits. Functionalist urban sociology, in the ecological tradition, read the city as a differentiated order whose zones, districts, and institutions sort populations and activities into an interdependent whole, its pathologies, disorganisation in the famous vocabulary, arising where rapid change outruns the institutions of social control, leaving districts in which neither family, school, nor church can install the shared framework. The Marxist urban tradition read the same districts from the other end: the zones are produced, not stumbled into, by rent gradients, investment decisions, and the organised abandonment of populations whose labour the economy no longer requires at that place, and disorganisation names the experience of communities whose material supports have been withdrawn while the demand that they organise themselves continues unabated. Redevelopment then divides the readings cleanly: adaptive renewal of a district whose functions have changed, or accumulation by dispossession, the clearance of the poor from land whose value their presence suppresses. The evidence of particular redevelopments, who was displaced, who profited, what was built and for whom, discriminates between the readings case by case with unusual directness, which is why urban case studies are prized in teaching: the beneficiary question, elsewhere inferential, here has addresses.

Generations supply the bridge case, because youth subcultures were claimed by both traditions and explained by a third figure whose placement instructs. Albert Cohen, a functionalist working in Merton's strain tradition and no relation to the philosopher of the analytical chapters, explained delinquent subcultures as collective solutions to status frustration: boys measured by middle-class standards they cannot meet invert the standards, winning status among themselves by the very conduct that forfeits it elsewhere. The explanation is functional to its core, a subculture accounted for by the problem it solves for its members. The later Marxist and cultural-studies tradition read the same subcultures as symbolic resistance, style as a coded refusal of subordinate position, rituals through which class experience is magically resolved. The two readings divide over the subculture's meaning, adaptation or resistance, and share, once again, everything else: the refusal of individual pathology, the location of the phenomenon in structure, the explanation of its persistence by the labour it does for those caught in a position they did not choose. A candidate who distinguishes the two Cohens in a single parenthesis, the strain theorist from the analytical philosopher, signals a command of the field's dramatis personae that markers register immediately, and the signal is earned, since the confusion is common and the figures' projects, though both touch this article's themes, are entirely distinct.

## War, peace, and the order between states

The grid's rows have all been domestic, and a reader might suspect the shared form of being a peculiarity of institutions inside a single society. The international arena tests the suspicion, because both traditions extended their grammars across borders and the extensions behave identically. Functionalist international theory, in its classical form, proposed that cooperation grows by function: technical agencies organised around specific tasks, health, posts, aviation, rivers, generate habits and interests that spill over from one domain to the next, integrating populations whose governments remain formally separate, so that peace is built sideways, function by function, beneath the notice of high politics. The proposal is the domestic doctrine translated without alteration: order explained by the work its parts perform for the whole, integration as the beneficiary, persistence as the evidence. Its critics inside international relations made Merton's move in another vocabulary, asking functional for whom, and noting that technical cooperation distributes its benefits and its agenda-setting power as unevenly as any domestic institution.

The Marxist international tradition, descending from the theories of imperialism, reads the same agencies and the same order from the beneficiary question outward. The international order's institutions, on this reading, stabilise a world economy organised by unequal exchange and metropolitan accumulation: they manage crises that would otherwise interrupt accumulation, legitimate the hierarchy of states by presenting its rules as universal technique, and discipline peripheral economies through the conditions attached to assistance. Peace, where it holds among the order's beneficiaries, is the order's successful reproduction; war, where it erupts, is rivalry among accumulators or resistance by the accumulated-against. Set the two international readings beside the domestic grid and the correspondence is exact, row for row, with the world order substituted for the national society and the core-periphery hierarchy substituted for class. The substitution's ease is the test's result: the common form is no domestic accident but the traditions' common export, and the beneficiary dispute travels with it unchanged, which closes the last plausible escape route from the article's thesis, the suggestion that the convergence was an artefact of the six institutions chosen. The institutions were not chosen to converge. Every territory either tradition has entered, it has entered with the same grammar, and met the other already there.

### Does the shared form survive translation to the international arena?

entirely, since functionalist integration theory and the Marxist theory of imperialism reproduce the domestic pattern at world scale: order explained by services to a whole, the whole redescribed as a hierarchy of accumulation, the dispute confined to the beneficiary and the moral sign of the services.

One domestic territory remains unvisited, and it is the one where the comparison began historically: the economy itself, treated not as background but as an institution among institutions. Functionalism's economy is the adaptive subsystem, the order's interface with its material environment, whose markets coordinate millions of decisions through prices and whose firms organise production at scales no plan has matched for flexibility; inequality within it is the stratification thesis applied, and its crises are strains, maladjustments to be absorbed by the political subsystem's interventions. Marxism's economy is the base itself, the relations of production from which the grid's other rows hang, whose markets coordinate by compulsion, the compulsion of the propertyless to sell and of firms to accumulate or die, and whose crises are contradictions, the system's successes returning as its breakdowns. The contrast at this depth is the comparison's bedrock, and it is worth noticing that even here, at maximum substantive distance, the explanatory grammar holds: markets persist, for one tradition, because they serve the whole's adaptation, and for the other, because they serve accumulation's reproduction, and the crisis theories divide as pathology against business-as-usual in the pattern the anomie and alienation section established. The bedrock confirms the architecture. Two theories of everything social, built from one grammar, divided by one question, whose system, and the long cross-examination that question has sustained is the discipline's inheritance and this article's subject, now fully surveyed.

## Ritual in depth: what the elementary forms still teach

Durkheim's study of elementary religious life is the functionalist tradition's most ambitious single work, and it rewards a slower reading than the religion row could give it, because its argument contains, in embryo, both the comparison's shared form and the strongest version of the functionalist claim about cohesion. The study's wager was methodological: examine religion in its simplest documented form, the totemic systems of Australian Aboriginal societies as the ethnography of the period described them, and the essence of the phenomenon would stand revealed without the accretions of theology and church. The essence, on the study's famous conclusion, is society itself transfigured. The totem is the clan's emblem; the god and the group are made of the same substance; when the assembled clan generates the effervescence of collective ritual, the force participants feel descending on them from outside, greater than any individual and commanding their reverence, is the group's own power experienced in symbolic form. Religion is the machinery by which a society represents itself to itself, renews its members' attachment, and marks the boundary between the sacred, where collective force concentrates, and the profane, where private life proceeds.

Grant the argument its full power before auditing it, as the steelman protocol requires. It explains, in one movement, religion's universality, its emotional intensity, its moral authority, and its persistence across the collapse of every particular theology, since the need it serves, a group's self-renewal, survives every creed. It generates the functionalist research programme for a century: find the ritual, identify the solidarity it manufactures, trace the symbols to the group that worships itself through them. It even absorbs secularisation without strain, since the function can migrate to new vessels, national ceremonies, sporting festivals, commemorations, wherever assembly and emblem combine. The Marxist audit of the argument then proceeds at the joints the article has taught. For whom does the manufactured solidarity work, in a society divided by property: does the ritual integrate the group, or integrate the subordinate into the group's hierarchy? What does the transfiguration conceal: if the god is the group in disguise, whose portrait of the group does the disguise canonise, the whole membership's or its dominant stratum's? And what mechanism selects the rituals that persist: the group's need, asserted, or the patronage of those whom the prevailing symbols flatter, traceable in endowments, calendars, and suppressions? The audit does not refute the elementary forms. It re-sites their beneficiary, which is the comparison's eternal operation, and the study survives the re-siting as the greatest single demonstration that explanation by contribution to a whole can illuminate, whatever name the whole is finally given.

### What does the elementary forms argument claim at its strongest?

that religion's essence is a society's self-representation: the sacred force worshipped in ritual is the group's own collective power, experienced as external and commanding, and ritual persists because assembly renews the attachment on which the group's existence as a moral community depends.

The study's afterlife inside Marxist writing is the detail that completes the section. Theorists of ideology took the transfiguration mechanism and inverted its beneficiary: yes, collective representations descend on individuals with external force and the appearance of sacredness; the question is whose representations achieve that status, through which institutions, against which competing accounts. Gramsci's account of common sense, Althusser's account of interpellation, the cultural studies tradition's account of hegemony in popular forms, all run the Durkheimian machinery with a class governor fitted. The machinery's portability across the divide is the strongest possible evidence for this article's anatomy, stronger than any programmatic statement, because tools migrate only where the receiving workshop is built to use them. A tradition does not borrow its enemy's essence-detector for a century unless the detector works on its own material. Candidates who narrate the borrowing, Durkheim's mechanism running inside Marxist ideology theory, possess the comparison's most sophisticated single illustration, and it costs one paragraph to deploy.

## Socialisation across the whole of life

Socialisation appeared in the grid as a function assigned to family and school, but both traditions treat it as a lifelong process, and following it across the life course exposes another stratum of paired analysis. The functionalist account sequences the agencies: the family installs the foundational orientations, language, trust, the first map of authority and affection; the school generalises them into universal standards and occupational identities; the peer group rehearses independence from adult surveillance; the workplace completes the installation with the specific disciplines of a role; and the agencies of later life, media, associations, the rituals of retirement, manage the transitions the sequence cannot avoid. Each agency's contribution is specified by the stage's problem, and the theory's elegance lies in the dovetailing: a single process, the manufacture of a member, distributed across the biography and across institutions specialised for its phases. Failure at any phase has its named pathology, and the system's repair agencies, therapy, retraining, the second chance of further education, stand ready in the theory as they do, unevenly, in the world.

The Marxist account follows the same biography with the beneficiary question held steadily in view. The family's installation is the production of labour power's first dispositions, and its class variation is the account's signature finding: the language codes, aspirations, and relations to authority installed in childhood differ by the parents' position in production, so that the school's universal standards meet children unequally prepared to display them, and the inequality of the meeting is then recorded as inequality of talent. Occupational socialisation completes what the correspondence principle described, the workplace's authority relations training the dispositions the next workplace will require, while the agencies of consumption and leisure train the wants the economy needs its workers to have. Retirement, in this telling, is the biography's audit: the member whose labour power is exhausted discovers the precise cash value of the membership the earlier phases celebrated. The paired accounts agree, once more, on the process's existence, its institutional distribution, and its explanatory importance. They divide over whether the manufactured member is a citizen integrated into a moral community or a bearer of labour power fitted to a hierarchy, and the division is adjudicable in the only way the article has ever proposed: by examining, phase by phase, whose requirements the installed dispositions serve and whose they frustrate, with the evidence of language, aspiration, and outcome allowed to vote.

### How does following socialisation across a life change the comparison?

it converts two static doctrines into rival biographies of the same person, phase by phase, agency by agency, so the beneficiary dispute becomes concrete: each installed disposition can be asked whose requirements it serves, the member's integration into a community or the hierarchy's need for a fitted bearer of labour power.

A closing observation on this section's material returns to agency, the shared debt neither tradition has discharged. The biography the two accounts narrate is conspicuously passive in both: orientations are installed, dispositions are trained, members are manufactured. The person's own work on the material, the selective uptake, the irony, the quiet sabotage, the creative misreading of every lesson, appears in both literatures mainly as a residual category, deviance in one vocabulary and resistance in the other. The interpretive traditions built their reputations on that residue, and the biographical methods that later sociology developed, life histories in which narrators visibly assemble their socialisation into a self, can be read as the discipline's attempt to repay the debt from a third direction. The comparison's honest summary on agency is therefore triple: both structural traditions understate it for the same reason, their grammar explains by systems and systems do not improvise; the understatement is the price of their genuine discoveries about persistence; and the correction, when it came, came as a supplement instead of a substitute, since a self assembled from materials is still assembled from materials, and the materials' provenance is exactly what the two traditions, in their rival vocabularies, spent a century tracing.

## A worked paragraph, dissected

Abstract method becomes teachable when it is watched operating on a single paragraph, so this section composes the comparison's model paragraph on a fresh institution, the public library, and then dissects its own construction move by move. The library persists, the functionalist sentence runs, because a complex society requires repositories of its recorded knowledge accessible to all members, agencies of informal education that supplement the school, and neutral public spaces in which the community's members meet on terms of formal equality; remove the library and the order's cultural transmission, its second chances, and its civic fabric all tear at visible points. The library persists, the Marxist sentence runs, because a class order requires the legitimation of its knowledge hierarchy by a formally open door, the production of literate and self-improving labour power at public expense, and a safety valve through which the excluded may pursue individual mobility rather than collective remedy; remove the library and the order loses a cheap and beloved proof that its ladders are real. The shared column completes the triad without effort: both sentences explain the building through the services it renders to an order, both look past its manifest account, free books, to latent services, and both must produce mechanisms, funding decisions, usage patterns, closure battles, before their explanations graduate from the functional form they share.

Now the dissection, which is the section's point. The paragraph's first move was symmetry of construction, the two sentences constructed to one grammatical specification so that their difference is confined to the beneficiary and cannot hide in style. Its second move was the selection of discriminating evidence in advance: closure battles, in which communities fight to save libraries whose measurable use is modest, support the functionalist emphasis on civic and symbolic services, since populations defend what integrates them; funding patterns, in which library provision tracks local wealth with embarrassing fidelity, support the Marxist emphasis, since a universal service distributed by property values is the class pattern wearing municipal dress. Its third move was the concession structure: the functionalist sentence conceded the second-chance function that the Marxist reading claims as legitimation's evidence, and the Marxist sentence conceded the belovedness that pure legitimation stories cannot explain, since populations do not riot to defend their own sedation. A paragraph built this way can be produced, by a prepared candidate, for any institution an examiner names, in the time a long essay allows, and its production requires no inspiration, only the grid, the audit, and the discipline of concessions. That reproducibility is the article's final practical claim: the comparison is a craft with a transferable procedure, and the procedure, not any verdict about the two doctrines, is what the examination has been testing all along beneath its doctrinal surface.

### What makes the worked paragraph reproducible for any institution?

its procedure rather than its content: paired sentences built to identical grammatical specification, discriminating evidence selected in advance from funding, usage, and defence patterns, and concessions exchanged explicitly. The grid supplies the rows, the audit supplies the standard, and the institution named in the question supplies only the vocabulary.

The dissection generalises into the article's last word on method, addressed to every audience the persona layers serve. Comparisons fail, in examinations and in journals alike, at three predictable points: they compare conclusions while calling them methods, they quote critics from the wrong addresses, and they crown winners the evidence has not crowned. The repairs are correspondingly three: state the level of the comparison and hold it, verify every critic's allegiance before deploying the criticism, and distribute the verdict across dimensions with the defeater attached. A writer who performs the three repairs on this most familiar of comparisons has acquired the discipline for every comparison the discipline contains, which is why this article, charged with a single pairing of theories, has spent its length on procedure. The doctrines will be revised, the grid's rows extended, the critics' reputations re-sorted by future scholarship. The procedure, level, addresses, dimensions, defeater, will still be the way a careful mind compares anything with anything, and it is the article's true bequest to the student, the teacher, and the researcher alike.

## Poverty: the institution nobody founded

Poverty is not an institution, and that is exactly why it tests the comparison so severely: both traditions must explain a persistent pattern that nobody designed, and their explanations reveal their grammars with unusual nakedness. The functionalist tradition produced the notorious thesis that poverty performs services for the non-poor, a catalogue associated with Herbert Gans, who listed the functions with deliberate provocation: the poor perform the economy's dirty work at wages no one else accepts, subsidise the affluent through cheap labour and services, absorb the costs of economic change, provide employment for the professions that serve and police them, and furnish the respectable with a cautionary spectacle and a guaranteed inferior whose existence certifies the respectability of everyone ranked above. The catalogue's tone is ironic and its politics are reformist, Gans drawing the conclusion that poverty persists partly because it is useful, and that abolishing it requires replacing or repricing its functions, an argument addressed to policymakers in the language of their own systems thinking. Whatever a reader thinks of the provocation, its form is the purest functionalism in the entire literature of either tradition: a condition explained, item by item, by its contributions to the comfort of a containing order.

The Marxist reading of the same pattern proceeds through the reserve army and the reproduction of labour power at differential cost. Poverty, on this account, is not a residue the economy has failed to clear but a product it continuously manufactures: the relative surplus population disciplines the employed, whose wage demands moderate under the visible alternative; the poverty of one stratum is the subsidy of another's accumulation, paid in low wages, dangerous work accepted under necessity, and the externalisation of reproduction's costs onto households and communities least able to bill for them. Where the functionalist catalogue lists services to the affluent as a scandal to be priced, the Marxist account derives them from the wage relation itself, predicting that the services will be reproduced under any policy that leaves the relation intact, which is why its adherents read anti-poverty programmes as the order managing its own product's most destabilising symptoms. The third column writes itself one final time: both accounts explain poverty's persistence by its usefulness to an order that could, in principle, arrange otherwise; both refuse the explanation by the poor's character that common sense supplies; both are embarrassed, symmetrically, by poverty's genuine abolition in particular times and places, episodes each must explain as functions repriced or relations transformed, and the episodes' scarcity in the record is the datum both accounts lean on hardest.

### What does the poverty case prove about the two grammars?

that neither requires a designed institution to operate. Faced with an undesigned pattern, both traditions explain persistence by usefulness to an order, one itemising what poverty does for the comfortable as an indictment awaiting a price, the other deriving the pattern from the wage relation and predicting its reproduction while that relation stands.

The case carries one further lesson, about tone, which candidates can convert into a paragraph on the posture divide. Gans's catalogue demonstrates that functional grammar can serve a critical, even an angry, politics: the services are listed precisely to indict the order they serve, and the essay's force comes from making the beneficiaries read their own comfort itemised. The episode therefore refutes the lazy equation of functionalism with apology, just as the analytical Marxists' self-audit refutes the equation of Marxism with methodological innocence. Posture, the article argued, is a stage of inquiry instead of an essence of a tradition, and the poverty literature is the exhibit: the same grammar, in different hands, indicts or administers, while the rival grammar, in different hands, maps or recruits. Candidates who close the posture question with the poverty exhibit have answered it with evidence rather than with temperament, which is the only way it deserves to be answered.

## Death, dying, and the management of endings

The last institution is the one every member eventually uses, and the paired readings of death's social organisation close the grid's extension with fitting gravity. The functionalist account treats the rituals of dying and mourning as the order's repair work at its most necessary: the funeral gathers the group at the moment a member's loss tears its fabric, redistributes the dead person's roles among the survivors, stages the community's continuity in the face of the strongest evidence against it, and licenses grief in forms and durations that return the mourner, gradually, to ordinary obligations. Hospitals and hospices, in the modern chapter of the account, specialise the management: dying is sequestered where its disruption of other wards and workplaces is minimised, professionalised under roles that spare families the full weight of decisions they are unfit, in their grief, to carry. The account's strength is its recognition that a society must do something organised with death, that the something varies in intelligible relation to the society's structure, and that the mourner's private devastation is met, everywhere, by public forms that precede and outlast any individual's sorrow.

The Marxist account reads the same arrangements through property and labour. Death's organisation tracks the order's economics with a fidelity the ritual account understates: the costs of dying, the quality of care at its approach, the space and time a family can purchase for mourning, all distribute by class as reliably as every other good in the grid; the funeral industry commodifies grief into packages whose gradations reproduce, at the graveside, the stratification the deceased inhabited; and the sequestration of dying serves, among its other work, the protection of production and consumption from mortality's interruption, removing the spectacle of ending from the spaces where the order displays its vitality. Even inheritance, the account insists, is death's economic core: the moment at which property transmits across generations, the family's property function consummated, and the legal ceremony surrounding the will is the state's guarantee that accumulation survives the accumulator. The third column performs its office a final time. Both accounts refuse to leave death as a private biological event. Both explain its public forms by the work those forms perform for an order that must continue without the member it loses. Both treat the participants' own account, love, duty, reverence, as real and as incomplete. They divide, as they have divided on every row since education, over whether the order being continued is a moral community repairing its fabric or a structure of property transmitting itself across the generations that staff it, and a reader who has travelled this far will recognise the division as the comparison itself, stated at the graveside with the same clarity it had in the classroom.

### What do the rituals of death show the comparison, at the end of the grid?

that no human territory lies outside the paired grammar. Even dying, the most private event, is organised publicly, and both traditions explain the organisation by an order's need to continue: fabric repaired and roles redistributed, or property transmitted and production shielded from mortality's interruption.

The extension of the grid now stands at a dozen territories beyond its original six rows, and the exercise can be closed with the observation the extensions were gathered to support. A comparison whose procedure works unchanged on medicine, media, sport, knowledge, law, work, cities, generations, war, poverty, and death is not a comparison of two doctrines about six institutions. It is the exhibition of two grammars of social explanation, permanently available, permanently rival, and permanently entangled, whose rivalry has organised a discipline's century and whose entanglement is that century's best-kept secret. The verdict stated the entanglement as a thesis and attached its defeater. The persona layers translate it for the article's audiences. Nothing in the extensions has disturbed the thesis; every new territory has repeated its pattern, rival verdicts mounted on a common frame, and the repetition across unchosen ground is the strongest warrant a comparative claim can earn.

## The comparison's blind spot: what neither grammar can say

A complete comparison owes its readers an account of what both perspectives, for all their rivalry, jointly fail to see, because a shared form implies a shared horizon, and beyond the horizon lies everything the form cannot register. Three territories lie beyond it. The first is the event: the unrepeatable conjuncture, the assassination, the accident of weather on the decisive day, the leader's hesitation in the corridor, through which history repeatedly turns on hinges no system required and no class ordered. Structural grammars handle events badly by design, since their currency is recurrence, and both traditions have produced embarrassed footnotes at the moments when a single afternoon rearranged a decade. The second territory is meaning at first hand: what a ritual, a classroom, a picket line feels like to the person inside it, in the grain of experience that contribution analysis must translate into systemic vocabulary before it can speak, losing in translation precisely the quality that made the phenomenon matter to its participants. The third territory is novelty: the genuinely unprecedented practice, the institution without ancestors, whose persistence cannot yet be explained because it has not persisted, and whose emergence both grammars, tuned to reproduction, systematically underrate until the new thing has lasted long enough to be mistaken for an old one.

Naming the blind spot is not a refutation of either perspective, and candidates should resist presenting it as one. Every grammar has a horizon; the interpretive traditions that see events, meanings, and novelty most clearly are correspondingly blind to persistence, which is the structural pair's home territory. The mature conclusion is a division of labour among grammars rather than a tournament among them, with the comparison's two contestants assigned the territory they demonstrably illuminate: why institutions endure, how orders cohere or dominate, what patterns of cost and benefit their endurance distributes. A script that closes the evaluation by marking the shared horizon, and naming what lies beyond it, performs the rarest move in examination writing on this topic, which is to evaluate the comparison itself rather than merely its contents. Markers reward the move because it cannot be memorised from a table. It can only be reached by thinking through what a form of explanation is, which is the destination toward which this entire article has been travelling.

### What lies beyond the shared form's horizon?

the singular event that no system required, the first-hand texture of meaning that translation into systemic vocabulary loses, and genuine novelty whose persistence cannot yet explain it. Both grammars, tuned to recurrence, register these territories weakly, which marks a division of labour among approaches rather than a refutation of either.

There is a historical consolation for the blind spot, and it is worth a paragraph because it returns the comparison to the human scale at which it began. The founders of both traditions knew the horizon existed. The Marxist founding texts contain a famous insistence that people make their own history, albeit under inherited circumstances, a sentence that holds agency and structure in one breath and refuses to let either swallow the other. The functionalist founder's late work on the sacred lingers over the creative effervescence of assembled crowds, moments in which new ideals are born that no existing structure commissioned, and treats those moments as the wellspring of social renewal. Each founder, at the edge of the system, gestured toward the event and the creative act that the system could not generate from its own resources. The gestures were never built into the architectures, which is why the blind spot persisted, but their presence in the founding texts licenses candidates to present the horizon not as an external gotcha but as a tension the traditions carry within themselves, acknowledged at their sources and inherited, unresolved, by everyone who has worked in them since.

## Reading the two literatures: a short apprenticeship

The article closes its substantive work with practical guidance on reading, because the comparison is finally a relationship between two literatures, and most students meet both only in extract. The apprenticeship proposed here is short enough to be real. From the functionalist side, read one founding study whole rather than in summary, the elementary forms if stamina allows, a suicide study if it does not, watching for the moment where the argument turns from the phenomenon's manifest account to its contribution, since that turn is the tradition's signature and learning to spot it is learning the tradition. Read Merton next, the essays on functional analysis and on deviance, and notice the temperature change: the same grammar, handled with gloves, units specified, alternatives admitted, dysfunctions itemised. From the Marxist side, read the founding passage on religion whole, all four clauses and the sentence after, then one developed institutional analysis, the apparatuses essay or the correspondence study of schooling, watching for the identical turn, from the institution's self-description to its service for an order, performed in the rival vocabulary. Read Cohen last, the chapters where consequence explanation is owned and audited, and notice that the temperature matches Merton's exactly: an insider with gloves on, repairing the family instrument.

The apprenticeship's yield is a reader who can no longer be fooled by the tables, because the hands have felt the grammar operating in both literatures at first hand. Such a reader knows that the functionalist turn and the Marxist turn are the same turn, knows the names of the two insiders who said so from opposite houses, and knows which questions to ask of any new institutional analysis encountered thereafter: what system is being served, on what evidence, through which feedback, compared with which alternative, at whose cost. Five questions, portable to every row of every grid the discipline will ever set, and acquirable in a fortnight of evenings. The article's wager, stated at its opening and now redeemable, was that being accurate and being useful are the same achievement here. The apprenticeship is where they coincide most concretely: the accurate reader is the useful one, in the examination hall, in the seminar, and in every institution whose paired readings the graduate will spend a working life performing, usually without noticing that the old comparison is doing the work.

## Five hard questions from the floor

Seminars on this pairing reliably generate a handful of questions that the set texts answer badly, and gathering them here, with the best available replies, stress-tests the whole construction once more before the audiences take it away. The first asks whether the common grammar makes the pairing trivial: if the machinery is identical, why study two theories rather than one machinery with a dial? The reply turns on what the dial controls. The beneficiary is not a detail bolted onto an explanation; swapping it reverses the moral sign of every institutional verdict, redirects empirical attention toward different records, and commits the inquirer to different remedies. Two engines with identical mechanics and opposite steering are not one vehicle. The pairing survives because the steering is where societies live.

The second question asks whether the mechanism demand, pressed hard enough, dissolves both theories into ordinary causal history and leaves nothing distinctive behind. Partly, and the article has never hidden it: an institutional account with every loop specified is causal history of a high order, whatever banner it marches under. What survives the dissolution is the pair of sensibilities that generated the accounts, the habits of attention toward integration's prerequisites on one flank and toward distributions of benefit and burden on the other. Sensibilities are not nothing. They decide which causal histories get written, which archives opened, which variables measured, long before any loop is traced. A discipline that kept only the finished histories and lost the sensibilities would soon stop producing histories of the kinds it most values, which is a functional argument for the theories' persistence that both camps can savour at the other's expense.

The third question concerns sincerity: when adherents announce that an institution serves integration or domination, do they believe the announcement, and does belief matter to its truth? The traditions divide instructively here. One treats the analyst's belief as irrelevant to the pattern's reality, since latent services operate regardless of anyone's awareness, the analyst's included. The other, through its doctrine of ideology, treats belief as part of the machinery, since an arrangement's service often depends on being misdescribed by the very people who sustain it. The question matters practically because it governs how a researcher should handle testimony: as data about experience, always; as evidence about function, cautiously, since both grammars predict that participants will misdescribe the services they perform, one through latency, the other through hegemony. Interviewees are witnesses to their lives and unreliable narrators of their institutions, and the pairing, unusually, agrees on the caution while disagreeing about its grounds.

### Does identical machinery make the pairing trivial?

no, because the beneficiary steers everything that matters downstream: the verdict's moral sign on each institution, the records a researcher opens, the remedies a reformer reaches for. Shared mechanics with opposed steering describe two vehicles, not one, and the journey each takes through the same city reveals different streets.

The fourth question is the student's favourite and the examiner's trap: which theory would each founder have recognised as his own descendant? The exercise is unanswerable and therefore useful only as a discipline against ancestor worship. Founders disown heirs with regularity across intellectual history, and the texts license rival inheritances in both camps, the humanist and the structuralist quoting the same volumes against one another. The pairing's mature heirs are recognisable not by their fidelity oaths but by their working habits: specify the unit, trace the loop, concede the residue, state the reversal conditions. A descendant who works that way honours the ancestors better than one who recites them, and the seminar that grasps this has grasped why the article spent its length on procedure rather than on piety.

The fifth question is the politician's: granted everything claimed here, what follows for action on Monday morning? Three consequences follow with reasonable firmness. Before abolishing or defunding any institution, inventory its latent services and name their current beneficiaries, since reforms that ignore the inventory reliably injure the people they advertise helping. Before defending any institution by its services, name the population paying for them, since a service billed to the voiceless is a subsidy wearing virtue's clothing. And before announcing that a pattern of inequality is necessary, produce the allocation mechanism that no alternative could operate, since necessity claims in this territory have a documented habit of dissolving, under audit, into familiarity claims. None of the three consequences belongs to a camp. All three are the cross-examination's practical residue, and a graduate who carries them into a ministry, a newsroom, or a boardroom carries the pairing's real legacy, which was never a doctrine. It was a way of asking, twice over, what an arrangement does and whom the doing is for.

## A note on translation: the pairing in other tongues

The duel examined throughout this article was fought in several languages before it reached the English classroom, and its travels left marks on the pairing that a monolingual reader rarely notices. German sociology gave both camps founding vocabularies, yet the German debates arranged the contestants differently, with historical materialism measured against a native tradition of interpretive understanding rather than against a systems doctrine imported later. French sociology, heir to the lecture-hall founder, long treated the materialist rival as a philosophy of history to be answered philosophically, so the institutional pairing that English textbooks stage as a fair fight arrived in Paris as an argument about whether sociology itself should exist as an autonomous science. Russian and later Soviet scholarship inverted the polarity altogether: there the materialist account held official status, and functional reasoning survived in disguise, inside planning doctrines and cybernetic management theory, wearing the uniform of the state it supposedly opposed. Each national arrangement rearranged the furniture while preserving the room. A pairing robust enough to survive translation into hostile academic cultures, official doctrines, and rival philosophical climates has demonstrated a portability that purely local controversies never achieve, and the portability is itself a datum about the pairing's grip on genuine problems rather than on curricular habit.

Translation also altered key terms in ways that still shape misunderstanding. The German word rendered as function carries engineering resonances, a part's assigned operation within a designed apparatus, which made consequence talk sound more intentional in German ears than its authors may have meant. The French vocabulary of solidarity arrived in English flattened, losing the legal sense of mutual obligation that gave the founder's usage its precision about debts among strangers. The Russian career of the reproduction concept passed through planning bureaucracies that converted a critical category into an administrative target, an irony the concept's originators would have recognised as data for their own theory of how ideas fare inside apparatuses. Candidates need none of this philology for the examination, and researchers need a great deal of it, since arguments conducted across translations inherit the slippages silently. The teacher's use for it is a single cautionary minute in a seminar: the pairing on the page is already a translation of a translation, and some of its apparent paradoxes are customs declarations where concepts crossed borders and were repackaged.

What survives every repackaging is the pair of questions. Ask of a school in any of the languages concerned what it does for the order enclosing it, and then ask whose order that is, and the seminar begins again, in whatever tongue, with the same electricity. That recurrence across cultures is the closest thing the human sciences possess to a replication result, and it closes the article's survey of territories: the pairing has now been followed through a dozen institutions, four continents of curricula, three centuries of founding scenes and afterlives, and the pattern has repeated in each setting with a fidelity that no advocate arranged and no critic has explained away. Verdict, audiences, and questions follow, in that order, and the reader who has accompanied the survey holds everything needed to judge them.

## Coda: the examiner's corridor

There is a corridor outside the examination hall where candidates gather afterward, comparing the questions they chose and the answers they fear they gave, and the conversation in that corridor, overheard across many seasons, teaches a final lesson about this pairing. Candidates who chose the comparison question divide, within minutes, into two companies. One company recounts the answer as a contest they refereed, listing the points each side scored, anxious about whether the winner they declared matches the winner the marker privately supports. The other company recounts the answer as an instrument they operated, describing the institution they chose for the grid, the critic they placed at a surprising address, the paragraph where the stratification thesis was allowed its full strength before the reply arrived. The second company's anxiety has a different flavour: less about allegiance guessed correctly, more about operations performed cleanly. Season after season, it is the second company whose members return for the next paper calmer, because an instrument can be practised and a guess cannot. The corridor is not evidence in any scientific sense, and the article offers it as atmosphere rather than argument, yet atmosphere has its own testimony: a topic that leaves its students holding a tool feels different, in the body, from a topic that leaves them holding a flag.

The pairing will outlive every reader of this article, in syllabuses not yet printed, in languages into which it has not yet been translated, argued by students whose grandparents were not born when its textbooks were written. It will outlive them because the perplexity animating it is permanent: arrangements endure, endurance demands accounting, and the accounting forks, now as in the beginning, between the whole that an arrangement sustains and the few whom the sustaining enriches. Whoever first feels that fork, in a classroom or a courtroom or a kitchen where the washing up is being divided, has joined the conversation this article has merely transcribed at unusual length. The transcription ends where it should, not with a victor's name but with the fork itself, held up to the light: two questions, asked in sequence, of every institution a life will pass through. What does this arrangement keep going? And for whom does the keeping count as gain?

A word, in closing, about the temper in which the fork should be carried. The two questions are diagnostic instruments, not insults, and they lose their edge when wielded as accusations against the people staffing an arrangement, most of whom inherited their posts, their procedures, and their blind spots from predecessors equally inherited. The school secretary, the parish volunteer, the junior clerk in the benefits office: none designed the machinery whose services and charges this article has taught its readers to inventory, and an analysis that cannot distinguish between arraigning an arrangement and sneering at its occupants has confused the traditions it studied with the polemics they attract. Both founders, at their best, kept the distinction bright. The critic of religion wrote with more tenderness toward believers than toward the world requiring their consolation. The theorist of solidarity wrote about deviants with more curiosity than condemnation, treating the outcast as the community's unwilling teacher. A graduate who pairs the two questions with that temper, forensic about structures and gentle with persons, has absorbed the deepest lesson the comparison holds, deeper than any thesis about form: that understanding how an order persists, and whom its persistence serves, is compatible with, and perhaps requires, a refusal to despise the people persisting inside it.

Carry the fork lightly, then, and use it often, since instruments rust in drawers and sharpen only on material. Every institution a reader enters this week, a workplace, a clinic, a classroom, a committee, is a fresh row waiting for its paired readings and its third column.

## What functionalism sees that Marxism can miss

Begin the reciprocal illumination with functionalism's distinctive sight. Its first gift is attention to the prerequisites of any order whatsoever, including orders its rivals hope to build. Coordination, socialisation, the allocation of persons to tasks, the management of deviance, the renewal of motivation across generations: these problems do not vanish when property relations change, and a tradition trained to ask how any society solves them carries a checklist that revolutionary theory ignores at its peril. The historical record of attempted transformations is, in part, a record of rediscovered prerequisites, new orders finding themselves rebuilding allocation mechanisms, legitimation rituals, and socialisation agencies under new names, sometimes with the Davis and Moore differentials reappearing in the currencies of status and access. Functionalism predicted the rediscovery in general form, because its theory says the problems are generated by social complexity itself rather than by any particular property regime. That predictive success, limited but real, is the strongest card in its hand, and Marxist writing that never acknowledges it is playing with a partial deck.

Its second gift is a phenomenology of order: an account of what cohesion feels like from inside, the unreflective ease of members who share a moral world. Marxist vocabulary, tuned to domination and interest, can make ordinary contentment illegible, forcing it into the categories of false consciousness or hegemonic capture even where the contentment is, by any humane standard, simply the experience of a life going adequately in a community that works. Functionalism's categories, solidarity, integration, ritual renewal, describe that experience without insulting it, and description without insult is a scholarly virtue as well as a courtesy. The gift has a corresponding discipline: functionalist writing at its best attends to the micro-texture of ceremonies, classrooms, and wards, the small repeated acts in which order is actually manufactured, with a patience that system-level Marxist writing rarely matches.

### Which perspective serves which kind of question?

the honest answer is conditional. Functionalism serves questions about how any complex order coordinates, socialises, and repairs itself. Marxism serves questions about who benefits, who pays, and how an order's harms are organised and concealed. Usefulness follows the question asked, which is why the perspectives persist as complements in practice despite their rivalry in principle.

Its third gift is a suspicion of manifestos. Because functionalism expects institutions to be entangled in webs of unintended consequence, it reads reform programmes with a standing question the reformers seldom ask: what else does the targeted institution do, latently, that its abolition would interrupt? Merton's insistence on net balance, functions weighed against dysfunctions for specified units, is the methodological form of this suspicion, and it has improved policy thinking far beyond the tradition's borders. Marxism possesses its own unintended-consequence tradition, the cunning of history, reforms strengthening what they attack, yet its programmatic moods often suspend the suspicion for its own proposals. A comparison that credits functionalism with the latent-function discipline, while noting that Marxism's best historical writing practises it too, allocates the credit where the record puts it.

## What Marxism sees that functionalism can miss

Marxism's distinctive sight begins with power's address. Functionalist writing characteristically asks how the system works; Marxist writing characteristically asks where the system's benefits are delivered and where its costs are dumped, and the second question, pursued relentlessly, uncovers patterns the first question's grammar cannot easily state. The pattern of law's enforcement, severe downward and lenient upward. The pattern of schooling's rewards, tracking origin while certifying merit. The pattern of the household's subsidy, flowing from unwaged carers to waged production. Each pattern was visible in the data before Marxist analysis named it, and each was rendered illegible by frameworks that aggregated consequences at the level of the whole society, where a benefit to one stratum and an injury to another sum into a misleading average. Merton's unit question opened functionalism's door to this analysis, and through the door walked much of what is valuable in later mainstream sociology, but the tradition that had always specified the unit as class arrived first, and priority matters to an honest ledger.

Its second gift is historicity. Functionalism's systems tend toward the timeless: needs are specified for societies as such, and particular arrangements are read as solutions to eternal problems, which flattens the difference between arrangements that emerged yesterday under contest and arrangements immemorial. Marxism's orders are historical through and through, each mode of production arising, maturing, and decaying within dated struggles, its institutions bearing the marks of the compromises and defeats that installed them. The historical sense disciplines explanation in a way candidates should articulate: an institution explained by an eternal need invites complacency about its present form, while an institution explained by a dated settlement invites inquiry into the settlement's terms and their possible renegotiation. The political valences differ accordingly, one tradition's grammar leaning toward conservation of what demonstrably works, the other's toward transformation of what demonstrably injures, and the leanings are not incidental decorations on the theories. They are consequences of the theories' temporal structures.

### How should the comparison be graded once the form is shared?

by relocating the marks. Once method is recognised as common, credit shifts to the quality of substantive claims: the accuracy of each side's beneficiary, the honesty of its concessions, the precision of its mechanisms. Scripts earn the highest band by grading those, not by rehearsing a contrast the evidence dissolves.

Its third gift is the critique of ideology as a research programme: the systematic study of how ideas present sectional arrangements as universal goods, history as nature, and contingency as necessity. Functionalism knows that values legitimate order, indeed its cohesion story requires them to, but it characteristically treats the values' content as given, the society's moral heritage, and asks only how effectively they are transmitted. Marxism asks the prior question, whose experience the values encode, whose injuries they leave unnamed, and how the encoding was accomplished through control of pulpits, presses, and curricula. The prior question has proved exportable far beyond its home tradition, into media studies, the sociology of knowledge, and the analysis of expertise, and a comparison that omits it understates what the Marxist tradition contributed to the common stock of sociological tools. Set the three gifts of each side in balance and the persistence of both perspectives stops being mysterious. Each is a searchlight with a characteristic beam and a characteristic shadow, and the working sociologist's craft consists partly in knowing which darkness one is standing in.

## Misreadings gathered in one place

The comparison attracts a stable set of misreadings, and collecting them serves candidates better than scattering corrections through the argument, so they are gathered here with their remedies attached. The first misreading holds that the perspectives differ in method, one reasoning from consensus and the other from conflict. The remedy is the article's thesis: consensus and conflict are conclusions about cohesion, while the method, explanation by contribution to a system's upkeep, is shared, and the difference lies in the beneficiary each conclusion names. The second misreading holds that functionalism denies conflict. The remedy is Merton: dysfunctions, unit-specific consequences, and strain theory build conflict into the perspective's mature form, and the denial belongs only to the fossil version preserved in elementary tables. The third misreading holds that Marxism escapes the objection to functional explanation. The remedy is Cohen and Elster: the objection was pressed hardest from inside Marxism, and unelaborated Marxist consequence claims stand under the same sentence as unelaborated functionalist ones.

The fourth misreading holds that the stratification thesis is easily dismissed, a rich man's excuse that a paragraph of scorn can retire. The remedy is the full-strength statement given earlier in this article: the thesis explains a universal phenomenon by a universal allocation problem, concedes nothing to particular distributions, and poses an operational question, how else to allocate scarce trained competence, that egalitarian alternatives must answer in detail. Tumin's critique damages the thesis gravely at measurement and at the talent pool, and the universality observation still stands afterward, which is why the exchange remains set reading rather than a closed file. The fifth misreading holds that one perspective is simply correct, the other simply an error, so that the comparison is a coronation rather than an analysis. The remedy is the reciprocal illumination of the preceding sections: each tradition sees a region the other misses, each pays for its sight with a characteristic blindness, and the evidence available does not crown either. A candidate's maturity on this topic is measurable precisely by resistance to the fifth misreading, since the examination's design, two searchlights and a grid, invites the coronation and rewards its refusal.

### What must a student keep apart when comparing theories?

the machinery of explanation from the claims the machinery delivers. Method here is the tracing of contributions to a system's persistence, common to both perspectives. Conclusions are the claims about whose system persists, a moral community or a class order, and confusing the second for the first manufactures the textbook contrast.

A sixth misreading, subtler than the five, concerns the critics' addresses, and it has been corrected at length already: Merton quoted as functionalism's external critic when he was its internal reformer, the analytical Marxists overlooked as functional explanation's sharpest assailants because the casting by camp hides them. The remedy is the habit of checking allegiance before deploying a quotation, a habit whose value extends to every comparison in the syllabus, since most living traditions house their own best critics and most textbook duels are, on inspection, two internal dialogues interleaved. Candidates who acquire the habit will find it paying marks across papers that never mention functionalism at all.

## The verdict and the evidence that would defeat it

The verdict can now be stated with the precision the preceding sections have earned. Marxism and functionalism share an explanatory form: both account for institutions through what each one contributes toward keeping a system in being, parting over whose system is at stake, the moral community of the whole society or the class order that organises production and property. The shared form is not a verbal coincidence or a debating trick. It appears in every row of the institutions grid, in the showcase cases of education and stratification as much as in the quieter rows, and it governs the practice of both literatures at their best as well as their most routine. Because the form is shared, the standard contrast of consensus against conflict must be redescribed: it names the perspectives' rival conclusions about what holds societies together, not rival methods of inquiry, and teaching or examining it as a methodological contrast propagates an error. Because the grammar is common property, the logical objection to functional explanation, that citing a benefit does not explain a presence without a selecting and sustaining mechanism, applies to both perspectives equally, and each tradition's maturity is best measured by the mechanisms its adherents actually supply, claim by claim, rather than by the confidence of its manifestos. And because the form is shared while the beneficiaries differ, the live intellectual contest between the perspectives is substantive and empirical: which beneficiary, in which institution, in which period, does the pattern of consequences actually serve, and at whose cost? That contest remains open, and its openness is not a failure of the comparison but its result.

Every verdict in this series carries its defeater, and this article's is exact. The shared-form claim would be defeated by a Marxist explanation of an institution that specifies a mechanism by which arrangements serving class interests are selected and others eliminated, since supplying the mechanism is precisely what would distinguish it from a functional explanation. Read the defeater carefully, because it cuts in a direction partisans may find surprising. It does not say the claim falls if Marxism is shown to be true, or if functionalism is shown to be false. It says the claim falls, for any given institution, where Marxist practice actually delivers the elaborated causal story, actors, feedbacks, and selection pressures specified, that functional explanation omits. Wherever that delivery occurs, the Marxist account of that institution has genuinely exited the shared form, and the article's thesis must be amended row by row. The defeater thus converts the verdict from a slogan into a research programme: audit the literatures institution by institution, mark where mechanisms are supplied and where promissory notes circulate, and let the shared-form claim shrink or stand according to the audit's findings. A claim that invites its own audit, and specifies the evidence that would amend it, has done what this series asks of verdicts. It has made itself corrigible in public.

### Why does the two-column table mislead its readers?

because they organise the contrast by conclusions while presenting the organisation as a contrast of approaches, and because two columns are easier to print than three. Adding the shared column, what both readings presuppose about explanation, corrects the misstatement at the point where students actually absorb it.

The practical consequences of the verdict distribute themselves across the article's audiences, and the persona layers that follow take them up in turn: for candidates, a comparison architecture that replaces the two-column table with the grid and the shared-form observation; for teachers, a seminar design built around the misattributed critics; for researchers, an audit programme and a set of literatures, two neighbouring fields where the problem of functional explanation is a standing concern pursued with tools sociology can borrow. The comparison also travels beyond the discipline's borders as a teaching device, since the institution-by-institution method, paired readings with a shared column, generalises to any pair of theories whose rivalry conceals a common grammar. What began as the most routine topic on the specification ends, handled this way, as an education in how theories relate: less often as strangers than as estranged relatives, disputing an inheritance neither can deny sharing.

## For students and exam candidates

This layer converts the article into instructions a candidate can act on, organised by the questions the examination actually sets on this material. The comparison between Marxism and functionalism is among the most frequently set theory questions in the subject, and it is also among the most predictably mishandled, because the standard preparation, a two-column table of contrasts, equips candidates to recite conclusions while leaving them unable to perform the operation the question rewards. The first instruction is therefore architectural. Build the answer on the institutions grid rather than on the table: choose the institutions the question names or, where it names none, education, religion, and stratification, and for each one give both rival readings together with the presupposition they share, in a single connected movement. An answer organised by institution compares continuously. An answer organised by theory, functionalism first in all its aspects and Marxism second, compares almost nowhere, and markers read hundreds of both kinds and grade them accordingly.

The second instruction concerns the opening. Spend the first paragraph on the shared form, stated in the article's terms: both perspectives account for an institution by what it contributes toward keeping a system in being, parting over the interests that upkeep serves, so consensus against conflict names conclusions rather than method. That opening earns its place before any table or grid because it announces the analytical altitude of everything following, and because it converts the question's most familiar material, the contrast every candidate will recite, into the script's first evaluated claim rather than its unexamined frame. The third instruction concerns the critics. Place them at their correct addresses. Merton belongs inside functionalism, its internal reformer, whose separation of manifest from latent functions, and whose insistence on specifying the unit a consequence serves for are repairs offered to his own tradition. Cohen and Elster belong inside Marxism, its analytical auditors, whose demand for mechanisms elaborating consequence claims is the sharpest criticism functional explanation has received from any quarter. A script that deploys Merton against functionalism, or that presents the teleology objection as a Marxist weapon against a functionalist vice, will be marked by examiners who know the addresses, and the correction costs nothing to make.

The fourth instruction concerns stratification, the question's most reliable discriminator. State the Davis and Moore thesis at full strength, with its authors and its date, its two variables of importance and scarcity, and its universality evidence, before deploying Tumin's critique at the measurement of importance, at the restricted talent pool, and at the assumption that differential reward is the only motivator. Then concede what survives: the universality of stratification as a fact requiring explanation, and the allocation problem as a question any alternative must answer operationally. Scripts that caricature the thesis lose the evaluation marks twice over, once for inaccuracy and once for the concession they are then unable to make. The kit for this topic assembles itself from the article's apparatus. The institutions grid becomes a set of revision cards, one institution per card with its paired readings and shared column on the reverse, and the Marxist study notes tool at [VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) is built for exactly that conversion, letting the candidate drill the grid in short sessions until the paired readings are reflexive. For candidates in the civil service stream, where theory comparisons arrive inside wider papers on sociological theory, the previous year question explorer at [ReportMedic](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html) lets the candidate test the architecture against questions actually set rather than invented ones, marking each attempt against the error catalogue: method confused with conclusions, Merton misplaced, the stratification thesis caricatured, teleology aimed at one side only. The full assessment map for the sociology papers sits in the series guide to [marxist sociology exam answers](/2021/08/15/marxist-sociology-exam-answers/), and candidates should take their question typology from there and their comparison doctrine from this article.

A final instruction concerns the verdict paragraph, where scripts most often surrender the marks the preceding pages earned. Do not crown a winner. Distribute the grades: functionalism stronger on persistence, cohesion's phenomenology, and the prerequisites any order must meet; Marxism stronger on beneficiaries, costs, and the patterned distribution of both; the shared objections, teleology, mechanism, determinism, falsifiability, charged against both accounts in equal measure. Then close with the defeater habit this series teaches across all its guides: state what would overturn the answer's central claim, here a Marxist institutional explanation that supplies the selection mechanism distinguishing it from functional explanation, since a judgement that names its own reversal conditions earns the evaluation credit that assertion alone cannot reach.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The teaching opportunity this comparison offers is disproportionate to its familiarity, because it lets a class watch a textbook contrast dissolve under inspection and a more interesting structure appear in its place, and the experience generalises into a habit no lecture on method can install as durably. The session architecture that teachers report as reliable begins with the two-column table itself, eliciting the contrast the class already owns, consensus against conflict, harmony against domination, and letting the class commit itself to the table's adequacy in discussion before any correction arrives. Commitment matters: a correction offered to the uncommitted slides off, while a correction offered to students who have publicly defended the table rearranges furniture. Then set the paired sentence exercise. Give the room one institution and require two explanations of its persistence, one in each vocabulary, written out in full sentences. Read them aloud side by side and ask the room what changed between the sentences and what refused to change. The rearrangement is audible. Follow with the grid's third column as the session's constructive task, each group supplying the shared presupposition for one institution's row, and let the room discover that the antidote to the table was a column the table had always omitted.

The seminar exercises build themselves from the article's apparatus. The addresses exercise, distributing quotations from Merton, Cohen, and Elster without attribution and requiring the class to place each critic inside or outside the tradition criticised, teaches the difference between a criticism's tone and its author's allegiance and takes twenty minutes. The steelman exercise, assigning the Davis and Moore thesis to the students most hostile to it and Tumin's critique to its warmest admirers, teaches the protocol by forcing each side to pass the other's examination before sitting its own. The audit exercise, taking one paragraph of functional prose from each tradition and marking every sentence that names a beneficiary without naming a mechanism, teaches the shared-form thesis as a detection skill rather than as a doctrine, and produces, in strong cohorts, the genuine scholarly pleasure of watching a claim from one's own side fail a fair test. Each exercise is portable to other theory pairings, and teachers should say so explicitly, since the transfer is the lesson's largest yield: a student who leaves able to audit any comparison has been given something no syllabus itemises and every subsequent course will silently reward.

Handling the room's allegiances requires the neutrality this article has observed. Students arrive with political sympathies that the material touches directly, and a teacher who performs a personal verdict in either direction, recruiting the comparison into a contemporary allegiance, forfeits the room's trust for the harder topics ahead and models exactly the confusion of mapping with judging that the article's posture section warns against. The discipline that works is sequential and public: first the paired readings at full strength, each given its best voice by the teacher's own summary, then the shared audit applied impartially, then, and only then, open judgement, in which students' verdicts are welcomed as verdicts and graded on the evidence marshalled rather than on their direction. Assessment literacy completes the layer. Teachers preparing candidates for the papers described in the student layer should mark practice answers against the error catalogue, method against conclusions, critics at wrong addresses, stratification caricatured, objections aimed one way, and should show candidates at least once a pair of scripts on the same question, one built from the table and one from the grid, marked blind by a colleague. The mark difference teaches what exhortation cannot.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Researchers meeting this comparison inside larger projects should treat the article as a checklist of verifications rather than as a conclusion, since each of its corrections rests on a distinct evidentiary base that a serious project will want to inspect at first hand. The textual base is the primary literature of both traditions, the stratification article of 1945 and its 1953 critique, Merton's essays on functional analysis, Cohen's reconstruction of historical materialism and Elster's audit of it, read in editions whose provenance can be stated. The reception base is the textbook tradition itself, whose tables can be sampled across decades to document when the consensus-conflict contrast hardened into its fossil form and which internal critics the tables omitted at each stage. The empirical base is the institutional literatures the grid summarises, attainment and mobility research for education, organisational studies for religion, time-use research for the family, sentencing and enforcement patterns for crime, each capable of confirming or embarrassing the paired readings row by row. A project that verifies the bases independently will find the article's shared-form thesis either confirmed in detail or instructively damaged in particular rows, and either outcome is publishable, which is the mark of a thesis worth testing.

Historians of sociology have the richest unworked seam here. The duel's career as a teaching device, its migration across national curricula, its hardening and occasional thawing, is traceable through syllabuses, examination archives, and textbook editions, and a full reception history would test this article's conjecture that the fossil contrast is a product of mass examination systems rather than of the scholarly literature, which corrected itself generations ago through the internal critics. The archives for the test exist in examination board records and in the successive editions of the textbooks through which most students meet the subject. A scholar who writes that history will settle several of the article's open questions, including the prognosis that the traditions converge on a mechanism-conscious structural sociology, as a by-product. Journalists inherit the comparison less often than its constituent slogans, consensus and conflict arriving as shorthand in coverage of education, crime, and welfare disputes, and this layer's advice is pitched at the working paragraph: when a dispute is framed as values against interests, check whether the parties share more method than the framing admits, since opposing camps in policy arguments habitually explain the same institution by its services to rival beneficiaries while agreeing, unnoticed, on the explanatory grammar. The column that notices the agreement will be corrected by nobody, because it is the rare observation in a polarised argument that both sides' specialists recognise as true.

A final note for all three professions concerns the defeater as a reusable standard. The article's verdict names the proof that would oblige it to change, a supplied mechanism, row by row, and the standard generalises to every theoretical claim a researcher reports, a historian reconstructs, or a journalist summarises: ask what the claim's holder would accept as counting against it, and treat the absence of an answer as information about the claim's status. Researchers who hold that discipline will find their work trusted across the divides this subject usually polarises, and trust, on a topic where every camp audits every other camp's footnotes, is the only currency that spends.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What do Marxism and functionalism have in common?

Far more than the standard contrast suggests. Both are structural and macro in scale, both subordinate individual agency to the requirements of a system, and both explain institutions by the contribution those institutions make to the system's maintenance. A school, a church, or a court is never self-explanatory for either perspective: each reaches past the account an institution offers of itself, toward the labour it carries out on behalf of a wider order. The perspectives diverge over the identity of that order, a moral community in one telling and a class structure in the other, which is a disagreement about the world rather than about how explanations are built.

### Q: Is Marxism functionalist in form?

In the precise sense this article defines, yes. Wherever Marxist writing explains an institution by its contribution to reproducing a class order, the law by its service to property, schooling by its renewal of labour power, ideology by its legitimation of domination, the explanation's form is functional, whatever its author's allegiance. The tradition's own analytical wing recognised this openly: Cohen's reconstruction of historical materialism rests on consequence explanation and defends it, while demanding the mechanisms that would elaborate it. Form and allegiance are separate questions, and answering the first honestly does not settle the second.

### Q: How do the two perspectives read religion differently?

They start from different primary phenomena. Functionalism, descending from Durkheim, starts from solidarity: ritual gathers a community into shared effervescence, renews its moral unity, marks its passages, and consoles its members as a by-product of binding them. Marxism starts from suffering: faith appears there as an oppressed creature's sigh, voicing genuine pain while also registering refusal of that pain, and its combined effect reconciles sufferers to endurance while clothing the arrangements behind their suffering in sacred authority. The readings share a sociological grammar, belief treated as a phenomenon whose place within a system explains it, and divide over whether the system's name is the moral community or the class order.

### Q: What is the Davis and Moore thesis on stratification?

The thesis, published by Kingsley Davis and Wilbert Moore in 1945, holds that stratification is functionally necessary in every society. Positions differ in how much depends on their competent performance and in how few people can be brought, through prolonged and costly preparation, to perform them, so differential rewards of wealth, prestige, and leisure work as the inducement drawing able candidates toward the posts carrying the heaviest demands. Its evidence is universality: every known society stratifies, which suggests a general requirement rather than a local imposition. Tumin's classic critique answered that importance is measured circularly by reward, that stratification wastes the talent pool it claims to allocate, and that reward is not the only motivator.

### Q: What criticisms apply to both perspectives?

Four objections strike both with equal force because both share an explanatory form. Teleology: each risks explaining a cause by its effect, an institution by the benefit it confers. The missing mechanism: each often omits the feedback process by which benefits sustain the arrangements producing them. Determinism: each subordinates agency to system requirements, a subordination the interpretive traditions protested against both. Falsifiability: each commands a flexible vocabulary able to recast almost any result as further confirmation of itself. The traditions' best defenders answer in parallel, distinguishing testable particular claims from the frameworks generating them, and the honest conclusion is that vigilance, not allegiance, is the remedy.

### Q: How do they explain crime differently?

Functionalism finds crime's significance in the community's reaction to it. Crime is normal, present everywhere, and within limits useful: punishment rituals dramatise moral boundaries and renew shared norms, while the deviant occasionally prototypes changes the community subsequently adopts. Marxism finds the significance in the way crime gets defined, generated, and prosecuted. The criminal law protects property more thoroughly than persons, inequality manufactures the conduct the law then punishes, and enforcement falls with such class unevenness that the prison population mirrors the hierarchy of classes more faithfully than it mirrors the spread of harmful behaviour. Both refuse to explain crime by offenders' wickedness; they divide over whose order the criminal process maintains.

### Q: Do functionalists deny conflict?

The perspective's caricature does; its strongest published form does not. Merton, functionalism's leading internal reformer, built conflict into the apparatus: a practice may be functional for one group and dysfunctional for another, and his strain theory grounds deviance in a structural contradiction between culturally prescribed goals and unequally distributed means. Coser devoted a study to the functions of social conflict itself, its boundary clarification, its safety valves, its revitalisation of norms. Denial of conflict, properly speaking, belongs to the elementary table in which everything harmoniously serves everyone, a version the tradition's own major figures had already dismantled while the textbooks were still printing it.

### Q: How do they read the family differently?

Functionalism reads the household as a refuge and a foundation: it performs the primary socialisation of children and the stabilisation of adult personalities, absorbing the strains of occupational life and returning members to the wider order repaired. Marxism reads the household instead as an unremunerated annex of the wider economy: it renews labour power daily and generationally, feeding, soothing, and raising the workforce at no cost to employers, while defining the work, performed overwhelmingly by women, as love rather than labour and thereby exempting it from wages. Feminist scholarship pressed both readings, keeping the attention to socialisation and to material organisation while refusing each tradition's romance.

### Q: Which perspective is more useful?

Usefulness follows the question being asked. For questions concerning coordination inside any complex order, the socialising of newcomers, the allocating of people to tasks, and self-repair under strain, the functionalist apparatus is the sharper instrument. For questions about who benefits from an arrangement, who pays for it, how its harms are distributed, and how its self-description conceals those distributions, the Marxist apparatus is sharper. Practitioners in fields that study institutions from inside describe switching between the two lenses while handling identical cases in a single working week. The examination answer that crowns one perspective has usually mistaken the nature of the choice.

### Q: What is the difference between manifest and latent functions?

The distinction is Merton's, drawn inside functionalism. Manifest functions are consequences that participants intend and recognise: the teaching a school avowedly delivers, the deterrent effect a court avowedly announces. Latent functions are consequences nobody intends or recognises: the friendships formed, the ranking by status quietly accomplished, and the safekeeping of children that schooling delivers under cover of instruction alone, or the solidarity a sentence renews under cover of deterrence alone. The distinction matters because it disciplines functional claims, requiring the analyst to specify which consequences are avowed, which are hidden, and for which unit each holds, a discipline that quietly moves sophisticated functionalism toward questions about interests that the elementary contrast assigns to Marxism alone.

### Q: How does the shared form grade the comparison of the two perspectives?

It moves the credit elsewhere. Once the method stands revealed as common property, no marks remain for reciting a methodological opposition, and credit moves to how good the substantive work is: how accurately each side names a beneficiary, how honestly it concedes ground under criticism, how precisely it specifies mechanisms for its consequence claims, and how discriminatingly it uses evidence row by row. Scripts earn the highest band by performing those operations, comparing paired readings institution by institution and grading each tradition dimension by dimension, rather than by rehearsing a duel whose methodological premise dissolves under inspection.

### Q: What should a student separate between a perspective's method and its conclusions?

The apparatus by which explanations are produced from the assertions that apparatus ends up delivering. The method, in this comparison, is the tracing of an institution's contributions to the persistence of a containing system, and it is common to both perspectives. The conclusions are the claims about what the containing system is, a moral community held by shared values or a class order held by property and coercion, and about whose interests its persistence serves. Mixing up the second with the first is what fabricates the familiar textbook opposition, where consensus and conflict get paraded as rival methods although they are rival answers to a single question about what keeps a society in one piece.

### Q: Why do comparison tables misstate the difference?

Because the tables sort the two perspectives by the answers they give while advertising the sorting as a difference in how they reason, and because a pair of columns is simpler to print, commit to memory, and set questions on than a triple arrangement. A two-column table juxtaposes the readings of education, religion, the family, crime, and stratification without ever performing the comparison, since comparison requires naming what the paired readings share as well as where they collide. Adding the third column, the shared presupposition that institutions are explained by their contribution to a system's maintenance, corrects the misstatement exactly where learners take it in, which is the table itself.

### Q: How does the institutions grid order the two readings?

Institution by institution, rather than theory by theory. Each row takes a single institution, the state, stratification, crime, the family, religion, and education among them, and sets the rival readings side by side before a final column records the presupposition both readings hold in common. Arranged this way, comparison cannot be postponed, because neighbouring cells have to be filled and the shared entry must name what the pair jointly assumes. Arranged by theory instead, with one doctrine expounded completely before the other begins, the exercise collapses into two summaries that never meet, the failure the grid exists to forestall.

### Q: What separates explaining by function from explaining by mechanism?

Whether a feedback process exists. Explaining by function identifies an advantage that some arrangement delivers to a wider system, legitimation perhaps, or integration, or reproduction. Explaining by mechanism traces the route by which that benefit keeps the arrangement itself in existence, through reinforcement by pleased audiences, imitation by rivals, deliberate design and revision, suppression of alternatives, or the compounding advantages of early adoption. Biology's functional explanations are respectable because natural selection supplies the loop; social explanations earn the same respect case by case, where a loop is actually specified. Cohen's demand that consequence claims be elaborated is the standard, and it applies to both perspectives in this comparison without favour.
