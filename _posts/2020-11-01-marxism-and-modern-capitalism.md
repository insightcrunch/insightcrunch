Ask whether Marx still explains modern capitalism and the answers arrive prepackaged, in two flavours that have changed little across generations of argument. One camp treats the present order as a vindication so complete that only wilful blindness could miss it. The other treats the same order as a refutation so complete that only nostalgia could deny it. Both camps share a method, and the method is the problem. Each selects the features of the present that suit its verdict, arranges them into a portrait, and invites the reader to mistake the portrait for a test. A serious assessment cannot proceed that way. Marx did not issue one prediction about capitalism. He identified a set of distinct tendencies within a mode of production, stated at differing levels of confidence, resting on differing mechanisms, and open to differing kinds of evidence. The honest question is therefore plural. Which tendencies were borne out, which were not, which were framed too loosely to be tested at all, and what follows for the framework when the answers are laid side by side rather than averaged into a slogan?

![A desk arranged with a tendency scorecard, volumes of political economy and a magnifier resting on an open ledger, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

The answer differs by claim, and this article will insist on that difference from its first page to its last. One tendency stands as the strongest confirmation available anywhere in the corpus: the concentration and centralisation of capital, the long movement by which production in sector after sector comes to be dominated by fewer and larger units, documented across the advanced economies by industrial organisation researchers who owe the Marxist tradition nothing and often know little of it. Facing it stands the strongest disconfirmation: the vanishing of the intermediate strata, the shopkeepers, independent producers, small employers, professionals, and salaried layers who were expected to be ground down into two great camps and who instead multiplied, diversified, and became the demographic centre of the very societies where their extinction was forecast. Between those poles lie claims that hold in a weak sense and fail in a strong one, claims whose truth depends entirely on how a contested magnitude is measured, and claims so entangled with their own counteracting factors that generations of specialists have failed to settle them. A reader who finishes this article should be able to grade each claim separately, name the evidence appropriate to each grade, and state what would have to be shown for any grade to change.

Underneath the grading sits a methodological point that decides what the whole exercise can mean, and it must be established before any verdict is announced. Marx analysed a mode of production as an abstraction. His object was not the economy of any single country at any single date, with its particular laws, accidents, inheritances, and improvisations. His object was a set of essential relations: production generalised for exchange, labour power bought and sold as a commodity, and accumulation operating as the system's governing compulsion, the pressure that subordinates every other purpose to the expansion of value. An abstraction of that kind is not a forecast. It does not say what will happen next year, or in which country, or through which crisis. It says what relations must be present for the system to be this system at all, and what pressures those relations generate wherever they hold sway. Testing a forecast means checking dates against events. Testing an abstraction means asking whether the relations it specifies still organise production, and whether the pressures it derives still operate through the noise of particular circumstance. Those are different tests, and confusing them has corrupted both the defence and the prosecution of Marx for well over a century.

The confusion is worth dwelling on because it flatters both sides. The defender who treats the abstraction as a forecast can claim every crisis as a fulfilment and every boom as a postponement, converting a theory into a diary that never records a missed appointment. The critic who treats it as a forecast can point to any decade in which a tendency paused or reversed and declare the whole apparatus dead, as though a claim about pressures were a claim about timetables. Both moves avoid the harder work. The harder work is itemisation. It requires taking the tendencies one at a time, stating each in the strongest form its text supports, asking what evidence would bear on it, consulting the literatures that actually gathered that evidence, and recording a verdict that is allowed to differ from the verdict recorded for its neighbour. That is the work this article performs. Its organising device, the tendency scorecard, is introduced in full below and revisited at the close, and its discipline governs every section in between: no tendency borrows credibility from another, no verdict is stated without the material that would reverse it, and no claim about the present is allowed to depend on a fashion, a headline, or a mood.

One more preliminary protects everything that follows. Comparisons between the present order and the world Marx wrote from are legitimate only when they concern structural relations. Set beside any nineteenth-century analysis, the present wins every comparison about living standards, machinery, medicine, communication, and institutional variety, and wins them trivially, because those comparisons measure the distance travelled by productive forces rather than the accuracy of an account of relations. Whether workers command goods their great-grandparents could not imagine is a fact of the highest importance for many questions, and it settles nothing about whether production is organised through the wage relation, whether accumulation drives investment decisions, or whether capitals concentrate. A nurse, a warehouse operative, and a software engineer inhabit material worlds no Victorian could have pictured. The structural question is whether each sells labour power to an employer, whether the employing organisation must accumulate or perish, and whether the sector each works in is consolidating or fragmenting. On those questions the distance between centuries shrinks dramatically, and it is on those questions, and those alone, that this article permits itself comparisons across time.

The architecture of the assessment can now be stated. The opening sections develop the methodological point at the length it deserves, since a reader who has not absorbed the difference between an abstraction and a forecast will misread every grade that follows. The middle sections work through the scorecard tendency by tendency: concentration and centralisation first, as the strongest confirmation; the worldwide spread of wage dependence second, as a confirmation of a quieter and more consequential kind; recurrent crisis third, upheld in its weak form and unproven in its strong one; immiseration fourth, split into an absolute version that failed and a relative version whose verdict dissolves into measurement disputes; the falling rate of profit fifth, the most technically contested item on the card; and the intermediate strata last among the tendencies, as the clearest failure and the one with the richest consequences for the rest of the theory. A further pair of sections turns from what Marx predicted to what he never saw coming: the scale of the state, the invention of central banking as systemic management, the rise of intellectual property as a distinct property form, the persistence of unwaged reproductive labour, and the ecological constraint on accumulation itself. The article then gives the supersession argument, the claim that the mode of production itself has been replaced, its strongest statement in the mouths of its own proponents, before confronting the deepest objection of all, the charge that a theory armoured in counteracting tendencies has placed itself beyond testing. Only after all of that does the article state what the assessment establishes, and it closes by routing students, teachers, and working researchers toward the companion pieces in this series where each tendency receives its own full treatment, including the companion studies of platform capitalism, financialisation, and automation, linked at the close, for the specific modern forms that a hub article must point toward rather than duplicate.

## The methodological point: an abstraction, not a forecast

Every assessment of Marx against the modern economy smuggles in an answer to a prior question, usually without noticing it has done so. The prior question is what kind of object Capital set out to analyse. If the book is read as a prophecy, a dated sequence of events that history was supposed to perform on schedule, then its assessment is a simple audit of appointments kept and missed, and the audit has been conducted a thousand times with predictable results. Read instead as an anatomy, an account of the relations that constitute a mode of production and of the pressures those relations exert, and assessment changes character entirely. It becomes an enquiry into whether the anatomy still matches the organism, whether the parts named in the dissection are the parts doing the work in the living system, and whether the pressures described on the page are the pressures observable in the clinic. The first reading makes Marx a failed futurologist. The second makes him something harder to dismiss and harder to defend: the author of a structural claim about what kind of system this is.

The textual basis for the second reading is not a charitable invention of later defenders. Marx repeatedly described his procedure as the analysis of a mode of production in its ideal average, a construction that deliberately sets aside the fluctuations, local peculiarities, and transitional hybrids that fill any actual economy at any actual moment. England served him as the chief illustration of the argument, the place where the relations stood most developed and least entangled with survivals of earlier orders, but illustration is not identity. He warned readers in less developed settings that the story told of the classic ground was not a master key to every nation's path, and he resisted, in correspondence late in his life, the conversion of his sketch of Western European development into a universal itinerary that all peoples were fated to march. A theory whose author refuses to universalise its historical narrative is an odd candidate for the charge of having issued a timetable. The charge survives because timetables are easier to grade than anatomies, and grading is what most assessments, friendly and hostile alike, are really attempting.

### What is a tendency in Marx's method?

A tendency is a pressure generated by the essential relations of a mode of production, operating continuously wherever those relations hold, yet always exposed to counteracting influences that can slow, divert, or mask it. Tendencies describe directions of compulsion rather than guaranteed outcomes, which is why their evidence accumulates across long spans rather than single episodes.

That definition deserves unpacking, because nearly every dispute in the assessment literature is a disguised dispute about it. A pressure is not an event. The pressure on a firm to cheapen its production process operates on days when the firm prospers and on days when it fails, in decades when its sector expands and in decades when its sector contracts. It operates because the firm's survival is conditional on costs that rivals are also striving to lower, so standing still is a form of retreat. Nothing in the existence of that pressure guarantees any particular firm's fate, any particular technology's triumph, or any particular decade's statistics. What it guarantees, if the underlying account of competition is right, is a direction of effort distributed across the whole population of capitals, visible in aggregate behaviour over spans long enough for accidents to average away. Tendency claims are therefore statistical in spirit long before they are statistical in presentation. They predict the drift of a system whose individual particles remain free, much as a claim about a gas predicts pressure without predicting the path of any molecule.

Counteracting influences are built into this architecture from the start, and this is the point at which hostile readings most often misdescribe the machinery they attack. When Marx treated the falling tendency of the rate of profit, he devoted a separate and substantial discussion to the factors that check the fall, and he treated the law itself as the resultant of the tendency and its counterweights rather than as a simple trajectory. Whatever one thinks of that particular law, the form of the reasoning is general across his work. A tendency is asserted together with the conditions that would mask it, and the assertion is that the pressure persists underneath the masking, reasserting itself when the counterweight exhausts its scope. Cheaper machinery and materials can offset a rising technical composition for a long while, but the cheapening is itself driven by the same competitive pressure, and its room to run is set by physical and organisational limits that the tendency is not. The architecture is not an escape hatch bolted on after embarrassing evidence arrived. It is the original design, present in the mature manuscripts, and any fair test of a tendency must engage the design rather than a cartoon of it.

None of this places tendency claims beyond judgement, and the sections on the strongest objection will press hard on the question of what could ever count against one. For now, two disciplines suffice. First, a tendency claim is weakened, genuinely weakened, when the periods in which counteracting factors dominate grow so long and so structural that the supposed pressure explains nothing the counterweights do not explain better. A pressure that never surfaces in any observable across any span has retired from science into metaphysics, whatever its defenders say. Second, a tendency claim is strengthened when its operation can be detected through independent evidence gathered for other purposes, by investigators with no stake in the claim, in settings the theory's author never contemplated. The concentration finding celebrated in this article passes the second test in an unusually pure form. The intermediate strata finding fails the first test in an equally pure form. The scorecard exists to register differences of exactly that kind, and a tradition that cannot register them has stopped assessing itself.

### How does an abstraction differ from a model of one economy?

An abstraction isolates the relations that define a system wherever it operates, stripping away national law, inherited institutions, and historical accident. A model of one economy retains those particulars and can therefore describe that economy's path, while the abstraction can only specify pressures every such economy must somehow accommodate, evade, or suppress.

The contrast matters because nearly every concrete disappointment laid at Marx's door belongs to the model category rather than the abstraction category. He did not foresee the welfare state, deposit insurance, fiat money managed by independent central banks, antitrust law, the mass university, or the limited liability corporation reaching its mature scale, and the list could be extended for pages. Each item on it is an institution, a device by which a particular society at a particular stage organised, softened, redirected, or occasionally intensified the pressures of accumulation. An anatomy of a mode of production does not compete with that list and is not refuted by its length. The question the anatomy poses about each device is different: does the device abolish the essential relations, or does it administer them? A welfare state that decommodifies part of a worker's subsistence changes the terms on which labour power is sold, sometimes profoundly. It does not, by that fact alone, end the sale. Deposit insurance changes the mechanics of banking panic. It does not end the subordination of credit creation to the expectation of profit. The assessment that matters runs device by device, relation by relation, and it is slower, duller, and more decisive than the sweeping verdicts it replaces.

There is a reciprocal error, less often named, that defenders commit when they absorb every institution into the abstraction and call the absorption a confirmation. If the welfare state counts as evidence for the theory because it responds to pressures the theory describes, and its absence would also have counted as evidence because naked pressures confirm the theory directly, then the theory has been rendered compatible with every possible institutional history and has thereby explained none of them. The discipline this article follows refuses both errors with the same rule. An institution confirms nothing by existing. It bears on the assessment only through its effect on the specified relations: whether generalised production for exchange widened or narrowed, whether the wage relation extended or retreated, whether accumulation remained the governing compulsion of investment or yielded that office to some other principle. Those are empirical questions with conceivable negative answers, and the verdict defeater attached to this article's central judgement, stated in full in a later section, names precisely the evidence that would overturn it.

A word is needed on levels of abstraction, because Marx worked at several and assessments routinely grade a claim pitched at one level against evidence drawn from another. The most abstract level specifies the value form itself: what it means for products to confront one another as commodities and for labour to be validated socially through exchange. A middle level specifies the dynamics of accumulation: competition, concentration, the reserve of unemployed labour, the credit system, the tendency of profit rates to equalise and to fall. A more concrete level, which Marx projected and never completed, would have treated the state, foreign trade, and the world market in their specificity. Claims at the first level are tested by asking whether the form still organises production at all. Claims at the second are tested against long-run behaviour of the magnitudes they concern. Complaints that the first level fails to predict the second level's details, or that the second level's contestability dissolves the first, confuse the architecture. A building can have a sound foundation and a disputed roof. It can also, and the intermediate strata will show this, have sound foundations and one structural wall built in the wrong place.

Geography belongs to the same discussion. The abstraction was drawn from the most developed capitalist ground available to its author, and its claims are claims about the logic of that ground generalised, not about the sequence any other territory must follow. Territories that entered the capitalist world market did so under conditions Marx did not analyse: colonial extraction, state-led industrialisation, resource rents, integration into supply chains designed elsewhere. Their histories therefore test the abstraction only at the deepest level, the level of whether wage labour and accumulation organise their production, and not at the level of whether they recapitulated a British path. Assessments that grade Marx against the development trajectory of any single late-industrialising country repeat the timetable error in spatial form. The world market was, for Marx, the presupposition and the result of the whole analysis, the arena in which national peculiarities are simultaneously expressed and overridden, and the worldwide extension of wage dependence examined below is the scorecard item where that framing earns its keep most visibly.

Method settled, the standard of success for this article can be stated without decoration. It will not ask whether Marx was right, in the singular, because that question has no answer finer than its own crudity. It will ask, of each tendency, four questions. What exactly was claimed, in the strongest form its texts will bear? At what level of abstraction does the claim operate? What evidence, gathered by whom and for what purpose, bears on it? And what would the world have to look like for the claim to be withdrawn? An assessment that answers those four questions for every row of its scorecard has done something neither the vindication literature nor the refutation literature habitually does. It has made itself corrigible in public, row by row, which is the only kind of authority worth having on a subject where the loudest voices on every side have arranged, in advance, never to be surprised.

The method has a further implication that assessments habitually miss, and it concerns the direction in which an abstraction can be wrong. A forecast errs about the future. An abstraction errs about the present: about which relations are doing the constitutive work in the system it describes. When Marx wrote, rival anatomies were on offer. Political economy in its Ricardian form located the system's essence in distribution among three great revenue classes, landlords, capitalists, and labourers, each defined by a revenue source rather than by a position in production. The vulgar economy he despised located it in exchange itself, in the surface circulation of commodities and money, where every agent appears as a free and equal owner meeting others in the market. His claim against both was that the decisive relations lie in production, hidden by the forms of circulation, and that the revenues and the market freedoms are intelligible only as the distributed results of what happens where labour power meets the conditions of labour under private ownership. Grading the abstraction therefore means grading that act of location. Did he put the essence in the right place? The scorecard's rows are, in the end, so many ways of asking whether production relations, rather than market surfaces or revenue categories, remain where the system's behaviour is decided. Concentration answers yes from the side of capital's internal organisation. The wage relation's planetary career replies in the affirmative from labour's side. The strata row answers that one map drawn from those relations mislocated the population. An abstraction can be right about where the engine sits and wrong about the shape of the vehicle, and most of the confusion in the literature comes from treating those as the same judgement.

There is also a temporal discipline built into abstraction that deserves explicit statement, since it protects the method from its most common abuse by friends. Because the object is a set of relations rather than a dated economy, the abstraction has no expiry date printed on it, and defenders have exploited that absence to treat it as valid for all seasons. The correct inference runs the other way. Precisely because no date is printed, the claim's holders owe the world a standing account of the observations that would show the relations had ceased to hold, renewed as the economy changes its costume. Marx himself modelled the obligation in miniature: his category of the industrial reserve army was tied to a mechanism, the expulsion and reabsorption of labour by accumulation's own rhythm, and he revised its expected behaviour as machinery changed the rhythm. A category whose mechanism is stated can be re-examined when the mechanism's conditions shift. A category repeated as an incantation cannot. The scorecard's insistence on defeaters is thus not an external standard imposed on the tradition by its critics. It is the tradition's own best practice, generalised and made compulsory, and the sections that follow hold every row to it without exception for seniority.

One more distinction completes the methodological equipment, the distinction between the abstraction's core and its penumbra of expectations. Around the specified relations there gathered, in the founder's writings and far more in his followers', a penumbra of hopes, extrapolations, and period judgements: that the system's crises would school its gravediggers, that the middle layers would melt, that the most developed country showed the less developed the image of its future, that the state's class character would stand ever more nakedly revealed. These expectations were believed, preached, and in some cases enacted by movements, and their failure is a historical fact of the first magnitude. But they are not the abstraction, and their failure does not grade it, any more than the abstraction's persistence redeems them. Much of the bitterness in the reception of Marx comes from the penumbra's habit of borrowing the core's authority while the core's defenders borrow the penumbra's pathos. The scorecard cuts the knot by grading the core as one claim, the tendencies as others, and the penumbra's most consequential expectation, polarisation, as a tendency row where it can be marked failed without ceremony. What remains after the cutting is smaller than the monument and larger than the ruin, which is to say it is approximately the size of the truth.

A note on sources belongs to the method, since the abstraction was assembled across a working life and its textual state affects what may fairly be graded. The first volume of Capital is the only one its author prepared for the press, and it went through editions he revised, with a French translation he reworked so substantially that he directed readers to consult it alongside the German. The second and third volumes were constructed after his death by Engels from manuscripts of varying completeness, some of them drafts from the decade before the first volume appeared, so that arguments sit side by side in the published text which their author might, given time, have reconciled or abandoned. Behind the whole stands the vast manuscript of the late eighteen fifties in which the system's categories were first sketched in their interconnected form, unpublished for generations and still startling readers with the range of its anticipations, including the fragment on machinery and knowledge that the automation article in this cluster examines. An assessment that grades claims must therefore grade them with a light editorial conscience: where a tendency is stated in the published first volume and sustained in the drafts, it carries full authority; where it appears only in a posthumous construction, in a passage whose neighbours contradict it, the grade should be recorded with the provenance attached. This article has followed that rule in silence, and states it here so that specialists can check the working. It matters most for the profit rate row, where the law's canonical statement is a posthumous construction from drafts, and least for concentration and the wage relation, whose statements stand in the volume the author himself gave to the world.

The word tendency itself carries a history that the method section should acknowledge, since the founder borrowed it from the sciences of his day and the borrowing shaped what he thought he was claiming. The laws of motion of the celestial mechanics he admired were tendencies in a strict sense: the moon's path is the resultant of attractions that never act alone, and no astronomer apologises for the perturbation theory that computes the resultants. The founder's ambition, stated in his preface with a confidence his century found thrilling and ours finds poignant, was to do for the economic formation of society what the astronomers had done for the heavens: to lay bare the law of its motion. The analogy misled his readers more than it misled him. Celestial tendencies operate in a system whose bodies and forces are few and whose perturbations can be exhaustively catalogued. Social tendencies operate in a system whose counteracting factors include the conscious responses of the system's participants, legislatures that read the theory and legislate against its tendencies, unions that organise against the reserve army's discipline, central banks that study the crisis anatomy and build fire brigades against it. A tendency that can be read by its objects and counteracted on purpose is a different kind of object from a perturbation, and the founder, to his credit, knew it: his political writings are precisely the record of an attempt to become one of the counteracting factors, at maximum scale. The assessment has graded the tendencies as though they were natural pressures, which is the only way to grade them fairly. It notes here, as the method's final subtlety, that several of them failed to dominate partly because the theory's own readers took up arms against them, and that a theory which alters the world it describes by being read has a claim on our attention that no merely accurate theory can match.

## The tendency scorecard: Marx's claims against modern capitalism

With the method settled, the instrument can be introduced. The tendency scorecard is this article's findable artifact, and its design embodies the whole argument of the preceding section. Each row states a claim in the strongest form the texts support. Each row then records what the subsequent development of the system actually shows, drawing on whichever research literature gathered the relevant evidence, whether or not that literature has any connection to Marxism. A third judgement follows: whether the claim, as stated, admits of testing at all, since some of Marx's formulations are precise enough to grade and others are pitched at a level of generality where grading would be theatre. A final column routes the reader to the article in this series where the row's evidence is treated at full length, because a hub article that tried to reproduce every dossier would become a library and persuade nobody. The scorecard's discipline is that its rows cannot trade places. A row graded strongly confirmed lends nothing to its neighbour. A row graded not confirmed takes nothing from its neighbour. The overall assessment, when it comes, is a profile rather than a verdict, and profiles are what honest stocktaking of a large theory always produces.

### What did Marx get right about modern capitalism?

most clearly, the concentration and centralisation of capital, with market concentration having risen across most sectors of the advanced economies, and the global extension of wage labour. The clearest failure is the predicted disappearance of intermediate strata, which expanded instead.

That compressed answer, the one a searcher needs in a hurry, contains the whole shape of the profile: a strongest confirmation, a broad confirmation standing beside it, and a clearest failure named without flinching. The sections that follow expand each row to the length its evidence deserves. Before they do, the scorecard itself should be visible entire, so that a reader can hold the profile in view while examining its parts and can return to it at the end to check that the parts, once expanded, still sum to the shape promised here.

| Tendency | What Marx claimed | What the record shows | Testable as stated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concentration and centralisation of capital | Competition and credit drive production toward fewer, larger capitals in each branch | Leading firms' market shares have grown across the advanced economies' sectors, documented within industrial organisation research | Yes, and strongly confirmed |
| Worldwide spread of wage dependence | Capital draws ever more of the world's population into wage dependence | Wage and salaried work has become the dominant relation across regions once organised by subsistence and petty production | Yes, and confirmed |
| Recurrent crisis | The system's own dynamics generate periodic breakdowns of accumulation | Crises recur across eras and regimes, though no terminal breakdown has arrived | Weak form yes; strong form unconfirmed |
| Absolute immiseration | Accumulation deepens the misery of the labouring population | Living standards of wage earners in the advanced economies rose across the long run | Yes, and not confirmed |
| Relative immiseration | Labour's share and standing decline relative to capital's | Verdict shifts with the measure chosen, the population covered, and the span selected | Only after measurement choices are fixed |
| Falling rate of profit | Rising composition of capital presses the general profit rate downward | Specialists dispute the trend, its measurement, and the direction of its causes | Contested at every level |
| Disappearance of intermediate strata | Middle layers between capital and labour are ground away into the two great camps | Intermediate occupations expanded, diversified, and became demographically central | Yes, and not confirmed |
| Persistence of the essential relations | Generalised commodity production, wage labour, and accumulation define the system | All three relations still organise production across the world economy | Yes, at the level of the abstraction |

Read down the final column and a pattern emerges that neither camp in the relevance wars advertises. The claims stated most precisely are the ones that can be graded, and their grades are mixed in the extreme: the two most testable tendency claims produced the card's strongest confirmation and its strongest disconfirmation. The claims that resist grading are not the peripheral ones. The rate of profit stands near the centre of the system's dynamics in Marx's own architecture, and it is precisely there that measurement disputes, definitional disputes, and causal disputes stack so deep that a century of expert effort has produced a literature rather than a verdict. A scorecard that reported uniform triumph would be lying. A scorecard that reported uniform failure would be lying in the opposite direction. What the table reports is a theory with a powerful core, a mixed record at the level of its named dynamics, and one structural expectation, the simplification of the class structure into two camps, that the development of the system flatly refused to perform.

### Why does the scorecard grade claims separately?

Each tendency rests on its own mechanism, operates at its own level of abstraction, and answers to its own body of evidence. Grading them together lets a strong claim smuggle a weak one past inspection, or lets a failed claim poison a sound one, and both smuggling operations are the standard moves of the polemical literature.

The smuggling metaphor deserves a paragraph of its own, because it identifies the exact mechanism by which public discussion of Marx stays sterile. In the vindication literature, concentration does the heavy lifting. A writer establishes, correctly, that markets have consolidated and that independent researchers documented the consolidation, and then allows the warmth of that established finding to spread across neighbouring claims that were never examined: crisis deepens, immiseration proceeds, the middle dissolves, the end approaches. The reader who came for an assessment leaves with an atmosphere. In the refutation literature the traffic runs the other way. A writer establishes, correctly, that the middle strata did not dissolve and that living standards rose, and then allows the chill of those findings to spread across claims they do not touch, as though the persistence of shopkeepers and salaried managers said anything at all about whether capitals concentrate or whether wage labour extended its dominion over the globe. It does not. The claims are logically independent because their mechanisms are independent, and an assessment worthy of the name holds them apart even when, especially when, holding them apart spoils a good paragraph.

Independence of mechanisms is also why the scorecard refuses to convert its profile into a score. It would be possible, and crowd-pleasing, to announce that Marx achieved some number of hits out of some number of attempts, and the announcement would be worthless, because the rows do not weigh the same. The persistence of the essential relations is not one row among eight in importance. It is the claim on which the entire framework stands, the claim whose failure would dissolve the object the other rows describe. The middle strata's refusal to disappear, conversely, is a claim about the shape the class structure would take, consequential for political expectations built on the theory, damaging to several of its derivative doctrines, yet compatible with the framework's core surviving intact, as indeed it did: the tradition spent generations repairing its class analysis around the stubborn fact of the middle layers, work examined in the article on whether the working class still exists, linked at the close. A profile preserves these differences of weight. A score erases them. The reader is asked to tolerate the profile's refusal of a headline number as the price of an assessment that can actually be used.

One further design choice should be made explicit, since it governs the evidence admitted throughout. The scorecard privileges findings produced outside the Marxist tradition wherever such findings exist. This is not a courtesy and it is not a concession. It is a testing discipline. A tendency confirmed only by the tradition's own journals, measured by the tradition's own categories, against the tradition's own baselines, has passed a weaker test than a tendency that surfaces, uninvited, in the working results of researchers pursuing unrelated questions with unrelated commitments. Concentration surfaces in exactly that uninvited way, in antitrust economics, in the industrial organisation literature's long argument about markups and market power, in business journalism's documentation of consolidation across sector after sector. The intermediate strata's persistence likewise surfaces uninvited, in occupational statistics and stratification sociology compiled by scholars who mostly believed they were describing the refutation of a dead doctrine. Evidence that arrives without being summoned by the theory it bears upon is the strongest evidence social science produces, and the scorecard is built to give it pride of place.

The scorecard's third column, the one recording whether a claim can be tested as stated, is its quietest and may prove its most durable contribution, because it names a failure mode that afflicts social theory far beyond this subject. Claims enter the world in varying states of finish. Some arrive with their measure attached, their population bounded, their direction specified, and the world can grade them the moment the data matures. Others arrive as gestures toward a region of concern, powerful as orientation and useless as assertion, and their authors, or more often their author's disciples, spend generations protecting the gesture from the grading it was never built to survive. The distinction explains a pattern any reader of the assessment literature will recognise: the claims of Marx that attract the most confident verdicts, in both directions, are disproportionately the unfinished ones, because an unfinished claim can be graded by rhetoric, and rhetoric is the one resource neither camp lacks. The finished claims attract less commentary precisely because commentary cannot move their grades. Nobody writes pamphlets about the middle strata anymore. The demographic record closed that file, and pamphleteers migrate to open files, to the profit rate's fog and the crisis claim's missing criterion, where the argument can continue forever because the claim was never completed enough to end it.

Completing a claim is work, and the scorecard's example suggests what the work involves. Take relative immiseration, the card's clearest unfinished row. To finish it, a claimant would have to name the magnitude whose movement constitutes the tendency: the labour share of income, say, or the ratio between the median wage and wealth per head at the summit. The claimant would have to bound the population: wage earners of the advanced economies, or of the world, whose trajectories differ enough to flip a verdict. The claimant would have to fix the treatment of the state's social wage, of supervisory salaries, of the self-employed, of housing. And the claimant would have to state the span across which the movement is to be judged and the size of movement that would count as the tendency failing rather than pausing. None of this is technically difficult. All of it is rhetorically expensive, because each specification creates a surface on which the claim can lose. The tradition's reluctance to pay that price, across a century, is the single most damning exhibit in the unfalsifiability prosecution, and the scorecard enters it into evidence without being asked. A framework's friends prove their friendship by finishing its claims. Its custodians prove the opposite by keeping them permanently, protectively vague.

The reader should also understand what the scorecard is not, since hub instruments invite misunderstanding. It is not a balance sheet of a life, and it awards no marks for the founder's industry, courage, or influence, all of which are immense and none of which bear on whether his claims about capitalism hold. It is not a prediction market: its grades describe the record to date and carry their reversal conditions openly, and several of them, the strata row's defeater above all, describe futures that would astonish nobody more than the graders. It is not a compromise engineered to offend both camps equally, though it will; the grades were fixed by the evidence standards stated in advance, and their distribution, two strong confirmations at the core, two clear failures among the dynamics, and a contested residue, is simply what those standards return when applied without fear or favour to this particular corpus. And it is not the last word. It is a first word of a particular kind: the kind that can be argued with productively, because every one of its judgements announces the evidence on which it rests and the evidence that would overturn it. Assessments that can be argued with are the only ones worth publishing on a subject where argument has so often been a substitute for assessment.

The routing column of the scorecard, the one directing readers to the fuller treatments elsewhere in this series, embodies a judgement about how knowledge of this subject is actually organised, and the judgement is worth surfacing. No single article, and no single book, can carry the evidence for eight distinct claims about a world economy, because the evidence lives in eight different scholarly worlds that publish in different journals, attend different conferences, and cite different ancestors. The hub's task is cartography: to draw the map on which those worlds appear as provinces of one territory, with the roads between them marked. Cartography has its own standards of honesty. A map that exaggerates the province its maker surveyed and shrinks the rest is propaganda with a legend. The scorecard has tried to draw each province at the scale its evidence warrants, including the provinces, and they exist, where the honest entry is that the territory remains disputed among its own surveyors. Readers who follow the roads will find the dossiers: the crisis literature's century of mechanism-building, the profit rate's archive of incompatible series, the stratification debates in which the middle strata were discovered, named, and theorised by scholars who believed they were writing the framework's obituary and were instead writing its most durable sociology. The map is offered in the confidence that the territory rewards the journey, and in the knowledge that every road on it was built by somebody who set out to prove the mapmaker wrong about something.

A word about the grades themselves, and the vocabulary in which they are expressed, will forestall a misunderstanding the scorecard's neatness invites. The grades are not measurements. No instrument exists that reads the confirmation of a tendency off the world the way a thermometer reads a temperature, and the scorecard's categories, confirmed, contested, not confirmed, are judgements rendered under stated rules rather than quantities computed from data. Their value lies not in pretending to a precision the subject forbids but in the discipline of their application: the same rules, the same evidence standards, the same defeater requirement, imposed on the claims the grader admires and the claims the grader regrets in exactly the same order and the same type. Readers who dispute a grade are therefore disputing something definite. They can point to the rule they believe was misapplied, the evidence they believe was underweighted, the defeater they believe was stated too weakly or too strongly, and the dispute can proceed on that ground, which is firmer ground than this subject usually provides. What readers cannot honestly do is dismiss the profile as a matter of opinion while offering, in its place, a verdict that declines to itemise at all. Between a judgement that shows its working and a verdict that shows only its confidence, the assessment knows which deserves the reader's trust, and it has arranged its entire architecture to make the difference visible on every page.

One design decision in the scorecard's construction should be defended explicitly, because hostile readers attack it first and friendly readers misunderstand it longest: the decision to give the essential relations' persistence a row of its own, beneath the tendencies and marked as the foundational claim. The decision is sometimes read as a thumb on the scale, a way of guaranteeing the framework a victory at the bottom of the table whatever happens above it. The reading inverts the design's intent. The foundational row is separated precisely so that its grade cannot contaminate the others and theirs cannot contaminate it: so that the strata row's failure is not softened by the relations' persistence, and the relations' persistence is not credited with the concentration row's evidence. It is also separated because its defeater differs in kind from the others. A tendency's defeater describes a movement in some magnitude. The foundational row's defeater describes a transformation in the organisation of production itself, and keeping the two kinds visibly distinct is the scorecard's way of teaching its readers to notice which kind of claim they are arguing about at any moment, a discrimination whose absence, as the methodology section argued, has wasted more ink on this subject than any other single confusion. The row is the table's foundation in the architectural sense: not a prize awarded in advance, but the level at which the building's survival is decided, inspected separately because everything else is built on it and because its inspection requires different instruments from the floors above.

Before the rows are examined one by one, a sceptical reader is entitled to ask why the grading should begin with method at all, rather than proceeding straight to the evidence like a court hearing witnesses. The reason is that evidence in this field never speaks unaccompanied. Every statistic about market shares arrives inside a definition of a market, every series for the profit rate inside a convention about what capital includes, every occupational table inside a classification somebody designed for a purpose. A court that heard the witnesses without first ruling on what each is competent to testify about would produce a verdict as confused as the quarrel it was meant to settle. The method sections of this article are its admissibility rulings. They decide that long spans outrank episodes, that findings gathered for unrelated purposes outrank findings gathered to prove a point, that a claim must finish stating itself before it may be graded, and that comparisons across the centuries are admissible only when they concern the form of dependence rather than the contents of the shopping basket. Readers who accept those rulings may still dispute individual grades, and the article welcomes the dispute, because it will then be a dispute about evidence. Readers who reject the rulings owe the argument their own, stated with equal explicitness, since a grading conducted under unspoken rules is merely a verdict wearing the costume of a procedure. What nobody is entitled to, on either side of this long argument, is the pretence that their assessment has no method. Every assessment has one. The only choice is whether it is printed where it can be examined.

## Concentration and centralisation: the strongest confirmation

The concentration confirmation stands first on the scorecard because it satisfies every condition a tendency claim can be asked to satisfy, and satisfies them in combination rather than by turns. The claim was stated precisely, early, and at the centre of the analysis rather than at its decorative margins. Its mechanism was specified: competition compels each capital to expand or be absorbed, credit accelerates the absorption, and the result is a secular movement toward production organised in fewer and larger units. The claim was then borne out across a span far longer than its author could have surveyed, in economies he never studied, through institutional arrangements he never imagined, and the bearing out was documented most thoroughly by a research literature that does not cite him, does not share his categories, and in many cases regards his framework as a museum piece. When a tendency passes a test administered by examiners who did not know they were examining it, the grade means something. That is the situation here, and it is why this article names the concentration confirmation as the single strongest confirmation of a specific Marxian tendency available anywhere in the record.

Begin with the claim itself, stated carefully, because later discussion has blurred two processes Marx kept distinct. Accumulation enlarges each surviving capital from within, as realised surplus is ploughed back into additional plant, additional materials, additional labour power. Running alongside that internal growth is a second process that works by redistribution rather than growth: capitals are combined, absorbed, and extinguished as independent centres of decision, so that the total social capital of a branch comes to be commanded by fewer hands even in periods when its aggregate size barely changes. The first process Marx called concentration in the strict sense. The second he called centralisation, and he regarded it as the more powerful lever of the two, because centralisation can accomplish in a merger season what internal growth would need a generation to achieve, and because it is driven not only by efficiency but by the direct struggle of capitals against one another, in which the larger routinely devours the smaller precisely when times are hardest. The distinction matters for assessment because the evidence for the two processes arrives through different channels: concentration shows in the growing scale of the largest productive units, centralisation in the shrinking count of independent firms and the rising share of output, employment, and assets commanded by the leaders of each branch.

### What is the difference between concentration and centralisation?

Concentration names the growth of individual capitals through their own accumulation, each firm expanding as it reinvests its surplus. Centralisation names the redistribution of existing capitals into fewer hands through merger, acquisition, and the ruin of competitors, which can consolidate a whole branch rapidly without any growth in the total capital employed.

Both processes were derived, not merely asserted, and the derivation is worth following because it explains why the tendency survived every institutional weather the system passed through afterward. Competition, in Marx's account, is not a market garnish. It is the coercive law by which the inner compulsions of capital are imposed on each individual capitalist from outside, as the condition of remaining a capitalist at all. The firm that fails to cheapen its commodities loses its market to the firm that succeeds. Cheapening at scale rewards scale: larger capitals can deploy machinery, research, logistics, and purchasing power in combinations closed to smaller ones, and can survive price wars that liquidate their rivals. Credit then multiplies the effect, because the financial system gathers the idle money of the whole society and places it at the disposal of the capitals judged most formidable, which is to say, in general, the largest. Each round of the process raises the minimum size at which a new entrant can hope to compete, so the barrier protecting the consolidated structure is rebuilt by the structure's own operation. A tendency with that self-reinforcing character does not need favourable policy, favourable culture, or favourable luck. It needs only the relations themselves, persisting, and it grinds forward through regimes that celebrate competition and regimes that apologise for it alike.

What does the record show? Described directionally, as this article's figures protocol requires, it shows the leading firms in branch after branch of the advanced economies commanding growing shares of their markets across long spans, with the pattern broad enough to appear in manufacturing, retail, finance, media, agriculture, and services alike, and robust enough to survive changes in how the shares are measured. The industrial organisation literature, the branch of mainstream economics charged with studying market structure, has treated rising concentration as one of the central descriptive facts requiring explanation in its field, and has generated an extended internal argument about its causes and its consequences for prices, wages, investment, and innovation. That argument is genuinely unsettled, and this article takes no side in it. What matters for the scorecard is the layer beneath the argument, the descriptive finding the disputants share: the consolidation happened, across most sectors, over decades, in the economies where the capitalist relations are most developed. Researchers have connected the finding to the rise of firms whose advantages rest on intangible assets and network positions, to the economics of scale in data and distribution, to the weakening of antitrust enforcement as a matter of doctrine, and to the simple cumulative logic of winner-take-most competition. The explanations compete. The phenomenon they compete to explain does not.

### Who documented the concentration trend outside the Marxist tradition?

Industrial organisation economists and antitrust researchers, working from census records, firm accounts, and market data assembled for regulatory and scholarly purposes unconnected to Marxism. Their literature debates causes and consequences, yet its descriptive finding, that leading firms command growing shares across most branches, was produced entirely outside the tradition it happens to confirm.

The provenance of the evidence deserves emphasis because it inverts the usual sociology of this debate. Defenders of Marx citing concentration are easily waved away as believers finding what belief requires. The finding, however, does not depend on their testimony. It sits in the working papers of central bank researchers, in the publications of competition authorities, in business school case literatures, in the anxious commentary of financial journalism watching one merger season succeed another. When economists with no Marxist commitments describe an economy increasingly dominated by a stratum of superstar firms, when regulators debate whether entire branches have passed beyond the reach of competitive discipline, when the standard textbooks revise their market-structure chapters to accommodate consolidation as the default condition rather than the exception, the tendency Marx named is being re-derived from the data by people who would mostly decline the company. There is a particular irony available here, and the article permits itself to note it once: the strongest confirmation of the tendency is almost never cited by either side of the relevance argument, by defenders because it has become too familiar to feel like a discovery, and by critics because it cannot be answered by pointing at the middle class.

Honesty about the row requires its complications to be stated with the same clarity as its headline. Concentration measured at the level of national industries can mask vigorous competition within local markets, and concentration measured locally can mask the national consolidation of ownership behind varied shopfronts. Some branches fragment even as the aggregate consolidates, particularly where digital tools lower the cost of founding a small enterprise, and the long tail of tiny firms in every economy is real. Entry continues, occasionally spectacular, and an entrant can occasionally overturn an incumbent order with a speed that looks like a refutation of the whole tendency until the entrant itself matures into a consolidating giant and begins acquiring its own challengers. None of these complications reverses the grade, because the tendency was never a claim that every branch consolidates monotonically or that no small firm can ever again be born. It was a claim about the direction of the system's centre of gravity, the location of its commanding heights, the distribution of the power to set prices, shape standards, and survive storms. By that standard the complications describe the weather inside a climate they do not alter.

A subtler complication concerns what concentration means for the rest of the framework, and a good assessment should register it rather than pocket the confirmation and move on. Marx expected the centralisation of capital to socialise production in form while leaving its appropriation private, preparing, in his phrase's spirit, the material premises of a differently organised economy. Consolidation has indeed socialised production to a degree he could only sketch: supply networks integrate the labour of continents, platforms coordinate millions of producers and consumers as a single system, and the largest firms plan internally on scales exceeding the planning apparatuses of many states. Whether that development carries the political charge Marx assigned it is a separate question, belonging to the assessment of his expectations about agency and transition rather than to this row. The scorecard records the tendency as confirmed. It does not record the eschatology as confirmed, and readers should notice the difference, because conflating the two is the vindication literature's characteristic sleight.

### What would reverse the concentration judgement?

Sustained deconcentration across the advanced economies' major sectors, persisting across whole business cycles rather than appearing as a brief interruption, with market shares dispersing toward smaller units and entry displacing incumbents as the normal pattern. Episodes of churn within a consolidating structure, or deconcentration confined to one branch, would not meet that standard.

Every row of the scorecard carries its defeater, and this row's is stated here in full as the pattern for the rest. The judgement that concentration is strongly confirmed would be withdrawn if the advanced economies entered a long era in which market shares dispersed as a rule, in which the typical branch fragmented into more and smaller independent capitals, in which incumbency conferred fragility rather than advantage, and in which that pattern held through booms and slumps alike rather than flickering during a single policy experiment. Nothing resembling that pattern appears in the literature this article draws upon. Local fragmentation episodes appear, and they are recorded in the row's discussion rather than suppressed, because a defeater honestly stated must be matched by an honest account of the evidence that approaches it and falls short. The distance between churn and reversal is the distance between weather and climate again. Firms rise and fall inside the consolidated structure with great drama, and the drama is compatible with the structure consolidating throughout, as a forest's composition changes while the forest spreads. It is the spread, not the drama, that the tendency describes, and the spread is what the independent literature keeps finding.

Two further features of the concentration record deserve registration before the row is left, because they protect the grade against the two misunderstandings most likely to be brought against it. The first concerns the unit at which consolidation is measured. Critics of the finding sometimes point to the profusion of small firms in every economy, the millions of enterprises employing a handful of people each, and ask how an economy so populated can be called concentrated. The answer is that the tendency made no claim about the count of firms. It is a claim about the distribution of command: over output, over employment in the decisive branches, over the investment decisions that shape the future, over the standards and infrastructures through which the small firms themselves must trade. A long tail of dependent smallness is compatible with, and frequently an instrument of, concentration at the commanding heights, as the subcontracting chains and franchise systems of the modern economy demonstrate daily: the nominally independent outlet, workshop, or carrier operates inside parameters set by a consolidating centre that captures the margin the arrangement generates. Counting the tail and missing the heights is the statistical version of counting the ocean's fish and missing its tides.

The second feature concerns the tendency's behaviour under hostile policy, which constitutes something close to a natural experiment in its favour. The advanced economies have not merely permitted consolidation. For extended periods they legislated against it, maintaining antitrust regimes whose express purpose was to preserve competitive market structures, breaking up celebrated combinations, blocking mergers, and policing conduct, with an earnestness that varied by era but never vanished entirely. The tendency ground forward through the hostile eras as well as the permissive ones, slower where enforcement bit, faster where doctrine relaxed, but reversing in neither. A trend that persists against the sustained opposition of the most powerful states on earth, equipped with law, courts, and dedicated agencies, is not an artefact of anybody's regulatory capture story, though capture stories explain particular chapters. It behaves exactly as a structural pressure is supposed to behave: deflectable in detail, expensive to resist, and undefeated in direction. The scorecard knows no stronger form of confirmation available to a social tendency, and it is content to let the row rest on the conjunction of the two: documented by indifferent witnesses, and maintained against interested resistance.

Labour economics arrived at the consolidation finding from an unexpected direction, and the route deserves a paragraph because independent convergence from unrelated starting points is the strongest corroboration a structural claim can receive. Researchers studying wage stagnation noticed that workers in highly consolidated local labour markets earned less than comparable workers elsewhere, and that the effect strengthened as employers merged, since a town with one hospital system or one meatpacking plant is a town where labour faces a single buyer. The monopsony literature, as this body of work came to be called, rebuilt from payroll data a proposition that the founder's generation would have recognised instantly: that the wage bargain is struck between parties of radically unequal power, and that the inequality is measurable in the wage itself. A parallel literature on superstar firms, originating in the study of productivity dispersion, documented a widening gulf between a stratum of exceptionally productive, exceptionally profitable leaders and the long tail of laggards behind them, with the leaders' workforces sharing less of the surplus their firms generate than comparable workers did in an earlier dispensation. Neither literature set out to test a nineteenth-century tendency. Both were answering the questions of their own moment, about wage growth and productivity puzzles. That their answers keep describing the same landscape, one in which command concentrates, bargaining power pools at the top, and the gains of the leaders decouple from the fortunes of the typical worker, is the kind of corroboration that cannot be organised by a tradition on its own behalf. The landscape was found, not drawn.

Agriculture offers the consolidation tendency its most quietly dramatic theatre, precisely because the family farm occupies such a warm place in the political imagination of the societies where the tendency has run furthest. Production there has consolidated along every dimension the theory would predict: fewer and larger holdings producing the bulk of output, the processing of food gathered into a handful of firms whose names recur at every stage from seed to shelf, the independent producer surviving increasingly as a contractor inside chains whose terms are dictated from above, supplying a buyer who sets the specification, the timetable, and effectively the price. The farmer who owns land, machinery, and debt, and who sells to one purchaser under standards written by that purchaser, occupies a position the classical categories strain to describe: formally an independent proprietor, functionally a piece-rate worker with capital at risk. Retail tells a cognate story from the other end of the chain. The independent shopkeeper, the very emblem of the old middle class whose extinction was predicted, did largely vanish from the high streets of the advanced world, absorbed not into the proletariat of the prediction but into the employment of the chains that replaced the shops, or into the franchise arrangements that preserve the shopkeeper's silhouette while transferring its substance, its pricing, ranging, and margin, to a consolidating centre. The row's grade was built on such stories in aggregate. They are worth pausing over individually because they show the tendency's characteristic modern signature: not the crude extinction of smallness, but its subordination, the preservation of small units as organs of a consolidated body whose brain sits elsewhere.

Finance deserves a final word within the concentration row, because the credit system that the founding analysis cast as centralisation's great accelerator has grown into something its author sketched and the modern world built at a scale that turns his sketch into an understatement. The gathering of society's idle money into pools of investment capital, managed by a stratum of intermediaries and allocated according to expected return, means that the savings of millions of wage earners, deferred through their pension arrangements, are continuously at work centralising the ownership of the productive apparatus in whose service those same earners labour. The worker's retirement fund holds shares in the worker's employer and in its competitors, and the fund's managers press all of them alike toward the returns that centralisation and cost discipline deliver. Ownership, in this arrangement, has not been socialised in any sense that disturbs the relations; it has been collectivised as a form and privatised as a power, since the beneficiaries of the funds exercise no governance over them and the governance that exists answers to the accumulation imperative in its purest available form, the quarterly demand for return unencumbered by any proprietor's sentiment or dynasty. The founder glimpsed the joint-stock company and called it, in a phrase his editors preserved, the abolition of capital as private property inside the capitalist mode's own confines. The pension-fund economy is that abolition completed and its sting drawn in the same motion: the form of social ownership achieved, the substance of command untouched, and the whole arrangement functioning as centralisation's most powerful engine precisely because it mobilises the savings of the propertyless to consolidate the property they do not control. A tendency that can recruit its apparent opposite into its own service has demonstrated the deepest kind of structural vitality, and the row's grade rests in part on the spectacle.

## The global extension of the wage relation: the quiet confirmation

If concentration is the scorecard's loudest confirmation, the extension of the wage relation is its most consequential one, and the two differ in temper. Concentration announces itself in merger headlines and regulatory quarrels. The spread of wage dependence happens household by household, village by village, generation by generation, as forms of life organised around subsistence production, family farming, artisan independence, and customary obligation give way, one after another, to the sale of labour power as the ordinary means of access to the means of life. Marx expected this. The opening movement of his analysis of primitive accumulation describes the forcible creation of a propertyless class in the classic ground, and the logic of the whole system implies the process repeating wherever capital establishes itself, because capital requires labour power as a commodity in the same way that it requires markets for its output, and it manufactures both requirements wherever it goes. The subsequent history of the world economy is, in large part, the history of that manufacture proceeding on a planetary scale, until the wage relation, once a regional peculiarity of parts of Europe, became the default economic condition of humankind.

The claim needs its terms fixed before its evidence can be weighed. A person stands inside the wage relation when access to subsistence runs through the sale of their capacity to work to an employer who directs its use and appropriates its product. The relation is compatible with great variety in everything else: with contracts long or short, with workplaces vast or tiny, with labour performed in a factory, an office, a hospital, a call centre, a delivery network, or a home connected to all of these by a screen. It is compatible with high wages and low, with security and with radical precarity, with union organisation and with its prohibition. What defines the relation is not any of those variables but the underlying structure: the worker does not own the conditions of production, cannot set them in motion independently, and therefore appears in the market as the seller of the one commodity they possess. Marx's name for the worker's peculiar freedom, free of the means of production and free to sell labour power, was double-edged by design, and the doubleness has only sharpened as the relation has spread, since each new population drawn into it has gained a genuine emancipation from customary bondage and personal dependence in the same movement that delivered it to the impersonal dependence of the market.

### How did wage labour become the dominant relation across the world?

Through the dissolution of subsistence and petty production wherever capitalist markets penetrated, as peasant households, artisan workshops, and communal tenures lost their capacity to reproduce themselves outside commodity relations, and as states reorganised land, taxation, and law around monetary obligations that only wage income or commodity sales could reliably meet, generation after generation.

The mechanisms varied by continent and century, and the variation matters to historians while leaving the tendency untouched. In some regions the driver was enclosure and the legal destruction of common rights. In others it was the tax demand of a colonial or modernising state, payable only in currency, which compelled households to send members into wage employment or cash cropping whether they wished it or not. In others it was the competitive ruin of local craft production by imported manufactures, the same mechanism Marx watched operate on handloom weavers as factory cloth undersold them. In the great industrialisations of the twentieth century's second half, the driver was frequently a developmental state herding rural populations toward factory employment as a deliberate national project. In the most recent transformations, whole agrarian economies of continental scale integrated into global production networks, and their rural populations moved into wage work in numbers that dwarf the entire industrial workforce of Marx's century. A tendency that operates through enclosure in one place, taxation in another, competition in a third, and state planning in a fourth is not four tendencies. It is one pressure wearing local dress, and the pressure is the one the abstraction specifies: production for exchange subordinates and dissolves production for direct use wherever the two compete, because the commodity producer commands the productivity of the socialised labour process and the subsistence producer commands only their own.

Against this confirmation stands a family of arguments that the wage relation has, on the contrary, been weakening, dissolving, or transcending itself from within, and the scorecard must record the row's grade only after those arguments have been heard at their strongest. The first observes the growth of self-employment mediated by digital platforms and asks whether the independent contractor, owning a vehicle or a laptop, selling services to many customers, represents a revival of petty production inside the shell of the new economy. The second observes the expansion of state transfers, pensions, and public provision and asks whether dependence on the wage has been diluted by dependence on the citizen's claim. The third observes the persistence, indeed the vast scale, of informal work across the developing world and asks whether a relation that arrives without contracts, protections, or stable employers deserves the name of the classical wage relation at all. Each argument identifies something real. Each, on examination, describes a variation in the wage relation's costume rather than a retreat of the relation itself, and the distinctions are worth drawing one at a time.

The platform contractor owns an instrument and sells, in substance, labour power by the task, under prices and disciplines set by the owner of the coordinating infrastructure, with the risks of idle time and worn equipment transferred from capital's books to the worker's. Strip the contractual label, which legal systems across many jurisdictions have repeatedly done when the substance was examined, and the relation underneath is wage labour with its guarantees removed and its subordination to an employer's direction exercised by software rather than by a foreman. The growth of such arrangements extends the wage relation into occupations that once sustained genuine independence, taxi driving and small retail among them, which is the opposite direction from the one the transcendence argument requires. The transfer recipient, meanwhile, stands in a relation to the state that presupposes the wage relation as the norm from which transfers deviate and to which they return their recipients: pensions are deferred wages in their contributory form, unemployment provision is calibrated to prior and prospective employment, and the whole fiscal apparatus rests on taxing the incomes the wage relation generates. A system cushioning its central relation has not superseded it. Informality, finally, is the wage relation in its least protected historical dress, the dress it wore in Marx's own descriptions of the classic ground, where contracts were precarious, hours unregulated, and employment casual by default. The informal worker of a modern megacity, selling labour by the day to builders, workshops, households, and traders, is not outside the relation the abstraction describes. That worker is its founding portrait.

### Why does the extension of wage labour matter more than its changing forms?

Because the framework's central claims concern the relation itself: that those without ownership of production's conditions must sell their capacity to work, and that this sale organises distribution, discipline, and the production of surplus. Variations in contract, protection, and workplace alter the relation's terms without touching its existence, which is what the tendency predicted would spread.

There remains the question of the relation's outer boundary, the point at which extension would stop and reversal begin, and the scorecard's discipline requires the boundary to be named. The wage relation would be in genuine retreat if substantial and growing populations were securing their subsistence through forms that bypass the sale of labour power altogether: through a revival of subsistence production capable of sustaining modern populations, through universal endowments of productive property distributed widely enough to live on, through common ownership arrangements that provision members as of right at a scale rivalling the labour market. Elements of each exist somewhere as experiment, inheritance, or enclave. None operates at a scale that registers against the planetary movement in the opposite direction, and the literatures of development economics, labour statistics, and economic sociology, none of them Marxist in allegiance, record that opposite movement as the master fact of the modern world economy: the share of humanity living by wages and salaries has climbed across every long span for which records exist, and the populations still outside the relation are concentrated where integration into global production remains incomplete, which is to say where the process the tendency describes has furthest left to run. The row is graded confirmed, and among all the rows it is the one whose confirmation least depends on contested measurement, since the underlying phenomenon, the reorganisation of whole civilisations around employment, is visible from an aeroplane window in the skylines of factory cities that did not exist within living memory.

One refinement closes the row. Marx expected the relation's spread to simplify the world's class geography as it advanced, drawing each new population into the same two-camp structure he projected for the advanced economies. The extension happened. The simplification did not, and the failure belongs to the intermediate strata row rather than to this one. Keeping the two apart is exactly the discipline the scorecard exists to enforce: the relation conquered the world while declining to arrange its conquerors and its subjects into the tidy formation the theory's political imagination preferred. A framework can chart the ocean correctly while misjudging the fleet that sails upon it, and the assessment that cannot hold both halves of that sentence at once will understand neither.

A further dimension of the wage relation's extension is easily missed because it proceeds inside households rather than across borders, and it deserves recording before the row is closed. The classic wage worker of the theory's founding portrait was a male breadwinner whose wage, however inadequate, was expected to support a dependent family, so that one person's entry into the relation carried a household with it. The mature form of the relation dissolved that arrangement from within: across the advanced economies and then far beyond them, the participation of women in wage employment rose across generations until the dual-earner household became the statistical norm and the single wage became, for most families, an arithmetical impossibility rather than a living standard. The extension thereby doubled itself without a single new territory being opened. Populations already counted as inside the relation's penumbra, maintained by a breadwinner's wage and performing the unwaged reproduction the inventory section examined, were drawn into direct wage dependence in their own persons, with consequences for the reproduction settlement itself that the system is still digesting: the care once supplied unpaid inside the household had increasingly to be purchased as a commodity, supplied by other wage workers in nurseries, kitchens, and care homes, so that the relation extended not only across the population but into the very activities that had constituted its outside. A tendency that colonises its own presuppositions is displaying the deepest kind of vitality the abstraction knows how to describe, and the scorecard notes the pattern here because it recurs: the state socialising the wage, the platform formalising the informal, the care economy commodifying the domestic. Again and again, what looked like the relation's limit turned out to be its next province.

The planetary career of the wage relation has a geography that the scorecard should sketch before leaving the row, because the sequence of its conquests explains the shape of the modern world economy better than any single tendency on the card. The relation was born in a corner of northwestern Europe, matured through the factory districts of the first industrial nation, and spread initially by colonisation's violent transplantations and by the emigration of populations already formed within it. Its second great expansion rode the industrialisation of continental Europe and North America, drawing peasantries into mill towns and mining camps within the span of single lifetimes. Its third, and by far its largest, came with the industrial transformations of East and South Asia and the urbanisation of Latin America and Africa, movements that brought more human beings into wage dependence within a few decades than the previous two centuries combined. At each stage the sceptics of the day announced that the process had reached its natural limit: that this peasantry was too rooted, that culture too communal, that economy too subsistence-bound for the wage to take hold. At each stage the mechanisms catalogued in this section, enclosure, taxation, competition, state direction, and the simple arithmetic of mouths outrunning plots, did their work, and the limit receded. A tendency that has been declared exhausted at every stage of a three-century expansion, and has expanded through every declaration, has earned a certain presumption when the declaration is made again, as it is being made in the post-work literature. Presumption is not proof, and the row's defeater stands. But the burden of the argument has an address, and after three centuries it does not sit with the tendency.

The extension also transformed the relation's interior in ways that feed back into other rows of the card, and tracing one such feedback is worthwhile. A wage-earning population numbering in the billions, distributed across every continent and concentrated in cities of unprecedented size, constitutes a market for wage goods on a scale that reorganised production itself: entire industries exist to provision the daily reproduction of labour power, from food processing to ready-made clothing to the manufacture of the devices through which workers communicate and are entertained. Marx glimpsed the germ of this in his distinction between the departments producing means of production and means of consumption, and in his observation that the worker's consumption, however privately enjoyed, functions for capital as the reproduction of its most indispensable instrument. The mature form exceeds the glimpse. The consumption of the wage-earning billions is now among the principal theatres in which accumulation seeks its realisation, which is why crises of the modern system so often present as crises of demand, and why the state, in its unanticipated role as manager of the whole, treats the wage earner's purchasing power as a variable of statecraft. The relation, having conquered production, proceeded to organise consumption around itself, and a framework that identified the relation as the system's core thereby identified, in advance, the terrain on which the system's characteristic problems would be fought. That is not a small thing for an abstraction to have done, and the scorecard, which has not hesitated over its failures, records it among the successes without discount.

No account of the wage relation's career is complete without its shadow population, the reserve of labour that the founding analysis placed at the mechanism's heart and that the modern economy maintains in forms its author would find at once familiar and uncanny. The classical reserve army was visible: the unemployed at the factory gate, the casual poor of the great cities, the surplus population of a countryside shedding its people faster than industry could absorb them. Its function in the analysis was disciplinary as much as economic. The existence of a crowd prepared to work for less policed the demands of those in work, and the rhythm of accumulation, alternately absorbing and expelling labour as it expanded and contracted, kept the crowd's size conveniently variable. The modern reserve wears different dress. Part of it is statistical, the counted unemployed whose numbers move with the cycle and whose desperation is cushioned, in the richer economies, by the insurance systems the unanticipated state constructed. Part of it is internal to employment itself: the worker on a zero-hours arrangement, the contractor awaiting the algorithm's summons, the part-timer seeking hours that do not come, all formally employed and functionally held in reserve, available at a message's notice and dismissible at no cost. Part of it is global in a sense the founding analysis only sketched: the vast pools of labour in the poorer economies, connected to the richer ones by supply chains and migration pressures, whose existence disciplines wage claims in countries they will never see, because the credible threat of relocation does the work that a crowd at a gate once did. And part of it is generational, the young queuing for entry into stable employment through internships and probationary years that stretch ever longer, a reserve in waiting whose cheapness and gratitude are structural facts of the labour market they enter.

The persistence of the reserve, in these mutated forms, matters to the assessment for a reason beyond completeness. Of all the mechanisms in the founding analysis, this is the one whose operation can be watched, in something close to real time, in the ordinary reporting of labour markets: in the employer's lament, during tight years, that workers have grown demanding, and in the swift correction of that demandingness when the reserve refills. Wage settlements across whole economies rise and stall with the reserve's level in a rhythm so regular that central banks, those unanticipated institutions, now steer by it, raising the price of money expressly to loosen labour markets whose tightness they fear, which is to say expressly to replenish the reserve. A mechanism described in the eighteen sixties, operating as the explicit target of policy instruments invented a century later, is a datum the assessment should weigh heavily. It does not appear as a row on the scorecard, because the founder stated it as a mechanism rather than as a tendency with a direction, but it underwrites several rows that do: it is part of why the wage relation's extension did not dissolve the relation's asymmetry, part of why relative immiseration's measures move as they do, and part of why crises, whatever else they destroy, reliably restore the conditions of profitability by refilling the reserve at a stroke. The anatomy, at this joint, is not merely defensible. It is the working anatomy of the institutions that oppose everything the anatomist hoped for, and they consult it, under other names, every time they set the price of money.

There is a human corollary that the assessment records because the founding analysis insisted on it and because omitting it would falsify the tone of the whole. The reserve is not an abstraction to those inside it. Its disciplinary function is performed through individual experiences of refusal, waiting, and replaceability, and its modern forms have refined the experience rather than abolishing it: the application unanswered, the shift withdrawn, the rating that falls without explanation, the contract that ends by silence. The founder's chapters on the working day and the reserve are filled with the testimony of inspectors, physicians, and the workers themselves, quoted at a length his modern summarists rarely match, because he understood that a mechanism which cannot be recognised in the experience it produces has been described at the wrong level. The scorecard method, with its grades and defeaters, risks exactly that wrongness, and this article accepts the risk consciously, as the price of an instrument that can be argued with. But it should be said once, in the assessment's own voice, that every row of the table corresponds to a way that millions of lives are actually organised and actually felt, and that the tendency language, however necessary, is a lens held up to that organisation and not a replacement for looking at it. The anatomist never forgot the body. His assessors should not either.

Migration is the wage relation's human circulatory system, and its modern scale adds a chapter to the row that the founding analysis, written in an age of mass emigration it certainly witnessed, still did not theorise in its contemporary form. Labour now moves across borders in flows that states alternately solicit and police, and the migrant worker occupies a distinctive position inside the relation: selling labour power in a labour market whose full rights, protections, and political voice are rationed by citizenship, so that the relation appears in a graded form, its terms worsening as the seller's papers weaken. The grading is not an accident of prejudice, though prejudice oils it. It is functional to the receiving economies in ways the founding categories describe with uncomfortable ease: a workforce segment whose deportability disciplines its demands is a reserve army with a legal address, and the periodic political campaigns against its presence coexist, in every receiving country, with the quiet dependence of whole industries, agriculture, construction, care, and hospitality at their head, on its continued arrival. The sending economies, meanwhile, are reorganised around the relation's export: remittances from emigrant wages rival or exceed the export earnings of goods, households plan their reproduction around the absent member's salary, and states cultivate the outflow as a development strategy. A relation that organises the economies of both the countries workers leave and the countries they enter, that structures the politics of citizenship in the one and the household economy of the other, has extended itself along a dimension, the international division of labour in the flesh, that the scorecard's row, graded on the relation's extent, is entitled to count among its strongest supporting evidence. The founder wrote movingly of the Irish in England and the reserve their misery created. The modern system has generalised his example to the planet, and the generalisation, like so much else in this assessment, confirms the anatomy while transforming every political question the anatomy was built to answer.

## Recurrent crisis: confirmed in the weak sense, unproven in the strong one

No tendency on the scorecard has been declared confirmed more often, by more jubilant voices, on the basis of less discrimination, than the crisis tendency. Every breakdown of accumulation, every banking panic, every collapse of output and employment, arrives accompanied by announcements that Marx has been vindicated, and every recovery arrives accompanied by announcements that the vindication has expired. Both announcements mistake the claim. Marx's crisis theory, which receives its own full treatment in the [marxist crisis theory guide](/2013/10/01/marxist-crisis-theory-guide/) linked from this article's close, is not one claim but a family of them, pitched at different strengths, and the scorecard grades them separately because their records differ. In its weak form, the claim is that crises are endogenous to the capitalist mode of production: not visitations from outside, not mere policy errors, not accidents of confidence, but recurrent products of the system's own dynamics, arising from the disproportions, credit expansions, profitability pressures, and realisation difficulties that accumulation itself generates. In its strong form, the claim adds a trajectory: crises deepen across the system's history, its recoveries grow feebler, and the sequence tends toward a breakdown from which the system's own resources cannot rescue it. The weak form has been confirmed so thoroughly that its content has been absorbed into the common sense of economics itself. The strong form remains unconfirmed after every opportunity the record has offered it.

Consider first how complete the weak form's victory has been, and how quietly it was conceded. The economics Marx wrote against treated crises as disturbances of a natural equilibrium, exogenous shocks or monetary mismanagement interrupting a harmony that would otherwise persist. The economics that developed across the following century moved, by its own internal route and largely without reference to him, to positions that grant the essential point. Business cycle research institutionalised the cycle as a permanent feature to be modelled rather than an aberration to be explained away. The study of financial instability produced accounts, from within the mainstream and its respectable margins, in which the expansion of credit during tranquil periods manufactures the fragility that the subsequent panic merely reveals, a mechanism whose family resemblance to Marx's treatment of credit as the great accelerator and great solvent of accumulation is plain to any reader of both. Macroeconomic management, the entire apparatus of countercyclical policy, exists because every state on earth now assumes as a premise what was once the Marxist provocation: left to itself, this system periodically convulses, and the convulsions are generated inside the machine rather than inflicted on it from outside. When a doctrine's central provocation becomes the operating assumption of its opponents' finance ministries, the doctrine's weak form has won, and the scorecard records the win without requiring anyone to use the winner's vocabulary.

### What distinguishes a recurrent crisis from a terminal one?

A recurrent crisis interrupts accumulation, destroys capital values, disciplines costs, and then gives way to a resumed expansion on a restructured basis, functioning as the system's violent method of restoring its own conditions. A terminal crisis would be one in which those restorative mechanisms themselves failed, so that no restructuring available within the relations could restart accumulation at all.

The distinction is the hinge of the whole row, because Marx's own texts supply material for both readings and his followers have never settled which carries his authority. The restorative reading has the better of the analysis: crisis, in the mature work, is the forcible reconciliation of contradictions that peaceful accumulation allows to widen, the moment when overproduced commodities, overextended credit, and overbuilt capacity are written down together so that production can resume on a corrected basis. On that reading, crisis is not the system's death agony but its brutal method of self-repair, and its recurrence confirms the analysis precisely by functioning as the analysis describes. The terminal reading draws on the rhetoric of the system's historical limits, on passages where the centralisation of capital and the socialisation of labour are said to reach a point of incompatibility with their integument. Rhetoric aside, no mechanism is specified anywhere in the corpus by which a crisis, as such, becomes final: no threshold of depth, no exhaustion of counterweights, no criterion by which an observer could distinguish the last crisis from the penultimate one while it was happening. A claim without a criterion cannot be graded as confirmed by any number of severe episodes, and the record, which contains episodes of genuine severity followed in every case by restructured expansion, grades it accordingly: unproven, with the burden unmet.

Severity deserves its own paragraph, because the strong form's defenders often argue from it directly, as though a sufficiently deep crisis were a down payment on a terminal one. Depth measures how much capital value a crisis destroys and how much suffering it distributes. It does not measure proximity to a systemic limit, and the two have repeatedly come apart in the record: the deepest convulsions were followed by the most vigorous restructurings, because destruction on that scale clears the ground for accumulation with unusual thoroughness, cheapening assets, labour, and credit simultaneously. A theory of terminal breakdown would need to show the restorative mechanisms weakening across successive episodes: recoveries shortening, the scope for devaluation narrowing, the state's rescue capacity shrinking toward impotence. The state capacity question is real and receives treatment in the section on what Marx did not anticipate, since the growth of that capacity is itself one of the great unanticipated facts. For this row the grade stands as the scorecard prints it. Crises recur, endogenously, as the weak claim requires. Nothing in their recurrence, so far, certifies the strong claim's trajectory, and each recovery that the strong claim's partisans explain as a postponement purchased at rising cost moves the claim closer to the unfalsifiable condition examined in the objection section below.

### Why do crises not settle the argument by themselves?

Because every party to the argument predicted them in some form once the cycle became undeniable, and a phenomenon all frameworks accommodate discriminates among none of them. What discriminates is the mechanism proposed: whether a given crisis arose from the dynamics a framework specifies, in the sequence it specifies, rather than from causes its rivals name.

That point generalises beyond this row and is worth carrying through the rest of the assessment. Confirmation is cheap when the confirming event is one that every surviving theory expects. The sun rises for Ptolemy and Copernicus alike. What earns a framework credit is the prior specification of mechanism and sequence: this pressure, building through that channel, discharged in this form. Marx's crisis writings are at their strongest exactly there, in the anatomy of how credit stretches the limits of realisation until the stretching itself becomes the fragility, how production outruns the consumption its own wage bill can validate, how the scramble for liquidity at the panic's height reveals that the whole glittering structure of claims rested on expected future surplus. They are at their weakest in the inherited revolutionary expectation that the anatomy implied a prognosis. The scorecard, faithful to its method, credits the anatomy and withholds the prognosis, and readers who find that division unsatisfying are invited to notice that it is the same division, applied impartially, that gave concentration its triumph and the intermediate strata their refutation. The instrument does not know whose row it is grading. That is what makes it an instrument.

One refinement of the crisis row remains, concerning the changing form of crises across the system's development, because the form's migration is itself evidence about the mechanism. The classic crisis of the founding descriptions announced itself in the market for goods: warehouses glutted, orders cancelled, mills stopped, and the financial panic followed the commercial one as its consequence and amplifier. Across the system's maturity the sequence progressively inverted. Crises increasingly originate inside the financial system itself, in the collapse of asset values, the seizure of credit markets, the discovery that chains of claims built upon expected future income cannot all be honoured at once, and the real economy's contraction follows the financial event rather than preceding it. The migration is sometimes cited as a departure from anything Marx described, and it is better read as the working out of his own analysis of credit's development. The third volume's treatment of the credit system describes, in the institutional vocabulary available to it, a machinery by which claims on future surplus multiply far beyond the surplus any present can validate, in which fictitious capital, paper claims traded as though they were wealth itself, acquires a life, a market, and a crash schedule of its own. The financialisation literature treated in the companion article linked from this hub documents the modern scale of that machinery. What the scorecard records here is the continuity of mechanism under transformation of costume: overaccumulation seeking outlets, credit stretching realisation beyond its warrant, and the eventual forcible reconciliation of claims with the surplus available to meet them. The costume department has been busy for a century and a half. The play, in its weak and confirmed form, is recognisably the one in the text.

The crisis row invites one more examination, of the uses to which its weak confirmation has been put by the system's own managers, because the spectacle of a doctrine absorbed by its object is among the strangest in the whole assessment. The modern state's countercyclical apparatus, its automatic stabilisers, its deposit guarantees, its lender-of-last-resort commitments, its fiscal interventions scaled to the size of the collapse it faces, amounts to an institutional confession that the economy's convulsions are endogenous, periodic, and dangerous enough to require a permanent fire brigade. That confession was not extracted from the state by Marxist parties. It was learned by finance ministries and central banks from their own ledgers, crisis by crisis, in the hardest school there is. Yet the doctrine the confession embodies, that the fire is a product of the building's design rather than of arsonists and accidents, is the doctrine whose assertion once marked its holder as a dangerous subversive of the economic order. The assessment should pause over the oddity. A framework may lose every political battle of a century and win its central descriptive claim so completely that the winners inscribe the claim on their own institutions, in a vocabulary carefully laundered of its origins. Victory of that kind does not appear in the vindication literature's trophy cabinet, because it cannot be celebrated without acknowledging how thoroughly the framework's other expectations, about what the confession would lead to, were disappointed. The fire brigade was built. The building did not burn down. Its owners learned to govern it as a structure that periodically catches fire, and the learning is simultaneously the weak crisis claim's confirmation and the strong claim's most elegant refutation, since a system that has institutionalised its own contradictions has demonstrated a capacity for self-repair that the terminal reading never budgeted for.

There is a corollary for the grading of future crises, and the scorecard states it now so that its own future conduct is on record. When the next severe breakdown arrives, as the weak claim assures us one will, it will confirm nothing that is not already confirmed, and it should move no grade on this card unless it exhibits the specific signature the strong claim requires: a failure of the restorative mechanisms themselves, a rescue apparatus that reaches its limits and finds the devaluation it exists to manage proceeding beyond its control, a restructuring that does not come. Severity alone will not suffice, nor will the suffering the episode distributes, nor will the chorus of announcements, from every point of the compass, that the end or the vindication is at hand. The scorecard has printed its criteria in advance. It intends to be graded by them.

Within the Marxist tradition itself, the crisis row conceals a civil war that the assessment should acknowledge, because the war's battle lines explain why the claim's strong form never acquired the criterion whose absence the scorecard records against it. The contending schools agree that crises are endogenous and disagree about nearly everything else. The underconsumptionist line, oldest of the family, locates the breakdown in the restricted consumption of the masses: production expands by the logic of accumulation while the wage bill that must validate it expands by the narrower logic of the wage bargain, until the gap discharges in glut. The disproportionality line locates it in anarchy: departments of production expand without a common plan, proportions among them drift beyond what exchange can reconcile, and the crisis is the forcible restoration of a balance nobody was charged with keeping. The profit squeeze line locates it in success: long booms tighten labour markets, strengthen workers' bargaining hand, and compress margins until investment falters. The falling profitability line locates it in composition, the rising weight of dead labour against living, working through the contested row examined elsewhere in this article. Each school can narrate the great crises in its own vocabulary, and each narration has passages of genuine illumination, because a complex breakdown exhibits every mechanism at once and a determined theorist can always find their favourite in the wreckage. What none of the schools produced, across a century of trying, is a discriminating test: an observation, specified in advance, on which the schools' accounts diverge and by which at least one could lose. The war therefore continues by other means, in journals and conferences, its fronts unmoved by the crises it exists to explain. The scorecard's treatment of the row, confirming the weak form that all schools share and withholding judgement among the mechanisms they contest, is not indecision. It is the accurate representation of a literature whose shared core has been absorbed into general knowledge while its contested superstructure remains, after all this time, exactly that.

The long wave debate, a quieter cousin of the crisis literature, deserves the assessment's notice because it represents the most ambitious attempt ever made, from within the tradition, to give the crisis claim the temporal structure its strong form needs. The Belgian economist Ernest Mandel, the tradition's most systematic twentieth-century builder, organised the system's postwar history into long waves of expansion and contraction, each driven by the profitability conditions the profit rate row examines, with the turns between waves explained by the exhaustion of one technological and institutional settlement and the violent installation of the next. The architecture is impressive, and its account of the postwar boom's long fade into the slower decades that followed retains genuine explanatory power. Its weakness is the family's weakness: the waves are far easier to identify in retrospect than in prospect, their turning points announced with confidence only after the turn, and their lengths variable enough to accommodate almost any periodisation a determined analyst prefers. A rhythm that can be found in the data at whatever tempo the analyst requires is a rhythm the data cannot confirm, and the scorecard, which graded the crisis row on exactly this distinction, files the long waves accordingly: as the strong form's most sophisticated costume, and as evidence, in their very sophistication, of how much ingenuity the tradition has spent supplying a criterion that the claim itself never contained. The ingenuity is honoured here. The grade does not move.

## Immiseration: the claim that split in two

Immiseration is the scorecard's most instructive failure, because the claim arrives in two versions whose fates diverged completely, and because generations of argument have consisted of each side quietly selecting the version that suits it. The absolute version holds that accumulation, as it proceeds, deepens the material misery of the labouring population: wages pressed toward subsistence, conditions deteriorating, the worker's share of a growing social product shrinking in real terms until life itself is meaner at the system's maturity than at its dawn. The relative version holds something subtler: that the worker's position declines in comparison with the wealth accumulating opposite, that the gap between the labourer's consumption and the social product per head widens even as the labourer's consumption rises, so that poverty becomes a relation of distance rather than of deprivation. Marx's texts contain material for both readings, deployed at different moments for different purposes, and the tradition that inherited them rarely bothered to say which claim it was defending when the evidence turned hostile. The scorecard says it for them. The absolute version, applied to the advanced economies across the long run, is not confirmed. The relative version is contested in a way that dissolves, on inspection, into disputes about measurement that the claim's own vagueness invited.

The absolute version's failure is among the plainest facts the assessment has to record, and this article records it without the cushioning that sympathetic accounts usually supply. Across the long development of the advanced capitalist economies, the real consumption of wage earners rose, not marginally but transformatively: in nutrition, in housing space and sanitation, in clothing, in medical survival, in the length of life itself and the portion of it surrendered to labour. Working hours fell across the same span by margins that would have seemed utopian to the operatives whose fourteen-hour days fill the factory chapters of Capital. Child labour, the normal condition of working-class childhood in the classic ground, was abolished in law and largely in fact across the advanced world. None of this was the gift of accumulation's spontaneous benevolence, and the scorecard does not grade it as such: the improvements were fought for by organised labour, legislated by states under pressure, and conceded by capitals that discovered healthier, better-schooled, longer-lived workers to be more productive instruments. Causation, however, is a separate row's business. For this row the question is whether the tendency, as stated, materialised, and it did not. A population whose material life improved across every dimension its supposed immiserator measured has not been immiserated in the absolute sense, and the tradition's attempts to rescue the claim by relocating it, to the colonial periphery, to particular decades, to the texture of experience rather than the contents of the basket, are relocations to different claims, each of which must then be graded on its own evidence rather than inherited credit.

Those relocated claims deserve a fair hearing rather than a dismissal, because two of them identify genuine phenomena that a complete assessment should register somewhere. The first holds that immiseration was exported: that the advanced economies' rising working-class consumption rested partly on super-exploited labour elsewhere, in the plantations, mines, and later the export factories of the subordinated world, so that the tendency operated globally while being masked locally. The global version is a different claim from the one on the card, which concerned the labouring population of the capitalist economies as such, and grading it would require the world-system apparatus treated elsewhere in this series. It is enough here to note that the global claim, in its turn, faces the evidence of rising real wages across the great industrialising economies of the periphery itself as their integration deepened, which complicates any simple geography of misery. The second relocation holds that immiseration is experiential: that insecurity, intensity, surveillance, and the subordination of life to the employer's clock constitute a misery that rising consumption conceals rather than compensates. That claim may well be true, and it belongs to the analysis of alienation, where this series treats it at length. It is not a claim about material immiseration, and presenting it as the tendency's vindication trades on the word while abandoning the thesis. Precision about which claim failed is not pedantry. It is the precondition of learning anything from the failure.

### What is the difference between absolute and relative immiseration?

Absolute immiseration means the labouring population's material condition deteriorates in its own terms: real consumption falling, hours lengthening, life itself growing meaner as accumulation proceeds. Relative immiseration means the labourer's condition improves in its own terms while declining against the wealth of the class opposite, so that the distance between the two grows even as the floor rises.

The relative version survives the evidence that killed its sibling, and its survival is genuine as far as it goes. Across long spans in the advanced economies, the share of the social product accruing to labour has indeed declined in many periods and by many measures, the evidence assembled in the [labour share evidence](/2021/03/01/labour-share-evidence/) article in this series, while the concentration of wealth at the summit of the distribution has proceeded to degrees that make the relative claim feel, to many readers, like the plainest description of their world. A worker whose real wage doubles while the wealth of the owning class multiplies beyond the reach of wages altogether has been relatively immiserated in the only sense the term can bear, and the social meaning of that distance, in political influence, in security against catastrophe, in the capacity to wait out a dispute or refuse a degrading order, is not a statistical illusion. Where the relative claim falters is not in its direction but in its precision. Marx never fixed the measure by which relative decline was to be judged: the wage share of national income, the ratio of wages to profits, the worker's consumption against the social average, the wealth distribution's upper tail. Each measure tells a somewhat different story across any given span, some reversing sign across decades that others show as continuous decline, and the claim's defenders have historically selected among them with a freedom that converts a testable assertion into a mood. The scorecard therefore grades the row contested and measure-dependent, which is not a shrug. It is a finding about the claim itself: a tendency stated without its metric has not finished being stated, and the unfinished statement is the author's responsibility, not the evidence's.

### Why is relative immiseration so difficult to measure?

Because every candidate measure embeds contestable choices: whether to count supervisory salaries as labour's share, how to treat the self-employed, whether housing services and state provision enter the worker's consumption, and which span of years defines the trend. Reasonable choices diverge, and the verdict follows the choices more obediently than it follows the world.

There is a lesson in this row that the assessment will draw again at the profit rate, and it concerns the difference between a tendency and a slogan. In the polemical tradition, immiseration functioned as a slogan of extraordinary power, compressing the moral charge of the whole critique into a single word that promised the system's victims would multiply and deepen until the structure became intolerable. Slogans are not required to specify their measures. Tendencies are. The historical misfortune of this claim is that it lived its public life as the first while being graded, eventually, as the second, and the gap between the two lives accounts for much of the confusion in the secondary literature, where the absolute version's failure is cited against the relative version's evidence and the relative version's evidence is cited in defence of the absolute version's memory. The scorecard's separation of the two is the entire remedy, and it is available to any reader willing to ask, whenever the word appears, the unfashionable question: in which sense, by which measure, across which span? A claim that can answer those questions can be graded. A claim that resents them has already told the examiner what it is.

The immiseration row has one more subtlety to surrender, and it turns on the direction in which the claim's surviving half points the rest of the framework. Relative immiseration, stripped of its slogan form and fixed to its most defensible measure, the labour share and the distribution of wealth, describes a system in which the worker's absolute condition can improve indefinitely while the worker's relative position, and with it the balance of social power, deteriorates. That combination, rising floors and widening distances, turns out to describe the advanced economies' actual trajectory more faithfully than either the immiseration slogan or its complacent negation, and it carries an implication the tradition was slow to absorb: the system's stability does not require the worker's misery, and may indeed be purchased by the worker's improving consumption, provided the improvement never closes the distance that makes the worker's position structurally subordinate. A critique that staked its expectations on deepening poverty was disarmed by rising living standards. A critique that stakes its analysis on the relation itself, on the persistence of a class that must sell its capacity to work to a class that owns the conditions of work, is untouched by the same evidence, because the evidence concerns the terms of the relation rather than its existence. The migration of the immiseration claim from its absolute to its relative form, properly understood, is therefore not merely a retreat under fire. It is the framework correcting its own centre of gravity, from a prediction about quantities of goods to an analysis of relations of power, and the correction leaves the analysis stronger at exactly the point where the scorecard's central judgement operates. Failures that teach a framework where its weight should rest are the most valuable failures a research programme can have, and this row, like the strata row after it, is recorded in that spirit: as a defeat that improved the army it defeated.

The social wage complicates the immiseration row in a direction the main section only touched, and the complication cuts against both camps' habits, which is usually a sign that it deserves the space. When the state taxes the wage and returns a portion of it as services, schooling for the worker's children, medicine for the worker's family, insurance against the wage's interruptions, pensions against its cessation, the worker's effective consumption rises by a route that bypasses the wage bargain entirely. Defenders of the absolute immiseration claim, in its day, dismissed such provision as a deduction from the worker's own product, which it is, as though the route of a benefit determined its reality; a hospital bed funded by taxation heals exactly as well as one funded by an employer. Critics of the claim, meanwhile, folded the social wage into the rising living standards they cited, rarely pausing to note that the improvement they celebrated was substantially the work of collective provision extracted through politics, not of the wage relation's spontaneous generosity, and that its existence therefore testified to the organised pressure of the very class whose condition was under discussion. The scorecard's grade stands above the quarrel: absolute immiseration failed, and the social wage is among the largest reasons it failed, and the reason's own history, a century of labour movements demanding decommodification and states conceding it under pressure, belongs to the biography of the system rather than to the anatomy of its tendencies. But the episode teaches a methodological lesson the assessment wants on record. Magnitudes in this field are not found in nature. The wage is a magnitude with a social shadow, the total provision a society routes to its workers through every channel, and any tendency claim about the worker's condition that does not state whether it is measuring the wage, the shadow, or the two together has left its most important term undefined. The unfinished-claim problem, again: it is the same defect wearing a different row's clothing, and recognising the family resemblance is most of what this article exists to teach.

The geography of the immiseration verdict deserves one refinement that the main section's candour should not be allowed to blur. The absolute claim failed, across the long run and in the advanced economies, on the evidence of consumption, hours, and survival. It does not follow that absolute immiseration describes nothing anywhere in the system's history. The founding chapters' own evidence was drawn from a real immiseration, the catastrophic deterioration of the first industrial generations' conditions, which historians have documented in falling stature, rising mortality, and the destruction of the handworkers' world. Pockets of the contemporary world economy, in the extractive frontiers and the unregulated industrial zones where the system's disciplines arrive without its accumulated counterweights, reproduce conditions the nineteenth century would recognise. The claim's failure is therefore specific in scope: as a tendency of the system's development in its mature heartlands, where the counterweights of organisation, legislation, and the social wage had generations to accumulate, the predicted deepening of misery did not occur and its opposite did. A tendency that operates only where its counterweights are absent is a conditional statement about power, not a law of accumulation, and restating it in that conditional form is the most that honest scholarship can recover from the row. The recovery is not nothing. It converts a refuted law into a researchable proposition about the conditions under which workers' absolute conditions deteriorate, and the proposition, unlike the law, has the merit of being true in the places and periods where its conditions hold. The scorecard leaves the row graded as it stands, since it grades the claim as stated, and records the recovery here as the row's bequest to the research frontier.

## The falling rate of profit: the most contested row on the card

Every scorecard has a row where the grading instrument itself begins to smoke, and on this card that row is the profit rate's falling tendency. The claim stands near the centre of Marx's dynamics. Accumulation, driven by competition, raises the mass of machinery, plant, and materials set in motion by each worker faster than it raises the living labour employed, and since living labour is, on the theory, the sole source of new value and therefore of profit, the ratio of profit to the total capital advanced comes under secular downward pressure, checked but not abolished by counteracting influences: the cheapening of the elements of constant capital, the depression of wages below their value, the expansion of foreign trade, the swelling of the reserve army, the increase in the rate of surplus value itself. Marx called this the most important law of political economy, and the tradition has treated its defence as a test of orthodoxy. The scorecard treats it as a test of something else: of whether a claim can survive contact with a century of hostile, friendly, and indifferent measurement. Its verdict is the most heavily qualified on the card. The tendency is contested empirically at every level at which contest is possible: in the trend's existence, in its measurement, in its causes, and in its theoretical coherence.

Begin with coherence, because the deepest challenge to the row is not statistical but logical, and it arrived from within the broad family of economists who took the labour theory of value seriously enough to examine its machinery. The challenge, associated with a line of criticism running through twentieth-century debates and sharpened by Japanese and European scholars working on the transformation of values into prices, observes that profit-maximising firms adopt only those techniques that reduce their costs at current prices, and that such adoption, generalised, tends to raise rather than lower the equilibrium rate of profit unless real wages rise. If that reasoning holds, the tendency as Marx derived it contains an internal difficulty: the microeconomic behaviour he himself ascribed to capitals does not aggregate into the macroeconomic result he announced, at least not without additional assumptions doing heavy and unacknowledged work. Defenders have replied on several fronts, disputing the equilibrium framing, the treatment of fixed capital, the definition of the value of labour power under technical change, and the temporal versus simultaneous valuation of inputs and outputs, and the replies have generated one of the most technically sophisticated literatures in the whole Marxist corpus. The scorecard does not adjudicate that literature, which fills volumes this hub article can only route toward. It records the state of play accurately: the law's derivation is disputed by competent readers of the same texts and the same logic, and a tendency whose derivation is in that condition cannot be graded confirmed whatever the statistics show, because the statistics would not know what they were confirming.

The statistics, in any case, do not speak with one voice, and the reasons they do not are themselves instructive about the row's difficulty. The rate of profit is not a magnitude any statistical office collects. It must be constructed, and every construction embeds decisions that the theory leaves underdetermined and that drive the result. The capital stock can be valued at historical cost or at replacement cost, and the two series diverge dramatically across eras of inflation and technical change. The profit numerator can be drawn before or after tax, before or after interest, including or excluding the financial sector, including or excluding the incomes of the self-employed and the rents of land and housing. The denominator can include or exclude dwellings, public capital, consumer durables, and the intangible assets whose growing weight in modern production the section on unanticipated developments will examine. Competent constructors, choosing reasonably at every fork, have produced series in which the profit rate falls across the long run, series in which it rises, series in which it oscillates without trend, and series in which it does all three in succession across different sub-periods. The literature's honest summary is that the trend of the profit rate is not a fact awaiting a theory but a family of artefacts awaiting a convention, and no convention has commanded the field. A tendency that can be confirmed or refuted by the choice of a depreciation schedule has not yet met the standard the concentration row met, where the finding forced itself on investigators who were not looking for it.

### Why is the falling rate of profit so difficult to test?

Because the magnitude itself must be constructed before it can be measured, and every construction choice, the valuation of the capital stock, the boundaries of profit, the treatment of finance, housing, and intangibles, is contested and outcome-determining. Beneath the measurement dispute sits a theoretical one about whether cost-reducing innovation, generalised, lowers the rate at all.

What, then, can fairly be said in the row's favour? Two things, and they are not nothing. The first is that the counteracting influences Marx catalogued have all been observed operating at scale, which testifies to the quality of the underlying anatomy even where the headline law falters: the cheapening of constant capital through the productivity of the machine-producing sectors is a documented engine of modern growth, the global expansion of labour supply has repeatedly restored profitability by widening the field of exploitation, and crises have repeatedly performed the devaluation of capital that the theory assigns them. A framework whose auxiliary mechanisms keep being found in the world has earned a hearing for its central claim even when the claim itself resists verification. The second is that long waves of profitability, eras in which returns sag across whole decades and revive after restructurings, are a recognised feature of the record in literatures far from Marxism, and the rhythm of those waves is at least consonant with a dynamics in which accumulation undermines its own returns and then violently restores them. Consonance, however, is the weakest currency the scorecard accepts, and it is recorded here as consonance and nothing more. The row's grade is contested, and the contest is the finding: of all the tendencies, this is the one where the tradition's confidence most exceeds its evidence, and where an honest assessment does the tradition the service of saying so in print.

The row also teaches, in passing, the sharpest version of a distinction the whole article depends on. A claim can be theoretically central and empirically undecidable at the same time, and the conjunction is not a paradox but a diagnosis: it marks the place where a framework's ambitions outran the measurability of the world it describes. Mature sciences are full of such places and learn to label them honestly. The scorecard's label for this row, contested at every level, is offered in that spirit, as a more useful contribution than either the triumphalism that cites a favourable series or the obituary that cites an unfavourable one.

The profit rate row deserves a fuller account of its counteracting influences, because they constitute, taken together, a shadow scorecard of their own: a list of mechanisms Marx identified whose subsequent careers can be graded with far less ambiguity than the law they were summoned to check. Consider how the elements of constant capital grow cheaper. The same productivity growth that raises the technical composition of capital in the using industries lowers the value of the machinery and materials those industries buy, because the machine-building and extracting sectors are themselves being revolutionised. The mechanism is not a conjecture. It is the arithmetic of every price index for capital goods across the era of computing, in which the power delivered per unit of expenditure multiplied beyond any precedent in the history of machinery, dragging the value composition of capital in directions its technical composition alone would never have taken it. Consider, next, the expansion of the labour supply available to capital, which Marx treated under foreign trade and the reserve army. The integration of the great agrarian populations into the world market multiplied the exploitable workforce within a generation, and the effect on profitability, documented in the business literature's own celebration of the era's margins, operated exactly as the counterweight predicts: a tendency pressed downward by composition was relieved by a widening field of exploitation. Consider, finally, the devaluation of capital in crises, which the theory assigns the role of periodic reset. Every major breakdown has performed the office, writing down the claims whose weight pressed on the rate and allowing accumulation to resume from a cheapened base. A law whose checks keep being found at work in the world, while the law's own resultant refuses to resolve into a measurable trend, presents the philosophy of science with a genuinely interesting object: not a falsehood, not a confirmation, but a dynamics described at a level where its components are visible and its sum is not. The scorecard's grade of contested is the honest name for that object, and the row may in the end be the card's most instructive, because it shows a great theory operating exactly at the frontier of what its century's, and our own century's, measurement can settle.

The theoretical challenge to the profit rate law has a name and a birthplace, and giving it both is fairer than the anonymous treatment it usually receives in the tradition's defensive literature. The Japanese economist Nobuo Okishio, working within the mathematical reconstruction of classical political economy, proved a theorem that bears his name: under the assumption that the real wage remains constant, any technical change that a profit-seeking firm would actually adopt, meaning any change that lowers its costs at prevailing prices, raises the equilibrium rate of profit for the system as a whole rather than lowering it. The theorem's elegance is the source of its sting. It does not deny that the composition of capital rises. It shows that rising composition, arriving through the cost-reducing choices the theory itself ascribes to capitalists, carries its own antidote within it, since the cheapening of wage goods and of the elements of production raises the rate of surplus value and the productivity of the whole apparatus faster than the composition drags the rate down. For the law to operate as its author intended, the rescue must come from the real wage rising, from bottlenecks in the production of capital goods, from the temporal structure of valuation, or from some other mechanism the theorem's clean assumptions exclude, and the subsequent literature is a map of those escape routes, each surveyed by able defenders and each narrowing, as it is surveyed, the territory the law can claim as its own.

The assessment's interest in the theorem is not partisan, and it should be stated what the theorem does and does not settle. It does not settle the empirical question, since actual economies violate its assumptions in every direction at once: real wages move, techniques are adopted for strategic as well as cost reasons, and the equilibrium whose rate it computes is a construction no economy visits. It does not settle the textual question, since the law's author worked with a temporal, disequilibrium intuition that the theorem's simultaneous framework may simply fail to engage, as the temporal single-system school of interpreters has argued at formidable length. What it settles is the burden of proof. Before the theorem, the law could present itself as the natural arithmetic of accumulation, needing only the world's cooperation. After it, the law's defenders owe an account of which assumption fails, where, and with what magnitude, and the account must be given in the theorem's own currency of models and measures rather than in the older currency of quotation and conviction. Some of that account has been supplied, and the row's grade of contested, rather than refuted, honours the supply. But the episode stands in the assessment as the cleanest demonstration of its recurring theme: the claims of this framework live or die, in the end, by the standards of the ordinary sciences it aspired to join, and its healthiest moments, including this uncomfortable one, are those in which it submits to them without flinching.

A historical footnote to the profit rate row illuminates the whole assessment, and it concerns the uses to which the law was put by the tradition's political wings, because the law's career as a political instrument explains part of its immunity to the measurement disputes that should have moderated its career as a scientific one. A falling rate of profit, deepening crisis, and eventual breakdown formed, in the hands of the tradition's catastrophist wing, a syllogism of inevitability: the system would collapse by its own arithmetic, and the movement's task was to organise the succession. The syllogism's comfort was obvious, and its cost was the one this article has documented in the penumbra's case, since a movement assured of the arithmetic's verdict could treat every recovery as a postponement and every postponement as a deepening, and could therefore learn nothing from the system's repeated refusal to collapse on schedule. The tradition's other wing drew the opposite moral and treated the law as an embarrassment to be minimised, which was equally a refusal to grade it. Between the wings, the law itself, a subtle and genuinely interesting proposition about the dynamics of accumulation, waited for readers willing to treat it as a question rather than as a banner or a skeleton. Such readers exist now in respectable numbers, on several sides of the measurement disputes, and their work has the character the scorecard honours: models stated so they can fail, series constructed so their choices are visible, conclusions held at the strength the evidence supports. The row's grade of contested is, in part, a report on their unfinished labour, and the assessment records its expectation, a hope rather than a grade, that the labour will one day finish the claim one way or the other and retire the row from the contested column where it has sat, embarrassing everyone impartially, for a century.

A last consideration on this row concerns the audience for its eventual resolution, because the profit rate question is sometimes dismissed, even by sympathetic economists, as an internal quarrel of the tradition with no bearing on anyone outside it. That dismissal underrates what rides on the answer. If accumulation does carry a built-in tendency for its own returns to sag, then the long eras of stagnation that punctuate the system's history have a common root, the frantic search for yield that precedes financial crises has a structural explanation, and the state's ever-expanding role as investor, guarantor, and customer of last resort appears as a compensation the system increasingly requires rather than a preference its politicians happen to hold. If, on the contrary, no such tendency operates, then stagnation eras need local explanations, financial manias are pathologies rather than symptoms, and the state's growth belongs to political history rather than to economic necessity. Few unresolved questions in political economy divide the world into such different wholes depending on their answer, and few have been pursued with such a mismatch between the importance of the prize and the inconclusiveness of the pursuit. The row stays open. The assessment commends it to the reader as the single most valuable unsolved problem the founder left behind, worth more to a young researcher than a dozen confirmations gathering dust in the table's upper rows.

The question also disciplines its neighbours on the table. Profitability is the hinge between the crisis row and the concentration row, since the great mergers so often arrive as responses to sagging returns, and between the profit rate and the wage relation, since every era of restored profitability in the record has been purchased partly by widening the field from which labour is drawn. To settle the hinge would be to tighten every row that hangs upon it, which is one more reason the assessment refuses to pretend the settling has occurred.

## The intermediate strata: the strongest disconfirmation

Every honest scorecard has a row its compiler would rather omit, and the measure of the instrument is whether the row appears in the same type size as the triumphs. The disappearance of the intermediate strata is this card's such row, and it is graded not confirmed in the plainest language the assessment uses anywhere. Marx expected the development of capitalism to simplify the class structure of the advanced societies toward two great camps. The small producers, master artisans, shopkeepers, and independent professionals who filled the social space between the owners of capital and the sellers of labour power were, on his account, transitional forms: too small to accumulate at the pace competition demanded, too exposed to survive the crises that periodically liquidated the weak, destined to sink in their great majority into the proletariat while a remnant climbed into the bourgeoisie. The Manifesto's famous polarisation is the rhetorical summit of the expectation, and the analytical works sustain its direction if not its tempo. Society was to become, steadily and visibly, a society of employers and employed, with the middle ground draining away beneath both. The advanced societies declined to comply. Their middle ground did not drain. It flooded.

The record, stated directionally as the protocol requires, runs against the expectation at every level at which it can be checked. The old middle class of independent producers contracted in agriculture and artisan manufacture, as predicted, and then the story the prediction did not contain began: a new middle class of salaried employees expanded on a scale that dwarfed the old one's decline. Managers and administrators multiplied as firms grew beyond the span of any owner's personal supervision, which is to say that concentration itself, the scorecard's most confirmed tendency, generated intermediate strata as its own by-product. Technicians, engineers, and specialists multiplied as production absorbed science into its daily operations. Teachers, health workers, and public administrators multiplied as states expanded into education, medicine, and welfare. Clerical and commercial employees multiplied as the circulation of commodities and the administration of credit grew more elaborate than the production they served. Credentialed professionals consolidated their positions behind licensing regimes and university gates that gave their scarcity a legal durability no guild had ever achieved. Small property, meanwhile, refused its assigned extinction: the small firm persisted in sector after sector, sustained by niches the giants found unprofitable, by subcontracting chains that made smallness a functional organ of largeness, by family labour's willingness to accept returns no wage bargain would tolerate, and by the perpetual founding of new small enterprises in the spaces new technologies kept opening. The result, across the advanced world, is a class structure whose centre of gravity sits precisely where the theory expected a vacuum: in the intermediate layers, neither owning the means of production in any commanding sense nor selling labour power in the unmediated condition of the classical proletarian, often doing something of both across a single career or a single household.

### What kinds of intermediate strata persisted and grew?

The salaried managers who administer capitals they do not own, the credentialed professions protected by licensing and scarcity, the technical and scientific employees whose knowledge functions as a personal asset, the public sector's administrative and caring occupations, and a perpetually renewed small business population occupying niches within and beneath the consolidated sectors.

The tradition's responses to this record constitute one of the great repair operations in the history of social theory, and the scorecard should acknowledge the operation's scale and ingenuity before recording its verdict. One response redefined the boundary: salaried employees, whatever their income and self-image, stand inside the wage relation and therefore inside the working class, properly understood, so that the middle's growth is the proletariat's growth wearing a collar of a different colour. There is substance in the move, since the wage relation row of this very card grades the extension of employment as confirmed, and a manager who can be dismissed is not an owner in any sense that survives a bad quarter. A second response stratified the middle itself, distinguishing a contradictory location, in a well-known formulation, occupied by those who share the characteristics of both great classes and must be analysed as a distinct formation rather than wished into either camp. A third response historicised the expectation, arguing that polarisation describes the logic of the pure model while concrete societies always generate mediating layers through the state, the professions, and the division between manual and mental labour. A fourth, the most candid, simply conceded the expectation as an error and rebuilt the tradition's political sociology around the middle's permanence. Each response preserved something worth preserving. None of them alters the grade of the original claim, because the original claim was not that intermediate positions would be complicated, contradictory, or dependent. It was that they would disappear as the system's development proceeded, ground between the millstones of concentration above and proletarianisation below. They grew instead, in number, in variety, in political weight, and in the share of the population whose life chances they define. The card's strongest disconfirmation is formidable precisely because it survives the repairs: after every redefinition has done its work, the expectation as stated stands unfulfilled in the plainest demographic sense.

Why did the expectation fail? A scorecard that records a grade without a diagnosis teaches half its lesson, and this failure's diagnosis reaches deep into the framework's assumptions about what drives class formation. Marx modelled the middle's disappearance on the fate of the independent producer under competition, and the model was accurate for the population it was drawn from: the handloom weaver, the small master, the peasant proprietor on marginal land really were ground down, in economy after economy, with a thoroughness that filled the century's literature of social catastrophe. The error lay in the extrapolation, in the assumption that the positions abolished by development would not be replaced by new positions generated by development itself. Capitalism, it turned out, does not merely destroy intermediate positions. It manufactures them, as a by-product of its own confirmed tendencies, with a fertility that outpaced the destruction. Concentration requires administration, and administration is an intermediate occupation. The application of science to production requires a technical intelligentsia, and technical expertise is an intermediate asset. The expansion of the state's fiscal and welfare functions requires a salaried apparatus, and public employment is an intermediate condition. The growing complexity of circulation, finance, law, marketing, and logistics requires coordinators who own nothing but their competence. Each engine of the system's development, examined closely, is found stamping out the very strata whose extinction the system's theorist predicted, and the irony is structural rather than incidental: the tendencies that confirmed themselves most powerfully are the ones that falsified the polarisation expectation, because a capital too large for its owner to manage is a capital that must employ managers.

### Why did small property survive inside a concentrating economy?

Because concentration governs the commanding heights rather than every niche, and because small capitals found durable functions the giants preferred to purchase rather than perform: flexible subcontracting, local services, craft and care work resistant to standardisation, and the perpetual experimental fringe where new branches are tested at small scale before consolidation claims them.

The consequences of the failure radiate through the derivative doctrines built on the expectation, and an assessment that confined itself to the row would understate what the row costs. A political strategy premised on a simplifying class structure, in which the middle's grievances would progressively dissolve into the proletariat's programme, faced instead a permanent electoral and cultural fact: the intermediate strata are the median citizens of the advanced societies, the audience every politics must address, the reservoir from which both reaction and reform recruit. A theory of ideology premised on two great class standpoints faced instead the fragmentation of standpoints across dozens of occupational worlds. A theory of the state premised on an instrument of the dominant class faced instead an apparatus staffed by the intermediate strata themselves, pursuing institutional interests no simple class instruction could predict. The tradition's best minds spent generations rebuilding on the corrected ground, and the rebuilding produced much of its most durable sociology, from the analysis of the new petty bourgeoisie to the study of credentialism and professional closure. Failure, in a living research programme, is information, and this failure was the most informative event in the programme's history. The scorecard records that too, because a grade of not confirmed, properly diagnosed, is worth more to a serious reader than a dozen confirmations left unexamined.

It remains to state the row's defeater in the direction of revival, since the discipline of this article attaches reversal conditions to judgements of failure as faithfully as to judgements of success. The disconfirmation would itself be overturned if the intermediate strata entered a long, broad contraction: if salaried administration were automated faster than new coordination occupations arose, if credentialed scarcity lost its legal and institutional protections across the professions together, if small enterprise's niches were absorbed by consolidated platforms and chains at a pace exceeding the founding of replacements, and if the population's centre of gravity shifted measurably toward the poles of ownership and unmediated wage labour across whole generations. Observers of the newest technologies periodically announce exactly this prospect, and the announcement deserves monitoring rather than mockery, since the mechanisms alleged, the automation of routine cognitive work chief among them, are aimed precisely at the intermediate occupations that earlier automation spared. Monitoring, however, is not grading. The record to date shows the middle adapting, migrating between sectors, and reconstituting its scarcity under new names each time an old stronghold falls. The row stands as graded, and the burden of its reversal rests where the scorecard places every burden: on the evidence, when and if the evidence arrives.

The strata row's consequences for political understanding deserve a fuller accounting than the main section gave them, because the expectation's failure quietly reorganised the politics of every advanced society, and the reorganisation is still the water in which democratic competition swims. A politics addressed to two great camps, one shrinking toward a possessing minority and the other swelling toward a propertyless majority, could be a politics of eventual majorities: the dispossessed needed only to recognise themselves and count. A politics addressed to a permanent, differentiated middle must be a politics of coalition among groups whose interests cross-cut: the salaried manager fearing taxation and the salaried nurse fearing cuts are both intermediate, both credentialed, both mortgage-holding, and their votes are available to opposite programmes in the same election. Every durable political formation of the modern era has been built by solving that coalition problem, and the formations that descend from the Marxist tradition solved it, when they solved it, by abandoning the demographic premise their founder supplied and addressing the middle as a permanent constituency with interests of its own rather than as a class in transit to somewhere else. The scorecard is an economic instrument, but it would be negligent not to register that its most negative grade corresponds to the point where the framework's political inheritance paid the highest price for an analytical error. Expectations are not free. Movements that organised around the middle's disappearance misread every electorate they faced, mistook their future majority for a demographic certainty, and were repeatedly astonished by the resilience of the layers they had scheduled for extinction. The astonishment is in the record alongside the demography, and both belong to the row.

A related consequence concerns the theory of the state, which the inventory has already visited from another direction. A state confronting two great classes might plausibly be modelled as the executive committee of one of them, and the founder's most quoted aphorism about government says nearly that. A state confronting a differentiated middle, whose strata staff its own apparatus, compete for its expenditures, and supply the median voter whose allegiance decides its elections, requires a subtler theory, and the tradition's later state theory, with its relative autonomies, its structural selectivities, its apparatuses ideological and repressive, is best read as the record of that requirement being met under the pressure of the strata row's verdict. The pattern generalises into something like a law of the framework's own development: its errors have been more fertile than its confirmations, because the errors forced reconstruction while the confirmations merely permitted applause. Concentration's triumph generated no new theory; the middle's persistence generated shelves of it. An assessment that valued the framework by its fertility would grade the rows in almost the reverse of their evidential order, and the scorecard notes the inversion without adopting it, as a reminder that the worth of a research programme and the truth of its claims are different questions, both legitimate, and disastrous when confused.

Among the intermediate strata, the credentialed professions repay a closer look, because their mode of persistence is the most instructive in the whole row: they survived not by hiding from the market but by organising scarcity within it, with the state's licence as their instrument. The physician, the lawyer, the architect, the chartered accountant, and their multiplying cousins across the modern occupational landscape do not sell a commodity the buyer can judge, in markets the entrant can freely enter. They sell a certified capacity, certified by bodies they themselves govern, behind educational gates whose length and expense ration the supply, under legal prohibitions that reserve whole categories of task to the certified. The sociologist Max Weber, no Marxist, supplied the tradition with its best vocabulary for the phenomenon in his account of social closure, the monopolisation of opportunities by a status group defending its market position through exclusionary rules. Randall Collins, building on that foundation, read the entire educational expansion of the modern world as a currency inflation of credentials, in which occupations defend and raise their status by demanding ever longer certification for work whose technical content has not changed in proportion. The reading explains what the polarisation expectation could not: why the stratum positioned between capital and labour did not merely persist but entrenched, converting its knowledge into a property-like asset that yields a rent, reproducing itself through the educational system it colonised, and intermarrying its fortunes with the state's licensing power. A founder who analysed the guilds of the feudal world as survivals destined for dissolution can be forgiven for not foreseeing their resurrection inside capitalism, in the white coats and framed certificates of the professions. The assessment does not forgive the expectation its failure, which stands. It records the mechanism of the failure with the respect due to a genuinely surprising piece of social engineering, improvised by its beneficiaries over generations and invisible, in its full architecture, to every nineteenth-century observer, friendly or hostile, who looked at the same society.

The public sector's own workforce forms a wing of the intermediate strata with a distinctive political economy, and the row's diagnosis is incomplete without it, because this wing's existence entangles the strata question with the state question in ways neither row can fully hold alone. Teachers, nurses, administrators, inspectors, social workers, and the clerks of a hundred agencies are wage earners, employed by the one employer that does not accumulate, paid from taxation rather than from the sale of their product, and organised, where they are organised, against an employer whose concessions are political events rather than commercial ones. Their interests cut across the classical map in every direction. As wage earners they share the proletarian condition of dependence on a salary and vulnerability to its erosion. As credentialed and often tenured employees they hold securities the private sector's wage earners envy. As the human face of the state they absorb the public's grievances against the order they administer, and as voters they constitute a constituency for the fiscal expansion on which their own employment depends, a constituency no party can ignore and no treasury can satisfy. The founder's framework, whose state was an instrument and whose classes were defined in production, has no native shelf for this population, and the tradition's attempts to shelve it, as unproductive labour, as a new petty bourgeoisie, as the state's ideological apparatus in human form, record the strain of the filing. The population itself, meanwhile, grew into one of the largest occupational blocs in every advanced society, delivered a substantial share of those societies' education, health, and care, and supplied a disproportionate share of their organised labour movements' membership and militancy. A stratum that cannot be filed, that numbers in the millions, and that strikes, votes, and organises as a recognisable force is not a footnote to the class structure. It is one of the class structure's load-bearing facts, and its absence from the founding map is the strata row's most concrete measure of how far the map's territory has travelled from its first survey.

## What Marx did not anticipate, first part: the state grown vast

The scorecard grades what Marx claimed. A complete assessment must also inventory what he never claimed because he never saw it coming, and the inventory begins with the largest single fact about modern capitalism that his framework treats only in passing: the state. The state of the classic ground in Marx's century was, by modern measure, a modest apparatus: it taxed lightly, spent narrowly, policed the boundaries of property and empire, relieved poverty with deliberate harshness, and left the reproduction of the labour force, the stabilisation of the currency, the education of the population, and the rescue of the financial system almost entirely to private arrangement and improvised response. The state of a modern advanced economy is a different species of entity. It commands a share of the social product that would have read as wartime mobilisation to a nineteenth-century finance minister, sustained permanently and in peacetime. It employs a substantial fraction of the workforce directly and sustains another fraction through its purchases and transfers. It educates nearly the whole population for years at public expense, underwrites the health of the workforce across the life course, insures deposits, pensions, unemployment, and catastrophe, regulates the conditions of production in exhaustive detail, and stands behind the monetary system as its ultimate guarantor. Whatever else modern capitalism is, it is capitalism administered, cushioned, and continuously co-produced by a fiscal and regulatory state of a scale for which Marx's own theory of the state, a theory focused on class domination and the executive management of bourgeois interests, provides no anticipation and only the thinnest resources.

The point must be stated precisely, because the tradition has often blurred it in self-defence. Later Marxists built elaborate theories of the capitalist state, of its relative autonomy, its structural dependence on accumulation, its role in securing the conditions of production that individual capitals cannot secure for themselves. Much of that work is formidable, and this series treats the state tradition in its own articles. The assessment here concerns Marx's anticipation, and on that question candour is compulsory: the founder did not foresee the fiscal state, the welfare state, or the regulatory state as permanent internal organs of the mode of production. His political writings treat the state as an excrescence upon civil society, powerful, parasitic, capable of Bonapartist independence, yet conceptually external to the economic relations it polices. The modern reality is a state woven into the relations themselves: the wage bargain presupposes its labour law, the firm's cost structure presupposes its infrastructure and its educated workforce, the currency in which every contract is written is its creature, and the demand that validates production is substantially its expenditure. An abstraction that brackets the state as superstructure can still identify the essential relations correctly, and this article's central judgement holds that it does. But the bracketing has a price, and the price is that the framework's account of how the system reproduces itself, stabilises itself, and periodically rescues itself is systematically incomplete in ways its author did not budget for.

### How large did the state become inside the capitalist economies?

Large enough to cease being an external referee and become a constitutive participant: taxing and spending shares of output that dwarf its nineteenth-century predecessor, employing and provisioning substantial fractions of the population, and supplying the legal, monetary, educational, and infrastructural preconditions without which the private economy's daily operations could not proceed at all.

The state's growth also bears on rows already graded, and tracing the connections is part of what distinguishes an assessment from a list. The absolute immiseration row failed partly because the state, pressed by organised labour and by its own need for healthy soldiers, workers, and citizens, socialised a rising share of the wage into services and transfers that no employer would have provided. The crisis row's strong form failed partly because the state acquired, and repeatedly used, the capacity to interrupt the devaluation mechanism, socialising losses that the nineteenth-century system would have liquidated, and thereby postponing or muting the restorative violence the theory described. The intermediate strata row failed partly because the state's own apparatus became one of the great employers of the intermediate occupations. Run a finger down the scorecard and the unanticipated state appears in the margin of row after row, as the deus ex machina that the original machinery did not include and that repeatedly altered the outcomes the machinery predicted. A fair assessment does not treat this as a scandal. Every framework brackets something that later proves load-bearing. It treats it as the single largest amendment the framework requires, larger than any correction to any single tendency, and it records that the amendment was supplied by history rather than by the theory's own development, which is the definition of an unanticipated fact.

Within the state's growth, one institution deserves separate treatment because its modern form is so entirely an invention of the system's maturity: central banking as the deliberate management of the monetary system. Marx knew central banks as institutions; the Bank of England's operations figure in his analysis of credit, and the currency controversies of his day run through the third volume's treatment of interest-bearing capital. What he analysed, however, was a bank of issue operating inside a metallic standard, constrained by convertibility, whose crises took the form of gold drains and whose room for discretion was narrow and contested. The modern central bank is something else: the issuer of an irredeemable currency, the setter of the price of money for the entire economy, the lender of last resort on a scale limited only by its own resolve, the purchaser of government debt and, in the extremities of crisis, of private assets across whole markets, and the institution whose announcements move the asset prices of the planet. This apparatus constitutes a standing refutation of any reading of Marx that treats the monetary system as a veil over real relations that must periodically tear. The veil learned to sew. Money in the modern economy is managed as a permanent countercyclical instrument, and the management has repeatedly altered the timing, depth, and distribution of crises in ways no nineteenth-century framework, Marx's included, budgeted for.

### What does central banking do that Marx did not foresee?

It manages the currency as a deliberate instrument of systemic stabilisation: setting the economy's foundational interest rate, supplying liquidity without metallic limit in a panic, and absorbing assets to hold markets open, thereby intervening in the crisis mechanism itself rather than merely presiding over a standard whose automatic disciplines the crisis enforced.

The assessment's interest in central banking is not antiquarian. It goes to the deepest question the scorecard poses, which is whether the essential relations still govern a system whose monetary constitution has been so thoroughly redesigned. The Marxist answer, and it is the answer this article's central judgement endorses, is that management administers the relations without abolishing them. The central bank's interventions are undertaken, avowedly and structurally, to preserve the conditions of accumulation: to keep credit flowing to capitals, to defend the value of the currency in which wages and debts are written, to prevent the devaluation of capital from proceeding to the point where the payments system itself collapses. Its celebrated independence is independence within a mandate whose outer boundary is the survival of the order it serves. Where its actions have redistributed wealth, they have done so overwhelmingly along the channels the relations dictate, supporting asset holders in the name of stability and disciplining wage claims in the name of price stability, an asymmetry its critics inside and outside the tradition have documented at length. A referee who can rewrite the rules mid-match has changed the game profoundly. He has not changed which teams own the stadium, and the distinction between administering a system and superseding it, first drawn in the methodology section, does the decisive work here as it does throughout the assessment.

A second unanticipated development belongs in this section because it shares the state's character as an institutional invention that altered the system's internal composition: the mature corporation itself, with its separation of ownership from control, its professional managerial hierarchy, and its access to the pooled savings of millions of households through pension funds and capital markets. Marx glimpsed the joint-stock form and read it, with characteristic dialectical appetite, as the abolition of private property within the confines of the capitalist mode itself, a transitional form socialising production while privatising its fruits. He did not, and could not, foresee the form's full career: the managerial stratum becoming a social power in its own right, the household's savings becoming the system's dominant pool of capital while the household's members remained wage earners, the corporation achieving legal personhood, multinational scope, and lifespans exceeding the states that charter them. The development cuts in two directions for the assessment, and both should be recorded. It complicates the class map, since the functions of ownership, control, and benefit now distribute across different populations in ways the two-camp expectation never imagined, reinforcing the intermediate strata row's verdict. And it confirms, at a level Marx would have recognised instantly, his reading of the joint-stock tendency itself: production socialised in form, appropriation private in result, the contradiction he named now operating at planetary scale through institutions that did not exist when he named it. The scorecard has no row for partial anticipations fulfilled in unrecognised shapes. If it did, this would be its richest entry.

The state's expansion has a fiscal mechanics that repays attention, because it reveals the modern state not merely administering the wage relation from outside but drawing its own substance from the relation's extension. The nineteenth-century state lived on customs duties, excises, and the taxation of land and trade, revenues compatible with a population mostly outside the money economy's core. The modern state lives on the income tax and the payroll contribution, revenues that presuppose a population of wage earners whose earnings are legible, regular, and withholdable at source. The fiscal state's growth and the wage relation's spread are therefore not two facts but one fact viewed from two sides: each employs the other. The employer became the state's unpaid tax collector, the payslip its census, the formal job its point of purchase on the citizen. This interlocking explains a feature of the modern order that puzzles purely economic accounts, the state's persistent interest in formalisation, in drawing informal work into the documented economy even where the output gained is small. Formal work is taxable work, contribution-bearing work, work that funds the apparatus that in turn underwrites the conditions for more of it. Marx's framework, which treated the state as financed by deductions from surplus and therefore as a claimant standing over production, has no native category for a state that co-produces the wage form as its own revenue base. The amendment required is not cosmetic. It changes the causal arrows in the theory of the state, and the tradition's later work on fiscal sociology, much of it conducted by scholars outside the Marxist fold, supplies materials the founder never imagined needing.

Demand management is the second fiscal fact the inventory should record in expansion, since it reverses one of the founding analysis's quiet assumptions. In the classic account, the consumption of the working class figures as a deduction from the social product, welcome to individual capitals selling wage goods and a constraint to the system as a whole, whose accumulation is limited by the surplus the wage bill leaves behind. The managed economy discovered, through depression and war mobilisation rather than through theory, that the wage earner's consumption is also the market in which a vast share of the product must be realised, and that a state willing to sustain that consumption through downturns could stabilise the accumulation it appeared to tax. The discovery did not abolish the tension Marx described; every finance ministry still lives inside it, torn between the wage as cost and the wage as demand. But it converted the tension from a contradiction the system suffers into a variable the state administers, with instruments, targets, and a professional priesthood. A contradiction administered is not a contradiction transcended, and the scorecard's central judgement survives the discovery intact. What does not survive is any simple version of the claim that the system's managers are blind to its dynamics. They study the same machine, from the other side of the glass, and their manuals, laundered of ancestry, are among the places where a modern reader can watch the framework's categories at work under assumed names.

Education is the state's quietest and most consequential intervention in the wage relation, and the inventory of the unanticipated would be incomplete without it, since the founder treated schooling mainly as a battleground of class demands, a clause in the factory acts, rather than as the colossal sorting and certifying machinery it became. The modern state educates nearly every child for a period that has lengthened across generations until it rivals the working day itself as the claim on the young person's time, and it does so at public expense on a scale that dwarfs every other cultural expenditure of every previous civilisation. The functions performed are genuinely multiple, and the assessment should resist the temptation, common in the tradition's own literature, to reduce them to a single sinister purpose. Schools mind children so that parents can work, which is a service to the wage relation's daily operation. They impart the literacy, numeracy, and discipline without which a modern workforce could not be employed at all, which is a service to accumulation's changing technical requirements. They sort the young into the occupational hierarchy with an appearance of fairness that reconciles the sorted to their destinations, and thereby serve the order's legitimacy. And they do all of this while also, irreducibly, teaching some of their charges to read the world critically, including against the order that taught them, which is why every generation's rulers regard the institution they fund with a suspicion they cannot quite act upon. A framework that filed education under superstructure, as a reflection of relations constituted elsewhere, missed the degree to which the modern relations are constituted in the classroom: the credential that makes the professional, the certificate that rations the technician's scarcity, the shared language and punctuality that make a national labour force employable as a single market. Later scholars inside the tradition, theorists of reproduction above all, rebuilt the account around that insight, arguing that the school's deepest product is not skill but the relation itself, renewed in each cohort with its hierarchies pre-installed. The rebuilding is among the tradition's genuine achievements. That it was needed at all is the inventory's point, and the scorecard leaves the point where it belongs, in the column of things the founder did not see coming and his successors had to supply.

War and welfare, the twin furnaces in which the modern state was forged, deserve a joint mention in the inventory because their partnership explains the timing and the irreversibility of the state's growth better than any doctrine does. The mass warfare of the last century required states to tax, borrow, direct, and provision on a scale that peacetime politics would never have authorised, and it taught populations to expect, in return for total mobilisation, a total reciprocity: the soldier's bargain, in which the state that could conscript the body owed the body security in age, health, and unemployment. The welfare settlements that followed the great wars were not concessions extracted from a reluctant system by a single class's pressure, though that pressure was real and organised. They were the demobilisation terms of whole peoples, written into fiscal constitutions that no subsequent government, of whatever colour, has succeeded in repealing, because the expectations they created became the floor of political possibility. The founder's state theory, built for an era when the state's social expenditure was a rounding error and its coercion was naked, cannot process a state whose legitimacy rests substantially on its role as insurer of the population against the wage relation's own risks. The amendment the tradition eventually built, in which the state's welfare functions figure as the price of the labour force's reproduction and the system's stability, is serviceable and appears throughout this series. The assessment's inventory insists only on the historical point: the price was set in the furnaces of total war and ratified at the ballot box by the strata whose growth the founder did not foresee, and a theory of the modern state that begins from class domination alone will always arrive late at the fact that the modern state's subjects defend its social functions as their own property, because in the only sense that finally matters, politically, they are.

## What Marx did not anticipate, second part: new property, old labour, hard limits

The inventory of the unanticipated continues with three further developments, each of a different character, and the differences among them are as instructive as the items themselves. The first is a new form of property that grew to rival the classical forms in economic weight. The second is an old form of labour whose persistence the framework noticed and then systematically undervalued. The third is not an institution or a relation at all but a boundary condition, a set of physical limits that the framework's productivism prevented it from treating as anything but scenery. Together they mark the three directions in which a nineteenth-century analysis, however powerful, was bound to be incomplete: toward forms its categories had not yet differentiated, toward labours its value theory bracketed, and toward a natural world its century still experienced as an inexhaustible stage.

Intellectual property is the clearest case of an undifferentiated category becoming a world of its own. Marx knew patents, copyrights, and trade secrets as legal furniture of his time, minor monopolies at the margin of an economy whose great properties were land, plant, and machinery. He had no occasion to imagine an economy in which the most valuable assets of the largest capitals would be intangible: portfolios of patents, copyrights and brands, proprietary data, and the legal rights to exclude others from using knowledge that costs nothing to copy. The income form corresponding to this property resembles rent more than profit in his own taxonomy, a payment for permission to use something whose scarcity is manufactured by law rather than imposed by nature or by the costs of production, and the resemblance has generated a substantial literature asking whether the commanding heights of the modern economy have quietly changed their economic character. That literature receives its own treatment in the platform and intangibles discussions elsewhere in this series. For the assessment, the point is narrower. A distinct form of property, with a distinct income form, a distinct politics, and a distinct relation to the production of new knowledge, grew from a marginal legal device into a central organising fact of accumulation, and the framework's property categories, drawn for a world of factories and fields, require genuine extension rather than mere application to contain it. Extensions of that kind are normal in a living theory. Their necessity is nonetheless a finding about the original, and the scorecard's inventory records it as such.

The second item runs in the opposite temporal direction. The unwaged labour of social reproduction, the cooking, cleaning, bearing and raising of children, nursing of the sick and the old, and maintenance of the households and emotional worlds from which workers emerge each morning and to which they return each night, is not a novelty the system invented after Marx. It is the immemorial foundation on which the wage relation has always rested, the labour that produces and reproduces labour power itself while appearing nowhere in the wage bargain and in no employer's accounts. Marx knew it existed. His analysis of labour power's value includes the costs of maintaining the worker and the worker's family, and his collaborator wrote the tradition's founding text on the family's history. Yet the mature economic work treats the reproduction of labour power largely as a presupposition, a process occurring offstage, in a domestic sphere whose internal organisation the critique of political economy could safely assume. The feminist scholars who later dragged that sphere onstage, many of them working inside or alongside the Marxist tradition, demonstrated that the assumption was not safe: that the wage's apparent payment for a day's labour conceals a second, unpaid working day performed overwhelmingly by women, that the boundary between production and reproduction is itself an artefact of the wage form, and that crises of care, of fertility, and of household exhaustion are not external shocks to the system but stresses in its hidden foundation. The persistence of unwaged reproduction is therefore graded in this inventory not as an oversight of fact but as a structural undervaluation, the more instructive because the materials for correcting it lay inside the framework's own founding insights, waiting for readers willing to apply the master's method to the master's blind spot.

### Why does unwaged reproduction matter to the assessment of Marx?

Because it shows the framework correctly identifying a relation while misjudging its weight: the reproduction of labour power was named as a presupposition and then left unanalysed, so that a labour process on which the entire wage system rests remained theoretically invisible until later scholars, largely feminists, forced it into the centre of the account.

The third item is the ecological constraint, and it is the gravest of the three because it challenges not a category or a weighting but a premise the whole framework shares with the century that produced it. Marx's analysis treats the growth of the productive forces as the engine of historical progress and the precondition of any emancipated order: abundance, the conquest of scarcity, the shortening of necessary labour through machinery, these are the material promises on which his politics rests. He was not indifferent to nature. His concept of metabolism between humanity and the earth, his analysis of soil exhaustion under capitalist agriculture, and his insistence that no generation owns the earth outright have supplied later ecological Marxism with genuine founding materials, richer than the caricature of a thinker who saw nature as a free gift to be plundered. Yet the framework contains no principle of limit. Its crises are crises of realisation, profitability, and proportion, never of throughput: the system, in the analysis, founders on its social contradictions, not on the exhaustion of the sinks and sources its production depends upon. The modern situation, in which the binding constraints on accumulation present themselves increasingly as physical, in the stability of the climate, the fertility of soils, the availability of water, and the integrity of the ecosystems that buffer all three, therefore confronts the framework with a class of problem it has no native machinery to process. Whether the ecological amendment can be integrated without dissolving the framework's core, or whether it stands as the point at which the abstraction itself requires rebuilding, is the largest open question this assessment leaves on the table, and it is left there deliberately, marked as open, rather than decorated with a verdict the evidence and the theory are not yet able to support.

Stepping back from the inventory, a pattern in the unanticipated deserves a section's pause, because it disciplines how much weight the inventory can bear against the framework. The unanticipated items share a common address. They concern the institutional and material environment within which the essential relations operate: the scale of the state that administers them, the monetary machinery that stabilises them, the property forms that extend them, the domestic labour that underwrites them, the natural limits that bound them. What the inventory does not contain is equally striking. It contains no alternative relation that displaced the wage relation as the organiser of production, no rival principle that displaced accumulation as the governor of investment, no successor form that displaced generalised commodity production as the medium through which society's labour is allocated. The surprises gathered at the level of the environment. The relations specified by the abstraction persisted through every surprise, bending around each new institution, colonising each new property form, surviving each rescue the state performed on the system's behalf. That asymmetry is the empirical heart of this article's central judgement, and it is why the inventory of the unanticipated, however long and however honestly compiled, does not sum to a refutation. A map can omit the weather stations, the sea walls, and half the coastline's defences, and still have drawn the continent's shape correctly. The omissions matter enormously for navigation. They do not redraw the continent.

The distinction also explains why the assessment's verdict differs so sharply from both the vindication and the refutation literatures in temper. The refutation literature reads the inventory as the verdict: the state, the central bank, the welfare settlement, the intangible economy, the ecological crisis, ergo the framework is a period piece. The vindication literature reads the persistence of the relations as the verdict and waves the inventory away as detail. The scorecard's method refuses both readings because it grades at levels. At the level of the essential relations, the framework's identification stands, and the inventory's items are so many demonstrations of the relations' adaptability. At the level of the dynamics, the record is mixed in the ways the rows record. At the level of institutional anticipation, the framework is simply silent where a complete theory of the modern system would need to speak, and its silence has had to be filled by successors, friendly and hostile, working with tools the founder did not supply. A reader who can hold all three levels in view at once possesses what this article set out to build: not a verdict on a man, but a map of where his analysis binds, where it bends, and where it ends.

One further unanticipated development resists filing under any single heading and earns its mention here, at the inventory's close, precisely because it straddles the levels: the globalisation of production itself, the organisation of single commodity chains across dozens of jurisdictions, with design, fabrication, assembly, logistics, and marketing distributed among continents according to the wage levels, infrastructures, and political disciplines each location offers. Marx anticipated the world market as capitalism's horizon and described its civilising and brutalising missions with unmatched vividness. What he did not anticipate was the fine-grained unbundling of the production process itself, the capacity of a single capital to slice its own operations across the planet while retaining unified command, so that the wage relation's global extension, graded confirmed on this card, proceeded not mainly through the replication of national capitalisms but through the integration of fragments of every nation into circuits governed from a few. The development confirms the abstraction's deepest instinct, that capital acknowledges no national boundary in its essence, while transforming every concrete question the tradition asked about class, state, and strategy, since the parties to the wage bargain now routinely sit in different hemispheres and answer to different states. It is the fitting final item for the inventory: a development the framework's spirit predicted and its letter never imagined, which is perhaps the most precise summary available of the framework's whole relation to the modern world.

The ecological item in the inventory deserves expansion, because it is the one unanticipated development that may yet promote itself from the environment of the essential relations to a challenge against the relations' continuation, and the assessment should state carefully what that promotion would involve. The framework's treatment of nature is double, and both halves are genuinely in the texts. On one side stands the productivist inheritance: nature as the free gift whose appropriation costs nothing, the earth as the original larder and workshop, progress measured by the growth of the forces that subdue her. On the other side stands the metabolic insight: labour as the mediation of a material exchange between humanity and the rest of nature, an exchange that can be conducted so as to rob the soil and the worker in the same motion, and a responsibility, stated in the third volume with startling modernity, of each generation to hand the earth down improved to its successors, as good heads of household rather than as owners. Ecological Marxism has built on the second half with real scholarship, recovering the founder's studies of soil chemistry and his fury at the town-country divide that shipped nutrients one way and sewage the other. The recovery is legitimate. It does not close the gap the inventory identified, which is not a gap of sentiment but of machinery: the value analysis has no term for a limit that binds regardless of how relations are arranged, no category in which a ton of atmospheric capacity appears as anything but a condition external to the circuit of capital. A capitalism that solved every social contradiction on the scorecard would still face the arithmetic of sinks and sources. A socialism built to the framework's specifications would face it too, which is why the ecological challenge is misdescribed whenever it is recruited as a point for either side in the older argument. It is a point against the century, the founder's and ours alike, and the scorecard files it accordingly: as the boundary at which the assessment's confidence in its own categories runs out, honestly marked, in the hope that marking it is the beginning of the work rather than a substitute for it.

The social reproduction amendment, once made, reorganised more of the framework than its makers initially claimed, and the assessment should follow the reorganisation a little way to show what a successful amendment looks like from inside. The founding analysis had treated the wage as the price of a commodity, labour power, whose production occurred in a private sphere the theory could legitimately bracket, as a chemist brackets the manufacture of his glassware. The feminist correction observed that the bracket concealed a subsidy: the wage, set by competition at the level of the individual worker's maintenance, has never in fact covered the cost of producing the next generation of workers, and the difference has been made up, across the whole history of the system, by unpaid labour performed predominantly by women inside households. The subsidy is not a detail of the wage's level. It is a structural feature of the relation, as constant as the relation itself, and it explains phenomena the unamended theory handled awkwardly: the persistent gap between men's and women's earnings, since the market prices the labour of those it assumes to be subsidised and subsidising differently; the ferocity of the politics surrounding care, childcare, and the family wage, which are negotiations over the subsidy's size and distribution; and the crisis that erupts whenever the subsidy is withdrawn faster than the market can replace it, in falling fertility, in exhausted households, in the care deficits that now shadow every rich economy's demography. An amendment that explains this much, that connects the labour market's fine structure to the household's interior through a single mechanism the original theory had all the concepts to state and never stated, is the strongest evidence available that the framework remains a living instrument rather than a reliquary. The hands that made the amendment were largely the hands of scholars the tradition's old guard had treated as a distraction from its main concerns. The scorecard, which has recorded the founder's failures without anaesthetic, records this one with its full moral attached: the framework's most important twentieth-century development was made by the readers it least expected, about the labour it least valued, and the pattern of that blindness and its correction is itself a datum for any honest assessment of what the tradition is.

A closing observation on the inventory concerns the order in which its items arrived, because the sequence carries a lesson about the kinds of surprise a framework should expect. The surprises did not arrive as refutations from outside the system's logic. They arrived as the system's own products: the state grew because accumulation's crises and wars demanded it, the central bank's powers grew because the credit system the founder analysed kept requiring a guarantor of last resort, intangible property grew because production's knowledge content grew, the care economy surfaced because the wage relation's own expansion consumed the household's unpaid reserves, and the ecological boundary announced itself because the productive forces whose growth the founder celebrated reached a planetary scale. A framework blindsided by its object's own development is in a different position from a framework blindsided by an alien world. Its categories failed, where they failed, by omission rather than by hallucination: the relations it named were real and remain real, and the developments it missed grew out of those very relations, as branches grow from a trunk the botanist described while failing to predict the branching. That is why the inventory, for all its length, does not read as an obituary, and why the tradition's best successors have been able to build the amendments from the founder's own materials more often than from anyone else's. The trunk held. The branching was not in the drawing. Both halves of that sentence are the finding, and an assessment that printed only one of them would be a poorer instrument than the instrument this article set out to construct.

## The supersession argument at full strength

Every tendency graded so far accepts, for the sake of the grading, that the system under examination is capitalism, and asks how its dynamics have run. A more radical family of arguments refuses the premise. On these accounts, the question is not whether Marx's tendencies describe the present system but whether the present system is still the one he described at all: whether the underlying mode of production has itself been superseded, quietly and without the revolutionary punctuation the tradition expected, by a successor order whose governing relations are no longer those of capital. The scorecard's method requires that this argument be stated at full strength, attributed to its actual proponents, before it is assessed, and this section complies in the spirit of the exercise: what follows is the supersession case as its ablest advocates would wish it made, in its most formidable contemporary forms.

The oldest form is the post-industrial thesis, associated above all with the sociologist Daniel Bell, who argued that the advanced economies were passing into an order organised around theoretical knowledge rather than property, in which the axial institution is the university rather than the firm, the axial resource is information rather than capital, and the class structure of the industrial era dissolves into a hierarchy of expertise. On Bell's account, the tendencies Marx described belonged to the industrial phase of social development and expire with it: when the majority of workers produce services and symbols rather than goods, when occupational status follows education rather than ownership, the categories of capital and labour lose their grip on the social structure, and continuing to apply them is an act of nostalgia disguised as analysis. The thesis was framed against Marx explicitly and has been renewed in every generation since, in the vocabularies of the information society, the knowledge economy, and the weightless world, each renewal announcing that the material basis of the old critique has finally evaporated.

The sharpest contemporary form is the technofeudal thesis, argued most prominently by the economist and former finance minister Yanis Varoufakis, who contends that capital has been displaced by something he calls cloud capital and its owners a new feudal class. On this account, the great digital platforms do not operate as capitals in markets at all. They own the digital fiefs within which exchange itself now occurs, extracting rent from every transaction conducted on their ground the way a feudal lord extracted dues from every mill and market in his domain, and subordinating the surviving capitals, which must rent access to the platforms' populations, to the status of vassals. Markets, the medium in which Marx's capitalism lived, are being replaced by privately owned digital estates where a single proprietor sets the terms of visibility, access, and exchange by algorithmic command. Profit, the system's old governor, yields to rent. Competition yields to enclosure. If the description is right, then the essential relations this article has been grading no longer hold: production for exchange persists in form, but the exchange occurs inside feudal jurisdictions, and the driving imperative is no longer accumulation through production but extraction through ownership of the ground on which production and exchange must stand.

A third form, gentler in rhetoric and in some ways deeper in implication, is the post-work argument developed by writers such as Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams and a wider literature around automation and basic income. Its claim is not that a new ruling class has displaced capital but that the wage relation itself, the relation this scorecard graded as triumphantly extended, is entering structural decline: that automation is destroying employment faster than new employment forms, that the system's demand for labour is becoming optional to its functioning, and that the political task is to manage the transition to an order in which income and activity are progressively decoupled from the job. Where the technofeudal thesis sees the wage relation subordinated to rent, the post-work thesis sees it withering from want of work to sell, and both theses converge on the same assessment-relevant conclusion: the essential relations of Marx's abstraction are ceasing to describe the operative core of the economy, whatever their persistence at its margins.

### Who argues that capitalism has already been superseded?

Daniel Bell argued the post-industrial case, that knowledge and expertise displace property as the organising axis. Yanis Varoufakis argues the technofeudal case, that platform owners extract rent as digital feudal lords. Post-work writers argue that automation hollows out the wage relation itself. Each names a different successor relation, which is itself diagnostic.

Stated together, the three theses exhibit the supersession family's characteristic strength and its characteristic weakness in a single view. The strength is that each identifies a genuine transformation that the scorecard's own inventory has already registered: the rise of intangible property and rent-like income, the migration of employment toward services and knowledge work, the state's assumption of functions that blur the boundary between market and provision. None of the three is describing nothing. The weakness is visible in the disagreement among them about what has supposedly replaced capitalism. A successor order should be identifiable by its own essential relations, stated as crisply as the relations they displace. Instead the literature offers a knowledge order in one telling, a feudal order in a second, and a post-work order in a third, each incompatible with the others, each drawn by extrapolating a different real tendency into a totality. Extrapolation is the family's method and its vulnerability: it takes a relation that has grown at the margin, or in a commanding sector, and pronounces it the new essence of the whole, while the bulk of the world's production continues to be organised, measurably and unglamorously, by employers buying labour power to produce commodities for sale at a profit. The assessment of the theses, which the next section completes, turns on that bulk and on the demanding standard the verdict defeater sets for any claim of supersession.

The assessment of the supersession theses proceeds by applying to each the same question the methodology section applies to every institution: does the alleged successor relation abolish the essential relations, or administer, extend, and occasionally disguise them? Take the technofeudal case first, since it is the most precisely stated and therefore the most honourably testable. The platform proprietor's income is called rent, and the platform's domain is called a fief. Yet the proprietor's enterprise employs wage labour on a vast scale to build and maintain the domain, invests accumulated revenue in expansion under the discipline of expected returns, competes ferociously with rival proprietors for the populations both seek to enclose, and is valued by capital markets precisely as a claim on future profits. Its extraction from the merchants who trade on its ground is real, and rent-like in form, but rent in Marx's own analysis was never external to capitalism: ground rent was a deduction from surplus value produced elsewhere, a form of distribution within the mode of production rather than a rival to it, and the platform's toll fits that architecture with uncomfortable neatness. The merchants paying the toll are capitals. The workers building the platform are wage labourers. The consumers are, in their overwhelming majority, wage earners spending wages. A feudalism in which every party to every transaction stands inside the wage relation or the accumulation relation is feudalism as metaphor, and the metaphor's undoubted vividness, its capture of the experience of dependence on unaccountable private jurisdictions, should not be mistaken for a demonstration that the underlying relations have changed their kind.

The post-industrial case dissolves, on the same test, into a confusion between the material a worker handles and the relation under which the handling occurs. That a nurse manipulates care, a programmer symbols, and a teacher knowledge rather than pig iron tells us what their labour produces and nothing about the form in which it is employed. All three sell labour power. All three work under the direction of organisations that must reconcile their costs with revenues ultimately governed by accumulation or by taxation of accumulation. The knowledge economy's characteristic product, information, turned out to be not the solvent of property but its most aggressively extended frontier, as the intellectual property discussion in the inventory recorded: the moment knowledge became the axial resource, capital enclosed it with a thoroughness no factory owner ever achieved over coal. Bell's deepest observation, that expertise and education would stratify the new order, has been amply borne out, and the scorecard's strata row records the fact with gratitude. His conclusion, that stratification by expertise displaces the relations of capital, does not follow and has not occurred: the expert strata are employed, salaried, dismissible, and pensioned by the same relations that employ the warehouse operative, at different terms the assessment has never denied.

The post-work case is the most serious of the three because it alone is aimed at the wage relation's material basis rather than at its costume, and because its mechanism, the automation of labour out of production, is one Marx himself theorised in the fragment on machines treated elsewhere in this series. Its assessment therefore deserves the care of a genuine concession before the verdict. The concession is that nothing in the framework guarantees the wage relation's permanence: a system that progressively expelled living labour from production would indeed undermine the relation on which the whole abstraction rests, and Marx's own speculations about the general intellect glimpse exactly that abyss. The verdict is that the abyss has not opened. Across the long history of automation alarms, each generation's displacement has been followed by the relation's reconstitution in new occupations, new sectors, and new geographies, and the aggregate demand for wage labour across the world economy stands at its historical maximum rather than at the edge of its collapse, as the wage relation row documented. The post-work literature's own political programme tacitly concedes the point: its demand for income decoupled from work is a demand addressed to a future its advocates agree has not arrived, to be achieved by political action against the grain of relations they describe as still dominant. A supersession that requires a political campaign to bring it about has not yet superseded anything.

This article's central judgement can now receive its verdict defeater in the exact form the brief for the series requires, stated as a condition on evidence rather than as a debating point. The judgement that the essential relations still obtain would be defeated by evidence that a substantial and growing share of production occurs outside the wage relation and outside production for exchange, which is the form the technofeudal and post-work arguments would have to take and which they have not established. Note the defeater's construction. It does not ask for the disappearance of wage labour, which no serious proponent alleges. It asks for a large and expanding share of output organised on other principles, measurable in principle by the same statistical apparatus that documents the wage relation's extent, and it thereby converts the supersession debate from a contest of metaphors into a question with a conceivable affirmative answer. Should the share of output produced by genuinely autonomous producers exchanging outside capitalist markets, by commons-based production provisioning its participants as of right, or by automated systems whose output is distributed without sale, grow from enclave to sector to a commanding fraction of social production, the judgement falls and this article's central claim falls with it. The scorecard records that no such growth, at any such scale, appears in the evidence its rows surveyed. The relations hold. The holding is a finding, not a faith, and the defeater is the receipt that proves the difference.

Two older members of the supersession family merit brief honourable mention before the family is dismissed, because their careers illustrate the family's life cycle with unusual clarity. The managerial thesis, argued in the middle of the last century by James Burnham and absorbed into a hundred textbooks, held that the separation of ownership from control in the great corporation had already transferred power to a new ruling stratum of managers, whose society would be neither capitalist nor socialist but managerial, organised around access to the state's and the corporation's administrative heights. The thesis had its evidence: the salaried executive really had displaced the owner-entrepreneur from the boardroom's daily command. Its error was the family error, the promotion of a change in the personnel and mechanics of rule into a change in the relations being ruled. The managers administered accumulation for owners they increasingly were not, yet the accumulation proceeded on the old imperatives, the owners' claims were honoured in dividends and share values, and in due course the owners reasserted direct discipline over the managers through the capital market itself, through takeovers, buyouts, and the doctrine of shareholder value, in a counter-revolution conducted entirely within the relations the thesis had declared superseded. The convergence thesis, the family's Cold War branch, held that industrial societies of both blocs were growing alike under the imperatives of technology, toward a mixed, managed, post-ideological order in which Marx's categories described neither side. Its evidence was the apparent similarity of factories, bureaucracies, and consumer aspirations across the divide. Its fate was to be refuted by the divide's resolution, which took the form not of convergence but of one side's relations absorbing the other's territory wholesale, extending the wage relation and the market over populations the thesis had counted as the vanguard of the post-capitalist world. Twice, then, the family announced the succession, and twice the incumbent relations absorbed the announced successor and continued. The current generation of theses is abler than its ancestors, and its evidence about platforms, intangibles, and automation is real. The family's track record nonetheless belongs in the assessment, because a form of argument that has cried succession at every reign for a century owes its audience a discount rate, and the scorecard applies one.

A gentler member of the supersession family deserves its paragraph, because it is the one most likely to be encountered by readers of this article in the wild and because its error is the most seductive. The postcapitalism thesis, argued with verve by the journalist Paul Mason and echoed across a wide literature of the digital commons, holds that information technology is corroding the price mechanism from within: that goods which can be copied at no cost cannot sustain prices, that networks breed collaborative production outside the market's discipline, and that a new economy of shared abundance is growing, unplanned, in the interstices of the old, as capitalism itself once grew within feudalism's interstices. The historical analogy is the thesis's charm and its trap. Capitalism grew within feudalism as a complete alternative organisation of production, with its own property, its own class, and its own state forms in embryo, over centuries, against the old order's resistance. The commons production the thesis celebrates is real and valuable, and any reader of this article has benefited from encyclopaedias written by volunteers and software written by collaborators. Its scale within total production, however, remains that of an enclave sustained by the surrounding economy's wages, since the volunteers eat, house themselves, and connect through infrastructures that wages and markets supply. An interstice that depends on the host for its participants' subsistence has not yet demonstrated the host-independent reproduction that made capitalism a successor rather than a supplement. The defeater printed in this article was written with precisely this case in view: substantial and growing, beyond wage labour and beyond market exchange, measured against the whole. The commons may yet meet it. Aspirations, however eloquent, are not measurements, and the scorecard grades the thesis, like its cousins, as a direction of travel asserted beyond the evidence of distance covered.

It is worth asking, before the supersession family is finally dismissed, why the announcement of capitalism's replacement recurs with such regularity across such different eras and temperaments, because the recurrence is itself a social fact that an assessment of the framework should be able to explain, and the framework, interestingly, can. Each generation's announcement fastens on the same experience: the visible novelty of the commanding sector, whose forms really are new, whose fortunes really are unprecedented, and whose proprietors really do appear to obey different laws from the mill owners and railway kings of the textbooks. The experience is veridical. What the announcement adds is the inference from the commanding sector's novelty to the whole system's transformation, and the inference fails for the reason the methodology section gave at the outset: the abstraction specifies relations, not sectors, and a new sector enters the world already inside them, hiring its labour, selling its product, and accumulating under the old compulsions in the new costume. The framework explains the recurrence, moreover, as an effect of the system's own dynamism: a mode of production that revolutionises its instruments every generation will perpetually present its observers with a surface that looks like a new world, and the temptation to mistake the surface for the structure is the occupational hazard of living inside it. The founder succumbed to a version of the temptation himself, in his expectation that the joint-stock form heralded the system's self-abolition, and the scorecard notes the symmetry without pleasure. Supersession is the compliment each era pays to its own novelty. The relations, unimpressed, continue underneath, and the assessment's task is to keep saying so only for exactly as long as the evidence permits, and not one announcement longer.

## The strongest objection: a theory that cannot lose

Against everything the scorecard has recorded stands an objection deeper than any single row's grade, and the assessment has postponed it until now because it challenges not the framework's findings but its right to have findings at all. The objection is associated most famously with the philosopher Karl Popper, who took Marxism as his paradigmatic case of a theory that had degenerated from science into pseudo-science. Its strongest form runs as follows. A theory whose central claims are tendencies, whose tendencies are permanently accompanied by counteracting factors, whose object is an abstraction rather than any observable economy, and whose defenders can therefore accommodate any outcome, boom as accumulation's vigour, slump as its contradictions, consolidation as its logic, fragmentation as its cunning, has placed itself beyond the reach of evidence altogether. It does not predict and survive. It interprets and endures, which is the survival strategy of a mythology. The confirmations its defenders celebrate are worthless, because a framework that would also have explained the opposite outcome cannot claim credit for this one: concentration confirms it, but had markets fragmented for a century, the fragmentation would have been absorbed as the anarchy of competition; the middle strata's disappearance would have confirmed it, and their expansion was absorbed as a complication of the class structure; rising wages were absorbed as relative immiseration, falling wages as the absolute kind. A key that opens every lock is not a key. It is a story about locks.

The objection must be given its full weight before it is answered, because in its full weight it is substantially right about the tradition's practice, whatever its merits against the theory's logic. The history of Marxist apologetics is, in embarrassingly large part, a history of exactly the absorptions the objection catalogues. Defeats were reclassified as detours, delays as deepenings, and refutations as confirmations viewed from a higher dialectical altitude, until the vocabulary of the school became a machine for converting any event into evidence of the school's correctness. Popper's charge drew its force from observation of that machine in operation, and any assessment that pretends otherwise disqualifies itself. Nor is the machine a relic. Its contemporary forms are politer, journal-reviewed, and conceptually upholstered, yet the family resemblance is unmistakable whenever a finding's awkwardness is dissolved by re-describing the level at which the theory was really pitched, or whenever a tendency's non-appearance across a long span is met not with a downgraded grade but with an enlarged inventory of counteracting factors whose own evidence is never independently demanded. The scorecard method exists, in this article, as a refusal to operate that machine. Its rows carry grades the tradition's publicists would not have chosen, and its defeaters are stated before its verdicts are celebrated. Whether the refusal can be sustained as a general discipline, rather than as this article's local virtue, is a question about the tradition's future that the assessment is content to pose.

### What is the strongest form of the unfalsifiability objection?

That the framework's protective machinery, tendencies with counteracting factors, claims pitched at adjustable levels of abstraction, outcomes reinterpreted after the fact, renders it compatible with every possible course of events, so that its apparent confirmations are unearned: a theory that cannot state what would count against it cannot claim credit for anything that counts for it.

The answer begins by separating the claims, as the scorecard has done throughout, because the objection's force varies enormously by row and its blanket form is therefore as crude as the apologetics it attacks. Some of Marx's claims are testable as stated and have been tested. The intermediate strata's failure to disappear is one: it specified a direction, a mechanism, and a population, and the world returned a negative verdict that this article has recorded without anaesthetic. The concentration tendency is another: it specified a direction and a mechanism, and the world returned a positive verdict documented by hostile witnesses. Absolute immiseration is a third, and its negative verdict stands in the table. A theory containing claims that can lose, and demonstrably have lost, is not a theory that cannot lose. It is a theory with a mixed record, which is the ordinary condition of ambitious frameworks in the social sciences, from the rational actor programme to the modernisation theory against which Bell's generation defined itself. The objection, to do its work, must therefore retreat from the theory's testable claims to its untestable residue, and there, on that narrower ground, it lands, and the assessment concedes the landing in terms. Several of the framework's most cherished formulations are not testable as stated. The falling rate of profit, as the scorecard found, is entangled in measurement choices that its own statement does not fix. The strong form of the crisis claim specifies no criterion distinguishing a terminal crisis from a severe one. The dialectical vocabulary in which the tradition discusses contradiction can be, and often has been, used to redescribe any state of affairs as its own opposite ripening. On those claims, the Popperian verdict stands: they function, as commonly deployed, as interpretations in search of discipline rather than as assertions at risk, and the tradition has resisted, across generations, the obvious remedy of specifying in advance what evidence would retire them.

The concession made, the answer's second movement can proceed, because the objection in its popular form proves more than its proponents usually intend. If compatibility with every outcome disqualifies a framework, then the frameworks against which Marxism is usually measured fare no better under the same audit. The theory of efficient markets absorbed its anomalies for decades by enlarging the definition of risk. The rational expectations programme accommodated persistent forecasting failure by questioning the data's construction. Modernisation theory survived the non-arrival of its predicted convergences by discovering that traditions were more resilient, sequences more variable, and prerequisites less binding than stated, each discovery enlarging the theory's protective belt in the manner Popper diagnosed. The point is not that everyone cheats and therefore cheating is permitted. The point is that mature research programmes, in the social sciences above all, consist of a hard core of commitments surrounded by revisable auxiliary claims, and they are judged, rationally, not by the impossible standard of instant falsification but by whether their revisions generate new findings or merely recycle old defences. By that standard, the standard the philosophy of science itself retreated to after Popper, the Marxist programme's record is genuinely mixed in an informative way. Its class analysis revisions, forced by the intermediate strata, generated durable sociology. Its crisis anatomy anticipated mechanisms later formalised by others. Its concentration claim directed attention to a phenomenon its rivals' textbooks long treated as peripheral. And its profit rate literature, whatever its inconclusiveness, produced measurement work of lasting value. A programme that sometimes learns is not a mythology, even when its custodians frequently behave as though learning were unnecessary.

The third movement of the answer concerns the abstraction itself, the deepest layer at which the objection aims. Is the claim that those relations still obtain testable, or is it the final unfalsifiable redoubt? This article has already answered by construction: the claim was issued with its defeater attached, naming the evidence, a large and expanding portion of production carried on outside wage labour and outside market exchange, that would retire it. The claim is therefore not a tautology. It could have been false, and in a world where the supersession theses described reality it would be false. Its truth is a contingent, empirical, revisable finding about the organisation of production across the planet, of exactly the same logical species as the finding that feudal relations no longer organise European agriculture: a claim nobody regards as metaphysical, which rests on the same kind of evidence, and which could in principle be reversed by a sufficiently strange turn of history. What the objection rightly demands of such claims is not that they be fragile but that they be honest about their load-bearing evidence and their retirement conditions. That demand has been met here, in print, in advance. A reader who believes the defeater's condition is closer to fulfilment than this assessment judges is invited to bring the production statistics that show it. The scorecard will change its grade when the numbers change their story, and a framework whose grading instrument behaves that way has answered the objection in the only coin the objection accepts.

There is a final, quieter point to record about the objection's provenance, because it bears on how much of the assessment it can reach. Popper's target was historicism, the claim to have discovered laws of historical development that license prophecy, and his Marx was largely the Marx of the historicist reading: the prophet of inevitable succession, stages, and outcomes. The Marx of this article's methodology, the anatomist of a mode of production who refused to convert his sketch into a universal itinerary, is a less natural target, and the objection's most quotable passages glance off the abstraction reading while striking the prophetic one squarely. That is not a dismissal. The prophetic reading has been held, loudly, by movements claiming the founder's authority, and a critique that demolishes it does real work in the world. It is a calibration. The objection defeats the Marx of the timetable. Against the Marx of the scorecard it achieves something more useful than defeat: it imposes the discipline, row by row, defeater by defeater, under which this entire assessment has been conducted, and the assessment acknowledges the debt by printing the objection at full strength in its own pages rather than leaving it to its enemies.

The philosophy of science did not stand still after Popper, and the assessment's use of his objection should acknowledge the refinement that followed, since the refinement supplies the fairest tribunal before which the framework can be tried. Imre Lakatos, who had watched the Marxist programme at close quarters and used it as one of his own cautionary exhibits, proposed that research programmes be judged not by single falsifications but by the character of their development over time: a programme progresses when its revisions predict novel facts, facts its rivals did not expect and its own earlier versions did not contain, and it degenerates when its revisions merely accommodate, after the event, whatever the world has just done. The tribunal is fair because it grants what Popper's cruder followers denied, that all living science protects its core with a belt of revisable claims, while still demanding rent for the protection in the currency of discovery. Before that tribunal the framework's plea is mixed in ways the scorecard has already documented without using the vocabulary. The concentration claim was a novel fact in Lakatos's strictest sense: a prediction about the structure of industries, made before the industries existed in their modern form, corroborated by a science that grew up independently. The class analysis revisions were accommodations that unexpectedly flowered into discoveries, as the study of the strata the theory had failed to foresee became a progressive sub-programme in its own right. The profit rate literature, by contrast, exhibits the degenerative pattern in its purest available form: each failure of the trend to appear has been met by a refinement of measurement whose principal yield is the trend's continued possibility. A programme may be progressive in one limb and degenerating in another, and the scorecard, translated into Lakatosian, is precisely the chart of which limb is which. That the translation exists, and that the chart survives it, is the deepest answer the assessment can give to the objection with which this section began. The framework is not a mythology, because parts of it can still lose and parts of it can still win. It is not a finished science, because parts of it have arranged never to do either. The scorecard's whole purpose is to keep the two conditions from being averaged into a fog in which nothing can be seen at all.

Candour about the objection requires one more admission, which the scorecard format makes easy to place because it concerns a claim the founder's followers, and on some readings the founder, actually did advance in falsifiable form and lost. The political expectation woven through the tradition's first century held that socialist revolution would arrive first and most surely in the most developed capitalist countries, where the relations were ripest, the working class largest, and the contradictions sharpest. History delivered the revolutions, where it delivered them at all, in the empire's peasant periphery and the war-shattered semi-developed states, and delivered in the advanced core, instead, a century of reform, incorporation, and rising consumption. Whatever one's judgement of those events, the expectation as stated was a prediction, it was dated by the lifetimes of those who held it, and it failed. The tradition's responses to that failure constitute much of its twentieth-century intellectual history: theories of imperialism explaining the core's quiescence by the periphery's exploitation, theories of hegemony explaining it by consent's manufacture, theories of the labour aristocracy, of embourgeoisement, of the welfare state's purchase of peace. Some of that work is brilliant, and some of it appears in this series' own pages with respect. Its collective shape, however, is the shape Popper taught us to recognise: a falsified expectation preserved by a sequence of auxiliary constructions, each plausible, each post hoc, and none ever permitted to count against the core from which the expectation was drawn. The scorecard's method was designed in full knowledge of that history. It is why the political expectation earns no row of its own: it belongs not to the analysis of capitalism's tendencies but to the penumbra of hopes the methodology section separated from the core, and its failure, honestly recorded here, grades the penumbra and the movements that staked everything on it. It does not grade the anatomy, and the assessment's refusal to let it do so is not a protective manoeuvre but the same itemising discipline, applied where it costs the tradition most.

One refinement of the objection remains unexamined, and it is the version most often heard from working scientists rather than philosophers: not that the framework cannot lose, but that it does not win in the way sciences win, by the prediction of phenomena nobody was looking for. The charge deserves a precise answer, because it identifies the framework's genuine poverty and its genuine wealth in a single comparison. The poverty is real. The tradition's confirmed rows are retrodictions more often than predictions in the strict sense: the concentration tendency was stated before the data existed to grade it, which is prediction of a kind, but the strata failure was absorbed rather than anticipated, the crisis anatomy explained the last crisis with more fluency than it forecast the next, and no Marxist model has ever issued the kind of dated, quantitative, falsifiable forecast, about an election, a market, or a demographic turn, that a mature science issues as a matter of routine. The wealth is also real, and it is of a different species: the framework's characteristic victories are discoveries of connection, the demonstration that phenomena catalogued separately, the wage's level and the household's interior, the merger wave and the wage stagnation of a company town, the soil's exhaustion and the worker's, belong to one another as moments of a single process. Sciences of complex wholes, geology and evolutionary biology among them, win in exactly this species more often than in the forecasting one, and are not denied the name of science for it. The assessment's verdict on the objection can therefore be given its final form. The framework is a science of the connective kind, with a mixed record, several unfinished claims, and a custodial tradition that has too often protected it from the standards its own method implies. That is a smaller thing than its believers claim and a larger thing than its obituarists allow, and it is, on the evidence assembled here, approximately the truth.

## What the assessment establishes

The rows are graded, the unanticipated inventoried, the supersession theses heard and weighed, and the deepest objection confronted. What, in sum, does the assessment establish? It establishes, first, a profile in place of a verdict, and it asks the reader to accept the profile as the answer to the relevance question in its only honest form. The framework's identification of the essential relations stands: generalised commodity production, the wage relation, and accumulation as the system's governing compulsion still organise the production of the world, and the evidence for their persistence is not the testimony of believers but the working material of every statistical office, development agency, and business school on the planet. Its named dynamics divide: concentration strongly confirmed by independent witnesses, the wage relation's extension confirmed at planetary scale, crisis upheld in its weak form with its terminal form unproven, absolute immiseration refuted, relative immiseration hostage to its measures, the profit rate contested from its derivation to its data, and the class structure's predicted simplification refuted in the plainest demographic terms. Its institutional anticipations are thin where the modern system is thickest: the fiscal state, the managed currency, the intangible property regime, and the ecological boundary all had to be supplied by successors. That is the profile. It resembles neither the monument of the vindication literature nor the ruin of the refutation literature, and its resemblance to the actual record is its entire recommendation.

It establishes, second, a standard for conducting such assessments that outlives this article's subject. The standard has four working parts, each demonstrated in the rows above. Itemise: never grade a framework wholesale when its claims have separate mechanisms and separate evidence. Fix the level: never grade a claim pitched as an abstraction against the fortunes of a decade, nor a claim pitched as a tendency against the fate of a firm. Privilege hostile witnesses: a finding documented by researchers with no stake in the framework outweighs a library of confirmations assembled by its friends. Attach the defeater: never publish a grade without the evidence that would change it, stated in advance and in public. An assessment culture that observed those four rules would find most of the relevance wars unwritable, which is perhaps the strongest argument for the rules. The wars persist because their combatants are rewarded for verdicts, and verdicts are precisely what the evidence, honestly itemised, refuses to supply.

### What would have to change for the essential relations to stop holding?

Production would have to migrate, at substantial and growing scale, outside the sale of labour power and outside production for exchange: commons provisioning as of right, autonomous production sustaining whole populations, or automated abundance distributed without purchase. Enclaves and experiments would not suffice; the migration would need to register in the organisation of whole economies.

It establishes, third, a corrected sense of where the framework's living frontier lies, which is not where either camp habitually looks. The frontier is not the defence of the refuted rows; the strata are not disappearing, the absolute immiseration is not resuming, and scholarship spent pretending otherwise is spent against the record. Nor is the frontier the celebration of the confirmed rows, which need no defenders, having acquired the entire industrial organisation profession as their unwitting research staff. The frontier lies at the three junctions where the framework's core meets what it did not anticipate: the junction with the state, whose fiscal and monetary machinery now co-produces the relations the abstraction brackets; the junction with intangible property, where the income forms of the commanding heights strain the taxonomy of profit and rent; and the junction with the ecological boundary, where a framework built on the premise of expanding productive forces confronts limits that are not social in origin and cannot be negotiated by any rearrangement of relations alone. Work at those junctions, some of it visible in the companion articles of this cluster, is where the assessment points a serious reader's attention. A framework is alive at its junctions or it is a monument everywhere, and the profile recorded here, confirmations and refutations alike, describes a framework with enough life left in it to have frontiers worth the name.

A final word belongs to the question with which the article opened, the searcher's question, whether his analysis still explains modern capitalism. The scorecard's answer can now be given in its compressed and defensible form. He explains its constitution better than its biography. The relations he isolated remain the relations that organise production, the pressures he derived from them remain detectable in the record his successors assembled, and two of his specific tendencies rank among the best-confirmed structural claims any nineteenth-century thinker made about the system that was then still young. The biography, the path the system actually travelled, surprised him in its institutions, consoled its subjects in ways his bleakest tendency denied, multiplied the middle layers his polarisation dissolved, and equipped itself with stabilisers his crisis theory never imagined. A reader who wants a prophet will find the record disobliging. A reader who wants an anatomy, the identification of the essential relations beneath a hundred and fifty years of institutional improvisation, will find that the dissector's hand was steadier than his century, his followers, and his enemies have usually managed to say in the same sentence. The general question of the tradition's relevance, beyond the assessment of its founder's claims about capitalism itself, is taken up in [is marxism still relevant](/2022/02/15/is-marxism-still-relevant/), to which this article defers for everything except the economic core it has graded here.

The comparison basis rule, stated briefly in the opening, deserves a final elaboration here because it is the rule readers most often break when they take the assessment away with them. To say that comparisons across the centuries must concern relations rather than living standards is not to say that living standards do not matter. They matter supremely, to the people living them, and a worker choosing between centuries would not hesitate. The rule governs inference, not importance. From the fact that a modern warehouse operative eats better, lives longer, and commands more goods than a duchess of the founder's century, nothing follows about the structure of the operative's dependence, the ownership of the warehouse, or the destination of the surplus the operative's labour creates, just as from the splendour of a Roman senator's villa nothing followed about whether his wealth rested on slave labour, which it did, beneath the marble. Standards of life are the product of the productive forces, and the productive forces have grown under capitalism at a rate without precedent, a fact the founder himself celebrated in the Manifesto's most famous pages with an enthusiasm his critics rarely quote. Relations are the social form within which that growth is organised and its fruits distributed. The assessment grades the analysis of the form. Judging it by the growth is like judging a map of a river's banks by the river's speed: the speed is real, it matters enormously to everyone on the water, and it tells you nothing about whether the banks were drawn in the right place.

Armed with that rule, a reader can also see why the assessment's two most dramatic grades are not in tension, though they are often quoted as though they cancel. The worker's material life improved beyond the founder's imagining, and the relations under which the worker labours persisted beyond his critics' obituaries. Both statements are true, and each is the other's context. The improvement was achieved within the relations, fought for through organisations the relations made possible and necessary, and distributed according to the balance of forces the relations structured. The persistence was achieved through the improvement, in part, since a system that can raise its subjects' consumption while widening the distance between them and its owners has purchased a stability that naked immiseration would never have afforded it. Frameworks that cannot hold both truths together will misread the modern world in one direction or the other: the triumphalism that sees only the improvement and declares the relations dissolved into general prosperity, or the catastrophism that sees only the persistence and must therefore deny or discount the improvement its own constituents have lived. The scorecard's profile, with its refuted immiseration row sitting two lines above its confirmed relations row, is the two truths printed in a single table, and learning to read them together, without flinching from either, is the closest thing to a conclusion this article offers.

Fairness to the assessment's method requires a glance sideways, at how the rival frameworks of political economy would fare if the scorecard's instrument were turned on them with the same impartial severity, because the comparison disciplines any temptation to treat this article's mixed profile as a singular disgrace. The neoclassical mainstream, graded row by row, would present a profile no tidier: its tendency claims about the self-equilibrating market failed their encounter with the great crises as completely as any claim graded here, its representative agent and efficient market constructions have the same unfinished, measure-dependent character as relative immiseration, and its core identification, that prices coordinate a decentralised order, has survived not by falsification's gauntlet but by the same protective belt of auxiliary refinement that Lakatos described. The Keynesian tradition would show a confirmed weak form, the endogeneity of effective demand's failures, beside an unproven strong one, the long stagnation its founder's heirs periodically announce. The Austrian school would show a crisis anatomy of real penetration beside a record of dated predictions, of inflations and collapses foretold on schedule, that makes the penumbra examined in this article look restrained. None of this is whataboutery, and the assessment does not offer it as a defence of any row's grade, each of which stands on its own evidence. It is a calibration of the instrument. Frameworks for understanding a whole economy are not the kind of thing that passes or fails entire. They are the kind of thing that accumulates a profile, and the demand that Marxism produce a spotless one, as the price of being taken seriously, is a demand made of no other inhabitant of the field. The scorecard's achievement, if it has one, is to have made the profile public and corrigible. The invitation to the rival schools to publish theirs, with defeaters attached, is extended in a spirit of genuine curiosity about whether they would look much different.

There is a reason the invitation is unlikely to be accepted, and naming it clarifies what this article has actually been doing. A framework's profile is most dangerous to the framework's custodians, because the custodians' authority rests on the whole cloth: on the impression that the master saw far, that the school has preserved the sight, and that the criticisms from outside are misunderstandings of a depth the critics cannot plumb. An itemised profile dissolves that authority into a set of checkable claims, some of which the custodians lose. It transfers authority from the school to the instrument, from those who know the texts to anyone willing to consult the record. That transfer is the deepest sense in which the scorecard is a Marxist exercise in spite of its grades, since the founder's own method, at its best, was exactly such a transfer: away from the authorities of political economy, toward the inspectors' reports, the factory returns, the parliamentary papers, the evidence that any reader could in principle examine. The tradition has not always honoured that method in its dealings with its founder. It has preferred, too often, the reliquary to the laboratory. An assessment that grades the master by the master's own evidentiary standard, and prints the grades where his followers must walk past them, is not an act of disloyalty. It is the loyalty the method demands, and the only kind the founder, who spared no authority that stood between his readers and the record, would have recognised as loyalty at all.

What the assessment establishes for the working researcher can be stated as a short research agenda, and stating it converts the article's findings into instructions for its own supersession, which is the fate every honest instrument should desire. First, finish the unfinished claims. The relative immiseration row awaits a claimant willing to fix its measure, population, and span in advance and accept the grade that follows; the profit rate row awaits a measurement convention defended on principle rather than selected for its result. Second, prosecute the junctions. The framework's encounters with the fiscal state, with intangible property, and with the ecological boundary are the places where new theory is actually being made, and they reward the researcher who arrives with the scorecard's discipline rather than with a verdict in search of a province. Third, monitor the defeaters. The strata row's reversal conditions, the supersession defeater's production share, and the concentration row's deconcentration test are live empirical questions whose answers will change this article's grades, and the researcher who watches them is doing the assessment's work in its intended tense, the future. An article that grades the past correctly and equips its readers to grade the future differently has done what an assessment can do. The rest belongs to the evidence, which is still arriving.

A reader who has followed the grading this far may reasonably ask what use a private citizen of the republic of letters is supposed to make of an instrument built for a single, enormous subject. The answer is that the instrument travels. Any large framework that claims to explain a domain of social life, any school with a founder, a canon, and a century of commentary, can be taken apart in the same fashion: its assertions separated from one another, each assertion's mechanism named, the evidence for each gathered from wherever it happens to live, and a reversal condition written down before the verdict is pronounced. The exercise is laborious, which is precisely why it is so rarely performed, and why reputations in the social sciences so often rest on the unexamined average of a dozen claims of wildly different quality. Whoever performs it once, on any doctrine they were raised inside, acquires a permanent suspicion of verdicts delivered wholesale, and that suspicion, more than any particular grade in the table printed earlier, is the inheritance this hub article hopes to leave behind. Doctrines fear itemisation the way monopolies fear itemised bills: not because every line is damning, but because the total, once the lines are visible, can never again be waved through as a single impressive sum.

There is a second use, closer to home. The grades recorded here are dated, in the only sense that matters: they describe the evidence available at the moment of writing, and several of them sit near boundaries where new evidence could plausibly move them. The automation of routine cognitive labour is pressing on the intermediate occupations from a direction earlier machinery never threatened. The ecological boundary is tightening from a background condition into an active constraint on policy in every capital. The intangible economy continues to blur the line between profit earned in production and rent collected at a gate. A reader who returns to this table in a decade should expect to regrade at least one row, and the article has tried to make that regrading easy by printing, beside every judgement, the observation that would overturn it. Instruments that cannot be reused are monuments. This one is offered as a tool with a date stamp and an instruction sheet, and its authors' successors, whoever they turn out to be, are invited to be as unsparing with its grades as it has been with the founder's.

## Misreadings the scorecard corrects

An assessment conducted at this length earns the right to a short catalogue of the errors it was built to prevent, since the same five misreadings recur in nearly every popular treatment of the subject and each has been corrected, at least once, in the sections above. They are collected here in one place so that a reader who arrived with one of them can find its correction without rereading the whole.

The first misreading is that Marx made forecasts, dated predictions about the sequence and timing of capitalism's development, so that his standing rises and falls with the accuracy of a prophet. He made tendency claims about a mode of production analysed as an abstraction, refused on the record to universalise the historical path of the classic ground, and specified mechanisms rather than timetables. Grading him as a forecaster is not a harsher test than the one this article applies. It is a different test, addressed to claims he did not make, and its familiar verdicts, triumph and obituary alike, are artefacts of the substitution.

The second misreading is the mirror image of the first: that a tendency is falsified by any period in which it fails to dominate, so that a decade of fragmentation refutes concentration or a generation of rising wages refutes every claim in the family. Tendencies are pressures operating through counteracting factors, and their evidence is the long span and the aggregate drift. The scorecard has not used that doctrine as a shelter: it graded the strata claim refuted precisely because the counter-periods became the permanent structure, which is the standard a tendency claim must fear. But the standard is the long view and the settled structure, not the episode, and readers who grade by episodes will misjudge every row in both directions.

The third misreading assigns the concentration finding to the Marxist tradition, as though believing it required joining something. The finding belongs to industrial organisation economics, to competition authorities, to the business press, and to the central bank research departments that documented consolidation while pursuing questions about prices, productivity, and market power that owe Marx nothing. The tradition's role in the story is the less glamorous one of having predicted, a century and a half early, the phenomenon the finding describes. Credit for a prediction and ownership of its evidence are different things, and the assessment has been careful throughout to give the tradition only the first.

The fourth misreading treats immiseration as a single claim with a single fate, and therefore either defends the rising living standards of the advanced economies' workers as a refutation of Marx entire or cites the widening distance between wages and wealth as his vindication entire. The claim is two claims. The absolute one failed. The relative one survives in a form that must specify its measure before it can be graded, and the specification, not the slogan, is where honest argument about it now lives.

The fifth misreading holds that the intermediate strata question has been answered, in either direction, with a finality that excuses further attention. It has been answered against the disappearance expectation, decisively, and the answer's consequences are still unfolding, because the strata whose persistence refuted the expectation are themselves being reorganised by the newest rounds of automation, credential inflation, and platform absorption. The scorecard's grade records the past tense of the question. Its defeater, printed with the row, keeps the future tense open, and the distinction between those tenses is the whole difference between an assessment and an epitaph.

Three further misreadings, subtler than the five already corrected, complete the catalogue, and they are worth correcting because they survive even in readers who have absorbed the scorecard's method. The first treats confirmation of the essential relations as confirmation of the politics historically attached to them, as though the persistence of the wage relation licensed any particular strategy, party, or prophecy that has marched under the framework's banner. It does not. The relations' persistence is a finding concerning how production is organised. What follows politically depends on arguments about agency, organisation, and circumstance that the founder's economic analysis underdetermines and that history has answered in ways no scorecard of tendencies can settle. The second misreading runs in reverse: it treats the refuted rows as a licence to discard the confirmed ones, as though a framework were a chain that fails at its weakest link. Frameworks are not chains. They are bundles of claims sharing a core, and the bundle here has been unbundled in public, row by row, precisely so that no reader need perform that all-or-nothing operation again. The third misreading is the most scholarly and therefore the most dangerous: it treats the abstraction's survival as a reason to stop testing it, to convert the scorecard's central judgement from a finding into a premise, and to read the modern economy through the relations rather than reading the relations against the modern economy. The defeater fastened to this article's central judgement is the standing refutation of that posture. The relations hold, on the evidence, for now, under stated conditions of reversal. A finding with an expiry mechanism is a tool. A finding without one is a creed, and the difference between the two is the difference between the Marx this article has assessed and the Marx of the monuments, who turns out, on inspection of the record, to be two different authors sharing a name.

A sixth misreading, so common in the classroom that it deserves its own correction, holds that the assessment's mixed profile must reflect a thinker of mixed ability, brilliant on Monday and confused on Friday, and that the student's task is to sort the gold from the dross by quality of insight. The profile's actual pattern is more interesting and more instructive. The confirmations cluster where the founder's method was at its most distinctive: in the analysis of the relations themselves and of the pressures that operate continuously and impersonally, through every agent's local rationality, whether or not any agent intends them. The failures cluster where the method was abandoned for the pleasures of extrapolation: where a direction observed in one population, the independent producers, was projected across populations the analysis had not examined, or where a moral charge, the deepening misery of the labourer, was allowed to stand in for a mechanism that the evidence of the founder's own factory reports should have complicated. The lesson for the student is therefore not that genius is uneven. It is that method is everything: the same mind, applying its anatomy of relations, produced claims that a century of hostile scrutiny has corroborated, and departing from that anatomy for the satisfactions of prophecy, produced the claims the same scrutiny buried. A reader who learns to tell, in any text of the tradition, which of the two operations is being performed has acquired the single most valuable skill this article can teach, worth more than any of its individual grades, because the grades will be revised and the skill will still be working.

## Where to start on Marx and the modern economy

A hub article owes its readers a map of the territory it has surveyed, and the survey is now complete enough for the map to be drawn. Readers whose interest is the founder's own architecture should begin from the analysis of the commodity and the wage relation and proceed to the accumulation chapters, where the tendencies graded here are derived rather than merely announced, and should read the third volume's treatment of credit and of the profit rate's tendency with the scorecard's cautions in hand. The reader whose interest is the evidence should begin from the independent literatures this article has privileged: the industrial organisation work on market structure for the concentration row, the labour statistics and development literature for the wage relation's extent, and the stratification sociology for the middle strata's stubborn career. A reader drawn to the living frontier should proceed directly to this cluster's companion articles, each of which takes one modern form and subjects it to the full treatment a hub can only summarise: [platform capitalism marxist analysis](/2020/11/15/platform-capitalism-marxist-analysis/) for the digital estates and their rents, [financialisation marxist theory](/2020/12/01/financialisation-marxist-theory/) for the credit machinery's modern scale, and [automation ai and marxism](/2020/12/15/automation-ai-and-marxism/) for the displacement question on which the post-work supersession thesis stands or falls. The reader preparing for assessment should turn to the persona sections immediately following, which are written for the examination room rather than the library, and to the exam guide that closes this cluster, where the scorecard's method is converted into a technique for answering questions under time pressure.

One caution governs all four routes, and it is the caution with which the article began. The literatures named here do not agree with one another, and several of them do not agree that the questions they answer are Marx's questions at all. That disagreement is not an obstacle to the assessment. It is the assessment's raw material, since a tendency documented by its enemies and a disconfirmation documented by its friends are worth more, combined, than any single school could produce by examining itself. Read across the lines, grade row by row, and let no verdict, including this article's, do the work that the evidence was assembled to do.

For the reader who intends to work through the primary texts rather than the commentary, a brief itinerary may save some wandering, since the founder's writings on the questions this article grades are scattered across forty years of journalism, notebooks, and unfinished construction. Begin with the Manifesto, not because it is the deepest text but because it is the one where the tendencies appear in their most prophetic dress, polarisation included, and a reader who has seen the dress can thereafter recognise the claims when they reappear in analytical clothing. Proceed to the pamphlet on wage labour and capital, where the wage relation receives its clearest short exposition, and then to the first volume of the major work, reading the chapters on the working day, on machinery, and on accumulation's general law with the scorecard open beside them, since those are the chapters where the crisis mechanism, the reserve army, and the concentration tendency are built rather than announced. The third volume, in its posthumous form, supplies the profit rate law and the credit analysis, and should be read with the editorial cautions this article has entered. The notebooks of the late eighteen fifties, available in full translation, supply the fragment on machines and the widest view of the system's world-market horizon. Alongside the founder, read his strongest critics in their strongest forms: the falsifiability charge in its author's own statement, the post-industrial thesis in its author's, the theorem against the profit law in a competent exposition. A scorecard is only as good as the opposition it has survived, and the reader who has met the opposition at full strength owns the grades in a way no summary can confer. The exam guide closing this cluster assumes exactly this itinerary and builds its answering technique upon it, which is why the routing at this article's close points there last.

The hub's routing has one final obligation, to the reader who came with a practical question rather than a scholarly one: the citizen, the worker, the voter who wants to know what any of this means for the judgements they must actually make. The assessment's answer is deliberately modest, because modesty is what its evidence supports. It does not tell that reader which party to join or which policy to favour, and any article on this subject that claims to derive such instructions from a scorecard of tendencies is selling something the evidence did not buy. What it offers is a set of calibrated expectations. Expect consolidation, and be suspicious of arrangements that assume competition will police itself. Expect the wage relation to persist through the next technological wave, whatever its costume, and judge proposals by what they do to the terms on which labour is sold rather than by their announcements of a world beyond work. Expect crises, and judge institutions by their fire brigades rather than by their promises that fire has been abolished. Expect the middle to remain the decisive constituency and to be courted, flattered, and divided accordingly. And expect every announcement that the old relations have been transcended to be, in the first instance, a description of their newest costume, until the production statistics say otherwise. A citizen armed with those expectations will be surprised by modern capitalism less often than the readers of either the vindication or the obituary literature, and being surprised less often, in a system whose surprises are rarely pleasant, is no small inheritance from a nineteenth-century anatomist.

One practical counsel remains for the reader who studies this subject in order to write about it, whether in an examination, a seminar paper, or the pages of a journal. Borrow the table's shape before borrowing any of its conclusions. The shape is a discipline of fairness that examiners and editors can detect within a paragraph: assertions kept apart, mechanisms named before evidence is offered, hostile witnesses given the floor, and every verdict accompanied by the observation that would reverse it. A piece of writing built that way can reach a conclusion its reader detests and still command that reader's respect, because the route to the conclusion is visible and each step can be challenged on its merits. Conversely, a piece that reaches the reader's own conclusion by the wholesale route, by atmosphere and affiliation, persuades nobody who was not persuaded already. The founder understood this in his practice if not always in his polemics: his masterpiece persuades across the centuries not because its readers share its hopes but because its evidence is laid out in inspectors' reports, wage books, and mortality tables that any sceptic may examine. Writing about him in his own evidentiary manner, grading his claims by the standards his best work exemplifies, is the sincerest form of the attention this series exists to encourage, and it is available to every reader regardless of the verdict they finally reach.

## For students and exam candidates

The scorecard is, among other things, an examination instrument, and this section converts it into one explicitly. Questions on this topic reward the candidate who itemises and punish the candidate who verdicts. ## For teachers and seminar leaders

The abstraction point is the teaching gift of this article, because it reframes the relevance question in a form students can actually argue about. Seminars on whether Marx was right decay reliably into exchanges of allegiance, since the question as posed invites a verdict and verdicts invite camps. Pose instead the anatomist's question: which relations did the abstraction specify, and does the economy in front of us still run on them? The spread of answers is the profile, drawn by the class itself.

The working value of this assessment for researchers lies in its routing: every row names the literature where the row's evidence actually lives, and those literatures rarely cite one another. The industrial organisation scholar documenting consolidation, the labour statistician mapping the wage relation's extent, the stratification sociologist charting the middle's expansion, and the historian of central banking are studying one system from four watchtowers, and the scorecard is offered as a rare plan of the whole terrain on which all four towers stand. Journalists will find the concentration confirmation the most usable single item in the article, precisely because of its provenance: a structural tendency of the modern economy, predicted by the nineteenth century's most contested economist, documented into the record by the research departments of institutions that have no interest in his reputation. Stories about consolidation are usually written as stories about particular firms, particular mergers, particular regulators asleep or awake. The scorecard supplies the longer frame: a pressure that operates through hostile policy and indifferent policy alike, whose defeater can be stated and whose absence from the public argument says more about the argument than about the pressure. Historians, finally, are invited to notice the assessment's asymmetry as a periodisation tool. It is the record of a logic being improvised upon, and the scorecard, with its rows graded at different levels and its surprises filed at the level of circumstance, is one attempt to keep both halves of that sentence in view at once.

## The whole assessment on one page

For the reader who has arrived at the end, or who began here, the profile can be compressed into the compass of a single screen without betraying the method that produced it, provided the compression keeps the grades separate and the defeaters attached. His object was a mode of production grasped in abstraction, not one economy at one date and not a timetable of events. Judged at that level, his identification of the system's essential relations, production for exchange, the sale of labour power, and accumulation as the governing compulsion, remains correct: those relations still organise production across the planet, on evidence drawn from the daily records of statistical offices rather than from his followers' testimony. Judged at the level of his named dynamics, the record divides sharply. Capital's concentration and centralisation stand strongly confirmed, documented by market-structure research with no stake in his reputation, through eras of policy hostile to consolidation. The extension of wage labour across the globe is confirmed at a scale he could not have surveyed. Crisis is confirmed as the system's endogenous rhythm and unproven as its terminal illness. Absolute immiseration is refuted by the rising material life of the wage-earning population. Relative immiseration is contested because its statement never fixed its measure. The falling rate of profit is contested root and branch, in derivation and in measurement alike. The intermediate strata's predicted disappearance is refuted outright, the middle having expanded into the demographic centre of the societies where its extinction was forecast. Weighed for institutional foresight, the framework is thin where the modern order is most elaborately built: its fiscal machinery, its managed money, its intangible property regime, its markets for care, and the ecological boundary were all supplied by history rather than foreseen by the analysis. The supersession theses, that a successor order has already replaced the relations, were heard at full strength and found to describe real transformations administered within the relations they announce as dead. The unfalsifiability objection was conceded where it lands, against the unfinished claims and the tradition's protective habits, and answered where it overreaches, against the claims that have demonstrably won and demonstrably lost. The central judgement stands with its defeater attached: the essential relations still obtain, and they would cease to be credited here if production shifted, on a substantial and growing scale, beyond wage labour and beyond exchange. Nothing in the record surveyed shows that migration. Everything in the record shows the relations absorbing a century and a half of improvisation and persisting, recognisably, as themselves.

Compressed assessments invite a final abuse, and the article closes its summary by refusing it in advance. The profile printed above will be quoted, in the wild, as a verdict with a percentage attached, as though the rows could be averaged into a mark out of eight and the mark announced as the verdict on the relevance question. The refusal is already on record in the scorecard's design: the rows carry unequal weight, the foundational claim outweighing any three tendencies, and the contested rows are not half-marks but open files. Anyone quoting a fraction from this table has not read it. What the table supports, and all it supports, is the sentence with which this article's own argument began and to which every section has been a footnote: the answer differs by claim. A reader who carries that sentence away, with the profile in hand to give it content and the defeaters in hand to give it discipline, carries away everything the assessment has to give. The rest, the application of the profile to the questions the reader actually faces, in the examination hall, the seminar, the newsroom, or the polling booth, is work the article has equipped and cannot perform, and it leaves that work, with respect, where it has always belonged: with the reader.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What did Marx get right about modern capitalism?

Most clearly, he identified how capital concentrates and centralises, production passing steadily toward fewer and larger units, a drift independent research in industrial organisation has documented across the advanced economies. He also identified wage labour's spread across the globe, the recurrence of crisis as a product of the system's own dynamics, and above all the essential relations themselves: exchange production generalised, labour power as a commodity for sale, and accumulation governing investment. His successes are specific and separable, and each rests on its own evidence rather than on the reputation of the whole.

### Q: What is the strongest disconfirmation of Marx's tendencies?

The failure of the intermediate strata to disappear. Marx expected the layers between capital and labour, the small producers, shopkeepers, independent professionals, and salaried employees, to be ground away as development proceeded, simplifying society toward two great camps. Instead the intermediate occupations expanded enormously: managers, technicians, credentialed professionals, public administrators, and a perpetually renewed population of small enterprises became the demographic centre of advanced societies. The disconfirmation is the strongest available because the claim was stated precisely, its mechanism was specified, the evidence against it is demographic and unambiguous, and no redefinition of class boundaries restores the original expectation.

### Q: Did Marx analyse a forecast or an abstraction?

An abstraction. His object was the capitalist mode of production considered in its essential relations, production generalised for exchange, the sale of labour power, and accumulation as the governing compulsion, not the economy of any particular country at any particular date. A forecast specifies events and times and is refuted when they fail to arrive. An abstraction specifies relations and pressures and is tested by asking whether production is still organised by those relations and whether the pressures still operate through particular circumstances. Marx refused to convert his account of the classic ground into a universal itinerary for all nations, which is the behaviour of an anatomist rather than a prophet.

### Q: What did Marx not anticipate about capitalism?

He did not anticipate the scale of the modern state as a fiscal, monetary, and regulatory participant inside the economy, nor central banking developed into a standing instrument of systemic management able to intervene directly in the crisis mechanism. He did not anticipate intellectual property becoming a distinct and commanding form of property with its own rent-like income, nor the fine-grained unbundling of production across global supply chains under unified command. His framework also undervalued the unwaged labour that reproduces the workforce on which the wage relation rests, and it contains no principle for the ecological limits that accumulation now encounters.

### Q: Has market concentration risen as Marx expected?

Yes, in direction, breadth, and duration: the leading firms command growing shares in most branches of the advanced economies, a pattern documented over long spans by industrial organisation economists, competition authorities, and central bank researchers working for purposes unconnected to Marxism. The tendency operated through eras of hostile antitrust policy as well as permissive ones, which marks it as a structural pressure rather than a policy artefact. Local fragmentation, vigorous entry in particular niches, and the profusion of small dependent firms qualify the picture without reversing it, since the claim concerns command over output and investment rather than the count of enterprises.

### Q: Is a tendency testable as stated?

Some tendencies are and some are not, and distinguishing them is the beginning of honest assessment. A tendency is testable when it specifies a direction, a mechanism, and the population it describes, as the concentration and intermediate strata claims do; both were graded, in opposite directions, on exactly that basis. A tendency resists testing when its statement leaves the measure unfixed, as with relative immiseration, or when no criterion distinguishes its strong form from its weak one, as with terminal crisis. The remedy is not to abandon tendency claims but to complete their statements before grading them.

### Q: Has the wage relation weakened or extended?

It has extended, on every long measure available. Wage and salaried employment now dominates regions once organised by subsistence farming, artisan production, and customary obligation, as agrarian economies integrated into global production and as populations already near the relation, notably women maintained within breadwinner households, entered direct wage dependence themselves. Apparent counter-examples dissolve on inspection: platform contractors sell labour power under contractual labels that disguise subordination, state transfers presuppose wage dependence as the norm they cushion, and informal work is the relation wearing its least protected historical clothing rather than an alternative to it.

### Q: Do the essential capitalist relations still hold?

Yes, on the evidence surveyed in this article. Production is still organised overwhelmingly for exchange rather than direct use. The great majority of the world's producers still access the means of life by selling their capacity to work to employers who own the conditions of production. Investment is still governed by the compulsion to accumulate, and institutions from the welfare state to the central bank administer those relations without displacing them. The judgement carries its defeater: it would fall if production moved beyond wage labour and beyond exchange at a scale both substantial and growing, a migration no supersession thesis has yet established.

### Q: Why did the intermediate strata not disappear?

Because the system generates intermediate positions, as a side effect of its own growth, faster than competition eliminates the old ones. Concentration creates administration, and administration is salaried management. Science applied to production creates technical and specialist occupations. The expanding state creates public employment across education, health, and welfare. Complex circulation creates coordinators in finance, law, logistics, and marketing. Small property survived alongside, sustained by niches the large firms prefer to purchase rather than perform, by subcontracting chains, and by perpetual new founding. The expectation failed because it extrapolated that producer's fate while missing the strata-generating fertility of the tendencies themselves.

### Q: Where should you start on Marx and the modern economy?

Start from the claims rather than the verdicts. Read the chapters on accumulation, where the tendencies are derived rather than merely announced, then test each against the independent literatures that gathered its evidence: industrial organisation research for concentration, labour statistics and development studies for the wage relation, stratification sociology for the middle strata. Within this series, the companion articles on platform capitalism, financialisation, and automation treat the modern forms in depth, the crisis theory guide handles the crisis claim, and the exam guide that closes the cluster converts the scorecard method into an answering technique for assessed work on the topic.

### Q: How does the concentration confirmation grade the tendency test?

It shows what a tendency claim looks like when it passes the test completely. The claim arrived fully specified, its mechanism of competition working through credit announced in advance, the span across which it operated far exceeds anything its author surveyed, and the confirming evidence was assembled by researchers with no commitment to the framework, much of it gathered for regulatory and scholarly purposes of their own. Against that standard, other rows can be honestly graded: the strata claim fails the same test just as completely, while the profit rate claim cannot yet be administered the test at all.

### Q: What should a student separate between a tendency and a forecast?

A forecast promises that specified events will occur by specified times, and it stands or falls with the calendar. A tendency identifies a pressure generated by a system's essential relations, operating through counteracting factors that can delay, divert, or mask it, and it is judged by the long-run drift of the aggregates it describes. The student should therefore never ask whether a tendency happened this decade, but whether the pressure is detectable across spans long enough for chance to cancel itself out, and whether the periods of its masking remain episodes or have hardened into the permanent structure.

### Q: Why is concentration the clearest confirmation of the tendencies?

Because it meets, in combination, every test a tendency claim can be set. It was central to the original analysis rather than peripheral. Its mechanism was derived rather than asserted. Its operation persisted through every institutional arrangement the system subsequently adopted, including extended periods of legislation aimed directly at preventing it. And its documentation comes from the tradition's indifferent or hostile witnesses, the market structure literature of mainstream economics, whose findings cannot be discounted as the testimony of believers. A confirmation assembled by one's opponents, against their own policy efforts, over a span of generations, is the strongest currency social science mints.

### Q: How does the tendency scorecard order the predictions?

It does not rank them by importance but lays them out as a profile, each row graded on its own mechanism and evidence. Concentration and the extension of the wage relation stand confirmed. Crisis stands confirmed weakly and unproven in its strong form. Absolute immiseration stands not confirmed, and so does the intermediate strata's disappearance. Relative immiseration and the falling rate of profit stand contested, the first for want of a fixed measure and the second at every level from derivation to data. The essential relations' persistence is recorded separately, as the framework's foundational claim rather than one tendency among others.

### Q: What separates a tendency that failed from a framework that failed?

A tendency fails when its specified direction is contradicted by the long-run record, as happened when the middle layers refused to vanish, and the failure is local: it damages the doctrines built on that claim while leaving neighbouring claims, resting on different mechanisms, untouched. A framework fails when its foundational identification collapses, when the relations it specifies cease to organise the world it describes, which for this framework would mean production shifting, at scale, beyond wage labour and beyond exchange. The distinction matters because polemics habitually convert every failed tendency into a failed framework, and every confirmed one into a vindicated system.
