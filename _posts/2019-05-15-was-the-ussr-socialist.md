Was the USSR socialist? The honest answer arrives in two parts, because the question conceals two different tests and the Soviet system passes one while failing the other. Apply the test of property form, asking who held legal title to factories, land, mines and banks, and the system was plainly not capitalist: private ownership of the means of production had been abolished, no capitalist class drew income from property, and allocation ran through a plan rather than a market. Apply instead the test of producer control, asking whether the people who worked in those factories decided what was made, how, and to what end, while the system was, just as plainly, not socialist on any account its defenders or its critics have produced: a party and state apparatus decided, and producers obeyed. Every serious position in the long argument over this question can be located by watching which test it treats as decisive, and the five positions mapped in this article divide, at bottom, over that single choice.

![A seminar table with the five positions grid, annotated criteria cards and a heavily marked definition of socialism, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

One discipline governs everything that follows, and stating it at the outset prevents most of the confusion the question usually generates. A definitional question is settled by criteria, not by verdicts on the record. The Soviet record contains achievements its defenders cite and catastrophes its critics cite, and both bodies of material are real; neither settles what the system should be called, because a name follows from a definition applied to a structure, not from approval or horror at outcomes. A system could be socialist and badly run, or not socialist and in some respects effective, and the argument of this article proceeds throughout on the structural evidence: property relations, the position of producers, the mechanism of allocation, and the character of the stratum that ruled. Where the record enters, it enters as evidence about structure, never as a substitute for the definitional work.

A second preliminary matters almost as much. Serious Marxists have held every available position on this question, including the affirmative one, and the affirmative position is not reducible to official apologetics recited by functionaries. It was argued by economists of standing, defended in print against hostile critics, and held by people who knew the system's coercive character from the inside of the argument rather than from ignorance of it. Any treatment that begins by assuming only one answer was ever honestly available has stopped doing analysis before it starts. The positions are therefore given here in their proponents' own terms, each attached to named writers and identified works, and each is allowed its full strength before any of them is weighed. The weighing, when it comes, turns on a text: Marx's own formulation of what socialism is, which proves to be the hardest obstacle the affirmative case has to clear.

## Was the USSR socialist: the question stated precisely

Stripped of polemic, the question asks whether a particular configuration satisfies a definition. The configuration can be described without controversy in its main lines. The means of production were owned by the state, with a secondary cooperative and collective farm sector holding a subordinate place. A central plan, expressed through ministries and planning organs, set output targets and allocated materials, labour and investment. Private capital, in the sense of privately owned productive assets yielding income to their owners, did not exist as a legal or economic category. Employment was universal as a matter of policy and of constitutional promise. Provision of housing, health care, education and basic foodstuffs ran substantially through collective and subsidised channels rather than through purchase at market prices. Against that description stands a second one, equally factual. Producers exercised no control over the enterprises in which they worked: managers were appointed from above, production targets arrived from above, and independent organisations of workers were not permitted. A single party held a constitutional monopoly of political power, and its apparatus, fused with the state administration, directed the economy and staffed its commanding posts.

The definitional dispute begins because the word socialism, inside the Marxist tradition that alone gives this question its sharpness, carries more than one criterion within it. One strand of the tradition defines the system by its property relations: socialism begins when the means of production pass out of private hands into social ownership, and the Soviet Union had performed that passage completely and irreversibly by the early nineteen thirties. Another strand defines the system by the position of the producers within it: socialism is the rule of the associated producers over their own labour and its products, and by that standard a system in which producers confronted production decisions as commands from a separate apparatus had not arrived at socialism whatever its property forms proclaimed. Both strands cite Marx. Both can quote chapter and verse. The argument between the five positions is, in large part, an argument about which strand carries the definition and which merely describes a hoped for consequence.

Precision about the question also requires precision about its scope. The question is not whether the Soviet Union was a good society, a successful economy, a faithful embodiment of anyone's hopes, or a refutation of Marxism as such. Each of those is a separate inquiry with its own evidence, and several of them are pursued elsewhere in this series. Nor is the question whether the Soviet state called itself socialist: it did, constitutionally and continuously, and a state's self description is a fact about the state rather than an answer about the system. The question is narrower and harder. Given what the Marxist tradition itself specifies as the marks of a socialist order, did the Soviet configuration exhibit those marks, and where it exhibited some and not others, which marks are constitutive and which are incidental? Everything in the pages that follow is an attempt to keep that question in focus while five different answers compete for it.

### Why does this question generate more heat than comparable definitional disputes?

Because the label carries the fate of a tradition with it. If the Soviet system was socialist, then socialism's historical record includes that system's record, with everything the record contains; if it was not, then the tradition's central experiment was never performed, and its claims remain untested rather than refuted. The stakes explain the temperature.

Heat, however, is a poor instrument for settling definitions, and the temperature of this dispute has repeatedly damaged the quality of its conduct. Participants have treated the classification as a loyalty test, so that conceding a point of structure felt like defecting to an enemy camp. They have also treated it as a verdict on persons, so that the classification of a system became an accusation against everyone who had lived under it or defended any part of it. And they have treated it as a proxy for every other dispute in the Marxist tradition, loading a question about property and control with the whole accumulated grievance of schisms going back generations. The remedy available to a reader is procedural rather than emotional: fix the criteria first, apply them in the open, and let the classification fall where the application puts it. That procedure is what the next sections perform, slowly and in the open, so that a reader who rejects the conclusion can identify the exact step at which the disagreement begins.

There is a further reason the dispute resists settlement, and it is internal to Marxism itself. The tradition never produced a canonical checklist for recognising a socialist society, because Marx wrote sparingly about the future order and wrote about it at a high level of abstraction. His remarks on the society that would succeed capitalism are scattered across works written for other purposes: a chapter on commodity fetishism, a set of marginal notes on a party programme, an account of a Parisian insurrection, letters and drafts composed late and published later still. Each position in the debate assembles its definition from this scattered material, weighting the fragments differently, and each can therefore claim textual warrant. The debate is not between readers of Marx and ignorers of Marx. It is between rival assemblies of the same fragments, which is why it has lasted a century and why the criterion question, rather than any new fact about the Soviet Union, remains the live issue.

## Two criteria, two answers: the shape of the whole debate

### Was the Soviet Union socialist?

it depends on the criterion. On property form it was not capitalist: means of production were state-owned and there was no private capitalist class. On producer control it was not socialist, and Marx's own definition of socialism as an association of free producers makes that criterion difficult for the affirmative case.

That short answer contains the entire architecture of the debate, and the remainder of this article is an elaboration of its two clauses. Consider the first clause slowly. Capitalism, in the Marxist definition that all parties to this dispute accept, is a system in which the means of production are privately owned, labour power is bought and sold as a commodity, production is undertaken for profit, and a class of owners appropriates the surplus produced by a class of non owners. Measured against that definition, the Soviet system diverged at every point of form. Ownership was state and cooperative, not private. Accumulation was directed by plan rather than by the profit seeking of competing capitals. No legally recognised class lived from property income. Whatever the Soviet Union was, classifying it as capitalism in the strict definitional sense requires stretching the category until its defining relations disappear, and the positions that do call it capitalist are obliged, as their own authors recognised, to rebuild the category around function rather than form. That rebuilding is examined in its place below; here the point is only that the property criterion, applied straightforwardly, returns a clear verdict of not capitalist.

Consider the second clause with equal slowness. Socialism, in the formulation Marx himself gave when he described the successor society positively rather than polemically, is an association of free producers who regulate their production consciously and in common, standing in control of their own social process rather than governed by it as by an alien power. Measured against that formulation, the Soviet system diverged at the decisive point. Producers did not regulate production. They did not choose its aims, its methods, its pace or its distribution. They confronted the plan as an external command, the manager as an appointee of a hierarchy they did not elect and could not remove, and the state as a power standing over society rather than as society's own organ. No account of the system, friendly or hostile, claims otherwise: the absence of producer control is the one structural fact in this entire dispute that nobody contests. The affirmative case therefore cannot rest on Marx's formulation. It must argue that the formulation is not the definition, that property form is the definition, and that producer control is a later flower of socialist development rather than its root. Whether that argument succeeds is the question on which the whole debate finally turns.

Between the two clauses lies the space the five positions occupy. A reader who treats property form as the whole definition answers yes without hesitation. A reader who treats producer control as constitutive answers no with equal confidence, and then faces the follow up question of what the system positively was, a question the negative answer alone does not settle. The remaining positions are attempts to answer that follow up: a workers' state deformed by its bureaucracy, a capitalism disguised by its state form, a new mode of production requiring a new name, or a formation in transition that never consolidated into any mode at all. Each attempt fastens on a genuine feature of the system, and each pays a price for its fastening, usually in the form of some other feature it must explain away. The grid that organises them appears after the criteria have been examined in detail, because the grid's rows are unintelligible until the reader knows what each position is measuring.

It is worth pausing on the oddity that the two criteria can diverge at all. In the expectation of the classical tradition, they were supposed to arrive together. Social ownership was expected to be the form that producer control took: the associated producers would hold the means of production in common precisely because they had taken control of them, and the two descriptions would name one condition from two sides. The Soviet experience drove a wedge between the descriptions by producing the form without the control, ownership socialised in law while command remained concentrated in an apparatus. That wedge is the genuinely novel historical datum in the whole affair, the thing the nineteenth century theory had not envisaged in this combination, and it is why a question that looks verbal is in fact substantive. Classifying the Soviet Union requires deciding what socialism essentially is, and the decision cannot be dodged by pointing at the property register, because the register is exactly what is in dispute as a criterion.

## The property-form criterion: what it inspects and what it finds

### What does the property-form criterion actually inspect?

It inspects legal and effective title to productive assets: who owns factories, land, mines, banks and transport, in whose name, and with what rights of use, transfer and income. Where title is social and no private owner draws revenue from assets, the criterion returns a socialist or at least a non capitalist verdict.

The property criterion has deep roots in the tradition's way of periodising history. Modes of production, in the classical scheme, are distinguished first by their property relations: slavery by ownership of persons, feudalism by conditional tenure of land, capitalism by private ownership of productive assets confronting free labour. A transition between modes is marked, in the first instance, by a transformation of property. On that scheme the Russian Revolution performed a transformation as thorough as any in the record. The decrees of its first months nationalised land and banks; the following years brought industry, transport and foreign trade under state ownership; collectivisation at the end of the nineteen twenties absorbed the peasant smallholding sector into collective and state farms; and the constitution of 1936 declared the resulting structure socialist in form, describing the economic foundation of the country as the socialist system of economy and the socialist ownership of the instruments and means of production. Private trade survived at the margins, in collective farm markets and personal plots, but as an enclave tolerated within a structure whose commanding heights were socially owned beyond serious dispute.

The strength of the property criterion is its objectivity. Title can be inspected. Ownership registers, constitutional provisions, the legal categories of an economy, the presence or absence of a stock exchange, of rentier income, of inheritable productive wealth: these are checkable features, and on every one of them the Soviet system scored as its defenders claimed. No investigator, however hostile, has produced evidence of a hidden private ownership beneath the state form, of officials holding factories as personal assets in law, or of a market in productive property operating behind the plan. Critics of the affirmative case therefore rarely contest the property facts. They contest their sufficiency. The dispute is not about what the property relations were; it is about whether property relations, alone, define a mode of production for the purposes of the socialist label. That is a theoretical dispute, and it has to be fought on theoretical ground, with the texts of the tradition as the contested territory.

Against the criterion's objectivity stands a difficulty its defenders have never fully answered. Property in the Marxist analysis was never merely a legal relation. Marx's treatment of ownership in his mature work insists that legal title is the juridical expression of relations of production, and that the relations themselves consist in the effective disposition over means of production and over the labour of others: who decides, who appropriates, who is excluded. A change of title that left effective disposition untouched would count, on that analysis, as a change of form rather than of relation. The critics' move is to apply exactly that distinction to the Soviet case: the titles changed utterly in 1917 and after, while effective disposition over production passed not to the producers but to a state apparatus that disposed of means of production and of labour with a completeness no board of directors ever enjoyed. Defenders reply that disposition by a state that owns in society's name is social disposition, whatever its political defects. The reply and the counter reply define the trench lines along which the whole debate has been fought, and a reader should notice that both sides are here arguing from within Marx's own conceptual scheme, one side stressing the juridical moment of property and the other its effective moment.

A second difficulty concerns the direction of travel. Property forms, on the affirmative reading, were not only socialist in themselves; they were supposed to be the foundation on which every other socialist characteristic would in time be built, including eventually the self government of producers. The difficulty is that a foundation which persists for the entire life of a system, without the expected structure rising on it, invites the question whether the architectural metaphor was ever sound. If producer control is a consequence that socialised property reliably produces, its absence across seven decades requires explanation, and the explanations offered, encirclement, backwardness, war, the cultural level of the population, are examined in later sections. If, alternatively, producer control is not a consequence but a constituent, part of what the word socialism means from the first day, then the foundation metaphor collapses and the property achievement, however real, was the achievement of something else. The criterion dispute is thus also a dispute about the internal structure of the concept: whether socialism names a single condition with several aspects, or a sequence of stages in which the aspects arrive in a fixed order with property first.

## The producer-control criterion: its inspection and its verdict

### What does the producer-control criterion actually inspect?

It inspects the locus of decisions about production: who sets aims, chooses methods, fixes pace, appoints and removes managers, and disposes of the surplus. Where those decisions belong to producers themselves, organised in their own associations, the criterion returns a socialist verdict; where they belong to a separate apparatus, it does not.

The control criterion draws its warrant from the strand of Marx's writing concerned with alienation and emancipation rather than with periodisation. In that strand, the evil of capitalism is not exhausted by private ownership; it consists in the domination of producers by their own product, the confrontation of the worker with the conditions of labour as an alien power, and the reduction of the labourer to an appendage of a process directed elsewhere. Emancipation, correspondingly, is not exhausted by a change of owners. It requires the producers to become, in the phrase the tradition repeats, masters of their own social movement: associated individuals appropriating the productive forces they have created and regulating their interchange with nature rationally and in common. A system that changed the owners while leaving the producers as directed appendages would, on this strand, have changed the personnel of domination rather than abolishing it. The control criterion simply operationalises that judgement. It asks whether the people who produce decide, and it accepts no answer in terms of who holds title on their behalf.

Applied to the Soviet Union, the criterion's inspection moves through the institutions in turn. The factory was governed by one man management, a principle adopted early, defended by Lenin himself as a necessity of large scale industry, and never subsequently reversed: the director was appointed by the ministry or trust above him, answered upward, and wielded authority over hiring, discipline, pace and the internal organisation of work. The trade unions, after the debates of the revolution's first years were settled against the syndicalist and opposition currents, functioned as transmission belts for production policy and administrators of welfare, not as organs through which producers governed production; strikes were not legally recognised as a normal instrument, and independent union organisation outside the official structure was not permitted. The soviets, which in 1917 had been the institutional form of producer and popular power, were absorbed into the state hierarchy and emptied of independent decision long before the period usually examined. The plan itself, the system's supreme economic act, was composed in planning organs and ministries, negotiated among institutions of the apparatus, and reached the enterprises as targets they were bound to fulfil. At no point in this chain did producers, as producers, decide anything of consequence about production. The verdict of the control criterion is therefore not a close call. It is the single most one sided result in the entire investigation, and every position in the debate, including the affirmative, either concedes it or changes the subject to the property register.

Defenders of the affirmative case have three replies to this inspection, and fairness requires stating them in their strongest forms. The first reply is developmental: control by producers is an achievement of a mature socialism, not a precondition of an immature one, and a backward country industrialising under siege could not have begun with it. The second reply is representative: the party and state, on the official theory, were the producers' own organs in political form, so that control exercised through them was control by the class in mediated shape, as a shareholder's control is mediated by a board. The third reply is sceptical: no large scale economy anywhere has exhibited direct producer control over the whole of production, so a criterion that nothing satisfies is a criterion that classifies nothing, and the tradition should prefer tests that discriminate among real systems. Each reply has force, and each is taken up again when the affirmative case is stated in full and answered. Here it is enough to mark the shape of the counter argument that this article's central claim will press: the developmental reply concedes that the criterion is genuine while pleading time; the representative reply must explain why the mediation never transmitted a single decision upward against the apparatus's preference; and the sceptical reply proves too much, since a definition is not refuted by the scarcity of its instances. A definition of socialism that no existing system met would be an embarrassment for the tradition's hopes. It would not be a logical defect in the definition.

The two criteria also differ in their relation to evidence, and the difference matters for how the debate is conducted. Property can be photographed, so to speak, in a statute book. Control must be inferred from the texture of institutions: appointment procedures, the direction of accountability, the fate of dissenting organisations, the actual pathway of a decision from conception to execution. Critics who press the control criterion are therefore obliged to do institutional history, and the best of them did, reconstructing how enterprises were actually governed and how the plan was actually made. Defenders who rest on property can rest on documents of a more public kind. This asymmetry has shaped the literature's tone, the affirmative side sounding legal and declarative, the critical side sounding sociological and investigative, and readers should be alert to the possibility that each side's preferred evidence is the evidence its criterion makes visible. A complete treatment needs both kinds, which is why the tests section below works through the institutional evidence test by test rather than resting on either register alone.

## Further criteria in circulation: planning, provision, and equality

The two principal criteria do not exhaust the definitional resources the tradition and its neighbours have brought to the question. Three further tests circulate widely, and a reader who knows them can diagnose most popular versions of the argument, which typically proceed by silently selecting whichever test favours the verdict the writer already holds. The first is the allocation test: socialism as production coordinated by a conscious plan rather than by the anarchy of the market. The second is the provision test: socialism as the collective guarantee of livelihood, full employment, universal services and security against the hazards of life. The third is the equality test: socialism as a distribution markedly more equal than capitalism's, tending toward distribution according to need. Each test returns a different verdict on the Soviet case, and the pattern of verdicts is itself instructive about why the headline question has no criterion free answer.

On the allocation test the Soviet system scores strongly, though not without an internal argument. A plan existed, it was binding, and it did allocate the decisive magnitudes of the economy: investment shares among industries, the location of new capacity, the output of producer goods, the movement of labour among sectors through assignment and incentive. No serious account denies that the economy was planned in this sense. The internal argument concerns the plan's character. Critics within the Marxist tradition, notably those who developed the state capitalist and bureaucratic collectivist readings, observed that a plan composed by bargaining among ministries, enterprises and planning organs, revised in execution, and enforced through a mixture of command and informal negotiation, differs from the conscious regulation of production by associated producers that the classical texts imagine. The plan was a mechanism of rule over producers as much as a mechanism of coordination among them. Defenders reply that coordination is coordination, whatever the political form of the coordinator, and that the allocation test asks about mechanism rather than about who holds the pen. The exchange reproduces, inside a single test, the master dispute between form and control, which suggests that the allocation test is not independent of the two principal criteria but a restatement of their quarrel in the vocabulary of mechanism.

On the provision test the score is also strong, and it is the test on which the system's popular reputation among its defenders chiefly rests. Employment was guaranteed in practice and unemployment, as a mass phenomenon, was abolished and did not return. Education and health care were provided universally and without charge at the point of use. Housing was allocated at rents held nominal for decades. Basic foodstuffs were subsidised, pensions were universal, and the catastrophic insecurities of market life, destitution through illness, eviction into homelessness, the workhouse shadow that haunted capitalist labour history, were absent. Set beside the capitalist economies of the same decades, especially in their treatment of the unemployed and the sick, the provision record is the affirmative case's most humanly persuasive material. Its definitional weight is another matter. Provision can be, and historically has been, supplied by systems nobody calls socialist: Bismarck's Germany built social insurance as an instrument against socialism, and the postwar capitalist welfare states matched much of the Soviet provision list while retaining private ownership throughout. Provision therefore marks a family resemblance among industrial societies under pressure to secure their populations, not the specific difference of socialism. A test that Bismarck passes cannot be the test that defines the tradition's goal, however much credit the Soviet system deserves for its scores on it.

### What does the equality test find when it is applied carefully?

It finds a society markedly more equal than its capitalist contemporaries in money income, with compressed wage scales and no property income at the top, yet stratified by access rather than by wealth: privileged distribution channels, closed shops, special clinics and allocated housing sorted the population by rank within the apparatus.

The stratification by access deserves a paragraph of its own, because it recurs in the tests below as the hinge of the class question. A money economy measures inequality in currency, and by that measure the Soviet order was egalitarian by the standards of its century. But a shortage economy distributes many goods outside the money circuit altogether, through allocation, entitlement and connection, and in that second circuit the population was ranked with great precision. The official whose position carried access to a special distribution network, a better flat, a dacha, a car with a driver, medical care in a closed institution and holidays in a restricted resort enjoyed a standard of life separated from the ordinary citizen's by a gulf that salary figures entirely fail to register. Defenders respond that such privileges were functional perquisites of office, modest beside the wealth of capitalist owners, and incapable of being bequeathed as property. Critics respond that a ruling stratum's consumption need not take the form of property to be real, and that the relevant comparison is not with capitalist wealth but with the condition of the producers beneath. The equality test thus ends, like the allocation test, by handing the argument back to the master dispute: whether rank within an apparatus of control constitutes the kind of social division the definition of socialism excludes.

One more test deserves mention because it appears constantly in the wider literature and in examination answers: the test of the withering of the state. Marx and Engels expected the coercive apparatus, under socialism, to begin losing its functions as class antagonisms disappeared, and Lenin's wartime notebook on the state made that expectation central to the revolutionary programme of 1917. Measured against it, the Soviet trajectory ran in the opposite direction for most of the system's life: the apparatus of coercion and administration grew, penetrated and outlasted every announced timetable for its own diminution. The affirmative tradition handled the embarrassment doctrinally, reclassifying the state as an instrument of a socialist society still surrounded by enemies, and formally distinguishing the state's external functions from its internal ones. Critics treated the trajectory as diagnostic: a state that grows over a society it claims to embody is evidence that the society and the state are different things, which is the control criterion's whole contention stated in institutional form. The withering test is rarely made the sole basis of a position, but it functions throughout the debate as corroborating evidence, and its verdict aligns with the control criterion's at every point.

## Comparison discipline: stating the dimension before comparing

### Why do unstated comparisons manufacture disagreement?

Because the Soviet system resembles capitalism on some dimensions and differs from it radically on others, so two writers comparing along different dimensions produce opposite verdicts from identical facts. A comparison that does not declare whether it concerns property, producers, allocation or outcomes is an argument waiting to cross itself.

The discipline this article observes, and recommends to any reader who argues the question elsewhere, is to declare the dimension of every comparison before drawing it. Four dimensions cover the debate. Property relations: here the Soviet system differs from capitalism categorically, and comparisons on this dimension favour the affirmative case. The position of producers: here the resemblance to capitalism is at its closest, since in both systems producers confront decisions made elsewhere and sell their capacity to work for a wage, and it is along this dimension that the critical cases draw their strength. The allocation mechanism: here the systems differ again, plan against market, though the difference narrows when the plan's bargaining texture and the market's corporate planning are both examined closely. Outcomes: here the comparison splits by outcome chosen, security favouring one side, consumer abundance and political liberty favouring the other, with the figures for the contested magnitudes excluded from this article by its protocol and treated in the companion study of the economic record, [Socialist States: Economic Record](/2019/06/15/socialist-states-economic-record/). A writer who announces the dimension converts an apparently factual quarrel into what it usually is, a quarrel about which dimension deserves to govern the classification.

The dimension discipline also exposes a recurring manoeuvre on both sides, which might be called dimension shopping. The affirmative writer, challenged on control, produces property; challenged on property's sufficiency, produces provision; challenged on provision's Bismarck problem, returns to property. The critical writer, challenged on the absence of a capitalist class, produces the wage relation; challenged on the plan, produces the privileges of access; challenged on privilege's non hereditary character, returns to the wage. Each move is legitimate as a contribution and illegitimate as a reply, because the question on the table was about a specified dimension and the answer supplied concerns a different one. Whole books have been written in this rotating fashion without the parties ever joining issue. Joining issue requires a prior agreement, or at least a declared disagreement, about which dimension is definitional, and that is why this article's centre of gravity sits where it does: not on any new evidence about the Soviet Union, but on the criterion question itself, and ultimately on the one text in the tradition that speaks to the criterion question with the author's own authority.

A comparison with the Chinese case, developed fully in a later section, illustrates the discipline's payoff. The two great classification disputes of the Marxist tradition concern systems that are mirror images along the dimensions: the Soviet system socialised property while withholding control and suppressing the market; the Chinese system retained the party's political monopoly while admitting markets, private accumulation and a capitalist class in formation. Ask the property question of the first and the control question of the second and the verdicts invert in a pattern that reveals more about the criteria than about either country. The parallel is flagged here because this article's commission requires it, and because it disciplines everything between: whenever a verdict is reached below, the reader should ask whether the same criterion, carried across to the parallel case, returns a verdict the writer would accept. A criterion applied only where it pleases is not a criterion but a conclusion in costume.

## The five positions grid: the map of the debate

The five positions grid organises the debate by what each position takes the ruling stratum to be, what it takes the property relations to be, what political conclusion it draws, and what evidence it accepts would count against it. The final column matters more than grids usually allow, because a position that can state its own falsifier is a position argued in good faith, and several of these can. The grid is a map, not a verdict: the sections after it give each row its full statement in its proponents' terms, and the verdict of this article, such as it is, arrives only after the tests have been run and Marx's formulation examined.

| Position | The ruling stratum | Property relations | Political conclusion | What would count against it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Socialist | A workers' state expressing the producers' interests | Socialised; no capitalist class exists | Defend the system as socialism's first, imperfect form | Evidence that a separate stratum appropriates the surplus against producers' interests |
| Degenerated or deformed workers' state | A bureaucratic caste parasitic on workers' property forms | Nationalised property remains a conquest of the working class | Political revolution against the bureaucracy; preserve the property forms | Evidence that the bureaucracy has restored private property or become a possessing class |
| State capitalist | A collective capitalist embodied in the state | State property functions as capital confronting wage labour | The system is a variant of the enemy; no defence is owed | Evidence that accumulation is not compelled by competition and labour power is not a commodity |
| Bureaucratic collectivist | A new ruling class of administrators and managers | A new mode of production, neither capitalist nor socialist | A third camp: neither system merits support | Evidence that the stratum lacks the cohesion and inheritance of a class |
| Transitional formation that failed | An unstable stratum ruling a formation between modes | Property forms suspended between capitalism and socialism, consolidated as neither | The formation's laws must be studied on their own terms; collapse was internal | Evidence that the formation possessed a stable, self reproducing logic of its own |

Reading the grid vertically teaches as much as reading it across. The first column of substance, the ruling stratum, shows the positions disagreeing about a sociological fact, or at least about the concept under which a fact should be filed: caste, class, collective capitalist, new class, unstable stratum. The second shows them disagreeing about the meaning of an agreed fact, since all five concede the property forms while interpreting them incompatibly. The third shows why the dispute was never academic for its participants: each classification issued in a different instruction for action, from defence of the Soviet state to its revolutionary overthrow to a plague on both houses of the Cold War. The fourth shows the debate's uneven intellectual quality, the rows for the workers' state and for state capitalism state their falsifiers crisply, the affirmative row's falsifier being the one its critics believe history supplied. A student who can reproduce the grid and gloss each cell possesses the debate's skeleton. The flesh, the arguments that make each cell defensible, occupies the next five sections.

### What does the grid's design make visible?

It makes the criterion choice visible. Property form governs the affirmative row; the workers' state row divides property from politics; the state capitalist row reads property through function; the collectivist row declares a new mode; the transitional row refuses every completed label. Each row then states what finding would count against it.

Two warnings about the grid's use complete its introduction. The first is that the rows are ideal types, and the literature's actual occupants sometimes straddle them: writers moved between rows across their careers, combined the workers' state analysis of one country with the collectivist analysis of another, or held a row's sociology while refusing its political conclusion. The grid disciplines the debate; it does not imprison the debaters, and the biographical notes in the following sections record the more instructive migrations. The second warning is that the grid omits, deliberately, the positions of those who classify the Soviet system from outside the Marxist tradition altogether: the totalitarian school's reading of it as a novel form of domination defined by ideology and terror, and the liberal reading of it as a command economy defined by the absence of markets and rights. Those readings answer a different question, what kind of polity the Soviet Union was, rather than the tradition internal question of whether it met the tradition's own definition of socialism. They enter this article only where they supply evidence about structure, and their absence from the grid is a scope decision, not a judgement on their merits.

## The first position: the system was socialist

### Who held the affirmative position, and on what grounds?

Its holders ranged from the Soviet state's own theorists to independent economists such as Maurice Dobb, and its grounds were constant: the means of production were socially owned, production was planned rather than anarchic, no class lived from property, employment was universal, and provision was collective in form.

The affirmative position deserves its full statement, because it is the position the rest of this article ultimately argues against at one specific point, and an argument against a weakened version would be worthless. In its strongest form the case runs as follows. Socialism, in the only definition the classical tradition operationalised, is the social ownership of productive wealth with production organised for use under a plan. The Soviet Union achieved that condition completely: the commanding sectors were state owned, the remainder held in cooperative forms, private productive property was extinguished, and with it the capitalist class, the capital market, the rentier, the landlord and the whole apparatus of appropriation through ownership. The consequences that the definition predicts followed. Production was not interrupted by crises of overproduction. Unemployment, the standing disgrace of the capitalist order and the mechanism of its labour discipline, was abolished. The surplus was appropriated socially and directed, by decisions taken in society's name, to investment, defence, science and collective consumption. Distribution, while unequal, contained no property income and no inherited productive wealth. On every relation by which Marx defined capitalism, the system differed from it; and a system that differs from capitalism on every defining relation, while exhibiting the property form the tradition specifies for its successor, has the strongest prima facie claim to the successor's name.

The position's official statement came in 1936, when the new constitution was promulgated with the declaration that socialism stood built in its essentials, and the doctrine was elaborated in the textbooks and party schools of the following decades into a complete theory: two forms of socialist property, state and collective farm; commodity production persisting in a limited sphere; distribution according to work as the socialist principle, distinguished from the communist principle of distribution according to need; and a state that remained necessary because of encirclement while withering in its internal class functions. Whatever a reader makes of the doctrine, it was a doctrine, internally articulated and taught to hundreds of millions, not a slogan improvised for parades. Its Western defenders added independent arguments. Maurice Dobb, the Cambridge economist whose studies of Soviet economic development spanned the system's middle decades, treated the planning mechanism as the decisive institutional innovation and the property transformation as the foundation on which a new mode of production rested; his work engaged the calculation debate seriously and answered the Austrian critics on the merits as he saw them. Paul Sweezy, for much of his career, defended a version of the affirmative reading against both Trotskyist and social democratic critics, though his position migrated over time toward a more critical account of the stratum's power, a migration the grid's warning about straddling rows anticipated.

Three supporting arguments give the affirmative case its resilience, and each should be registered before any of them is answered. The first is the argument from comparison: set the Soviet system beside any capitalist economy of its era and the differences in kind accumulate, in security, in the absence of a propertied class, in the subordination of production to stated social aims, until the shared label with capitalism becomes the one requiring justification. The second is the argument from trajectory: the system was, on its own theory, a lower phase, socialism as Marx's Gotha critique describes the first phase of the new society, stamped with the birthmarks of the old, distribution according to work rather than need, the state persisting, scarcity unmastered; defects that critics catalogue are, on this reading, the predicted features of a phase rather than evidence against the classification. The third is the argument from the alternative's cost: every rival label, the affirmative writers observed, imports a theory of its own, state capitalism requiring a rebuilt concept of capital, bureaucratic collectivism requiring a new mode of production invented for a single case, and parsimony favours the label that takes the property relations at face value. These arguments are serious. The answer to the position, when it comes in this article, does not deny the property facts, does not deny the comparative differences, and does not deny the phase doctrine's textual basis. It denies one thing: that the definition can be completed without the producers, because Marx's own positive formulation puts the producers at its centre. That denial must wait until the formulation has been examined on its own terms; the positions first.

A note on the affirmative position's internal critics completes the picture. Within the Soviet Union and its allied states, economists and philosophers periodically pressed at the doctrine's edges, in the debates over commodity production under socialism, over the law of value's operation, over enterprise autonomy and material incentives, and these debates were the system's own recognition that its categories strained against its practice. The Kosygin reforms of the middle nineteen sixties, with their talk of profit indicators and enterprise funds, and the Liberman discussion that preceded them, showed official economics groping toward mechanisms the doctrine said a planned economy should not need. Affirmative writers outside read the same episodes as adjustments within socialism; critical writers read them as the property form confessing its insufficiency. The episode matters here because it shows the definitional question was alive inside the system itself, argued by people with careers at stake in the categories, and not merely a weapon forged by the system's external enemies.

## The second position: a degenerated workers' state

The second position was built by Leon Trotsky in exile, principally in The Revolution Betrayed of 1936, and it remains the most influential critical classification ever produced from within the tradition. Its architecture is a deliberate split. The property forms established by the October Revolution, nationalised property and the planned economy, are conquests of the working class and remain, whatever has happened politically, the foundation of a workers' state. The political regime, by contrast, has been usurped: a bureaucratic caste, crystallised out of the apparatus under conditions of backwardness, scarcity and isolation, rules in its own interest, suppresses the working class politically, and distributes privilege to itself, while stopping short of restoring private property because its own position rests on the nationalised foundation it administers. The Soviet Union is therefore a contradictory formation, a workers' state degenerated, socialist in its economic base and anti socialist in its political superstructure, and the political conclusion follows the split exactly: the bureaucracy must be removed by a political revolution of the working class, while the property forms must be defended against both the bureaucracy's own possible restorationism and imperialist attack.

The wider contest between the two inheritances is traced in [Trotskyism vs Stalinism](/2014/11/15/trotskyism-vs-stalinism/), where the frameworks meet in their own right. The position's analytical core is its refusal to call the bureaucracy a class. Trotsky argued the point with care, because his whole construction depends on it. A class, in the Marxist criterion, is defined by its place in production and its property: it owns, it bequeaths, it reproduces itself through its own relation to productive property independently of the state's personnel decisions. The bureaucracy does none of these things. It owns nothing in its own name; its privileges attach to office and vanish with dismissal; its children inherit advantages of education and connection but not a title to productive wealth; it is, in Trotsky's image, a parasite and a caste, a stratum whose power is real but derivative, resting on the property forms it did not create and cannot convert into its own. From this analysis flows the position's distinctive prediction, stated repeatedly in the exile writings: the bureaucracy is unstable across the long run, and must either be overthrown by the working class in a political revolution that restores soviet democracy on the nationalised foundation, or will itself, seeking to convert privilege into property, become the agent of capitalist restoration. The events of the system's final years gave that prediction a hearing no other position's forecast enjoyed, and the claim is weighed in the companion article on the collapse, [Soviet Collapse: Marxist Explanations](/2019/05/01/soviet-collapse-marxist-explanations/), rather than here; for the classification question, what matters is the architecture, property socialist, regime usurped, stratum a caste and not a class.

### What separates a degenerated from a deformed workers' state?

A single variable: the manner of the property transformation's arrival. Degeneration describes a workers' state born in revolution and later usurped, the Soviet case. Deformation describes states where nationalised property was installed from above or from outside, without a healthy revolutionary period, across Eastern Europe after the war.

The deformed variant was constructed by Trotsky's successors, in the Fourth International's debates of the late nineteen forties, to classify the states of Eastern Europe where Communist parties, backed by Soviet power, nationalised industry and collectivised along Soviet lines without an indigenous revolutionary seizure of power by the working class. Ernest Mandel became the variant's most systematic defender, arguing that the property transformation was the decisive fact regardless of its midwife, and that these states were workers' states deformed from birth, requiring the same political revolution as the degenerated original. The variant was fiercely contested inside the movement, since it appeared to sever the classification from the working class's own action altogether, and some of the movement's currents refused it and classified the Eastern states as capitalist or collectivist instead. The dispute matters for this article because it exposes the parent position's deepest commitment: if a workers' state can be created by an army and a ministry, with the working class as spectator, then the classification rests entirely on property form, and the control criterion has been abandoned not merely for the Soviet case but in principle. Trotsky's own construction had kept one foot on the control side of the line, since the degeneration it described was precisely the loss of a control the class had once exercised in 1917. The deformed variant steps wholly onto the property side, and its critics inside the tradition said so at the time.

The workers' state position also carries the debate's most instructive account of how the usurpation happened, and the account deserves summary because it competes with the other positions' explanations on evidence rather than on definition. Backwardness is the first term: a socialist revolution in an isolated, devastated, largely illiterate peasant country faced a scarcity of everything, including administrators, and scarcity, in Trotsky's famous analysis, breeds the policeman, the queue and the official who allocates the queue. Isolation is the second: the expected European revolution did not arrive, and the besieged state's apparatus hardened into a permanent garrison mentality. The third is the party's own history: the bans, purges and monolithic discipline adopted under civil war conditions persisted into peace and supplied the machinery through which a stratum could rule without account. The explanation's strength is that it treats the bureaucracy as a product of circumstances rather than of the property forms themselves, which preserves the forms' socialist character by relocating the pathology. Its weakness, pressed by every rival position, is the length of the persistence it must explain: a degeneration explained by emergency that outlives the emergency by generations begins to look like a property of the structure, and the position's later defenders spent much ingenuity on exactly that wound.

## The third position: state capitalism

The state capitalist position holds that the Soviet Union was a variant of capitalism in which the state itself functioned as the collective capitalist, confronting a working class that sold its labour power for wages, extracting a surplus, and accumulating under compulsion. Its most systematic author was Tony Cliff, whose study State Capitalism in Russia, composed in the late nineteen forties and published in fuller form in the following decade, built the case as an explicit answer to the workers' state analysis from within the same Trotskyist milieu. Cliff's mechanism, stated here exactly as its author stated it, as this article's method demands, is competition displaced rather than abolished. Within the Soviet economy, enterprises did not compete in markets; but the Soviet state as a whole stood in competition with the capitalist world, above all military competition, and that external rivalry performed the function market competition performs inside a capitalist economy: it compelled accumulation, forced the subordination of consumption to investment, drove the extraction of the maximum surplus from the direct producers, and imposed the law of value on the economy from outside, through the one market the system could not secede from, the world competition in arms. Russia, in the analysis's compressed formula, was a single gigantic corporation, and its workers stood to the state as wage labourers stand to capital.

### How does military competition do the work of the market in this account?

By supplying the compulsion that markets supply internally. A firm that fails to accumulate is destroyed by rivals; a state ringed by hostile military powers that fails to accumulate the means of defence is destroyed by invasion. The arms race thus disciplines the bureaucracy as the market disciplines a board.

Raya Dunayevskaya developed a parallel state capitalist analysis in the United States during the same years, arriving at the classification through a study of the Soviet economy's operation of the law of value and breaking with Trotskyism over it, and the convergence of two independent constructions on one label gave the position a weight beyond either author's following. The position's evidence is the texture of the labour relation. Wage labour persisted in form and substance: workers were hired, paid differentiated wages, disciplined by management, subject to piece rates and output norms, and separated from the means of production as completely as any Western workforce, a separation the position treats as the definition of the proletarian condition. Accumulation dominated the economy's priorities at rates exceeding most capitalist performances, and consumption was the residual claimant, squeezed whenever the plan's investment drive required. The surplus was appropriated by a stratum that decided its use without reference to the producers. On the position's reading, every essential relation of capitalism is present once the category is defined by relations rather than by the number of owners, and the multiplication of owners in the West is an accident of capitalism's history rather than its essence: capital is a relation, not a headcount of capitalists, and a single collective owner confronting wage labour reproduces the relation in its purest form.

The position pays for its elegance at two points, and its critics have always known where to press. The first is the law of value: if prices inside the economy are administered, labour is allocated by assignment and incentive rather than by a market, and enterprises cannot fail, then the mechanism by which value regulates production is attenuated to the single channel of military competition, and critics ask whether a law operating through one external channel deserves its name. Cliff's answer, that the channel is sufficient because the ruling stratum experiences the compulsion as absolute, is an argument about the stratum's motivations elevated into an economic law, and even sympathetic readers have marked the strain. The second pressure point is the crisis theory: capitalism, in Marx's analysis, generates crises of overproduction from its internal contradictions, and the Soviet economy's pathologies, shortage, hoarding, disproportionality, quality collapse, are the opposite family of disorders, the disorders of a command system rather than of a market. Defenders reply that crisis takes the form the system permits and that arms driven accumulation has its own breakdowns. The exchange is among the most technically serious in the whole debate, and it illustrates the grid's falsifier column in action: the state capitalist row can be, and has been, tested against the behaviour its theory predicts, which is more than can be said for every classification on offer.

## The fourth position: bureaucratic collectivism

Bureaucratic collectivism holds that the Soviet system was neither capitalist nor socialist but a new mode of production, historically novel, in which a bureaucracy constituted as a ruling class exploited the producers through its collective control of the state and thereby of the means of production. The position has a triple origin, and the independence of its sources is part of its interest. Bruno Rizzi, an Italian Trotskyist, published his account of the bureaucratisation of the world in 1939, arguing that the Soviet bureaucracy had become a possessing class through the state and that the future belonged to bureaucratic collectivist forms. James Burnham, arriving from the same movement and departing from it, generalised the observation in The Managerial Revolution of 1941 into a theory of a new ruling stratum of managers across all advanced societies, a book whose influence ran far beyond Marxist circles. Max Shachtman, who had been Trotsky's close collaborator, broke with the workers' state analysis in 1940 over the classification of the Soviet Union's expansion, and built the bureaucratic collectivist position into the doctrine of a political tendency, drawing the conclusion that neither camp in the Cold War merited support and that a third camp of the working class had to be constructed against both.

### What makes bureaucratic collectivism a new mode rather than a variant?

Its claim about the stratum. Capitalism's rulers own; socialism's producers would rule themselves; the collectivist stratum, on this account, rules through office alone, collectively, without private title, and exploits through its monopoly of administration. A ruling class defined by control rather than ownership requires a new mode to house it.

The position's strength is its refusal of two discomforts at once. Against the affirmative and workers' state positions, it takes the bureaucracy's power and privilege at full value and declines to explain them as excrescences on a healthy base: a stratum that decides everything, consumes preferentially, reproduces its position through control of appointments and education, and shoots its opponents is, on any ordinary use of language, a ruling class, and a theory that cannot call it one has purchased its categories too dearly. Against the state capitalist position, it takes the absence of capitalism's mechanisms at full value: no market, no competition among capitals, no crisis of overproduction, no law of value in its classical operation, and it declines to rebuild the concept of capital around a single external compulsion. What remains, the position argues, is the honest course of admitting a new formation: history is not obliged to choose between the two modes the nineteenth century catalogued, and a bureaucracy that has expropriated both the capitalists and the working class, ruling through the state it collectively possesses, is a formation with its own name. The position also generated the debate's most consequential political derivative, the third camp, and its analysis fed, through Shachtman's later trajectory and through the migration of several of its adherents, into currents well outside the Marxist tradition, a biographical afterlife the grid cannot show.

Its weakness is the mirror of the workers' state position's strength, and it sits exactly on the class criterion. If a class must be defined by property, transmissible and independent of office, then the bureaucracy fails the test, and bureaucratic collectivism has named a mode of production whose ruling class is no class at all by the tradition's own standard. The position's defenders answered by revising the standard: class, they argued, is at bottom a relation of control over the production process and appropriation of the surplus, and property in the juridical sense is one historical form of that control, not its essence; a stratum that collectively monopolises control and appropriation is a class in the material sense whatever the land registry says. The answer is not frivolous, and it draws support from the same effective versus juridical distinction that critics of the affirmative case deploy about property. But it exacts a price the position's critics itemised: a mode of production identified in essentially one country and one period, without the expansive dynamic, the world transforming drive, that the classical modes exhibit, looks less like a mode and more like a predicament. The collectivist literature's predictions about the formation's spread and stability were, in the event, among the debate's least successful, and the position survives in the literature more as an indispensable provocation, the refusal to let either comfortable label pass, than as a settled classification.

## The fifth position: a transitional formation that failed

The fifth position declines the premise that the Soviet system must be assigned to a completed mode of production at all. On this reading, the system was a transitional formation, a society suspended between capitalism and socialism, which never consolidated the laws of motion of either and never developed a stable logic of its own, and whose defining characteristic was the absence of the very thing every other position attributes to it: a coherent mode of production. Its most sustained exponent was Hillel Ticktin, whose journal Critique, founded in the early nineteen seventies, elaborated the analysis across two decades: the Soviet economy was dominated by waste as a systemic feature rather than an accident, its product was often not a use value fully realised, its planning was a form of control without the substance of either market regulation or conscious social direction, and its ruling stratum was too divided and too insecure in its appropriation to constitute a class in formation, let alone a formed one. The formation was, in the analysis's key contention, in decline from its early decades onward, because a system that is neither one thing nor the other cannot reproduce the conditions of its own continuation indefinitely.

### Why does the transitional reading refuse every completed label?

Because each completed label credits the system with a coherence it never possessed. Capitalism implies the law of value; socialism implies the producers' association; collectivism implies a formed ruling class. The transitional reading finds fragments of each and the substance of none, and takes the incoherence itself as the finding.

The position reframes several of the debate's standard exhibits. Shortage, which the state capitalist reading struggles to house and the affirmative reading treats as a planning defect, becomes on this reading the formation's signature: a system in which the stratum's control is exercised negatively, through blocking, withholding and veto, rather than positively through either accumulation discipline or social direction, will generate waste and shortage as its normal output. The stratum's privileges, which the collectivist reading elevates into class rule, are reinterpreted as the insecure takings of a group that cannot convert its position into property and knows it, hence its consumption oriented, short horizon character. The political conclusion differs from every other row of the grid: there is no healthy base to defend, no capitalist essence to oppose as one opposes capitalism, and no formed class whose overthrow completes a known script; the formation will end, the analysis held, through its own internal exhaustion, and what follows it is an open question rather than a restoration foretold. When the end came, this reading's adherents claimed a vindication of method, the collapse having arrived through internal decay without the political revolution one rival predicted or the confident consolidation another implied, though the claim is contested and belongs to the collapse article's territory.

Assessed as a classification, the transitional reading is the debate's most honest and its least portable. Honest, because it registers the genuine strangeness of the object, the way the system frustrated every category carried to it, and refuses to saw off the inconvenient corners of the evidence to fit a nineteenth century drawer. Least portable, because a formation defined by incoherence explains everything and predicts little in detail, and because transition, in the classical scheme, is a passage between modes with a direction, while a passage lasting the whole life of a state and ending in the restoration of the mode it departed from strains the concept past its working limits. The position's enduring value for this article's purposes is diagnostic. It is the standing reminder that the classification difficulty is not a failure of the classifiers alone: the object itself was, in the strict sense, anomalous, a property revolution without a producers' revolution, frozen at the moment of its incompleteness. That anomaly is what the criterion split, examined after the tests, finally explains.

## The new class thesis: Djilas's study and its career

Between the positions stands a book that belongs fully to none of them and armed several: Milovan Djilas's The New Class, published in 1957. Djilas wrote from a vantage no other analyst in this debate possessed. He had been among the highest leaders of the Yugoslav party, a vice president of his country and a wartime partisan commander, and his book is an insider's anatomy of the stratum he had helped to build and from which he had broken. Its thesis is compact. The Communist party bureaucracy, having monopolised political power in the name of the working class, became a new class: it owns, in effect, through its monopoly of administration, since ownership in substance is the right to control and to distribute income, and that right the party stratum exercises exclusively; it appropriates special privileges as the form its surplus takes; and it reproduces itself through the party's control of appointment to every significant post in society. The study's date matters to its argument's reception: appearing when it did, from the pen of a former insider, in the aftermath of the secret speech and the Hungarian rising, it landed as confirmation from the summit of what the critical positions had argued from outside, and it cost its author prison.

### What did Djilas claim, and what was new in the claim?

He claimed the administering party itself had become a class, owning through its monopoly of control rather than through title. The novelty was the source and the frankness: a former member of the stratum stating, as sociology, that privilege was not an abuse of the system but its organising principle.

The critical literature received the book along lines the grid predicts. The workers' state tradition answered with the inheritance test: Djilas's class cannot bequeath its position, holds its privileges at the pleasure of the apparatus, and is periodically purged by the very power it supposedly owns, none of which is true of a possessing class; what he described, on this reading, is a caste with unusual self awareness in one of its defectors. The collectivist tradition received the book as a late arriving ally, noting that its account of ownership through control was the collectivist class concept stated by a practitioner. The affirmative tradition treated it as treason literature and answered its author rather than its argument, the weakest of the three receptions and the one that has aged worst. For the purposes of this article, Djilas's importance is that he posed, in its sharpest form, the question the second test below must answer: by what criterion does a stratum count as a class, and does the Soviet stratum meet it? The new class thesis also illustrates a general feature of the debate's evidence. Its author needed no archives; he had sat in the rooms. Much of the strongest material for the control criterion has this character, testimony about how decisions were actually made and privileges actually distributed, while the affirmative side's strongest material is documentary and juridical. The asymmetry of evidence, noted earlier, is one reason the debate's two halves so often pass without engaging.

Yugoslavia's own experiment adds a coda that no treatment of the control criterion should omit. The Yugoslav system after its break with Moscow introduced workers' self management in form: enterprises were handed, in law, to their work collectives, councils were elected, and the official theory presented the arrangement as the producers' association in construction. Djilas wrote as this experiment's internal critic, arguing that the party's monopoly emptied the councils of power, and later analysts, friendly and hostile, have used the Yugoslav case as the nearest available test of whether formal producer institutions alter the classification when a single party retains political command. The bearing on the Soviet question is indirect but real: if councils under a party monopoly did not constitute producer control in the one system that proclaimed them, then the Soviet system's absence of even the forms cannot be argued into control by any stretch, and the control test's Soviet verdict stands reinforced by its neighbour's experiment. The Yugoslav material also supplies the debate's one controlled comparison, two systems sharing a party form and differing in their proclaimed economics, and its literature is the natural next reading for a student whose appetite the five positions have whetted.

## The tests, first: was labour power a commodity?

The positions talk past one another until their claims are converted into tests, and the debate's literature supplies four that between them cover the contested ground. The first asks whether labour power was a commodity in the Soviet Union, because the commodity status of labour power is capitalism's signature relation in Marx's analysis: where the capacity to work is bought and sold, where its price is formed in a market, and where a reserve army of the unemployed disciplines those in work, the capital relation exists whatever the property register says. The Soviet evidence points in two directions at once, which is why the test's verdict is genuinely contested rather than merely disputed. For the commodity reading: workers were hired for wages, wages were differentiated by skill, sector and output, piece rates and bonuses tied pay to performance, labour was directed by management the worker did not choose, and the worker's separation from the means of production was total, the defining precondition, in Marx's account, for labour power to become a commodity at all. A Soviet worker owned nothing but the capacity to work and lived by exchanging it with the state employer for a wage, and the state capitalist literature rests its case substantially on that sentence.

### Why is labour power's commodity status contested rather than settled?

Because the sale occurred without the market that defines the commodity form in Marx's account. There was no competition among employers bidding wages up, no reserve army pressing them down, and no unemployment to enforce the bargain's discipline. The wage was administered, not formed by exchange between free parties.

For the non commodity reading, the institutional detail accumulates impressively. Employment was guaranteed, and the guarantee was not a formality: enterprises hoarded labour rather than shedding it, the chronic condition of the system being labour shortage, the photographic negative of the reserve army. Workers could not in practice be dismissed into destitution, and the disciplinary instruments of the capitalist labour market, hunger and the queue of replacements, were absent by design. Movement between jobs was frequent and largely worker initiated, with turnover functioning as the workforce's own sanction against bad conditions, a sanction exercised on a scale that planners deplored and could not suppress. Wage levels were set administratively within a national structure, and the enterprise that wished to attract labour did so through housing, amenities and informal concessions rather than through open wage competition. The commodity form, on this reading, requires the market as its medium, and where the medium is absent the form is a shell: the wage persisted as an accounting and incentive device inside a system that had abolished the labour market as an institution. The debate's honest verdict on the first test is therefore a split one, and the split is informative. The relation between worker and employer bore the marks of the wage form in the worker's separation and dependence, and lacked the marks of the commodity form in the absence of its market. Positions that cite the test as decisive, in either direction, are citing half of it.

The test also exposes, in miniature, the debate's recurring methodological fault, which is the conversion of a matter of degree into a matter of definition. Commodity production in Marx's own analysis admits of extent: a society can exhibit commodity relations in one sphere and not another, and the Soviet system itself, on the official doctrine, retained commodity production in the consumer goods sphere while denying it to the means of production, a distinction its economists debated with real sophistication. Labour power sits awkwardly across that official line, since it is neither a consumer good nor a means of production but the living agent of the whole process. The serious literature on both sides treats the test as diagnostic rather than dispositive, evidence about the character of the labour relation that must be combined with the other tests before a classification follows. The popular literature treats it as a gavel. A reader who has grasped why the test cannot settle the question alone has grasped the article's method, and can be trusted with the remaining three.

## The tests, second: was there a ruling class?

The second test takes up the question Djilas posed: did the stratum that ruled constitute a class? The affirmative position and the workers' state position answer no, the collectivist and new class positions answer yes, and the state capitalist position answers with a refinement, that the stratum was a class in the specific form of a collective capitalist. The test cannot be run until its instrument is calibrated, and the calibration is the tradition's own definition, developed at length in the [Marxist Theory of Class Guide](/2011/09/01/marxist-theory-of-class-guide/). A class, in the standard Marxist criterion, is a group defined by a common relation to the means of production, a relation that is objective rather than conferred, that carries the appropriation of surplus labour, and that reproduces itself across generations through the transmission of that relation, paradigmatically by inheritance of property. Each clause does work. Objectivity excludes mere office holders as such. Appropriation includes the bureaucracy, which plainly directed the surplus. Reproduction by transmission is where the argument concentrates, because it is the clause the Soviet stratum most conspicuously fails and the clause its defenders as a class most need to reinterpret.

### What test decides whether a stratum counts as a class?

The transmission test, in the tradition's standard form: a class hands down its relation to productive wealth across generations by right, in the manner of an inheritance. A stratum whose children must requalify for position through education and appointment, and whose members lose privilege with office, fails it.

Run the test on the nomenklatura, the appointment list system through which the party staffed every significant post, and the results are genuinely two sided. Against class status: the official's privileges were attached to the post and extinguished with it; dismissal, demotion and, in the terror years, arrest could annihilate a family's position in a day; no official could bequeath an enterprise, a ministry or a seat; the stratum's membership was recruited substantially from below in every generation, through education and party advancement, and its boundaries were policed by the party itself rather than defended by law as a caste's would be. For class status: the stratum's children enjoyed systematically privileged access to the education and the postings through which position was reproduced, so that transmission occurred in fact through the credential channel where the property channel was barred; the stratum collectively controlled appointment, and therefore controlled the conditions of its own recruitment, which is the substance of self reproduction whatever the form; and its appropriation of privilege was not incidental corruption but a structured entitlement, graded by rank and known to all. Djilas's answer to the transmission objection remains the position's sharpest: a class in formation, he argued, should not be measured by the standards of classes in their maturity, and the party stratum was a class becoming, its property relation embryonic because its political monopoly had to do the work that title does elsewhere.

The test's verdict, stated with the care the calibration demands, is that the stratum was not a class by the tradition's standard criterion and was a ruling stratum by any criterion at all. Those two findings are compatible, and holding them together dissolves much of the apparent contradiction between the workers' state and collectivist literatures: Trotsky's caste and Djilas's class are descriptions of the same object taken at different moments of the definition, the first stressing the missing property relation, the second the present appropriation and control. The classification question is not advanced by choosing between the descriptions but by noticing what the object's resistance to the category signifies. A system whose rulers cannot be classified by the tradition's sociology of rule is a system the tradition's categories were not built for, which is the transitional position's founding observation returning through the evidence. For the headline question, the second test's contribution is negative and important: the absence of a ruling class in the strict sense blocks the simplest version of the not socialist verdict, the version that proceeds by naming the exploiters, and forces the critical case onto its stronger ground, the position of the producers, where it does not need the stratum to be a class at all.

A subsidiary question, often confused with the test itself, concerns the stratum's size and the scale of its privilege, and this article's figures protocol excludes the numbers in both directions. The exclusion is not a handicap to the argument, because the classification does not turn on magnitudes. A stratum comprising a small fraction of the population, consuming modestly by the standards of Western wealth, would still be the system's ruler if it monopolised decision; a numerous and lavish stratum that decided nothing would be a decoration rather than a class or a caste. Magnitude matters to moral judgement and to the economic record, both of which live elsewhere. What matters here is the structure of the relation: decision monopolised, appropriation structured, transmission contested. On structure the evidence is not in dispute among the serious positions, and the dispute is confined, as the calibration showed, to the concept under which the structure should be filed.

## The tests, third: was production oriented to accumulation?

The third test asks whether production was oriented to accumulation, the expansion of the productive apparatus as an end dominating consumption, because accumulation's dominance is, in the critical literature, the functional signature of capital: a system that squeezes its producers to pile up means of production behaves as capital behaves, whatever its owners are called. On the fact, there is no serious dispute. The Soviet economy, across most of its life, devoted an extraordinary share of its product to investment, privileged the producer goods sectors in the plan's very architecture, treated consumption as the residual after accumulation and defence had taken their shares, and reproduced that priority through every change of leadership and doctrine. The growth of the capital stock was the system's proudest claim and its population's standing grievance, and the two facts are one fact viewed from the two ends of the relation. Where the test divides the literature is on the mechanism and therefore on the meaning. Accumulation under capitalism is driven by profit through competition: each capital must accumulate or be destroyed by capitals that do. Accumulation in the Soviet case was driven by decision, by the plan's priorities, under the pressure the state capitalist analysis identifies, military competition with the capitalist world, and under the pressure the affirmative analysis identifies, the development strategy of a backward country catching up.

### How can accumulation dominate a system without profit?

Through decision backed by rivalry. Where a state must match the military and industrial capacity of hostile powers, accumulation is imposed as a condition of survival, and the plan transmits the compulsion to every enterprise and worker. The pressure is external and political rather than internal and commercial, with identical effects on consumption.

The mechanism question decides what the test proves. If accumulation's dominance is capitalism's signature only when it issues from the profit mechanism, then the Soviet case shows a different system arriving at a similar allocation under a different compulsion, and the resemblance is a convergence of industrial states under rivalry rather than an identity of mode. If accumulation's dominance is the signature wherever it appears, because what defines capital is the subordination of living labour to the expansion of dead labour, then the Soviet case is capitalism in its state form and the mechanism's variation is a detail. The state capitalist literature argues the second with great force, and its argument from the worker's standpoint has a bleak persuasiveness: to the producer whose consumption is deferred and whose effort is intensified, the distinction between accumulation compelled by a market and accumulation compelled by a ministry may appear as a distinction among the compulsions' letterheads. The counter argument, pressed by the workers' state and transitional literatures, is that a theory which classifies by effects on the producer alone will classify every industrialising state of the century together, including states in the act of building capitalism and states in the act of dismantling it, and a classification that cannot distinguish construction from its opposite has abandoned the historical specificity that made the tradition's categories worth having.

The test's balanced verdict is that accumulation dominated, that its dominance is a genuine and deep resemblance to capital's behaviour, and that the resemblance runs through a different mechanism whose identity is itself contested between the military competition thesis and the development strategy thesis. That verdict supports the state capitalist position's evidence while withholding its conclusion, and it leaves the affirmative position with a fact it has never comfortably housed: a socialism whose first claim on the product, decade after decade, was the expansion of the apparatus rather than the welfare or the free time of the producers sits awkwardly beside the tradition's own statements of socialism's purpose, in which the reduction of necessary labour and the expansion of the realm of freedom figure as the point of the whole enterprise. The awkwardness is not a refutation. It is, however, one of the places where the affirmative case's reliance on the property register shows its cost, since a definition silent about purposes cannot explain why the purposes were so consistently postponed.

## The tests, fourth: did producers control production?

The fourth test is the control criterion operationalised, and its conduct can be brief because its evidence is one sided in a way the other tests' evidence is not. The question is whether producers, the people who performed the society's productive labour, controlled production: whether they decided what was produced, by what methods, at what pace, under whose direction, and to what distribution of the product. The institutional inventory was sketched when the criterion was introduced, and the test consists in running it as an examination rather than as a sketch. The enterprise was directed by an appointed manager exercising one man authority, accountable upward to a trust or ministry, armed with disciplinary powers over the workforce, and judged by the fulfilment of targets he had not set but was required to meet. The workforce's formal organs within the enterprise, the union committee and the production conference, administered welfare, mobilised effort and transmitted policy; they did not appoint the director, veto the plan, or determine the product. The collective farm's members stood in a formally cooperative relation to their institution and in a substantively similar subordination to its appointed or approved chair and to the state's delivery quotas. At the level of the whole economy, the plan that decided the proportions among industries, regions and consumption funds was composed within the planning and party apparatus and ratified by bodies in which producers, as producers, had no organised voice distinct from the apparatus itself.

### What institutional evidence settles the control test?

The direction of accountability at every level. Directors answered to ministries, ministries to the planning organs and the party, and at no point in the chain did an elected body of producers hold a power of appointment, removal or veto that it could exercise against the apparatus's preference.

Against this inventory, the affirmative tradition's representative argument must be stated once more in its precise form, because the test is where it does its work. The argument holds that control was exercised by the working class through its state and its party: the state owned in the class's name, the party was recruited from the class and bound to its interests by doctrine and by origin, and the plan expressed the class's collective will in the only form a continental economy permits, mediated and planned rather than direct and fragmented. Control, on this account, is like the control of citizens over a modern state's budget: real in its constitutional form, invisible in any individual workplace. The test's answer is an empirical demand disguised as a conceptual one. Mediation transmits control only if the mediated can correct the mediator: remove the director, reject the target, replace the leadership, publish the dissent. Each of those correctives was absent, not as an abuse but as a structure, and a mediation without correctives is not a transmission of control but its replacement by the mediator's own. The point can be put as a counterfactual that the literature has used in several forms: if, at any moment in the system's mature decades, the producers of a great enterprise or a whole industry had resolved, through their own organisations, to produce differently, to elect their direction, or to divert a share of the surplus to their own consumption against the plan, the institutional order provided no channel through which the resolution could take effect, and several channels through which it would be treated as an offence. A control that cannot be exercised against the controller's will is a courtesy title.

The verdict of the fourth test is therefore unambiguous, and it is the only unambiguous verdict the four tests produce: producers did not control production, by any account, including the accounts of the system's defenders, whose arguments concede the fact while contesting its definitional weight. That distribution of verdicts across the four tests is the empirical heart of this article. Labour power's commodity status: contested. The stratum's class status: fails the strict test, passes every looser one. Accumulation's dominance: established, mechanism disputed. Producer control: absent, undisputed. A classification that must integrate these findings cannot rest on the first three tests, each of which returns a mixed or contested result. It rests, if it rests anywhere firm, on the fourth, and the fourth points one way. What remains is to ask whether the fourth test's criterion is entitled to its decisive role, and that is a question about Marx's text rather than about Soviet institutions. The next two sections take it up, and they carry the article's central claim.

## Marx's own formulation: the association of free producers

Every position in the debate claims Marx, but the claims are not symmetrical, because Marx wrote about the successor society in two distinguishable registers and the positions divide by register. In the register of transition, he wrote about property: the expropriation of the expropriators, the conversion of the means of production into social property, the abolition of the bourgeois property relation. In the register of characterisation, he wrote about what the new society is like from inside, and there the recurring figure is not the register of titles but the association of the producers themselves. The most famous passage stands in the first chapter of Capital, where, having anatomised the fetishism of commodities, Marx asks the reader to picture, by contrast, an association of free men, working with the means of production held in common and expending their labour power in full self awareness as one social labour force. The passage's point is transparency: in the association, the social relations of the producers to their labour and its products are simple and intelligible, because the producers themselves arrange them. The contrast term is not private ownership as such. It is opacity, domination by the product, the rule of the producers' own creation over its creators.

### Where does Marx state the producer criterion?

In the characterising passages rather than the transitional ones: the association passage in the first volume of Capital, the account of the Commune as the form through which the producers govern, and the Gotha notes, where the higher phase is defined by the producers' all round development rather than by title.

The other characterising texts point the same way. In The Civil War in France, the Commune's significance is made to lie in its form, a working body of elected and revocable delegates through which the producers' class governed itself, and Marx's enthusiasm fastens on the institutional mechanics of self rule, election, recall, workmen's wages for officials, rather than on any schedule of nationalisations, which the Commune in its short life barely began. The Critique of the Gotha Programme, the tradition's most cited text on the phases of the new society, describes the first phase as emerging from capitalism with its birthmarks upon it, distribution still governed by the contribution of labour, and even there the presupposition is that the means of production are already the common property of the producers themselves, held by them as an association, with the individual's labour directly social in character. The shortcomings Marx lists for the opening phase are shortcomings of distribution under scarcity. Absence of the producers from the government of production is not among them, because the association is the premise of the whole description, not one of its variable features. A reader assembling the definition from the characterising texts will therefore build producer control into the foundation. A reader assembling it from the transitional texts will build in the property transformation and treat control as part of the scenery the transformation eventually supplies. The debate's longevity is explained by the fact that both assemblies are genuinely Marxian. The question this article presses is which assembly has the better claim to state the definition, and the answer it defends is that a definition of a society's character is more authoritatively drawn from the texts that characterise the society than from the texts that describe the road toward it.

One further text deserves its place in the dossier, because the affirmative tradition leans on it and the leaning deserves examination. In the third volume of Capital and in the drafts around it, Marx describes the realm of freedom as beginning beyond the sphere of material production proper, with the shortening of the working day as its basic prerequisite, and the associated producers appear in the same passage as the subjects who rationally regulate their interchange with nature, bringing it under their common control instead of being ruled by it as by a blind power. The passage is the mature Marx's most complete single statement of the goal, and its grammar is exact: the associated producers are the subject of the regulating, common control is the predicate, and freedom is the result. No reading of the sentence can demote the producers to beneficiaries of a regulation performed by others in their name without reversing its grammar. The affirmative case, to its credit, rarely attempts the reversal directly. It argues instead that the passage describes the goal's maturity, the communist horizon, while socialism as a phase is defined by the property transformation that sets the journey going. That argument, the phase argument, is the affirmative case's strongest card, and it is answered in the next section, where the criterion claim is stated in full.

It is also worth recording, because the debate's conduct often obscures it, that the formulation's authority does not depend on treating Marx as an oracle. The argument of this article is not that a classification is settled because a revered text utters it. It is that the question under examination is internal to a tradition: whether the Soviet Union was socialist is a question posed in the tradition's vocabulary, and the tradition's vocabulary takes its meanings from its founding texts, among which the characterising passages are the only ones that define the term at issue positively and directly. A chemist asking whether a sample is an acid consults the definition of acid, not the biography of the sample. The definition the tradition actually contains, when its positive statements are assembled, makes the producers' association constitutive. Positions that classify by property alone are therefore not wrong about the Soviet Union's property. They are using a thinner definition than the tradition's own fullest one, and the burden falls on them to justify the thinning. That burden, and whether it can be carried, is the subject of the claim now to be stated.

## Marx's own criterion: the claim at the centre of this article

The claim can be stated in one sentence, and the grid's readers will recognise it as the entry this article exists to defend. Producer control is constitutive in Marx's formulation of socialism, not a consequence expected to follow from it, and since no account of the Soviet system claims that producer control existed there, the affirmative case is obliged to argue that property form, rather than producer control, is the decisive criterion, which is a departure from Marx's own formulation of the society whose name is in dispute. The claim is deliberately narrow. It does not assert that the Soviet Union was capitalist, or collectivist, or any other positive classification. It asserts where the burden lies and what the affirmative argument must do to carry it: it must justify, against the characterising texts, a definition of socialism from which the producers' self government is absent. Everything else in the debate, the tests, the class question, the accumulation mechanism, is evidence marshalled around that central justificatory task.

Three considerations support the claim's reading of the texts. The first is the order of definition in the characterising passages themselves. When Marx pictures the association, he does not picture a property settlement from which association later flowers; the association is the picture, and common holding of the means of production appears inside it as the association's own arrangement of its conditions. Property, in the formulation, is a predicate of the associated producers' order, not its premise. The second consideration is the Commune text's standard of judgement. Marx measured a real, embattled, improvised regime against the criterion of the producers' self government and found it, in form, to be the political form at last discovered for working out the economic emancipation of labour; the measurement he actually performed on the one case he judged was a measurement of control, conducted while the regime's property transformations were embryonic. The third consideration is the tradition's own negative usage. When Marx and Engels criticised the state socialisms of their day, the nationalisations proposed or enacted by governments above society, their objection was never that the property form was wrong but that the producers remained subjects of a power standing over them; Engels's warning that state ownership does not do away with the capital relation, and that the workers remain wage workers under a state employer, is the criterion applied as criticism a generation before the Soviet case existed. A criterion the founders used to refuse the label to state owned systems in their own century cannot be waved aside as an anachronistic imposition when it is applied to the greatest state owned system of the next.

The phase argument, the affirmative case's reply, now deserves its most careful hearing, because it is the hinge on which the whole article turns. The argument observes, correctly, that Marx's Gotha notes describe an opening phase of the new order bearing the defects of its origin, and it proposes that the Soviet Union be classified as that phase: socialism in its immature form, property transformed, distribution by labour contribution, the state persisting, and the producers' full self government belonging to the phase's future rather than its present. The reply to the reply is textual and specific. The defects Marx enumerates for the first phase are defects within the association: unequal endowments making contribution based distribution unequal in its results, the persistence of the division of labour, scarcity constraining the whole. At no point does the Gotha text describe, as a phase feature, the producers' exclusion from the government of production, nor the direction of the economy by a stratum they cannot correct. The first phase is the association under conditions of scarcity, not an apparatus under conditions of scarcity claiming the association's name in advance. A phase is a stage of a thing, and the thing whose stage is claimed must be present in the stage's defining structure; otherwise the classification is not phased but promissory, a note drawn on the future and presented as a present asset. The Soviet claim, on the criterion this article defends, was exactly such a note, and its seventy years of currency without redemption is the historical form the definitional problem took.

Let the concession that balances the claim be stated with equal plainness, since this article's commission demands it and the argument is stronger for making it. The affirmative case correctly identifies that the Soviet property relations were not capitalist. No honest reading of the evidence returns a capitalist verdict on the property dimension, and the positions that classify the system as capitalism must, as their own best authors acknowledged, rebuild the concept of capital on function and compulsion to make the classification stand. Nor does any available label fit the system well: the workers' state label must house a usurpation that lasted the system's life, the collectivist label must house a class that fails the inheritance test, the transitional label must house a transition longer than most modes' maturity, and the socialist label must house producers who never governed. The classification difficulty is real, and it is the system's own anomaly reproduced in every vocabulary carried to it. Marx's own criterion does not dissolve that difficulty. It does something more limited and more valuable: it settles the headline question's negative half on the tradition's own strongest textual ground, and it locates the remaining dispute, what the system positively was, where it belongs, among the four rival answers that the grid's lower rows supply.

## The strongest objection: the affirmative case at full strength

The objection to everything just argued must now be stated as its proponents state it, at full strength and in their own terms, because the claim of the last section is worth only what it can survive. The affirmative case, so stated, runs as follows. A system in which the means of production were not privately owned, in which there was no capital market, no unemployment, and no class deriving income from property, differs from capitalism on every relation Marx used to define it. The definition of capitalism in the tradition is a definition by relations: private property in the means of production, the commodification of labour power, production for profit, the appropriation of surplus by owners. The Soviet system negated the whole list. To deny such a system the name socialist because its producers lacked control is, the objection continues, to substitute a political criterion for the economic one that historical materialism specifies. Historical materialism classifies societies by their relations of production, by the economic structure, and the political forms, the presence or absence of democracy, the character of the state, belong to the superstructure that the method teaches us not to mistake for the base. A classification performed by inspecting the polity rather than the economy is, on the method's own terms, idealist: it judges a society by its political physiognomy instead of its material structure. The producers' lack of control is a political fact, grave as it may be, and a method that subordinates politics to economics cannot permit a political fact to overturn an economic classification.

### Why does the affirmative case call the control test political?

Because control, as the critics describe it, concerns who governs, and government belongs to the political sphere. The objection insists that a classification of modes of production must be performed on the relations of production, and that importing a standard of self government confuses the base with its superstructure.

The objection gathers force from three supporting considerations, and a fair statement must include them. A first support is the argument from Marx's reticence: Marx refused, famously and repeatedly, to write recipes for the cook shops of the future, and the characterising passages on which the control criterion rests are few, brief and embedded in works of criticism rather than presented as definitions; to erect two paragraphs of Capital and a pamphlet on the Commune into a constitutional test that the largest socialist experiment in history then fails is, the objection suggests, a disproportion between the textual foundation and the weight it is made to bear. A second support is the argument from the tradition's practice: the parties and states that built the Soviet system understood themselves, and were understood by friend and foe for decades, as constructing socialism, and a classification that retrospectively strips the name from the entire project on the strength of a criterion its builders did not apply has the character of a definition revised after the game to alter the score. A third support is the argument from consequences for the concept itself: if producer control in the strong sense is constitutive, then no large scale society has ever been socialist or shown a working sketch of how one could be, the Yugoslav experiment in self management having operated under a party monopoly that emptied it, and a concept with no possible instance at scale is a regulative ideal rather than a classification, more like the philosopher's republic than like feudalism or capitalism, which classify real formations. The tradition, the objection concludes, should prefer the criterion that connects its central concept to the real movement of history, and that criterion is the transformation of property, the one event in the whole story that indisputably occurred.

Stated at that strength, the objection is the most serious argument in the entire debate, and readers should notice what it has accomplished dialectically. It has forced the dispute onto the terrain of method: not what the Soviet Union was like, but how a Marxist classification is properly performed. It has also exposed the genuine vulnerability of the control criterion, its dependence on a small stock of characterising texts and its silence about scale. Any answer that contents itself with repeating the association passage louder has not understood the objection. The answer must show, first, that the base and superstructure distinction does not do the work the objection assigns it, because the producers' position within production is itself a relation of production and not a political decoration upon one; second, that the textual foundation is broader and more structural than the objection allows; and third, that the argument from the concept's emptiness proves the opposite of what its users intend. The next section attempts those three tasks in order, and then states the concession that the objection has fairly earned.

## Answering the objection: where the producers stand in the relations of production

The answer begins with the objection's methodological hinge, the claim that producer control is a political criterion external to the relations of production. The claim fails on the tradition's own analysis of what a relation of production is. A relation of production is not exhausted by the title to assets. It comprises the whole structured relation between the direct producers and the conditions of their labour: whether they are united with the means of production or separated from them, whether they dispose of their own labour process or have it disposed for them, whether the surplus they produce is appropriated by themselves in association or extracted by a distinct agency. Marx's historical chapters trace the forms of society precisely by variations in this relation, the slave united to the owner as an instrument, the serf partly united to the land and partly master of his own plot, the wage worker separated from the conditions and formally free. The producer's position inside production is the base's own content, not a superstructural comment upon it. When the control criterion asks who directs the labour process and disposes of its product, it is asking the most economic question the tradition contains, and the objection's attempt to deport it to politics trades on an equivocation: control is political when the question is who holds state office, and it is economic, in the strictest materialist sense, when the question is who commands the production process and appropriates its surplus. The Soviet producers' exclusion was of the second kind before it was of the first, and it is therefore admissible, indeed central, to a classification by relations of production.

The second task concerns the textual foundation's breadth. The objection presents the control criterion as a construction from two paragraphs. The dossier assembled earlier in this article is wider: the association passage in Capital's first volume, the Commune analysis with its institutional mechanics, the Gotha notes with their presupposition of the associated producers holding productive assets in common, the third volume's statement of the realm of freedom with the associated producers as the regulating subject, and the critical usage against the state socialisms of the founders' own century, in which Engels refused the socialist label to state ownership that left the workers as wage workers. Five independent sites, across the whole span of the mature work, in descriptive, programmatic and polemical registers, converge on one structure: the producers associated and in command of their common process. Convergence of that kind is not a slender foundation. It is what a definition looks like in a body of work that declined to print definitions in a glossary, and the objection's demand for a more explicit textual warrant is a demand that Marx write in a genre he deliberately refused, with the refusal then cited as evidence that no criterion exists. The tradition's other classifications rest on textual assemblies no stronger: the concept of the Asiatic mode, of feudalism's variants, of capitalism's own periodisation, are all reconstructions from scattered passages, and nobody proposes abandoning them for their scatteredness.

The third task concerns the argument from emptiness, the claim that a criterion nothing satisfies at scale cannot classify. The argument inverts the relation between a definition and its instances. Feudalism classified a real order because the order existed; but the definition of the order was fixed by its relations, and had history produced no feudalism the definition would have been a coherent description of an unrealised form, not a logical scandal. Socialism, in the tradition, names a form that its theorists believed history was gestating; the form's definition is fixed by the theory's characterising texts, and the discovery that a particular claimant does not instantiate the form is a finding about the claimant, not a defect in the definition. There is, moreover, an asymmetry the objection overlooks. Its own preferred criterion faces the mirror image difficulty: a definition of socialism by property form alone classifies together formations whose producers' positions differ absolutely, the associated producers of the theory and the directed workforce of the Soviet enterprise, and a concept that cannot register the difference between ruling one's labour and being ruled in it has lost the distinction the entire tradition exists to make. Between a criterion that may have no instance yet and a criterion that cannot see the tradition's central distinction, the choice for a Marxist classification is not difficult. The emptiness, if emptiness it is, is a fact about the twentieth century, and facing it is the beginning of the tradition's honesty about that century rather than a reason to redefine its terms until the century passes.

The concession the objection has earned must now be paid in full, because this article's brief requires it and because the argument's credibility depends on the payment being visible. On the dimension the objection chooses to inspect, the affirmative case is right, and every rival position that forgets its central fact builds on sand: the Soviet property relations were, on inspection, not capitalist: there was no capitalist class, no capital market, no production for private profit, no appropriation through ownership, and any classification with the word capitalism in it must perform elaborate repair work on the concept before the word can be used, repair work whose seams show. The affirmative case also correctly identifies that no available label fits the system well, and that the socialist label's failure, on the criterion defended here, leaves the positive classification genuinely open among the grid's remaining rows. What the affirmative case does not establish is its own label, because its criterion is thinner than the tradition's definition at the definition's centre. The verdict of this article is therefore deliberately two storeyed. On the headline question, applying Marx's own criterion: the Soviet Union was not socialist, because the producers whose association constitutes socialism in Marx's formulation never governed production there, and the affirmative argument for the label must demote that formulation in favour of a property test the founders' characterising texts do not support. On the positive question, what the system was: the honest answer is that it was a formation the tradition's categories fit badly in every direction, not capitalist in its property relations, not socialist in its producers' position, ruled by a stratum that was not a class by the strict test and a ruling stratum under every other description, and the grid's four critical rows remain in legitimate competition for its name. A reader who finds the two storeyed verdict unsatisfying has understood it correctly. The dissatisfaction is the accurate emotional register for the object.

## The Chinese parallel: the same split, mirrored

This article is the second instance of a pattern in the series, and the brief requires the parallel to be stated explicitly because the symmetry is itself evidence. The companion study of the Chinese case, [Is China Still Socialist?](/2018/07/15/is-china-still-socialist/), asks whether a system that retains a ruling party's monopoly while admitting markets, private firms, wage labour on a vast scale and a class of owners in formation can be socialist, and it locates the answer in a criterion split that is the mirror image of this article's. There, the property criterion, applied to an economy in which private ownership had been readmitted and celebrated, returns a troubled verdict for the affirmative, while the affirmative case migrates to other ground: the party's stated aims, the state's capacity to discipline capital, the commanding heights retained in public hands. Here, the property criterion returns the affirmative's strongest verdict, and the case collapses instead on control. Set the two systems side by side and a structure emerges that neither case shows alone. The Soviet system performed the property transformation and withheld the producers' government. The Chinese system proclaims the producers' government's political form, the party state, while reversing much of the property transformation. Each affirmative case rests on the criterion its system passes and demotes the criterion its system fails, and the demotions are mirror images: the Soviet affirmative demotes control as political, the Chinese affirmative demotes property as merely formal. A criterion that is decisive only when convenient is not being used as a criterion by either side, and the parallel exposes the convenience in a way that studying either case in isolation cannot.

### What does the Chinese parallel reveal about this question?

That the criterion split is a property of the concept, not of either country. Two systems failed opposite halves of the tradition's definition, and each defence canonised the half its system passed. The split must therefore be settled in the definition itself, in the texts, or loyalty will settle it in every case.

The parallel also clarifies what a settled criterion would purchase. If producer control is constitutive, as this article has argued from the characterising texts, then both cases receive the same negative verdict by the same road, and the interesting questions become the positive ones, the classification of two novel formations that between them dominated the history of the tradition's practical century. If property form is decisive, the cases split, one in and one out, and the tradition must then explain why the formation that readmitted the capitalist class retained the name while the formation that abolished it is the one whose classification this article's readers dispute. Neither purchase is available to the writer who decides each case on its own sympathies. The series' architecture, which treats the two questions in companion articles, is designed to make the sympathy method impossible to sustain across both, and a reader who has come this far through the argument is invited to test the criterion defended here against the parallel case in full, where the evidence and the counter arguments are set out at the same length. The test of a criterion is that it travels. This one has been built to travel, and its ticket is the characterising texts rather than the fortunes of any state.

One asymmetry between the cases deserves record before the parallel is left, because it prevents a misunderstanding of the mirror. The Soviet question is closed in a way the Chinese question is not: the Soviet system ended, its archives opened in part, its participants wrote their memoirs, and the classification dispute proceeds over a finished object whose whole trajectory is available for inspection. The Chinese system continues, and its classification dispute proceeds over an object still in motion, where the decay risk that attends writing about a living formation is at its maximum and where the analyst's discipline must be correspondingly stricter. The companion article observes that discipline by treating doctrine and structure historically and declining to characterise the present government, and the present article, whose object is beyond harm, has been free to concentrate entirely on the definitional machinery. The machinery is the shared element. The two articles are one argument conducted over two cases, and their joint conclusion is the series' position on the criterion: the definition is settled in the texts, the producers are at its centre, and every state's classification follows from that settlement rather than preceding it.

## Why no label fits: the anomaly stated plainly

It is worth gathering, in one place, the reasons each label fails, because the pattern of failures is the strongest evidence that the classification difficulty lies in the object rather than in the classifiers' ingenuity. The socialist label fails on the producers: the associated producers of the definition never appear, and their absence is not a delay that events were in the process of repairing but a structural feature stable across the system's whole mature life. The capitalist label fails on the property relations and the mechanism: no owning class, no market in capital, no production undertaken for profit, no crisis of overproduction, and a law of value that must be routed through a single external channel to operate at all. The workers' state label fails on duration and direction: a degeneration is an episode in a life, and this episode was the life, ending not in the political revolution the analysis predicted as one horn of its dilemma but in the other horn, restoration, which the analysis had assigned to the bureaucracy's initiative and which arrived instead through the bureaucracy's collapse and self conversion in fragments. The collectivist label fails on the class test: its ruling class cannot bequeath, cannot secure itself against purge from its own summit, and never achieved the self consciousness as a class that the label's logic leads one to expect. The transitional label fails on the concept of transition: a passage between modes that occupies the entire existence of the formation and terminates at its point of departure is a passage in name only, and the label survives by converting a direction into a description of permanent suspension.

A pattern that uniform suggests a conclusion about the object itself, and the conclusion should be stated without flinching. The Soviet formation was historically novel in a specific and limited sense: it was the product of a revolution made in socialism's name by a party that performed the property half of the programme under conditions that made the control half impossible to perform and then impossible to admit as unperformed. The novelty is not a new mode of production in the strong sense, with laws of motion comparable to the classical modes; the transitional literature is right that no such laws have been convincingly stated. The novelty is a new combination of old elements, socialised property, directed labour, accumulation under rivalry, rule by an apparatus, whose combination the classical theory had not envisaged because it had assumed the property transformation and the producers' emancipation were one event. History separated them, and the theory's categories, built for their union, have been straining at the separation ever since. That is why the debate has lasted a century without exhaustion and why it remains worth conducting with the seriousness this article has attempted: the classification of the Soviet Union is the place where Marxism is obliged to decide what it means, and the decision, once made honestly, rearranges far more than one country's label.

## The counterfactual: what would have made it socialist

The counterfactual question, what would have had to be different for the classification to come out affirmative, is the most clarifying exercise the debate affords, because it forces each position to specify the feature whose presence would settle the matter, and the specifications turn out to be the criteria wearing work clothes. Run the exercise by institution. Had the enterprises been governed by their workforces through elected and recallable managements, with the plan's proportions debated and ratified by congresses of producers' delegates rather than delivered as commands, the control criterion would have been met in substance whatever the property register said, and the affirmative verdict would have followed on the definition this article defends even had the property forms been more mixed than they were. Had the unions been independent organisations able to strike against a ministry as against a board, the labour relation would have acquired the self defence whose absence the commodity test registers ambiguously. Had the surplus's allocation been decided in public by bodies the producers elected, the appropriation question would have lost its sting. The counterfactual's lesson is that the changes required are all changes in the producers' position, and none of them is a change in the property register, which needed no change to satisfy every party to the dispute. A classification whose counterfactual repairs all lie on one dimension has located its decisive dimension by the shortest possible route.

### Why does the counterfactual clarify more than the label?

Because it converts an argument about a word into an inventory of institutions. Ask what would have to change for the answer to change, and every position must name its criterion in concrete form: a council with power, a union with independence, a plan with ratification. The inventory can then be compared with the definition.

The exercise also disciplines the opposite temptation, which is to define socialism by an inventory of horrors avoided or goods delivered. Had the Soviet system been gentler, richer, freer in its cultural life and more abundant in its shops, while its producers remained as directed as they were, the classification on the criterion defended here would be unchanged, because the definition concerns the producers' relation to production and not the benevolence of their direction. Had it been harsher and poorer while its producers genuinely governed, the classification would be affirmative and the society a grim warning of a different kind. These implications are uncomfortable in both directions, which is the mark of a criterion honestly applied: it classifies by structure and lets the moral and historical judgements be made separately, in their own vocabularies, where they can be made with full force. The conflation of the classification with the judgement is the original sin of the debate's popular conduct, and the counterfactual is the readiest instrument for separating them, in the classroom and in the argument alike.

## How the system was built: the sequence that made the question possible

The classification dispute is sometimes conducted as if the Soviet configuration had descended complete, but it was assembled across two decades by decisions taken under pressures that the positions weigh differently, and a reader who knows the assembly sequence can see where each position fastens its interpretation. The revolution of October 1917 was made through the soviets, and its first economic decrees ran with the grain of producer control: the decree on workers' control authorised factory committees to supervise management, land passed to those who worked it under the land decree, and the new state's founding documents spoke the language of the producers' own rule. Within months, the pressures of collapse and civil war drove the opposite current. Lenin argued for one man management in the spring of 1918, for the subordination of the factory committees to the unions and the unions to the state, for Taylorist discipline and piece wages, defending each as a necessity that history would later relax. The trade union debate of 1920 and 1921 settled the institutional question for the duration: against Trotsky's proposal to militarise labour and the Workers' Opposition's demand that the unions administer production, the winning formula made the unions schools and transmission belts within a state that directed the economy itself. The Workers' Opposition, led by Alexander Shlyapnikov and Alexandra Kollontai, is the road not taken that matters most to this article's question, since its programme was, in substance, the control criterion institutionalised, and its defeat and the ban on factions that accompanied it closed the organisational channel through which producer control might have been constructed from inside the revolution's own camp.

The New Economic Policy of the nineteen twenties then built a mixed economy under the party's political monopoly: private trade and small manufacture revived, foreign concessions were invited, and the commanding heights remained in state hands. For the classification question, the NEP years are the debate's control experiment, since nobody, including the state's own theorists, called that configuration socialist in the completed sense; it was a retreat, avowed as such, and its existence proves that the tradition's Soviet custodians could distinguish property mixtures from socialism when the mixture was undeniable. The turn at the decade's end abolished the mixture. Collectivisation swept the peasantry into collective and state farms under conditions of violence whose scale is part of the record treated elsewhere; the first Five Year Plan subordinated the whole economy to accumulation targets; private trade was extinguished; and by the middle nineteen thirties the property transformation was total. The constitution of 1936 then performed the classification officially, declaring socialism built in the main, and the declaration's timing is itself evidence in the definitional dispute: the label was conferred at the moment the property transformation was completed, by the criterion of property, at precisely the period when the producers' institutional position had reached its most subordinate. The sequence thus embodies the criterion split in historical form. The property line rises monotonically across the two decades; the control line falls across the same span; and the official classification was pronounced by reading the first line only.

Each position reads the sequence according to its criterion, and the readings are worth distinguishing because they show the debate is about evidence as well as definition. The affirmative reading stresses continuity of intention and the external pressures, war, invasion, encirclement, backwardness, that bent every decision toward command. The workers' state reading accepts the pressures and dates the degeneration to the stratum's consolidation across the nineteen twenties, treating the sequence as a tragedy of circumstances rather than a logic of the property forms. The state capitalist reading treats the command decisions of 1918 onward as the system's essence emerging, the wage relation and the accumulation drive present from the first and merely perfected later. The transitional reading sees in the sequence its founding claim, a revolution that performed half its programme and froze, the freeze then hardening into a structure that reproduced the half performance indefinitely. A student who can narrate the sequence and then state, for each position, which years it treats as decisive, has acquired the debate's historical skeleton, and the skeleton is load bearing: most retellings of the argument fail not on definition but on an unexamined assumption that the system was born as it died.

## One man management and the unions: the institutional evidence in depth

Because the control test carries this article's verdict, its institutional evidence deserves a fuller hearing than a sketch allows, and the enterprise is the right place to conduct it. Soviet industrial management rested, from the early nineteen twenties until the system's end, on the principle of one man authority: the director bore sole responsibility for the enterprise's plan fulfilment and wielded corresponding powers over hiring, assignment, discipline and the disposition of the enterprise's internal funds. The principle was not a Stalinist improvisation. It was argued by Lenin against the collegial management favoured in the revolution's first months, defended as the condition of accountability in large scale production, and retained through every subsequent reform wave, including the experiments with collective bodies that periodically decorated it without displacing it. Around the director stood the enterprise's other powers: the party committee, representing the ruling organisation inside the workplace; the union committee, administering welfare, housing queues, holiday allocations and the mobilisation of effort; and, in the later decades, various councils and conferences with consultative voices. The distribution of real decision among these bodies is the subject of a substantial scholarly literature, and its finding, across the political spectrum of its authors, is consistent in structure: decisions about what the enterprise produced, at what pace, with what labour force and what investment, were made in the negotiation between the director and the organs above him, the trust, the ministry, the planning agencies, and the workforce's bodies entered the process as administrators of its consequences and petitioners at its margins.

The unions' position completes the picture, and it is the position the commodity test and the control test both consult. A trade union, in the tradition's understanding, is the producers' defensive organisation against the employer, and its independence is measured by its capacity to oppose the employer's decisions in the producers' interest, up to the withdrawal of labour. The Soviet unions, after the settlement of 1921, were constitutionally inside the employer's structure: their central council was an organ of the same state that employed their members, their officials were appointed through the same nomenklatura channels, and their stated functions ran to production mobilisation, labour discipline, socialist competition and the administration of the social wage. Strikes occurred in the system's life, as episodes of desperation, and were treated as disorders rather than as the exercise of a right. The significance for the classification is precise. The one institution through which producers in every other industrial system exercise a measure of collective control over their conditions was, in the Soviet case, an arm of the directing apparatus, so that the producers confronted production without an organisation of their own at any level, enterprise, industry or state. Defenders of the affirmative classification reply, as they must, that a union's independence is a political form and that the substance of the producers' interest was carried by the party and the plan. The reply returns the argument to the representative claim examined earlier, and the counterfactual test applies with the same result: a workforce that could not dismiss a director, reject a norm or defend a struck demand through its own organisation did not control production in any sense the word control survives translation into institutions.

Two qualifications keep the account honest, and both are documented in the scholarship the debate draws on. The first is that the director's authority, absolute on paper, was in practice negotiated daily with a workforce whose cooperation could not be commanded into existence: labour shortage gave workers the sanction of exit, turnover was chronically high, effort was bargained informally on the shop floor, and managers who drove their collectives too hard found their plans unfulfilled for want of hands. Students of the system called this the workers' negative power, and it was real. The second qualification is that the plan itself, the director's command from above, was likewise negotiated, as the next section describes, so that the enterprise sat inside a web of bargaining rather than at the end of a chain of pure command. Neither qualification rescues the control criterion's affirmative answer, and the reason should be stated exactly: negative power, the capacity to withhold, to leave, to slow, is a property of labour in every system including the most coercive, and it differs in kind from the positive government of production, the setting of aims and the disposition of the product, which is what the definition's association exercises. The Soviet producers had the first in unusual measure, because the system's labour hunger armed them with it. They never had the second at all.

## The wage system in detail: piece rates, norms and differentiation

The wage system is the test bed where the commodity question and the accumulation question meet, and its detail rewards attention because both sides of the debate mine it. Soviet pay was built, from the industrialisation drive onward, on differentiation as a deliberate instrument: wage scales graded by skill and sector, heavy industry favoured over light, piece rates tying earnings to output, output norms set by technical bureaux and periodically revised upward, bonuses for plan fulfilment at enterprise and individual level, and a Stakhanovite movement in the middle nineteen thirties that celebrated record breaking producers and rewarded them with earnings and privileges far above the norm. The system's egalitarian counterweights were real and located elsewhere: in the social wage of free services, subsidised food and housing, and in the compression of the money scales by comparison with capitalist economies. The structure as a whole therefore presented the analyst with a deliberate hybrid: money wages behaving as incentives in a labour process organised like capitalism's, inside an economy whose consumption was substantially decommodified at the base. Each position in the debate reads the hybrid according to its lights. The state capitalist reading takes the piece rate and the norm as the capital relation's fingerprints, the purchase of labour power by the unit of effort with the surplus extracted through the norm's ratchet. The affirmative reading takes the social wage and the scales' compression as the system's signature, the wage persisting as a distributive form, distribution according to work, inside relations that property had already transformed. The workers' state reading splits the exhibit, treating the wage forms as part of the revolution's inheritance under scarcity and the Stakhanovite cult as the bureaucracy's instrument for dividing the workforce it feared.

What the detail adds to the classification argument, beyond exhibits for the prosecution and the defence, is a clarification of the commodity test's split verdict. The wage in the Soviet enterprise was not a price formed by the confrontation of buyers and sellers of labour power, since there was one buyer, acting through administered scales, and the sellers could not combine to raise it. It was, however, the medium through which labour was allocated among sectors and effort was extracted within them, and the planners manipulated it for both purposes with complete self consciousness, widening differentials when effort or migration was wanted and narrowing them when doctrine or discontent required. A price administered for allocative purposes by a monopsonist is a strange commodity, and the strangeness is the finding: the wage form persisted as an instrument after the labour market that gives the form its classical meaning had been abolished. Positions that call the system capitalist on the wage evidence must explain the missing market; positions that call it socialist on the social wage evidence must explain the ratchet and the piece rate; and the transitional position, which explains both by the formation's incompleteness, collects another exhibit. The tests, taken together, keep returning the same mixed report, which is why this article's argument finally rests the classification on the one test whose report is not mixed and on the textual question of that test's authority.

## The plan in practice: bargaining inside the command

The plan is the affirmative case's central institutional exhibit, and it deserves examination as it actually functioned, because the classification argument turns partly on what kind of act planning was. The mechanism's anatomy is set out in full in [Soviet Planning: How It Worked](/2019/04/15/soviet-planning-how-it-worked/); what follows concerns its bearing on the definition. In the textbook account, the planning organs established the economy's proportions, the ministries translated them into enterprise targets, materials were allocated by certificate, and the plan's fulfilment was the system's measure of success. In the documented practice, every stage of that sequence was a negotiation. Enterprises bargained for lower output targets and higher input allocations, hoarded materials and labour against future demands, concealed reserves of capacity, and cultivated the supply expediters and informal networks through which shortages were actually relieved. Ministries bargained with the planning organs for their sectors' shares. The plan that emerged was therefore not the product of a single social intelligence surveying the whole, as the classical image of planning imagines, nor was it a market outcome; it was the sediment of institutional bargaining among the apparatus's own levels, ratified in documents of immense specificity. Scholars of the system across the interpretive spectrum, from its sympathetic economists to its hostile anatomists, converge on this description, and the convergence matters more than any of their classifications.

For the definitional question, the plan's texture cuts in two directions, and both should be registered. Against a naive affirmative use, the bargaining plan is not the associated producers regulating their own production, as the founding texts picture it: the producers appear in the process neither as its authors nor as its ratifiers, and the intelligence doing the regulating is the apparatus's, distributed among its offices and distorted by their interests. Against a naive state capitalist use, the bargaining plan is equally not a market in disguise: the bargainers were not owners, their currency was plan indicators rather than profit, enterprises could not be bankrupted out of existence, and the whole process ran inside a shell of command that could and did override any bargain when the centre's priorities demanded. The plan, accurately described, is the institutional form of the anomaly this article keeps encountering: a mechanism of social coordination that was neither the market's anarchy nor the association's self government, but a third thing, administration, whose classification is the debate. The point generalises beyond the plan. Again and again, the Soviet evidence presents the investigator with mechanisms that are genuinely third things, and the five positions can be understood as five strategies for refusing the thirdness, each pressing the object into a known category by main force and paying for the pressure with a characteristic distortion. The grid's falsifier column is the reader's protection: a position that can say what evidence would embarrass it has admitted the thirdness in advance, and the positions differ honourably in how much of it they admit.

## The debate inside the movement: from the first polemics to the schism of the giants

The classification dispute has its own history, and the history explains the positions' shapes better than any summary of their doctrines, because each position was forged against a predecessor in a sequence of polemics whose occasions were as much political as theoretical. The dispute's first form appeared inside the revolution's own camp, in the arguments of 1918 to 1921 over workers' control, the unions and the militarisation of labour, where the control criterion's institutional programme was defeated organisationally before it was answered theoretically. Its second form was the exile polemic of the nineteen thirties, in which Trotsky's workers' state analysis was constructed against both the official doctrine and the early new class and state capitalist anticipations, including the Italian and German opposition currents that had begun to describe the bureaucracy as a class while the decade still had years to run. The third form was the great schism of 1939 and 1940 inside Trotsky's own movement, when the Soviet Union's expansion into Poland and Finland forced the classification question into an immediate practical dilemma, defence or no defence, and Burnham and Shachtman departed to build the collectivist alternative while the orthodox defended the degenerated workers' state formula. The fourth form was the postwar construction of the systematic alternatives, Cliff's state capitalism and the Fourth International's deformed workers' states, each answering the other's founding texts across the nineteen fifties.

The fifth form, and the one that carried the dispute to its widest audience, was the Sino-Soviet polemic of the nineteen sixties, in which the Chinese party, breaking with Moscow, classified the Soviet system as a restoration of capitalism effected by a revisionist clique, and the Soviet party replied in kind. The polemic matters to this article for two reasons beyond its scale. It demonstrated that the classification weapon could be wielded by ruling parties against each other with the whole apparatus of state publishing behind it, which permanently stained the dispute's vocabulary with the suspicion of instrumental use. And it posed, from within the affirmative camp's own family, the sharpest version of the question this article has pressed: the Chinese charge, whatever its motives, was precisely that property forms administered by a stratum ruling in its own interest do not constitute socialism, which is the control criterion's contention translated into anathema. The Soviet reply, that the Chinese had confused socialism's essence with the accidents of a development stage, was the property criterion's contention in the same register. The giants' schism was thus the criterion split conducted by other means, and a reader who grasps that has grasped why the companion article on the Chinese question and the present article are a pair: the two states spent a decade classifying each other with the two halves of the definition, each exempting itself by the half it happened to satisfy.

The dispute's later forms, the academic consolidation of the nineteen seventies and eighties and the retrospective literature after the system's end, changed its temperature without settling its substance. Ticktin's transitional analysis and the Critique school's work belong to this phase, as does the mature statement of the state capitalist case in Cliff's later writings and the workers' state tradition's defence of its founder's dilemma against the evidence of the collapse. The posthumous phase, after 1991, added the archives and the memoirs to the evidence base without altering the criterion dispute at all, which is the strongest confirmation available of this article's opening claim: the question was never going to be settled by facts about the Soviet Union, because the facts were substantially agreed among the serious parties for half a century. It is settled, where it is settled, at the level of the definition, and the definition is settled in the tradition's founding texts or nowhere.

## The political record and the definitional question: a separation maintained

A methodological commitment announced at the outset must be honoured explicitly at the point where it is most likely to be suspected of evasion. This article has not used the Soviet political record, the terror, the camps, the purges, the suppression of nations and of dissent, to settle the definitional question, and the omission is principled rather than squeamish. The record is real, it is documented, and it is treated at length elsewhere in this series, in the articles on the system's history and its collapse. It does not classify. A socialist society, if one existed, could commit crimes without ceasing to be socialist, just as capitalist societies have encompassed both liberal and murderous regimes without the variation disturbing the classification of their economies; and a society innocent of every charge in the record would not become socialist by its innocence if its producers did not govern its production. Classification by moral revulsion is the mirror image of classification by loyalty, and the debate has suffered from both in equal measure. The control criterion's use in this article is not a laundering of the record into a definition. It is a structural test, drawn from the tradition's own texts, which would return the same verdict on a Soviet Union of exemplary mildness, and it would return an affirmative verdict on a hypothetical associated producers' order of considerable harshness. Readers who require the record's full weight will find it in the companion articles, where it belongs and where it is given without dilution. Readers who suspect that the separation is a device for acquitting the system should test the suspicion against the verdict the separation produced here, which is the negative one, reached by the road that does not pass through the record at all.

The separation has a second edge, directed at the affirmative tradition's own habits. Just as the record's horrors do not classify, neither do the record's achievements: the industrialisation of a backward country, the defeat of the Nazi invasion at a cost the record elsewhere counts, the spread of literacy and of higher education, the conquest of space. These are the affirmative literature's favourite exhibits, and they are as irrelevant to the classification as the terror, because a definition is not a balance sheet. A system does not become socialist by achieving greatly in socialism's name, any more than it becomes capitalist by trading profitably. The achievements testify to the capacities of a directed economy and to the exertions of the Soviet peoples; the classification testifies only to the structure within which the exertions were organised. Keeping the two testimonies apart is the whole of this article's method, and it is offered to the reader as the transferable skill the article most hopes to impart: in any dispute where a label carries honour or shame, fix the criterion, apply it to the structure, and let the honour and the shame be argued afterward, in their own terms, where they cannot corrupt the classification and the classification cannot be used to smuggle them away.

## Misreadings to correct

Five misreadings recur so reliably in the wider discussion that correcting them is a service in itself, and each correction has been prepared by the sections above. The first holds that the question is settled by what the state called itself. A self description is evidence about a state's legitimation, not about its structure; the Soviet constitution's declaration of 1936 is a document in the history of the doctrine, and the classification question begins precisely where the declaration ends, with the criteria the declaration presupposed. The second holds that state ownership alone settles the question affirmatively. State ownership is a property fact, and the whole argument of this article is that the tradition's definition contains a second constituent, the producers' association, which the property fact cannot supply; Engels was refusing this exact inference, against the state socialists of his own day, before the Soviet state existed. The third holds that all Marxists outside the Soviet bloc agreed the system was not socialist. The record shows the opposite distribution: the affirmative position was held by mass parties, by governments, and by independent scholars of the standing of Dobb, and several of the critical positions were minority reports even within the anti Stalinist left that produced them. The fourth holds that the new class thesis is uncontested, a plain fact awaiting acknowledgement. Djilas's thesis is an argument with a defined weak point, the transmission test, and it was contested at the time by critics who shared its author's hostility to the stratum he described; treating it as settled misrepresents both the thesis and its critics.

### Which misreading does the most damage in ordinary argument?

The fifth: that the answer to this question determines whether Marxism stands refuted. The classification of one state, under criteria drawn from a tradition's texts, is a finding about the fit between a formation and a definition. The tradition's truth or falsity turns on its analyses and predictions across the whole of its subject matter, not on a label.

The fifth misreading deserves its expansion, because it drives the popular form of the dispute and explains its temperature. If the Soviet Union was socialist, the argument runs, then socialism has been tried and has produced that, and the tradition is answered by its own experiment. If it was not, the counter runs, then the tradition escapes by definition, and the escape is the notorious manoeuvre in which every failure is reclassified as not the real thing. Both forms assume that the classification is doing the work of a verdict on the tradition, and both are therefore corruptions of the inquiry. The classification, honestly performed, does something smaller and harder: it establishes what the Soviet formation was, in the tradition's own vocabulary, and it leaves the tradition's standing to be argued on the totality of its claims, its analysis of capitalism, its theory of history, its record of prediction, where a single case, however vast, is one exhibit among many. The manoeuvre suspicion, the fear of the definitional escape, is answered not by refusing to classify but by fixing the criteria before the classification is performed and publishing them with the result, which is what this article has done. A criterion fixed in advance, drawn from texts that predate the case, and applied where it leads, including to the concession that the property relations were not capitalist, is the opposite of an escape hatch. It is a wager the tradition's critics are invited to take up on the same terms: state the criterion, apply it, and abide by the result in the cases that embarrass their own side.

## What evidence would change each verdict

The grid's final column promised falsifiers, and honouring the promise in prose is a useful audit of the debate's intellectual condition, because the positions differ markedly in how exposed they leave themselves to evidence. Take each row in turn and ask what discovery, in the archives or in the record, would oblige its holders to revise. The affirmative position would be embarrassed, on its own stated terms, by evidence that a separate stratum appropriated the surplus systematically against the producers' interests and reproduced that appropriation as a structure; its critics reply that the privilege system documented in the scholarship is exactly that evidence, and the affirmative literature's response, that privilege was functional, modest and non hereditary, concedes the appropriation while disputing its significance, which is a retreat from the falsifier to a threshold the position never specifies. The workers' state position stated its falsifier with prophetic clarity: the bureaucracy would either fall to a political revolution or convert its power into property in a restoration; the second horn arrived, and the tradition's honest members have spent the years since arguing over whether the arrival vindicates the analysis's structure or exposes its central evasion, that a caste which can become a class at the moment of the system's death was perhaps a class in waiting all along. The state capitalist position would be embarrassed by evidence that accumulation was not in fact compelled by external rivalry, that the arms burden was a choice the stratum could have declined without systemic consequence; the archival record of the leadership's own deliberations, saturated with the rivalry's pressure, has not supplied that embarrassment. Evidence that the stratum lacked cohesion and self reproduction would embarrass the collectivist position, and the record of purges, of the stratum's periodic decimation by its own summit, has embarrassed it continuously, which its holders answer by pointing to the stratum's survival of its personnel. The transitional position, finally, is the hardest to embarrass, because incoherence is its thesis and every new anomaly is a confirmation; its falsifier would be the demonstration of a stable logic of reproduction in the formation, and the system's collapse is its standing reply.

### What should a reader do with a position that states no falsifier?

Treat it as a allegiance rather than an analysis. A classification that no evidence could disturb is a loyalty wearing the costume of a conclusion, and the grid's final column exists to expose exactly that condition. The reader should ask of every writer on this question, before reading the argument, what finding would change the writer's mind.

The falsifier audit yields a ranking the debate's participants rarely acknowledge. The positions most exposed to evidence, the workers' state and state capitalist rows, are the positions with the most developed empirical literatures, because exposure obliges research: generations of their adherents have mined the Soviet record for the facts their theories told them to seek, the bureaucracy's recruitment patterns, the accumulation mechanism's operation, the wage system's texture. The positions least exposed, the affirmative in its official form and the transitional in its most elastic form, generated the least new knowledge per decade, the first because its classification was a premise of state rather than a conclusion of inquiry, the second because its thesis metabolises every finding. A reader choosing a guide through the literature could do worse than choose by this ranking, reading the exposed positions for the evidence and the sheltered positions for the warnings. And the criterion defended in this article submits itself to the same audit willingly: it would be embarrassed by a characterising text in which Marx defines the new society by property form while treating the producers' government as a dispensable ornament, and its holders invite the production of that text. None has been produced in a century of motivated searching, and the invitation's standing unanswered is part of the argument.

## Definitions beyond the tradition: the neighbours' tests

The question has been treated throughout as internal to the Marxist tradition, because that is where its sharpness lives, but the neighbouring definitions deserve a hearing, both for completeness and because their verdicts illuminate the internal dispute by contrast. The social democratic definition, as it crystallised in the Western European parties of the postwar decades, identified socialism with a mixed economy, a welfare state and political democracy, and by that definition the Soviet Union failed on the democracy clause while passing on public ownership and provision, a split verdict its holders expressed by calling the system communist rather than socialist and reserving their own label for the Scandinavian road. The market socialist definition, descending from the calculation debate's socialist respondents, identified socialism with public ownership combined with market allocation and enterprise autonomy, and by that definition the Soviet system failed on the mechanism: Lange's model required managers responding to prices within rules, a configuration the command economy specifically refused. The liberal definition, which identifies the system's essence negatively, as the abolition of private property and of the rights that accompany it, returns the affirmative verdict by a road its holders intend as condemnation: the system was socialist, on this reading, precisely because it performed the abolition completely, and the label functions as the name of the catastrophe rather than of an aspiration.

Each neighbouring definition, inspected closely, smuggles in the same choice the internal dispute makes openly. The social democratic test canonises the political form. The market socialist test canonises the allocation mechanism. The liberal test canonises the property transformation and reads it in reverse. None escapes the criterion problem, because the criterion problem is not a Marxist eccentricity but the general condition of classifying by a contested concept: the word socialism names a family of conditions whose members the various traditions weight differently, and every classification is a weighting before it is a finding. What distinguishes the internal Marxist dispute is that its weightings can be arbitrated by an authoritative textual tradition, the founders' writings, in a way the neighbours' cannot, there being no founding text of social democracy or of liberalism that defines the term with comparable specificity. That arbitrability is the tradition's advantage in its own dispute, and it is why this article has been able to move from the criterion question to a verdict with more confidence than the neighbours' debates usually sustain. The advantage should not be overstated: the texts arbitrate the definition, not the facts, and the facts, as the tests showed, return mixed verdicts on every criterion except one. But the one criterion on which the facts are unambiguous is the one the texts make central, and the conjunction of those two findings, textual centrality and factual clarity, is as strong a position as classification disputes ever reach.

## The stratum's consumption: privilege as structure

One body of evidence has been touched at several points and deserves a consolidated treatment, because it is the affirmative case's most persistent irritant and the collectivist case's most vivid exhibit: the system of privilege through which the ruling stratum consumed. Its structure, as documented by insiders including Djilas and by the scholarly literature that followed, was a graded order of access superimposed on the money economy. Position in the apparatus carried entitlements calibrated to rank: housing of a category and in districts closed to ordinary allocation, medical care in institutions reserved by level, distribution networks supplying goods unavailable in ordinary shops, official vehicles, dachas, holiday facilities, and access to imported goods through special channels. The system's ideological sensitivity about the arrangement is itself evidence: the entitlements were unpublished, their scales were not debated in any forum the producers could reach, and the official doctrine's account of distribution, according to work, made no mention of the second circuit through which the stratum's real consumption flowed. A distribution that cannot be avowed by the doctrine that supposedly governs it occupies a peculiar position in a classification dispute, since the affirmative case rests on the doctrine's categories being the system's real ones.

The analytical question is what the privilege structure proves, and the answer must be as disciplined as the question is tempting. It does not prove the stratum was a class, for the transmission reasons already given: the entitlements died with the office, and the system's history is littered with the stratum's fallen, whose families descended the order of access as fast as they had risen it. It does not prove the system was capitalist, since privilege of access is not profit and its currency was rank rather than capital. What it proves is narrower and, for the headline question, sufficient in combination with the rest: the stratum stood in a relation of structured, unequal appropriation to the social product, appropriation determined by its monopoly of decision and concealed by the doctrine of distribution according to work. In the tradition's own vocabulary, a surplus was being appropriated by a group distinct from the producers, through the group's control of the state, and the manner of the appropriation, collective, graded, office bound, is a description of the formation's novelty rather than a refutation of the appropriation's existence. Every critical position on the grid can house that finding, which is why the privilege evidence appears in all their literatures; the affirmative position houses it least comfortably, since its classification requires the stratum to be the producers' instrument, and instruments do not usually require concealed distribution networks to sustain themselves. The finding's proper weight in the verdict is corroborative. The verdict rests on the control test. But corroboration matters in classification as in law, and a reader who has absorbed the privilege structure's description will understand why the representative argument, the claim that the apparatus was the producers in mediated form, has persuaded so few readers who were not already persuaded.

## Reading the positions: a guide to the primary texts

A reader who wishes to test this article's statements of the five positions against their sources deserves a map of the primary literature, arranged by row, with the character of each text stated plainly. The affirmative position's canonical documents are the Soviet textbooks and the 1936 constitutional settlement, dry reading that repays the effort precisely because its categories are the ones the system's economists actually used, supplemented by Dobb's studies of Soviet economic development, which present the planning achievement through the eyes of a sympathetic and technically serious Western economist. The workers' state position rests on a single indispensable book, Trotsky's The Revolution Betrayed, whose chapters on the bureaucracy's rise and on the Soviet Union's contradictions remain the debate's most powerful piece of sustained analysis, and on the exile journalism around it, in which the prediction of the bureaucracy's dilemma was refined year by year. The state capitalist position's founding text is Cliff's study of state capitalism in Russia, a work whose compression rewards slow reading, since its whole theory is carried in the mechanism of competition through arms; Dunayevskaya's writings supply the parallel American construction and its philosophical framing. The collectivist position is read in Burnham's Managerial Revolution, a book whose prophecies failed in instructive ways, in Shachtman's essays on the Soviet Union as a new exploitative society, and in Rizzi's earlier and stranger volume, which reaches the position before its name existed. The transitional position lives in the journal Critique and in Ticktin's essays on the political economy of the Soviet Union, a literature that assumes the other four positions as its starting point and is best read last.

Two features of this library deserve remark. The first is its internationalism of circumstance: the debate's texts were written in exile, in prison's shadow, in schism and in flight, by authors several of whom were killed by the argument's subject or by its enemies, and the literature's intensity is not separable from those conditions. Trotsky wrote with an assassin's ice axe in his future; Djilas wrote his way into prison; Rizzi and Shachtman wrote against movements that had been their lives. A reader is not obliged to discount arguments for their authors' stakes, but the stakes explain the debate's characteristic vice, the treatment of classification as allegiance, against which this article's falsifier discipline is the proposed antidote. The second feature is the library's asymmetry of access, which the next section formalises: the official Soviet position exists in complete and authorised translation, manufactured for export, while parts of the critical literature, especially the Eastern European contributions written inside the system's own world, circulated in fragments, in samizdat, or not at all in English for decades. The debate a Western reader inherits is therefore not the whole debate, and the map above should be read with that distortion marked on it, as honestly as the grid marks its own.

## The argument from backwardness: scarcity, encirclement and the queue

No account of the classification dispute is complete without the explanation that the affirmative and workers' state traditions share, in different measures, for the control criterion's damning inventory: the argument from backwardness. Its classic statement is Trotsky's own, which makes its presence in the workers' state armoury natural, but the affirmative tradition deploys a version of it as well, and the two deployments should be distinguished. The argument's core is a sociology of scarcity. Where goods are insufficient for needs, distribution requires a policeman; where a revolution occurs in a ruined and illiterate country, isolated by the failure of revolutions elsewhere, besieged by hostile powers and compelled to industrialise at forced speed, the apparatus of allocation and command grows by necessity, staffed by the only literate strata available, and congeals into a stratum with interests of its own. On this account the producers' exclusion was not chosen as a principle by anyone; it precipitated out of conditions, as frost forms on a window, and the classification should therefore be read in the light of the trajectory's causes rather than its snapshot: a workers' state strangled by its circumstances, or a socialism in its besieged infancy, rather than a formation whose essence the exclusion expresses.

The critical traditions answer the argument at three levels, and the levels should be kept apart because they have different strengths. The first answer is chronological: the emergency explanations cover the civil war and perhaps the first plan, but the configuration persisted, essentially unaltered in its control structure, through decades of industrial maturity, military security after the great war's victory, and rising literacy that abolished the original scarcity of administrators; an explanation by infancy that is still being offered at the patient's seventieth year has become a description of the adult. The second answer is comparative: other revolutions and other backward countries under siege produced different configurations of control, more and less producers' power in different mixes, which suggests that conditions constrained without dictating, and that choices, including the choice to ban factions and to defeat the Workers' Opposition organisationally, did causal work the scarcity argument assigns to fate. The third answer is the criterion answer, and it is the one this article rests on: explanation is not classification. Grant the backwardness argument everything it claims about causation, and it explains why a socialist formation failed to consolidate, or why a revolution degenerated; it does not convert the resulting structure into the thing it failed to become. A bridge that collapses from bad materials in a storm is explained by the storm and the materials, and it is still not a bridge in working order. The classification question asks what stood on the site, and the answer, a property transformation governed by an apparatus over producers who did not rule, is compatible with every word of the causal story. The debate's confusion of these two inquiries, the causal and the classificatory, accounts for a large share of its circularity, since the affirmative tradition habitually answers classification challenges with causal explanations, as if a sufficient reason for a structure's existence altered the structure's name.

There is, finally, a version of the backwardness argument that turns it against its usual employers, and the transitional literature developed it with relish. If scarcity and isolation explain the apparatus, they explain it as a permanent feature of any transition attempted in one backward country, which converts the exception into the rule for the only kind of case history supplied for generations. A theory of transition whose transitional formations reliably congeal into apparatus rule has not been unlucky in its instances; it has misdescribed its object, and the misdescription is the same one this article located in the criterion dispute: the assumption that the property transformation, once performed, carries the producers' emancipation in its train as a consequence. The Soviet sequence is the experiment that tested the assumption at continental scale. The property transformation was performed completely. The emancipation did not follow, across seventy years and every variation of leadership and doctrine. Whatever a future theory of transition concludes from that result, it cannot retain the assumption in its unexamined form, and a classification that registers the result honestly, by withholding the name whose definition includes the missing half, is the theory's first act of learning from its own century.

## Deutscher and the biographical school: the debate's third voice

Between the grid's rows stands a body of writing that shaped more readers' sense of the question than any single position: the work of Isaac Deutscher, whose biographies of Stalin and of Trotsky and whose essays on the Soviet formation declined every label on offer while supplying the materials from which thousands of readers built their own. Deutscher's stance is worth a section because it represents a method the grid cannot file: classification by historical narrative, in which the formation is understood as a process with a direction and a tragedy rather than as a specimen with a name. His Soviet Union was a revolution devoured by its own backwardness and bureaucratised by its isolation, yet carrying, in its property forms and its planned industrialisation, a historical progressiveness that its political regime betrayed daily; his bureaucracy was a parasitic caste in Trotsky's sense, but his reading of its prospects was more patient, expecting reform from within the apparatus across generations rather than the dilemma's sharp horns. The biographical school's influence on the wider culture was immense, and its method's strength is the one the grid lacks: it keeps the formation's motion in view, the sense that 1925, 1937, 1956 and 1985 are not interchangeable exhibits but moments of a life. Its weakness is the grid's strength inverted: a narrative that declines to classify cannot answer the classification question, and Deutscher's readers who needed a verdict, for a syllabus, a party, or an argument, had to manufacture one from his materials, usually the workers' state verdict with the temperature lowered.

The school's existence also corrects a possible misimpression left by the grid's tidy rows, that the debate was conducted only by tendency publicists with conclusions to defend. The serious historiography of the Soviet Union, Western and eventually post Soviet, proceeded largely outside the classification dispute's terms, accumulating the institutional knowledge on which the tests in this article draw, and its practitioners often regarded the labels quarrel as a scholastic survival. That judgement is half right. The labels quarrel is scholastic when it is a quarrel over honour. It is not scholastic when it is what this article has tried to make it: an inquiry into what the tradition's central concept means, conducted over the largest case the tradition produced. The historiography and the classification need each other. Without the historiography, the classifiers argue over a phantom assembled from programmes. Without the classification, the historiography accumulates a mountain of fact with no summit from which to see what the mountain was. The reader who finishes this article and proceeds to the narrative literature, Deutscher first among the older writers, will be performing the synthesis the debate itself rarely achieved.

## Sweezy, Bettelheim and the argument among the sympathetic

A further episode deserves record because it shows the criterion dispute operating among writers who began from sympathy with the Soviet project rather than from opposition to it, and who therefore cannot be filed as the system's enemies manufacturing a verdict. In the nineteen seventies, the Monthly Review circle and the French economist Charles Bettelheim conducted a celebrated exchange on the character of the Soviet Union and of the transition to socialism generally. Bettelheim, whose studies of class struggles in the Soviet Union applied a rigorous reading of Marx's categories to the Soviet enterprise, argued that the juridical socialisation of property coexisted with relations of production that reproduced the separation of the producers from the means of production, and that the classification had to follow the real relations rather than the juridical forms; his work supplied the criterion argument of this article with its most technically developed modern statement, from a writer whose commitments were to the socialist project and whose earlier work had defended planning against its liberal critics. Sweezy, moving across a long career from the affirmative position toward an increasingly critical account of the Soviet stratum's power, debated the classification with Bettelheim in print, the two converging from different directions on the insufficiency of the property criterion while differing on the positive label and on the Chinese case's bearing. The exchange's significance for a reader of this article is its demonstration that the criterion split is not a weapon forged by anti communists or by Trotskyists alone. It opened inside the affirmative camp's own most serious intellectual organs, driven by the same evidence and the same texts, and the affirmative position has never since been statable in its innocent form by a writer aware of the exchange. After Bettelheim, defending the label required answering the distinction between juridical and real relations, which is to say it required entering the criterion dispute on the ground this article has occupied throughout.

The episode also illustrates the debate's generational rhythm, which a student of the series will recognise from other clusters. Each generation of the tradition reopens the classification when a new case or a new archive forces the criteria into the open: the Eastern European states forced it in the nineteen forties, the Sino-Soviet split in the sixties, the Monthly Review exchange in the seventies, the collapse at the century's end. The reopenings converge on the same fissure because the fissure is in the concept, and the concept's two constituents, property and the producers, were joined in the founding texts by an expectation rather than by an argument: the expectation that expropriation and emancipation are one act. Every reopening is the expectation meeting a case in which the act came apart. A theory's maturity is measured by what it does with such meetings, and the positions on the grid are the record of the meetings to date, from denial that the halves can separate, through the caste and class constructions, to the transitional reading's conclusion that the separation is the formation's essence. The next reopening, whenever a new case forces it, will begin from the grid whether its participants know the grid or not, which is the practical justification for the labour this article has spent on drawing it accurately.

## The question after the end: the classification in retrospect

The Soviet Union's dissolution closed the object of the dispute without closing the dispute, and the retrospective literature's first decade is a study in how classifications behave when their object dies. Each position read the death certificate in its own favour. The workers' state tradition pointed to the restoration horn of its founder's dilemma, fulfilled in the bureaucracy's self conversion and the property forms' dismantling, and claimed the analysis's architecture vindicated by the ending it had sketched half a century before. The state capitalist tradition read the transition to market capitalism as a change of management within a continuing system of accumulation, noting the speed with which the stratum became owners as evidence that it had been a possessing class in substance. The collectivist tradition, older and by then largely absorbed into other currents, could point to the stratum's corporate self preservation across the regime change as its own evidence. The transitional tradition read the collapse's internal, unresisted character, a system abandoned by its own rulers without a hand raised in its defence, as the signature of a formation that had never consolidated a class or a class's commitment to it. The affirmative tradition, where it survived institutionally, treated the end as a defeat inflicted from outside and by betrayal, a reading that preserved the classification by removing the death from the evidence altogether.

A neutral observer of this retrospective carnival learns the falsifier lesson from the other side. A death that confirms every position confirms none, and the carnival is the predictable result of positions whose falsifiers were specified, where they were specified at all, for the system's life rather than for its end. The exception is instructive: the workers' state dilemma did specify the end, in both its horns, and its claim on the retrospective argument is correspondingly the strongest, whatever a reader concludes about its classification of the life. This article's own criterion is untouched by the death in either direction, and the independence is worth stating as a virtue of definitional method. Producer control was absent in 1936 and in 1985, under terror and under stagnation, in growth and in decline; the classification the criterion returns is a classification of the formation's structure across its whole existence, and the manner of the formation's end, whether by revolution, restoration or exhaustion, could not have altered it by one degree. Arguments that need the ending to make their case are arguments about the ending. The definitional question was always about the life, and it is answerable, as this article has answered it, from the life's structure and the tradition's texts, with the end serving only as the final, melancholy confirmation that the producers whose association was awaited never arrived to claim the formation as their own.

## The untranslated shelf: a note on sources and standing

This article's commission requires the position on sources to be stated, and it can be stated compactly. The Soviet official position is available in translation in full, indeed in authorised editions manufactured for foreign readers, so that the affirmative case's documentary base is the most accessible in the entire debate. Several of the critical analyses were written in English by their authors, Cliff's and Dunayevskaya's among them, and Trotsky's exile writings exist in complete and careful translation. The Eastern European critical literature is partly untranslated: works composed inside the formation's own world, by economists and philosophers arguing in Polish, Hungarian, Czech and Serbo-Croat about the nature of the formations they inhabited, reached English readers late, in extracts, or not at all, and the Yugoslav Praxis school's contributions suffered the same partial eclipse despite their direct bearing on the control criterion. The consequence for the reader is a standing distortion in the debate's apparent geography: the argument looks, in English, like a quarrel between Moscow's exports and the Western left's pamphlets, when a third literature, written from inside the formation by its own intellectuals, exists and complicates both. Where this article cites that literature's existence it does so to mark the distortion, not to pretend to a command of texts it cannot quote; claims in the preceding sections rest on sources available in English and identified in the reading guide above.

## Names and variants: what the question is also called

The question travels under several aliases, and recognising them prevents a reader from mistaking one dispute for several. Asked whether the Soviet Union was communist, the question usually trades on the popular equation of the two terms; in the tradition's own usage the Soviet state itself distinguished them, claiming socialism as its achieved stage and communism as its horizon, so the alias question, answered strictly, receives an answer nobody disputes, that by its own doctrine it was not communist, and the interesting question remains the socialist one. Asked whether it was really existing socialism, the question imports the official phrase of the later decades, actually existing socialism, which functioned inside the doctrine as a concession and a shield: a concession that the formation fell short of the ideal, a shield against measuring it by the ideal at all. The phrase deserves its notoriety, since it performs in four words the criterion manoeuvre this article has anatomised, the demotion of the definition in favour of the fact. Asked whether the system was state capitalism, the question selects the grid's third row and should be answered from that row's mechanism rather than from the label's polemical heat. Asked, in the forum form, whether the verdict of not real socialism is an evasion, the question raises the falsifier issue in its popular dress, and the answer given in the misreadings section applies: a criterion fixed in advance from texts that predate the case is a wager, not an evasion, and the wager's terms are published with this article for any reader to take up.

The social and forum life of the question, the arguments in comment threads and seminar corridors, has its own recurring shapes, and a reader equipped by this article will recognise them as the grid's rows in casual dress. The claim that Marxists defend the Soviet Union wholesale mistakes the tradition's internal distribution, in which the affirmative is one position among five and the critical positions are Marxist products. The claim that it was obviously state capitalism mistakes a mechanism argument for a self evident label. The claim that there was plainly a ruling class mistakes the appropriation finding for the class finding, skipping the transmission test where the argument actually lives. And the counterfactual question, asked in good faith, is the exercise of an earlier section, the single most productive form in which the dispute can be conducted by non specialists, since it obliges every participant to exhibit a criterion in institutional form. The article's hope for its forum afterlife is modest: that its readers will ask, of any verdict offered without one, which criterion produced it, and will treat the absence of an answer as the answer.

## The calculation debate: whether planning could be what the definition imagines

Behind the allocation test stands an older argument that the classification debate inherited and rarely acknowledges: the socialist calculation debate of the nineteen twenties and thirties, in which Ludwig von Mises and Friedrich Hayek argued that rational allocation without market prices for the means of production was impossible, and Oskar Lange and others answered with the model of a market socialism in which planners and managers simulate the price mechanism under public ownership. The debate matters to the classification question for a reason neither side intended. Both camps assumed that the definitional question and the feasibility question were distinct: Mises did not argue that a planned economy was not socialist, he argued that socialism, so defined, could not calculate, and therefore could not exist as a rational order; his opponents accepted the definition and contested the impossibility. The Soviet system entered the argument as the standing exhibit of an economy that allocated without a capital market and did not collapse, and the exhibit's career is instructive. Defenders of planning pointed to the industrialisation and the war economy as proof of feasibility. Critics replied that allocation occurred, but at the price of chronic irrationality in detail, the shortages and surpluses that the bargaining account of the plan describes. For the classification, the debate's residue is a clarification of the allocation test's limits: an economy can be planned in the sense of being allocated by command and still fail to be planned, in the fuller sense, as the rational self regulation of a knowing social subject, and the classical image of planning in the founding texts is of the second kind, the associated producers surveying their process whole. The Soviet plan's defenders and critics thus divide, once again, over whether the word in the definition names the mechanism or the subject who wields it, form or control in the vocabulary of mechanism.

The calculation debate also supplies the classification dispute with its most respectable external witness. Hayek's mature judgement treated the Soviet system as socialism in earnest, the road whose logic he traced to the suppression of the price system and of the liberties entangled with it; his classification was affirmative for reasons opposite to the affirmative tradition's, the property transformation read as catastrophe rather than achievement. A reader should notice what the convergence of Hayek and the official doctrine on the label signifies. Both define socialism by the property and allocation structure and decline to make the producers' government a constituent, one because the definition serves a warning, the other because it serves a legitimation. The control criterion's defenders, arriving from inside the tradition, are thus in the odd position of denying the label to the system for the same structural reason that the system's liberal enemies affirm it, the identity of the system with its property form. Odd alliances of that kind are usually a sign that a definition is doing unexamined work, and the work here is the assumption, shared by the doctrine and its liberal mirror, that a society is its property register. This article's whole argument is the refusal of that assumption, and it is a measure of the assumption's grip that refusing it aligns the refuser, at different moments, with every camp in the dispute and with none for long.

## The economists' own quarrel: the law of value under socialism

The Soviet system's internal theoretical life offers a neglected body of evidence about the classification, because its economists were obliged, by their own doctrine's categories, to describe mechanisms that the doctrine said should not exist, and their contortions map the formation's anomalies from inside. The central document is Stalin's Economic Problems of Socialism in the USSR of 1952, the system's last canonical theoretical text, which conceded that commodity production persisted under socialism, in a limited sphere, because two forms of property, state and collective farm, confronted each other as owners exchanging products; that the law of value operated within that sphere and influenced production through consumer goods; and that these survivals would disappear only as the two property forms merged into one. The concessions were carefully fenced, and the fencing is the interest. A doctrine that must explain why commodities, money and value relations persist seventy years' worth of development after the property transformation, in the new society's first phase, is a doctrine registering the distance between its categories and its object. The official explanation, the duality of property forms, located the anomaly in the collective farm sector, a shrinking enclave, while the wage relation in the state sector, the anomaly this article's commodity test examines, was handled by the simple device of denying that labour power was among the commodities, a denial whose grounds were the absence of unemployment and of a labour market, which is to say the grounds the critical literature also cites, put to the opposite use.

The discussions that followed, in the Soviet Union and in the allied states, kept returning to the same sore points: whether profit could serve as an enterprise indicator without restoring the capital relation, whether prices should reflect scarcity or embody doctrine, whether the plan's commands or the economy's signals should govern the enterprise's margin of decision. The Kosygin reforms and the Liberman proposals were the practical issue of these debates, and their fate, announced, diluted and abandoned, is usually told as a story of bureaucratic conservatism. For the classification question the debates matter differently. They show the system's own specialists discovering, inside their categories, that the formation's mechanisms did not match the doctrine's description of a socialist economy's mechanisms, and groping toward hybrid instruments that the doctrine could neither authorise nor forbid. A formation whose operators must perpetually improvise between its theory and its practice is exhibiting, in its intellectual life, the same incompleteness the transitional position asserts of its structure. The affirmative classification requires the doctrine and the formation to coincide in essentials. The economists' quarrel is the record of their non coincidence, kept by the doctrine's own custodians, and it deserves a larger place in the classification literature than the polemical traditions, which prefer their evidence from outside, have usually given it.

## The countryside: the second form of property and its producers

The classification debate is usually conducted over the industrial economy, but a third of the formation's producers worked the land, and the countryside's institutions test the criteria in a second laboratory whose results corroborate the first. Soviet agriculture rested on the two forms of socialist property the doctrine distinguished: the state farm, an enterprise like any other, whose workers were wage employees of the state; and the collective farm, formally a cooperative of its members, owning its product and its non land assets in common, electing its chair in form, and standing in a relation to the state defined by compulsory deliveries, machine tractor stations in the earlier decades, and procurement prices set from above. The collective farm was the doctrine's exhibit for cooperative property as a road to socialism, and its members' actual position reproduced the industrial pattern with rural variations: the chair was in practice confirmed through the party's channels, the workday unit through which members were remunerated tied their income to the collective's residue after the state's claims, and the members' celebrated personal plots, a fraction of the land supplying a disproportionate share of the country's food, demonstrated annually where the producers' own direction of their labour produced its most intensive results, a datum every position in the debate has had to digest in its own way.

Apply the two criteria to the countryside and the pattern of the towns repeats with one sharpening. On property, the countryside was socialised later, more violently and less completely than industry, with the personal plot and the collective farm market surviving as licensed enclaves of private production and exchange; the affirmative case's property verdict is therefore marginally weaker in the countryside, a weakness its literature acknowledges by treating agriculture as the formation's laggard sector. On control, the countryside's verdict is the towns' verdict in a harsher light, since the collective farm's members confronted not only an appointed direction and a binding plan but a procurement system that decided, before the harvest, what share of their product they would be permitted to retain, and for decades a passport regime that bound them to the village by denying them the internal documents required to move. The producers of the countryside were, if anything, further from governing their production than the producers of the factories, and a classification of the whole formation that rested on the producers' position would find its strongest evidence where the debate least often looks. The neglect is itself diagnostic: the classification literature was written by urban intellectuals about an industrial archetype, and the countryside entered it as an illustration. The formation's own centre of gravity, for most of its life, lay where its least consulted producers worked, and the criteria, honestly applied to the whole object, return their verdicts with the countryside's weight added to the same side of the scale.

## Did the classification change over time: the periodisation problem

A question the grid suppresses, by presenting the formation as a single object, is whether the classification should vary across the formation's life: whether the Soviet Union of 1925, of 1937, of 1956 and of 1985 is one system for definitional purposes or several, and whether an answer that fits the whole fits each part. The positions divide on the question in ways that reveal their structures. The affirmative tradition asserts continuity of essence beneath changes of policy: the property foundation laid in the revolution persisted, and the formation was socialist in the same sense throughout, its stages marked by development rather than by kind. The workers' state tradition builds periodisation into its name: degeneration is a process with a before, when the soviets lived and the party argued, and an after, when the caste consolidated, and its literature dates the turn with a precision its rivals mock and envy, to the middle nineteen twenties, the defeat of the oppositions, the exile of its founder. The transitional tradition periodises by decline, holding that the formation's incoherence deepened as its initial revolutionary momentum dissipated, so that the later system is the earlier one's truth revealed by exhaustion. The state capitalist tradition asserts the strongest continuity of all, finding the capital relation present from the revolution's first economic decrees and treating the whole span as one accumulation regime with varying political weather.

The control criterion, applied period by period, returns a result that should discomfort every party a little. In the revolution's first years, producer control existed in fragments: factory committees with real powers, soviets that decided, a party that contained organised factions arguing the producers' cause, unions whose allegiance was contested in open debate. The fragments were embattled, chaotic and entangled with a collapsing economy, and no honest account calls the result a functioning associated producers' order; but the criterion's needle moved, in those years, as it never moved again. Thereafter the needle rests at the same reading for six decades, through terror and thaw, stagnation and reform, war and peace, a stability of the control structure beneath every variation of policy and personnel that is itself among the formation's most remarkable features. The thaw changed what could be said; it did not change who decided what was produced. Reform changed the indicators; it did not change the direction of accountability. For the classification, the periodisation problem therefore dissolves in an unexpected way: the one dimension this article treats as decisive is the dimension along which the formation did not change, after its brief opening, for the whole of its life. A classification that must be revised by decade on other dimensions can be stated for the whole period on this one, and the statement's stability is a further argument for the criterion's centrality, since a definition whose decisive variable never varies across the object's life is a definition that has found the object's constant. The object's constant, on the evidence, was the producers' exclusion, maintained by every leadership, in every phase, under every doctrine, until the formation ended with the exclusion intact.

## The criterion travels: Yugoslavia, Cuba and the other claimants

A criterion earns its keep by travelling, and the control criterion's journeys to the other claimants of the socialist name repay a brief tour, since each destination tests a different joint of the argument. Yugoslavia is the hardest case for the criterion's negative use, because there the producers' institutions existed in law and in form: self managing enterprises, elected councils, a doctrine that proclaimed the factories handed to their collectives. The criterion, applied institutionally rather than rhetorically, asks the counterfactual question of the Yugoslav council as of the Soviet ministry: could the collective dismiss its director against the party's preference, set its product against the plan's, dispose of its surplus against the federation's fiscal claims? The Yugoslav literature's own internal critics, Djilas earliest among them, answered that the party's monopoly bounded every one of those powers, and the criterion returns a negative verdict softened by degree: more of the form, the same substance of bounded control, a difference in the producers' daily experience that the classification registers as a difference within the negative rather than across its line. Cuba presents the opposite configuration: a revolution whose leadership proclaimed the producers' cause, experimented with moral incentives against the wage forms the commodity test examines, and built its economy under a siege whose reality no serious account denies, while concentrating decision in a party and a leader whose authority the producers' organisations transmitted rather than checked. The criterion returns its negative there too, and the return is the criterion's impartiality made visible, since it falls on a case for which many of the criterion's usual employers feel a sympathy they do not feel for the Soviet original. A test that acquits one's friends is a loyalty; a test that classifies by structure classifies friends and enemies by the same reading, and the travelling criterion's willingness to disappoint in Havana as in Moscow is the strongest available evidence that it is a criterion and not a verdict in search of premises.

The tour's lesson for the Soviet question is direct. The classification dispute's participants have historically applied their criteria selectively, the affirmative tradition discovering the virtues of the control test when examining Yugoslavia's deviations or China's markets, the critical traditions discovering the virtues of the property test when a favoured case required defence. Selectivity of that kind is the death of classification, because a criterion's whole function is to be indifferent to the case's identity. This article has therefore been careful to state its criterion before its verdict, to source the criterion in texts that predate every claimant, and to record the criterion's unwelcome implications where they fall, including its negative reading of cases outside this article's scope and its concession, on the property dimension, to the affirmative case it finally refuses. The reader who applies the same discipline elsewhere will find the grid's rows rearranging themselves into a single question, asked of every claimant in turn: did the producers govern? The tradition's century suggests that the question, once asked without exception, is the beginning of its maturity about its own experiments.

## Five more objections: the anarchist, the democrat, the liberal, the Maoist and the economist

The strongest objection has had its sections; five further objections circulate with enough force to deserve individual answers, each stated fairly and answered briefly. The anarchist objection is the oldest, descending from Bakunin's warning that a Marxist state would produce a new aristocracy of the exalted, and it claims the Soviet outcome as a prophecy fulfilled: any revolution that builds a state, however named, delivers the producers to a stratum, and the classification dispute is a family quarrel among statists about the name of their common error. The answer available within this article's frame is that the prophecy, if credited, strengthens rather than dissolves the control criterion, since the criterion is precisely the instrument that detects the delivery Bakunin feared; the dispute between the traditions is over whether the delivery was necessary, a question about strategy on which the classification takes no side. The democratic objection, descending from Kautsky's polemics against the Bolsheviks, holds that socialism without democracy is a contradiction because the producers cannot rule through a state that rules them, and it classifies the formation as a dictatorship over the proletariat in its founder's phrase. The answer is that the objection and this article's criterion converge on the Soviet verdict by adjacent roads, Kautsky's road running through political form, this article's through the relations of production, and the convergence of independent roads is corroboration rather than redundancy.

The liberal objection holds that the entire dispute is a quarrel over the ownership of a word, and that the formation is adequately described as a totalitarian command economy whose classification within a tradition it victimised is a matter for that tradition's survivors. The answer concedes the description's adequacy for many purposes and denies the conclusion: traditions are entitled to audit their own concepts against their own cases, and the audit's results, as the Chinese parallel shows, govern live questions beyond the audit's origin. The Maoist objection, in its classical form, holds that the formation was socialist until a datable restoration, the death of Stalin and the secret speech marking the turn, after which a revisionist clique converted it to capitalism by policy. The answer is the periodisation section's finding: the control structure that decides the classification did not turn at that date or any other, and a restoration that changes everything except the producers' position has restored nothing the definition values. The economist's objection, finally, holds that classification by definition is scholastic beside the measurable record, growth, welfare, technical achievement, and that the formation should be judged as an economic system among others by its performance. The answer is the dimension discipline of an earlier section: performance is a dimension, with its own article in this series, and a judgement of performance, however complete, does not classify, because systems of opposite classifications have shared performances and systems of one classification have diverged in performance absolutely. Each objection, answered, leaves the criterion standing where the texts put it, and the collection's value is cumulative: the criterion has now survived the strongest objection at length and the five standing objections in brief, which is as much survival as a definitional claim can be asked to demonstrate in one article.

## How to argue the question: the method in one place

The article's method, gathered for the reader who intends to use it, consists of five moves in a fixed order. Fix the criterion before the verdict, and take the criterion from the tradition's characterising texts rather than from the case's convenience. Name the dimension each comparison uses, property, producers, allocation or outcomes, and refuse answers that change dimension mid argument. Run the tests in the open, reporting mixed verdicts as mixed, and let the classification rest on the tests whose evidence is unambiguous rather than on those whose evidence flatters it. State the strongest opposing case in its proponents' terms, with its concessions extracted and paid, before answering it. And publish the falsifier with the conclusion, the finding that would change the classification, so that the reader holds the means of disagreeing productively. The five moves are not a ritual. Each corresponds to a documented failure of the debate's first century: verdicts in search of criteria, comparisons that crossed dimensions, tests reported by halves, opponents answered in caricature, and conclusions insulated against every possible finding. A dispute conducted by the five moves would not necessarily end, since the criterion choice itself can be contested at the level of the texts, but it would end its circular phase, in which the same exhibits have been carried around the same track for a hundred years. The classification this article has defended, not socialist on Marx's own criterion, not capitalist on the property evidence, ruled by a stratum that was not a class in the strict sense and was a ruling stratum in every other, is offered as the product of the method rather than as a replacement for it. The method is the transferable asset. The verdict is its demonstration on the largest case the tradition affords, and the demonstration stands open to any reader who will run the moves in the order given and show where the running diverges.

## The doctrine revising itself: thaw, textbooks and the long afterlife of a definition

The affirmative classification had a career after its promulgation, and the career is evidence about the classification's character, since a definition that functions as a state's legitimation is revised by the state's needs rather than by inquiry. The secret speech of 1956 and the thaw that followed revised the doctrine's account of the past, attributing the terror to a cult and to violations of the norms the doctrine itself supplied, while leaving the classification of the formation untouched: the system had been socialist while the crimes were committed in its name, and the crimes were therefore filed as accidents of personnel. The textbooks of the following decades elaborated the stage theory into its final form, a developed socialism that the formation was said to have attained by the nineteen seventies, a stage inserted between the socialism of 1936 and the communism that receded as the stage lengthened. The inserted stage deserves the classification student's attention, because it is the phase argument of the affirmative case bureaucratised: where the Gotha critique's first phase was a stage of the association under scarcity, developed socialism was a stage of the apparatus under its own permanence, its defining feature being that nothing in the control structure was scheduled to change within it. A phase whose content is the indefinite postponement of the definition's second constituent has ceased to be a phase of the thing defined and become a name for its absence, and the doctrine's own evolution thus supplies, from inside the affirmative tradition, the materials for the critique this article has pressed from the texts. The tradition's critics did not invent the observation that the horizon kept receding. The doctrine's custodians built the recession into the stage theory and taught it to the formation's schoolchildren.

The afterlife of the classification in the allied states adds a comparative footnote. Each ruling party maintained the affirmative classification of its own formation with local variations, and the variations tracked politics with embarrassing fidelity: the Yugoslav doctrine discovered self management as socialism's essence when its road diverged from Moscow's, the Chinese doctrine discovered the restoration of capitalism in the Soviet formation when the schism required it, and the smaller parties adjusted their definitions as their alignments shifted. A definition that varies with the foreign policy of its holders is functioning as a flag rather than as a criterion, and the spectacle is the strongest practical argument for this article's procedure, in which the criterion is fixed from texts that predate every flag and applied to friends and strangers by the same reading. The flags will go on changing. The texts remain where they were, and a classification anchored in them can be wrong, but it cannot be merely obedient.

## Shortage as a system property: what the queue testified

The economics of shortage, given its systematic statement by János Kornai in the formation's later decades, belongs in this article because it converts a mass of anecdote into a structural finding that the tests can use. Kornai's analysis, built from the Hungarian case and generalised across the classical socialist economies, described a system whose enterprises operated under soft budget constraints, facing no bankruptcy and no hard limit on their demands, and therefore generating chronic excess demand, hoarding, forced substitution and shortage as normal outputs rather than as planning errors. The relevance to the classification is twofold. Against the state capitalist reading, shortage is the photographic negative of capitalism's characteristic disorder, the crisis of overproduction, and a system whose pathologies are shortage's pathologies is not reproducing capital's laws of motion in any straightforward sense; the transitional literature's claim that the formation's disorders were its own, neither mode's, collects its most rigorous economic support here. Against the affirmative reading, the shortage economy's texture testifies about the producers' and consumers' position within it: the queue, the favour, the informal network and the second economy through which the population actually provisioned itself were the daily experience of a society whose members stood as petitioners before allocation rather than as its authors, and the experience corroborates, at the level of everyday life, the control test's institutional finding. A definition of socialism drawn from the association of producers must reckon with the fact that the formation's members spent a measurable share of their lives queuing for goods their own labour had produced, allocated by a process none of them governed. The queue is not an argument by itself. It is the control criterion translated into the vernacular of the shop and the depot, and its testimony agrees with the ministries' organisation charts.

Kornai's own classification of the system is worth a sentence, because it models the discipline this article recommends from an unexpected quarter. An economist with no stake in the Marxist tradition's internal honour, he classified by mechanism and by the structure of power together, treating the party's monopoly and the property forms as one system whose properties followed from their combination, and declined to force the object into either of the inherited names. The decline was not evasion but precision, and it converges with this article's two storeyed verdict from an entirely different starting point: the formation is described completely only by a description that keeps its dimensions separate, and every single storey label yet proposed purchases its brevity by suppressing a dimension. The convergence of the textual method and the economic method on the same architecture of answer is the closest thing to independent confirmation that a classification dispute can offer.

## The producers themselves: a sociology the debate forgot to consult

The classification literature argues about the producers incessantly and consults them rarely, and a corrective section should record what is known of the producers' own relation to the formation, since a definition centred on the producers owes them at least a hearing. The evidence is of several kinds. The oral histories and the post Soviet interview literatures record a working class whose attitudes divided by generation and by sector: an older cohort for whom the formation's security, the guaranteed job, the pension, the clinic, constituted a real social contract honoured in their lifetimes; a younger cohort for whom the same formation appeared as a ceiling, a queue and a lie told in the newspapers. The behavioural evidence is louder than the attitudinal. The producers voted with their feet inside the system continuously: turnover as a weapon, absenteeism and the drinking the system's own press lamented, the enormous second economy in which the population produced and traded outside the plan's categories, and, at the end, the miners' strikes of the formation's final years, in which the class in whose name the state ruled organised independently, against the official unions, and discovered in the act the organisational capacity the control structure had denied it for seventy years. The strikes of the end are the period's most poignant exhibit for this article's criterion: the producers' association, whose absence the classification registers, appeared at the formation's deathbed as an improvisation, directed not at governing production but at burying the formation that had precluded it. A system is not refuted by its subjects' verdicts, since subjects can be mistaken about their own structures, but a definition that makes the producers the centre of the formation's claim to a name cannot be indifferent to the producers' own discovered relation to it, which was, across the evidence, one of endurance, evasion and eventual refusal, rather than of the conscious regulation the definition describes.

The sociology also disciplines a temptation of the affirmative literature, the appeal to the producers' support as evidence for the classification. Support, where it existed, attached to the formation's provisions and protections, the dimension of outcomes, and the dimension discipline applies: a population can value a system's security without the system meeting a definition centred on the population's government of production, just as a population can curse a system that formally empowers it. The classification follows the structure, and the structure is indifferent to gratitude and resentment alike. What the sociology adds is not a vote but a texture: the knowledge that the producers experienced the formation as something done in their name and over their heads, which is the control test's finding stated in the first person plural.

## The verdict's limits: what this article has not decided

Honesty about a conclusion includes an inventory of what the conclusion leaves open, and the inventory here is substantial. The article has not decided what the formation positively was, beyond excluding the two labels its two criteria govern: the competition among the workers' state, state capitalist, collectivist and transitional rows continues, and the article's sympathy, insofar as its evidence leans, is a sympathy it has deliberately not converted into a verdict, because the positive classification requires weighing mechanisms, the accumulation compulsion, the stratum's reproduction, the formation's dynamics, at a length the definitional question has already consumed. The article has not decided whether a producers' association is possible at the scale of a modern economy, the question the affirmative objection raised in its strongest form; it has decided only that the definition requires it, leaving the possibility as the tradition's open wound rather than its refutation. It has not decided the tradition's general standing, for the reasons the fifth misreading states. And it has not decided the historical questions of responsibility and credit, the weighing of the formation's achievements and crimes, which belong to the record's own articles and to the reader's judgement informed by them. What it has decided is one question, the one in its title, by one method: the criterion taken from the tradition's characterising texts, applied to the formation's structure, with the opposing case stated at full strength and its valid concessions paid. A reader who accepts the method and rejects the reading of the texts knows exactly where to aim. A reader who accepts the reading and rejects the method's premise, that the tradition's own definition governs the tradition's own question, owes an alternative account of what the question means. Either disagreement is progress over the dispute's first century, in which the question was answered a thousand times and argued, in the strict sense, remarkably seldom.

One further limit should be confessed, because a reader who has travelled this far has earned the whole inventory. The article has treated the tradition's texts as the arbitration court for its own question, and that treatment presupposes that the texts speak with enough unity to arbitrate. A sceptical reader may reply that the corpus is too scattered, too occasional and too internally various to bear the weight placed on it here, and that the characterising passages have been privileged over the transitional ones by a choice that is itself a position in the dispute rather than a finding above it. The reply has force, and the honest answer is partial. The privilege was argued, not assumed: a definition of a society's character is more authoritatively drawn from the passages that characterise the society than from those that describe the road toward it, and that principle of reading, once stated, can be contested on its merits like any other. What cannot be contested is that some principle of reading must be chosen, since the corpus will not assemble itself, and that the property criterion's assembly is a choice of exactly the same kind, usually made silently. This article's assembly is on the table, with its principle stated and its falsifier published. That is the most that textual arbitration can offer, and it is enough to be going on with.

## The founder's last doubts: the apparatus in the revolution's own mirror

The control criterion's Soviet application is sometimes resisted as a judgement delivered from outside the revolution's tradition, by its enemies or its disappointed heirs. The record contains an internal witness whose standing no party to the dispute can dismiss, and his testimony belongs in the evidence. In his final writings, composed in illness during the winter of 1922 and 1923, Lenin returned repeatedly to the character of the state apparatus the revolution had built, and his descriptions are unsparing in a vocabulary the later debate would inherit. The apparatus, he wrote, had been borrowed from the old regime and only slightly anointed by the new; the state's machinery stood at a distance from the society it administered; the commissariats were a hodgepodge in which the revolution's personnel drowned; and the proposed remedies, the reorganisation of the inspection organs, the demand for better but fewer, the insistence that the apparatus be rebuilt rather than trusted, all presuppose a diagnosis in which the producers and their soviets did not govern the machine that governed them. The letter to the congress, with its warnings about the concentration of unlimited authority and about the relations among the leaders, belongs to the same dossier: the founder's last political acts were attempts to correct a structure he described as escaping the control of those in whose name it ruled.

The affirmative tradition handles this evidence by periodisation: the doubts belong to the formation's infancy, to the civil war's wreckage, and were answered by the construction that followed. The critical traditions answer that the structure Lenin described was not dismantled by the construction but perfected by it, and that the trajectory from the apparatus he lamented to the apparatus of the mature formation runs downhill along the gradient he identified, more distance, more monopoly, less correction, with each decade adding the sediment he feared. For the classification, the founder's testimony performs a specific service. It establishes that the control deficit was not an interpretation imposed by hindsight but a condition registered at the origin, by the origin's author, in the tradition's own terms. When the workers' state analysis later described a usurpation, it was describing a process the usurped revolution's leader had watched beginning and named in his last dictations. The criterion this article applies is therefore not an import. It is the standard the founding generation applied to itself and found itself failing, before the failure was renamed a foundation and the standard was retired to the ceremonial texts. A classification that recovers the standard is recovering the tradition's own first judgement on its experiment, and the recovery has a claim on the tradition's loyalty that no external critique can match.

## How the stratum described itself: the official sociology and its silences

A formation's self description is evidence of a peculiar kind, since it shows both what the formation claimed and what it could not afford to say, and the Soviet official sociology's treatment of its own ruling stratum is among the classification debate's most revealing documents. The doctrine's mature account of the social structure recognised two friendly classes, workers and collective farm peasantry, and a stratum, the intelligentsia, defined by its mental labour and explicitly denied the status of a class. The party and state apparatus appeared in this sociology hardly at all as a social group with interests: it figured as an organ, a leading force, a detachment of the classes it led, its members distributed in the statistics among the workers and employees from whose ranks they were drawn. The effect of the classification was to dissolve the rulers into the ruled at the level of description, so that the formation's official portrait contained no group whose existence the class question could be asked about. Djilas's book detonated against exactly this silence: its offence was not the discovery of privilege, which every citizen could observe, but the naming of the apparatus as a group with a collective relation to property and power, a naming the official sociology had been constructed to preclude.

The silences repay a researcher's attention beyond the Djilas affair. A doctrine that must describe its society's structure while omitting its rulers is confessing, in its architecture, the point at which its categories fail its object, and the failure repeats the one this article found in the economists' quarrel over the law of value: the categories of the doctrine, built for a society of producers and owners, had no cell for a stratum that ruled without owning, and the doctrine's response, in sociology as in economics, was to file the anomaly where it would not be seen. The critical positions can be redescribed, from this angle, as rival attempts to build the missing cell: caste, collective capitalist, class, new class, unstable stratum are so many names for the object the official sociology could not house. The affirmative classification's dependence on the doctrine's categories is therefore not a neutral inheritance. It accepts a map drawn by one party to the dispute, with the disputed territory left blank, and the blank is where the classification question lives. A reader who has seen the blank will understand why this article built its own instrument, the grid, rather than adopting any party's map, and why the grid's first substantive column is the one the official portrait omitted: the ruling stratum, named and examined before any verdict is reached about the whole.

## The second economy: what informal production testified

Beneath the formation's official economy ran a second one, unplanned, unrecorded in the doctrine's categories, and indispensable to the population's survival: the repair done for cash after hours, the produce of the personal plots moving through the collective farm markets, the favours exchanged through networks of acquaintance, the goods diverted from state channels into private hands by arrangements every participant understood and no document admitted. The second economy's classification significance is easily missed, because it figures in the literature chiefly as colour or as corruption. Consider it instead as the producers' own economic activity, the one sphere in which the formation's members allocated their labour, chose their products and disposed of their surplus by their own decisions. In that sphere, and only there, the population behaved as associated producers in miniature, and the sphere's productivity, above all the plots' disproportionate contribution to the food supply, was the standing demonstration that the producers' incapacity was not the constraint the official account implied. The constraint was institutional: the sphere in which the producers decided was confined to the economy's margins and denied legitimacy, while the sphere that employed their working lives was governed without them. A defender of the affirmative classification must explain why the formation's producers were trusted with the margin and never with the whole, and the explanations return, once again, to backwardness and siege, the causal story whose classificatory irrelevance has been established. The second economy also embarrasses a lazy version of the state capitalist reading, since its exchanges were the formation's most genuinely market like activity and they flourished precisely in the interstices the state economy left, suggesting that where the formation resembled capitalism most closely in mechanism, it did so unofficially, against its own design. The anomaly reproduces itself at every level of inspection: official socialism without the producers, unofficial production with them, and the classification dispute is the argument about which level names the whole.

## The social wage and the family: the producers in their other lives

The producers whose government of production this article has found absent were also the recipients of the formation's provision, and the provision's texture deserves a section because the affirmative case's popular strength rests on it and because its structure, examined closely, corroborates rather than relieves the control finding. The social wage was real: housing at nominal rents, transport priced below cost, education and medicine free at the point of use, pensions and allowances that made the Soviet family budget a different artefact from its capitalist counterpart. Its administration, however, ran through the same channels as everything else in the formation. Housing was allocated by the enterprise and the municipality, which made the worker's home a function of the employer's favour and a standing instrument of discipline; the celebrated benefits were entitlements of the employed status, distributed by the apparatus that employed; and the family's dependence on the allocation system extended the workplace's hierarchy into the household's most intimate decisions, where to live, in what space, with what prospects of improvement, all mediated by the organs the producers did not govern. The formation's women carried the double burden that the literature documents exhaustively, full participation in production joined to the household's labour in a shortage economy whose queues consumed the hours the social wage was supposed to liberate, and the emancipation the doctrine proclaimed was measured, in the producers' own lives, against a reality in which the state's provision and the state's command were two faces of one relation. None of this cancels the provision's value to those who received it, and the oral histories are clear that the security was prized, above all by the generation that remembered what preceded it. It locates the provision correctly in the classification: as the benefit side of a relation whose other side was direction, the wage of a workforce that was cared for as it was commanded, by the same hands, for the same reasons of state. Paternalism is the word the comparative literature reaches for, and its precision should be acknowledged: a father provides and decides in one act, and the definition of socialism in the founding texts is, among other things, the refusal of that unity, the insistence that the producers' maturity consists in providing and deciding for themselves, in association. The formation gave them the provision and withheld the association, and the classification question is, at the last, the question of which half of that sentence the name should honour.

## What this debate teaches about definitions generally

The article closes its argument with the general lesson, because the Soviet question is the best available school for a skill every reader needs in an age of contested labels. A definition is a wager about what matters. To define socialism by property is to wager that title is the essence and the rest is consequence; to define it by the producers' association is to wager that the essence is a relation among people in production, of which title is one instrument. The Soviet formation's historical service, unintended by everyone who built and defended it, was to separate the wager's terms so completely that the choice between them could no longer be avoided or fudged: it delivered the title without the relation, at continental scale, for a human lifetime, and thereby forced its tradition to say which one it meant. Traditions, like individuals, are known by what they do when their terms come apart. The five positions are the five things this tradition did, and a reader who has stayed with them has learned more than a classification. The reader has learned to ask, of any contested concept, democracy, freedom, populism, terrorism, the market itself, the Soviet question in its general form: which constituent of the concept is being treated as decisive, by whom, from which founding texts, and what happens to the verdict when the constituents come apart in a real case, as in the end they always do. The habit of asking is the article's final bequest, and it survives every verdict, including the revision of this article's own, should a reader produce the characterising text that demotes the producers and promote the register in their place. The invitation stands open, as it has stood through the whole argument. Its century of remaining unanswered is not proof. It is, however, the kind of fact that classifications are built from, and the building, on the evidence assembled here, is sound.

## The army, the party and the state: three institutions, one direction of accountability

The direction of accountability, which the control test takes as its decisive evidence, can be traced through the formation's three great institutions as a final consolidation of the institutional case. The party stood at the summit, constitutionally the leading force, its central organs deciding the economy's priorities and its cadres department staffing every significant post in state, enterprise, army and union through the nomenklatura lists. The state apparatus executed: ministries governed their industrial empires, the planning organs composed the balances, the finance organs controlled through the rouble, in the system's own phrase, and the whole machine's accountability ran upward to the party's summit and, at the summit, inward to itself. The army, the formation's most privileged institution and the state capitalist mechanism's supposed taskmaster, consumed the accumulation the plan extracted and answered, like every other institution, to the same summit. Nowhere in this architecture does an arrow of accountability point downward toward the producers or the citizens in a form they could operate: elections ratified single candidacies, soviets ratified decisions composed elsewhere, recalls existed as constitutional furniture without occasions of use. The point of rehearsing the architecture once more, in consolidated form, is to forestall a misunderstanding of the control test as a complaint about individual workplaces. The test's finding is systemic. A formation can decentralise its enterprises, soften its plans, enrich its consumer sector and relax its cultural grip, as the Soviet formation did in successive waves, while leaving the direction of accountability untouched, and it is the untouched direction, constant across every wave, that the classification reads. Reforms that do not reverse an arrow are weather. The climate of the formation, for definitional purposes, was the arrow's direction, and it blew one way for the formation's entire life.

## A note on the evidence of fiction and memoir

The classification debate's evidence includes a stratum that formal scholarship handles gingerly and that a complete treatment should acknowledge: the testimony of literature and memoir, in which the formation's inner experience was recorded by its most attentive inhabitants. The great novels and the camp literature belong to the record's treatment elsewhere; their bearing here is narrower and concerns the control test's texture. What the memoirists record, across every political shade of the literature, is the experience of a world in which decisions arrived from an elsewhere that could not be addressed: the manuscript accepted or refused by an unseen instance, the career made or ended by a telephone call from a floor above, the plan figure altered in a ministry the enterprise never saw. The uniformity of the testimony across writers who agreed about nothing else is itself a datum about the structure, since a formation in which the producers governed their process would have generated, somewhere in its vast literature, the memoir of participation, the account of a collective deciding its fate and living with the decision. That memoir does not exist in the formation's library. Its shelves hold the memoir of endurance, of evasion, of complicity and of resistance, the complete literature of subjects, and the absence at the centre of the shelf, the missing genre, is the control criterion's verdict rendered as bibliography. Evidence of this kind cannot carry a classification alone, and this article has not asked it to. It corroborates, in the register of lived experience, what the organisation charts establish in the register of structure, and a classification confirmed in both registers has the completeness that the debate's best work, on whichever side, has always sought.

## The question's afterlife: why the argument will not retire

A classification dispute that has survived its object by three decades and shows no sign of exhaustion is itself a phenomenon worth a closing explanation, because the explanation returns the reader to the stakes with which this article began. The question will not retire, first, because it is the custody dispute over a tradition's past, and traditions fight over their pasts because their futures are argued in its vocabulary: to concede the Soviet formation's classification is to concede, or to win, the premise of every subsequent argument about what the tradition should attempt, avoid or disown. It will not retire, second, because new claimants keep arriving to be classified, and each arrival reopens the criterion choice under fresh political pressure, the Chinese case being merely the largest of a series that includes every state and movement that has raised the tradition's banner since. It will not retire, third, because it has escaped the tradition entirely and become a fixture of general political argument, where the label is wielded by people with no stake in the founding texts and no knowledge of the criteria, as a trophy or a stigma in disputes about policies the Soviet formation never heard of. In that popular arena the question's quality reaches its lowest ebb, verdicts being traded without criteria as a matter of course, and the article a reader has just finished is offered as a corrective addressed to that arena as much as to the seminar: the demand that a verdict produce its criterion is a demand any citizen can make of any classification, in any argument, at no cost but the embarrassment of the person who cannot comply.

There is a fourth reason, quieter than the others, and it may be the deepest. The question will not retire because it is genuinely hard in the way that matters: not hard for want of evidence, nor hard for the subtlety of its concepts, but hard because it asks a tradition to judge its own largest deed by its own best lights, and every party to the dispute can feel, beneath the scholastic surface, the weight of what is actually being decided. To classify the formation as socialist is to bind the tradition's name to a structure in which the producers never ruled, and to accept that the name can survive that binding. To classify it otherwise is to declare that the tradition's central practical century was, by its own standard, a detour into a formation nobody intended, and to accept the vertigo of that declaration. The five positions are five ways of bearing one of those weights or of distributing it, and the distribution chosen reveals as much about the chooser's relation to the tradition as about the formation's structure. This article has chosen its distribution openly, resting the name on the producers because the texts rest it there, and accepting the vertigo as the price of the resting place. Readers who choose otherwise now know the price list of every alternative, which is all that an honest treatment can give them, and more than the dispute's first century usually managed.

## For students and exam candidates

This question is an examiner's gift, because it rewards exactly the operations examinations exist to detect: a definition stated before it is applied, positions attributed to named writers, and a conclusion that follows from a criterion rather than from sympathy. Build every answer on the criterion split. Open by separating the property test from the control test, run both, and let the verdict fall out of the separation; an answer that begins with a verdict and shops for a criterion afterward reads as allegiance and is marked as such. Name the grid's occupants precisely: Trotsky for the degenerated workers' state and the caste analysis, Cliff for state capitalism and the mechanism of military competition, Djilas for the new class and its transmission problem, Burnham and Shachtman for bureaucratic collectivism, Ticktin for the transitional formation. Deploy Marx's own criterion as the answer's hinge, since it is the one move that converts a survey into an argument: the association of free producers is the definition's centre, and the affirmative case's need to demote it is the debate's decisive fact. ## For teachers and seminar leaders

The article's pedagogical centre is Marx's own criterion, because it offers the cleanest available demonstration that a definitional question can be settled textually: the class assembles the definition from the characterising passages, watches the property criterion fail to cover them, and discovers that a century of polemic was a dispute about which fragments of a scattered corpus should govern. Stage the seminar as a criterion auction. Assign each position to a group with its founding text, Trotsky's chapters on the bureaucracy, Cliff's mechanism chapter, Djilas on the class that owns through control, and require each group to state, before its presentation, the evidence that would change its classification; the falsifier requirement transforms the exercise from a pageant of opinions into an inquiry, and students remember the requirement long after they forget the rows. The counterfactual exercise works as the second hour: what institution, altered, would alter the verdict, and the discovery that every group's repairs lie on the control dimension teaches the article's conclusion more durably than any lecture stating it. Handle the political record by the separation this article observes, naming it, locating its treatment in the companion articles, and refusing to let it classify, a discipline students initially resist and then recognise as the condition of arguing the question honestly at all. The Chinese parallel, set as the closing comparison, exposes criterion shopping in the students' own drafts, which is the moment the topic's real lesson lands.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

The research frontier of this question no longer lies in the classification itself, which the archival decades left essentially where the polemical decades put it, but in the evidence the tests consume: the enterprise archives on how direction actually worked, the recruitment and reproduction studies that the transmission test requires, and the still partly untranslated Eastern European literature in which the formation's own intellectuals conducted the argument in the formation's own languages. Researchers should note the evidentiary asymmetry this article flagged, between the juridical documentation on which the affirmative case rests and the testimonial and institutional material on which the control case rests, and should treat any new source with the question of which criterion's visibility it alters. The parallel Chinese case, treated in the companion article, is the classification's live frontier, since there the object still moves and the criteria are being selected in real time by their users; the Soviet dispute's value to that inquiry is as a completed control experiment in criterion selection, showing what each choice costs across a formation's whole life. Journalists covering anniversaries and revivals of the question serve their readers best by refusing the label first format: report the criterion with the verdict, attribute each position to its living or historical holders, and resist the compression in which a two storeyed finding, not socialist by the tradition's own test, not capitalist by the property evidence, becomes a headline in either single storey. The formation's anomaly is the story; the labels are its footnotes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Was the Soviet Union socialist?

On the criterion drawn from Marx's own formulation, no: the associated producers who constitute socialism in that formulation never governed production, and no account of the system claims they did. On the property criterion, where the affirmative case makes its stand, the answer is yes, since productive wealth was held socially and no capitalist class existed. The debate is the dispute between those criteria, and this article has argued that the tradition's characterising texts make producer control constitutive, which places the burden on the affirmative case to justify a thinner definition than the one Marx stated.

### Q: What criteria decide whether the Soviet system was socialist?

Two principal criteria divide the debate. The property-form criterion asks who held title to the means of production, and returns a non capitalist verdict, since ownership was social and no class drew income from property. The producer-control criterion asks who directed production and disposed of its product, and returns a negative verdict, since an appointed apparatus decided and producers obeyed. Secondary tests circulate around them: planning as the allocation mechanism, collective provision, equality of distribution, and the withering of the state. Each position in the debate is a weighting of these tests, and stating the weighting is the first step in any honest answer.

### Q: Was labour power a commodity in the Soviet Union?

The evidence divides, and the division is itself the result. On one side stand the wage relation's unmistakable marks: a workforce owning nothing but its capacity to work, pay graded by skill and output, effort extracted through norms and piecework. On the other side stands everything the commodity form classically requires and the system lacked: rival employers bidding for hands, a pool of unemployed enforcing discipline, wages settled by bargain rather than by scale. One buyer faced the whole workforce, joblessness had been abolished as a condition, and scarcity of labour armed workers with departure as their sanction. What persisted was a wage doing the work of allocation and incentive inside an economy that had dismantled the labour market, a hybrid each literature reads by the half that suits it.

### Q: Was the nomenklatura a ruling class?

It was a ruling stratum under every description, and a class under some descriptions but not under the tradition's standard one. The standard criterion asks whether a group can pass its hold on the instruments of production down the generations by legal right, in the manner of an inheritance. The nomenklatura's privileges attached to office, died with dismissal, and could not be bequeathed, so it fails that test, which is the ground of Trotsky's rival description of it as a caste. Djilas's new class thesis answers that control is ownership in substance and that a class in formation should not be measured by mature standards. The appropriation of privilege by the stratum is not in dispute; its classification is.

### Q: What is a deformed workers state?

A deformed workers' state, in the vocabulary of Trotsky's successors, is a state in which the property forms of a workers' state, nationalised means of production and planning, were imposed from above, or imported from outside, rather than conquered by the working class in its own revolution, so that the formation was bureaucratic in its regime from birth. The term was built for the Eastern European states after the Second World War, as the counterpart to the degenerated workers' state, the Soviet Union itself, where a healthy revolutionary beginning was held to have been usurped later. Ernest Mandel defended the classification most systematically, holding that the transformation of property settles the label whatever agency performed it.

### Q: What is bureaucratic collectivism?

Bureaucratic collectivism is the position, built independently by Bruno Rizzi, James Burnham and Max Shachtman around the turn of the nineteen forties, that the Soviet system was a historically novel mode of production, falling under neither the capitalist nor the socialist heading, in which the bureaucracy amounted to a ruling class in its own right. Its claim is that the stratum lived off the producers by gripping the state collectively, and through the state the whole apparatus of production, ruling by office where a bourgeoisie rules by title. The position refuses both comfortable labels: against the affirmative it takes the bureaucracy's power at full value, and against state capitalism it takes the absence of markets, competition and crises of overproduction at full value, concluding that history had produced a formation the classical catalogue did not contain.

### Q: Did producers control production there?

No, and this is the one structural finding in the entire dispute that no serious position contests. Enterprises were directed by appointed managers exercising one man authority and answering upward to ministries and planning organs. Trade unions functioned as administrators of welfare and transmitters of production policy, not as independent organisations of the producers, and strikes enjoyed no recognised standing. The plan that governed the economy's proportions was composed inside the party and planning apparatus and delivered to enterprises as binding targets. The affirmative tradition's reply is not a denial of the inventory but a claim about its meaning: that control was exercised representatively, through the party and the state, on the producers' behalf.

### Q: What does Marx's own definition of socialism require?

It requires the producers' own association. In the characterising passages of the mature work, Capital's opening volume and its picture of free producers working with productive assets held in common, regulating their labour in full awareness, the account of the Paris Commune as the political form the producers' self government took, the Gotha critique's presupposition of the associated producers as the new society's subject, the new society is defined by the producers' conscious and common regulation of their own production. Property held in common appears inside that picture as the association's arrangement of its conditions. Producer control is therefore constitutive in the formulation, not a benefit expected to follow from a property settlement made by others on the producers' behalf.

### Q: Why do Marxists disagree about the Soviet Union?

Because the tradition's founding texts supply two criteria that the Soviet case drove apart. Marx wrote of the successor society both as a transformation of property and as the association of free producers, expecting the two to arrive as one event. The Soviet formation performed the property transformation completely while withholding the producers' government entirely, so readers who weight the texts differently reach opposite classifications from substantially agreed facts. The disagreement is therefore about the definition before it is about the country, which is why a century of additional evidence has not settled it and why the parallel Chinese case, where the two criteria fail in mirror image, reproduces the identical dispute.

### Q: What would have made the system socialist?

On the criterion defended in this article, changes in the producers' position rather than in the property register, which already satisfied every party. Workplaces run by their own collectives under managers the workforce chose and could remove; a plan whose proportions were argued over and approved by assemblies of producers' delegates rather than handed down as orders; unions free to strike against a ministry; the surplus divided by decisions taken openly in bodies answerable to those who produced it. Any one of these would have moved the formation toward the definition; together they would have constituted it. The counterfactual is instructive because every repair it names lies on the control dimension, which locates the decisive criterion more quickly than any direct argument.

### Q: How does Marx's own criterion grade the affirmative case?

It grades it as a case built on the wrong constituent. The affirmative argument establishes, soundly, that the property relations were not capitalist, and then asks that finding to carry the classification. Marx's own criterion asks a prior question, whether the producers associated and governed, and on that question the affirmative case has no evidence to offer, only the plea that the criterion is political, premature, or a description of a later phase. The criterion therefore does not refute the affirmative case's facts; it refuses its inference, and leaves it holding a true premise about property beneath a conclusion about socialism that the definition, as Marx stated it, does not support.

### Q: What should a student separate between the form of property and the control of production?

The student should separate the question of title from the question of government, and should refuse every argument that answers one with the other. Form of property asks in whose name the means of production are held and whether a class lives from owning them; control of production asks who settles what gets made, how it gets made, at what tempo, and who disposes of the surplus. The Soviet case is the great historical demonstration that the two can come apart for the whole life of a system, and an examination answer that keeps them separate can classify the formation on both dimensions, state which criterion it treats as decisive, and defend the choice from the texts. That separation is the answer's architecture and its marks.

### Q: Why does no available label fit the system well?

Because the formation combined elements the classical categories assumed could not combine. It socialised property while directing labour from above, which the socialist label cannot house; it abolished the capitalist class and the market while subordinating producers to accumulation, which the capitalist label cannot house without rebuilding the concept of capital; it was ruled for its whole life by a stratum that fails the inheritance test for class status, which embarrasses the collectivist label; and its supposed degeneration or transition lasted as long as the formation itself, which strains the workers' state and transitional labels past their concepts' working limits. Each label captures a genuine feature and pays for it by suppressing another, and the uniformity of those failures is itself the datum: expropriation carried to completion while emancipation never began, the formation suspended permanently between the two.

### Q: How does the five positions grid order the definitions?

The grid orders the five positions by the criterion each treats as decisive and by the account each gives of the ruling stratum. The affirmative position rests on property form and reads the stratum as the producers' instrument. The degenerated and deformed workers' state position splits its answer, property for the base and usurpation for the regime, and reads the stratum as a caste. The state capitalist position reads property through function, accumulation under military competition, and the stratum as a collective capitalist. Bureaucratic collectivism declares a new mode and a new class ruling through office. The transitional position suspends every completed label and reads the stratum as the unstable governor of a formation between modes. Each row states what evidence would count against it.

### Q: What separates a political criterion of socialism from an economic one?

The distinction turns on where a criterion looks. An economic criterion inspects the relations of production: property, where the producers stand inside the labour process, how the surplus is appropriated, how allocation is decided. A political criterion inspects the forms of rule: who holds office, by what selection, with what liberties attending. The affirmative case in this debate charges the control test with being political, and the charge trades on an equivocation this article has resisted: the producers' command of their own production process is a relation of production, as economic as any title deed, while their exclusion from state office would be the political fact. Keeping the two apart is what allows the classification to proceed on materialist ground without smuggling in a preference among constitutions.
