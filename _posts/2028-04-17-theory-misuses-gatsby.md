The misuses of theory when reading Gatsby are not exotic failures committed by careless undergraduates alone. They appear in published criticism, in classroom handouts, in the confident paragraph a strong student writes at eleven at night, and in the machine-generated summaries that now circulate faster than the criticism they imitate. A critical lens is a device for making evidence visible, and like any instrument it can be pointed at nothing, calibrated wrongly, or used to manufacture the reading it was supposed to discover. When that happens the result is not a neutral loss. It is a positive distortion: a reading that sounds more sophisticated than an honest paraphrase while telling you less about the book.

This article is the corrective companion to the method guide. Where the practical walkthrough of [how to apply a critical lens to Gatsby](/2028/03/27/apply-critical-lens-gatsby/) teaches the procedure, this one anatomizes the ways the procedure goes wrong, names each failure precisely enough to recognize in your own draft, and pairs every error with the specific practice that repairs it. The claim it defends is blunt: theory misused is worse than no theory at all, because a bad paraphrase merely fails to illuminate the novel while a badly handled framework actively obscures it, and does so behind a vocabulary that discourages challenge.

## What a Critical Lens Is Supposed to Do

Before the failures can be described, the success condition has to be stated. A critical lens is a set of prior questions. Marxist criticism arrives asking who owns what, who works, and how the text represents the relation between them. Feminist criticism arrives asking how the text distributes agency, voice, and narrative attention across gender. Psychoanalytic criticism arrives asking what desire the text stages and what it cannot say directly. Each school brings a vocabulary and a set of expectations about where meaning hides.

The lens does not supply the answers. It supplies the questions, and the text supplies the answers. This is the whole distinction on which every misuse turns. A lens applied honestly is a way of noticing evidence that a plain reading walks past. Marxist attention notices that Nick's opening account of his own advantages is phrased as a moral inheritance rather than a financial one, and that the hardware business he mentions goes unexamined while the moral lesson gets a paragraph. Feminist attention notices that Daisy's most quoted line about her daughter arrives inside Nick's report of Daisy's report, doubly mediated, so the novel's single moment of maternal candor reaches the reader through two male filters. Psychoanalytic attention notices that Gatsby's desire is structured to require distance, and that the novel says so directly when it observes that his count of enchanted objects had diminished by one.

None of those observations was invented by the lens. Each was already in the text, waiting for a reader with the right prior question. That is what a productive framework does. It changes what you can see, not what is there.

### What separates a good lens reading from a bad one?

A good lens reading uses the framework to find evidence and then argues from that evidence, allowing the text to complicate the theory. A bad one decides the conclusion first and then hunts for illustrations, discarding whatever resists. The test is simple: could the reading have come out differently after you looked?

That test sounds obvious and is almost never applied. Ask of any theoretical paragraph you write whether the argument was falsifiable at the moment you started reading. If the framework guaranteed the verdict before you opened the book, you were not reading through a lens. You were decorating a conclusion.

The seven failures catalogued below are seven ways of failing that test. They overlap in practice, and a genuinely bad essay usually commits four of them at once, but they are separable in principle and each has a distinct corrective. The broader catalogue of errors that afflict Gatsby readings generally, including the plot-level and character-level confusions that have nothing to do with theory, sits in the survey of [everything students get wrong about the novel](/2022/06/20/great-gatsby-misreadings-corrected/). What follows is narrower and harder: the errors available only to readers who have learned enough theory to hurt themselves with it.

## The Seven Misuses of Theory When Reading Gatsby

The findable artifact of this article is a diagnostic table. Each row names a misuse, states the symptom by which you can recognize it in a draft, gives the specific form it takes when applied to this novel, and specifies the corrective practice. Call it the misuse ledger; it is designed to be run against a draft the way a proofreading pass is run against spelling.

| Misuse | Symptom in the draft | The Gatsby-specific form | Corrective practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imposition | The verdict was fixed before the evidence appeared | Declaring the novel a critique of capitalism and then selecting only the passages that comply | State the reading as a claim the text could have refuted, then find the strongest passage against it and answer it |
| Jargon substitution | Removing the technical terms leaves no argument behind | Writing that Gatsby is a subject constituted by lack without saying what he wants or what the book shows about wanting | Translate every theoretical sentence into plain English; keep the term only if the plain version loses precision |
| Cherry-picking | Every quotation supports the thesis and none complicate it | Using the valley of ashes and ignoring the Midwest, or using Daisy's fool line and ignoring her silences | Build the counter-case from the same chapters before deciding, and cite the passage that costs you most |
| Anachronism | The framework's modern vocabulary is treated as a fact about 1925 | Reading Nick's discomfort through terminology unavailable to Fitzgerald and calling that historical description | Distinguish what the text shows from what the framework names; date every claim about intention or reception |
| Decoration | The theory appears in the introduction and conclusion only | Naming a school in the thesis, then writing eight paragraphs of ordinary close reading | Let the framework govern paragraph structure, evidence selection, and the objection you answer |
| Biographical collapse | The character's behavior is explained by the author's life | Explaining Gatsby's longing entirely by Fitzgerald's courtship of Zelda | Use biography to explain what a writer had available, never to settle what a passage means |
| Detail inflation | One small textual feature carries the entire argument | Building a whole class reading on a single adjective attached to a shirt | Weight evidence by frequency and structural position, and require at least three independent supports |

Every row of that table is a claim about method, and the sections that follow argue each in turn against specific passages. The table is meant to be portable: it applies as readily to a Bourdieu reading of the Buchanan household as to a queer reading of the Plaza scene, because the failures are failures of procedure rather than of school.

## Misuse One: Imposing the Framework Against the Evidence

Imposition is the foundational error, and the other six are variations on it. It occurs when the framework is treated as a conclusion rather than a question, so that reading becomes the process of collecting illustrations for something already decided.

The Gatsby version is easy to demonstrate. A Marxist reading that begins by announcing the novel as an indictment of capital will find its evidence with no trouble at all. The valley of ashes is there, described as a place where ashes take the forms of houses and men. Tom's money is inherited and Gatsby's is criminal, and the novel makes the distinction structural rather than incidental. The Buchanans smashed up things and creatures and then, in the narrator's famous accounting, they retreated back into their money or their vast carelessness. Every one of those observations is genuine, and the full argument they support is developed in the [Marxist reading of the novel](/2027/07/26/marxist-reading-great-gatsby/) that this series maintains as the lens article for that school.

The imposition happens at the next step, when the reading declines to meet what resists it. Fitzgerald's novel is not a systematic critique of an economic order, and it declines to become one in ways that a serious class reading has to metabolize rather than suppress. The narrator's disgust is aimed at persons, not structures; his final judgment sorts characters into the careless and the decent rather than into classes. The book's most sustained lyric energy goes to the beauty of wealth, not to its production. Gatsby's fortune is criminal, which complicates any account of him as the exploited party. And Nick's own closing gesture is a retreat to a regional and familial identity, not to a political one.

A reading that imposes will either not mention these facts or will mention them in a subordinate clause and move on. A reading that argues will treat them as the problem to solve, and the solving is where the interesting criticism lives. The strongest class readings of Gatsby are strong precisely because they explain why a book so alert to economic structure keeps translating structural facts into moral ones, and they locate that translation in Nick rather than in Fitzgerald.

### How do you know a framework has been imposed rather than applied?

Delete the theory from the essay and see whether the evidence still points where the essay says it points. If the passages only mean what the argument claims once the framework has been asserted, the framework was imposed. Applied theory changes which evidence you notice; imposed theory changes what the evidence is allowed to say.

The corrective is a two-step discipline. First, phrase the reading as a claim the novel could have refuted. Not "the novel critiques capitalism," which no possible passage could contradict, but "the novel represents inherited wealth as morally protective in a way it never represents earned wealth," which a passage showing protected new money would falsify. Second, go looking for that falsifying passage on purpose, and include the best one you find in the essay. An argument that has answered its strongest objection is a different kind of object from an argument that has never met one.

## Misuse Two: Jargon Standing In for Argument

The second misuse is the most common in student work and the most socially protected in professional work, because technical vocabulary functions as a credential. A sentence packed with terms of art looks like the product of expertise, and a reader who does not recognize the terms often assumes the failure of comprehension is theirs.

Consider a sentence of the type that appears constantly in undergraduate essays on this novel: Gatsby is a subject constituted by a constitutive lack, whose desire circulates around an object that functions as the objet petit a within a symbolic economy of class. Every term there has a real meaning inside its own tradition. The trouble is that the sentence makes no claim about the book. It does not say what Gatsby wants, when he wants it, what the text shows about the shape of his wanting, or what changes when he gets close to the thing wanted. It is unfalsifiable, unhelpful, and could be attached with equal accuracy to any longing character in any novel.

Now compare a plain sentence built from the same tradition's insight: Gatsby's desire depends on distance, and the novel demonstrates this by having the green light stop being a symbol at the moment it becomes reachable, so that his count of enchanted objects had diminished by one. That sentence takes a psychoanalytic prior question, applies it to a specific passage, and produces a falsifiable claim about narrative structure. It uses no jargon and loses nothing. The framework did its work upstream, in deciding what to look at.

That is the diagnostic. Technical vocabulary earns its place when the plain paraphrase is longer or less precise than the term. It fails when the plain paraphrase is shorter, clearer, and identical in content, because the term was then doing social work rather than analytical work.

Fitzgerald's own prose supplies an argument against the habit. The novel's most theoretically loaded moments are written in ordinary words. When Nick describes the service of a vast, vulgar, and meretricious beauty, he compresses a full account of aesthetic ideology into three adjectives a fifteen-year-old can parse. When he says Gatsby had an extraordinary gift for hope, he names a psychological structure with no technical apparatus whatever. A critical reading that cannot match the plainness of its object has usually stopped reading the object.

There is a second cost, and it falls on the reader who is being persuaded. Jargon suppresses disagreement by raising the price of entry. A reader who wants to object to the claim about objet petit a must first establish that they have understood it, which is expensive, so most readers concede. That is not a virtue of the writing. It is a defect that happens to be advantageous to the writer, which is exactly the sort of thing a critical tradition ought to be suspicious of on its own principles.

### Should students avoid theoretical vocabulary altogether?

No. Technical terms compress real distinctions, and a reader who refuses them will keep reinventing worse versions of ideas the tradition already has. Use the term when the plain phrasing would take a sentence and lose accuracy, define it once on first appearance, and never let it stand where an argument should be.

The corrective is mechanical and takes ten minutes. Go through the draft and rewrite every theoretically loaded sentence in words your grandmother would follow. If the plain version says the same thing, keep the plain version. If the plain version is clumsy and imprecise, restore the term and add the one-clause definition that lets a nonspecialist follow. If the plain version says nothing at all, you have discovered that the sentence was empty, and the repair is not stylistic. You need a claim about the book.

## Misuse Three: Cherry-Picking and the Suppressed Counter-Evidence

Cherry-picking is imposition executed at the level of quotation. The framework may have been chosen honestly and the argument may be phrased falsifiably, but the evidence has been assembled by a search that stopped as soon as it found agreement.

The Gatsby versions are predictable enough to list, and each is a favorite of a particular school. A class reading cites the valley of ashes and George Wilson while omitting the novel's Midwest, where Nick's family fortune rests on a wholesale hardware business whose labor conditions the book never examines and never condemns. A feminist reading cites Daisy's wish that her daughter be a beautiful little fool while omitting Jordan Baker, whose economic independence and physical competence complicate any account of the novel as uniformly confining to women. A reading of Gatsby as pure victim of a rigged system cites his poverty and omits that he acquired his money through fraud, and that the novel knows it and tells you so through Wolfsheim.

Each omission is individually defensible. No essay can quote everything, and selection is not a sin. The failure is not selection but the direction of selection: evidence chosen because it agrees, with no comparable search for evidence that disagrees. The tell is statistical. If a reading has assembled twelve quotations and all twelve point the same way, the reading has almost certainly not been tested, because no novel of this density is that uniform.

The correction is a positive practice rather than a prohibition. Before settling a theoretical claim, build the counter-case from the same chapters, in good faith, as though you were being paid to defeat yourself. Then write the essay with the strongest counter-evidence included and answered. This is not a concession to fairness. It is the mechanism by which a reading becomes worth reading, because a claim that has survived its best objection carries information that an unopposed claim does not.

The novel makes this discipline unusually productive because it is built out of statements that cut both ways. Nick's declaration that he is one of the few honest people that he have ever known is either a reliability guarantee or the strongest possible warning about the narration, and which it is cannot be settled by the sentence alone. Gatsby's assertion, when challenged about the past, that you can repeat it, of course you can, is either delusion or the novel's deepest insight about how the past actually behaves, and the closing page argues for the second even as the plot argues for the first. Any framework that resolves these tensions too quickly has stopped reading.

### Why does a theoretical reading need counter-evidence?

Because a framework that cannot be embarrassed by the text is not analyzing the text. Counter-evidence proves the reading was tested, converts assertion into argument, and usually improves the claim by forcing it to become more precise about scope, chapter, and character rather than staying comfortably general.

There is a practical benefit worth naming for anyone writing under assessment. Examiners and graders are trained to look for exactly this move, and an essay that raises and defeats its own best objection is scored as a different category of work from one that does not. The habit that makes criticism honest is also the habit that raises marks, which is a convenient alignment and rarely mentioned.

## Misuse Four: Anachronism and the Vocabulary Trap

Anachronism is subtler than the first three because it usually accompanies a genuine insight. The reader notices something real in the text, names it with a modern term, and then, without deciding to, begins treating the modern term as a description of what Fitzgerald or his 1925 readers thought.

An example that recurs every semester. A reader observes, correctly, that Nick's account of his evening with Mr. McKee ends with a famously elliptical sequence in which the narration jumps from a bedroom to a railway station with a gap where an explanation should be. The observation is textual and precise. The trouble begins when the reading slides from what the passage does, which is withhold, to a claim about identity categories organized in a vocabulary that did not function the same way in 1925 as it does now, and then treats that claim as historical description rather than as interpretation.

The point is not that such readings are illegitimate. The queer-critical tradition on this novel is serious and has produced some of the sharpest attention anyone has paid to Nick's evasions and to the strange affective temperature of his admiration for Gatsby. The point is that two different claims are being run together. One claim is about the text: the narration withholds at this moment, and withholds in a patterned way that recurs. That claim is verifiable by anyone with the book. The other claim is about history and intention: this is what the withholding meant then, to those readers, in those categories. That claim requires evidence of a different kind and is much harder to establish.

Collapsing the two is the vocabulary trap. It happens with class terms as well. A reader who describes Gatsby's position using twenty-first-century language about social mobility and credentialing may be describing something real about the novel while implying that Fitzgerald's contemporaries would have recognized the description. They might not have. The novel's own vocabulary for the same structure is older and stranger: it says that Gatsby sprang from his Platonic conception of himself and was about his Father's business, the service of a vast, vulgar, and meretricious beauty. That is a religious idiom applied to social ascent, and any modern paraphrase should register what it costs to translate.

The corrective is to date every claim. When you make a claim about what the text does, no dating is needed, because the text is present tense and available. When you make a claim about what a passage meant to its first readers, what Fitzgerald intended, or how a category functioned in the period, say so and support it with historical evidence rather than with the framework's vocabulary. The distinction between the two is the entire difference between a historicist reading and an anachronistic one, and it costs a single clause to observe.

### Is it wrong to read Gatsby through a theory Fitzgerald never encountered?

Not at all. Frameworks developed after a book can reveal structures the author built without naming, and a novel is not limited to what its writer could articulate. What is wrong is presenting the framework's modern vocabulary as evidence about 1925 intention or reception rather than as a modern instrument of analysis.

There is a version of this misuse that runs in the opposite direction and deserves a mention, because it is often mistaken for rigor. Some readers refuse any framework the author could not have known, on the grounds that anything else is imposition. That position, applied consistently, would forbid every reading of any book by anyone who is not the author's contemporary, and it misunderstands what interpretation is for. Structures are not limited to the vocabulary available to describe them. The novel's economics work the way they work whether or not Fitzgerald had read a word of political economy.

## Misuse Five: Theory as Decoration

Decoration is the misuse that produces the most essays and the least harm to the reader, because a decorated essay is usually just a competent close reading with a theoretical label glued to the front. It is worth naming anyway, because it wastes the writer's effort and because it is the failure that grading rubrics catch least reliably.

The shape is unmistakable once you know it. The introduction announces a lens. The conclusion returns to the lens. The eight paragraphs in between are ordinary analysis of imagery, character, and structure that would be unchanged if the lens were deleted or swapped for a different one. The theory functions as packaging.

Test it on a real example. An essay announces a feminist reading of Daisy and then spends its body arguing that Daisy is shallow, that her voice is repeatedly described in terms of money, and that she chooses security over love. Every observation is true and none is feminist. They are the observations any attentive reader makes. A feminist reading would ask a different question and would therefore look at different evidence: not whether Daisy is shallow but how the novel constructs the conditions under which her shallowness becomes her only available strategy, why the narration grants her almost no unmediated interiority across nine chapters, and what it means that her single moment of stated self-knowledge reaches us as a quotation inside a quotation. That reading changes which sentences you have to cite, and the argument it produces is developed at length in this series' [feminist reading of the novel](/2027/08/09/feminist-reading-great-gatsby/).

The distinction is structural rather than tonal. A lens genuinely applied determines three things: which passages the essay quotes, how the paragraphs are ordered, and which objection the essay has to answer. If a framework is not making those three decisions, it is not operating. It is a hat.

Decoration is also the misuse most encouraged by the way theory is often taught, as a set of positions to have rather than a set of questions to ask. A student who has learned that Marxist criticism attends to class will conclude that mentioning class is Marxist criticism. It is not, any more than mentioning the weather is meteorology. The difference is whether a systematic prior question is generating the analysis or a topic word is being sprinkled over it.

The corrective is to write the essay's evidence list before the essay. Choose the framework, generate its prior questions, and then go through the novel selecting passages those questions make salient. If the resulting list is the same list you would have made without the framework, the framework has added nothing and you should either change frameworks or drop the pretense and write the close reading. The full procedure for generating a genuine evidence list from a chosen school is set out in the method article on applying a lens, and the companion guidance on picking the right framework in the first place sits with the discussion of [choosing a critical lens for a Gatsby essay](/2028/04/03/choosing-critical-lens-gatsby/).

## A Worked Example: One Paragraph Misused and Repaired

Abstract correctives are easy to agree with and hard to apply, so it helps to watch one paragraph go wrong and then be repaired. The example below is a composite of paragraphs that appear in student work on this novel every year, and the repair uses nothing but the practices already described.

Here is the misused version. A psychoanalytic reading of Gatsby reveals a subject whose desire is structured around an absent object, in which the Other functions as the guarantor of a symbolic order the subject cannot enter, and whose repetition compulsion drives him toward a fatal encounter with the Real. Gatsby's obsession with Daisy is therefore not about Daisy at all but about the impossibility of desire's satisfaction, which Fitzgerald, drawing on his own frustrated courtship, encodes in the green light.

Count the failures. The vocabulary is doing the arguing, and translation empties it: strip the terms and the paragraph says Gatsby wants something he cannot have, which is not a reading but a plot summary. The claim is unfalsifiable, since no passage in any novel could contradict the proposition that desire is unsatisfiable. There is no evidence at all, only assertion, so cherry-picking has been skipped in favor of citing nothing. The biography appears as an explanation rather than as context. And the green light arrives at the end as decoration, mentioned rather than read.

Now the repair, built from the same tradition's genuine insight. Gatsby's desire in this novel depends structurally on distance, and Fitzgerald builds the dependence into the narrative rather than asserting it. In the first chapter the green light is available to Gatsby only across water, at night, as a point of light too far to resolve, and the narration describes him reaching toward a single green light, minute and far away. The reaching is the whole content of the scene; nothing is obtained and nothing is meant to be. In chapter five, when Daisy stands beside him and the light becomes an ordinary object with a dock attached, the narration marks the loss precisely: his count of enchanted objects had diminished by one. The novel does not merely say that Gatsby wants what he cannot have. It stages the moment when having begins and the wanting starts to die, and it treats that as a subtraction rather than a fulfillment.

The pattern recurs in a form that has nothing to do with the light. Nick reports that there must have been moments even that afternoon when Daisy tumbled short of his dreams, and attributes the shortfall not to any failure in her but to the colossal vitality of his illusion, which the narration says had gone beyond her, beyond everything. Desire here is explicitly described as having outrun its object, which is a structural claim about the shape of wanting rather than a psychological observation about one afternoon. And the same shape governs the plot: Gatsby's project requires not that Daisy come to him but that she erase five years, which is a demand for a condition rather than for a person, and a condition is by construction unavailable.

The repaired paragraph makes a claim that could fail. If the novel showed Gatsby's satisfaction increasing as proximity increased, the reading would be wrong, and the reading tells you where to check. It cites three passages from three chapters, one of them at a structural hinge. It uses no technical vocabulary, not because the tradition's terms are worthless but because nothing here needed them. And it says nothing about Fitzgerald's courtship, which contributed materials but settles no question about what these pages do.

The two paragraphs make the same broad point about desire. Only one of them is a reading.

## Misuse Profiles by School

The seven failures are general, but each school has a characteristic way of falling into them, and knowing the profile of the framework you have chosen shortens the diagnosis considerably.

Class-attentive criticism, whether Marxist or Bourdieu-derived, fails most often through cherry-picking and imposition, because the novel supplies so much agreeable material that a reader can fill an essay without ever meeting resistance. Its specific corrective is to insist on the passages where the book converts economics into morality. Nick's judgment of the Buchanans is a judgment of character, not of class position, and the phrase about their vast carelessness is a moral term standing where a structural one would go. A class reading that explains that substitution is doing criticism; one that quotes around it is collecting.

Feminist criticism most often fails through decoration, because the topic word is easy to sprinkle. Noting that Daisy is trapped, that Myrtle is punished, and that Jordan is judged for her independence is accurate and is not yet a feminist reading. The framework earns its place when it asks structural questions instead: why the narration grants Daisy so little unmediated interiority, what it means that her most self-aware line about her daughter reaches the reader through two layers of male report, and how the novel distributes the capacity to be interesting across gender. Those questions change the evidence list, which is the test.

Psychoanalytic criticism fails through jargon more reliably than any other school, for the historical reason that its vocabulary is the most technical and the most portable. The repair is the translation pass, applied ruthlessly. It also has a distinctive second failure, the slide from the character's psyche to the author's, which is biographical collapse arriving through a side door. A psychoanalytic reading of Gatsby should be a reading of a construction on a page, not a diagnosis of a man who died in 1940.

Historicist and new historicist reading fails through anachronism, which is a peculiar irony given that the school exists to prevent it. The mechanism is that the reader acquires genuine period knowledge, about immigration anxiety, about the postwar bond market, about the specific texture of Prohibition enforcement, and then allows the modern framework organizing that knowledge to be mistaken for the period's own self-understanding. The corrective is the dating pass, and historicism's own standards supply it.

Queer criticism fails through detail inflation more than through anything else, because the textual evidence is genuinely sparse and genuinely suggestive, which is a combination that tempts a reader to make one elliptical passage carry an entire argument. The stronger versions of this reading do not rest on the McKee sequence. They build from a pattern: the narration's recurring evasions, the affective temperature of Nick's attention to Gatsby's body and clothing, the fact that Nick's stated romantic entanglements are all reported at a strange remove, and the structural oddity that a novel organized around a heterosexual obsession gives its narrator no comparable interest of his own.

Reader-response and reception-oriented approaches fail through a version of imposition that is easy to miss, in which the reader's own reaction is treated as evidence about the text rather than about the reader. Saying that the ending feels hopeful is a report; arguing that the ending is constructed to produce a divided response, and showing the syntactic mechanism by which the last three sentences pull against each other, is a reading.

Biographical criticism, finally, fails through the collapse that shares its name, and its corrective is the directional rule stated earlier. Used properly it is one of the most productive approaches to this particular book, because Fitzgerald's letters and revisions document choices the finished novel conceals. Used improperly it is the fastest available route to explaining away a text instead of reading it.

The profiles matter because self-diagnosis is easier when you know what you are likely to have done. A student writing a class essay should check quotation clustering first. A student writing psychoanalytically should run the translation pass first. The general checklist still applies, but the order in which you run it can be chosen intelligently.

## Spotting the Misuses in Criticism You Read

Most students meet theory secondhand, in an article assigned for a seminar, a chapter recommended by an instructor, or a summary encountered while searching. Learning to audit somebody else's theoretical reading is therefore more immediately useful than learning to write one, and it is easier, because you are not defending the argument.

Start with the evidence inventory, which takes two minutes. Skim the piece and note every passage from the novel it quotes, then note the chapters. Serious criticism on this book almost always ranges across it, because the novel's design is cumulative and any claim about the whole requires touching the whole. A chapter on Gatsby drawing exclusively on the party scenes and the valley of ashes is either a deliberately narrow study, in which case it will say so, or a partial survey presented as a total one.

Check next whether the piece ever loses. Every genuinely argued essay contains a moment where the writer concedes something, adjusts a claim, or admits that a passage complicates the reading. The concession is the fingerprint of thought. Criticism that proceeds from confident opening to confident close without ever meeting resistance is either summarizing a settled consensus, which is a legitimate genre and usually announced, or it never tested its argument.

Apply the deletion test to somebody else's prose as readily as to your own. Read a dense theoretical paragraph and try to say what claim it makes about events, characters, or sentences in the novel. If you cannot, the paragraph may still be doing legitimate work in a larger argument, so check the surrounding pages before judging. If you still cannot, you have found jargon standing where an argument belongs, and you should not feel obliged to cite it.

Watch for the intention slide. Criticism frequently begins with a textual observation and drifts, over a page or two, into claims about what Fitzgerald was doing, what he understood, or what his readers felt. The drift is often unmarked. When you find it, separate the two halves. The textual observation may be excellent and quotable while the intentional claim is unsupported, and a careful student can take the first and leave the second without rejecting the source.

Notice how the piece treats the criticism it disagrees with. A reading that characterizes its opponents fairly, in terms they would accept, is more likely to have characterized the novel fairly too. A reading that describes rival positions only in their weakest form has demonstrated a habit that will also govern its treatment of evidence.

Finally, distinguish age from error. Older criticism on this novel often operates with assumptions that now look plainly limited, particularly about gender and about whose experience counts as representative. That does not make its close reading worthless, and some of the finest sentence-level attention this book has received appears in essays whose framing has aged badly. The mature response is to take the observation and argue with the frame, which is also the response that produces the most interesting new work. The debates that shaped the novel's critical fortunes, and the specific essays that moved them, are surveyed elsewhere in this series so that a student can see which arguments are still live and which have been superseded.

There is one further habit worth building, and it applies to the fastest-growing category of secondary material. Summaries and analyses generated at scale reproduce the surface features of theoretical criticism, including the vocabulary and the confident structure, without the evidentiary discipline underneath. They are extremely good at the decorated essay and extremely bad at the tested one, because the tested one requires going and looking. The audit above catches them reliably: the evidence inventory is thin, nothing is ever conceded, and the deletion test empties the paragraphs. Use them as prompts for your own reading if you like, and never as sources, because a reading that has not met resistance cannot transmit anything you could not have assembled yourself.

The value of the audit is not that it lets you dismiss things. It is that it lets you use partial sources properly. A cherry-picked essay may still contain the best available reading of a single passage. A jargon-heavy chapter may contain one translated sentence worth its weight. Knowing which failures a source commits tells you exactly how far to trust it and exactly which parts to take, which is more useful than a verdict.

## Misuse Six: Collapsing the Novel Into the Author's Life

Biographical reduction is the oldest misuse and the one that survives best outside the academy, because it satisfies a real curiosity and produces sentences that feel like explanations. Gatsby longs for a rich girl who marries someone else; Fitzgerald courted a rich girl who nearly did not marry him; therefore Gatsby's longing is Fitzgerald's longing and the novel has been explained.

It has not been explained. Two things have been correlated, and correlation has been dressed as interpretation. The biographical facts are genuine and worth knowing, and this series treats them seriously in its account of [Fitzgerald's life for readers](/2026/11/09/f-scott-fitzgerald-biography/) and in the lens article devoted to [biographical criticism of the novel](/2027/12/20/biographical-criticism-gatsby/). What biography establishes is availability: which materials, feelings, and social observations a writer had access to. What it cannot establish is meaning, because meaning is made by arrangement, and arrangement is exactly what the biography does not contain.

The demonstration is easy. Fitzgerald's courtship was rewarded; Gatsby's is fatal. Fitzgerald wrote from inside the class he described; Gatsby is destroyed by not being inside it. Fitzgerald's narrator is not Fitzgerald, and the novel spends its opening page establishing a narrator with a particular family history, a particular moral vanity, and a particular claim to reserve judgment that the rest of the book proceeds to test. If the novel were biography, the differences would be errors. They are not errors. They are the design.

There is a subtler version that catches better readers. Rather than explaining a character by the author, it explains a formal choice by the author's psychology: the retrospective narration is attributed to Fitzgerald's nostalgia, the ambivalence about wealth to his ambivalence about wealth, the ending's lyricism to his ambition to write something permanent. Each attribution may be true as literary history and none of them settles a question of meaning. The retrospective frame does specific work in the novel that can be described without reference to its author at all: it places every event inside a judgment already formed, which is why the book can be a mystery and an elegy at once.

The corrective is a rule about direction. Biography may explain how a passage came to exist. It may not be cited as evidence for what a passage means. When you find yourself writing that Fitzgerald must have felt something and therefore the passage means something, reverse the order and ask what the passage does on the page, then decide whether the biography adds anything the page did not already supply. Usually it adds context and color. Occasionally it resolves a genuine textual ambiguity, and when it does, say why the textual evidence was insufficient before reaching for the life.

## Misuse Seven: Over-Reading the Single Detail

The last misuse is the most flattering to commit, because it feels like the height of close reading. A reader notices something small, extracts a large claim from it, and builds an argument on a foundation the detail cannot bear.

Gatsby invites this because its surfaces are so dense. The shirts scene alone has carried more interpretive weight than any three-paragraph episode in American fiction. Readers have made the shirts stand for commodity fetishism, for Daisy's inability to distinguish objects from feelings, for the tactility of new money, for Gatsby's transformation of self into display, and for the novel's whole theory of desire. Several of those readings are good. The failure is not any one of them but the practice of resting an essay's central claim on that scene and nothing else, so that a single episode is asked to prove a thesis about nine chapters.

The corrective has two parts, and both are about weighting evidence rather than finding it. The first is frequency: a feature that recurs carries more argumentative weight than a feature that appears once, because recurrence is evidence of design rather than of accident. The colors recur. The eyes recur. The clock, the telephone, the cars, and the weather recur. A claim built on a pattern that appears in six chapters is a claim about the novel; a claim built on one adjective is a claim about a sentence.

The second is structural position. Evidence taken from a hinge point carries more weight than evidence from an aside, because the novel has invested more in it. The final page is a hinge, which is why the sentence about boats against the current can support argument that a randomly chosen sentence cannot. The moment when the green light stops being a symbol is a hinge, because the narration explicitly marks the change. Gatsby's death and the funeral are hinges. Klipspringer's phone call is not, however delicious it is.

The working rule this series uses is three independent supports. Before a claim goes into a thesis, it should be supported by evidence from at least three separate places in the novel, ideally in different chapters and preferably including one hinge. If the claim cannot find three supports, it is a good observation about a passage rather than a thesis about the book, and it should be demoted to a body paragraph where it will do honest work.

### How much can a single passage prove about the whole novel?

A single passage can prove that the novel does something once. It can illustrate a claim, sharpen it, or provide the memorable instance an argument needs. It cannot establish a pattern, and a thesis about the book as a whole requires evidence from several chapters, including at least one structurally weighted moment.

## Why the Misuses Do Not Convict Theory Itself

A catalogue of failures invites a false conclusion, and the conclusion is common enough to deserve direct refutation. Having watched theory go wrong seven ways, a reader may decide the safest course is to abandon frameworks and read the book plainly, letting the text speak for itself.

There is no such thing as reading plainly. Every reader arrives with prior questions, and the only variable is whether those questions have been examined. The reader who declines theory does not read without a framework; they read with an unexamined one, usually assembled from the assumptions of their own education, their moment, and whatever criticism reached them secondhand. That framework is not more neutral for being invisible. It is less accountable, because it cannot be argued with.

The evidence is in the reception history. For decades the standard classroom reading treated Gatsby as a tragedy of the American Dream in which the dream is noble and the corruption external, a reading that required ignoring how much of the novel is about who gets to have a dream and at whose expense. That reading did not come from theory. It came from an unexamined framework so pervasive that it looked like the book itself. What dislodged it was theory: class-attentive criticism that asked who was working while the parties were happening, and feminist criticism that asked why the tragedy belonged to the man.

The misuses catalogued above are also, revealingly, correctable. Every one of them has a specific remedy that costs the writer nothing but attention. Imposition is corrected by falsifiable phrasing. Jargon is corrected by translation. Cherry-picking is corrected by adversarial evidence-gathering. Anachronism is corrected by dating claims. Decoration is corrected by letting the framework choose the evidence. Biographical collapse is corrected by a rule about direction. Detail inflation is corrected by frequency and position weighting. A discipline whose failures all have known remedies is not a discipline in trouble.

The honest summary is that theory is a power tool. It removes material faster than hand work and it damages the workpiece faster too, and the correct response to that is training rather than abstinence. What a disciplined, evidence-anchored synthesis looks like when all of this goes right is the subject of the companion piece on [the most defensible reading of the novel](/2028/04/24/most-defensible-gatsby-reading/), which is the constructive counterpart to this article's catalogue of failures.

## Diagnosing a Misused Lens in Your Own Draft

Recognition in other people's writing is easy and useless. The difficulty is catching the failures in a draft you have already convinced yourself of, and the only reliable method is procedural, because judgment is exactly what has already been compromised.

Run the deletion test first. Cut every sentence containing a theoretical term and read what remains. If the argument still stands, the theory was decoration. If nothing remains, the theory was doing all the work and no reading was happening. What you want is an essay that loses precision without the terms but retains a spine of textual claims.

Run the reversal test second. Take the thesis and write its denial as a single sentence. If the denial is obviously absurd, the thesis was unfalsifiable and therefore empty. If the denial is arguable, find the best evidence for it, and check whether the draft engages that evidence anywhere. Most drafts do not, and the repair is a paragraph.

Run the count next. Tally the quotations and mark which chapters they come from. Clustering is the signature of cherry-picking. A reading drawing entirely on chapters two, seven, and nine is a reading about the novel's harshest material, which is fine as long as the essay knows that is what it is doing and says so rather than presenting a partial survey as a total one.

Run the translation pass fourth, sentence by sentence, converting technical vocabulary to plain English and keeping only what the plain version cannot carry.

Run the dating pass fifth. Underline every claim about intention, reception, or period meaning, and check that each has historical support rather than framework vocabulary standing in for it.

Finally, run the weight check. For the central claim, list the supports. Count them, note their chapters, and mark whether any sits at a structural hinge. Fewer than three supports, or three supports all from one chapter, means the claim is not yet a thesis.

Six passes, none requiring judgment you have not already compromised, all mechanical enough to run at midnight. The point of a procedural checklist is that it works when you are too invested to be fair.

## Building an Essay That Survives the Correctives

The practical question behind all of this is how to write the thing. An essay built to survive the seven correctives has a recognizable shape, and the shape is worth stating because it differs from the shape most students are taught.

It opens with the falsifiable claim rather than the framework. Instead of announcing that the essay offers a Marxist reading, it announces what the essay will argue about the novel, in words that could be contradicted, and lets the framework become visible through the questions the argument asks. This is not coyness. It is the structural expression of the principle that the lens generates the argument rather than replacing it.

Its body paragraphs are organized by the framework's questions rather than by chapter order or by character. A class-attentive essay whose paragraphs run chapter one, chapter five, chapter nine is a chronological essay wearing a hat. A class-attentive essay whose paragraphs run production, consumption, and inheritance is an essay whose structure the framework determined, which is the test set out earlier.

It quotes across the novel and includes at least one passage that costs it something. This is the single most reliable marker of a tested reading, and it is also the most persuasive move available, because a reader who watches an argument absorb its worst case extends more credit to everything else it says.

It answers one strong objection at length rather than three weak ones briefly. The weak-objection habit is a way of appearing fair while conceding nothing, and experienced readers see through it immediately.

It states its scope. A reading that says what it does not cover is stronger than one that implies total coverage, because the implication is always false and the reader knows it. Naming the limit converts a vulnerability into a demonstration of control.

And it ends on the reading rather than on the theory. The last paragraph of a good theoretical essay says something about Gatsby, not something about the school of criticism that helped the writer see it. The framework was the ladder. The last page should be the roof.

For anyone assembling several frameworks at once, the coherence problems multiply in ways worth understanding separately, and the discipline required is set out in the guidance on [combining critical lenses on the novel](/2028/04/10/combining-lenses-gatsby/). The failure modes described here compound when frameworks are layered, since a decorated reading that is also a combined reading produces two hats and no head.

Readers who want to test any of these claims against the text can work through the annotated novel directly. You can [read and annotate The Great Gatsby free on VaultBook](https://vaultbook.org/tools/great-gatsby-annotated-text.html), where the full text sits alongside close-reading and annotation tools, a searchable quotation bank, and character and theme trackers, so a claim about frequency or structural position can be checked rather than assumed. The library keeps expanding, and for the specific work this article demands, which is testing a reading against evidence you did not choose, having the whole text searchable is the difference between an argument and an impression.

## Closing Verdict

The misuses of theory when reading Gatsby are seven, they are separable, and each has a corrective that costs attention rather than talent. Imposition is answered by falsifiable phrasing and a deliberate search for the passage that would refute you. Jargon is answered by translation into plain English, keeping only the terms that earn their precision. Cherry-picking is answered by building the counter-case before deciding, and by citing the evidence that costs most. Anachronism is answered by separating claims about what the text does from claims about what it meant in 1925. Decoration is answered by letting the framework choose the evidence and govern the paragraph order. Biographical collapse is answered by a rule of direction: the life explains availability, never meaning. Detail inflation is answered by frequency and structural position, with three independent supports before a claim becomes a thesis.

The verdict this article defends is that these failures indict practitioners rather than practice. A framework applied without discipline produces a reading that sounds authoritative and tells you less about the novel than an honest paraphrase would, which is a worse outcome than not using theory at all, and that is why the misuses matter enough to catalogue. But the alternative on offer is not innocence. It is an unexamined framework that cannot be argued with, and the history of this novel's reception is a record of what such frameworks miss.

The reader who wants to write well about Gatsby through any lens needs one habit above all the others: the willingness to look for the passage that would prove them wrong, and to put it in the essay when they find it. Everything else in the misuse ledger follows from that single discipline, and no amount of theoretical sophistication substitutes for it.

It is worth naming what the discipline buys, since the cost is real and the payoff is easy to underestimate. An argument that has absorbed its worst case is not merely more defensible; it is more informative, because the reader learns not only what the writer believes but what the belief survived. That is the difference between a position and a finding. Fitzgerald's novel happens to reward the habit better than most books, because it was built out of judgments that will not hold still: a narrator who claims reserve and delivers a verdict, a protagonist described as representing everything for which the narrator has an unaffected scorn and then declared worth the whole damn bunch put together, an ending that grieves and exalts in the same three sentences. A framework that cannot accommodate those reversals has not failed to be clever. It has failed to be about this book. Choose the lens that makes the reversals legible, argue the reading the evidence rewards, and let the passage that resists you stay in the essay where a reader can see you meet it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What are the common misuses of theory in reading Gatsby?

Seven recur often enough to be named. Imposition fixes the verdict before the evidence arrives. Jargon substitution replaces argument with technical vocabulary that vanishes under translation. Cherry-picking assembles only agreeable quotations. Anachronism treats a modern framework's vocabulary as a historical fact about 1925. Decoration announces a school in the introduction and then writes ordinary close reading. Biographical collapse explains characters by the author's life rather than by the page. Detail inflation rests a whole thesis on one small textual feature. Each has a specific corrective, and a genuinely weak theoretical essay usually commits four of them at once, which is why diagnosing them one at a time makes revision manageable rather than overwhelming.

### Q: How does imposing a framework misuse theory?

Imposition treats the framework as a conclusion instead of a set of prior questions, so that reading becomes a hunt for illustrations rather than an investigation. The symptom is that the verdict could not have come out differently no matter what the novel contained. A reading announcing that Fitzgerald's book indicts capitalism will find supporting passages easily, because they exist, but it becomes imposition the moment it declines to meet the material that resists: the narrator's habit of converting structural facts into moral ones, the lyric energy spent on the beauty of wealth rather than its production, and Gatsby's own criminal fortune. Honest application phrases the claim so a passage could refute it, then goes looking for that passage on purpose.

### Q: How does jargon replace argument?

Technical vocabulary functions as a credential, and a sentence dense with terms of art often looks like expertise while making no claim about the book at all. The diagnostic is translation. Rewrite each theoretical sentence in plain English. If the plain version says the same thing more clearly, the terminology was doing social work rather than analytical work. If the plain version says nothing, the sentence was empty and the repair is a new claim, not a new phrasing. Fitzgerald himself is the argument against the habit: the novel compresses a full account of aesthetic ideology into the phrase about a vast, vulgar, and meretricious beauty, using words any reader can parse.

### Q: How does cherry-picking distort a reading?

Cherry-picking is imposition executed at the level of quotation. The framework may have been chosen honestly, but the evidence was gathered by a search that stopped at the first agreement. Class readings cite the valley of ashes and skip the unexamined Midwestern hardware fortune. Feminist readings cite Daisy's wish for a beautiful little fool and skip Jordan Baker's independence. The statistical tell is uniformity: if twelve quotations all point the same way, the reading was probably never tested, because no novel of this density is that consistent. The corrective is adversarial evidence-gathering, building the strongest case against yourself before deciding, and then including the passage that costs you most.

### Q: Does the misuse of theory make theory useless?

No, for two reasons. First, every misuse listed here has a known and cheap corrective, and a discipline whose failures are all remediable is not a discipline in trouble. Second, the alternative is not neutrality. There is no reading without prior questions, so a reader who refuses frameworks operates with an unexamined one assembled from their education and their moment, which is less accountable rather than more. The reception history proves the point: the long-standard classroom account of the novel as a tragedy of a noble dream corrupted from outside was itself an unexamined framework, and what dislodged it was class-attentive and feminist criticism asking better questions.

### Q: How can each theoretical misuse be corrected?

Each pairs with a specific practice. Imposition is corrected by stating the claim so the text could refute it, then hunting the refuting passage. Jargon is corrected by translating every loaded sentence into plain English and keeping only terms that lose precision when simplified. Cherry-picking is corrected by building the counter-case first. Anachronism is corrected by dating every claim about intention or period reception. Decoration is corrected by letting the framework select the evidence and govern paragraph order. Biographical collapse is corrected by a directional rule: the life explains what materials a writer had, never what a passage means. Detail inflation is corrected by requiring three independent supports across chapters.

### Q: What is anachronism in a theoretical reading of Gatsby?

Anachronism occurs when a modern framework's vocabulary slides from being an instrument of analysis to being a description of what 1925 readers or Fitzgerald thought. The observation may be entirely sound. Nick's narration does withhold at certain moments, in a patterned way that recurs, and that is verifiable by anyone with the book. The anachronism enters when the reading treats the modern category naming that withholding as historical evidence about how the period understood itself. The repair is cheap: separate the claim about what the text does, which needs no dating, from the claim about what it meant then, which requires historical support of a different kind entirely.

### Q: How do you tell a genuine lens reading from a decorated one?

Check whether the framework is making three decisions: which passages the essay quotes, how the paragraphs are ordered, and which objection the essay must answer. A decorated essay names a school in its introduction, returns to it in the conclusion, and fills the middle with analysis that would be identical if the label were swapped or deleted. A genuine application produces an evidence list you would not have assembled otherwise. The practical test is to build that list before writing. If it matches the list you would have made without any framework, the framework has contributed nothing and you should either change it or drop the pretense and write the close reading honestly.

### Q: Why must a theoretical reading confront counter-evidence?

Because a framework that cannot be embarrassed by the text is not analyzing the text. Engaging counter-evidence proves the reading was tested rather than assumed, and it usually improves the claim by forcing precision about scope, chapter, and character instead of comfortable generality. This novel rewards the discipline unusually well because it is built from statements that cut both ways: the narrator's declaration of his own honesty is either a guarantee or a warning, and Gatsby's insistence that the past can be repeated is either delusion or the book's deepest structural insight. A reading that resolves those tensions too quickly has stopped reading. Assessors also reward the move heavily.

### Q: What is the difference between a framework and a formula?

A framework supplies prior questions and lets the text supply answers, so the outcome remains genuinely uncertain until the reading is done. A formula supplies both, so the outcome was fixed before the book opened. The difference shows up in the evidence. Framework-driven reading notices things a plain reading walks past, and some of those things complicate the framework itself, which is how criticism advances. Formula-driven reading produces the same essay about every novel, with the proper nouns swapped. A useful check is whether your reading of this book taught you anything about the framework, rather than only the reverse.

### Q: Can a Marxist reading of Gatsby be misused?

Easily, and it is one of the most frequently misused approaches to this novel because the supporting material is so abundant. The valley of ashes, the distinction between inherited and criminal money, and the closing judgment about people who retreat into their money all sit ready to be quoted. Misuse begins when the reading declines to meet what resists it: the narrator's persistent translation of structural facts into moral ones, the book's lyric investment in the beauty of wealth, and the fact that Gatsby's fortune is itself criminal. The strongest class readings are strong because they explain those resistances rather than suppressing them, and they generally locate the translation in Nick.

### Q: How does over-reading a small detail damage a theoretical argument?

It builds a thesis on a foundation that cannot bear it. The shirts episode has carried more interpretive weight than any comparable three paragraphs in American fiction, and several of the readings resting on it are good, yet an essay whose central claim about nine chapters rests on that scene alone has confused a memorable instance with a demonstrated pattern. Evidence should be weighted two ways. Frequency matters, because recurrence indicates design rather than accident. Structural position matters, because material at a hinge point carries more weight than an aside. Three independent supports across different chapters, including one hinge, is the working threshold before an observation becomes a thesis.

### Q: What warning signs show a lens has been forced onto the text?

Uniform evidence is the first: twelve quotations all pointing the same way in a novel this dense means the reading was never tested. Chapter clustering is the second: evidence drawn only from the harshest material presented as a total survey. Unfalsifiable phrasing is the third, where no possible passage could contradict the thesis. Vanishing arguments are the fourth, where deleting the technical vocabulary leaves nothing behind. A fifth is the absent objection, an essay that never names anything that would count against it. Any one of these is repairable in a paragraph; the combination usually means the argument needs rebuilding from the evidence upward.

### Q: Should a student avoid theory entirely to stay safe?

No, because the safety is illusory. Every reader arrives with prior questions, and refusing an explicit framework means operating with an implicit one drawn from your education and your moment, which is harder to argue with precisely because it is invisible. The long-dominant classroom reading of this novel, in which a noble dream is corrupted by external forces, was exactly such an unexamined framework, and it survived for decades by looking like the book itself. Theory is a power tool. It removes material faster than hand work and damages the workpiece faster too, and the correct response is training rather than abstinence.

### Q: How much jargon belongs in a student essay about Gatsby?

Enough to be precise and no more. Technical terms compress real distinctions, and a writer who refuses them will keep reinventing worse versions of ideas the tradition already holds. The working rule is that a term earns its place when the plain paraphrase would be longer or less accurate. Define it once on first appearance in a single clause, then use it. If the plain version is shorter and identical in content, use the plain version, because the term was functioning as a credential. If the plain version says nothing at all, the sentence was empty and needs a claim about the book rather than a better phrasing.

### Q: Do graders penalize a badly handled theoretical reading?

Consistently, and usually more heavily than they penalize a competent essay with no framework at all, because a misapplied lens signals that the writer has learned vocabulary rather than method. Rubrics reward the specific habits this article recommends: engaging a strong objection, quoting across the text rather than from one cluster, and making an argument the evidence could have defeated. The convenient alignment is that the practices making criticism honest are the same practices that raise marks. An essay that raises and defeats its own best objection is scored in a different category from one that never meets resistance, and that single move is usually the cheapest available improvement.

### Q: How do you repair a theoretical reading that has gone wrong?

Diagnose before rewriting, using six mechanical passes that work even when your judgment is already compromised. Delete every sentence containing a technical term and see whether an argument remains. Write the denial of your thesis and check whether it is arguable rather than absurd. Tally your quotations by chapter and look for clustering. Translate loaded sentences into plain English. Underline every claim about intention or period meaning and check it has historical support. List the supports for the central claim and confirm there are at least three across different chapters. Each failed pass points to a specific repair, usually a paragraph rather than a rebuild.

### Q: Is it a misuse to read Gatsby through a lens Fitzgerald never knew?

No. Frameworks developed after a book routinely reveal structures its author built without naming them, and a novel is not confined to what its writer could articulate. The economics of this book work as they work whether or not Fitzgerald had read any political economy. The misuse is narrower and easily avoided: presenting the framework's modern vocabulary as evidence about what the novel meant in 1925 or what its author intended. Keep the two claims separate and both remain available. The opposite position, refusing any framework the author could not have known, would forbid nearly all interpretation and misunderstands what reading is for.

### Q: How does psychologizing the author distort a theoretical reading?

It converts a correlation into an explanation. Fitzgerald courted a wealthy woman who nearly refused him; his protagonist pursues a wealthy woman and is destroyed; the resemblance feels like an answer and is not one. The differences are the design: Fitzgerald's courtship succeeded, his narrator is not himself, and the novel's opening page constructs a speaker with a specific moral vanity the book then tests. A subtler version attributes formal choices to authorial psychology, explaining the retrospective frame by nostalgia. The frame does describable work on the page, placing every event inside a judgment already formed, and that description needs no reference to the author at all.
