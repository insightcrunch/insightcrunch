Claims that mechanised production will abolish work altogether rest on an assumption so familiar that it seldom gets examined: the assumption that equipment creates wealth in the same sense and by the same process through which wealth gets measured under capitalism. Marxist analysis denies precisely that assumption, and the denial is neither a quirk nor a defensive manoeuvre bolted onto an older doctrine. It sits at the centre of the account of value developed across the mature writings, and everything distinctive in the Marxist treatment of automation follows from it. Machines, on this account, create no value at all. A machine contributes its own previously embodied worth to the goods it helps produce, surrendering that worth gradually as friction, heat, and time consume its working life, yet the stream of finished articles leaving a highly mechanised workplace carries no increment of value attributable to the equipment itself. New value enters production through one door only, the expenditure of living human effort during the working day, and the quantity of new value corresponds to the duration and intensity of that effort rather than to the sophistication of the apparatus surrounding it.

![A workshop table with the value question table, machinery sketches and output ledgers, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-82.webp)

Hold that claim steady for a moment and several consequences arrange themselves in sequence. If equipment creates nothing, then installing more of it cannot by itself enlarge the mass of value a society produces, however dramatically it enlarges the mass of useful things. Output in physical terms and output in value terms part company, and the parting explains a family of puzzles that otherwise look like paradoxes. A workplace can double the quantity of goods flowing from its doors while the value embodied in each unit falls, because each unit now absorbs a smaller share of the human effort expended across the whole line. Prices can drift downward for decades in branch after branch of manufacture while profits remain stubbornly dependent on how much living effort an owner manages to set in motion. Displacement of workers follows as a structural tendency rather than a malfunction, since every owner gains a temporary edge by producing with less effort per unit than rivals, and the aggregate of those private edges is a shrinking demand for hands relative to the scale of production. None of this requires hostility toward machinery. The theory treats equipment as a prodigious amplifier of human powers, a multiplier of what an hour of effort can accomplish in material terms, while insisting that amplification and creation are different operations and that confusing them dissolves the very problem political economy exists to solve.

A reader meeting the claim for the first time often hears it as wordplay, a definition arranged so that machines lose by stipulation. That reaction deserves a serious answer, and the tradition supplies one grounded in observable behaviour rather than in terminology. Suppose, for the sake of argument, that equipment did originate value in proportion to its cost or its output. An owner who replaced workers with apparatus would then possess a durable advantage, a private spring of value that rivals could not touch without making the identical replacement. Rivals, being free to purchase the same apparatus from the same suppliers at comparable prices, would make that replacement, and whatever premium the first adopter enjoyed would shrink as the method spread. Experience conforms to the second pattern and not the first. Advantages drawn from superior equipment behave like melting ice: real, sometimes large, and reliably temporary. The adopter pockets an extra profit while producing more cheaply than the social average, and competition then drags the average down toward the new method, extinguishing the premium and leaving behind only a lower value per unit across the branch. That observed pattern, the rise and erosion of adopter advantage, is what the strict position predicts. A doctrine in which machines originated value would predict the opposite persistence, and the mismatch between that prediction and commercial experience is the empirical spine of the argument developed throughout this article.

Two further disciplines govern everything that follows, and both run against the grain of popular discussion. The first concerns evidence at the aggregate level. A transformation of production as sweeping as the one frequently announced ought to register in the broadest measures of economic performance, above all in the growth of output per hour of effort across the advanced economies, since raising that ratio is the entire material point of mechanisation. Measured growth of that kind has been slow over the decades in which the transformation is said to have unfolded, a finding documented within mainstream growth economics by writers with no stake in defending Marx. Any narrative of revolutionary change must therefore explain an absence before it may assert a presence, and most narratives, celebratory and apocalyptic alike, skip the explanation. The second discipline concerns visibility. Enquiries into the operation of systems presented as self-acting repeatedly uncover substantial quantities of poorly paid, geographically scattered, and deliberately inconspicuous human effort performing the training, correction, moderation, and exception handling on which the appearance of autonomy depends. Where the boundary between mechanical and human contribution falls turns out, in case after documented case, to be a question of where effort has been placed out of sight rather than whether effort continues to be expended.

This article proceeds by holding the theory and the evidence in a single frame. It states the strict position on machinery and value with its reasoning exposed, follows the contradiction Marx himself formulated in his notebooks of the late eighteen fifties, submits the transformation narrative to the productivity check, examines the concealed effort behind apparently autonomous operation, presents the strongest rival interpretations at full strength with their authors named, confronts the most damaging objection without softening it, and closes by marking honestly what remains unknown. No particular apparatus, product, firm, or named technique will appear anywhere in the discussion, and no capability of any particular device will be described. That restraint is deliberate. The questions at stake are structural: they concern what value is, where surplus originates, how competition disciplines adopters, and what an economy organised around the measurement of wealth by labour time does to itself as it economises on labour time. Structural questions outlast every particular device, and an answer framed at their level remains usable long after the devices that prompted the question have been forgotten.

## Automation and the strict claim that machines create no value

Begin with the distinction the whole analysis presupposes, between wealth and value. Wealth, in the vocabulary of the critique of political economy, means the stock of useful things a society commands: food, shelter, clothing, transport, instruments, amusements, means of further production. Value names something narrower and stranger, a social relation expressed in exchange, the form wealth assumes when products are made for sale by independent producers whose efforts must be rendered commensurable. A coat and a quantity of linen can be equated in exchange not because they share any physical property but because each embodies a portion of the total effort a society expends, measured in time and averaged across producers of ordinary skill working with ordinary equipment. That averaging is the social mechanism the theory calls socially necessary labour time, treated in full in the account of the [labour theory of value explained](/2010/07/01/labour-theory-of-value-explained/), and it supplies the yardstick against which every claim about machinery must be tested. A machine plainly increases wealth. Whether it increases value is a separate question, and the theory answers it in the negative.

### Do machines produce value in Marxist theory?

no. Machinery raises the productivity of labour and transfers its own value to the product as it wears out, but creates no new value. If machines created value, competition would erode the advantage of adopting them, whereas adopters obtain only a temporary surplus profit that competition removes.

Unpack the negative answer slowly, because its components are frequently collapsed into a slogan. A machine is itself a product of past effort: ore extracted, metal smelted, components shaped, assemblies fitted, transport arranged, installation performed. Its price, under the simplifying assumptions of the theory, corresponds to the effort socially necessary for its production. When the machine enters a workplace it does not cease to embody that effort; it carries the embodied effort into a new setting and releases it piecemeal. A device built to serve through the making of a million units surrenders roughly a millionth of its embodied worth to each unit, alongside the raw materials consumed and the fresh effort applied by the workers attending it. Accountants recognise the same process under the heading of depreciation, and the resemblance is not accidental: both frameworks record a capital good giving up its worth gradually rather than vanishing at first use or persisting undiminished. The Marxist claim adds one further step. The machine gives up only what it contains. It cannot surrender more worth than was embodied in it, any more than a reservoir can release more water than was gathered behind its dam. Whatever the finished product is worth, the machine's contribution to that worth is a transfer, a relocation of value created earlier and elsewhere, performed by workers who mined, smelted, machined, and assembled long before the final product took shape.

Contrast the behaviour of living effort within the same workplace. A worker hired for a day sells the capacity to work for a wage corresponding to the value of the goods required to sustain and reproduce that capacity: food, housing, clothing, training, the support of dependants. Suppose those necessities embody four hours of social effort. The wage, in value terms, equals four hours. Yet the working day lasts considerably longer than four hours, and during every hour of it the worker adds fresh value to the product in proportion to time expended and intensity sustained. The difference between the value the worker adds across the full day and the value returned in the wage is surplus value, the source of profit once the transfers from materials and equipment are stripped away. Living effort thus possesses the peculiar property that its consumption creates more value than its own reproduction costs. Equipment possesses no such property. Its consumption creates nothing beyond the relocation of its own embodied worth, and this asymmetry, between an input whose use adds value and inputs whose use merely conveys value, is the analytical hinge on which the entire Marxist treatment of mechanisation turns.

Productivity enters the argument at exactly this point, and its role is widely misunderstood. A superior machine permits the same crew to finish more units within the day, or a smaller crew to finish the same units. The fresh value added during the day does not rise in step with the physical output; it remains governed by the number of workers, the length of their day, and the intensity of their effort. What changes is the distribution of that value across units. Each unit now carries a thinner slice of new value, together with its slice of transferred worth from materials and equipment. Units multiply while unit values fall, which is why mechanisation cheapens commodities as a general law of the system rather than as an occasional curiosity. Cheapening, in turn, reaches back into the wage relation itself, because the goods workers consume grow cheaper as the methods producing them improve, lowering the value of the capacity to work and enlarging the surplus portion of the day even when hours and money wages stand still. Marx called that pathway relative surplus value, and he regarded machinery as its principal engine: the route by which capital raises the rate of exploitation not by lengthening the day, which meets physical and political limits, but by shrinking the portion of the day in which the worker merely replaces the wage.

Notice how little of this machinery of concepts depends on any particular historical apparatus. The argument ran first on spinning frames and power looms, because those were the devices transforming production while Marx wrote, yet its terms are deliberately general: any produced means of production, any device interposed between effort and its object, behaves in the analysis as accumulated past effort releasing its contents gradually. The generality is a strength when the aim is structural understanding, since it prevents the analysis from expiring with each generation of equipment. A steam engine, a transfer line, a warehouse conveyor, and a device whose workings its own operators cannot inspect all stand in the same relation to value within the theory: each transfers, none creates. Differences among them matter enormously for engineering, for the organisation of work, for the skills demanded and discarded, and for the lived experience of the people tending them. For the value question, those differences are secondary, and insisting on that ordering, value first and engineering second, is what separates the Marxist treatment from the technological enthusiasm and technological dread that dominate popular discussion, both of which begin from the device and its powers rather than from the social form into which the device has been inserted.

## Transfer, depreciation and the anatomy of a working machine

Follow a single machine through its service life and the transfer doctrine stops looking abstract. At installation the device embodies a definite quantity of social effort, fixed by the conditions under which it was produced. Every subsequent day of operation consumes a fraction of its useful existence: bearings wear, surfaces abrade, tolerances drift, components fatigue, and eventually the whole assembly must be scrapped, rebuilt, or retired to lighter duties. The value account mirrors the physical account. Across its lifetime the machine can contribute to products no more value than it brought through the factory door, and it contributes that value in rough proportion to its consumption, a steady leakage of embodied effort into the stream of output. Maintenance complicates the bookkeeping without altering the principle, since the effort of fitters, cleaners, and repair crews is fresh living effort added during production, counted alongside the effort of the operators rather than credited to the device they service. Spare parts likewise arrive embodying their own production effort and transfer that worth in their turn. Peel the process apart as finely as accounting permits and the same two categories recur: value arriving from the past, embodied in materials and apparatus, and value originating in the present, embodied through effort expended now.

### Why does a machine give up its worth gradually rather than at once?

because consumption in production is itself gradual, spread across the whole span of service the device was built to deliver. Each unit of output absorbs a small share of the embodied effort the machine carries, matching the small share of its working life that unit consumed, until the accumulated shares exhaust the total and the device stands worn out.

The gradual character of transfer carries a consequence that adopters feel as pressure. Money laid out on equipment returns slowly, recovered unit by unit across years of output, and during those years the design frontier keeps moving. A rival who installs a superior device later may produce so much more cheaply that the market value of output falls below the level at which the earlier adopter can recover the unamortised remainder of the original investment. Marx described equipment suffering a kind of moral depreciation, a loss of value arising not from physical wear but from the arrival of better or cheaper successors, and the phenomenon explains a rhythm familiar in every mechanised branch: owners sweating ageing plant beyond its efficient life because its book value remains unrecovered, owners scrapping serviceable equipment because clinging to it costs more than abandonment, and a permanent undertow of anxiety attaching to any large commitment of funds to apparatus. The undertow matters for the larger argument because it shows capital experiencing its own creations as threats. The device that cheapens production for its owner simultaneously cheapens the conditions under which every other owner's sunk investment must justify itself, so mechanisation advances through a sequence of private rescues and private ruins even when its social effect, an abundance of cheaper goods, appears benign in aggregate statistics.

Consider next the raw materials and energy passing through the same process, since their treatment clarifies the boundary of the transfer category. Cotton entering a mill, ore entering a furnace, fuel entering an engine: each arrives embodying effort expended in its extraction and preparation, and each surrenders that embodied worth to the product wholly or in proportion to consumption. Nobody supposes the cotton creates the cloth's value beyond what the cotton itself was worth, and the theory extends the same treatment to the loom without remainder. The loom differs from the cotton in duration, not in kind: one is consumed across a million units, the other within a single unit, yet both figure in the final account as past effort changing its location and form. What neither can do is multiply. Multiplication belongs exclusively to the living activity set in motion during the working day, and it occurs there for a specific institutional reason rather than a metaphysical one: the worker is paid for the capacity to work, priced by the cost of sustaining that capacity, while the exercise of the capacity lasts longer than the time its price represents. Change the institution, abolish the wage relation, and the category of surplus value disappears with it, whatever machines remain in place. Keep the institution and remove every machine, and surplus value persists in handicraft production, as it did for centuries before the factory. That thought experiment, simple as it sounds, isolates the variable the theory regards as causal.

### Where does the line fall between transferring value and creating it?

the terms on which each enters production. Equipment is purchased at its value and consumed as a store of past effort, returning no more than it contains. The capacity to work is purchased at the value of its own reproduction, while its exercise extends beyond the hours that value represents, and the excess is newly created value appearing in the product.

A further refinement guards against a common misreading. Saying that equipment creates no value does not mean equipment contributes nothing to production, nor that its presence leaves output unchanged, nor that a society could dispense with it and prosper equally. Usefulness and value creation are different predicates, and the theory assigns machinery the highest possible standing under the first while denying it the second. A fertile field creates no value in the theory's sense either, yet harvests depend on it utterly; a navigable river charges no toll in value terms for the transport it makes possible, yet commerce organises itself around its banks. Nature and apparatus alike furnish conditions under which effort becomes fruitful, multiplying the useful yield of each hour without altering the measure by which value is reckoned. Critics who triumphantly observe that a mechanised line outproduces a hundred hand workers have established a proposition about wealth that Marx states more emphatically than they do. The disputed proposition concerns the social accounting of a system that prices effort by the hour and distributes its product through exchange, and on that terrain the observation about physical yield simply does not engage the claim.

There is also a historical observation embedded in the doctrine, worth surfacing because it disciplines speculation about the future. Marx noted that machinery in capitalist hands serves purposes beyond cheapening: it serves as a weapon in disputes over the length and intensity of the working day, a means of breaking the bargaining power attached to scarce skills, and a method of rendering the workforce more readily replaceable by drawing previously excluded groups into production under new divisions of tasks. An owner confronting a strike among skilled men could reorganise production around equipment operable by less practised hands, and the historical record of mechanised branches contains episode after episode of investment decisions whose timing makes sense only when that disciplinary motive is admitted alongside the motive of cheapness. The point generalises. Equipment choices inside a wage system are never purely technical choices among neutral methods; they are simultaneously choices about dependence, replaceability, and control within the workplace, and an analysis that treats adoption as the automatic triumph of efficiency misses the social calculation running through the engineering. Later sections return to that calculation when examining who performs the effort that apparently self-acting arrangements conceal, since concealment itself turns out to be one of the social uses to which a technical arrangement can be put.

## Competition, surplus profit and why the adopter advantage melts

The strongest evidence for the strict position comes not from definitions but from watching profits behave under competition, and the reasoning deserves to be laid out in full because it converts a doctrinal claim into a testable one. Imagine a branch of production in which every firm works with comparable equipment and comparable methods. Output sells at prices governed by the average conditions of the branch, and each firm earns roughly the ordinary return on the funds it has committed. Into this settled landscape steps an innovator with a superior device, perhaps acquired at considerable expense, perhaps developed behind a wall of secrecy. The innovator's costs per unit fall below the branch average while the selling price, still set by the older average conditions, remains where it stood. The gap between the innovator's individual cost and the prevailing social price becomes an extra profit, a surplus profit over and above the ordinary return, and for a season the innovator prospers conspicuously. Rivals observe the prosperity, investigate its source, and acquire equivalent equipment as soon as suppliers, credit, and installation schedules permit. As the superior method spreads, the social average itself migrates downward. Prices follow the average. The innovator's gap narrows, then closes, and the branch settles at a new equilibrium in which goods are cheaper, unit values are lower, and nobody retains the extraordinary return that briefly rewarded the first move.

Every element of that sequence matters. The extra profit was real, and the theory does not explain it away; it explains it, as a redistribution toward the innovator of value created across the economy, captured because the innovator's individual production conditions temporarily beat the social standard by which prices are set. The erosion was equally real, and equally necessary within the theory, because the advantage never resided in the device as a self-expanding spring of value. It resided in a temporary discrepancy between one firm's conditions and the social average, and discrepancies of that kind are exactly what competitive investment exists to eliminate. Secrecy, patents, and scarce installation capacity can delay the equalisation, sometimes for years, and during the delay the surplus profit can be large enough to build fortunes and fund further innovation. Delay, however, is not exemption. The direction of the process never reverses: methods diffuse, averages migrate, premiums decay. An economics in which equipment originated value would face no reason for the decay to occur, since each adopter would add a new spring rather than merely sharing an existing stream more cleverly. The observed universality of decay, across branches and centuries, is the sort of plain, unglamorous regularity on which durable theories are built.

### How does rivalry among producers dissolve a pioneer's premium?

because the advantage consists of a gap between one producer's costs and the social average that governs price, and every rival can narrow that gap by adopting the same method. As adoption spreads the average itself falls toward the improved cost level, prices adjust downward with it, and the temporary premium is competed away completely.

The same mechanism, viewed from the side of the laggard, explains the coercive character of mechanisation. A firm that declines to adopt does not preserve an older, cosier equilibrium; it watches the social average migrate away from its own cost structure until its output, priced by conditions it no longer meets, fails to return even the ordinary profit. Bankruptcy, takeover, or forced modernisation follows, and the choice owners describe as voluntary investment reveals itself as compulsion administered by the market. Marx expressed the point by saying that competition makes the immanent laws of the system felt by each individual capitalist as external coercive laws, and the formulation captures something anyone who has watched a branch mechanise will recognise: the adopters are not visionaries freely choosing progress, nor are the resisters simply stubborn. Both move inside a field of forces that punishes hesitation and rewards speed only briefly, a field whose pressures intensify precisely because each participant responds rationally to the pressures already bearing down. The aggregate result, a relentless cheapening of goods and a relentless displacement of effort per unit of output, emerges from private decisions none of which aimed at that aggregate result.

Surplus profit has a second life in the theory that sharpens the argument further, in the analysis of rent. Where an advantage rests on a condition that cannot be reproduced by investment alone, a fertile plot that cannot be manufactured, a waterfall that cannot be duplicated, the premium does not decay through diffusion, because diffusion is impossible. It persists, and it crystallises as rent paid to whoever owns the unreproducible condition. The contrast is instructive. Durable premiums in economic life attach to monopolised scarcities, to things rivals cannot simply buy or build, whereas premiums attached to purchasable equipment decay on schedule. That contrast is difficult to reconcile with the notion that equipment creates value, since a genuine source of value, once purchased, should yield its stream to every purchaser indefinitely. It fits the transfer doctrine exactly: equipment yields only the temporary fruits of beating an average, and the moment the average catches up, the fruit is gone, leaving the owner with a depreciated asset and the society with cheaper goods.

### What would count as evidence that equipment originates value?

a producer retaining an above-average return indefinitely under open competitive conditions, with rivals free to acquire equivalent apparatus at comparable prices and no monopoly, secrecy, or scarce natural condition protecting the position. Persistence of that kind, rather than a long delay in diffusion, would contradict the transfer doctrine at its foundation and force its abandonment.

State the test in that form and the verdict defeater for the whole article comes into view, since the strict position stands or falls with it. The position would be defeated by a demonstration that a producer using machinery retains an above-average profit rate indefinitely under competitive conditions, which is the observable implication of machines creating value and which the theory predicts will not occur. Note what the defeater excludes. A long-lived premium protected by law, by secrecy, by control of an input nobody else can obtain, or by the sheer slowness of an industry to reorganise, does not defeat the theory, because the theory predicts persistence exactly where diffusion is blocked. Nor does a spectacular fortune built during a diffusion window defeat it, since the window is the theory's own prediction. The defeating observation must show persistence after diffusion has had its chance, persistence with the exits open and the rivals equipped. Framing the defeater this precisely is not pedantry. A theory that cannot say what would refute it is a mood rather than an analysis, and the Marxist account of machinery, whatever its other difficulties, has always been willing to stake itself on the behaviour of profits under competition. Readers who doubt the doctrine should therefore watch the right variable: not the size of fortunes made during transitions, but their durability after transitions complete.

One more consequence of the melting advantage deserves attention before the argument moves on, because it connects the value doctrine to the lived instability of mechanised economies. Since the premium decays, owners must run to stand still, committing fresh funds to fresh equipment in an endless sequence whose intervals shorten as diffusion accelerates. Investment becomes compulsory and continuous, and the funds for it must be extracted from the surplus the workforce produces, which ties the pace of mechanisation to the rate of exploitation in a direct accounting sense. Branches differ in how brutally this treadmill operates, depending on how quickly methods diffuse and how much sunk cost each generation of equipment imposes, yet the treadmill itself is general. It also explains a feature of mechanised economies that purely technical accounts find puzzling: the coexistence of dazzling productive capacity with chronic insecurity among the firms wielding it. Capacity dazzles in aggregate and terrifies in particular, because every particular stock of equipment is a wasting asset whose wasting is hastened by the ingenuity of competitors. The theory does not merely tolerate that paradox; it predicts it, from premises about transfer and competition that were fixed before any of the apparatus now debated existed.

## Rising machinery ratios, displaced workers and the reserve of labour

From the transfer doctrine and the melting advantage together there follows the tendency that dominates the social experience of mechanisation: a rising proportion of past, embodied effort relative to living effort within production, which the tradition calls a rising organic composition of capital and treats in its consequences for profitability in the discussion of the [falling rate of profit debate](/2013/11/15/falling-rate-of-profit-debate/). The logic is arithmetical once the premises are granted. Each round of competitive adoption replaces some living effort with embodied effort, because replacing effort is how the adopter beats the average. Equipment stocks grow relative to payrolls. The value advanced for materials and apparatus swells compared with the value advanced as wages, while surplus continues to originate solely in the living portion. Owners respond by squeezing the living portion harder, lengthening or intensifying the day where possible, cheapening the wage goods that fix what working capacity is worth, and hunting for fresh reserves of labour whose price is lower. The composition tendency is therefore not a statistic to be admired from outside; it is the accounting shadow cast by the competitive process itself, lengthening as long as that process runs.

Displacement belongs inside the same frame. When a superior method lets a smaller crew produce what a larger crew produced before, the workers released do not vanish from the economy; they become available, and their availability itself performs economic work. A pool of unemployed or intermittently employed people, the reserve army of labour analysed in the article on the [reserve army of labour explained](/2010/12/15/reserve-army-of-labour-explained/), disciplines the wages and conditions of those still employed, since every employed worker knows that refusal has a price measured in replaceability. The reserve also supplies the hands for sudden expansions, for new branches, for seasonal surges, letting capital grow in bursts without bidding wages up to levels that would strangle accumulation. Marx treated this reserve not as an accident of imperfect adjustment but as a product of the accumulation process and a condition of its continuation, a population rendered surplus relative to the needs of capital at the going composition while remaining absolutely necessary to capital as a reservoir and a threat. Mechanisation feeds the reserve continuously, even in prosperous times, because the composition rises through prosperity as reliably as through crisis; indeed prosperity, with its abundant credit and confident investment, often mechanises fastest.

### Does displacement mean the displaced never work again?

no, and the theory has never claimed anything so simple. Released workers circulate through new branches, expanded branches, casual employment, and periods without work, while their availability presses on the terms obtained by everyone still inside employment. The harm the theory identifies lies in insecurity and weakened bargaining power, not in a prediction of permanent universal idleness.

Historical experience, read at the level of mechanism rather than episode, fits that account better than either of the popular alternatives. The apocalyptic alternative expects mechanisation to produce ever-growing masses of permanently excluded people, a prediction embarrassed by the repeated absorption of displaced workers into employments their grandparents could not have imagined. The reassuring alternative expects frictionless reallocation, displaced workers gliding into equivalent positions after brief retraining, a prediction embarrassed by the wage losses, regional devastation, and prolonged detachment from employment that follow large displacements in the documented record of deindustrialised districts. The Marxist account sits between the two and explains the embarrassments of each. Absorption happens, because accumulation creates new branches and expands old ones, and the theory's category of the reserve describes a circulating pool rather than a stagnant swamp. Absorption hurts, because the pool's existence is functional for the system precisely through the pressure it exerts, so the costs of transition are not unfortunate frictions on the way to harmony but part of how the mechanism works. Wages in the receiving employments, the security attached to them, and the bargaining power of the people entering them all bear the mark of the reserve through which those people passed.

The composition tendency also reframes the question of skill. Mechanisation characteristically decomposes crafts that once united conception and execution in a single practised person, distributing the fragments among specialised attendants whose individual tasks demand less training and command lower wages, while concentrating the integrative knowledge in a smaller stratum of designers, engineers, and managers. That decomposition, analysed by Harry Braverman in Labour and Monopoly Capital in 1974, explains why mechanised production so often pairs sophisticated apparatus with impoverished jobs: the sophistication migrated from the worker into the equipment and the managerial plan, leaving the operative role narrowed to feeding, watching, correcting, and clearing. Braverman's account has been criticised for understating worker resistance and for treating managerial intention as more unified than it is, yet its central mechanism, the separation of conception from execution as a strategy of cheapening and control, remains the standard starting point for analysing how job content changes as composition rises. The point matters for later sections, because the effort concealed behind apparently autonomous arrangements follows the same pattern: conception concentrated and visible, execution dispersed and rendered inconspicuous, with the boundary between the two drawn by organisational choice rather than by technical necessity.

### Why does a rising composition squeeze profitability?

because surplus originates only in the living portion of the capital advanced, while competition compels every owner to enlarge the embodied portion relative to the living one. The mass of surplus must therefore be drawn from a relatively shrinking base of value-creating effort, even as the total funds committed grow, which presses on the rate of return across the system.

Resist one temptation at this point, the temptation to convert the tendency into a timetable. The tradition's serious writers treat the rising composition as a pressure operating through countertendencies: cheapening of the elements of constant capital as the same productivity growth lowers equipment prices, expansion of the workforce through population growth and the drawing in of new groups, foreign trade opening cheaper inputs and wider markets, and the depression of wages below the worth of labour power during crises and in weakly organised sectors. Whether the pressure or the countertendencies dominate in a given period is an empirical question about magnitudes the theory deliberately leaves open, and the article on profitability linked above exists precisely because generations of able interpreters have answered that empirical question differently. What the value doctrine contributes is not a date for a crisis but an anatomy of the forces in play, an insistence that the same process which multiplies wealth also multiplies the ratio between dead and living effort in production, and that any account of mechanised capitalism which ignores that ratio has skipped the variable the system's own accounting treats as decisive.

A second temptation deserves equal resistance: reading displacement as a purely technical event befalling a passive population. Workers respond to mechanisation collectively and individually, contesting the pace of introduction, the staffing of new equipment, the distribution of its gains in hours and wages, and the right to share in productivity growth through shorter working time. The historical shortening of the working day across the industrial economies was not a gift of the machine; it was extracted through organisation and legislation against owners who preferred to bank the whole productivity dividend as surplus. That history matters for the political conclusions drawn later, because it establishes that the social results of mechanisation are mediated by power and organisation rather than dictated by the apparatus. The same device, inserted into different settlements between labour and capital, yields different distributions of leisure, security, and income. A theory that began by denying that machines create value ends up denying that machines dictate outcomes, and the two denials are one: outcomes flow from the social relations within which equipment operates, relations that human beings made and can remake, however stubbornly those relations present themselves as natural facts.

## The moving contradiction in the notebooks of the eighteen fifties

Every debate about machinery and value eventually returns to a passage Marx wrote in the notebooks he filled during the winter of 1857 and 1858, notebooks that remained unpublished for generations and that appeared in English as the Grundrisse only long after the arguments they contain had been reconstructed, imperfectly, from the published volumes of Capital. In a stretch of those notebooks devoted to machinery, sometimes called the fragment on machines and treated in its own right in the article on the [general intellect explained](/2021/01/15/general-intellect-explained/), Marx pushed the tendencies traced in the preceding sections toward their limit and stated the result as a contradiction lodged inside capital itself. Capital, he wrote, is itself the moving contradiction, because it presses to reduce labour time to a minimum while simultaneously positing labour time as the sole measure and source of wealth. The sentence deserves its fame. It compresses the entire problem of automation under capitalism into a single grammatical arc, and no later formulation, sympathetic or hostile, has stated the problem more sharply.

Unfold the compression slowly. The first clause describes the drive every preceding section has examined: competition compels each owner to economise on living effort, to produce with fewer hours per unit, to substitute embodied effort for living effort wherever substitution lowers cost. Pursued without limit, that drive aims at a production process from which direct human effort has been almost entirely expelled, a process in which people supervise, maintain, and design while apparatus performs. The second clause describes the measure that the same system applies to its own success: wealth counts as value, and value is reckoned in labour time, so the social standing of every product, every firm, and ultimately every national economy is assessed by a yardstick made of the very quantity the first drive is busy minimising. A system that measures wealth by labour time while striving to abolish labour time is sawing through the branch on which its own accounting sits. Marx's word for that condition was contradiction, used in his strict sense: not a logical slip by a careless thinker, nor a temporary imbalance awaiting correction, but a real opposition between two tendencies that a single process generates and requires, each feeding on the other, each undermining the other, neither able to win without destroying the form that contains them both.

### How did Marx phrase the contradiction lodged inside capital itself?

the opposition Marx located inside capital itself, between its drive to shrink the labour time embodied in production toward a minimum and its simultaneous reliance on labour time as the only measure and source of value. Each tendency follows from the competitive mechanism, and each, pursued to its limit, undermines the condition the other requires.

The notebooks push further than the published work in one respect that commentators have argued about ever since. As direct effort shrinks toward a supervisory residue, Marx suggests, the knowledge embodied in the production process, the accumulated science and cooperative know-how of the society, becomes the decisive productive force, dwarfing the contribution of the individual hours still expended at the point of production. Production then rests on what he called the general intellect, the social knowledge objectified in the system of machinery and in the educated capacities of the population. At that point the theft of alien labour time, his blunt description of surplus extraction, ceases to be an adequate foundation for measuring wealth, because the ratio between the tiny direct effort expended and the enormous output achieved makes the labour-time yardstick look absurd even from inside the process. The passage foresees the collapse of production based on exchange value if the tendency runs to completion, while stopping short of describing any successor arrangement in detail. Readers have mined those pages for prophecy ever since, some finding a blueprint for a society of abundance administered by knowledge, others finding a rhetorical excursion that the mature Marx quietly abandoned when he came to write Capital for publication. The textual facts support a middle reading: the contradiction passage states a tendency and its internal limit, in language more speculative than the published volumes allow themselves, while the published volumes retain the same tendencies in soberer dress, the rising composition, the displacement of workers, the pressure on profitability, without dramatising the endpoint.

What matters for the automation debate is that the passage refuses both of the easy positions that dominate popular discussion. Against technological dread, it insists that expelling effort from production is capital's own drive, not an external accident, and that the misery attached to displacement arises from the social form, the dependence of livelihood on selling the capacity to work, rather than from the apparatus. Against technological utopianism, it insists that the same drive corrodes the value form itself, so a society cannot simply ride mechanisation to abundance while leaving its accounting, its property relations, and its distribution of income untouched; the yardstick breaks in the hand that wields it. The fully automated economy, so often presented in popular argument as a refutation of Marx, appears inside his own notebooks as the limiting case his theory itself generates and names. That is why the tradition, when confronted with the limiting case, characteristically responds not by retreating but by pointing to the passage: the problem was stated there first, and stated as a contradiction to be lived through historically rather than as a puzzle to be dissolved by definition.

### Why do the notebooks matter more than later summaries of them?

because they contain the contradiction in its author's own compression, with the speculative reach and the hesitations intact, whereas summaries tend to harden one side of the opposition into a prediction. Reading the passage whole shows a thinker tracing a tendency toward its limit while declining to describe the society beyond that limit in any detail.

There is a discipline of reading to observe here, and it protects against a misuse common on all sides. A tendency statement is not a forecast with a date attached, and treating the notebooks as prophecy invites the cheap triumph of noting that the prophesied endpoint has not arrived. Marx's tendencies operate like the tendencies of a physical system with counteracting influences: real, directional, and perpetually entangled with opposing forces that the same analysis names. The rising composition coexists with the cheapening of equipment; displacement coexists with the opening of new branches; the corrosion of the value measure coexists with the system's frantic efforts to reimpose that measure through new forms of waged work, new enclosures of previously free goods, and new methods of surveillance over the effort that remains. Whether the contradiction sharpens or is displaced into fresh forms across a given period is a historical question, answerable only by examining the period. The notebooks supply the questions, and they are unusually good questions: Where is direct effort shrinking, and where is it being reconstituted out of sight? Which activities does the value measure capture, and which socially necessary activities escape it entirely? Who appropriates the productivity dividend, and through what institutional channels? An automation debate conducted with those questions in hand looks very different from one conducted with slogans about jobs ending or abundance arriving, and the difference is the notebooks' continuing gift to anyone willing to read them slowly.

One further feature of the passage repays attention, its treatment of knowledge. By locating the decisive productive force in social knowledge objectified, the fragment anticipates a world in which the wealth-producing power of a society resides less in the muscles at the point of production than in accumulated, shared, cumulative understanding: designs, methods, standards, trained capacities, organised cooperation. That anticipation is the portion of the fragment most often quoted by writers eager to recruit Marx for a theory of knowledge-driven production, and the linked treatment of that theme examines the recruitment critically. For the value argument, the essential point is narrower. Knowledge, however decisive for wealth, does not rescue the value measure; it strains it further, because knowledge is notoriously resistant to measurement in hours, reproducing at negligible cost once produced and yielding its benefits across unlimited applications. A society whose productive powers rest on such a foundation, yet which continues to distribute income according to hours of effort sold, has built its distribution mechanism on a quantity that its own production process has marginalised. The strain shows up wherever access to knowledge goods is artificially restricted so that a price can be charged, wherever scarcity must be manufactured to keep a market functioning. Those artificial scarcities, examined in their own right in the article on intellectual property within this series, are the value form defending itself against the implications of its own productive achievements, and the notebooks saw the defence coming.

## The fully automated limit and what the theory actually predicts there

Take the tendency to its endpoint in imagination, as hostile and sympathetic readers alike enjoy doing, and ask what the theory says about an economy from which direct human effort has wholly vanished. The answer is more interesting than either camp usually reports. Such an economy would produce wealth in unprecedented abundance, assuming the apparatus maintained and improved itself, yet it would produce no value at all in the theory's sense, because value is congealed labour time and no labour time would be expended. Prices, profits, and wages, the entire signalling system of capitalism, presuppose value relations among producers exchanging embodied effort; remove the effort and the signals lose their referent. The conclusion sounds like a reductio, and critics have wielded it as one for over a century. Within the theory, however, the conclusion is not an embarrassment discovered by opponents but a consequence the notebooks themselves announce: production based on exchange value collapses when its foundation in labour time is undermined by its own drive to economise on that foundation. The limiting case therefore cannot refute the theory by pointing at a result the theory prints on its own frontispiece. What the limiting case genuinely tests is different, and harder: whether the tendencies toward that limit operate as described in the real economies that exist now, far from any endpoint, and whether the contradictions the theory predicts along the path, displacement, pressure on profitability, manufactured scarcity, concealed effort, actually appear. Those are empirical questions, and the sections that follow take them up in order.

Distinguish three propositions that popular argument habitually fuses. The first is that the endpoint is logically implied by the tendencies, which the theory affirms and its critics affirm, making it common ground rather than contested territory. The second is that the endpoint is approaching at some specifiable pace, a proposition the theory, carefully read, does not affirm, since tendencies entangled with countertendencies yield no timetable. The third is that movement along the path, however incomplete, generates the specific strains the analysis describes, which is the proposition on which the theory stakes its credibility and the one evidence can judge. Conflating the three lets critics demolish the second proposition, which nobody serious holds, and declare the whole structure refuted, while letting enthusiasts celebrate the first as though it established the second. A reader equipped with the distinctions can grade any automation argument quickly: ask which proposition the argument actually addresses, and discount the applause or derision directed at the others.

### What becomes of the value form at the fully mechanised limit?

on the theory's own terms, it disappears, while wealth multiplies. Value is embodied labour time, and a production process expending no labour time embodies none, so the categories built on value, price as its expression, profit as redistributed surplus, wages as the price of the capacity to work, lose the substance they measure at exactly the limit the system's drive approaches.

The value and wealth distinction does the heavy lifting in that answer, and it is worth pressing once more from a fresh angle. A waterfall that drives a mill produces wealth, in the form of ground grain, without producing value, and nobody regards the mill's output as paradoxical on that account. Sunlight grows crops, rivers carry barges, fertile soil yields harvests, and in each case nature's contribution to wealth is enormous while its contribution to value is nil, since value counts only the human effort socially expended. Machinery stands in the same class as those natural agents for the purposes of the value account, with the single difference that machinery itself had to be produced by effort before it could serve, which is why it transfers a definite embodied worth rather than contributing from a value of zero. The critic who exclaims that an automated plant manifestly produces enormous output has said something true about wealth and nothing at all about value, and the exchange of those two words is the oldest equivocation in this debate. Marx built the distinction into the opening pages of Capital, where the dual character of the commodity, useful thing and bearer of value, organises everything that follows. Arguments that ignore the opening distinction cannot reach the conclusions built upon it, however impressive their command of engineering detail.

None of this requires denying that the limiting case poses genuine difficulties for the tradition, and honesty requires naming the largest. If the endpoint dissolves the value form, then the transition beyond it involves a transformation of property, distribution, and social accounting for which the tradition offers principles rather than mechanisms: production for use, distribution by need, the free development of individuals. How an actual society traverses from a value economy straining against its own measure to an arrangement beyond that measure, through what institutions, at whose initiative, against whose resistance, the texts leave largely unspecified, and later writers within the tradition have filled the silence with everything from parliamentary gradualism to revolutionary rupture to the hope that abundance itself would make the old accounting quietly obsolete. The silence is a real gap. It is also a gap the notebooks acknowledge in their own way, since the moving contradiction passage describes a foundation crumbling rather than a building rising. A fair verdict holds both truths together: the diagnosis of the strain is precise and early, the prescription for what follows the strain is thin, and readers who need a prescription should know they are asking the theory for something it gestures toward instead of supplying.

### Is the fully automated economy an error in the theory or its own conclusion?

its own conclusion, reached by its author in the notebooks before any critic formulated the objection. The result follows from premises about value that can be disputed at the root, by denying that value is embodied labour time, but it cannot be presented as an oversight, since the tradition has displayed the consequence openly since the passage entered circulation.

Practical argument rarely visits the endpoint, however, and the theory earns its keep in the middle distances, where effort is shrinking in some places and swelling in others, where whole occupations are reconstituted rather than abolished, and where the interesting question is never whether machines in general replace people in general but which specific efforts, under which ownership, with gains flowing to whom. The middle distance is also where evidence lives. Two bodies of evidence discipline everything claimed about the middle distance, and the article now turns to them: the aggregate productivity record, which tests whether a transformation of production is occurring at the scale announced, and the research on concealed effort, which tests whether apparently effortless operation is what it appears to be. Between them, those two checks convert the automation debate from a contest of imaginations into an enquiry with findings, and they are the reason this article can afford its refusal to name devices or describe capabilities. Mechanisms and aggregates do not date. Devices do.

## The productivity check and the revolution that does not appear in the aggregate

State the check plainly, because its plainness is its power. Mechanisation exists, materially speaking, to raise the quantity of output obtained from each hour of human effort. A transformation of production sweeping enough to end work, remake every occupation, or found a new economic epoch must therefore appear, before anywhere else, in the growth rate of output per hour across the economies undergoing it. The productivity check simply looks. What it finds, across the advanced economies and across the decades during which the loudest transformation claims have circulated, is growth of output per hour that is slow by historical standards, slower than during the mid-twentieth-century decades of electrified factories and assembly lines, and stubbornly resistant to the acceleration the transformation narrative requires. Robert J. Gordon documented the pattern at length in The Rise and Fall of American Growth, published in 2016, arguing that the great inventions of the century between 1870 and 1970 transformed living standards more profoundly than anything since, and that the digital-era innovations, for all their visibility, have delivered comparatively modest gains in measured productivity outside narrow sectors. Gordon writes as a mainstream growth economist, not as a Marxist, and his finding is echoed in the statistical publications of the advanced economies themselves: the era announcing an automation revolution is, by the aggregate measure a revolution would have to move, an era of disappointing productivity performance. Economists have a name for the tension, the productivity paradox, a phrase attached to Robert Solow's celebrated observation that the new machinery of information could be seen everywhere except in the productivity statistics.

The check cuts against every camp impartially, which is the mark of a good instrument. Against the enthusiast who proclaims that abundance is at hand, it asks why the aggregate refuses to corroborate the proclamation. Against the catastrophist who warns that the machines are taking all the jobs, it asks how a displacement so total can coexist with output per hour growing so modestly, since mass displacement through superior methods is, arithmetically, one of the routes by which output per hour rises. Against the Marxist triumphalist tempted to announce that the moving contradiction is visibly detonating, it asks the same question in another form: the wearing away of the value measure should accompany a surge in the productivity of the remaining effort, and no surge appears. What appears instead is a long plateau of slow improvement, punctuated by sectoral dramas, a picture consistent with the tendencies operating at ordinary strength amid powerful countertendencies, and wholly inconsistent with a revolution accomplished or imminent. The namable claim of this article, the productivity check, is exactly that discipline stated as a rule of enquiry: a transformative automation revolution should appear as accelerating productivity growth, it has not in the advanced economies over the relevant decades, and any account of a transformation must explain the absence rather than assume the transformation.

### How do the aggregate figures judge the transformation claim?

no, in its strong form. Output per hour across the advanced economies has grown slowly over the decades in which a sweeping transformation is said to have occurred, slower than during the great mechanisation of the mid twentieth century, and a revolution in production methods that left the aggregate measure unmoved would be a revolution that failed at its defining task.

Fairness requires stating the replies the transformation narrative makes to the check, at their strongest. The first reply alleges mismeasurement: output in a digital economy includes vast quantities of free or cheap services whose value to users escapes the price-based statistics from which productivity is computed, so the aggregate understates the transformation. The reply has substance; measurement of quality change and of zero-price goods is genuinely difficult, and official statisticians wrestle with it openly. Yet the mismeasurement defence faces a quantitative problem, since the gap it must fill is not a rounding error but the difference between a revolution and a plateau, and studies attempting to price the unmeasured benefits generally recover amounts too small to close that gap. The defence also cuts awkwardly for its usual sponsors, because if the new abundance largely escapes measurement by escaping price, then the abundance is occurring substantially outside the value form already, a concession the Marxist analysis is happy to accept and explore. The second reply alleges delay: transformative technologies historically take decades to reorganise production around themselves, as electrification did, with the productivity surge arriving only after factories, skills, and management practices are rebuilt for the new power source. That reply is historically literate and may even prove correct. Notice, though, what it concedes: on the delay account, the revolution has not happened yet in the aggregate, its arrival is a promissory note, and arguments premised on its accomplished presence, whether utopian or apocalyptic, are premature by the narrative's own admission. The check does not forbid anyone from expecting a future acceleration. It forbids describing an unobserved acceleration as a present fact.

A third reply, subtler than the others, holds that productivity growth is being suppressed by the very success of employment expansion: as mechanisation draws marginal workers into low-productivity services, the composition of employment shifts toward activities where output per hour is hard to raise, dragging the aggregate even while the mechanised frontier advances brilliantly. Something like that composition effect is real in the statistics and deserves its place in a complete account. Observe, however, that the reply relocates the transformation rather than rescuing it: the frontier advances while the economy as a whole, the thing workers and citizens actually inhabit, stagnates in its measured capacity to convert effort into output. A revolution confined to enclaves, surrounded by a swelling periphery of effort-intensive, low-productivity work, is precisely the landscape the Marxist analysis predicts when rising composition in the core coexists with a reserve of labour absorbed into whatever employment the periphery offers. The reply, pressed to its conclusion, becomes evidence for the framework it was marshalled against, which happens often enough in this debate that readers should learn to expect the manoeuvre and to follow arguments all the way to their resting places.

### By what standard should a transformation claim be marked?

by asking a single prior question of any transformation claim: where is the acceleration in output per hour that a production revolution must produce? Claims accompanied by aggregate acceleration earn a hearing on their other merits. Claims accompanied by stagnation are graded as assertions awaiting their evidence, whatever the brilliance of the particular examples they display.

Comparison, to mean anything, must specify its terms, and the comparison between the present period and earlier mechanisation is usually conducted in a fog of unspecified terms. Conducted properly, the comparison runs as follows. The economies compared are the advanced economies for which long statistical series exist, the group whose statistical offices publish harmonised measures of output and hours. The decades compared are the middle decades of the twentieth century, when electrification, mechanised agriculture, and the moving assembly line completed their diffusion, against the opening decades of the twenty-first, when the transformation now under discussion is said to have occurred. The measure compared is output per hour worked, the standard productivity magnitude, tracked in the publications of national statistical offices and international economic organisations. On those specified terms, the earlier period wins decisively: output per hour grew briskly enough to double living standards within a generation, while the later period's growth, though positive, proceeds at a pace that would take far longer to achieve the same doubling. Anyone wishing to contest the comparison should contest the terms openly, by naming different economies, different decades, or a different measure, rather than by gesturing at the felt intensity of change in daily life. Felt intensity is a real phenomenon with its own causes, among them the visibility of devices carried on the person and used in leisure, which saturates perception while contributing little to the measured product of the working economy. A revolution in consumption gadgetry and a revolution in production are different events, and the check exists to keep them separate.

## Hidden labour and the manufacture of apparent autonomy

The second empirical check begins from a deceptively simple research practice: instead of asking what a system is said to do, investigators ask who is working, where, at what pay, whenever the system operates. Mary L. Gray and Siddharth Suri gave the resulting literature its best-known synthesis in Ghost Work, published in 2019, documenting the human effort sustaining services marketed as automatic: people classifying and labelling material so that automated processes have something reliable to learn from, people reviewing content that automated filters flag or miss, people transcribing, verifying, correcting, impersonating, and standing ready to intervene when an automated sequence encounters a case outside its repertoire. Antonio Casilli's parallel research reached comparable findings from the European side, emphasising the global supply chains of small, fragmented, badly paid tasks that flow from wealthy economies to poorer ones through layers of intermediation. The details vary by service, and this article, faithful to its structural discipline, describes no particular system's workings. The pattern is what matters, and the pattern is consistent: arrangements presented to their users as self-acting depend, at load-bearing points, on human effort that has been dispersed, fragmented, and placed where users will never encounter it. Automation, in a significant class of real cases, turns out to be a method of organising labour so that it becomes invisible, rather than a method of abolishing labour outright.

Understand the mechanisms of invisibility, since each corresponds to a technique with a history. Spatial dispersal moves the effort far from the user, across borders and time zones, so that the person correcting a result at three in the morning by their clock belongs to a different social world from the person who requested the result at noon by theirs. Fragmentation breaks jobs into tasks so small that no participant sees the whole process or can identify the final product, dissolving the experience of employment into a stream of disconnected micro-operations paid by the piece. Intermediation inserts platforms, agencies, and subcontractors between the firm whose name the user knows and the worker whose effort the user consumes, so that responsibility for wages and conditions can be disowned at every level. Interface design completes the concealment by presenting the output as the product of the system alone, with the human contributions edited out of the story the service tells about itself, a presentation that is not incidental decoration but a selling point, since effortlessness is precisely what is being sold. Each mechanism has ancestors in earlier rounds of mechanisation: the putting-out system dispersed effort into cottages, the factory's internal contracting fragmented responsibility, and the brand has always edited the workshop out of the commodity's biography. The commodity form itself, Marx argued, performs the original concealment, presenting social relations among producers as relations among things. Concealed digital piecework extends that logic with new tools; it does not invent it.

### Whose effort sustains arrangements presented as self-acting?

the training, labelling, moderation, correction, transcription, and exception handling performed by dispersed and poorly paid workers on which apparently self-acting arrangements depend. Research that follows the work rather than the marketing finds this effort at the load-bearing points of system after system, organised through fragmentation and intermediation so that users never see it and firms can disown it.

The finding disciplines the debate in several directions at once. It rebukes the simple displacement story, in which effort exits production as apparatus enters, by showing effort reappearing in altered form at the edges of the apparatus, often at lower pay and with fewer protections than the employment it supplemented or replaced. It rebukes the post-scarcity story in advance, since an abundance resting on concealed toil in distant places has not abolished the realm of necessity but exported it beyond the consumer's field of vision. It vindicates the framework's insistence on asking where the living effort is, rather than whether it exists, because the framework predicts exactly this shell game: capital economises on effort at the visible point of production while reconstituting effort wherever it can be obtained more cheaply, more flexibly, or with less organised resistance, and the rising composition at the core coexists with labour-intensive peripheries as a single strategy rather than as a contradiction in the data. It also gives the reserve army a contemporary anatomy. The dispersed task workers of the ghost-work literature are a reserve in near-perfect form: available on demand, paid only when used, bearing their own waiting time and equipment costs, competing globally against one another for fragments of work, and invisible to the end users whose convenience their availability purchases. Marx's category, framed for the industrial reserve of nineteenth-century cities, describes the functional position with uncomfortable precision even though every surface detail has changed.

Two cautions keep the finding honest. First, concealed effort is not the whole of any system's operation, and the research does not show that nothing is automated anywhere; it shows that the boundary between the automated and the manual is drawn by organisations, moved for reasons of cost and presentation, and systematically misdescribed by marketing. Some processes genuinely run with minimal human involvement for long stretches, and the interesting analytical question is always where a given arrangement draws its boundary and why. Second, the existence of hidden labour does not by itself settle the trajectory question. A transitional phase in which human effort scaffolds an immature arrangement, with the scaffolding later withdrawn, is conceivable in principle, and the ghost-work researchers themselves are careful about prediction. What the finding settles is the present tense: descriptions of accomplished effortlessness, applied to arrangements presently scaffolded by effort, are false descriptions, and any theory of the present built on them inherits the falsity. The structural habit of asking who works, where, at what pay, under whose direction, detects the scaffolding regardless of how the trajectory eventually runs, which is why the habit outlasts every particular exposure.

### Who trains the machines, in the framework's vocabulary?

living labour, expended in the production of the conditions under which apparatus can operate: effort that selects, labels, orders, and corrects the material from which automated processes are built and against which their outputs are checked. The framework classifies that effort as value-creating work like any other, however the purchasing firms choose to describe or conceal it.

Notice how the hidden-labour finding and the productivity check interlock. If a significant share of apparently automated output is in fact produced by reconstituted human effort at the periphery, then the failure of aggregate productivity to accelerate stops being mysterious. Output per hour cannot surge when the hours are not actually being saved but relocated, fragmented, and repriced downward. The two checks are one check viewed from two sides: the aggregate says the effort has not been economised on anything like the announced scale, and workplace research finds the effort, still being expended, in places the announcement declined to mention. A debate conducted with both instruments in hand would spend less time on the metaphysics of machine intelligence and more on the accounting of hours, which is where the Marxist tradition has always insisted the reality of an economy resides. Hours are stubborn things. They can be moved across borders, split into fragments, renamed as tasks, or edited out of a sales pitch, but they cannot be argued out of existence, and a society's total expenditure of them, their distribution, and their price remain the ground on which every grander claim must eventually stand.

## The post-scarcity position stated at full strength

The strongest contemporary descendant of the notebooks' speculative pages argues that the tendencies Marx traced have matured to the point where scarcity itself, the premise of all previous economics, can be abolished as a social condition. Aaron Bastani gave the position its most cited statement in Fully Automated Luxury Communism, published in 2019, contending that the converging development of mechanised production, cheap energy, and abundant information makes a society of generalised plenty technically available, in which work shrinks toward a minor and largely voluntary activity, goods and services approach free provision, and the remaining obstacles are political: property relations and habits of thought inherited from an age when scarcity was real. The position deserves presentation at full strength rather than in the caricatured form its opponents prefer, because it draws on genuine features of the landscape. The marginal cost of reproducing information goods does approach zero. Mechanised production has demonstrated, across two centuries, a capacity to cheapen staple after staple until goods once reserved for elites became disposable. Energy from renewable sources, once harnessed, arrives without a fuel bill. A defender can fairly ask what, in principle, prevents the extension of that cheapening logic across the whole field of necessities until provision detaches from income altogether, and can fairly note that Marx's own notebooks sketched the destination, in the collapse of labour time as the measure of wealth, long before the technologies invoked were imaginable.

The Marxist response, conducted from inside the tradition the position claims to extend, begins by separating the technical premise from the social conclusion. Cheapening is real, and the framework not only accepts but predicts it: competition forces unit values downward wherever methods improve, and a long enough run of improvement can indeed push some goods toward practical freeness. What the framework denies is that cheapening accumulates automatically into post-scarcity, because the value form defends itself at each step. As necessities cheapen, the frontier of socially recognised necessity expands; goods and services that were luxuries become requirements for ordinary participation in society, and the working population's needs grow alongside the productivity that was supposed to liberate it. Marx built that expansion into the worth of labour power itself, which contains what he called a historical and moral element, the conventional standard of life of a given society at a given time. A basket of necessities is not a physiological constant; it is a social settlement, renegotiated by each generation's expectations, and the history of consumption since the first power looms is the history of the basket swelling as fast as productivity cheapened its contents. Post-scarcity must therefore outrun not a fixed target but a moving one, and the movement is not a conspiracy or a failure of imagination; it is the ordinary operation of a society in which standing is relative and needs are formed socially.

A second defence of the value form operates through scarcity manufacture. Where a good threatens to become too cheap to sustain a market, because its reproduction costs almost nothing, property law intervenes to construct exclusivity: the good is fenced, licensed, metered, and rented back to its users under conditions that preserve a price. The framework reads such arrangements not as aberrations but as the predictable immune response of a system that must keep value scarce in order to keep value at all, and the response is visible wherever abundance presses hardest against the price mechanism. A third defence operates through the labour market itself. Incomes in a wage economy arrive through employment, so any cheapening that threatens employment threatens the distribution mechanism, and societies respond, through policy and through the private creation of new service employments, by reconstituting work faster than mechanisation dissolves it. The swelling of administrative, promotional, supervisory, and care employments across the advanced economies, whatever their individual usefulness, testifies to a system that manufactures roles because its members need wages, not because the material tasks of survival require the hours. Bastani's position acknowledges the political obstacle in general terms; the framework's contribution is to specify the obstacle's mechanisms, to show that scarcity under capitalism is not merely a leftover awaiting technical abolition but a condition actively reproduced by the relations through which goods and incomes are distributed. The dispute between the post-scarcity writer and the stricter Marxist is therefore not about whether plenty is technically imaginable. It is about whether a social form can be expected to permit its own foundation to dissolve, and the tradition's answer, from the notebooks onward, is that the form resists, adapts, and displaces its contradictions rather than surrendering to them politely.

### Can productive capacity by itself deliver a society beyond scarcity?

in the material sense, the tradition answers yes without hesitation: productive powers capable of meeting social needs with a fraction of present effort are exactly what the rising productivity of labour means. In the social sense the answer is conditional, since a distribution system built on wages and prices must be replaced rather than merely outgrown, and replacement is a political event, not a technical by-product.

Judge the exchange by the empirical checks and both sides lose something. The post-scarcity position, premised on a transformation of productive capacity, must face the productivity check: output per hour in the advanced economies has grown too slowly, for too long, to support the claim that the material premise is already in hand at the required scale, whatever the trajectory of particular enclaves. Enclave brilliance is not in dispute; the aggregate is, and societies live in the aggregate. The strict position, for its part, must concede that the cheapening tendency it predicts has genuinely transformed the material basis of life across two centuries, that goods do approach freeness at the margin in domain after domain, and that the defences of the value form, however ingenious, consume real resources and generate real absurdities that a defender of the present order must explain away. A debate in which each side's favourite evidence embarrasses the other is a healthy debate, and the reader who holds both checks in view, aggregate productivity and manufactured scarcity, possesses a better map of the territory than either camp's pamphlets supply.

## Tasks, jobs and the mainstream framework that matters

The most consequential mainstream contribution to the automation debate reorganises the unit of analysis. Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo, in a series of papers including The Race Between Man and Machine in 2018 and Automation and New Tasks in 2019, argue that automation displaces labour from particular tasks rather than abolishing jobs wholesale, and that the net effect on employment and wages depends on a race between that displacement and the creation of new tasks in which human effort holds a comparative advantage. Jobs, on their account, are bundles of tasks; a new method typically captures some tasks within a bundle, reconfigures the rest, and simultaneously generates fresh tasks elsewhere in the economy: designing, supervising, maintaining, selling, and servicing the new arrangements, along with tasks in entirely new lines of activity that expanding incomes make viable. Where new-task creation runs ahead, employment and wages can grow alongside mechanisation; where displacement runs ahead, because innovation concentrates on replacing effort rather than augmenting it, the results are the stagnation and polarisation the advanced economies have displayed. The framework's authors are explicit that the direction of innovation is chosen, shaped by tax systems that subsidise equipment while taxing employment, by research incentives, and by managerial fashion, so the race's outcome is a policy result rather than a technological fate.

A Marxist reader finds much to recognise in that architecture, translated into another vocabulary. The bundle-of-tasks picture matches the decomposition of crafts described in the discussion of Braverman above: production advances by unbundling integrated activities and redistributing the fragments among apparatus, narrowed operative roles, and a supervisory stratum. The insistence that displacement is the default direction of profit-seeking innovation restates, in econometric dress, the claim that owners adopt methods to cheapen production relative to the social average, which means, in the first instance, to economise on the wage bill. The category of new tasks corresponds to the absorption mechanism the older theory located in the expansion of branches and the circulation of the reserve, with the mainstream version adding a valuable precision: absorption is not automatic, its pace is measurable in principle, and its failure has identifiable causes in the incentive structure facing innovators. Where the frameworks part company is over the deep variable. The task framework treats the race's balance as a contingent outcome of incentives that better policy could correct within the existing order. The Marxist framework treats the displacement bias as structural, rooted in the fact that the wage is both a cost to be minimised and the source of all surplus, so the system is driven to economise on the very input it lives from, the moving contradiction restated at the level of the task. Policy can tilt the race, on the Marxist account, as policy has tilted it before through hours legislation and public employment, yet the tilt works against a gradient that reasserts itself whenever the policy relaxes, because the gradient is generated by competition itself rather than by any correctable oversight in the tax code.

### At what unit does displacement actually operate?

tasks, in the first instance, on the evidence assembled by the task framework's authors: methods capture particular operations within jobs, bundles are reconfigured, and employment outcomes depend on whether new tasks arise at a matching pace. The Marxist account accepts the mechanism and adds that the bias toward displacement over augmentation is structural rather than accidental.

The exchange between the frameworks is most productive where each supplies the other's missing term. The task literature gives the older theory a finer instrument for reading particular episodes: instead of asking whether a method destroys employment in aggregate, an analyst can inventory the tasks captured, the tasks created, the wage levels attaching to each, and the direction of the incentive structure, producing an account with moving parts that evidence can confirm or refute severally. The older theory gives the task literature an explanation for its own most puzzling finding, the persistence of what its authors call so-so automation, methods that displace workers while raising productivity barely at all. From a purely technical standpoint, adopting a method that saves little and displaces much looks irrational. From the standpoint of the value framework, the adoption makes immediate sense: a method need not raise social productivity to profit its adopter, since profit follows from beating the social average and from weakening the bargaining position of the workforce, and a method that renders workers more replaceable can pay for itself through the wage settlements it disciplines even when its contribution to output per hour is negligible. The productivity check and the so-so finding are the same observation at different scales: an economy can be busy mechanising, visibly and expensively, while its aggregate productivity stagnates, because the private returns to adoption and the social returns to productivity are different quantities that competition does not force into alignment. That sentence, if the reader retains only one from this section, reconciles more of the automation debate's apparent contradictions than any prediction about the future of work.

## What Marx wrote about machinery across the published work

The notebooks' speculation rests on a foundation of sustained analysis in the published writings, and a reader who wants to know what Marx said about machinery should begin where he said most: the long chapter on machinery and modern industry in the first volume of Capital, a chapter built from factory inspectors' reports, parliamentary papers, and the technical literature of the mechanised trades. Its argument moves on several levels at once. At the level of the labour process, Marx distinguishes the tool, which extends the worker's hand and remains subordinate to the worker's skill, from the machine system, in which the mechanism itself embodies the skill and the worker attends upon a process whose pace and sequence are set elsewhere. The transfer of skill from person to apparatus is, for him, the defining event of modern industry, more fundamental than any question of power sources, and it explains both the productivity of the factory and the transformation of the worker's position within it: the lifelong specialist of the workshop gives way to the interchangeable attendant, whose particular craft knowledge has been absorbed into the design of the system and whose labour becomes, in his phrase, abstract in practice before it is abstract in theory.

At the level of social consequence, the chapter traces machinery's effects through the workforce with a reporter's patience. Mechanisation draws women and children into the factory by dissolving the strength and skill requirements that had excluded them, thereby multiplying the number of family members whose capacity to work the owner purchases while spreading the family's subsistence across more earners, a cheapening of the individual wage achieved through the reorganisation of the household. It lengthens the working day in the first instance, because expensive apparatus lying idle is capital wasted, and owners press for continuous operation to extract the apparatus's value before moral depreciation overtakes it, until legislation and resistance impose limits that then redirect the pressure into intensity: fewer hours, worked faster, with the machine's pace dictating the human rhythm. It creates the industrial reserve in its modern form, as each improvement expels a cohort of attendants whose skills the improvement has devalued. And it arms the owner in disputes, a function Marx states without euphemism, observing that a history could be written of the inventions introduced as weapons against strikes, devices whose economic rationale cannot be separated from the industrial conflict they were designed to win. Self-acting equipment, in the chapter's most quoted judgement, is capital's most powerful weapon for suppressing resistance to its demands, a judgement framed about nineteenth-century apparatus that later readers have found uncomfortably portable.

### Which judgements does the machinery chapter of Capital reach?

The chapter holds that skill passes from the worker into the system, commodities cheapen, new populations are drawn into wage labour, the working day first lengthens and then intensifies, workers are expelled into a reserve, and owners gain a weapon in industrial disputes, while no value is created by the apparatus itself. Its evidence is factory inspectors' reports and parliamentary papers.

The chapter also contains the germ of the productivity discussion, in Marx's attention to what the factory system does to the measure of a day's work. When the pace is set by the system, an hour of labour is no longer the hour of the handicraft era; it is a denser hour, more expenditure of nerve and attention compressed into the same clock time, and the theory's category of intensity exists to register the difference. Intensity matters to the value account because value follows expended effort, and a denser hour expends more, so mechanisation can raise the value produced per worker per day even as it lowers the value per unit of output, two movements that popular discussion habitually confuses. It matters to the human account because the denser hour is experienced as exhaustion of a particular modern kind, the fatigue of keeping pace with a process that never tires, and the factory literature of every mechanising society records that fatigue in its medical reports and its fiction alike. Readers who approach the machinery chapter expecting a philosopher's abstractions encounter instead an empiricist at work among blue books and inspectors' testimony, which is worth remembering when the tradition is accused of deducing the world from definitions. The definitions came last, hammered out on the anvil of the evidence, and the evidence was gathered by the state's own inspectors, whose politics were not Marx's and whose findings he could therefore cite against the system's defenders with special effect.

Later writings add two refinements that the automation debate needs. The analysis of cooperation and manufacture shows that productivity gains attributed to machinery often originate in reorganisation, in the simple concentration and sequencing of efforts, so that the machine receives credit for the yield of a social arrangement; the caution applies with full force wherever a new arrangement's results are credited to its most visible device. The discussion of fixed capital in the second volume, published after Marx's death from his manuscripts, examines the turnover of apparatus value and the crises that the long life of fixed investments imposes on the system's rhythm, since commitments made for a decade must be honoured through conditions nobody can foresee. Together the refinements support a habit of analysis this article has practised throughout: attribute results to arrangements before devices, ask whose effort an arrangement organises and whose it expels, and follow the value as it moves, transfers, and occasionally vanishes, rather than as it is advertised.

## Relative surplus value and the cheapening of the wage basket

Return to the mechanism that makes machinery the engine of the system rather than a mere convenience within it. Surplus value can grow in two ways. It grows absolutely when the working day lengthens while the portion replacing the wage stays fixed, the method of the early factory and of every sweatshop since. It grows relatively when the portion replacing the wage shrinks, which happens when the goods composing the wage basket grow cheaper, which happens when productivity rises in the branches producing those goods or their inputs. Relative surplus value is the distinctively modern method, compatible with a working day that is stable or even shortening, and machinery is its instrument because machinery is how productivity rises. The elegance of the mechanism explains the system's restlessness better than any psychology of greed. An owner need not desire a larger share for its own sake; competition suffices, since the owner who fails to cheapen falls behind the social average and is ruined by rivals who did not fail. The surplus extracted by the successful is then the fund from which the next round of equipment is purchased, so the process feeds itself: surplus buys apparatus, apparatus cheapens the basket, cheapening enlarges surplus. Political economy calls such a loop accumulation, and the Marxist account adds that the loop has no internal resting point, no level of plenty at which the pressure relaxes, because the pressure is generated by the relations among the participants rather than by the absolute level of output achieved.

Follow the loop into the wage basket and a subtlety emerges that the automation debate usually misses. The basket is not composed only of food and shelter; it includes the means of participating in ordinary social life, and as productivity cheapens the material core of the basket, the social periphery expands to fill the space. Transport to reach employment, communication to remain employable, credentials to qualify for positions, care services that release household members for work: each generation's basket contains items the previous generation would have classified as luxuries or as free goods of the household and the commons. The expansion is not a trick played on workers by advertisers, though advertising assists it; it reflects real changes in what a person must command in order to appear in the labour market as a normal seller of the capacity to work. The consequence for the automation question is direct. Productivity growth cheapens the basket's core while social development enlarges the basket's extent, and the race between cheapening and enlargement determines whether the necessary portion of the working day actually shrinks. In periods when cheapening wins, real wages can rise while the rate of surplus value also rises, a combination that confuses commentators who assume the two must move oppositely. In periods when enlargement wins, workers can feel poorer amid rising productivity, a pairing that baffles observers who expect rising productivity to feel like prosperity. The framework predicts both combinations under their respective conditions, which is the kind of conditional, mechanism-level prediction a mature theory ought to supply.

### Which two movements must a student keep apart when reading mechanisation?

the physical yield of an hour from the social measure of that hour. Raising productivity multiplies the useful output an hour delivers while leaving the new value the hour creates governed by its duration and intensity, so unit values fall as yields rise, and confusing the two movements makes mechanisation look like a source of value when it is a source of wealth.

The cheapening mechanism also explains why the benefits of mechanisation arrive in the peculiar distribution they do: prices fall for consumers, who are also workers, while the surplus concentrates with owners, and the same person experiences the process simultaneously as cheaper goods and as a more precarious hold on employment. Popular argument tends to assign each experience to a different cause, praising innovation for the prices and blaming globalisation or policy for the precariousness. The framework assigns both to one process viewed from two positions within it. The worker as consumer gains exactly what the worker as seller loses the ground for claiming, since the cheapening that fills the shopping basket also cheapens the capacity to work and swells the reserve competing to sell it. Whether the net position of a given workforce improves across a mechanising generation depends on organisation, legislation, and the state of the reserve, variables the framework treats as genuinely open, which is why its adherents have historically invested as much in unions and hours laws as in treatises. A theory of machinery that ends in a theory of organisation is not changing the subject. It is following the mechanism to the point where human decisions govern its outcomes, and refusing to pretend the apparatus decides alone.

## Intensity, surveillance and the denser hour

Mechanisation's least visible effect operates inside the working day rather than upon its length. When legislation and collective resistance closed the road of absolute surplus value by capping hours, the pressure of accumulation diverted into the day's interior, and the history of production since the first factory acts is substantially a history of that diversion. The pace of the line quickens. Pauses that belonged to the rhythm of handwork, the breathers absorbed invisibly into a craftsman's tempo, are identified as waste and engineered away. Tasks once separated by walking, fetching, and waiting are fused into continuous sequences in which the attendant's attention is the scarce resource being mined. Measurement follows the same path inward: where the early factory counted hours and pieces, the mature system counts motions, seconds, deviations, and idle intervals, assembling from those fragments a picture of the working day detailed enough to manage effort at a granularity the nineteenth-century overlooker, watching a floor of operatives from a raised desk, could not have imagined. The framework's category of intensity exists for exactly this territory. An intensified hour expends more labour, in the physiological and attentional sense, than a slack hour, and therefore creates more value, while the clock records the two hours as identical. The distinction allows the theory to explain a combination that puzzles simpler accounts: output per worker rising, value per worker rising, hours constant, and exhaustion deepening, all at once, without any new device needing to be credited with magical properties.

The worker's experience of the denser hour has its own literature, and the framework reads that literature as evidence rather than decoration. Fatigue studies, occupational medicine, and the testimony collected by labour inspectors across successive mechanising generations converge on a pattern: the burden shifts from the muscles to the nervous system, from weight lifted to attention sustained, from the risk of the dramatic accident toward the slower injuries of repetition, vigilance, and pace without respite. A system of production that sets its rhythm mechanically externalises the cost of that rhythm onto the bodies and minds keeping pace, and the costs surface in clinics and households rather than in the accounts of the firms whose methods generate them. Marx noticed the pattern in the inspectors' reports available to him, remarking on the way machinery, by lightening muscular exertion while tightening the tempo, could leave the operative more exhausted at the end of a shorter day than the labourer had been at the end of a longer one. The observation generalises beyond any particular apparatus because its mechanism is institutional: whatever sets the pace, and whoever owns the pace-setter, the gains from quickening accrue as surplus while the costs of quickening accrue as wear on the people, and the accounts of the two parties never meet on a single ledger.

### How does intensification raise surplus without lengthening the day?

by compressing more expenditure of effort into each hour, so that the worker produces more value within the same clock time while the portion of the day replacing the wage remains unchanged. The surplus portion grows in substance although the day's boundaries stand still, which is why struggles over pace succeed struggles over hours in mature mechanised industries.

Surveillance deserves separate treatment within this section, because the monitoring of effort has become one of the principal sites where new arrangements are actually deployed, whatever grander claims are made for them elsewhere. The framework approaches monitoring through the wage relation's oldest problem: the owner purchases a capacity, the ability to work for a period, while what the owner needs is the activity, effort actually expended at an acceptable pace and quality, and the gap between capacity purchased and activity delivered is the permanent contested terrain of the workplace. Every supervisory technology in history, from the overlooker's desk to the foreman's stopwatch to the dashboard of exceptions, addresses that gap, attempting to convert a purchased potential into a guaranteed performance. Workers, for their part, have always defended the gap as the space in which the working day remains humanly bearable, and the customs of restriction of output, of pacing oneself against the day, of concealing the true ease of a task from time study, are the defensive traditions of that defence. Read through the framework, the spread of fine-grained monitoring is neither a mystery nor a prelude to effortlessness; it is the newest campaign in the oldest workplace war, waged with new instruments over the same strip of territory, the difference between what was bought and what will be given. The campaign also has a value dimension the debate rarely notes. Monitoring effort is itself effort, performed by supervisors, analysts, and the concealed correctors of the monitoring's own errors, so the attempt to squeeze the gap generates a growing supervisory overhead whose cost must be carried by the surplus it exists to enlarge. Systems differ in where that trade-off settles. None escapes it.

Set against that analysis, claims that monitoring arrangements herald the end of work invert the evidence. The denser hour and the watched hour are what mechanisation under the wage relation characteristically produces in its maturity: not the abolition of effort but its concentration, measurement, and discipline, applied to a workforce whose numbers at the point of production may shrink while the intensity demanded of each remaining member climbs. The framework therefore instructs its readers to ask of any celebrated arrangement a question the celebration never poses: what happened to the total hours, the pace within them, and the price paid for them, across the whole chain of people the arrangement touches, including the invisible correctors at its edges? An arrangement that survives that question with its claims intact has earned attention. Most celebrated arrangements, examined whole, turn out to have moved the hours, squeezed them, or cheapened them, and the framework's vocabulary, transfer, displacement, intensification, was built to say exactly which.

## Manufactured scarcity and the knowledge problem

A second defensive reaction of the value form deserves a section of its own, because it grows more important as production leans more heavily on knowledge. Knowledge has an awkward economic property: once produced, it can be applied again at negligible cost, shared without being diminished, and improved by its users in ways that feed back into the common stock. A design, a method, a formula, a body of organised information behaves unlike a sack of grain; consumption does not deplete it, and the socially necessary effort for its reproduction approaches the cost of copying, which technical development drives toward zero. Left to itself, such a good would be priced at its reproduction cost and would therefore be practically free, which is splendid for wealth and fatal for any business whose revenue depends on selling it. The system's solution is to manufacture the scarcity that nature and technique decline to supply. Legal monopolies fence bodies of knowledge and charge for admission. Secrecy walls off what law cannot fence. Access is metered, licensed, time-limited, and revoked, converting a good that could be a commons into a stream of rents. The framework reads the entire apparatus of manufactured scarcity as the value form defending its territory against the implications of the productive forces it has itself called into being, the moving contradiction wearing institutional dress.

The notebooks anticipated the shape of the conflict when they located the decisive productive power in the general intellect, the social stock of knowledge, rather than in direct effort at the point of production. A society whose wealth flows mainly from a collectively produced and collectively held stock, yet which distributes income mainly through the sale of individual effort by the hour, has mismatched its production basis and its distribution basis as thoroughly as the passage describes. Manufactured scarcity is one bridge across the mismatch, converting the common stock back into private revenue. Concealed effort is another bridge, reconstituting hourly toil at the edges of arrangements advertised as knowledge-driven. The productivity plateau is the mismatch appearing in the statistics, since an economy busy fencing its knowledge and scaffolding its automation has diverted much of its ingenuity from raising output per hour to defending the forms through which output is appropriated. None of those bridges resolves the contradiction; each relocates it, at a cost, into law, into geography, into the statistics themselves. The tradition's claim is not that the bridges must collapse on schedule. It is that a reader who can see the bridges understands the landscape, while a reader who sees only the devices understands the marketing.

### Why must scarcity be manufactured for knowledge goods?

because knowledge reproduces at negligible cost once it exists, so its price would collapse toward that cost under open competition, destroying the revenue of anyone whose business is selling it. Legal monopoly, secrecy, and metered access rebuild the scarcity that technique abolished, converting a potential commons into a stream of privately collected rents.

The point connects backward to the analysis of surplus profit and forward to the question of what an alternative would even mean. Surplus profit decayed, in the earlier analysis, because rivals could acquire the same equipment; knowledge monopolies exist precisely to prevent that equalising acquisition, to hold the diffusion window open indefinitely by force of law. Where the law succeeds, the premium does not decay and rent replaces surplus profit as the appropriate category, a refinement the tradition developed for land and applies without strain to fenced knowledge. Where the law fails, through copying, leakage, or rival jurisdictions, the premium decays with unusual speed because the underlying reproduction cost is so low, which is why the enforcement apparatus surrounding knowledge goods grows ever more elaborate and why its costs, legal, technical, and administrative, mount into a significant industry of their own. An economy devoting a rising share of its effort to preventing the free movement of its most productive resource is exhibiting, in the framework's terms, the fettering of productive forces by relations of production that the tradition's historical writings describe in other contexts. The phrase is old. The phenomenon it names, ingenuity spent on scarcity while abundance waits outside the fence, is a serviceable one-sentence summary of a great deal of the modern economy's stranger architecture, and it required no knowledge of any particular device to formulate, only attention to the price mechanism's needs.

Resist, once again, the conversion of that analysis into a timetable. Fences can stand for centuries; land enclosure, the ancestor of all manufactured scarcity, organised whole societies for generations and still does. The framework's claim concerns the direction of the strain and the character of the costs, not the date of any resolution, and it is content to predict the proliferation of fencing, scaffolding, and enforcement as the knowledge basis of production deepens, a prediction the reader can test against the growth of the legal and security industries surrounding intangible goods. Predictions of that conditional, institutional kind are the currency in which the theory legitimately trades, and they contrast favourably, in testability if not in drama, with predictions that a particular device will abolish work by a particular year. The article's remaining argumentative business is to face the strongest objection to the whole construction, and then to gather its findings into the value question table and an honest statement of what is not known.

## The strongest objection: the reductio and the charge of evasion

State the objection at full strength, in the form its ablest critics give it, because an article that answers only the weak version of its opposition has answered nothing. The objection runs as follows. A theory holding that value originates solely in living labour implies that a highly mechanised economy produces almost no value while manifestly producing enormous and growing output. The implication, critics argue, is not a profound paradox but a reductio ad absurdum: any accounting that assigns near-zero value to the output of a rich economy has lost contact with the phenomenon it purports to measure, and the sensible conclusion is that value originates in production generally, in the cooperation of labour with capital equipment and natural agents, rather than in labour alone. The objection then presses a second charge, aimed at the tradition's characteristic reply. When Marxists respond that the shrinking of value amid growing wealth is a real contradiction in the system rather than a defect in the theory, the critics hear an evasion dressed as profundity: a failed prediction rebranded as a dialectical insight, a theory immunising itself against refutation by declaring that its most embarrassing consequence was its deepest truth all along. Versions of the objection appear across the critical literature, from the marginalist economists who first disputed the labour theory's account of price to later analytical critics who formalised the charge that the theory either reduces to a truism about accounting identities or fails as an explanation of profit. The names attached to the strongest versions vary by generation; the logical skeleton does not, and the skeleton is what an answer must engage.

The answer begins by accepting the objection's first move and denying that it lands where critics think. The theory does hold that value and wealth diverge as productivity rises, and it holds this not as an embarrassed concession but as a theorem stated at the outset: the same process that multiplies useful things cheapens each of them in value terms, and a society can therefore grow richer in every material respect while the value embodied in its total product grows slowly, stagnates, or in the imagined limit vanishes. Whether that theorem is a reductio depends entirely on what value is supposed to be. If value were a measure of material abundance, the theorem would indeed be absurd. The theory defines value otherwise, as the social form wealth takes under generalised commodity production, the mechanism by which a society of independent producers allocates its collective effort among branches and disciplines each producer to the social average. A measure of allocated effort is not a measure of output, and there is no absurdity in the two diverging; a census of hours worked in a country can stagnate while the country's output doubles, and nobody declares the census refuted by the output figures. The critic who finds the divergence absurd has usually smuggled in the assumption that value must track wealth, which is precisely the assumption under dispute. Begging the question is not refuting the answer to it.

### Is the mechanised economy a refutation of the value theory?

no, although it poses the theory's hardest internal problem in its sharpest form. Mechanisation cheapens commodities and shrinks the labour embodied in each unit exactly as the theory requires, and the fully mechanised limit in which value vanishes was stated by the theory's author as the system's own contradiction, so the phenomenon the objection cites is the phenomenon the theory predicts and names.

The second charge, evasion, requires a more careful answer, because it identifies a genuine temptation within the tradition and honest adherents should concede the temptation exists. Any theory with a concept of contradiction can be abused by lazy defenders who relabel every difficulty a contradiction and every refutation a confirmation, and the history of Marxist apologetics contains passages that commit exactly that abuse. The defence against the charge is not indignation but the defeater discipline this article has practised from its early sections. The strict position on machinery was stated with its refutation condition attached: an adopter keeping a superior return forever while rivals remained free to copy the method would defeat it. The productivity check was stated as a test that the transformation narrative fails as matters stand and that the Marxist narrative must also pass, and the article recorded that triumphalist readings fail it too. The hidden-labour finding was stated with its limits, as a claim about where boundaries are drawn and how they are misdescribed, not as a proof that nothing is ever automated. A theory whose defenders specify, in advance and in public, the observations that would defeat its central claims is not immunising itself; it is doing the opposite, and the charge of evasion sticks only where the specification is absent. Readers encountering versions of the tradition that omit the defeaters are entitled to press the charge against those versions, and this article's verdict on its own doctrine is offered under the same discipline: the strict position stands while adopter advantages keep melting under diffusion, and falls if they ever stop.

Concede, finally, what the objection gets right, because the concession marks the theory's true frontier. No account within the tradition specifies, with anything approaching the precision of the value analysis itself, what happens if the drift toward the limit is carried far enough in practice: through what sequence of crises or reforms the value form would actually be superseded, what institutions would allocate effort and distribute product in its place, how the immense apparatus of production would be governed in the transition, and what would prevent new scarcities and new hierarchies from crystallising inside the successor arrangements. The notebooks gesture; the published work analyses the existing form exhaustively and its successor hardly at all; later writers divide among incompatible completions. A reader who finishes the machinery chapter persuaded of its diagnosis may still reasonably judge the tradition's account of the destination inadequate, and the tradition's strongest modern representatives have generally accepted that judgement as fair. The objection therefore succeeds as a boundary marker even where it fails as a refutation: it locates the point beyond which the theory's admirers are extrapolating, and an article committed to honesty will plant a flag at that point rather than pretend the territory beyond it has been surveyed. The sections that remain are devoted to the surveyed territory: a table that orders the findings, a statement of what is unknown, and guidance for the students, teachers, and journalists who will use the argument.

## Scepticism as a position: the transformation asserted rather than demonstrated

Between the post-scarcity enthusiast and the task framework's careful accountants stands a third contemporary position that this article's evidence largely supports, the sceptical position, which holds that the transformation of production by new automation is asserted far more often than it is demonstrated, and that the assertion serves interests that a reader should identify before crediting it. Scepticism of that kind has a distinguished pedigree inside economics itself. Gordon's work, cited earlier, is its heavyweight statement for the productivity side, concluding from the historical record that the innovations of the digital era, judged by their measurable effects on living standards and output per hour, rank below the great inventions of the earlier industrial waves. To Gordon's statistical scepticism the ghost-work research adds an ethnographic scepticism: where the statistician finds no acceleration, the workplace investigator finds the concealed effort whose existence explains the missing acceleration, and the two scepticisms corroborate each other from independent directions. A third strand, associated with historians of technology, notes the promotional cycle by which each generation of equipment is announced as revolutionary by those selling it, adopted unevenly, absorbed into ordinary practice, and quietly reclassified as infrastructure, while the announcement apparatus moves on to the next revolution with its vocabulary intact. The sceptical position does not deny that production changes, sometimes profoundly. It denies that the change under discussion has been shown to be what its announcers claim, and it places the burden of proof where the productivity check places it, on the aggregate.

The framework absorbs the sceptical position without strain, and indeed improves it by supplying the explanation scepticism alone lacks. Pure scepticism can establish that the revolution is not visible in the aggregate and that its showcase examples conceal effort, yet it struggles to explain why the announcements are so persistent, so well funded, and so uniformly structured if nothing is happening. The value framework answers: something is happening, continuously, namely the ordinary competitive mechanisation that has proceeded for two centuries, with its genuine cheapening, displacement, intensification, and concealment, and the announcements are that ordinary process dressed in revolutionary costume for identifiable reasons. Revolutionary costume raises investment, justifies valuations that ordinary improvement would not support, disciplines workforces who believe their replacements are ready, and flatters purchasers with participation in an epoch. Ordinary process, honestly described, does none of that work. The costume is therefore not an accidental exaggeration layered over the process; it is one of the process's instruments, and the framework, which has always treated the stories an economy tells about itself as part of its machinery, reads the announcements as data about the interests of the announcers. A reader trained by that reading approaches each new proclamation with the check in hand and the question ready: show the aggregate, show the hours, show who works at the edges. Proclamations that survive the questions earn belief. The rest are costume.

### Which questions does this debate leave genuinely open?

whether the productivity plateau breaks, and in which direction the balance between displacement and new-task creation settles across coming decades; how much concealed effort the scaffolding of apparently autonomous arrangements will retain as they mature; and through what institutions a society would pass if the tendency toward the limit ever approached it in earnest.

That catalogue of unknowns is not a confession of failure but the article's final act of discipline, and it applies symmetrically. The tradition does not know the trajectory, and it should stop allowing its popularisers to imply otherwise. The enthusiasts do not know the trajectory either, and their confidence is the less excusable because the aggregate evidence presently available points against the confidence. What can be known, and what this article has tried to supply, is the structure within which any trajectory will unfold: value created by living effort and transferred by apparatus, advantages melting under competition, composition rising, reserves forming, effort concealing itself at the edges of celebrated arrangements, scarcity manufactured where abundance threatens prices, and a measure of wealth, labour time, that the system's own development keeps straining. A reader who commands that structure can evaluate each new announcement, each new study, each new workplace arrangement as it arrives, without needing to be told in advance what the devices involved will be. The structure is the durable possession. The announcements are weather.

## The geography of effort: relocation as the other automation

Any map of mechanisation drawn only within one country's borders will misread the process, because the displacement of effort operates across space as powerfully as it operates across time. When production in a wealthy economy appears to shed labour dramatically, the framework instructs the reader to ask where the effort went before concluding that it vanished, and the answer, across industry after industry, is that much of it moved: to poorer regions within the same country in earlier phases, and across borders in the mature phase of global supply chains, following the gradient of wages downward as reliably as water follows a gradient of height. The mechanism is the same one operating inside a single labour market. Owners seek the cheapest adequate supply of the capacity to work, competition punishes those who pay more than the social conditions require, and the social conditions are set globally once transport and communication make distant workforces accessible to the same production process. A factory that closes in a high-wage region and reopens in a low-wage one has not automated the effort away; it has arbitraged the price of the effort, and the goods returning through the supply chain embody foreign hours that the importing country's statistics record only as cheap products. National productivity figures, read without that correction, flatter the importing economy twice over: its own output per hour rises because the labour-intensive stages have left its denominator, while the cheapened imports raise its real consumption without any corresponding domestic achievement. The productivity check, to be conducted honestly at the level of the world economy, must therefore be specified globally as well as nationally, and the article's earlier comparison was framed for the advanced economies as a group partly to blunt that distortion, since the group as a whole trades mainly with itself. Even so, the reader should hold the correction in mind: some portion of every wealthy economy's apparent liberation from effort is an accounting shadow cast by effort performed elsewhere at lower prices.

The concealed effort of the ghost-work literature is the same geography operating at a finer grain. Where the older relocation moved whole factories, the newer one moves fragments of tasks, and the fragments can travel farther and faster than factories ever could because nothing physical needs to be shipped: the effort is summoned through a connection, expended on material that arrives as data, and returned as a result, with the worker's location entering the transaction only through the price. Fragmentation and relocation compound each other. A task small enough to be detached from any whole job is also small enough to be priced in a global spot market, and a worker bidding for fragments against the whole world's reserve has a bargaining position weaker than any factory hand confronting a single local employer. The framework's categories travel well here precisely because they were built from the wage relation rather than from any technology: wherever the capacity to work is bought by the hour or by the piece, from a seller who must sell to live, in competition with a reserve of other sellers, the same tendencies assert themselves, the same pressure on price, the same concealment of the relation inside the finished product. The product on the wealthy consumer's screen and the fragment worker's dawn hours in a distant time zone are two ends of one relation, and the commodity form performs its old trick of presenting the relation as a property of the thing.

### Why does apparent liberation from effort in one place so often mean relocation?

because the pressure driving mechanisation is the pressure to cheapen production, and relocating effort to a cheaper supply cheapens it as surely as replacing it with apparatus. Owners pursue both routes under the same compulsion, and national statistics, which count the effort only where it is performed, register the relocation as a domestic productivity miracle.

Political conclusions drawn from the geography must respect its symmetry. Workers in the receiving regions are not the villains of the sending regions' losses; they are sellers of the same commodity under worse conditions, gaining employment that is real and often desperately sought while supplying the lever by which wages elsewhere are disciplined. The framework's internationalism, often treated as a sentimental appendage to its economics, is in fact an analytical consequence of the geography: a reserve that is global can only be bargained with globally, and organisations confined within one border face a purchaser who can always threaten to move the work beyond the border's reach. The history of labour organisation since the supply chains globalised is substantially the history of that asymmetry and of the attempts, partial and instructive, to overcome it through coordinated standards, through solidarity funds, through the slow construction of bargaining structures that match the geography of the purchaser. None of that history is simple, and the framework does not pretend otherwise. It insists only that the automation question and the geography question are one question, and that an analysis which treats a wealthy economy's labour shedding as a technological event, without following the hours across the map, has mistaken a relocation for a disappearance and will draw every subsequent conclusion wrong.

There is a further, quieter consequence of the geography for the value argument itself. The social average that governs prices, the socially necessary labour time against which adopters gain and laggards lose, is increasingly a world average for traded goods, formed across workforces whose wages differ by large multiples. Value magnitudes in the importing economy therefore come to embody a global mixture of hours priced at radically different rates, and the transfer of value from low-wage to high-wage regions through exchange, the phenomenon the dependency tradition called unequal exchange, operates inside the automation story as well: the cheapened goods that raise real consumption in wealthy economies carry hours that were purchased far below the wealthy economy's own standard. A complete Marxist account of mechanisation must hold the national and global frames together, since the same device, the same method, the same announcement of effortlessness can signify displacement at home, intensification abroad, and concealment in the seam between them. The reader who learns to ask, of every celebrated efficiency, the three geographical questions, where did the hours go, at what price are they now bought, and who was made invisible in the move, possesses the framework in its working form.

## Services, care and the activities that resist the productivity race

The composition of employment in the advanced economies has shifted massively toward services, and any account of automation that ignores that shift will misjudge both the productivity plateau and the future of work. William Baumol, with William Bowen, identified the governing mechanism in Performing Arts: The Economic Dilemma, published in 1966, and generalised it in later work: activities divide between those in which output per hour can rise indefinitely through mechanisation and those in which the human presence is itself the product, so that raising output per hour means destroying the thing produced. A string quartet cannot be played faster without ceasing to be the work it is; a nurse's attention, a teacher's presence with a struggling pupil, a carer's unhurried competence with a frightened patient, all degrade precisely in proportion as they are sped up or thinned out. Baumol's cost disease follows arithmetically. Wages across an economy tend to rise together, pulled up by the productivity gains of the mechanisable sectors, so the labour-intensive sectors face relentlessly rising relative costs while their own productivity stagnates by design. They absorb a growing share of employment and of expenditure, and the aggregate productivity of the whole economy is dragged toward their pace. The advanced economies' employment structure, dominated by health, education, care, hospitality, and personal services, is Baumol's mechanism made visible, and the aggregate productivity plateau examined earlier is in significant part its statistical signature.

The framework and Baumol's mechanism fit together with unusual neatness, each supplying what the other lacks. Baumol explains where the displaced go: into the activities that resist mechanisation, where they swell employment while lowering the aggregate growth of output per hour, reconciling the productivity check with the undoubted fact of continuing mechanisation at the frontier. The framework explains the social character of the destination sectors: why so much of the absorbing employment is badly paid despite its social necessity, why its workers' bargaining position is weak, why public provision and private provision generate such different experiences of the same activities. Care and education sit awkwardly inside the value form because their product is a change in a person, difficult to standardise, impossible to inventory, and resistant to the social averaging that commodity production requires. Where they are organised as commodities, the pressure to cheapen expresses itself as intensification, larger classes, shorter appointments, thinner staffing, the denser hour applied to activities whose quality is their duration. Where they are organised as public services, the pressure expresses itself as fiscal crisis, since Baumol's rising relative costs meet a tax base whose politics resist the increase, and the resulting squeeze is then misdescribed as public inefficiency. Either way, the activities that absorb the workforce become the terrain on which the moving contradiction is lived by the largest numbers: socially indispensable effort, expanding without limit, inside an accounting system that registers it as cost.

### Why do the jobs that multiply tend to be the ones machines cannot cheapen?

because employment grows where productivity cannot, by the arithmetic Baumol identified: mechanisable activities shed workers as their output per hour rises, while activities whose product is human presence must add workers to add output, and an economy's job structure migrates steadily toward the second kind however its frontier performs.

For the automation debate, the service analysis carries a deflating implication and a humane one. The deflating implication is that the end of work is arithmetically implausible on any near horizon even if the frontier mechanises brilliantly, because the economy's centre of gravity sits in activities where the frontier's methods do not apply, and those activities' demand grows with income, with age, and with the very displacement that releases workers into them. The humane implication is that the quality of working life for most people will be decided in the resistant sectors, by struggles over staffing, pace, pay, and professional autonomy, rather than at the mechanised frontier that monopolises the headlines. A politics of automation worthy of the name would spend its energy there, on the terms under which care is given and teaching happens, instead of rehearsing prophecies about the frontier. The framework, which has insisted throughout that outcomes are mediated by organisation rather than dictated by apparatus, finds in the service economy its largest confirmation: the decisive questions about work in the advanced economies are old questions of power and provision, wearing new sectoral clothes, and they will be answered by the same means that answered them before, organisation, legislation, and the slow renegotiation of what a society counts as necessary.

## Absorption, new branches and the historical rhythm of mechanisation

The historical record of mechanisation, read whole rather than in episodes, displays a rhythm that neither the apocalyptic nor the reassuring narrative captures, and the framework's account of absorption exists to describe that rhythm. A new method captures a branch, expels a cohort, and cheapens the branch's product. Cheapening expands the market for the product, sometimes enormously, and expansion reabsorbs part of the expelled cohort into the enlarged branch itself. Simultaneously, the income freed by cheapening, and the new needs the cheapened product creates, call fresh branches into existence: the apparatus must be built, maintained, supplied, and eventually replaced; the product must be distributed, explained, repaired, and complemented; entirely new wants crystallise around the new capabilities, and each want is a potential employment. Marx catalogued the mechanism in the machinery chapter itself, noting how the factory system multiplied employments around its core even as it expelled the hand workers at its centre, and the two centuries since have repeated the pattern at larger scales. The weavers expelled by the power loom were not reabsorbed as weavers, and their suffering was not a transitional fiction; yet the textile economy that destroyed their trade employed, within a generation, more people in aggregate than the trade it destroyed, in mills, warehouses, transport, retail, and the machine shops that built the looms. Absorption is real, and so is the expulsion, and the framework's refusal to choose between those truths is what distinguishes it from both pamphlet traditions.

The rhythm has a temporal structure that matters for judging present claims. Expulsion is fast, concentrated, and visible: a method arrives, a cohort is released within years, a district or an occupation bears the whole cost in one blow. Absorption is slow, dispersed, and nearly invisible: the new employments accumulate across decades, in places and occupations nobody connects with the original displacement, held by people who never know they are the compensation for a loss suffered elsewhere by strangers. The asymmetry of visibility corrupts public judgement systematically. Displacement has faces, towns, and dates; absorption has statistics. A debate fed on visibility will therefore always overstate the net destruction and understate the reconstitution, while a debate fed on aggregate employment totals will commit the opposite error, erasing the concentrated losses inside the reassuring sum. The framework's discipline is to hold both ledgers open at once and to ask the distributional questions the totals suppress: who bore the expulsion, who received the absorption, how long was the interval, and what happened to wages and security across the transfer. Judged by those questions, the historical rhythm is neither a tragedy of progress nor a triumph of adaptation. It is a recurring transfer of costs onto the displaced and of gains toward owners and consumers, mediated, when the displaced are organised, by compensation and shortened hours, and unmediated, when they are not, by destitution.

### What ordering principle governs the article's central table?

by separating what the theory claims from what the evidence shows and from what remains open, row by row: the contribution of machines, the path of profitability, the fate of employment, and the implication of the fully mechanised limit. Arguments that keep the three columns distinct can be graded; arguments that blur them can only be applauded or booed.

That table, the findable artifact of this article, appears in full in a later section, and its discipline is worth stating here because it governs the article's remaining judgements. Every popular automation claim scrambles the columns: a theoretical possibility is reported as an observed fact, an observed fact in one enclave is projected as a universal outcome, an open question is settled by tone of voice. The table's rows force the scrambling into the open. Take any headline claim and assign its parts to cells, and the claim's actual epistemic weight becomes visible at once, usually to its embarrassment. The exercise is the article in miniature, and readers pressed for time can perform it on the next announcement they encounter and obtain most of the benefit of the longer argument.

A word on cycles completes the historical picture. Mechanisation does not proceed at an even pace but in waves, as clusters of methods mature together, credit expands to fund their installation, and a generation of fixed investment commits the economy to their logic. The waves crest in overinvestment, since every owner installs capacity against the same expected market, and break in crises that scrap the weakest plant, devalue the sunk apparatus, and cheapen the elements from which the next wave builds. The framework's crisis theory, developed elsewhere in this series, treats those breaks as the system's method of restoring the conditions for accumulation at a higher composition, with the reserve swelling in the trough and shrinking, though never vanishing, in the boom. Automation announcements cluster predictably near the crests, when investment is flush and the future feels installable, and scepticism about them is therefore partly a matter of knowing where in the rhythm one stands. None of that rhythm requires prophecy. It requires only the memory that the present announcement has ancestors, that the ancestors sounded identical, and that what survived of them was the mechanism, not the timetable.

## Fixed capital, turnover and the crisis rhythm of apparatus

The long life of machinery imposes a rhythm on the whole economy that the second volume of Capital analysed under the heading of turnover, and the analysis repays revival in the automation debate because it explains the peculiar violence of investment cycles in mechanised production. A circulating input, raw material or fuel, is consumed and replaced within each production cycle, and its value returns to the owner with the sale of each batch of output. Fixed apparatus behaves otherwise. Its value is advanced in a lump at installation and returns in driblets across years of output, while the apparatus itself remains locked in a particular technical form, embodying the methods of its installation year with decreasing adequacy as the frontier moves. The owner of fixed capital is therefore a hostage of the future in a way the owner of circulating capital is not: demand must persist, methods must not leap too far, credit must remain available for the replacements that wear and obsolescence schedule regardless of market conditions. Marx treated the replacement cycle of fixed capital as one material basis for the periodicity of industrial crises, since investments bunch when a generation of apparatus wears out together or a new method makes replacement urgent, and the bunching synchronises the boom and the subsequent glut across whole economies. Later economists rediscovered versions of the mechanism under other names, in the accelerator principle and in theories of investment clustering, usually without the value framework that gives Marx's version its bite: the crisis is not merely a miscalculation of quantities but a devaluation of embodied past effort, a social verdict that hours expended building the scrapped apparatus will not, after all, be honoured at their booked worth.

Devaluation is the concept the automation debate needs from this quarter and rarely imports. When a superior method strands an installed base, the loss is not confined to the owners' balance sheets, though it appears there first. The embodied effort in the stranded apparatus, the mining, smelting, machining, and assembling performed by real workers in real years, is socially written off, treated as though it had never been necessary, and the workers whose skills were specific to the stranded methods suffer a parallel personal devaluation, their accumulated training repriced toward zero by a verdict they had no part in forming. Capitalism performs these write-offs continuously and calls the process creative destruction in its celebratory moods. The framework does not deny the creativity; the new methods are genuinely superior, and clinging to the stranded apparatus would waste more. It insists on counting the destruction honestly and on asking who is made to bear it, since the distribution of the write-off is a social choice disguised as a technical necessity. Owners diversify, insure, and pass losses to creditors and shareholders; workers concentrated in a method, a firm, or a town cannot diversify their one asset, the capacity to work in a particular practised form, and the asymmetry between the diversifiable asset and the undiversifiable one is the human core of every mechanisation wave.

### Why does long-lived apparatus synchronise booms and crises?

because replacement needs bunch: a generation of equipment installed together wears out and falls obsolete together, so investment surges in waves as owners replace in clusters, capacity overshoots the market the wave was built to serve, and the glut forces the devaluation that clears the ground for the next generation.

The turnover analysis also disciplines a familiar exaggeration in the announcements literature, the suggestion that new apparatus can be adopted costlessly and continuously, as though an economy were a device whose parts upgrade themselves in place. Real apparatus is embedded in buildings, supply contracts, trained habits, regulatory approvals, and debt schedules, and its replacement is correspondingly lumpy, expensive, and resisted by everyone whose position the existing installation protects. The friction is not a failure of the system's rationality; it is the system's substance, the weight of past decisions in embodied form. Forecasts that ignore the friction, extrapolating a demonstration's performance across an economy as though installation were instantaneous and free, repeat the error of every previous wave's prospectuses. The framework's older vocabulary, fixed capital, turnover, devaluation, moral depreciation, was built from watching that friction operate, and a reader equipped with it will ask of any announced transformation the unglamorous questions that decide real outcomes: what must be scrapped, who holds the debt on it, how long does the installed base live, and what happens to the people whose skills are part of the installation. Revolutions that cannot answer those questions have not yet begun, whatever their demonstrations show.

Use the table as its makers intend, as a grading instrument rather than a summary to be memorised. An announcement that a new arrangement will abolish a category of employment enters the third row, where it must produce its evidence on task creation's pace. A claim that a new method will enrich its adopters permanently enters the first row, where the defeater awaits it. A forecast of imminent abundance enters the fourth row and is asked for its institutions. A promise of effortless operation enters the third row's evidence cell, beside the concealed effort already documented there, and is asked where its own edges are staffed. The instrument is deliberately unglamorous. It converts wonder and dread, the two emotions the announcements industry manufactures, into bookkeeping, and bookkeeping, patiently kept, is how the framework has always preferred to fight.

### How should a reader use the table against a new announcement?

by assigning each element of the announcement to a row and a column before reacting to it: theory, evidence, or open question. Announcements collapse under the assignment with instructive regularity, since their force usually depends on presenting an open question as settled evidence or a theoretical possibility as an observed fact.

## Ownership, hours and the political mediation of machinery

Every mechanism traced so far operates inside a distribution of ownership, and the framework's political conclusions follow from noticing that the same apparatus yields different societies depending on who owns it and who therefore appropriates the productivity dividend. Under private ownership with a wage system, the dividend arrives as surplus to owners, as cheapened goods to consumers, and as displacement and intensification to workers, with the proportions among those three settled by organisation and law. Imagine, as a pure thought experiment, the identical apparatus under common ownership with distribution detached from employment: the dividend would arrive as reduced necessary effort for everyone, the displacement problem would dissolve into a leisure dividend, and the moving contradiction would lose its sting because the measure of distribution would no longer be the labour time the apparatus economises. The experiment is not a programme, and the tradition's internal arguments about how any such arrangement could be reached and governed are, as the objection section conceded, unresolved. The experiment's purpose is diagnostic. It isolates the variable that decides what mechanisation means socially, and the variable is not in the machine. Two societies with identical equipment and opposite settlements on ownership and hours will experience the equipment as opposite historical forces, one as a threat hanging over every livelihood, the other as a collective inheritance reducing everyone's necessary toil. Popular debate, which treats the apparatus as the protagonist of the story, cannot even state that difference, which is why the framework insists on its unfashionable priority: relations first, devices second, always.

The historical lever through which the dividend has actually been shared, whenever it has been shared, is the length of the working day and week, and the automation debate's silence about hours is one of its strangest features. Productivity growth makes a shorter standard week arithmetically possible at constant living standards; whether the possibility is taken as leisure or as expanded output and employment churn is a social decision, taken repeatedly across two centuries, mostly in favour of output, with the great reductions in hours arriving as conquests of organised labour rather than as automatic dividends of the machine. The nineteenth-century factory acts, the eight-hour movement, the weekend, paid holidays: each converted a slice of the productivity dividend into free time against the resistance of owners who preferred the slice as surplus, and each conversion demonstrated that the machine's gift is delivered only to those organised to receive it. A contemporary debate that asks endlessly whether work will end, while never asking whether the standard week should shorten by an hour as productivity permits, has displaced a decidable political question with an undecidable speculative one, and the displacement serves everyone who prefers the dividend unshared. The framework restores the decidable question to the centre. Whatever the apparatus does, the hours settlement decides what it does to people's lives, and the settlement is made in legislatures and bargaining rooms, not in laboratories.

### Why does the same equipment threaten one society and liberate another?

because the equipment determines only the size of the productivity dividend, while ownership and the hours settlement determine its distribution: as surplus and displacement where livelihood depends on selling effort by the hour, or as shared free time where distribution has been detached from the sale and the week shortens as productivity permits.

There is a corollary for the reserve army that completes the political picture. A society that treats the reserve as a disciplinary instrument will experience mechanisation as a ratchet of insecurity, each wave enlarging the pool whose existence cheapens everyone's terms. A society that instead charges the reserve to the collective product, through income maintenance detached from immediate employment, through public provision of the absorbing services, through hours reductions that spread the available work, converts the same pool into a sabbatical fund, a training reserve, a care reserve. The technologies invoked in the two societies can be identical to the last bolt. The difference is institutional all the way down, and it is therefore contestable all the way down, which is the most hopeful proposition the framework contains and the one its critics, fixed on the reductio, seldom notice it contains. Marxism's automation teaching, stripped of costume, is that machinery under capitalism manufactures insecurity as reliably as it manufactures goods, that the insecurity is a property of the relations and not of the machinery, and that the proof of the distinction is available to any society willing to run the experiment of changing the relations while keeping the machines. Societies have run partial versions of the experiment whenever they have shortened hours or socialised provision, and the partial results, more leisure without collapse, more security without stagnation, stand in the record for anyone who prefers evidence to incantation.

## Measuring value when the yardstick itself is moving

A methodological section belongs in any honest treatment, because the theory's central magnitude, socially necessary labour time, is not a quantity anyone can read off a stopwatch, and mechanisation complicates its determination in ways both defenders and critics exploit. The social necessity of the effort embodied in a commodity is set by the average conditions of its production: the prevailing equipment, the average skill, the normal intensity. A producer working with superior methods expends less time per unit than the social average, and the market credits the unit with the average anyway, which is the precise mechanism of surplus profit examined earlier. A producer working with inferior methods expends more, and the market refuses the excess, crediting only the average and leaving the laggard's extra hours socially unrecognised, wasted effort from the standpoint of value however real the exertion was. The yardstick is therefore an outcome of the production process itself, continuously recalculated as methods diffuse, and the theory embraces that reflexivity rather than apologising for it: value is a social relation, and social relations are exactly the kind of thing that the participants' own actions continuously remake. Critics who demand that the magnitude be measurable independently of the market process are demanding that a social relation behave like a physical constant, and the demand, however scientific it sounds, mistakes the category of the thing being measured.

Mechanisation stresses the yardstick at two points that repay attention. The first is the new product problem. When an arrangement produces something genuinely without precedent, no social average exists against which its embodied effort can be reckoned, and its early prices are set by monopoly, guesswork, and the incomes of eager purchasers rather than by embodied time. The theory treats such episodes as the yardstick in formation, with the average crystallising as production routinises and rivals enter, a process the historical record of new industries follows with fidelity: extravagant early margins, entry, standardisation, margin collapse, and eventual treatment of the product as a staple whose price hugs its costs. The second stress is the skill problem. Mechanisation decomposes skilled crafts into simple operations while creating new skilled strata elsewhere, and the reduction of skilled to simple labour, the conversion rate by which an hour of the engineer's time counts against hours of attendant time, is itself socially determined through training systems, credential markets, and bargaining. The theory has always known that the conversion is a social fact rather than a natural one; the machinery chapter's account of skill draining from the operative into the system is simultaneously an account of the conversion rate being forcibly rewritten. Neither stress refutes the yardstick. Both specify the work an analyst must do before wielding it, which is the work this article has modelled: establish the average conditions, locate the diffusion stage, identify whose hours count at what rate, and only then pronounce on value.

### How is socially necessary labour time set under mechanisation?

by the average conditions of production prevailing as methods diffuse: the equipment in general use, the ordinary skill, the normal intensity. Producers beating the average capture the difference as surplus profit; producers trailing it lose the excess hours they expend, which the market declines to recognise as value however real the effort was.

The measurement discussion has a practical payoff for the debate's conduct. Many popular arguments fail not on their facts but on their unstated averages, comparing a frontier demonstration against a handicraft baseline, or a single plant's performance against an economy's totals, or a gross displacement figure against a net employment claim. The framework's habit of specifying the comparison, which economies, which decades, which measure, was exercised in the productivity section and applies universally: every magnitude in this debate is an average over something, and the something must be named before the magnitude can be argued with. Analysts who name their averages can be wrong informatively. Analysts who hide them can only be loud.

## Deskilling, reskilling and the polarisation of the workforce

The fate of skill under mechanisation is usually told as a simple decline, and the framework's own Braverman inheritance encourages the simple version, yet the fuller record supports a more articulated account that the tradition's careful writers have developed in dialogue with the evidence. Mechanisation deskills the tasks it captures, by design, since capturing a task means extracting its knowledge content into the arrangement and leaving the human residue simpler than the craft it replaced. Simultaneously, it creates skilled work at its boundaries: the design, construction, programming in the generic sense of configuration, maintenance, and supervision of the arrangements themselves, together with the managerial and analytical strata that coordinate production at the new scale. And it swells, through the Baumol mechanism, the great middle territory of interpersonal services whose skills are real but uncertified, the competence of care, of instruction, of hospitality, systematically undervalued because it resembles the unpaid labour of the household from which it was drawn. The aggregate picture is therefore not decline but polarisation and revaluation: conception concentrating upward, execution narrowing at the mechanised core, and a vast service stratum whose genuine skills the credential market refuses to price. Wage patterns follow the revaluation more faithfully than they follow any measure of actual human capability, a fact the framework predicts, since wages price the capacity to work as a commodity under specific social arrangements rather than rewarding capability in the abstract.

The polarisation account corrects two errors at once. Against the simple deskilling story, it acknowledges the new skilled strata and their real expertise, refusing the romanticism that treats all pre-mechanised work as craft and all mechanised work as degradation; much hand labour was brutish, and its abolition is not mourned by those who performed it. Against the reskilling story beloved of policy documents, in which the workforce glides upward into the new skilled strata through training programmes, it observes the arithmetic: the skilled boundary strata are small relative to the execution and service masses, their entry is rationed by credentials and by the cost of the training years, and a programme that retrains a fraction of the displaced for a fraction of the boundary positions has not solved the distribution problem but renamed it. The honest question is never whether new skills are created, since they plainly are, but in what quantities, at whose expense, with what barriers, and at what wages relative to the positions destroyed. Asked in that form, the question has empirical answers that vary by episode, and the framework's contribution is to keep the question in that form against the pressure of both lobbies, the one selling decline and the one selling uplift, each of which prefers the aggregate slogan to the distributional ledger.

### Does mechanisation destroy skill or move it?

it moves skill, extracting knowledge from the captured tasks into the design of the arrangement, concentrating conception in a smaller stratum, narrowing execution at the core, and swelling the uncertified skills of the service sectors. The wage consequences follow the social pricing of the relocated skills rather than their real difficulty or social usefulness.

Education enters the argument here, in a role the debate assigns it too innocently. If the boundary strata are rationed by credentials, then the education system becomes the gate through which the displaced and their children must pass to reach the protected positions, and the gate's width, cost, and fairness become central automation questions. The framework's analysis of education, developed in the article on schooling within this series, treats the gate as a sorting mechanism whose formal openness coexists with systematic advantage for families already holding cultural and material capital, so that mechanisation's promise, that anyone may retrain for the new positions, is redeemed for a minority while legitimating the majority's exclusion as a failure of individual effort. That legitimation is itself one of mechanisation's social products and among its most durable: a workforce persuaded that its position reflects its skills has been relieved of the suspicion that its position reflects its bargaining power. The tradition's insistence on asking who pays for the training years, who can afford the credential, and whose existing knowledge the credential system refuses to count, is the automation debate's missing chapter, and it requires no speculation about any device to write, only attention to the gate.

## Ricardo's recantation, the machine breakers and Keynes's grandchildren

The automation debate did not begin with its present vocabulary, and recovering its earlier rounds is the fastest way to see that the arguments, the evidence, and even the rhetorical costumes have recurred for two centuries with only the apparatus changing. The machine breakers of the English textile districts, remembered under the name of Luddites, were long caricatured as enemies of progress smashing what they could not understand. The historical scholarship, Eric Hobsbawm's essay on the machine breakers prominent among it, recovered a different picture: skilled workers destroying selectively the particular frames that embodied a threat to their customary terms, sparing others, and using destruction as collective bargaining by riot in a period when combination was illegal. The breakers were not wrong about what the frames would do to their livelihoods; the handloom weavers' subsequent immiseration is among the best documented declines of a workforce in economic history. They were wrong only about the remedy available to them, since the frames embodied a social reorganisation that local destruction could delay but not reverse. The framework reads the episode as the first modern demonstration of its central distinction: the workers' enemy was never the apparatus as a thing but the terms on which the apparatus was introduced, terms that assigned the whole productivity dividend to the owners and the whole transition cost to the displaced, and the tragedy of the episode is that the distinction was unavailable to its participants in any organised form.

David Ricardo supplied the debate's second founding moment, and its most honourable one, when he used the third edition of his Principles of Political Economy and Taxation, in 1821, to add a chapter on machinery reversing the opinion he had published in the earlier editions. Ricardo had previously held that mechanisation, by cheapening goods, benefited all classes. The new chapter concluded that the labouring class's apprehension of machinery as injurious to its interests was not a prejudice but a conclusion consistent with correct principles, since the substitution of apparatus for labour could diminish the fund from which wages were drawn even while national income grew. The recantation matters beyond its content. The leading economist of the age examined his own earlier assurance, found the mechanism wanting, and printed the correction in his masterwork, at the height of his authority, in full view of the class his correction discomforted. Marx, who disagreed with Ricardo on the theory of value's foundations, honoured the recantation as scientific integrity of a rare order, and the episode stands in this article as the model of what the defeater discipline looks like when practised by a master: a stated position, a mechanism re-examined, a public reversal. Debates in which no participant ever recants are not debates but marketing campaigns, and the reader can grade the present automation literature by Ricardo's standard simply by asking when any of its prominent voices last printed a correction.

John Maynard Keynes gave the debate its third founding text in Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren, published in 1930, an essay that named technological unemployment as a phase of maladjustment while prophesying that within a century the economic problem itself, the struggle for subsistence, could be solved, leaving humanity to confront the permanent problem of how to use its freedom. Keynes's prophecy is the post-scarcity position in an earlier and more literary dress, and its subsequent history is the most instructive evidence the debate possesses. The productivity premise of the prophecy performed roughly as Keynes hoped across the advanced economies for the century's middle decades, yet the leisure conclusion did not follow: hours fell substantially but nothing like proportionally, consumption expanded to absorb the surplus, new employments arose in the resistant sectors, and the distribution of the dividend was settled, exactly as the framework would predict, by organisation and legislation rather than by the arithmetic of plenty. Keynes himself half-foresaw the catch, remarking that humanity's needs fall into absolute needs, which satiate, and relative needs, the desire for superiority over one's fellows, which may be insatiable. The framework translates that remark into its own vocabulary of the historically expanding wage basket and manufactured standing, and claims Keynes, on this point, as an unwitting ally. The prophecy's failure mode, plenty produced and leisure withheld, is the moving contradiction experienced as biography by the grandchildren to whom the essay was addressed.

### What did Ricardo's machinery chapter change?

the founding assurance of classical economics that mechanisation benefits every class automatically. Ricardo concluded in print, reversing his earlier editions, that substituting apparatus for labour can injure the labouring class's interests even while national income rises, because the fund supporting employment can shrink as output grows, a correction Marx honoured as a model of scientific honesty.

Set the three moments in a row and the debate's deep structure becomes visible. The breakers establish that displacement costs are real and concentrated. Ricardo establishes that aggregate gain and class loss are compatible, so that citing the aggregate settles nothing about the distribution. Keynes establishes that even fulfilled productivity prophecies do not deliver their social conclusions automatically, because needs, employments, and distributions are socially mediated. The framework assembled in this article is, in one sense, nothing but the systematic union of those three establishments, plus the value theory that explains why the mediation takes the forms it does. A reader who meets the next century's automation announcements with the breakers, Ricardo, and Keynes in mind will find that the announcements contain nothing the three have not already taught the reader to question, and that is the deepest sense in which the article's structural method justifies itself: the questions outlast the apparatus because the questions were formed before the apparatus, in confrontation with its ancestors.

## The household test: mechanisation where no wage is paid

One laboratory has run the automation experiment for a century under conditions that strip away most confounding variables, and its results deserve a central place in any honest treatment: the household. Ruth Schwartz Cowan examined that laboratory in More Work for Mother, published in 1983, documenting how the mechanisation of domestic production across the twentieth century, the arrival of powered and plumbed assistance for washing, cooking, cleaning, and heating, transformed household work without reducing the hours devoted to it by the person principally performing it. The mechanisms Cowan identified generalise beautifully. Standards rose with capacity: cleanliness expected of a home, variety expected of meals, freshness expected of clothing all inflated to absorb the productivity the new apparatus offered, so the dividend was taken as quality rather than as time. Tasks previously delegated returned to the householder as the servants and commercial services of an earlier arrangement disappeared, their work reabsorbed into the mechanised home and onto the shoulders of its manager. And the ideology of the home intensified in step, assigning the householder moral responsibility for outcomes that the new standards defined. Hours persisted. The work changed its character, became less brutish and more managerial, and did not end.

The household is the automation debate in miniature, purified of the wage relation, and every camp should find its reflection uncomfortable. The enthusiast sees the prophecy's failure mode in its clearest form: capacity multiplied, hours unmoved, because the society surrounding the apparatus converted the dividend into expanded expectations rather than into free time, exactly as the expanding wage basket converts industrial productivity into new necessities. The catastrophist sees displacement failing to appear even where displacement should be easiest, since no employer stood between the householder and the full benefit of the apparatus, yet the work reconstituted itself around the new standards and the vanished servants. The framework sees its own mechanisms operating without any capitalist in sight, which refines rather than embarrasses it: the expansion of socially defined necessity, the reabsorption of delegated tasks, and the conversion of dividends into quality rather than leisure are social processes that the wage relation amplifies and organises but does not invent. What the wage relation adds, and what the household laboratory therefore cannot show, is the distributional struggle: in the household, the person performing the work also decided, within the era's constraints, how the dividend would be taken. In the factory and the office, that decision belongs to the parties' relative power, which is why the framework keeps returning the debate to organisation. Cowan's householder, choosing standards over leisure by a mixture of pride, pressure, and love, is the benign case of the conversion. The wage worker, whose dividend is converted by others, is the case the politics exists for.

### What does the mechanisation of housework show about automation generally?

that multiplied capacity does not by itself reduce hours, because standards expand to absorb the dividend, delegated tasks return to the remaining worker, and responsibility intensifies around the new expectations. Cowan's household evidence isolates the conversion of productivity into quality rather than time, operating even where no employer profits from the conversion.

There is a further lesson in the household record about visibility, which ties this section back to the concealed effort examined earlier. Domestic labour was the original ghost work: socially indispensable, economically uncounted, and rendered invisible by being assigned to a category of person whose effort was treated as a natural resource rather than as work. The statistical invisibility of the household in national accounts, its output unmeasured because unpriced, is the template for every later invisibility the article has examined, the fragment workers edited out of the service's story, the correctors behind the interface, the distant hours inside the cheap import. In each case the mechanism is the same: effort that is not priced at the point where it is consumed disappears from the accounting, and arrangements built on the disappeared effort can then be celebrated as effortless. The feminist economists who first insisted that the household's hours be counted were practising, before the phrase existed, the discipline this article calls the productivity check's other half: follow the hours, all of them, including the ones the accounts were designed not to see. A debate that learned that lesson in the household would not need to relearn it at every new interface, and the fact that it does relearn it, generation after generation, is evidence that the invisibility is functional for someone, which is the framework's oldest and most reliable clue.

## Energy, materials and the physical substrate of the argument

Value analysis floats above a physical world it does not describe, and a complete treatment should acknowledge the substrate, because mechanisation's material requirements impose limits and costs that neither the value form nor its critics can wish away. Apparatus is made of mined and refined substances, assembled with expenditure of energy, operated with continuing inputs of power and material, and discarded into waste streams at the end of its service. The cheapening that the value account celebrates is therefore always, in physical terms, a transformation rather than a creation: effort and energy organised through matter, with losses at every conversion and residues at every stage. Marx's own writings contain the seed of the physical reflection in the concept of metabolism, the material exchange between human society and the rest of nature that labour mediates, and in his attention, drawn from the agricultural chemistry of his day, to the depletion of soils by production organised for distant markets. Later writers in the tradition developed that seed into an ecological Marxism concerned with the rifts that accumulation opens in material cycles. The connection to automation is direct. A production system that economises on the priced input, labour time, while drawing without limit on the unpriced inputs, energy, materials, and the absorptive capacity of air and water, will predictably substitute toward the unpriced inputs, mechanising by burning and extracting at scales the value accounts register only as cheapness. The productivity statistics, which count the labour saved and not the throughput spent, then flatter the process in a second way beyond the geographical flattery examined earlier.

The framework's categories organise the physical facts without strain. Energy and materials enter the value account as constant capital, transferring their embodied worth, and their physical abundance or scarcity registers in value terms only through the effort required to obtain them. A depleting resource whose extraction demands rising effort raises the value of every product embodying it, a countertendency to cheapening that operates silently inside the composition story. Waste, by contrast, historically escaped the value account almost entirely, being discharged into commons that no owner had to pay to use, so the system's cheapening proceeded partly by exporting its residues outside the ledger, a concealment structurally identical to the concealment of effort at the periphery: whatever escapes pricing when decisions are made does not exist for the decision. Climate disruption is, in that reading, the largest ghost ledger in the economy's history, the accumulated unpriced residue of two centuries of mechanised cheapening arriving for payment at once. The tradition's ecological writers argue that the moving contradiction has a material twin: a system driven to minimise the input it prices will maximise its draw on the inputs it does not, until the unpriced substrates themselves become the binding constraint. Whether that twin contradiction overtakes the original is an empirical question of the coming century, and the article records it as belonging to the open column, while noting that the structural method, follow what is priced and what is hidden, generated the question without any prompting from the physical sciences.

### How does the value framework treat energy and material costs?

as embodied effort transferred through production, registering in prices only insofar as obtaining them requires labour, while unpriced residues discharged into shared air and water escape the account entirely. The framework therefore predicts systematic overuse of whatever the price system leaves uncounted, effort's concealment repeated at the level of matter.

Restraint is required in the other direction as well. The physical substrate does not refute the value analysis, because the two accounts answer different questions: the value account explains the social form, the distribution, and the dynamics of a market economy, while the material account tracks the throughputs that any economy, market or otherwise, must organise. A planned economy would face the same thermodynamics and could waste the same substrates, as the environmental record of the twentieth century's planned economies attests with uncomfortable clarity. What the framework claims is narrower and stronger: that the price mechanism's specific blindnesses, to unpriced effort and unpriced residue alike, are systematic rather than accidental, generated by the same form that generates the displacement and concealment examined throughout, so that an adequate politics of machinery must be simultaneously a politics of accounting, deciding what will be counted, what will be priced, and what will be protected from pricing altogether. The automation question, pursued to its physical root, becomes a question about the ledger itself, which is where a tradition that began by interrogating the commodity form always expected it to end.

## The state, public provision and the mediation of the dividend

Between the apparatus and the society stands the state, and the framework's account of mechanisation is incomplete until the state's mediating roles are enumerated, because the historical record shows those roles deciding, at several junctures, whether a mechanisation wave arrived as catastrophe or as transition. The state sets the hours within which intensification may operate, through the factory legislation whose history Marx chronicled as a struggle between the classes conducted in legal form. It maintains the reserve, or relieves it, through poor relief in the earlier period and through insurance and income maintenance in the later, with the design of relief deciding whether the reserve disciplines wages by destitution or merely by availability. It absorbs labour directly through public employment, which in the advanced economies grew across the mechanising century into one of the largest destinations for displaced and new workers alike, concentrated in precisely the Baumol sectors, health, education, care, administration, where productivity growth is structurally slow. It shapes the direction of innovation through the tax treatment of equipment against employment, through research funding, and through procurement, tilting the task race whose outcome the mainstream framework treats as decisive. And it operates the education gate, deciding the width of the passage to the protected strata and the price of passing. A debate about automation that mentions the state only as a source of adjustment assistance has missed the main structure: the state is not the referee of the mechanisation process but one of its principal players, and its choices are among the mechanisms by which the productivity dividend is distributed or withheld.

The framework adds its characteristic caution about the state's neutrality. In the tradition's analysis, state action in a class society bears the imprint of the relations it administers, so the mediation is never a disinterested optimisation: hours laws arrive when organisation forces them, relief is designed to preserve the wage relation's incentives even at the cost of suffering, public employment expands under pressure and contracts under fiscal doctrines that treat the Baumol sectors as burdens. Yet the same analysis insists that the mediation is real, that outcomes differ enormously across states facing identical apparatus, and that the differences track the organisation and political weight of the people exposed to displacement. The comparative record of the advanced economies supports the insistence: similar methods, introduced into different settlements of hours, relief, provision, and bargaining rights, have produced distributions of security and leisure different enough to constitute different lived worlds. That comparative fact is the empirical foundation of the article's recurring claim that relations decide what devices mean. It is also the practical foundation for any reader's politics: the variables that decide the outcome are the variables that collective action can reach, which is not a consolation but a finding.

### What does the state decide in a mechanisation wave?

nearly everything that determines how the wave feels: the legal duration and pace of the working day, the design of relief for the displaced, the scale of public employment in the resistant sectors, the tax tilt between equipment and employment, and the width of the education gate to the protected occupations.

One further state function deserves naming, because the announcements literature systematically ignores it: the state is the insurer of the devaluation cycle. When a wave breaks and the stranded apparatus, the stranded skills, and the stranded debts threaten to cascade through the financial system, it is public authority that socialises the losses, guarantees the deposits, funds the unemployment, and underwrites the next wave's credit. The pattern privatises the surplus profit of the diffusion window and socialises the write-off at the trough, and the framework reads that pattern not as corruption of an otherwise neutral process but as the process's own logic at the level of the whole society: the class that appropriates the dividend commands, in the crunch, the institutions that allocate the loss. Readers who find that reading too strong are invited to test it against the allocation of losses in the crises they know best, asking in each case whose assets were made whole and whose hours, homes, and pensions absorbed the adjustment. The ledger, as ever, is the argument.

## Wage forms, piece rates and the disguises of the working day

The wage itself wears disguises that mechanisation exploits, and Marx's chapters on wages supply tools the automation debate needs whenever pay is reorganised around new arrangements. The time wage purchases the capacity to work by the hour and leaves the intensity question to supervision. The piece wage purchases output directly and converts the worker into their own supervisor, since earnings now depend on the pace the worker imposes on themselves, while the rate per piece can be trimmed as methods improve so that the faster pace yields the same daily earnings as before. Marx judged payment by the piece the wage form best fitted to the capitalist mode of production, because it makes the extraction of surplus appear as a natural property of the work rather than as a relation between classes, and because it sets workers competing against one another's pace with the rate as the prize. Every later reorganisation of pay around output metrics, quotas, ratings, and per-task bounties is a descendant of the piece form, and the fragment markets of the concealed economy are its purest descendants yet: payment by the classified item, the reviewed case, the completed fragment, with the rate adjustable downward as the global reserve bidding for fragments grows. The worker in such a market experiences the arrangement as freedom, choosing hours and tasks, while the value relation operates underneath with unusual nakedness, the capacity to work sold in slices so thin that the sale itself becomes invisible.

Understanding the wage forms clarifies several features of the mechanised landscape that purely technical accounts leave mysterious. It explains why productivity improvements so often coincide with stagnant earnings at the point of production: the rate is recalibrated to the new method, capturing the improvement for the purchaser, and the worker must run faster to stand still, the treadmill of the piece rate matching the treadmill of surplus profit at the level of the individual wage. It explains the peculiar intensity of fragment work, in which no supervisor is visible yet the pace is relentless, since the form has internalised the supervisor into the payment structure. And it explains the political difficulty of organising such workforces, since the form presents each worker's problem as a private negotiation with a rate rather than as a shared condition, atomising the sellers of the capacity to work at exactly the moment when their buyer's organisation is most integrated. The tradition's remedy has historically been to attack the form itself, through standard rates, through the conversion of piece work to time work under collective agreements, through minimum floors beneath which the rate cannot push earnings, and the automation debate's practical wing, where it exists, is engaged in the same attack under new names: floors for fragment pay, transparency for rates, portability of ratings, the right to organise across the intermediating layers. The continuity is the point. The forms change; the relation persists; the countermeasures rhyme.

### Why did Marx call the piece wage the form most adequate to capitalism?

because it disguises the wage relation as a simple purchase of output, makes workers discipline their own pace and compete against one another, and lets the purchaser capture productivity gains by trimming the rate as methods improve, so that faster work yields unchanged earnings while the surplus grows.

A related disguise operates at the level of the job title and the employment contract, and mechanisation waves reliably multiply it. Workers performing identical functions under identical direction find themselves reclassified as independent sellers of services, as partners, as entrepreneurs of themselves, with the reclassification stripping away the protections, contributions, and collective rights that attached to the status of employee while leaving the substance of subordination untouched. The framework treats the reclassification as a change in the wage form rather than in the relation, a legal costume whose function is to cheapen the capacity to work by removing the social charges built into its price across a century of legislation, and to dissolve the collectivity of the workforce into a cloud of individual contractors for bargaining purposes. Courts and legislatures in various jurisdictions have repeatedly been asked to look through the costume to the relation, with mixed results that track, as the state section predicted, the relative organisation of the parties. For the purposes of the automation debate, the lesson is that a rising share of apparently novel work arrangements are wage-form innovations rather than production innovations, and that their contribution to measured productivity is correspondingly suspect: output per employee rises when the employees are reclassified out of the count, just as it rises when their hours are relocated abroad or their effort is edited out of a service's story. The productivity check, applied with the wage forms in mind, asks of every celebrated gain whether the denominator was improved or merely redefined.

## A method for interrogating any automation claim

The article's analyses converge on a portable method, and stating it compactly serves readers who will meet claims this article cannot anticipate, attached to arrangements that do not yet exist and described in vocabularies not yet coined. The method consists of questions, ordered so that each depends on the answers before it, and its power lies in its indifference to the particular apparatus involved. Ask first what the claim predicts at the aggregate: which measure, output per hour, employment share, wage level, should move, in which economies, across what period, if the claim is true. Ask second where the hours went: whose effort, previously visible, has become invisible, relocated, fragmented, or repriced, and what the arrangement's edges look like when staffed by the people actually staffing them. Ask third who captures the difference: if a genuine saving exists, trace its distribution among owners as surplus, consumers as cheapness, and workers as leisure or security, and identify the institutions that performed the distribution. Ask fourth what the claim's defeater would be: what observation, specified in advance, would persuade its maker to withdraw it, and treat the absence of an answer as the claim's most informative feature. Ask fifth which row of the value question table the claim occupies, and whether its evidence column has been filled with observations or with other claims. A claim that survives all five questions with its substance intact deserves belief and probably deserves the reader's money, vote, or career. The method's experience, across two centuries of announcements, is that few claims are ever asked the second question, and almost none survive the fourth.

The method is not a debunking machine, and its proper use includes recognising the genuine article when it appears. Real transformations of production do occur; the mechanising of farming across the twentieth century really did release the overwhelming majority of the workforce from the land while multiplying output, and the framework's own categories describe that transformation without strain: massive displacement, absorption through new branches and the service sectors, a swelling then shrinking reserve, hours legislation converting part of the dividend into leisure, and a productivity surge that the aggregate statistics registered unmistakably, which is precisely how a genuine revolution announces itself to the productivity check. The contrast between that transformation's statistical signature and the present plateau is the single most instructive comparison the debate affords. Revolutions in production are not subtle at the aggregate level. They bend the great curves of employment share and output per hour within a generation, visibly, in every statistical office's publications, without needing publicists to explain that the revolution is real but shy. A reader who internalises that contrast possesses a calibrated instrument: the curves are the claim's examiner, and they do not accept costumes.

### How can a reader test an automation claim without knowing the technology?

by interrogating its aggregates, its hours, its distribution, and its defeater, none of which require technical knowledge: which measure should move if the claim holds, whose effort became invisible, who captured the saving, and what observation would make the claimant withdraw. Claims are graded by their answers, and most fail before the technical questions begin.

There remains the question of what to do with the method's results, and the article's answer is deliberately unglamorous. Believe the aggregates over the announcements. Follow the hours. Expect the dividend to be distributed by power unless institutions say otherwise. Treat every claim of effortlessness as a request to inspect the edges. Hold the theory itself to the same discipline, defeaters attached, split verdicts recorded, open questions admitted. Those habits will not make anyone a prophet, and they will occasionally make their holder seem slow beside the excitable. They will, however, keep the holder's beliefs tethered to the economy that exists, which is the only economy in which wages are paid, hours are worked, and the value question is decided. The personas to whom the following sections are addressed, students, teachers, and the writers who mediate the debate for the public, each have their own use for the tether, and the article closes its argument by speaking to them in turn.

## Competition among workers and the segmentation of the reserve

One mechanism remains to be drawn before the personas are addressed, because it governs how displacement is experienced by the people among whom it falls. The reserve of labour is not a homogeneous pool, and capital does not draw on it uniformly. Workforces are segmented by skill, by credential, by geography, by legal status, by the thousand gradations of employability that employers perceive and that workers internalise, and mechanisation strikes the segments differently: the captured tasks belong disproportionately to the middle segments, whose routinised cognitive and manual operations are the most readily standardised, while the segments below face the competition of the displaced middle pressing downward, and the segments above consolidate their scarcity behind the credential gate. The result is the polarised structure examined earlier, experienced from inside as a hollowing: the respectable middle occupations that once converted ordinary schooling into security lose their floor, and the workforce sorts toward protected expertise at one pole and unprotected service at the other. The framework reads segmentation as functional for the wage relation in a specific way: a divided reserve cannot bargain as a whole, each segment fears replacement by the segment below and envies the segment above, and the energy that might confront the purchaser is spent in the defence of relative position. Employers need not conspire to produce the division; the ordinary operation of hiring, credentialing, and task capture produces it, which is why the tradition treats segmentation as a structural product rather than as a plot.

Segmentation also explains a recurring puzzle in the politics of automation, the failure of displacement to generate the unified opposition that earlier theory expected. The displaced do not experience a common event. The middle segment experiences a fall, the lower segment experiences intensified competition, the upper segment experiences opportunity, and the concealed fragment workers experience an income, however meagre, where none was available. Each experience is genuine, and a politics that addresses only one of them will be heard by the others as foreign. The historical movements that did unify across segments did so by fastening on the demands that cross the divisions: a shorter working day, which touches every segment, a floor beneath wages, which protects the bottom and steadies the middle, and publicly provided absorbing services, which employ across the range. The demands that divide, protection of one segment's tasks against another segment's claimants, have reliably served the purchaser instead. A reader assessing any proposed response to automation can therefore apply a segmentation test alongside the productivity check: does the proposal unite the segments around a common term of the wage relation, or does it defend one segment's position at another's expense? The framework's sympathies are structural rather than sentimental here. United segments change the distribution of the dividend; divided segments bid for it.

### Why does displacement divide the workforce it harms?

because the reserve is segmented and mechanisation strikes the segments unequally, expelling the middle, crowding the bottom, and enriching the protected top, so the harmed experience different harms and can be played against one another. Unity has historically formed only around demands that cross the segments, above all hours, wage floors, and public provision.

With that mechanism in place, the article's survey of the territory is complete: the strict value position and its defeater, the moving contradiction, the fully mechanised limit, the productivity check and the concealed effort, the rival positions at full strength, the objection answered and its valid residue conceded, the table, and the mediations of ownership, state, geography, wage form, and segmentation through which every tendency must pass before it touches a life. What remains is to equip the article's distinct readerships for their distinct uses of the material, and then to answer, in the reference section, the questions under which the whole discussion is indexed.

## Circulation: mechanising the movement between production and sale

The debate fixes its gaze on the factory and the office, yet half the economy's hours are spent neither making nor administering but moving: hauling materials to the point of production, carrying finished goods through warehouses and ports and shops, and performing the immense clerical labour of ordering, billing, tracking, and reconciling that movement requires. Marx assigned circulation its own analysis, observing that transport is a productive industry in its own right, since a commodity's usefulness includes its presence where it is wanted, while the purely commercial operations of buying and selling, however indispensable to the system's functioning, add no worth and are paid for out of the surplus the productive sectors generate. Mechanisation in circulation therefore splits into two stories with opposite signatures. Where movement itself is mechanised, in freight, in warehousing, in the handling of containers and parcels, the familiar pattern governs: embodied effort displaces living effort, unit costs fall, crews shrink relative to tonnage, and the displaced gather in the reserve. Where the clerical superstructure of commerce is mechanised, the result is stranger, because the activity being economised was itself a deduction from surplus; economising a deduction enlarges the net surplus available for accumulation without adding a single useful article to the world's stock, a pure gain to the system that appears in no measure of national product honestly kept, and a standing temptation to mistake financial and commercial streamlining for productive progress.

The ports and warehouses also supply the debate's most legible modern parable of the hours question, legible precisely because no exotic apparatus is involved. When containerisation standardised the handling of freight in the middle decades of the previous century, the labour required to load a ship collapsed by orders of magnitude, and the dockers whose strength and skill the old methods had consumed faced the purest form of the displacement event: a method whose superiority nobody disputed, abolishing an occupation whose communities were built around the dock gates. The settlements that followed differed by port and by country according to the organisation of the dockers and the posture of the state, some purchasing the transition with guaranteed incomes and early retirements funded from the enormous saving, others letting the saving pool with the shipping lines while the dockside districts absorbed the ruin. The apparatus was identical in every port. The outcomes were not, and the variation across ports, documented in the labour histories of the period, is as clean a natural experiment as the debate possesses on the article's central claim, that relations decide what a method does to the people in its path. Students of the subject could do worse than begin there, where the mechanism is visible to the naked eye and the ledgers were kept by both sides.

### Why does mechanised circulation mislead the productivity statistics?

because half of what it economises is the commercial superstructure, activity that was always a charge against surplus rather than a source of product. Streamlining a deduction swells the surplus available for accumulation while adding nothing useful to the world's stock, so gains in circulation can masquerade as growth in production itself.

Warehousing adds a final twist worth recording. The great distribution sheds that now stand at the junctions of the motorway networks are factories in everything but name, their interiors organised by the same decomposition of tasks, the same pacing by system, the same monitoring of intervals that the machinery chapter described for the mill, and their workforces experience compressed, monitored hours in forms indistinguishable from those of manufacturing. The classification of such employment as services, and its exclusion from the mental image of industrial labour, is a statistical costume of the same kind the article has repeatedly stripped: the relations are industrial, the discipline is industrial, the reserve that staffs the sheds at peak seasons is the industrial reserve wearing a logistics badge. A reader who classifies by relation rather than by costume will find the industrial working class of the advanced economies considerably larger than its obituaries suggest, differently dressed, differently located, and subject to every mechanism the tradition describes.

## Quality, novelty and the measurement quarrel revisited

The mismeasurement reply to the productivity check deserves a fuller hearing than dismissal, because buried inside it is a genuine problem about what productivity statistics can ever show in an economy whose output keeps changing its nature. Output per hour compares quantities across time, and the comparison presumes that the basket being counted is stable enough for the ratio to mean something. Where the basket's contents transform, where goods acquire qualities their predecessors lacked and shed qualities their predecessors had, the statistical offices must impute the worth of the changes, and every imputation embeds a judgement that reasonable experts contest. Defenders of the transformation narrative argue that the imputations systematically understate quality gains in the newest goods, so the plateau is partly an artefact. The argument is not frivolous, and the honest response concedes its limited force before stating its limits. The first limit is scale: the revisions the quality argument can plausibly claim are fractions of the gap between a plateau and a revolution, and studies that price the unmeasured benefits of the newest goods, including their free varieties, recover amounts that narrow the gap without closing it. The second limit is symmetry: quality change runs in both directions, and an accounting that credits every novelty must also debit the degradations, the services thinned, the attention harvested, the durability engineered away, which the same statistical conventions leave uncounted on the other side. The third limit is the one the framework insists upon: the debate's subject is not consumer satisfaction but the organisation of production, and a transformation of production should register in the organisation's own magnitudes, in hours, in employment shares, in the composition of capital, even if every quality imputation in the consumption basket is granted to the narrative in advance. On those magnitudes the plateau's testimony is unambiguous, and it does not depend on how any statistician prices a novelty.

Novelty itself deserves a cooler assessment than the announcements give it, since a significant share of what presents as transformation is the recombination of existing methods into fresh commercial forms, new costumes for the wage relation and the sales effort rather than new forces of production. The framework's test for distinguishing costume from force is the test applied throughout: does the arrangement change the ratio between embodied and living effort in production, does it cheapen a necessity, does it move the aggregate, or does it merely rearrange who collects the margin on an unchanged process? Rearrangement is not nothing; fortunes and livelihoods turn on it, and the sales apparatus it feeds is a real employer. But a debate that cannot tell rearrangement from transformation will grade every new costume as a revolution, and its participants will live in a permanent revolutionary present that the statistics, decade after decade, decline to confirm. The tradition's sobriety on this point is sometimes mistaken for conservatism, a refusal to see the new. It is better understood as bookkeeping discipline learned across two centuries of costumes, each announced in its season as the end of the old economy, each absorbed in its turn into the old economy's next costume.

### How should quality change figure in the productivity dispute?

as a genuine but bounded correction: concede that imputations may understate the worth of novel qualities, then observe that the plausible corrections are smaller than the gap a revolution requires, that degradations belong on the same ledger as improvements, and that production's own magnitudes, hours and employment shares and capital composition, testify independently of any pricing of novelties.

## The double character of the working day under mature mechanisation

One refinement of the tradition's analysis, developed by its later writers and worth importing into the debate, concerns the double character that the working day acquires as mechanisation matures. In the early factory, the day's division was brutally legible: so many hours replaced the wage, the rest yielded surplus, and the struggle over the day's length was a struggle over a visible frontier. Mature mechanisation fogs the frontier from both sides. From the side of intensity, the day's hours cease to be uniform units, the watched and paced hour delivering more than the unwatched one, so that two workers serving identical shifts under identical contracts may expend markedly different quantities of effort and create markedly different quantities of worth. From the side of extension, the day leaks beyond its contractual banks: preparation, availability, the answering of messages, the maintenance of credentials and ratings, the unpaid labour of remaining employable, all migrate into hours that no wage purchases and no inspector counts, a lengthening of absolute surplus extraction accomplished without touching the legal day at all. The tradition's categories handle the fog without new inventions, since intensity and unpaid extension are both native concepts, yet the fog has a political consequence the categories alone do not capture: a frontier that cannot be seen cannot easily be defended, and the historical victories of the hours movement were won at frontiers marked by factory gates and statute books. Much of the practical ingenuity of contemporary labour organisation consists in making the fogged frontier visible again, counting the leaked hours, pricing the intensified ones, and dragging both back within the reach of collective agreement.

The leakage has a household dimension that joins this section to Cowan's evidence. As paid work leaks into the home, unpaid work is simultaneously reorganised by the market's advance into territory the household once governed: meals, care, entertainment, and instruction are purchased as services, freeing household hours that are then available for the leaked demands of employment, in a circulation of time between the wage economy and the domestic economy whose net direction is rarely audited. The framework audits it with the usual question. Total human effort, paid and unpaid together, is the quantity a humane accounting would track, and a mechanisation that reduces the paid portion while swelling the unpaid, or the reverse, has rearranged necessity rather than reduced it. Societies that count only the paid portion will chronicle liberation while their members experience an unchanged or deepening servitude to the clock, and the discrepancy between the chronicle and the experience is one source of the peculiar modern conviction, attested across the advanced economies, that life is accelerating even as the statistics of hours stand still. The conviction is not an illusion to be corrected by the statistics. It is evidence about the uncounted columns of the ledger, and a framework that taught its readers to follow all the hours, including the ones no wage purchases, predicted the conviction's existence long before the surveys recorded it.

### Why do people feel time pressure rising while measured hours stand still?

because the day's frontier has fogged in both directions: intensity packs more expenditure into the counted hours while unpaid extension, availability, preparation, and the maintenance of employability, leaks effort into the uncounted ones. Statistics that track only the contractual day record calm; the lived ledger, which the framework insists on auditing whole, records the squeeze.

## The announcement cycle as an institution in its own right

It is time to treat the announcements themselves as an object of analysis rather than as a backdrop, because a phenomenon that recurs on a reliable schedule, in a reliable form, with reliable effects, has earned the standing of an institution. The cycle's phases can be described without reference to any particular apparatus. A possibility is demonstrated under controlled conditions. The demonstration is generalised, in speeches and prospectuses, into a transformation of production as a whole, with the interval between demonstration and generality left politely unspecified. Investment follows the generalisation, workforces are warned, rivals are stampeded into matching commitments, and a literature of inevitability accumulates in which scepticism is cast as a failure of imagination. Diffusion then proceeds at the pace the installed base, the debt schedules, and the friction of real organisations permit, which is to say slowly and unevenly, while the aggregate measures register whatever they register. Disappointment is metabolised quietly: the transformation is redated rather than retracted, the vocabulary migrates to the next demonstration, and the institutions that profited from the generalisation phase, the promoters, the consultants, the conference circuit, the vendors of adjustment, retain their earnings from a prophecy whose fulfilment has been postponed. The cycle's reliability across two centuries suggests that it performs functions for the system that have little to do with forecasting. It raises capital on terms sober projections could not command. It disciplines workforces in advance, softening resistance to reorganisations whose real content is the ordinary cheapening described throughout this article. It supplies governments with a story in which distributional choices masquerade as adaptations to necessity. And it provides each generation of managers with a mandate for changes they wished to make anyway, laundered through the authority of the inevitable.

The framework's contribution to understanding the cycle is to insist that its phases be priced. Who was paid during the generalisation phase, and for what? Whose assets appreciated on the prophecy, and whose were written down when the prophecy was redated? Which workforces accepted worse terms under the shadow of a replacement that was never scheduled, and what would those terms have been had the shadow not been cast? Those questions convert the cycle from weather into accounting, and the accounting usually reveals that the prophecy's falsity, where it was false, was not a victimless error: the costs of believing it were distributed downward with the usual fidelity, while the gains from propagating it pooled at the usual addresses. None of that requires bad faith on any individual's part, which is what makes the cycle an institution rather than a conspiracy. Each participant responds to incentives the cycle itself creates, the promoter to the market for visions, the manager to the board's appetite for inevitability, the journalist to the audience's preference for revolutions over denominators, the worker to the rational fear that this time the shadow might be substance. Institutions are precisely the arrangements under which decent people, pursuing legible incentives, produce outcomes none of them would defend in aggregate, and the announcement cycle has the additional elegance of producing, as its waste product, a public that grows more cynical with each iteration and therefore less able to recognise the genuine transformation when one finally bends the curves.

### How can a reader tell a genuine transformation from another turn of the cycle?

by refusing the demonstration as evidence about the economy and demanding the economy's own testimony: sustained movement in hourly output, in employment shares, in the composition of investment, across the economies concerned. Genuine transformations pass that examination without advocacy; cycle turns require a continuous supply of advocacy to remain plausible.

There is a corollary about memory. The cycle depends on the forgetting of its previous turns, and its institutions invest in that forgetting, since a public that remembered the last redating would discount the next generalisation. The historian's and the framework's shared vocation is to be the memory the cycle cannot afford: to keep the old prospectuses on file, to compare their language with the language of the present season, and to note, drily, where the sentences match. Readers who perform that comparison once rarely need to be persuaded of the method again, because the discovery that a prophecy is a reprint does more to calibrate judgement than any quantity of statistical instruction. Keep the clippings. The next announcement is already being typeset, and it will be graded, by the curves and by the memory, whether or not its authors consent to the examination.

## What to watch: a short field guide for the coming seasons

Close the argument with the dashboard, the handful of magnitudes whose behaviour over the coming seasons will grade every party to the debate, the tradition included, more fairly than any further exchange of manifestos. Watch first the growth of output per hour in the wealthy economies, in the statistical offices' own publications, against the brisk mid twentieth century benchmark this article specified: a sustained quickening toward that benchmark, attributable to the diffusion of the new methods, would wound the sceptical reading and oblige the framework to explain an acceleration its mechanisms did not forecast. Watch second the durability of the leaders' returns as their methods diffuse, distinguishing carefully between margins protected by fences and margins surviving open rivalry, since the second kind, if it ever appears and persists, strikes at the transfer doctrine's root. Watch third the staffing of the edges: whether the concealed cohorts shrink as arrangements mature, which would suggest scaffolding genuinely being dismantled, or grow and darken, which would confirm that concealment is a permanent feature of the model's economics rather than a symptom of its youth. Watch fourth the hours settlements and the public provision budgets in the economies most exposed to the new methods, because those are where the dividend's distribution will be decided in practice, and where the difference between a mechanisation experienced as liberation and one experienced as dispossession will actually be manufactured. Watch fifth the gates of the residue, the credentialing fights, the training levies, the recognition of experience, where the politics of protection will be conducted in the language of standards. A reader who checks those five dials once a season possesses a better automation analysis than the entire announcements industry produces in a year, because the dials cannot be dazzled, redated, or rebranded, and they grade the powerful and the dissident with the same indifference.

The tradition's own exposure on that dashboard should be recorded without flinching, as the article's final act of the discipline it has preached. If the first dial quickens durably and the third dial empties, the framework's adherents owe the debate a Ricardo's courtesy: a printed revision of the expectations this article has defended, acknowledging that the mechanisms operated more weakly, or the countertendencies more strongly, than the analysis supposed. If the second dial ever shows its defeating configuration, the strict position on value itself must be reopened, and no quantity of dialectical ingenuity should be permitted to convert that reopening into a confirmation. Those commitments are entered here in advance, in the author's hearing, so that the article cannot later pretend to have expected whatever happens. A theory that will not sign such commitments is asking for faith. This one asks only for a reader with a long memory and a short list of dials, and it is content to be judged, as it has judged everyone else in these pages, by what the aggregates do next.

## A note on the kinds of evidence this argument rests on

An article that grades other people's evidence owes its reader an account of its own, and the account is brief enough to state plainly. Four kinds of source carry the argument's weight, and each has a characteristic strength and a characteristic corruption against which the reader should be warned. The first kind is the official inquiry: the factory inspectors' reports and parliamentary papers from which the machinery chapter was built, and their descendants in the statistical publications of the wealthy economies. Their strength is that they are produced by institutions with no interest in vindicating the framework, frequently with an interest in refuting it, so their testimony against interest deserves the premium courts assign to such testimony. Their corruption is the obverse of their origin: they count what the state has decided to count, in categories the state has chosen, and the uncounted, the household's hours, the fragment worker beyond the border, the leaked evening labour of the salaried, fall outside their columns by design rather than by accident. The second kind is the scholarly monograph and the peer-reviewed literature, Gordon on growth, Braverman on the labour process, Cowan on the household, Gray and Suri on concealed work, Acemoglu and Restrepo on tasks. Their strength is method made explicit and findings exposed to professional contradiction. Their corruption is fashion: literatures reward the striking result and the novel mechanism, and a finding that an entire field finds congenial should be discounted by exactly the warmth of its reception until independent hands have reproduced it.

The third kind is testimony, the record of the people inside the processes, gathered by journalists, by labour historians, by the inspectors' interviews and the ethnographers' fieldwork. Testimony's strength is that it registers what the aggregates cannot, the texture of the denser hour, the meaning of a devalued skill, the experience of a gate closing. Its corruption is selection: the memorable case crowds out the representative one, and every camp in the debate maintains a stable of witnesses whose stories, however true, were chosen for their serviceability. The framework's rule for testimony is to use it for mechanisms and never for magnitudes, to let it show how a process works while refusing to let it estimate how much of the process there is. The fourth kind is the tradition's own texts, and their standing in the argument should be neither exaggerated nor apologised for. The notebooks and the published volumes are treated here as sources of mechanisms and hypotheses, not as authorities whose assertions settle questions; wherever a claim drawn from them could be tested against the other three kinds of evidence, the article has preferred the test to the citation, and wherever no test exists the claim has been left in the open column. That ordering, evidence above text, test above loyalty, is the only terms on which a nineteenth century framework deserves a hearing in a debate about the twenty-first, and the reader is invited to hold the article to its own ordering wherever the two appear to conflict. What survives the ordering is an argument. What does not was decoration, and the article is better without it.

## Why this article names no device and describes no capability

A word, in closing the argument proper, about the restraint that has governed every page, since some readers will have felt it as an absence and others as a mannerism. No apparatus has been named here, no maker identified, no product described, and no capability attributed to any particular contrivance, and the omissions are the argument's method made visible. Three reasons justify the discipline beyond the series rule that imposed it. The first is durability: an analysis fastened to named devices inherits their obsolescence, and the literature of every previous wave is a graveyard of confident treatments whose subjects are now museum pieces, unreadable except as period documents, while the treatments that fastened on relations and mechanisms, Ricardo on the wage fund, the inspectors on the pace of the line, remain in print and in use. The second reason is honesty about the direction of causation. Naming a device invites the reader to reason from the device's properties to the society's future, which is precisely the inference the whole article has been concerned to block; reasoning must run from the relations into which any device will be inserted, since those relations decide what the device will be used to do, whose hours it will save, whose it will squeeze, and whose name will be attached to the saving. The third reason is a wager about the reader. A person who finishes a long article on automation without having been told a single thing about any actual machine, and who nonetheless finds that the next announcement they encounter has become legible, its claims sortable, its silences audible, has acquired something no briefing on the season's devices could have given: an instrument that does not expire. The article has tried throughout to be that instrument's instruction manual disguised as a history and an economics. If the disguise has worn thin in places, the intention can afford the exposure. The devices will change beyond recognition, and change again, and the questions will be waiting for them, patient as bookkeeping, indifferent to costume, concerned only with who works, who gains, who decides, and what would change the answer.

The restraint has one further warrant that the debate itself supplies afresh each season. Every named device arrives wrapped in the claims of its makers, and a writer who names it inherits the wrapping, repeating the maker's vocabulary, the maker's framing of what the thing is for, and the maker's selection of what about it deserves attention. Refusing the name is refusing the wrapping. It forces the analysis back onto the terrain where the makers hold no advantage, the terrain of hours and wages and diffusion and law, where their announcements can be weighed rather than merely relayed. Critics of the method will object that it sacrifices concreteness, and the objection has force wherever a mechanism genuinely differs between one contrivance and another. The article's answer is that such differences, where they matter, can be stated as differences in organisation, in the division between conception and execution, in the location of the concealed edges, in the wage forms at the periphery, without borrowing the maker's nouns, and that anything which cannot be so stated is usually not a mechanism but a marketing property. What remains after the nouns are surrendered is the structure, and the structure, this article has argued from its first page to its last, is the part of the subject that was ever worth a reader's years.

There is also a courtesy owed to the future reader, whoever opens these pages when the season's apparatus has become archaeology. That reader deserves an argument whose references still resolve, whose evidence can still be rechecked against the statistical record, and whose judgements can still be graded against what happened next. Articles written around named devices fail that reader twice over, first by expiring with their subjects and then by concealing, behind the expired names, whether their authors were right. An article written around mechanisms fails its future reader only if its mechanisms were wrong, and it leaves the evidence of the failure lying in plain sight, in the dials it told the reader to watch. That is the most any piece of analysis can honestly promise across time, and it is the promise on which this one is content to be filed.

One more consideration belongs to the record, concerning the audience that argument of this kind so often forgets: the people whose working lives supply its evidence. The correctors at the edges, the attendants on the paced lines, the fragment workers bidding at dawn in distant time zones, the clerks watching a frontier move through their own occupation, do not need a theory to tell them that the announcements and their experience diverge. They live inside the divergence. What an analysis can offer them is not awareness but articulation, a vocabulary in which a private unease becomes a public claim: that the hours are the ledger, that the dividend has an address, that the fence and the frontier are different things, and that the settlement, not the apparatus, is the proper object of their politics. If the article's abstractions earn their keep anywhere, it is there, in the moment a reader recognises a mechanism at work in their own week and finds, in the recognition, the beginning of a demand.

## Agriculture: the one completed revolution and its lessons

If a reader wants to know what a genuine automation revolution looks like in the statistics, the mechanisation of agriculture across the twentieth century supplies the specimen, and the article has withheld it until now because it calibrates everything else. Within living memory of the oldest farming families, the production of food occupied the largest share of the workforce in every advanced economy; across a few generations that share collapsed to a small residue, while output multiplied beyond anything the expelled workforce could have produced by hand. The transformation bent every curve the productivity check watches. Output per hour in the sector rose at rates that doubled and redoubled within working lifetimes. Employment shares moved by amounts no publicist needed to exaggerate. Whole landscapes, settlement patterns, and class structures reorganised around the change, and the displaced generations' migration into towns and services remade the receiving economies as thoroughly as the departure remade the countryside. Nobody who lived through it required a demonstration to know a revolution had occurred, and nobody reading its statistics requires one either. That is the signature of the real thing, and the contrast with the plateau recorded for the present announcements is the most instructive single comparison available to the debate.

The framework's mechanisms describe the agricultural case without strain, which is evidence for the mechanisms. Displacement proceeded on a scale dwarfing any factory wave, cohort after cohort released as methods captured task after task, from the harvest to the milking shed. Absorption proceeded through the expanding branches and the Baumol sectors, taking generations and inflicting the familiar concentrated suffering on the displaced districts, whose younger members left and whose older members endured the devaluation of skills accumulated across lifetimes. The reserve swelled and was worked down. Hours legislation and public provision converted portions of the dividend into leisure and security where organisation was strong, and failed to where it was weak. The state mediated at every point, subsidising the methods, researching them in public institutions, managing the exodus, and absorbing its casualties. Even the concealed effort has its agricultural form, in the seasonal and migrant labour that the mechanised harvest still summons at its edges, housed invisibly, paid by the piece, edited out of the pastoral image the food industry prefers. A revolution, it turns out, does not abolish the framework's categories. It exercises them at full amplitude.

### What does the agricultural transformation prove about the present claims?

that genuine production revolutions are unmistakable in the aggregates: employment shares collapse, output per hour surges, landscapes and class structures reorganise within generations, and no corps of publicists is needed to argue the revolution into visibility. The present announcements, measured against that specimen, show enclave change without the aggregate signature, which is what the productivity check records.

The agricultural specimen also corrects a sentimental error about the direction of mechanisation's human effects. The expelled farm workforce did not, in the main, fall into permanent idleness, and its grandchildren mostly live longer, healthier, and more variously furnished lives than the grandparents who worked the land. Nor did the transition's beneficiaries include, in the first instance, the people who performed it: the gains pooled with landowners, with the suppliers of apparatus and inputs, and with urban consumers, while the costs pooled in the departing villages. Both halves of that record are the framework's point. Mechanisation on the grand scale is compatible with enormous net human benefit across generations and with unjust distribution within them, and a politics worthy of the process attends to the second fact without denying the first. The present debate's participants might usefully be asked which half of the agricultural record their proposals address. Those who promise only the benefits are selling the first half; those who prophesy only ruin are selling the second; the ledger, as ever, contains both, and the distribution between them was decided, in agriculture as elsewhere, by organisation and law rather than by the tractor.

## Clerical work and the routinisation of the office

The office offers the debate's second great case study, quieter than agriculture and closer to the experience of the article's likely readers. Clerical work in the nineteenth century was a skilled, scarce, and relatively privileged occupation: the clerk commanded literacy, numeracy, a fair hand, and knowledge of the firm's affairs, and stood on the lower rungs of a ladder that could rise into management or partnership. The mechanisation of the office across the twentieth century proceeded by the now familiar sequence. Tasks were standardised and decomposed; apparatus captured the standardised fragments; a large, cheaper, and predominantly female workforce was recruited to operate the arrangements at wages the old clerk would have refused; the occupation's status and pay descended as its numbers swelled; and the integrative knowledge migrated upward into a managerial stratum and outward into the design of the systems themselves. Braverman's account drew heavily on the office for exactly that reason: the clerical transformation demonstrated that the decomposition mechanism was not a peculiarity of the factory but a general logic of the wage relation applied to any work whose tasks could be rendered routine, including work performed in clean rooms, at desks, by people who did not think of themselves as workers in the industrial sense at all.

The office case matters because it refutes the comforting doctrine that education and the movement into mental labour constitute an escape from the mechanisms this article describes. Mental labour is not a substance; it is a set of tasks, and tasks divide, under the pressure of cheapening, into the routine and the residue. The routine portions of any occupation, however cerebral its self-image, are candidates for capture whenever a method arises that can standardise them, and the history of the professions is a history of that frontier moving: bookkeeping before accounting, drafting before design, searching before research, the preparation of standard documents before the advisory judgement that no standard document contains. The frontier's movement does not abolish the professions; it reconstitutes them, narrowing the protected residue toward judgement, responsibility, and client trust while swelling the routinised base beneath, and swelling alongside it the concealed support workforce that keeps the residue's practitioners supplied with prepared material. Readers employed in the professions will recognise their own organsiations in that description without any device needing to be named, which is the structural method's test of itself: the pattern should be visible from inside, in the changing composition of one's own working week, before it is visible in any statistic.

### Why does the office refute the idea that mental work escapes mechanisation?

because the mechanism operates on tasks rather than on the dignity of the setting: wherever a task can be standardised it can be decomposed, captured, and reassigned to cheaper hands or to apparatus, and the clerical occupations underwent exactly the deskilling, feminisation, and wage descent that the factory occupations had undergone before them.

There is a status psychology attached to the office case that the debate ignores at its peril. The displaced factory hand knew the name of what was happening; the displaced or degraded clerk is encouraged to experience the same process as personal failure, a failure to retrain, to specialise, to climb into the residue fast enough. The individualisation of a structural process is among the wage relation's most effective defences, converting a distributional event into a biography of inadequacy, and it operates with special force among educated workers whose identity is invested in the belief that their position was earned by merit alone. The framework's counterclaim is not that merit is illusory but that the frontier's movement is indifferent to it: the routine portions of an excellent professional's work are as capturable as anyone's, and the security of the residue depends on scarcity, credentials, and bargaining arrangements rather than on the moral worth of its holders. A workforce that understands its own position structurally is harder to divide and slower to blame itself, which is why the tradition has always treated the diffusion of its categories, unglamorous as they are, as a practical intervention and not merely as scholarship.

## Credit, speculation and the financing of the waves

Mechanisation waves are bought before they are built, and the buying is done with credit, which inserts the financial system into the automation story as a co-author rather than a spectator. The lumpiness of fixed investment, its concentration in installation booms, and the long interval before the investment returns its value through sales, together mean that every wave is a wager placed with borrowed funds on a future configuration of prices and demand. Credit expands to accommodate the wager when confidence is high, and the expansion itself validates the confidence temporarily, since the spending of the borrowed funds constitutes the demand that makes the new capacity look needed. Marx's scattered remarks on credit, gathered by later editors into the third volume's treatment, describe the mechanism's double character: credit accelerates the development of the productive forces beyond anything retained earnings could fund, socialising the mobilisation of capital while privatising its direction, and simultaneously stretches the system's fictions, allowing claims on future surplus to multiply far beyond any plausible future, until the reckoning synchronises with the devaluation cycle of the apparatus itself. The automation announcement belongs to that financial machinery as surely as to the engineering: a sufficiently stirring announcement raises the funds that build the capacity whose existence is then cited as evidence the announcement was true, a loop that operates honestly in its early turns and hallucinates in its later ones.

The reader can use the credit dimension as one more instrument on the dashboard. Waves financed predominantly from the operating surpluses of profitable firms behave differently from waves financed by speculative issuance against projected dominance, and the difference shows in the trough: the first kind of wave leaves useful apparatus and survivable debts, the second leaves stranded installations, collapsed intermediaries, and a public rescue. The historical record contains both kinds in sequence within single industries, exuberant issuance building genuine networks whose bankrupt owners were replaced by sober successors who bought the apparatus at its devalued worth and operated it profitably, so that the social product of the wave survived the financial verdict on its promoters. That pattern, wasteful and effective at once, is the system's characteristic way of socialising the cost of its own experimentation: the losses are real, the apparatus is real, and the distribution of the losses, toward the small investors and employees of the failed promoters and away from the purchasers of the devalued assets, follows the usual gradient. A framework that began with the question of who creates value ends, in the credit system, with the question of who is made to pay for value's miscalculations, and the two questions have never had the same answer.

### How does credit change the character of a mechanisation wave?

by detaching the pace of installation from the pace of realised surplus, so that capacity is built against projected rather than demonstrated demand; the projection's failure then arrives as a financial crisis synchronised with the devaluation of the apparatus, whose useful residue passes at written-down values to the survivors.

Speculation also explains a feature of the announcements that purely technical readings find puzzling, their indifference to the productivity aggregate. A promoter raising funds against a projected transformation is not rewarded for the economy's output per hour; the promoter is rewarded for the plausibility of the projection to the next purchaser of the claim. Accuracy about aggregates is therefore not merely unnecessary to the announcement's function but potentially damaging to it, since the aggregate is the one witness that cannot be dazzled. The framework's advice to watch the aggregate is, in the credit context, advice to watch the one variable the promotional machinery cannot manufacture, and the longer the divergence between the announcements and the aggregate persists, the more of the wave's financing should be presumed to rest on the projection rather than on the product. Presumptions of that kind are not certainties, and the article's discipline requires recording that the presumption, too, carries a defeater: a wave whose installations demonstrably raise the aggregate as they diffuse has earned its financing retrospectively, whatever its promoters' motives were. The curves, once again, are the examiners, and they grade promoters and critics with the same pencil.

## The verdict this article defends

An article that has spent its length insisting on defeaters owes its reader a verdict stated with its own defeater attached, in one place, without the protection of surrounding qualifications. The verdict is this. The strict Marxist position on machinery, that apparatus transfers value and creates none, that competition melts the adopter's advantage and thereby drives a rising ratio of embodied to living effort, that displacement and concealment and manufactured scarcity are the characteristic social forms of the process, stands unrefuted by the automation debate and is corroborated at the points where the debate's evidence is strongest: in the decay of adopter premiums, in the aggregate productivity plateau that the transformation narrative cannot explain, and in the documented human effort staffing the edges of arrangements sold as effortless. The moving contradiction correctly identifies the strain those mechanisms generate, a system sawing at the branch of its own measure, and the rival positions, post-scarcity enthusiasm, the task framework, and scepticism, each capture a real fragment of the territory while missing, respectively, the value form's defences, the structural source of the displacement bias, and the explanation of why the announcements persist. The verdict would be defeated, in whole, by the observation specified at the outset: any firm holding returns above the branch norm without limit while diffusion stayed open and no scarcity rent shielded it, since that observation would show apparatus originating value after all. It would be defeated in its empirical parts by a durable quickening of hourly output across the wealthy economies that the new methods could claim as their own, accompanied by a genuine shrinking of concealed effort at the edges. Neither defeating observation is presently available, and the verdict is offered in the expectation, not the fear, that the future will grade it.

What the verdict does not claim should be stated with equal plainness. It does not claim that the tendency toward the limit will complete itself, that any timetable attaches to the contradiction, that the tradition knows what institutions should succeed the value form, or that the human meaning of mechanisation is settled by the accounting that explains its dynamics. It claims that the questions the tradition taught its readers to ask, who works, who gains, who decides, what would change your mind, remain the questions that sort true claims from costumes in this debate, and that two centuries of apparatus have changed the debate's vocabulary without displacing a single one of them. A reader who closes the article persuaded of nothing except the questions has received the article's intended gift, since the questions, unlike the answers, were built to survive their subject.

## Labour scarcity, demography and the Habakkuk problem

Mechanisation is usually narrated as capital's assault on an abundant workforce, yet the historical record contains a powerful counter-current in which scarcity of labour, not its surplus, supplied the spur. H. J. Habakkuk gave the counter-current its classic statement in American and British Technology in the Nineteenth Century, published in 1962, arguing that the young United States mechanised faster than Britain in several branches precisely because American labour was scarce and dear relative to land and resources, so that American producers faced stronger incentives to substitute apparatus for hands and to prize methods that economised on the expensive input. The thesis has been qualified by later scholarship, which found the wage gaps narrower and the mechanisms murkier than the original statement allowed, yet the core insight survives every qualification: the direction and pace of mechanisation respond to the relative prices of the inputs, and a workforce that is scarce, organised, and expensive accelerates the very substitution that a workforce abundant and cheap discourages. The framework absorbs the insight without strain, since its own account makes adoption a response to the cost of beating the social average, and the price of labour is a principal term in that cost. Where labour is cheap enough, owners rationally persist with labour-intensive methods for generations, a fact visible in every economy where sophisticated apparatus and sweated handwork coexist in adjacent streets, the choice between them made afresh by each owner according to what hands cost locally.

Demography gives the Habakkuk problem a forward edge that the automation debate handles clumsily. The advanced economies, and increasingly the economies that supply them with manufactured goods, face workforces that are ageing and, in several cases, shrinking in absolute terms, as the smaller cohorts born under low fertility replace the larger cohorts retiring out of employment. In such a setting the social meaning of displacement inverts in a way the older debate never contemplated: apparatus that substitutes for scarce hands may relieve a genuine shortage rather than create a surplus, and the reserve army may shrink toward the point where its disciplinary function weakens and the bargaining position of the remaining workers strengthens correspondingly. The framework predicts the system's responses to that inversion with some confidence, because they are the responses it has always made to a tightening reserve: intensified recruitment of previously excluded populations, extension of working lives, migration encouraged by the same states that police it rhetorically, and an acceleration of the substitution methods themselves, each response restoring the looseness of the labour market that accumulation prefers. Whether the responses succeed in the coming demographic configuration is an open question of the first rank, and the point is recorded here as such. What the framework rules out is only the naive reading in which shrinking workforces and advancing apparatus must produce either effortless abundance or mass idleness. The mechanisms are older than the configuration, and they will mediate it as they have mediated every configuration before it, through wages, hours, migration, and the going rate for hands.

### When does mechanisation relieve workers instead of displacing them?

when labour is the scarce input: where workforces shrink or organisation makes hands expensive, substitution apparatus answers a shortage, the reserve thins, and the bargaining position of remaining workers can strengthen. Habakkuk's comparison of American and British methods made the mechanism classic, and demography is now staging it again across the ageing economies.

The demographic turn also revises the emotional register in which the debate is conducted, and the revision is worth noticing because it exposes how much of the debate's dread was always about distribution rather than about quantity. A society short of carers, builders, and nurses does not fear that apparatus will make its members useless; it fears that the apparatus will not arrive fast enough to spare its members the overload of covering the shortages with their own bodies. The same society, told that its offices and warehouses are mechanising, fears the opposite. The two fears coexist in one population and often in one person, and they are reconciled by the framework's insistence that the quantity of work and the terms of work are different variables: shortage improves the terms of the scarce while worsening their load, surplus cheapens the terms of the abundant while lightening nothing, and the apparatus moves both variables at once in different sectors. A politics that understood the demography would bargain over load and terms together, trading substitution where the work is destroying bodies against hours and staffing where the work is human presence, and would treat the aggregate job count, the debate's favourite number, as the least informative figure in the entire discussion.

## Dangerous work, safety and the body's ledger

One claim made for mechanisation deserves to be honoured without qualification before it is analysed, because it is true: apparatus has removed human beings from some of the most lethal and maiming employments ever devised, from the faces of mines, from the handling of molten and toxic substances, from heights, depths, and pressures that consumed workers at rates the factory inspectors recorded with horror. Every honest ledger of mechanisation carries that entry in large figures, and the framework, whose founding texts were written amid the industrial slaughter of the nineteenth century, has never begrudged it. Marx's own pages on the factory dwell on the mangling of operatives by unfenced machinery with a fury that assumes the reader shares it, and the tradition's demand was never that the dangerous work be preserved for the sake of employment but that its dangers be engineered away and its remaining burdens shared and compensated. Where substitution removes a person from a lethal task and the displaced person is carried into other employment at maintained income, the event is an unambiguous entry on the credit side, and the framework says so in its own vocabulary: necessary labour has been reduced, and the reduction has been taken, for once, in the coin of intact bodies.

The analysis begins where the celebration ends, with the observation that the body's ledger has more columns than the fatality count. The denser hour examined earlier writes its entries in the chronic columns: the repetitive strain, the vigilance fatigue, the hearing ground down by decades of moderate noise, the lungs seasoned by dusts whose harm ripens after the employment ends. Mechanisation characteristically improves the acute columns while worsening or merely relocating the chronic ones, since the surviving human roles concentrate exactly the exposures the apparatus cannot take: the irregular, the exceptional, the maintenance performed inside the machine's envelope, the cleaning of the places the process fouls. The concealed workforce carries the pattern to its extreme, its characteristic injuries being those of posture, eyesight, and the psyche's exposure to the material it must review so that others need not see it, harms that are real, documented by the researchers who followed the work, and almost perfectly invisible to the consumers whose innocence the work protects. A complete safety accounting therefore asks the same question as the value accounting: not whether the harm vanished, but where it went, onto whose body, at what price, and with what compensation, and the answers sort themselves along the familiar gradients of the reserve.

### Does mechanisation make work safer?

it removes bodies from the most lethal tasks, a genuine and large gain, while concentrating the chronic exposures, strain, vigilance, dust, and psychological burden, in the roles that remain, above all in maintenance, cleaning, and the concealed correction work at the edges. The net verdict depends on whose body keeps the ledger, which is why the question must be asked segment by segment.

The political conclusion mirrors the article's general one. Safety, like leisure, is a form in which the productivity dividend can be taken, and like leisure it is taken only where it is claimed: through regulation that prices the harm into the purchaser's accounts, through organisation that gives the exposed a voice in the method's design, through liability that makes the chronic columns as legible to the firm as the acute ones. Left to the unaided mechanism, the dividend is taken as surplus and the harm is allocated by the reserve's gradient, downward and outward toward the workers least able to refuse it. The apparatus is innocent in the matter and the celebration of its innocence is therefore beside the point; the allocation is the politics, and it is decided, as every allocation in this article has been decided, by the relative power of the parties whose bodies and purses are at stake.

## Subcontracting chains and the small producer's fate

Between the giant firm and the fragment worker stretches a chain of intermediate producers whose fate under mechanisation illuminates the whole system in cross-section. The small workshop, the family firm, the artisan with a handful of journeymen: the tradition's early writings expected such producers to be ground away between the millstones of large capital, and in branch after branch the expectation was fulfilled as the rising scale of viable production priced the small producer out of the market for the product itself. Yet the small producer did not vanish; the role mutated. Large firms learned to externalise the volatile, the labour-intensive, and the legally hazardous stages of production onto chains of nominally independent small producers, whose competition with one another, unrestrained by the giants' administered prices, drove the terms at each link toward the minimum the link's workers could be made to accept. The chain performs for the giant the same service the fragment market performs for the celebrated service: it relocates effort to where it is cheapest and least protected, while interposing enough legal independence between the beneficiary and the conditions that responsibility dissolves. Sweating, the nineteenth century's word for the system in the garment trades, named the mechanism before the theory of the firm had vocabulary for it, and the inspectors who documented the sweating dens were documenting, in embryo, the supply chain as a technology of concealment and cheapening that complements the machinery inside the factory walls.

Mechanisation interacts with the chain in both directions. At the chain's head, the giant's apparatus sets the specifications, the pace, and the prices to which every link must conform, so that the small producer experiences the giant's mechanisation as a tightening of tolerances and a shortening of deadlines rather than as any equipment of their own. Within the links, mechanisation arrives unevenly, purchased second-hand, sweated beyond its designed life, maintained by ingenuity where the head office would maintain by replacement, so that the same generation of methods exists simultaneously at every stage of its life cycle distributed along the chain, gleaming at the head and patched at the tail. The value analysis reads the chain as a single production process wearing several legal costumes, its total embodied effort priced by a social average formed across all the links, with the surplus pooling at the head where the brand, the design, and the channel are owned, and the risks pooling at the tail where the perishable things, the workers' bodies and the small owners' savings, are concentrated. That reading explains a fact that purely organisational accounts leave as a curiosity: the chain's total arrangement is nobody's design and everybody's interest at the head, emerging from thousands of purchasing decisions each of which merely sought the cheapest adequate link, which is to say, from the same competitive mechanism that melts the adopter's advantage and fills the reserve.

### Why do small producers survive mechanisation by becoming links in chains?

because large firms gain by externalising the volatile and labour-intensive stages onto competing small producers, whose mutual rivalry drives terms toward the minimum while legal independence dissolves the giant's responsibility for conditions. The small producer's independence becomes the costume in which subordination is dressed, and the surplus pools where the brand and channel are owned.

For the reader's method, the chain adds a geographical question at the scale of the firm to match the one asked earlier at the scale of the world: when a celebrated producer reports a workforce astonishingly small for its output, ask how many of the hands that make its product appear on other firms' payrolls, under other names, in the links below. The answer, in industry after industry, restores the missing workers to the picture and the missing hours to the denominator, and the productivity miracle subsides toward the productivity fact. The framework's categories were built for a world of factories and have survived translation into a world of chains precisely because they were always about the relations the costumes conceal, and a chain is a costume with many seams, each seam a place where responsibility is tailored away.

## The residue: judgement, trust and responsibility at the frontier

Earlier sections described the frontier of capture moving through occupation after occupation, leaving a residue of tasks that resist standardisation. The residue deserves a closer anatomy, because its composition explains both the security of the protected strata and the limits of that security. Three kinds of task compose most residues. The first is judgement under genuine uncertainty, where the relevant considerations cannot be enumerated in advance and the decider must weigh incommensurables against experience: the diagnosis that does not fit the presentation, the negotiation in which the counterpart's real constraint is unspoken, the design choice whose consequences unfold across decades. The second is trust-bearing presence, where the human identity of the performer is part of the product: the signature that pledges an institution, the testimony that stakes a reputation, the care whose value lies partly in its being given by a person who could have been elsewhere. The third is responsibility in the legal and moral sense, the occupancy of a position that can be blamed, sued, shamed, or jailed, since a social order that allocates praise and punishment to persons requires persons stationed at the points where outcomes are owned. Each kind resists capture for a different reason, epistemic, relational, and institutional respectively, and the differences matter for prediction: institutional residues can be abolished by legal redesign whenever a society decides to let outcomes be owned by organisations or by nobody, relational residues erode as expectations adjust to substitutes, and epistemic residues shrink as the frontier of standardisation advances into territory that once seemed irreducibly judgement-laden, which it has done often enough to forbid complacency in any residue's occupants.

The framework adds its characteristic observation about the residue's pricing. The protected strata's incomes are conventionally explained by the scarcity of their capabilities, and the explanation is half true; the other half is that the residue's occupants have historically succeeded in fencing their scarcity, through credentialing bodies that ration entry, through professional associations that discipline competition among the occupants themselves, through liability regimes that make the uncredentialed practice of the residue illegal. The fence, not the capability alone, converts skill into rent, and the framework's analysis of manufactured scarcity applies to the professions exactly as it applies to knowledge goods. That is why the politics of the residue so often takes the form of a defence of standards whose protective function is real and whose scarcity function is inseparable from it, and why the occupants experience any proposal to widen the gate as an attack on quality itself. Both experiences are sincere. The ledger distinguishes them anyway.

### What protects the tasks that remain after the frontier passes?

a compound of genuine resistance, since judgement under uncertainty, trust-bearing presence, and personal responsibility standardise badly, and manufactured protection, since credentialing rations entry to the residue and converts capability into rent. The two protections are entangled in every profession, and honest analysis separates them before praising or blaming the occupants.

For the automation debate, the residue analysis yields a prediction more useful than the usual headcount forecasts. Expect the frontier to keep moving, expect each occupation's routine base to be captured in turn, expect the residue to concentrate and its fences to be contested, and expect the fiercest political struggles of the mechanisation process to occur not at the frontier of capture but at the gates of the residue, where the displaced middle seeks entry and the occupants defend the ration. Those gate struggles, over credentials, over the recognition of experience, over the price and duration of training, are the automation politics of the coming decades in their actual institutional form, and they will be conducted in the language of standards and safety, as such struggles always are. The reader who has followed the argument this far will know how to read that language: as the surface of a distributional conflict whose terms the framework names, and whose outcome will decide, for another generation, who is sheltered from the mechanisms this article has described and who is left exposed to them.

## Free time as the measure: the tradition's positive horizon

The article has dwelt on defences, strains, and ledgers, and a reader may fairly ask whether the tradition holds any positive account of what mechanisation is for. It does, and the account is worth stating in its strongest form before the personas are reached, because it converts the whole analysis from a critique into a standard against which arrangements can be measured. The positive horizon appears in the notebooks' speculation that, once production rests on the general intellect and the social stock of knowledge, the measure of wealth ceases to be labour time and becomes free time: the disposable hours in which individuals develop capacities, pursue ends of their own choosing, and participate in the common life as something other than sellers of effort. Marx returned to the thought in the third volume's published text, in the distinction between the realm of necessity, the labour required to sustain life, which persists in every social form, and the realm of freedom, which begins beyond it and flourishes only as the realm of necessity is rationally organised and shortened. The shortening of the working day, in that text's famous compression, is the basic prerequisite of freedom's expansion. The horizon is not a prediction and not a utopia in the dismissive sense; it is a criterion. Judge any mechanisation settlement by whether it expands the realm of freedom for the many or merely expands the surplus of the few, and the criterion ranks settlements that the productivity statistics treat as identical.

The criterion has practical teeth. Two economies with the same output per hour can differ utterly in the distribution of free time, one converting its productivity into a shorter week and longer holidays across the workforce, the other converting the identical productivity into expanded output, expanded consumption norms, and an intensified pace within unchanged hours. The statistics of production pronounce the two economies twins; the statistics of time pronounce them opposites, and it is the time statistics that describe the lives. The advanced economies differ among themselves on exactly that axis by margins large enough to constitute different civilisations of work, some having taken generations of the dividend as leisure and others having taken almost none of it since the middle of the previous century, a divergence that tracks, as the framework predicts, the organisational strength of the respective workforces and the settlements their politics produced. A debate about automation that never asks how the dividend was taken in the past, country by country and decade by decade, deprives itself of the best evidence about how it might be taken in the future, since the past settlements are the experiment whose results are already in.

### What would count as mechanisation succeeding, on the tradition's criterion?

a measurable expansion of free time for the many at maintained or rising material standards: shorter standard hours, longer holidays, earlier release from the labour market at life's end, and security during the intervals, all purchased from the productivity dividend rather than from anyone's destitution. Settlements are ranked by that expansion, not by output alone.

The horizon also reframes the article's most contested concept, the fully mechanised limit. Read through the criterion, the limit's significance is not the metaphysical vanishing of value but the practical question of what a society does as necessary labour shrinks: whether it clings to the labour-time measure by manufacturing scarcity, inflating necessities, and reconstituting toil at the edges, or whether it lets the measure go and distributes the shrinking necessity as shared free time. The tradition's wager, from the notebooks forward, is that the clinging is what the existing relations produce and that the letting go requires their transformation, and the wager is testable in miniature wherever a productivity gain is bargained over. Every negotiation in which a workforce trades a method's introduction for hours rather than for headcount reductions is the wager being settled locally, and the accumulated local settlements, not any device's capability, will decide which side of the wager the century confirms. The article's long argument thus ends where its author's politics began: with the working day, its length, its intensity, and its division between necessity and freedom, the oldest variables in the tradition and still the ones on which the automation question will actually be decided.

## For students and exam candidates

Students meeting this material in an examination should organise their preparation around the distinctions the article has built, because the questions set on automation and Marxism reward distinction-making above almost everything else. The first distinction is between wealth and value, and an answer that states it cleanly in its opening movement, with the waterfall or the fertile field as its illustration, has already separated itself from the scripts that treat output and value as synonyms. The second is between transfer and creation, with the depreciation of the machine as the concrete image and the melting surplus profit as the proof. The third is between a tendency and a timetable, which protects an answer from the two symmetric errors of announcing the end of work and dismissing the contradiction as refuted by its non-arrival. The fourth is between displacement of tasks and disappearance of jobs, where the Acemoglu and Restrepo framework should be named, dated, and then situated against the older theory's structural explanation of the displacement bias. The fifth is between the columns of the value question table, theory, evidence, and open question, and a candidate who visibly sorts material into those columns is performing exactly the operation the marking scheme exists to reward.

The evidence paragraphs of a strong answer deploy the two checks with their sources attached. The productivity check cites Gordon's 2016 history of American growth and the Solow observation behind the phrase productivity paradox, specifies the comparison properly, naming the economies, the decades, and the measure, and states the replies, mismeasurement and delay, with the concessions each reply makes. The hidden labour paragraph cites Gray and Suri's Ghost Work from 2019 alongside Casilli, names the functions the concealed workforce performs, training, moderation, correction, exception handling, and draws the conclusion the evidence supports: the boundary between automated and manual is organisational, and its misdescription is a selling point. Candidates should also be ready with the historical depth charges, Ricardo's 1821 recantation for the compatibility of aggregate gain with class loss, Keynes's 1930 essay for the prophecy whose productivity premise performed while its leisure conclusion was socially withheld, and Cowan's 1983 household study for the conversion of dividends into standards. A script that ranges across those four centuries of evidence while keeping its distinctions labelled will outscore a script twice its length that merely rehearses positions.

Two traps recur in weak answers and both are avoidable. The first is describing particular devices and their supposed capabilities, which dates the script, invites factual error, and abandons the structural level at which the marks live; examiners reward the candidate who writes about the wage relation's response to any apparatus over the candidate who retells a product announcement. The second trap is the verdict without a defeater. Every evaluative answer on this material should close its main judgements with the observation that would reverse them: the strict position falls if adopter advantages persist under open diffusion; the transformation narrative falls, for the present, on the productivity aggregate; the tradition's picture of the destination is conceded to be thin. Stating defeaters is not hedging. It is the visible signature of a mind that knows what its claims rest on, and it connects this topic to the wider treatment in the [modern capitalism exam answers](/2021/03/15/modern-capitalism-exam-answers/), where the same discipline is applied across the cluster. Students revising the value question itself will find the structured notes at [VaultBook's Marxism study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) useful for keeping the table's rows and the checks' sources in order, and the question banks at [ReportMedic's UPSC previous-year question explorer](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html) useful for testing whether the distinctions survive contact with real examination wording. The employment consequences touched on here are developed in the article on [precarity and the working class](/2021/02/01/precarity-and-the-working-class/), and the whole discussion sits inside the hub treatment of [marxism and modern capitalism](/2020/11/01/marxism-and-modern-capitalism/), to which a revising student should return once the distinctions are secure.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

Teachers possess, in the moving contradiction, the most compact statement of a real contradiction in the whole corpus, a single sentence from the notebooks of 1857 and 1858 that a seminar can unfold for a full session without exhausting. A productive sequence begins by withholding the sentence: ask the group first what an order that reckons wealth in hours of effort must do to labour time in order to prosper, and let the two answers, minimise it and depend on it, emerge from the room before revealing that Marx compressed the collision into one line and named the system itself as the moving contradiction. The seminar can then test the sentence against the article's evidence in sequence, the melting advantage as the same opposition inside the firm, the reserve as its form within the labour market, the productivity plateau as the opposition surfacing, unexpectedly, as a missing explosion, and the concealed workforce as the opposition hiding from its own statistics. Students who have performed that sequence own the concept in a way that students who have memorised a definition do not, because they have watched one mechanism generate apparently unrelated phenomena across four terrains.

The classroom's second asset is the debate's unusual supply of honourable opponents and honourable corrections. Ricardo recanting in the third edition gives students a model of intellectual conduct worth more than most methodology lectures. Gordon, a mainstream economist whose findings discomfort the transformation narrative his own profession's publicists promote, gives them a model of evidence outranking affiliation. The ghost-work researchers give them a method, follow the work, that transfers to any field they will ever study. A teacher can build an entire assessment around the defeater discipline alone: require every essay to append, for each of its three largest claims, the observation that would withdraw it, and mark the appendices as seriously as the arguments. The exercise generalises beyond Marxism, beyond economics, into every subject where students will meet confident claims, which is to say every subject, and it converts this topic from a body of doctrine into a training in how doctrine should behave. Discussion prompts that reliably catch fire include whether the household evidence strengthens or weakens the wage-based analysis, whether so-so automation vindicates Marx or merely embarrasses engineers, and whether the productivity plateau should make the tradition more confident in its mechanisms or less confident in its expectations, a prompt with the rare virtue that both answers can be excellent.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Researchers and journalists covering automation inherit a beat on which the gap between announcement and aggregate is the story, and the article's instruments were designed for their deadlines. The productivity check fits in a paragraph and improves any report that contains it: the claim, the measure the claim implies, the measure's actual behaviour across the specified economies and decades, and the claimant's explanation of the discrepancy, requested on the record. The hidden-labour enquiry fits in a reporting trip: find the edges of the celebrated arrangement, the labelling, checking, moderating, and exception work, and establish who staffs those edges, on what pay, under whose contract. The two enquiries together have the rare property of being ideology-proof in application, since they embarrass the utopian and the apocalyptic release with the same questions, and they give the journalist something better than balance, namely a standard against which both sides' claims can be weighed in public. Historians of the debate will find the longer arc equally serviceable: the recurrence of identical announcements across two centuries, the recantations and the refusals to recant, the conversion of each wave's dividend according to the era's settlements, constitute a documentary record in which the present moment can be located with unusual precision, and locating the present moment in a documented recurrence is the single most valuable service historical writing can perform for a hype cycle.

For the analyst reader, the article's residual advice is to watch the unfashionable variables. Watch the diffusion-adjusted durability of the leaders' returns, which tests the transfer doctrine where it can actually fail. Watch output per hour in the specified comparison, which will announce any genuine revolution before the publicists do, since revolutions bend that curve whether or not anyone issues a release. Watch the staffing of the edges and the wage forms at the periphery, where the economy's real innovations in the organisation of effort are occurring while attention is directed elsewhere. Watch the hours settlements and the public provision budgets, where the dividend's distribution is actually decided. And watch the tradition itself with the same unsentimentality: its mechanisms have earned their keep across two centuries of apparatus they were not designed for, its destination remains unwritten, and the distance between those two facts is the honest measure of what Marxism knows about automation, which is a great deal about the present and very little, despite its reputation, about the future.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Do machines produce value in Marxist theory?

No. A machine hands over to each product a slice of the worth that was built into it when it was manufactured, losing that worth bit by bit as friction and fatigue consume its service life. Origination of fresh worth belongs to living effort alone, for a reason grounded in how wages work: the worker sells a day's working capacity for a sum set by what that capacity costs to sustain, yet works for more hours than the sum embodies. Were apparatus a source of worth in its own right, the first firm to install it would keep its edge permanently. Rivalry instead erases the edge once the method generalises, which is what the doctrine expects.

### Q: What is the moving contradiction?

Marx's notebooks from the winter of 1857 and 1858 contain his sharpest single sentence on the subject. Capital, he argued, drives working time down toward the smallest possible quantity, since every owner profits by producing with less effort than rivals, and yet the same system treats working time as the exclusive yardstick and origin of wealth. The two pressures spring from one mechanism, and each corrodes the precondition of the other. Because the formulation fastens on the social form rather than on any particular device, it has outlived every generation of equipment whose arrival prompted fresh announcements that work was about to disappear.

### Q: Does the productivity evidence support the automation thesis?

The aggregate figures argue against the strong version of the claim. Were production undergoing a transformation deep enough to abolish work or inaugurate a new epoch, output per hour would be climbing at an accelerating pace across the wealthy economies, for lifting that ratio is the whole material point of installing apparatus. Growth economists working far outside the Marxist tradition, Gordon most prominently, record the opposite pattern: a long, slow plateau stretching across the very decades assigned to the supposed revolution, with the mid twentieth century's mechanisation posting the brisker gains. Appeals to mismeasurement or to delayed effects may yet be vindicated, but both appeals grant that the expected acceleration remains unobserved.

### Q: What is the hidden labour behind automated systems?

Behind many arrangements advertised as self-acting stands a workforce performing the chores those arrangements cannot yet shed: assembling and tagging the examples from which automated procedures are calibrated, checking outputs, reviewing flagged material, transcribing speech, and absorbing the irregular cases that fall outside standard routines. Gray and Suri named this stratum in Ghost Work, and Casilli documented its European counterpart. Its members are commonly scattered across continents, hired through layers of intermediaries, paid per fragment, and omitted from the account a service gives of itself, so whether a process counts as automated often depends on where its operators have been placed rather than on whether operators exist.

### Q: What happens to value in a fully automated economy?

Pushed to its logical end, the theory answers that wealth would abound while value vanished. Worth, in this vocabulary, is congealed working time; a process consuming none would embody none, and the apparatus of prices, profits, and wages would lose the substance it exists to measure. Far from being an embarrassment sprung on the doctrine by its enemies, that destination is one Marx announced in his own notebooks, where the collapse of production organised around exchange value follows from capital's drive against its own yardstick. The genuinely unanswered part concerns the passage: which institutions could organise production and distribution on the far side of such a limit, the tradition gestures toward without describing.

### Q: Does automation displace jobs or tasks?

The unit captured is the task. Acemoglu and Restrepo established that pattern across their studies of the subject: a new method absorbs specific operations inside an occupation, the surviving bundle is reassembled, and the employment outcome hangs on whether fresh tasks, in which people retain an edge, appear quickly enough to offset the absorption. Their framework also shows why the offset often disappoints, since incentives reward methods that substitute for effort over methods that augment it. The older theory accepts that machinery of analysis and supplies the deeper cause, pointing to a wage that figures simultaneously as the cost each owner must cut and as the fountain of the surplus each owner lives on.

### Q: Is post-scarcity possible?

As a statement about productive capacity, yes; as a statement about social arrangements, only conditionally. Bastani's Fully Automated Luxury Communism argues that cheapening has advanced far enough for generalised plenty to be a political choice rather than a fantasy, and the tradition concedes the capacity while disputing the automaticity. A distribution order built on wages and prices protects itself against plenty: recognised needs swell as fast as goods cheapen, legal fences rebuild scarcity around whatever reproduction has made nearly free, and fresh employments are generated to keep incomes flowing. Plenty therefore awaits a transformation of how goods and incomes are distributed, which is a political undertaking, not a by-product of engineering.

### Q: What did Marx say about machinery?

His fullest treatment occupies the machinery chapter of Capital's first volume, assembled from factory inspectors' reports and parliamentary evidence. There he traces how the machine system lifts skill out of the operative and deposits it in the apparatus, cheapens every commodity it touches, pulls women and children into the mills by dissolving the strength older crafts demanded, stretches the working day until legislation intervenes and then compresses more exertion into each permitted hour, throws cohort after cohort of attendants into the reserve, and places in owners' hands an instrument for defeating strikes. The notebooks add the reflection that accumulated social knowledge becomes production's decisive force as direct effort dwindles.

### Q: Does automation refute the labour theory of value?

The charge misfires because it attributes to the theory a claim the theory never made. Value was never offered as a gauge of material abundance; it gauges the social effort a commodity economy allocates and enforces among its producers. A doubling of physical output alongside stagnant embodied effort contradicts nothing in that scheme, any more than a census of hours is contradicted by a harvest. Mechanisation's cheapening of each unit and thinning of the effort inside it are motions the doctrine predicts in advance, and the endpoint where worth disappears altogether was printed by the doctrine's author as the system's own inner contradiction. What critics legitimately expose is a silence about successor institutions, not an inconsistency in the account of the present.

### Q: What remains unknown about automation and value?

Begin with the aggregate: nobody knows whether the long stagnation of productivity growth in the wealthy economies will break, or through which mechanism, though a true production revolution would register there before it registered anywhere else. Continue with the scaffolding: nobody knows how much human effort the apparently autonomous arrangements will still require once they mature, nor whether their edges will empty or merely darken. End with the passage problem: nobody, inside the tradition or outside it, has described convincingly the institutional route by which a society organised around the sale of working time would travel toward the limit its own development keeps proposing. Those three ignorances, honestly kept, are worth more than most of the certainties on offer.

### Q: How does the productivity check grade the automation thesis?

It converts every transformation claim into a single examinable expectation: growth of output per hour, in named economies over a named span, moving at revolutionary speed. A claim whose expected acceleration can be produced from the statistical record proceeds to be judged on its remaining merits. A claim whose acceleration is missing is marked as an assertion in search of its evidence, and the marking falls equally on every camp, since a Marxist announcement that the value form is visibly collapsing must face the same empty column. Gordon's history supplies the comparison that gives the check its bite, the brisk mid twentieth century against the sluggish decades assigned to the supposed revolution, and no reply yet offered has filled the gap the comparison opens.

### Q: What should a student separate between raising productivity and creating value?

Keep apart what an hour yields and what an hour counts for. Better apparatus, tighter organisation, or a faster pace can multiply the useful articles an hour delivers without altering the fresh worth that hour contributes, since that contribution is set by the hour's length and density alone. Each article consequently carries less worth even as articles multiply, which is why mechanised branches cheapen their products as a rule. Candidates who state that separation at the outset, illustrate it through a machine surrendering its embodied worth slice by slice, and close by naming the observation that would overturn it, an adopter's premium surviving open rivalry indefinitely, write the answer the marking scheme rewards.

### Q: Why does competition remove the adopter's advantage?

The pioneer's profit is a discrepancy, and discrepancies invite imitation. Selling prices are governed by the branch's average conditions; the pioneer, producing beneath that average, pockets the spread. Nothing about the pioneer's apparatus is unpurchasable, so rivals acquire equivalents, the average sinks toward the pioneer's costs, prices trail it down, and the spread that constituted the fortune evaporates. Fortunes that do endure invite a different diagnosis: somewhere a fence stands, a legal monopoly, a guarded secret, a channel nobody else may use, a scale of entry few can finance, and the yield of a fence is rent in the theory's vocabulary. Endurance with every fence removed would break the doctrine; endurance behind fences confirms its classification.

### Q: How does the value question table order the automation questions?

Four questions form its rows: the contribution apparatus makes, the trajectory of profitability, what becomes of jobs, and the meaning of the completely mechanised limit. Against each row stand three columns that the table exists to keep unmixed, the doctrine's claim, the record's testimony, and the territory still unmapped. Row one shows claim and record in agreement. Row two shows a deduction pressing against countertendencies strong enough to hold it off for generations. Row three makes the pace at which new tasks appear the hinge of the employment future. Row four separates an implication the theory owns from the distance economies still stand from it and from the institutional riddles nobody has solved. Grading a fresh announcement means assigning its parts to cells and watching which columns it has actually filled.

### Q: What separates a machine that transfers value from labour that creates it?

Look at the bargain each side of the factory gate has struck. The machine arrives already paid for at the worth its makers embodied in it, miners, smelters, machinists, assemblers, and across its career it can hand products nothing beyond that inheritance, released gradually as the apparatus wears toward scrap. Working capacity is bought on strikingly different terms: its price reflects the cost of keeping the worker alive, housed, and trained, a sum embodying fewer hours than the worker is then set to work. The overflow between hours worked and hours embodied in the wage is fresh worth, and it arises only where living effort is consumed. Hence the asymmetry at the doctrine's heart: strip away every machine and surplus persists in handicraft; strip away the wage bargain and it disappears with any equipment whatever.
