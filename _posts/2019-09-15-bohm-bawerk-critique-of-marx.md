Bohm-Bawerk's critique of Marx is the most famous attack ever mounted on the labour theory of value, and it is usually summarised badly. An Austrian economist published it in 1896, the year after the third volume of Capital reached print, and it makes four distinct arguments of very unequal strength. One of them, the celebrated contradiction between the first volume and the third, fills the textbooks. Another, quieter and far more damaging, asks whether exchange requires the exchanged things to be equal in anything at all, and answers that it does not. Identifying which of the four arguments actually bites, and which are answered by the standard Marxist reply, is the whole subject of the pages that follow.

![The four arguments assessed in Bohm-Bawerk's critique of Marx, the 1896 essay beside the volumes of Capital on a reading desk, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-50.webp)

A reader who knows only that a famous critique exists deserves the map before the territory. The essay appeared as a contribution to a festival volume honouring the economist Karl Knies, and its title announced a verdict: Karl Marx and the Close of His System. Its author held that the completion of Capital by Engels had not rescued the doctrine but exposed it, because the finished system could now be checked against itself. Four charges were pressed. The system contradicts itself between its opening and closing volumes. Its foundational derivation, the argument of the first chapter, is invalid as a piece of logic. Exchange, the phenomenon the derivation starts from, does not require equality of any magnitude between the things exchanged. And the theory's empirical implication about profit rates fails, since branches of production that employ much labour do not earn higher profits than branches that employ little.

The reply came eight years later from Rudolf Hilferding, then a young Marxist economist, in an essay that became the standard answer and remains the one quoted in classrooms. Whether that reply answers all four arguments, or only some of them, or in one case answers a question nobody had asked, is a matter this article grades argument by argument. The grading matters because the critique is still cited as though it had demolished Marxist economics entire, and still dismissed as though the reply had disposed of every charge. Neither summary survives contact with the texts. What survives is narrower and more interesting: one argument that the standard reply never touches, because the reply defends the theory by relocating what the theory claims to be about, while the argument attacks the reasoning by which the theory was first established.

## Bohm-Bawerk and the closing of a system

Eugen von Bohm-Bawerk belonged to the second generation of the Austrian school, the circle around Carl Menger that rebuilt economics on subjective valuation and the margin. He taught at Innsbruck and then at Vienna, served three times as Austria's minister of finance, and wrote with a patience that opponents conceded even while resisting his conclusions. His Capital and Interest had already examined Marx among the exploitation theorists in 1884, a year after Marx died, and found the doctrine wanting. When Engels published the third volume in 1894, completing the system Marx had left in manuscript, Bohm-Bawerk treated the event as an invitation. The system's defenders could no longer plead that the reconciliation of the volumes waited on a book nobody had seen. The book had arrived. The checking could begin.

The essay's manner deserves notice, because it shaped how the critique was received. There is no polemic against socialism as a movement and almost no rhetoric at all. James Bonar, who prefaced the English translation of 1898, praised the calm and close reasoning, and the compliment was widely shared. Bohm-Bawerk proceeds by stating Marx's argument in Marx's own terms, quoting extensively, granting definitions for the sake of the discussion, and then pressing on the joints. His charge, in his own summary of it, is that the completed system fails twice over: it fails against itself, since the third volume abandons the law laid down in the first, and it fails in its foundations, since the derivation that was supposed to establish the law never proved what it claimed. A critique framed this way cannot be met by denouncing its author's politics, and the seriousness of Hilferding's reply, which engages the reasoning page by page, testifies that the Marxists of the period understood this.

### Who was Bohm-Bawerk and what did he publish?

He was an Austrian economist of the school founded by Menger, a professor at Innsbruck and Vienna and three times his country's finance minister, who in 1896 published his Close of His System, an essay arguing that the completed Capital contradicts itself and rests on an invalid derivation of value from exchange.

Timing gave the essay its leverage. Marx had promised in the first volume that the apparent conflict between his law of value and the equality of capitals' profits would be resolved as the analysis advanced from the abstract to the concrete. For twenty seven years the promise stood unredeemed in print, and critics who pointed at the gap were told to wait. The wait ended in 1894. Bohm-Bawerk's essay is best read as the audit of a promise: he takes the first volume's law at full strength, takes the third volume's prices of production at full strength, and asks whether one system can contain both. Around that central audit he arranges the supporting charges, the attack on the derivation, the refusal to grant that exchange implies any equality, and the observation about profit rates, each of which would matter even if the audit itself could be answered.

His standing in the economics of the period also mattered. This was not a journalist's pamphlet but the work of the Austrian school's most systematic mind, the theorist of interest whose positive doctrine of capital was the chief rival construction of the age. When such a figure announced that a system had closed, the announcement carried institutional weight across Europe. Translations followed, the essay entered the seminar rooms of several countries, and it fixed the terms in which the labour theory was discussed for a generation: the contradiction, the derivation, and the standing of the reply. Those terms are the ones graded here.

## The Jevonian skirmish before the Austrian assault

The English speaking world had rehearsed the dispute a decade earlier, in a smaller theatre with a famous casualty. In 1884 Philip Wicksteed, a Unitarian minister and disciple of Jevons, published a compact critique of the labour theory in the magazine To-Day, arguing from Jevons's principles that value is governed by utility at the margin and that Marx's derivation confuses the conditions of exchange. George Bernard Shaw, then a young Fabian who lectured on Marx in working men's clubs, rose to defend the doctrine in print, and the exchange that followed ended with Shaw's public conversion: he announced that Wicksteed had demolished the economic foundation on which he had been preaching, and rebuilt his socialism on Jevonian ground. The episode matters here for two reasons. It shows that the marginalist objection to the labour theory was in the air Bohm-Bawerk breathed, available in cruder forms before he gave it its canonical one; his originality was not the objection but its completeness, its extension from the theory of value to the consistency of the whole system. And it shows the objection's peculiar power over honest opponents. Shaw was not a fool and not a trimmer; he was the most formidable platform advocate the doctrine had in London, and he surrendered the labour theory while keeping the socialism, a separation the Fabian tradition thereafter maintained. When Bohm-Bawerk's essay reached England in Macdonald's translation four years after the German original, with Bonar's preface commending its reasoning, it entered a debate whose lines had already been drawn by the skirmish, and it was read as the heavy artillery arriving after the scouts.

## The earlier front: the critique of exploitation theories

The essay of 1896 was the second campaign of a war Bohm-Bawerk had opened twelve years before, and the first campaign explains the second's confidence. In the first volume of Capital and Interest, his history and critique of interest theories published in 1884, he had examined the exploitation theories of Rodbertus and Marx as a family: doctrines holding that the worker, as producer of the whole product, has a right to the whole product, and that interest and profit are deductions effected by the owners' monopoly of the means of production. His counter argument turned on time. The finished product the worker helps to make is a future good, available only when the process completes; the wages the worker receives are present goods, available at the week's end. Between future and present goods stands the agio, the premium of the present, grounded in the three reasons his system elaborates. The capitalist who advances wages is therefore not deducting from a product that exists; he is buying a future product at its discounted present value, and the difference between the wage bill and the eventual proceeds is the price of the interval, not a theft within an exchange of equivalents. Exploitation, on this account, mistakes an intertemporal price for an interpersonal robbery.

The Marxist answer to the discount argument was given by the tradition in several forms, and its strongest form is Hilferding's: the argument presupposes precisely what it must explain. The worker sells across time at a discount because he cannot wait; he cannot wait because he owns no means of production and no reserve; his inability to wait is not a fact of human psychology but a consequence of a property order. A theory that explains the capitalist's premium by the worker's impatience has explained the effect of the property order by one of its symptoms, and the discount, so far from refuting the exploitation account, requires it as a premise. The grading of this older exchange lies outside the 1896 essay's charter, since the later essay brackets the exploitation theory to concentrate on value and price. It is recorded here because readers of the famous critique should know it is a sequel: the critic arrived at the close of the system having already, in his own judgement, disposed of its moral engine, and the essay's concentration on the logical foundations reflects a division of labour within his own work. The defenders who answered only the second campaign, as Hilferding largely did, left the first unanswered in the same gesture with which the critic left their social theory untouched, and the whole thirty year exchange has the structure of two sieges conducted against different walls of the same city.

## The method quarrel behind the value quarrel

The assault on Marx's economics was fought inside a larger war over how the science itself should proceed, and a reader who misses that war misjudges the weapons. In the eighteen eighties the German speaking universities were divided by the Methodenstreit, the quarrel of methods, between Gustav Schmoller's historical school, which held that economic knowledge grows from archives, statistics, and the patient description of institutions, and Menger's theoretical school, which held that exact laws, deduced from the elementary facts of human want and scarcity, were the science's only firm possession. Bohm-Bawerk was the theoretical camp's heavy artillery, and his polemical habits were formed in that quarrel: the insistence that an opponent's concepts be defined before they are debated, the refusal to let a historical narrative substitute for a mechanism, the courtesy that barely veils contempt for scholarship which mistakes accumulation for explanation. Marx, dead before the quarrel peaked, belonged by formation to neither camp, and that is part of what made him such an awkward target. His work is historical in its materials and deductive in its ambitions, a system of laws erected on a philosophy of history, and it could be attacked from both camps' positions at once: by the historians for the thinness of its evidence about actual price formation, and by the theorists for the looseness of its central deductions. The 1896 essay attacks from the theoretical side, and its author's formation shows in its confidence that a system's fate can be settled by inspecting its proofs. A Schmollerite would have demanded the ledgers of actual firms; the Austrian demanded the logic of the first chapter, believing, as his school believed, that if the logic failed nothing built on it could stand however many ledgers were produced.

The quarrel of methods also explains the strange afterlife of the exchange in the twentieth century's economics. The theoretical camp won the universities, and its victory carried the Austrian questions into the discipline's bloodstream while discarding the Austrian answers that had been framed in the quarrel's idiom. Deduction from elementary facts became mathematical modelling; the valuing individual became the optimising agent; the suspicion of aggregates that cannot be reduced to individual acts became the microfoundations programme, pursued by economists who had never read a page of the Vienna debates. On the other side, the historical school's instincts survived inside economic history and the institutional traditions, where Marx's questions about property, class, and the shape of whole societies continued to be asked by scholars innocent of the derivation that had once certified them. The 1896 essay sits at the hinge of this double inheritance. It is the theoretical camp's most accomplished polemic and, read with the quarrel in view, a document of the moment when economics decided what kind of knowledge it would be. That decision, not any verdict on socialism, is why the essay still repays attention from readers with no stake in either doctrine: it shows a science choosing its standards by combat, and the standards chosen in that combat, rigour about concepts, suspicion of unexamined magnitudes, insistence that a mechanism be shown, governed the grading of theories for a century afterward, including the grading of the victors' own.

## The world the essay entered

To feel the weight the essay carried in 1896, a reader needs the landscape of the science it entered. Economics in the German speaking world was then two generations into a transformation. The classical tradition of Smith and Ricardo, in which value had been tied to labour and cost, had been displaced in the universities by the marginalist reconstruction begun in the 1870s by Jevons in England, Walras in Lausanne, and Menger in Vienna. On the new foundation, value arose from utility at the margin, price from the interplay of subjective estimates, and the labour theory survived mainly as an inheritance inside the socialist movement, where it was not one doctrine among many but the economic core of a worldview. The Social Democratic parties of Germany and Austria were growing at every election, their newspapers and pamphlets taught the exploitation theory to hundreds of thousands of readers, and the theory's scientific prestige rested on Capital as its proof.

An attack on the labour theory was therefore never merely academic, and Bohm-Bawerk, who served a state that regarded social democracy as its principal internal adversary, cannot have been innocent of the stakes. What distinguishes his essay from the era's abundant anti socialist journalism is that it refuses every shortcut the stakes invited. It does not mock, it does not moralise, and it does not rest on the political character of the movement whose text it examines. It behaves as though the only question were whether a book's arguments hold, and it holds the book to the standards the book sets itself. That restraint was strategic as well as temperamental. A critique that could be dismissed as ministerial propaganda would persuade nobody who mattered; a critique that met Marx on Marx's own ground, quoting him at length and granting his definitions, might persuade the seminar rooms of Europe, which is where the doctrine's prestige was manufactured.

The immediate occasion sharpened the opportunity. Engels had spent a decade editing the posthumous volumes and defending the system in print, and his preface to the third volume issued a public challenge: the reconciliation of the law of value with an equal rate of profit for equal capitals had been promised, and anyone who could show how it failed was invited to try after seeing the text. Werner Sombart's respectful review and Conrad Schmidt's attempted solution had already shown that the reconciliation would be the battleground. Bohm-Bawerk's essay, arriving as a birthday offering to Karl Knies, the Heidelberg economist whose seminar had formed a generation, thus landed in a debate already framed and waiting. Its author's eminence did the rest. The finance minister of Austria Hungary did not need to shout to be heard in every economics faculty on the continent.

There is a further feature of the moment that explains the essay's peculiar structure, its division between the contradiction and the foundations. By 1896 the marginalist victory in the theory of value was, among academic economists, nearly complete; few professors still taught that labour embodied was the regulator of price. A critique addressed to academics alone could have confined itself to the contradiction, the internal matter. But the labour theory's social life was not in the faculties. It was in the movement, where the derivation from exchange to labour functioned as the doctrine's certificate of scientific status, the proof that socialism rested on demonstrated law rather than on moral protest. Bohm-Bawerk understood his audience precisely. The contradiction argument addresses the scholars, showing them the system cannot be completed consistently. The derivation and inequality arguments address the certificate, showing the movement's readers that the proof on which the certificate rests was never valid. The essay is two critiques wearing one cover, and the grading of its parts has to keep the two audiences in view.

## The classical inheritance: Ricardo's unfinished problem

Marx did not invent the difficulty his critic exploited; he inherited it from the classical school whose heir he claimed to be, and the inheritance explains both his system's shape and its vulnerability. Adam Smith had measured value by labour in two senses that his successors pried apart: the labour embodied in producing a good and the labour the good could command in exchange. For the rude state of society before capital and landownership, Smith let the embodied measure rule, deer and beaver exchanging by the labour of the hunt. Ricardo tightened the doctrine into a theory of relative prices governed by embodied labour, then spent his career worrying at its exceptions: the influence of capital's differing proportions, of time, of durability, which bent prices away from labour ratios in ways he acknowledged and could not systematise. His search for an invariable measure of value, a commodity whose own value never changed and against which all others could be truly measured, was the confession of the difficulty in its purest form, and it died with him unsolved.

Marx presented Capital as the completion of the classical project, the solution of Ricardo's problem by the distinction Ricardo lacked, between labour and labour power, and by the twofold character of labour itself. The claim to have completed Ricardo is what gives Bohm-Bawerk's audit its leverage, for the critic can hold Marx to the classical standard Marx accepted: a theory of relative prices. On that standard the third volume's outcome is legible as Ricardo's difficulty returning in Marx's own house, the bending of price away from labour by the proportions of capital, now systematised as prices of production but at the cost of the embodied measure's governance. Where Ricardo confessed an unsolved problem, Marx announced a solution; the critique's first argument is the demonstration that the announced solution relocates the problem into the aggregates. Readers who know the classical background will see why the Marxist tradition could not treat the charge lightly. To concede it was to concede that the completion of classical economics had failed at the point where the founder of the school had failed, and that two generations of Marxist certainty rested on a promissory note drawn against a volume that, when it finally appeared, paid in a different currency.

## The Austrian counter inheritance: Menger and the orders of goods

Symmetry requires the rival inheritance to be stated with the same care. Menger's Principles of 1871 rebuilt value theory from the side of the valuing subject, and its architecture is the mirror image of the classical one. Goods are ranked in orders: goods of the first order satisfy wants directly, while goods of higher orders, raw materials, tools, machines, serve only through the first order goods they help to produce. Value originates in the first order, in the dependence of satisfactions on available units, and is imputed backward to the higher orders, whose worth is derived from the worth of their expected products, diminished by uncertainty and time. Prices of producer goods, wages among them, are on this account reflections cast forward from the anticipated prices of consumer goods, and the direction of explanation runs opposite to the labour theory's: not from production outward to exchange, but from the valuing consumer backward into the structure of production.

Imputation matters to this article because it is the marginalist school's answer at exactly the level where the verdict defeater demands one. The defeater asks for a demonstration that regularities in exchange ratios require explanation pitched at production's level, of a kind divergent valuations cannot supply. The Austrian rejoinder is that production enters the marginalist account already, through imputation: the prices of labour and materials are not arbitrary but are disciplined by the values of the products they jointly make possible, so that the observed correlation between costs of production and prices, the regularity the labour theory claims as its evidence, is predicted by the rival theory as well, by a different route. Whether imputation can carry the full weight of the observed regularities, including the gravitation of prices when techniques change, is the live form of the old dispute, and it is a form in which the labour theory's defenders must do empirical work rather than dialectical. Bohm-Bawerk, Menger's successor in the school's leadership, built his theory of capital and interest as the imputation doctrine's completion, which is why his critique of Marx has the unusual character of an attack from a fully furnished rival position rather than from mere scepticism. He was not asking Marx's readers to abandon a theory for a void. He was asking them to change houses, and the house he offered had its own unexamined foundations, as the section on his positive theory records.

## Three births of the margin

The marginalist alternative that the critique presupposes was itself a triple invention, and the differences among its inventors matter for understanding what Bohm-Bawerk's version could and could not do. Jevons in Manchester built the theory as a mechanics of pleasure and pain, frankly utilitarian and mathematical, aimed at the Ricardian tradition of his own country. Walras in Lausanne built it as a system of general equilibrium, the interdependence of all prices rendered in simultaneous equations, in which the determination of any ratio involves the whole economy's valuations and endowments at once. Menger in Vienna built it as a causal and genetic account, suspicious of mathematics, tracing prices back through the orders of goods to the satisfactions of concrete persons, and insisting that economic magnitudes are not measured substances but outcomes of human estimations. Bohm-Bawerk is Menger's heir, and his critique bears the inheritance's marks: the suspicion of aggregates that are not reducible to individual acts, the insistence that exchange be understood from the parties' side, the treatment of capital as a structure of dated goods rather than a fund. Against Marx, the Mengerian version was in one way the most dangerous of the three, because it contested not the labour theory's mathematics but its ontology, denying that value is the kind of thing that could be embodied, stored, or transferred at all. A Walrasian could grant Marx his magnitudes and dispute their relations; the Austrian denied the magnitudes. The inequality argument is the ontological denial in its sharpest form, and its longevity, outliving the systems of both its author and its target, reflects the depth at which it was aimed.

## The claim under attack: value as embodied labour

The theory Bohm-Bawerk attacked holds that the value of a commodity is determined by the labour socially necessary for its production, and that commodities exchange, in the long run and beneath the fluctuations of market price, in proportion to the labour embodied in them. Surplus value, the source of profit, arises because labour power is itself a commodity whose value, set by the cost of reproducing the worker, is smaller than the value its exercise creates during the working day. The full exposition of this doctrine, with its distinctions between concrete and abstract labour and its account of the value form, belongs to the article on the [labour theory of value explained](/2010/07/01/labour-theory-of-value-explained/), and only the load bearing claims are restated here.

### What does the labour theory of value claim?

It claims that commodities exchange in proportion to the socially necessary labour time embodied in them, that this labour, abstracted from every concrete trade, is the substance of value, and that profit arises because labour power costs less to reproduce than the value its use creates across a working day.

Two features of the doctrine matter for everything that follows. The first is that the theory presents itself as a theory of exchange ratios. The opening chapter of Capital begins from the proportion in which one commodity exchanges for another and asks what regulates that proportion; the law of value is announced as the answer. Whatever else the theory later becomes, its birthplace is the exchange relation, and its first claim is a claim about why definite quantities of unlike goods trade against each other. The second feature is the role of the derivation. Marx does not merely assert that labour is the substance of value. He argues for it, in a celebrated passage, by elimination: exchange implies a common element, use value cannot be that element, and once use value is set aside only one common property remains. The critique's second and third arguments are aimed at that passage and at the premise beneath it, which is why the derivation, a few pages in a very long book, carries so much of the dispute.

A third feature should be flagged now, since the standard reply leans on it. The theory also functions, in Marx's hands, as an account of the social relations of a commodity producing society: of how private labours are socially validated through exchange, of how the product of labour dominates its producer, of why exploitation can coexist with formally equal exchange. Hilferding's reply develops this side of the doctrine into the whole defence. Whether that development answers Bohm-Bawerk depends on whether the charges were aimed at this side of the theory or at the other one, and the grading below keeps the two targets separate throughout.

## The opening chapter and the search for a third thing

Marx opens Capital with the commodity, a thing that is at once a use value, satisfying some want by its properties, and a bearer of exchange value, trading against other commodities in definite proportions. A quarter of wheat exchanges for varying quantities of silk, gold, iron, and everything else, and this variety seems to make exchange value accidental, a matter of time and place. Yet the proportions are not arbitrary at any given moment, and Marx asks what they express. His move is to write the exchange as an equation. A given quantity of wheat set equal to a given quantity of iron tells us, he argues, that something common exists in both, of the same magnitude in each, and that both are therefore reducible to a third thing which is neither wheat nor iron.

The elimination then proceeds. The common element cannot be any natural property of the goods, geometrical, physical, or chemical, because such properties matter only insofar as they make the goods useful. Within the exchange relation, one use value counts for exactly as much as another provided it is present in due proportion; as exchange values, commodities differ only in quantity and contain, Marx says, no atom of use value. Set use value aside, and "there remains to them only one common property, that of being products of labor". Even as products of labour they have been transformed by the abstraction itself: the carpenter's work and the spinner's work lose their distinct characters, and what remains is human labour in the abstract, expenditure of labour power without regard to the form of its expenditure. Commodities are values as crystals of this common social substance, and their magnitudes are measured by the labour time socially necessary for their production.

### What is the third thing in the derivation?

It is the common element that two exchanged commodities are said to share in equal magnitude, the factor to which both must be reducible for their equation in exchange to make sense, which Marx identifies, after use value is set aside, as abstract human labour embodied in each.

### What remains when use value is set aside?

Abstracting from everything that makes a commodity useful strips away its material form and the concrete character of the labour that produced it, leaving, on Marx's argument, a single shared property: the goods are products of human labour rendered homogeneous, labour counted purely by the time it takes.

Notice what the derivation needs in order to work. It needs the premise that an exchange equation expresses an equality, that the two sides are equal in some magnitude which a common substance measures. It needs the field of candidates to be completely surveyed, so that elimination can certify the survivor. And it needs the field itself, the class of things whose common property is sought, to be drawn without favour. Bohm-Bawerk's second argument attacks the survey and the drawing of the field. His third argument attacks the premise. The distinction between those two attacks is the most instructive thing in the whole critique, because the survey could be repaired, in principle, by a more careful elimination, while a failed premise cannot be repaired at all: if exchange does not imply equality, there is nothing for a common substance to be the measure of, and the search that opens Capital is a search for something the transaction does not require.

The derivation also carries a quiet restriction, which the critique drags into the open. The goods Marx considers are commodities, products of labour made for the market. Gifts of nature that enter exchange, land above all, together with waterfalls, coal seams, and standing timber, are left outside the field from the first page. If the common element is supposed to explain why exchangeable things bear exchange ratios at all, excluding a large class of exchangeable things from the search looks less like method and more like arrangement: put only labour products into the sieve, and the property of being a labour product is guaranteed to survive the sifting. Whether that charge is fair is graded with the second argument below.

## Labour commanded against labour embodied

Smith had left his successors two measures of value wearing one name, and Marx's choice between them is the fork at which the whole dispute begins. Labour embodied is the labour a thing cost its producer. Labour commanded is the labour a thing can purchase in exchange, the quantity of other people's effort its owner can set in motion by trading it. Where producers are independent the two coincide, since a man who spends a week making a tool can trade it for a week's work embodied in other goods. Under capitalism they part company, because the labour a wage can command and the labour embodied in the wage goods are different magnitudes, and the difference is the surplus. Marx chose the embodied measure as the substance of value and treated the commanded measure as a confusion of the classical school. The choice was principled: only the embodied measure ties value to production and makes exploitation a measurable residue. But the choice carried a cost the critique collects. The embodied measure is invisible in every actual transaction, where what a thing commands is the operative fact, and a theory whose central magnitude must always be inferred behind the back of the observable one invites the Austrian question: what work is the inferred magnitude doing that the observable relations do not do without it? Bohm-Bawerk's derivation argument is that question aimed at the theory's first page. A defender may answer that sciences routinely posit unobservables, and the answer is sound in general; the specific difficulty is that the labour theory's unobservable is posited by a derivation that claims to extract it from the observable by logic alone, and the extraction is what failed. Smith's fork thus runs under the entire controversy. The tradition that chose commanded magnitudes, through the marginalists' valuations, built its theory on what transactions display. The tradition that chose embodied magnitudes built on what production deposits. The critique of 1896 is the moment the first tradition audited the second's founding document and found the audit trail broken at the first step.

## The physiocratic ancestor of the pool

The aggregate structure of Marx's solution, the pool of surplus produced in one place and divided by a rule in another, has an ancestry older than classical economics, and recognising it clarifies what kind of theory the third volume contains. Quesnay's Tableau, the founding diagram of the physiocratic school, depicted the economy as a circulation in which a net product arose in one sector, agriculture, and flowed outward to sustain the rest. Marx admired the Tableau extravagantly, calling it an extremely brilliant conception, and his reproduction schemes and his pooling of surplus value inherit its architecture: a magnitude generated at a source, then distributed across the system by the system's own mechanics, with the distribution concealing the source from every individual recipient. Bohm-Bawerk, who wrote the history of these doctrines before he criticised their heir, understood the ancestry perfectly, and it informs his verdict's deepest layer. A physiocratic architecture answers the question where the surplus comes from and how it circulates. It does not answer, and in its physiocratic original never pretended to answer, the question what determines the ratio at which two commodities exchange on a market day. Marx's system, on the critic's reading, is a physiocratic engine of production and distribution fitted with a Ricardian theory of exchange at its entrance, and the two parts were never welded: the entrance derivation promises a regulator of ratios, the engine delivers a theory of the pool, and the third volume's transformation is the record of the weld failing under load. The standard reply, which defends the engine and abandons the entrance, is on this reading not a betrayal of the system but a recognition of which part of it was sound. Naming the ancestry gives the recognition its proper dignity and its proper limit at once.

## The equation under the microscope

Slow the opening chapter down and watch the derivation's hands. Marx begins with a single commodity and its many exchange proportions: a quarter of wheat trades for a certain weight of iron, a certain length of silk, a certain quantity of gold, and the list can be extended indefinitely. Since the same wheat stands in all these proportions, the proportions must be expressions of one thing, the wheat's exchange value, which each equivalent measures from outside. Marx then takes two commodities only, wheat and iron, and fixes attention on the equation between them. A definite quantity of the first is set equal to a definite quantity of the second. What, he asks, does the equation assert? His answer: it asserts that something common, of the same magnitude, exists in both things, and that each, so far as it is an exchange value, is reducible to that common third.

Every later step depends on how this moment is read, and Bohm-Bawerk's critique can be summarised as a refusal to let the moment pass unexamined. An equation in mathematics asserts identity of magnitude between its sides. An exchange, however, is not a measurement but an event, the transfer of goods between owners, and writing the event in the notation of an equation imports the suggestion of equality without arguing for it. The notation does quiet work. Once the transfer is written with an equals sign, the mind goes looking for the respect in which the sides are equal, and the hunt for a shared substance has been licensed by a typographical decision. Bohm-Bawerk's insistence, developed across his fourth chapter, is that the licence was never earned: before asking in what respect the two sides are equal, one must ask whether they are equal in anything, and the behaviour of the parties, each surrendering the less valued for the more valued, answers that they are not.

The lineage of the equality assumption helps explain its grip. Aristotle, considering exchange in the Ethics, had held that exchange requires equality and equality requires commensurability, and had puzzled over what could make a house and a bed commensurable. The scholastic doctrine of the just price inherited the puzzle and gave it a moral form: a fair exchange was an exchange of equivalents, and cheating consisted in disturbing the equivalence. Marx quotes the Aristotelian formula with evident sympathy, and his derivation can be read as the last great attempt to solve Aristotle's puzzle by finding the substance that makes goods commensurable. Bohm-Bawerk, who quotes the same passage, treats the puzzle itself as the error. Commensurability in exchange is not a property the goods bring to the market; it is an artifact of the market's arithmetic, the fact that a ratio can be written down. A ratio between two quantities of different things no more implies a shared substance than the ratio between a man's height and his weight implies a fluid common to both. The equation records an outcome. It does not disclose an ingredient.

Set against this reading, the derivation's remaining steps change character. The exclusion of use value, which Marx performs by observing that exchange abstracts from the particular usefulness of goods, becomes on the critical reading a second unearned step: the parties do not abstract from usefulness, they act on it, since usefulness differently ranked is their whole motive for trading. The residue that survives the exclusion, labour, is then not a discovery but the last candidate left standing in a survey whose exclusions were arranged by the analyst. And the magnitude that labour is said to contribute, socially necessary labour time, enters as the measure of a substance whose existence the equation was supposed to establish and did not. None of this shows that labour time fails to regulate prices as an empirical matter; gravitation through competition might still tie prices to costs in labour. What it shows is that the opening chapter's celebrated proof, the deduction of value from the form of exchange, performs no proving. The theory's real arguments, if it has them, live elsewhere, in the analysis of production and competition, and the critique's service was to force that relocation into the open decades before the theory's defenders acknowledged it.

## The four arguments assessed

The critique is one essay, but it is not one argument, and treating it as one is the root error of most summaries. Its four charges differ in target, in strength, and in the fate they met at the hands of the standard reply. One charges the system with self contradiction across its volumes. One charges the foundational passage with faulty logic. One denies the premise on which that passage rests. One points at the observable behaviour of profit rates and asks the theory to square itself with the record. The table below states each charge, the reply the Marxist tradition standardly gives, whether that reply meets the charge, and the verdict this article defends. The sections after the table argue each row in full.

The charge that fills survey courses, the contradiction between the volumes, meets a reply with genuine content, and the dispute it names continues in technical literature to which this article links rather than contributes. The charge that receives a paragraph in most summaries, the denial that exchange implies equality, meets no reply at all, only a change of subject. A reader who leaves with that ordering reversed has been mis-served by the summary tradition, and the sections below exist to justify the ordering row by row.

## The rock of offence: a decade of attempted solutions

The contradiction was not discovered by Bohm-Bawerk, and the essay's originality lies elsewhere than in noticing it. Marx himself had flagged the difficulty with remarkable candour. In the second volume's editorial history the problem was already posed publicly: Engels, preparing that volume for the press in 1885, told its readers that the third volume would resolve the apparent conflict between the law of value and the uniform rate of profit, and he challenged the economists who had written on the subject to produce the resolution in advance, promising that the successful solver would have anticipated Marx and the unsuccessful would stand exposed. The challenge turned the reconciliation into a public test of the school. Wilhelm Lexis, in a review Engels himself praised, sketched substantially the answer the third volume would give, the pooling and redistribution of surplus, and Engels acknowledged the anticipation with good grace. Others, among them Conrad Schmidt, published solutions that Marx's literary executor judged failures, and the problem acquired a name in the literature, the great contradiction, years before the Austrian's essay appeared.

This history matters to the grading because it fixes what the first argument could and could not claim. Bohm-Bawerk was not exposing a discrepancy the Marxists had overlooked; he was auditing a solution they had published with ceremony, and his contention was that the ceremony had disguised a surrender. The defenders' position had been staked in advance: the third volume would show value's law and the equal profit rate reconciled, with the law intact. The critic's counter was that the volume reconciles them by demoting the law, and that the demotion is visible in Marx's own worked example. An audit of a published solution is a stronger dialectical position than the discovery of a hidden flaw, because the defence cannot plead that the problem was never faced. It was faced, with a decade of anticipation and a public challenge, and the critic's question is only whether the facing succeeded.

### What was Sombart's apology for the law of value?

Werner Sombart, in a celebrated review of the third volume, defended the law by recasting value as a fact of thought rather than of experience, a logical instrument for comprehending the economy, which Bohm-Bawerk answered by observing that a law so recast can neither be contradicted nor confirmed by prices, and so explains none.

Sombart's apology deserves its own paragraph because Bohm-Bawerk devoted his final chapter to it and because it previews the standard reply's deepest move. Sombart, the most sympathetic academic reviewer the third volume found, proposed that value in Marx should not be read as an empirical magnitude at all. It is a Denkfaktum, a fact of thought, an auxiliary construction by which the mind grasps the economic process as a whole, not a quantity that prices could either obey or disobey. On that reading the contradiction dissolves, since a logical instrument cannot conflict with an empirical regularity. Bohm-Bawerk's response is the essay's most prophetic passage. He grants the ingenuity and refuses the bargain: a law of value that has been promoted into logic has been retired from economics, since the original claim, the claim that made the theory a theory, was that labour values govern the actual movement of prices in the actual world. Every defence that saves the system by reclassifying its central law, from magnitude to method, from fact to form of thought, purchases consistency at the price of the claim's content. The standard reply's relocation of value from exchange ratios to social relations is, in the critic's taxonomy prepared eight years early, another payment of the same price, and his chapter on Sombart stands as the critique's advance answer to Hilferding before Hilferding had written a word.

## The editor's challenge: Engels and the staging of the test

No account of the critique is complete without the figure who staged the confrontation, Friedrich Engels. As Marx's literary executor he spent the decade after 1883 turning manuscripts into volumes, and he understood his editorial work as advocacy: the prefaces he wrote for the second and third volumes are defences as much as introductions. In the preface to the second volume he set the trap that the third was meant to spring, announcing that the coming book would reconcile the law of value with the equal rate of profit and inviting the professional economists, by name in some cases, to attempt the reconciliation first and measure themselves against Marx's solution when it appeared. The invitation was a wager on the system's completeness, laid publicly, with the profession as witness. When the third volume appeared in 1894 with the pooling solution, Engels presented the wager as won, and his preface answered the early reviewers, among them critics who had already scented the surrender, with undiminished confidence.

Bohm-Bawerk's essay is best understood as the acceptance of Engels's wager on Engels's own terms, which is why its tone is so free of the usual polemical static: the auditor does not need to raise his voice when the audited party has published both the promise and the performance. The essay quotes the preface's challenge, quotes the first volume's law, quotes the third volume's example, and lets the three documents argue with one another. This also explains the peculiar pain the essay caused. A hostile discovery can be resented; a failed public wager must be answered, and the obligation to answer it, rather than to denounce its author, is what produced Hilferding's essay and the whole subsequent literature. The Marxist movement's ablest economist spent his youth on the reply because the movement's own editor had made silence impossible.

## What the first volume actually promised

The defence that the third volume merely concretises the first depends on what the first volume's own architecture promised, and the promise deserves a fair statement. Marx's method, announced in his afterword to the second German edition, rises from the abstract toward the concrete, beginning with the simplest determinations and rising toward the whole in its complexity. The first volume analyses the commodity and capital in general, abstracting from the competition of capitals among themselves; the equalisation of profit rates belongs to a later level, where many capitals confront one another. On this architecture, the assumption that commodities exchange at their values in the first volume is a deliberate simplification, flagged as such, and the prices of production of the third volume are the same law viewed after the abstraction is relaxed. Defenders argue that Bohm-Bawerk, formed in a different methodological tradition, mistook a staged exposition for a pair of contradictory assertions, grading the scaffolding as though it were the building.

The critic's answer to the architecture defence is the burden of his third chapter, and it is textual rather than methodological. The first volume does not present its law as a scaffolding assumption to be discarded at a higher level. It presents the law as the truth about exchange, calls deviations from it accidental breaches, describes it in the vocabulary of natural necessity, and builds the theory of surplus value on exchange at values as a real feature of the system, insisting that exploitation is compatible with, indeed requires, the exchange of equivalents. A simplification is a device the author controls; a claim fortified with this rhetoric is a commitment. Moreover, the movement from abstract to concrete in a sound exposition preserves the earlier determinations as the truth of the later ones, deepened but not reversed: the concrete is the abstract with its conditions supplied. Prices of production do not deepen the proposition that commodities exchange according to embodied labour; they replace it, for every individual commodity, with a different determinant. Bohm-Bawerk's phrase for the relation between the volumes is that the third stands to the first not as completion but as contradiction, and the architectural defence, for all its sophistication, has never quite answered the textual point about the first volume's own language. The grading given in the table, serious and partly answered, reflects this balance: the architecture explains how Marx could have intended consistency, while the texts record that the intention was not achieved in the form the first volume's readers were promised.

## The first argument: two volumes, two laws of exchange

The contradiction charge is easily stated and was felt, long before Bohm-Bawerk, by everyone who read the third volume against the first. In the opening volume, commodities exchange according to their values. The law is not offered as a rough tendency but as the governing principle of exchange, the inner law to which market prices conform beneath their oscillations, described by Marx as the eternal law of the exchange of commodities and as a natural law of equilibrium. Profit, on that volume's account, originates in production: the capitalist buys labour power at its value, sets it to work beyond the hours needed to reproduce that value, and appropriates the surplus. Nothing in the first volume's machinery prepares the reader for what the third volume then does with prices.

The difficulty is arithmetic before it is anything else. Capitals of equal size employ very different proportions of labour. A capital laid out mostly on machinery and materials sets few workers in motion; a capital laid out chiefly on wages sets many. If surplus value springs from labour alone, and if the rate of exploitation is broadly uniform, the first capital should yield little profit and the second much. Rates of profit, reckoned on total capital advanced, should therefore differ wildly between branches, highest where hands are many and plant is cheap, lowest where plant is dear and hands are few. Observation refuses to cooperate. Competition among capitals drives profit rates toward equality across branches, since capital migrates from low return employments to high return ones until the advantage is competed away. Equal capitals earn roughly equal profits whatever their composition. The first volume's premises and the world's behaviour cannot both be fed into the same calculation.

### What exactly contradicts what between the volumes?

The first volume's law, that goods trade in proportion to embodied labour, contradicts the third volume's finding that they exchange at prices of production, cost price plus average profit, which coincide with labour values only for capitals of average composition and diverge from them systematically everywhere else.

Marx's resolution, presented in the third volume, runs as follows. Take the surplus value produced across the whole economy as a single pool. Competition divides that pool among capitals in proportion to their size, not in proportion to the labour each employs, so every capital receives the average rate of profit on its advance. Prices then form as cost price plus this average profit, and such prices, prices of production, deviate from values in both directions: commodities from capital rich branches sell above their values, commodities from labour rich branches below. Two aggregate identities are preserved through the redistribution. The sum of all prices of production equals the sum of all values, and the sum of all profits equals the sum of all surplus values. Nothing is created or lost in the transfer; the pool is merely shared out on a different principle than the one that filled it.

Bohm-Bawerk's verdict on this resolution is the core of his first argument, and it is more precise than the slogan of contradiction suggests. He does not deny that the aggregates can be made to match; the matching is built into the construction, since the average rate is computed from the totals. What he denies is that a law of exchange survives the operation. The first volume's claim was about individual commodities: this coat exchanges for that linen because the labour in them stands in a definite proportion. The third volume replaces that claim with a claim about totals, which no individual exchange ever tests. A theory that began by explaining why one commodity trades for a definite quantity of another ends by explaining only why total prices match total values, a proposition consistent with almost any pattern of individual prices. The reconciliation, he argues, is purchased by abandoning the very assertion that made the theory interesting, and a system that saves its totals by surrendering its law has contradicted itself in substance whatever its bookkeeping shows.

### What do the two aggregate identities preserve?

They preserve the totals through the redistribution of surplus: summed across the economy, prices of production equal values and profits equal surplus value, so the pool of unpaid labour is shared among capitals by size rather than by origin, while no individual commodity need exchange at its value.

The charge gains force from Marx's own earlier language. The deviation of price from value in the first volume is called accidental, a breach of the law of exchange, compensated in the long run by the law's reassertion. In the third volume the deviation is neither accidental nor temporary but systematic, permanent, and required by the theory's own account of competition. Bohm-Bawerk presses the verbal point hard: a deviation that never cancels for any individual commodity, that follows necessarily from the composition of the capital producing it, cannot be described in the vocabulary of accident without emptying that vocabulary. Either the law of the first volume governs exchange, in which case the third volume's prices are impossible, or prices of production govern exchange, in which case the law of the first volume governs nothing observable at the level where exchange happens.

Engels, editing the third volume, had anticipated the difficulty and invited solutions in his preface, offering a prize of sorts to anyone who could reconcile the equal rate of profit with the law of value before the volume appeared. Several attempts were published, and Bohm-Bawerk examines the most ingenious of them, by Conrad Schmidt and others, with courtesy and finds them wanting. His treatment of Werner Sombart's defence, which recast value as a logical fact of thought rather than an empirical regularity, occupies the essay's final chapter and introduces a theme the standard reply would later take up: the retreat from value as a magnitude governing prices to value as a category organising thought. Bohm-Bawerk saw the retreat coming and named its cost in advance. A law of value that is a fact of logic cannot be contradicted by prices, but neither can it explain them, and a theory immunised against the evidence has traded its scientific claim for safety.

## The five capitals: the contradiction in worked form

Abstract statements of the contradiction persuade fewer readers than the arithmetic, so the arithmetic deserves a slow walk in prose. Marx's own illustration in the third volume sets five spheres of production side by side, each working with a capital of the same total size and each exploiting its workers at the same rate, while the proportions of the capitals differ as widely as technique dictates. Follow two of them. In the first sphere, of every hundred units of capital, eighty are laid out on plant, materials, and wear, and twenty on wages. The workers, exploited at a rate that doubles the value their wages represent, produce twenty units of surplus. The commodities of this sphere embody the used up portion of the plant together with the wages and the surplus, and their value comes to ninety. In the third sphere the proportions are reversed toward labour: sixty units stand in plant and materials, forty in wages. The same rate of exploitation now yields forty units of surplus, since twice as many workers are set in motion, and the value of this sphere's commodities rises to one hundred and thirty one. If commodities sold at their values, the first capital would earn a profit rate of twenty per cent on its advance and the third a rate of forty, a spread no competitive economy tolerates.

Now run the third volume's machinery over the same figures. Pool the surplus of all five spheres: it comes to one hundred and ten units against a total capital of five hundred, giving an average rate of profit of twenty two per cent. Let every capital take profit at that uniform rate on its advance, and let prices form as cost price plus the uniform profit. The first sphere's commodities, valued at ninety, now sell at ninety two; the third sphere's, valued at one hundred and thirty one, sell at one hundred and thirteen. The deviations are not noise. They are dictated by composition, positive for the capital heavy sphere, negative and large for the labour heavy one, and they persist for as long as compositions differ, which is to say permanently. Add the five prices together and they return the sum of the five values; add the five profits and they return the pooled surplus. The totals reconcile beautifully. The individual commodities have deserted their values completely, and they have done so not through the accidents of a market day but through the theory's own central mechanism, competition itself.

### What happens to two capitals of different composition in the example?

The labour heavy capital produces twice the surplus of the capital heavy one, yet competition awards both the same rate of profit on their advances, so the first sphere's goods sell above their value and the other's far below, while the totals of price and value still agree.

Bohm-Bawerk lingers over this example because it converts an interpretive quarrel into a demonstration the reader can check. Defenders sometimes present the transformation as a subtlety that hostile readers exaggerate, a matter of emphasis between levels of abstraction. The numbers refuse that presentation. A law that predicts ninety and one hundred and thirty one, in a world that delivers ninety two and one hundred and thirteen by the theory's own corrected account, has not been reinterpreted; it has been replaced at the level where it was stated, and retained only in the aggregates where no single transaction can test it. Whether the replacement is a legitimate deepening or a surrender is the interpretive question the first argument leaves open, and reasonable readers divide on it. That something was replaced is not open, and the example is the proof.

## The reconciliation and its price

Fairness requires the strongest version of the defence before any grading. The defence runs that the movement from values to prices of production is not a retraction but a concretisation, the standard procedure of a science that begins with an abstraction and then restores the complications the abstraction set aside. On this reading the first volume analyses capital in general, where the simplifying assumption that commodities sell at their values isolates the production of surplus value from its distribution. The third volume restores competition among capitals, and the redistribution of the surplus through the average rate of profit is a discovery about how the law operates at the surface, not a confession that the law fails. The totals anchor the surface to the depths: because aggregate profit is aggregate surplus value, the profits of the capital rich branches are explained as transfers from the labour rich ones, and exploitation remains the source of the whole pool even where an individual capitalist's profit bears no proportion to the labour his own workers perform.

This defence has real content, and the grading in the table reflects it. The aggregate identities are not nothing. They connect the distribution of profit to the production of surplus in a way that a pure price theory does not attempt, and they give the theory an answer, at the level of the whole economy, to the question where profit comes from. Critics who repeat the contradiction slogan without acknowledging the identities are arguing against a caricature. Bohm-Bawerk himself did not make that mistake: his argument concedes the identities and attacks their sufficiency, which is why his version of the charge has outlasted cruder ones.

The price of the reconciliation is nonetheless real, and it can be stated without polemic. What the first volume promised was a regulator of exchange ratios, a magnitude embodied in each commodity that explains the proportion in which it trades. What the third volume delivers is a regulator of totals. Between those two deliverables lies the whole distance the first argument measures. Defenders who accept the distance and reconceive the theory accordingly, as an account of where the profit pool socially originates rather than a mechanics of relative prices, have in effect accepted Bohm-Bawerk's description while declining his tone. Defenders who deny the distance owe an account of individual prices that the theory, after the transformation, does not supply. The technical sequel, in which Bortkiewicz and his successors formalised the transformation and disputed whether both aggregate identities can hold together once inputs are transformed as well as outputs, is the province of the article on [transformation problem explained](/2016/04/15/transformation-problem-explained/), and is deliberately not pursued here. For the grading of the critique, what matters is the shape of the exchange: a serious charge, a reply with substance, and a residue of surrender that the reply does not deny so much as redescribe.

## Market value: the third volume against itself

The critic's use of the third volume extends beyond the transformation chapters, and one further exhibit shows his method at its most damaging, the recruitment of Marx as witness against Marx. In the third volume's discussion of market value and market price, Marx analyses how the value of a commodity produced under differing conditions is settled by the conditions of the mass of output, and how supply and demand, when they balance, cease to explain anything about the level at which they balance, a level that must be explained by the conditions of production. Bohm-Bawerk fastens on the structure of this reasoning. Where the analysis is concrete, Marx himself explains price by an interplay in which the motives and circumstances of buyers and sellers do real work, and the law of value operates as one determinant among others rather than as the distilled essence of exchange. The critic's question writes itself: if the concrete analysis needs the interplay, what exactly did the abstract derivation at the system's entrance contribute, beyond a substance that the concrete chapters then handle with ever more qualifications? A theory whose foundations are honoured in its preface and bypassed in its practice exhibits the same gap between programme and performance that the contradiction argument finds between its volumes.

This exhibit also answers a defence sometimes raised against the critique in its entirety, that Bohm-Bawerk read Marx with a literalism deaf to the dialectical character of the work, grading a fluid, developmental exposition by the standards of a formal treatise. Whatever that defence is worth against the contradiction argument, it fails against the exhibits drawn from Marx's own analytical practice. The critic's standard, in these passages, is not imported from Vienna; it is Marx's own standard when Marx is explaining an actual market. The demand that the foundation be as good as the practice is one the practice itself authorises.

## The inputs problem: bought at values, sold at prices

Inside the contradiction argument sits a technical sub charge that later literature elevated into the transformation problem proper, and it deserves a statement here at the level of principle, its formal career being reserved to the linked article. Marx's transformation converts the outputs of production from values into prices of production. The inputs of production, the constant capital consumed and the labour power purchased, enter the calculation at their values. But the inputs are themselves outputs of other branches, purchased in the same market where prices of production rule. A sphere that sells its product at a price of production buys its machinery and materials at prices of production too, and its wage goods likewise, so the cost price from which its own price is built should already be transformed. Marx's procedure transforms one side of the ledger and not the other, and the critic fastened on the asymmetry: the system's solution is computed in a hybrid currency, values on the left hand page and prices on the right. Marx was not unaware of the point; a passage in the third volume acknowledges that the cost price of a commodity can stand above or below the value of the means of production consumed in it, and files the complication as a modification to be borne in mind. Bohm-Bawerk's judgement is that the modification is not a detail but the problem itself returning, since once inputs are transformed the aggregate identities that anchored the solution must be rederived, and the rederivation, as Bortkiewicz would show a decade later in the formal literature, cannot preserve both identities together in general. The sub charge thus converts the first argument from an interpretive complaint into a research programme, and its fertility is the strongest evidence for the grade this article assigns the contradiction: a charge that generates a century of formal work is not a slogan, even where the work ends by revising the charge's own terms.

## The joint stock image turned against its author

Marx himself supplied the image that best exposes the transformation's cost, and the critic's use of it is a small masterclass in arguing from an opponent's text. To explain how profit detaches from the labour a capital employs, Marx compares the capitalists of the different spheres to members of a joint stock company, in which the total profit is divided as a dividend in proportion to the shares each holds, regardless of which branch of the common undertaking generated it. The image is offered as illumination, and it illuminates more than its author intended. A dividend is not a magnitude produced by the share; it is a claim on a pool, assigned by a rule of the company's constitution. If the profit of an individual capital stands to its own production of surplus as a dividend stands to a branch's earnings, then the explanatory work has moved entirely to the pool and the rule, and the individual magnitudes, the prices at which actual commodities change hands, are explained by the constitution of the whole rather than by anything embodied in the goods. That is, in the critic's vocabulary, the surrender of the law of exchange in favour of a theory of distribution. Defenders accept the description and prize it: the theory of distribution over the pool is precisely the social content they value, the demonstration that each capitalist's income is a share of a class revenue extracted from the class of workers. The image thus divides the dispute's parties by their criteria one final time. Judged as a doctrine of exchange ratios, the joint stock company is a confession. Judged as a theory of class income, it is a triumph. The critique's first argument and the reply's defence are both true of it, at different levels, and the failure of the subsequent literature to keep the levels separate is the joint stock image's revenge on both.

## The average that is not an average

Buried inside the contradiction argument is a smaller, sharper charge about a single word, and it repays extraction because it shows the critic's quality at its best. Marx, presenting price's divergence from value, reaches for the language of averages: deviations cancel, prices oscillate around values, the law asserts itself through the fluctuations as through a medium. Bohm-Bawerk replies that two entirely different things are being sheltered under one word. There is the average of fluctuations, where a magnitude varies around a centre it genuinely tends toward, as the price of a harvest commodity varies around the level its costs impose across good years and bad. And there is the arithmetical mean struck between permanently and fundamentally unequal quantities, a number that can always be computed and that explains nothing, since the quantities do not tend toward it, do not oscillate around it, and are not governed by it in any way.

His illustration is deliberately crude. A coat sells in one shop at forty shillings and in another at sixty; a mathematician may strike an average of fifty, and the deviations from fifty cancel each other exactly, by construction. Nothing has been learned. The average is a property of the arithmetic, not of the coats. Apply the point to the transformation and the aggregate identities change their aspect. That total price equals total value is guaranteed by the way the average rate of profit is computed, from the totals themselves, and a guarantee manufactured by the computation cannot then be cited as the law's confirmation in the world. The cancelling of deviations, which in the first volume described a real tendency of market prices to gravitate, has become in the third a bookkeeping identity, true by definition and empty of the tendency it once named. Bohm-Bawerk's verdict is that Marx confounds the two averages, trading on the plausibility of the first to sell the second.

### What is wrong with calling prices of production an average of values?

An average of fluctuations describes a centre that real prices tend toward, while the mean of permanently divergent prices is only an arithmetical construction; the transformation's totals agree by the manner of their computation, so their agreement cannot confirm a law of gravitation that no individual price obeys.

The charge matters beyond its immediate target because it identifies a manoeuvre that recurs in theoretical disputes of every school: the migration of a term from a sense in which it carries evidence to a sense in which it carries none, with the argument's confidence travelling unchanged across the border. Readers of the reply will search it in vain for an answer to the two averages distinction. Hilferding defends the transformation as a social process, competition really does redistribute the surplus, and that defence is responsive to the charge of inconsistency at the level of mechanism. But the epistemological point, that an identity produced by construction cannot testify for a law, stands apart from the mechanism's reality, and it is a point the standard reply never takes up. In the grading of this article the distinction counts as a sub argument of the first, won by the critique and conceded by silence.

## The historical escape hatch: simple commodity production

Marx left himself an exit from the contradiction, and Bohm-Bawerk follows him through it in order to close it. The exit is historical. In societies where capitalist production does not yet dominate, where the workers own their means of production as peasant proprietors and independent artisans, commodities do exchange according to their values, because there is no mass of capital demanding an average return and no competition among capitals to enforce one. The independent producer keeps the whole product of his labour; differences in the return to his day's work between trades are, for him, a matter of comparative indifference beside the security of his independence, rather as a wage labourer is indifferent to the rate of profit his employer extracts from him. Values, Marx concludes, are not only theoretically but also historically prior to prices of production: the law governed exchange in the long prehistory of the commodity, and the capitalist transformation overlays it without abolishing its foundation.

The manoeuvre is elegant, and it converts an apparent refutation into a periodisation. Bohm-Bawerk attacks both its history and its logic. As history, the claim that artisan exchange followed embodied labour times is an assertion without evidence, and the probabilities run against it. The artisan's product embodies not only his labour but the scarcity of his materials, the favour of his location, the reputation of his workshop, and the urgency of his customers, and no mechanism in a society of independent producers forces the price toward labour time when a coveted material or a fashionable maker can command a premium that persists for a career. Differences of skill alone break the rule at once: the master craftsman's day embodies, on any reckoning the market will validate, more than the journeyman's, and the conversion of skilled days into multiples of simple days is performed by the very prices the law was supposed to determine. As logic, the exit concedes the critic's main point for the society the theory was built to explain. A law of value that governs exchange only where capitalism is absent, and is suspended by the arrival of the system whose laws Capital professes to discover, has an odd claim to be that system's foundation. The foundation holds up a vanished building.

The deeper difficulty is the indifference premise, the claim that independent producers tolerate permanently unequal returns to equal labour because profit rates do not interest them. Bohm-Bawerk answers that the height of the return is never a matter of indifference to the persons who receive it, whatever their legal form. An artisan whose trade yields half the income of a neighbouring trade for the same exertion faces a standing invitation to change his trade, to apprentice his sons elsewhere, to let his own line die with him. Entry and exit among crafts perform, slowly and without any capitalist, the same levelling that competition among capitals performs quickly, and a society in which returns to labour differed permanently and grossly between occupations, without movement between them, would require barriers to movement that the picture of independent producers does not supply. The escape hatch thus fails twice: the prehistory it invokes would not have obeyed the law either, and the mechanism it relies on, indifference, is one nobody who has watched a craftsman choose a trade for his child can credit.

It is worth pausing on the dialectical shape of this exchange, because it previews the whole dispute's later career. A theory meets a falsifying observation. The theory replies by restricting its claim to a domain where the observation cannot be made, a vanished past or an abstract level. The restriction may be legitimate; sciences do delimit their laws. But each restriction must be paid for in the coin of scope, and the accounts must be kept honestly. The law of value after the historical restriction governs a world that no longer exists and an abstraction that cannot be observed, while the world that exists is governed by prices of production. Bohm-Bawerk's bookkeeping of these payments, his insistence that each rescue be entered as a cost, is the habit of mind that makes his essay still worth reading when its polemical occasion has faded. The standard reply, for its part, treats the historical claim briefly and without enthusiasm, as though sensing that this limb of the defence bears less weight than the others.

## The second argument: a sieve that keeps what it was fed

Suppose the premise is granted, that exchange does imply a common element of equal magnitude in the things exchanged. Marx's derivation must still show that the element is labour, and it proceeds by elimination: survey the properties commodities share, strike out each that cannot serve, and crown the survivor. Bohm-Bawerk's second argument examines the survey and finds it rigged twice over, once in the choice of what enters the field and once in the treatment of the candidates that do.

The first rigging concerns the field itself. The search for the common factor is confined from the outset to commodities, defined as labour's products destined for the market. But exchange value attaches to far more than labour products. Land sells, and sells at prices that behave lawfully, capitalising the rent it yields at the going rate of interest. Standing forests, mineral deposits, waterfalls, and building sites all bear prices without embodying a stroke of labour. If a common factor explains why exchangeable things stand in definite ratios, that factor must be present in everything that exchanges, soil included. By restricting the inquiry to goods that are labour products by definition, the derivation guarantees its conclusion before the elimination begins. A natural philosopher who sought the cause of weight by examining only transparent bodies, and announced transparency as the cause because no other property of transparent bodies survived his sieve, would be laughed at; the procedure here, Bohm-Bawerk argues, is the same procedure wearing a dialectical coat.

### Which common properties did the critique say were passed over?

Scarcity, being an object of demand, and being appropriated were all named as properties shared by exchangeable goods quite as widely as being produced by labour, alongside usefulness itself, and the derivation excludes each without argument, by abstraction or by silence, rather than by any test that labour alone passes.

The second rigging concerns the candidates inside the field. Even among labour products, being produced by labour is not the only universal property. Commodities are also, all of them, useful, since a thing nobody wants bears no price; they are scarce relative to the demand for them, since a good available without limit, like air in ordinary circumstances, is a free good and no commodity; they are objects of demand; they are appropriated, held as property that must be surrendered by an owner. Marx's elimination deals with usefulness by declaring that exchange abstracts from it, but abstraction is a decision of the analyst, not a discovery about the goods, and the same gesture could retire any candidate whatever, labour included. The other candidates are not retired even by gesture. They are simply never admitted to the survey. An elimination that works by declining to list the rivals does not establish its survivor; it exhibits a preference and calls the exhibition a proof.

Circularity completes the charge. The derivation, even on its own terms, reaches only reproducible goods, commodities that can be produced again at a measurable cost in labour time. A unique painting, a rare stamp, an antique, a plot in a favoured district: these bear prices, sometimes enormous ones, with no socially necessary labour time behind them that could regulate the figure. Marx concedes the restriction in places, treating such prices as irrational forms or as governed by other principles. Bohm-Bawerk draws the consequence. A derivation of the substance of value that covers only the goods whose prices the labour theory can already explain, and steps aside from the rest, has assumed the shape of its conclusion in the drawing of its boundaries. The theory of value was supposed to explain exchange value wherever exchange value appears. A proof that works only where the theory works is not a proof of the theory.

### Why does the sieve only reach reproducible goods?

Because labour time can regulate a price only where production can be repeated and cost compared, so unique objects, rare survivals, and gifts of nature fall outside the derivation entirely, and a search for the common substance of exchange value that excludes them has drawn its boundaries around its conclusion.

How damaging is this second argument? Less than the third, for a reason worth stating. A defective proof of a proposition does not falsify the proposition. The labour theory might be true, or illuminating, even if the passage that introduces it argues badly, and defenders have accordingly treated the derivation as dispensable: a heuristic entrance, a piece of Hegelian stagecraft, a summary in dialectical dress of conclusions established elsewhere by the analysis of production. That treatment is available, and many sympathetic readers take it, as a later section records. But notice what the treatment concedes. It concedes that the opening chapter's most famous argument does not bear the weight placed on it, and it relocates the theory's foundations to ground the critique's other arguments then contest. The second argument thus does its work even when it is granted: it strips the derivation of its status as a demonstration and demotes it to an announcement. What remains standing after the demotion is the premise the derivation began from, the claim that exchange implies equality, and that premise is the third argument's target.

## The candidates at the gate

The derivation argument deserves a second, slower pass, because its full strength appears only when each excluded candidate is given its hearing. Marx's elimination retires use value by abstraction and never admits the remaining candidates at all. Bohm-Bawerk, following an objection he credits to Karl Knies, lines the candidates up at the gate of the sieve and asks why each was turned away.

Scarcity stands first. Every commodity, without exception, is scarce relative to the wants directed at it; that is what distinguishes a commodity from a free good. Air, the standard instance, is supremely useful and utterly valueless in exchange because it is not scarce; let it become scarce, in a diving bell or a besieged city, and it acquires a price at once. A property present in all priced goods and absent in the paradigmatic unpriced one has at least as good a claim to be the common factor as labour has, and Marx's survey never tests it. Being an object of demand stands beside scarcity: nothing exchanges that nobody wants at the offered terms, and the intensity of want visibly moves ratios in every market. Appropriation stands third: commodities are owned, and exchange is the transfer of ownership, so the property of being held as exclusive property is universal among them in a way that invites the same eliminative logic. Utility itself, the candidate Marx does retire, is retired by a stipulation, that exchange regards goods only as quantities, which the parties' behaviour contradicts at every step, since each party's whole reason for entering the exchange is a judgement about the usefulness of what he will get against what he will give.

The Knies objection sharpens the field restriction into an equation Marx cannot write. If exchange proportions express a common factor of equal amount, then the equation should hold for every pair of exchangeable things, and Knies proposed the test case: a quarter of wheat set equal to some hundredweights of wild grown wood and to some acres of virgin soil. The wood was never laboured on; the soil is nature entire. Both bear prices, both enter equations with the wheat, and the common factor, if the derivation is sound, must be found in them as surely as in the wheat. Labour is not there. The derivation can escape only by declaring that such equations fall outside its field, which is to confess that the field was drawn to contain the conclusion. Bohm-Bawerk's image for the procedure is the urn: a man determined to draw a white ball from an urn makes certain of his result by putting white balls only into it, and the property of having issued from labour is distilled as the common property of a group from which every exchangeable thing lacking that property has first been removed.

### Why does the price of land embarrass the derivation?

Land embodies no labour, yet it bears a lawful price, behaving as a capitalised multiple of its rent at the prevailing rate of interest; a common factor meant to explain why priced things stand in definite ratios must be present in land too, and labour is absent there.

None of this is answered by observing that Marxist theory has resources elsewhere for land, in the theory of rent, where price of land is treated as capitalised rent and rent itself is traced to surplus produced elsewhere in the system. That treatment may be sound economics; it is not a repair of the derivation. The derivation claimed to find, in the form of exchange as such, the substance that all exchange values share. A substance that must be supplemented, for an enormous class of priced things, by a second theory explaining their prices as claims on value produced elsewhere, was never the universal common factor the elimination announced. The point is about what the opening chapter proved, not about what the system as a whole can explain, and keeping those apart is the discipline this article maintains. Judged as a proof, the elimination fails on its exclusions. Judged as a pointer toward the analysis of production, it survives as rhetoric. The second argument's grade in the table records exactly that split verdict.

## Skilled hands and the reduction problem

A further count in the derivation charge concerns not what the sieve excludes but what it silently assumes: the homogeneity of the labour that survives it. The derivation's residue is abstract human labour, labour stripped of every concrete difference, the spinner's hour and the jeweller's hour rendered identical as expenditures of labour power in general. Exchange, however, confronts the products of labours that differ enormously in skill, training, and scarcity, and the theory must convert those differing labours into multiples of a common simple labour before the law can operate. Marx acknowledges the conversion and assigns it to a social process established behind the backs of the producers: complex labour counts as multiplied simple labour, in proportions fixed by custom and competition. Bohm-Bawerk asks the obvious question. By what measure are the proportions fixed? If the market's valuations perform the conversion, the wage of the jeweller standing at some multiple of the spinner's wage because jewellers are valued more highly, then the values the theory set out to explain have been used to construct the unit in which value is measured, and the explanation travels in a circle. If some other process performs it, the theory owes an account of that process which it never gives.

The point is not a pedant's quibble about units. Every quantitative theory needs its unit to be independent of the magnitudes it measures; a thermometer whose scale was set by the temperatures it then reports would certify nothing. The labour theory's unit, the hour of simple labour, is defined through reductions whose coefficients are observable only in the exchange relations under explanation. Defenders have answered that the reduction is a real social process, the outcome of training costs and labour market competition, and that its coefficients can in principle be grounded in the labour required to produce the skill itself, the teacher's hours and the student's forgone production embodied in the trained worker like the cost of a machine. That answer, developed by later writers more than by Marx, moves the theory toward a cost of production account in which skilled labour is a produced input with a production history. Whether it escapes the circle is contested, since the training account must still value the student's maintenance and the teacher's time, and the regress is stopped by convention rather than by demonstration. For the grading of the 1896 critique, the point stands as part of the second argument's territory: the derivation delivers a homogeneous substance only by assuming conversions it cannot perform from its own resources.

There is a companion difficulty about intensity and about the social necessity that qualifies the labour time. Labour counts toward value only insofar as it is socially necessary, performed with average skill and intensity under normal conditions. Work done slowly, or on products nobody wants, creates no value however long it lasts. Bohm-Bawerk notes the escape clause's convenience: whenever embodied labour and price part company, the labour can be declared not socially necessary after the fact, and a law equipped with a clause that reclassifies every counterexample as outside its scope has insured itself against refutation at the price of content. The demand side, banished from the derivation as use value, returns through the back door wearing the mask of social necessity, since whether labour was necessary depends on whether its product finds buyers in the right proportions. The theory that began by abstracting from want ends by smuggling want into its central magnitude, unacknowledged. Later defenders embraced the consequence openly, treating value as constituted in exchange rather than merely expressed there, which is again a relocation: the substance that was supposed to be embodied in production turns out to be conferred by the market, and the opening chapter's direction of explanation quietly reverses.

## Use value's revenge

One thread of the derivation argument deserves to be pulled separately, because it connects the logical charge to the economic substance. Marx's elimination retires use value on the ground that the exchange relation abstracts from it: within the relation, goods count only as quantities of exchange value, their particular usefulness set aside. Bohm-Bawerk's objection is that the abstraction misdescribes the phenomenon at the exact point where the phenomenon does its work. A good's exchange value is conditional on its use value to others; a thing useful to nobody commands no price however much labour it embodies, a fact Marx himself states when he remarks that nothing can have value without being an object of utility. The parties to an exchange do not set usefulness aside. Each consults nothing else, comparing the usefulness of what he would get against the usefulness of what he would give, in his own circumstances. To abstract from use value in analysing exchange is to abstract from the motive force of the transaction, and then to wonder at the residue.

The point ramifies through the theory's treatment of demand. Because the derivation banishes want at the entrance, the completed theory must readmit it through qualifications: labour creates value only when its product is wanted, only when the labour was socially necessary, only when the proportions of social labour allocated to the branch match the social need for its product. Each qualification is reasonable; together they amount to the confession that the magnitude of value is codetermined by the demand side the derivation excluded. The marginalist construction begins where the qualifications end, with want and scarcity as the primitives, and derives the role of production as a constraint within a theory of valuation. Bohm-Bawerk's deepest suggestion, never quite stated as a slogan, is that the two theories differ in the order of their explanations and that Marx's order cannot be sustained: production explains the supply of goods, but the ratios at which they exchange are fixed where supply meets the valuations of those who want them, and no property deposited in the goods during production can settle that meeting by itself.

## Two senses of equal

The inequality argument can be given a linguistic edge that sharpens its point. The word equal, applied to an exchange, slides between two senses. In the first sense it means identical in magnitude: two lengths are equal, two weights are equal, and the assertion can be checked by a measure that both instantiate. In the second sense it means equivalent for the purposes of a transaction: the parties treat the bundles as exchangeable at par, the way a bank treats a note as equal to the coin it promises, without any claim that the paper and the metal share a magnitude of anything. Marx's derivation requires the first sense. The equation between wheat and iron must assert an identity of magnitude in some respect, or the inference to a common measure of that magnitude has nothing to fasten on. But the only equality an exchange itself supplies is the second sense, the practical equivalence established by the parties' willingness to trade at the stated terms. Bohm-Bawerk's charge, restated in this vocabulary, is an equivocation charge: the derivation borrows the dignity of the first sense from a notation, the equals sign, that the phenomenon fills only with the second. Once the senses are separated, the search for the common substance loses its warrant, since practical equivalence between bundles needs no shared magnitude behind it, any more than the equivalence of a ticket and a journey needs a substance common to paper and motion.

Defenders sometimes respond that the equivocation charge itself equivocates, because Marx's claim is not about the psychology of a single trade but about the system of ratios as a whole, whose stability across millions of transactions demands a regulator, and a regulator is a magnitude in the first sense. That response is the strongest available to the derivation, and it should be noticed that it abandons the derivation's own ground: the argument no longer runs from the form of a single exchange to a substance, but from the observed stability of a price system to a hypothesised governor, an empirical and theoretical claim of a different kind. It is, in short, the defeater's road, the demonstration that systematic regularity requires a production side magnitude. The response therefore confirms this article's grading from the defenders' side: to answer the inequality argument at all, one must leave the opening chapter and do the work the defeater specifies. The chapter itself, read as it presents itself, does not survive the separation of the two senses of equal.

## The unit problem: what kind of magnitude is labour time?

One more foundational question underlies the dispute and surfaces in both texts without ever being isolated: what sort of magnitude is labour time supposed to be? A measure must be homogeneous, additive, and independent of the things measured. Clock time satisfies the conditions trivially, which is the labour theory's attraction: hours are hours. But the theory's hours are not clock hours. They are hours of abstract labour, of average intensity, of socially necessary duration, performed by labour of normalised skill, and each qualifier imports a determination that the clock cannot supply. Intensity varies and is normalised by the pace a society enforces, which is a fact about its labour process and its markets. Skill is normalised by reductions whose coefficients, as the earlier section showed, the theory cannot generate internally. Social necessity is normalised by the demand the derivation excluded. The unit, fully specified, is a social construction wearing the clock's clothing, and Bohm-Bawerk's scattered remarks on the qualifiers amount to the charge that the theory's celebrated measurability is borrowed: it measures with a ruler whose graduations are drawn by the phenomena under measurement. Marginalism's unit, utility, fails the same tests more frankly, being ordinal, interpersonal comparisons disallowed, a ranking rather than a magnitude at all; the Austrian school wore the failure openly and built accordingly, with ratios emerging from rankings rather than from measured substances. The contrast is instructive for the grading. A theory whose unit is a ranking can still explain ratios, because ratios need only the parties' orderings and their constraints. A theory whose unit purports to be a substance must defend the substance's credentials, and the credentials are what the critique spent its force examining. The examination's result stands at this article's centre, and no subsequent defence has restored them.

## The third argument: exchange without equality

Everything so far has been preliminary to the argument this article exists to grade. Bohm-Bawerk traces the derivation's premise to Aristotle, whom Marx quotes with approval: exchange cannot exist without equality, and equality cannot exist without commensurability. The thought feels ancient and solid. Two things are set equal in exchange; therefore they must be equal in something; therefore a common measure of that something must exist; therefore the hunt for the common substance is legitimate and its quarry real. The third argument denies the first step. An exchange, it holds, is not an equation between equals at all, and the appearance of equality in the written form, so much of one good against so much of another, conceals the actual mechanism, which runs on inequality from beginning to end.

Consider what must hold in the minds of the two parties for a trade to happen. The holder of wheat who surrenders a quarter of it for iron must prefer the iron to the wheat at the margin, or he will keep his grain. The holder of iron who surrenders a hundredweight must prefer the wheat to the iron, or she will keep her metal. Each gives up something valued less in order to obtain something valued more. If the two bundles were equal in the estimation of both parties, neither would gain by the swap, neither would bear its trouble and risk, and no exchange would occur. Trade is not the meeting of equals. Trade is the exploitation of a divergence: the same goods carry different subjective valuations in different hands, and the movement of goods from lower valued uses to higher valued ones is the whole phenomenon to be explained. The equality sign in the economist's equation records the terms on which the movement stops, not a sameness of substance that causes it.

Bohm-Bawerk states the point in his own words, and the words deserve quotation because paraphrase usually softens them: "Where equality and exact equilibrium obtain, no change is likely to occur". A change of ownership, he continues, points rather to some inequality or preponderance that produces the alteration, as chemical combination follows not from equal affinities but from stronger ones displacing weaker. The scholastic doctrine of equivalence in exchange, the idea that justice or logic requires the things traded to be of equal value, he treats as an inheritance the science should discard. Modern economists of his school agreed: the parties to an exchange are moved by opposed valuations, and the ratio at which they settle is a resultant of those valuations, not the disclosure of a common magnitude hiding inside both goods.

### What is the strongest argument in Bohm-Bawerk's critique?

that exchange requires no equality at all. Each party gives up something they value less for something they value more, so the divergence of valuations is what makes the exchange happen, and the search for a common substance in which the goods are equal seeks something the transaction does not require.

### Why would nobody trade equals for equals?

Because exchange costs effort, time, and risk, and a swap of things held to be exactly equal offers neither party any gain to set against those costs, so the goods stay where they are; movement begins only when each side values what it receives above what it surrenders.

The argument's target must be fixed precisely, because its force depends on it. The inequality argument does not, by itself, disprove the claim that labour time regulates prices in some statistical or long run sense; a regulator could operate behind the parties' backs, through competition and the mobility of capital, whatever the parties think they are doing. What the argument destroys is the derivation's route to that claim. Marx's opening chapter does not argue from competition or from the behaviour of producers. It argues from the form of exchange itself: because goods equate in exchange, some shared element of equal magnitude must exist in them, and the only candidate is labour. If the equation expresses no equality of magnitude, the inference to a common element collapses, and with it the only proof the first chapter offers. The labour theory is left as an assertion in search of a foundation, to be supported, if at all, by arguments of a wholly different kind about production, costs, and the gravitation of prices.

This is the sense of the claim at the centre of this article: the inequality argument is the one that bites. It bites because it is untouched by the manoeuvre that defuses the others. The contradiction charge can be met by pointing at aggregates. The derivation charge can be met by demoting the passage to exposition. The empirical charge can be met by invoking the equalisation of profit rates. The inequality charge meets a different fate: the standard reply does not engage it at all, but redefines the theory so that the charge no longer applies, conceding in the process that value theory is not chiefly concerned with exchange ratios. Yet the opening chapter's argument is conducted entirely in terms of exchange ratios; it begins from a proportion between quantities of goods and infers a substance from that proportion. A defence that abandons the ratios abandons the chapter. The reply and the charge pass each other without contact, and a charge that passes undeflected through the best defence its target ever produced is the strongest charge in the set.

One refinement protects the argument from a common misreading. Bohm-Bawerk does not claim that the parties' valuations are arbitrary, or that exchange ratios float free of all constraint. Costs, scarcity, and the conditions of production hem valuations in, and his own positive theory gives production a large role in determining where ratios settle. The claim is narrower and purely logical: whatever regulates ratios, the regulation cannot be inferred from a supposed equality inside the exchange, because no such equality exists. The pursuit of a shared substance, in which both goods come out equal, pursues an item the bargain never needed, and a science of exchange should start from the divergence that moves goods rather than from an identity that would freeze them in place.

## The method refused: motives, psychology, and the road not taken

Behind the logical charges stands a methodological one, and Bohm-Bawerk states it with unusual sharpness: Marx, he argues, studiously avoided the two methods by which a proposition like the law of value could actually be established. A claim connecting exchange ratios to quantities of labour could be supported empirically, by showing that prices in fact track labour contents across the run of goods and over time. Or it could be supported psychologically, by tracing the motives of the parties to production and exchange and showing how those motives, operating through competition, drive ratios toward labour proportions. Marx attempts neither in the foundational argument. The empirical route is left to assertion and illustration. The psychological route is not merely neglected but declined, in a remark Bohm-Bawerk quotes from the third volume, where the deeper analysis of demand and supply, the very forces through which motives act on price, is set aside as not apposite to the inquiry at hand. In place of both routes Marx substitutes the dialectical deduction from the concept of exchange, the derivation, whose failure under examination is the critique's central exhibit.

The charge of avoidance is then pressed into an accusation about motive, and this is the one place where the essay's courtesy thins. Bohm-Bawerk suggests that the psychological route was avoided because its results were known in advance to be unfavourable: an honest analysis of why people exchange leads to the divergent valuations of the marginalist account, not to a common substance, and the dialectical method was retained precisely because it could be steered to the destination the empirical and psychological methods would not reach. He calls the derivation a subtle and artificial afterthought, contrived to make a preconceived opinion appear as the outcome of prolonged investigation. Readers will grade that accusation for themselves; it attributes design where intellectual habit, the Hegelian formation Marx shared with his generation of German thinkers, might suffice as explanation. But the methodological core of the charge does not depend on the attribution. A foundational proposition supported by none of the methods that could support it, and supported instead by a deduction whose steps fail under scrutiny, is a proposition held on other grounds than its stated proof, and a science is entitled to ask what those grounds are.

### Why did the critique say Marx avoided the motives of exchange?

Because tracing why parties actually trade, each seeking what they value more than what they surrender, leads toward divergent subjective valuations and away from any common substance, so the psychological analysis of motives threatened the conclusion the dialectical derivation was built to reach.

The methodological dispute outlived both parties and organises much of the later debate. The Austrian school made the analysis of individual action and subjective valuation its founding discipline, while the Marxist tradition, following Hilferding, made the refusal of that starting point its own: the individual and his preferences are themselves social products, and a method that begins from them begins in the middle of the story. Each side's methodological commitment guarantees its substantive conclusion with suspicious completeness, which is why the controversy of 1896 and 1904 rewards study as a case in the philosophy of economics and not only in its history. The grading practised in this article has tried to keep one foot outside both commitments, by asking of each argument the narrow question whether it engages its stated target. On that austere standard the methodological charge scores a partial success: it correctly identifies that the derivation substitutes for the missing empirical and psychological supports, while its stronger insinuation, that the substitution was a conscious stratagem, exceeds what the texts can prove.

## What the marginalist account puts in place of a substance

An argument that removes a foundation owes the reader the rival construction, stated fairly. On the marginalist account that Bohm-Bawerk inherited from Menger and Jevons, value is not a substance embodied in goods but a relation between a good and a valuing mind. Goods matter to people because they satisfy wants, wants differ in intensity, and the satisfactions secured by successive units of a good diminish as the stock grows. The value of a unit is therefore set at the margin, by the least urgent satisfaction that the available stock still covers. Two people facing the same goods rank them differently because their stocks, needs, and circumstances differ, and from that difference exchange is born: each can gain by surrendering units that stand low in his own ranking for units that stand high.

Price, on this account, forms inside a range. The wheat holder will not part with his quarter for less iron than the wheat is worth to him; the iron holder will not part with her hundredweight for less wheat than the iron is worth to her; between those limits the bargain settles where competition, numbers, and bargaining strength push it. Nothing in the mechanism requires or reveals a common substance. Ratios are explained by the interplay of subjective valuations with objective conditions, the stocks available, the costs of bringing more to market, the urgency of the wants in play. The systematic comparison of this construction with Marx's, concept by concept, is the business of the article on [the comparison of Marx with marginalist value theory](/2010/09/15/marx-vs-marginalist-theory-of-value/), and only its bearing on the inequality argument is in view here.

### What fixes an exchange ratio on the marginalist account?

The opposed valuations of the parties at the margin, bounded by their stocks and by what each surrender costs them, with competition narrowing the range in which the bargain can settle; no equality between the goods is posited, and no substance common to both is invoked.

The bearing is this. Marginalism does not merely assert that exchange runs on unequal valuations; it builds a complete theory of price on that assertion, dispensing with any common substance from the first page. Whether the construction succeeds is a separate question, and its own difficulties, with aggregation, with capital, with the explanation of distribution, are considerable and were pressed hard by later critics. But its existence changes the dialectical position. Marx's derivation asked: exchange equates goods, so what are they equal in? The marginalist answers: the question rests on a false description of exchange, and here is a theory that describes it correctly and explains ratios without the supposed equality. To defeat that answer, a defender of the derivation must show that something in the phenomenon still demands the common substance, some regularity that divergent valuations cannot explain. That showing is exactly what the verdict defeater, stated in a later section, requires, and exactly what the standard reply never attempts.

## The diamond and the water: the paradox the margin dissolved

The marginalist alternative that underwrites the inequality argument had its own founding puzzle, and a reader should see it worked once, because its solution is the positive content behind the critique's negative point. Classical economics had been embarrassed for a century by the paradox that water, supremely useful, is cheap, while diamonds, frivolous, are dear. A theory tying value to usefulness in bulk could not explain the inversion; a theory tying value to labour could explain it only where labour contents happened to align, and stumbled over the unworked diamond found by chance and the worked trinket that nobody wants. The margin dissolves the paradox by relocating value from the class of goods to the unit in the concrete situation of a valuer. Water is valued unit by unit against the most pressing uses first; because it is abundant, the marginal unit serves a trivial want and is priced accordingly, while the total usefulness of all the water in a life remains immeasurable. Diamonds, being scarce, are valued at a margin where wants are still intense. Price reflects the marginal significance, not the total, and the inversion ceases to be paradoxical.

Bohm-Bawerk's role in this story is that of the systematiser who carried the marginal principle into the theory of capital and distribution, the fields where Marx's construction was strongest. The inequality argument is the marginal principle applied to the act of exchange itself: as the value of a good is fixed at the margin of a stock for a valuer, so an exchange is moved by the marginal estimates of two valuers facing each other, and in neither case does a substance common to the objects enter the explanation. The critique of Marx and the positive theory are one intellectual gesture, the substitution of relations among valuing persons for properties inhering in things. That is why the essay cannot be answered by pointing out, correctly, that Marx too was a relational thinker about value in his social register. The dispute is over which relations do the explaining in exchange: relations between persons and goods, ranked by want and scarcity, or relations among producers, mediated by a substance their labours deposit in things. The opening chapter asserts the second by means of the equation. The third argument replies that the equation, honestly read, already contains the first.

## The horse market: the critic's own illustration

Bohm-Bawerk's positive account of price has a canonical illustration, worked in his larger treatise on capital, and no reader can grade the inequality argument fairly without watching the machinery it belongs to actually run. Imagine a market for horses in a small town. A number of sellers bring animals, each with a floor below which he will not part with his horse, set by what the animal is worth to him in his own stable. A number of buyers attend, each with a ceiling above which he will not go, set by what a horse is worth in his own service. Rank the buyers by their ceilings, highest first, and the sellers by their floors, lowest first. Trade occurs between those whose ceilings exceed their partners' floors, and it stops where the ranking crosses: the last pair that can still trade profitably, and the first pair that cannot, fix the zone within which the market price must settle, narrowed further when competition among the included parties is keen. The price that emerges is nobody's valuation and everybody's outcome. It expresses no substance in the horses. It is the resultant of divergent estimates, ranked and cut off by the numbers on each side.

The illustration's bearing on the critique is direct. Here is a definite exchange ratio, the very phenomenon Marx's derivation starts from, produced in full view by a mechanism in which the exchanged things are equal in nothing at all: not in the seller's estimate, where the money outweighs the horse, nor in the buyer's, where the horse outweighs the money, nor in any third magnitude the analyst can extract from the animal. The ratio is definite because the estimates are definite and the competition structured, not because a common substance measures both sides. A defender of the derivation who confronts the horse market must either find the hidden equality the mechanism never mentions or concede that definiteness of ratio does not imply equality of substance, which is the inequality argument's whole contention. The illustration also displays the rival theory's characteristic honesty about its limits: the mechanism fixes a zone, not a point, and the exact resting place within the zone is surrendered to bargaining and circumstance. The labour theory promised a point, a magnitude, a law. The promise was grander, and the grandeur was the vulnerability, since a theory that promises a point can be refuted by a zone.

## The coat and the linen

Set the rival illustration beside it. Marx's own working example in the opening chapter is twenty yards of linen set equal to one coat, and the entire form analysis unfolds from the pair: the linen expresses its value in the coat, the coat serves as the material in which value appears, and the polarity of relative and equivalent forms is developed with a dialectical patience that generations of readers have found either luminous or maddening. Two features of the example deserve notice in the light of the critique. The first is that Marx chooses, as the derivation requires, two reproducible products of straightforward labour, woven cloth and tailored garment, goods for which an embodied labour account is intuitively plausible; the example never risks a Raphael, a rare vintage, or a building plot, the cases where the intuition fails and the derivation's exclusions begin. Examples in theoretical economics are arguments by selection, and the selection here performs the field restriction the second argument charges, at the level of the reader's imagination rather than the text's definitions. The second feature is that the example's real work in Marx's hands is the form analysis, the coat as equivalent, which the critique largely ignores. A reader who approaches the chapter through the example thus meets two texts at once: a derivation of substance that fails under the critic's examination, and an analysis of form that the examination never reaches. The chapter's double character, proof and phenomenology bound in one argument, is the deepest reason the dispute about it has never closed. Each side grades the text it came for, and the texts are different.

## The fourth argument: profit rates refuse to follow labour

The fourth argument is the most empirical of the set and the least independent. If surplus value arises only from labour, and the rate of exploitation is uniform, then a branch of production employing a large share of its capital on wages should generate a large mass of surplus relative to its total advance, and so a high rate of profit, while a branch dominated by machinery and materials should generate little. Bohm-Bawerk states the expectation and sets it against the record: branches do not display profit rates graded by their labour intensity. Capital intensive branches earn what labour intensive branches earn, once the dust of competition settles, and the grading the theory predicts is nowhere observed as a durable feature of economic life.

### What would the theory lead one to expect about profit rates?

Profits proportional to the labour each capital sets in motion, hence high rates where wages form a large share of the advance and low rates where machinery and materials dominate, a durable grading of returns by labour intensity across the branches of production.

The theory's answer is ready made, and it is the machinery of the first argument: competition equalises the rates by transferring surplus through prices of production from the branches that produce much of it to the branches that produce little. Bohm-Bawerk's point is that the answer consumes the theory it defends. Equalisation explains the record only by establishing that commodities systematically exchange away from their values, which is the contradiction charge returning through the empirical door. The fourth argument therefore has no force of its own beyond the first; it is the first argument observed from outside, in the data rather than in the text. Its value in the critique is rhetorical and pedagogical. It shows the reader the contradiction wearing the clothes of an observable fact, and it blocks a lazy escape, the suggestion that the tension between the volumes is a scholastic puzzle with no footprint in the world. The footprint is the uniformity of profit rates itself, the most ordinary fact in capitalist life, and the theory must explain that fact by the very divergence its opening volume called a violation of the law of exchange.

## The journals and the guardians

On the continent the dispute ran through identifiable institutions, and their characters shaped its course. The Marxist side was guarded by Die Neue Zeit, the Stuttgart weekly edited by Karl Kautsky, which functioned as the theoretical organ of international social democracy and in whose pages Hilferding's reply appeared in 1904. Kautsky's own popular exposition of Marx's economic doctrines had taught the labour theory to a generation of party readers, which gave the guardians a stake beyond scholarship: the doctrine under attack was the doctrine as the movement's own textbook taught it, derivation included. The academic side spoke through the Archiv für soziale Gesetzgebung und Statistik and the yearbooks of the historical school, venues where Sombart's review and the discussion of the third volume had set the terms. Between the two stood the Austrian Marxists, bilingual in the dispute's two idioms, at home in the university's categories and the party's alike, and it is no accident that the standard reply came from Vienna rather than from Berlin or Stuttgart. Hilferding could read Bohm-Bawerk as a colleague because, in the republic of German letters, he nearly was one. The reply's distinctive combination, orthodox in its verdicts and academic in manner, is the product of that position, and the grading of its manoeuvres should keep the position in view: a writer who must satisfy a party weekly and a university seminar at once will defend on the ground where both audiences can follow, and the social reading of value, preachable in the movement and arguable in the faculty, was that ground. The inequality argument, a point of pure logic about the parties to an exchange, belonged to neither audience's preferred register, and it slipped between the two stools of the reply's situation.

## The evidence of the period: assertion in place of measurement

A modern reader, formed by an economics of datasets, will notice that the entire controversy proceeds almost without evidence in the contemporary sense. The fourth argument asserts that labour intensive branches do not earn higher profits, a claim about the world that neither side supports with anything a later age would call data: no tabulation of rates of return by industry, no census of compositions, only the shared impression of informed observers that competition equalises returns, an impression both sides accept because both need it, Marx for the transformation and the critic for the refutation. The empirical studies that the defeater's satisfaction would require, built on input and output tables that did not exist until the middle of the twentieth century, were beyond the period's instruments. This is worth recording in the grading because it explains the dispute's logical character. Unable to measure, the parties argued from the form of the theory and the testimony of arithmetic examples, and the argument that could be settled at that level was settled: the derivation fell. The argument that needed measurement, whether labour contents in fact govern ratios once rival costs are given their chance, could not be settled, and was not, and the appearance that the whole question had been decided in 1896 is an artifact of the period's instruments. A dispute's decidable part is not its whole, and the critique's reputation for finality rests on confusing the two.

## The standard reply: value as a social category

The reply that defined the Marxist defence appeared in 1904, written by Rudolf Hilferding, later the author of Finance Capital and a finance minister of the Weimar Republic, then a young theorist answering on the movement's behalf. Its full title announces its method: a criticism of the critic, proceeding through Bohm-Bawerk's arguments in order and contesting his reading of Marx at every step. Neutrality in grading requires the reply to be given at full strength before it is assessed, and its strength is genuine. Generations of students have met the critique through Hilferding's framing, and the framing survives because parts of it are right.

The reply's foundation is a claim about what kind of theory the labour theory is. Value, for Hilferding, is a social category, not a technical one. The labour theory does not compete with marginalism as a rival mechanics of price formation, because its subject is different: it analyses the social relations that organise production in a society of private producers, the way the labour of isolated individuals becomes social labour only through the exchange of their products, and the domination of producers by the movement of the things they produce. To judge such a theory as a failed contribution to price theory is, on the reply's account, a category error, like judging a theory of the state as a failed contribution to engineering. Bohm-Bawerk, a methodological individualist, begins from the valuing individual and his preferences. Historical materialism begins from the society that forms the individual, and asks where the preferences, the property relations, and the very form of exchange come from. Marginal utility theory takes all of that as given. The labour theory explains it. The two theories are not answers to one question; they are answers to different questions, and the critique's appearance of refutation arises from grading one theory by the other's examination paper.

### What did the reply claim value to be?

A social category rather than a technical magnitude: the form in which the labour of private producers becomes social, the relation that binds a society of independent producers together through exchange, and therefore a key to the relations of production rather than a formula for computing prices.

Developed, the reply has several working parts, and each deserves its due. Against the contradiction charge, Hilferding insists on the distinction between the level of abstraction at which each volume works, and on the social meaning of the transformation: prices of production are the form value takes once capital, mobile and competing, confronts the individual producer, and price's deviation from value is itself a social fact the theory predicts rather than an embarrassment it suffers. Against the derivation charge, the reply treats the opening chapter as the unfolding of a concept whose warrant lies in the analysis of the commodity form as a whole, not as a syllogism to be graded premise by premise. Against the marginalist alternative, the reply presses a counter charge that has echoed ever since: a theory that begins from individual preferences explains nothing about why the individuals confront each other as owners and non owners, why labour power is on the market at all, or why the preferences of the propertyless take the form of a need to sell themselves by the week. The social question, on the reply's telling, is not an ornament added to the price question. It is the prior question, and a theory silent about it has purchased its precision by narrowing its field.

The reply also lands genuine blows on the critique's handling of detail. Bohm-Bawerk's treatment of value in simple commodity production, the society of independent artisans Marx invokes as the historical soil of the law, is shown to flatten the differences between that society and capitalism. His reading of the third volume's aggregates is contested line by line. His own positive theory is pointed at, with the observation that the Austrian doctrine of value, for all its psychological refinement, ends in its own unresolved problems about capital and interest, a theme the balance of this article takes up in its own right. A grading that ignored these successes would be as partisan as the summaries it corrects. The reply is a formidable piece of work. The question the next section asks is the only one that matters for the centre of this article: formidable against what? A reply can win every engagement it fights and still lose the campaign, if the decisive argument was never engaged. The full map of replies, including later defences that go beyond Hilferding, is drawn in the article on [marxist replies to critiques](/2019/12/01/marxist-replies-to-critiques/); the assessment here is confined to the standard reply's handling of the four arguments in the table.

## Vienna answers Vienna: the reply in its circle

The reply of 1904 was not a solitary performance. Hilferding wrote from inside the most intellectually ambitious Marxist milieu in Europe, the circle of Austrian social democracy that produced Otto Bauer, Karl Renner, and Max Adler, men who read the academic literature of the empire as natives and answered it in kind. Austro Marxism, as the school came to be called, made a speciality of engaging the strongest bourgeois scholarship on its own terrain: marginalist economics, neo Kantian philosophy, the new jurisprudence. Bohm-Bawerk was the empire's commanding economist, a former minister lecturing at the university in the same city where the Marxist theorists held their seminars, and his essay was a standing challenge in the most literal sense, a text the circle's members could not walk past. Hilferding, a medical student turned economist in his twenties, took the assignment, and the movement understood the essay that resulted as its official answer, reprinted and cited as such for decades.

The timing placed the reply inside a second battle as well. Eduard Bernstein's revisionist challenge, published at the end of the eighteen nineties, had questioned the movement's orthodoxies from within, and the defence of Marxist economics against Bohm-Bawerk doubled as a defence of orthodoxy against the revisionists: if the system's foundations could be shown intact against its most formidable academic critic, the case for revising them collapsed. Hilferding's essay therefore carries a polemical load beyond its arguments, and its confidence, its refusal to concede any point of substance, reads differently once the load is recognised. A reply written to hold a movement's line will relocate a claim rather than surrender it, because surrender is the one outcome its commission forbids. That sociology does not refute the reply; its arguments stand or fall on their merits, and several of them stand. But it explains the reply's shape, the completeness of its claimed victory, better than the supposition that its author simply failed to notice the inequality argument sitting unanswered in the text he was refuting page by page.

## Hilferding's counter-attack in detail

The reply's offensive passages deserve the same closeness as its defensive ones, since a grading that treated Hilferding only as a defendant would repeat the summaries' error in mirror image. His counter-attack on the marginalist theory has three prongs. The first charges the Austrian doctrine with circularity of its own: marginal utility explains price by the valuations of buyers and sellers, yet those valuations, in a market economy, are formed in the light of prices already ruling, so the explanation presupposes at every step the phenomenon it claims to produce. The second charges it with an empty formalism: a schema that can accommodate any outcome whatever, by adjusting the unobserved schedule of wants after the fact, explains no outcome in particular, and the psychology it invokes is too thin to bear the name, a set of postulates about preference that no observation could disturb. The third, the deepest, charges it with beginning in the middle: the valuing individual of the Austrian construction arrives equipped with property, with goods to exchange, with wants shaped by a definite social order, and the theory takes the equipment as nature. Where did the labourer who owns nothing but his capacity to work come from, and why does he confront the owner of the means of production as a contracting equal? A theory of exchange that cannot ask these questions, Hilferding argues, has defined its field so as to exclude the social relations that make exchange between such parties intelligible, and its precision within the field is the precision of a map that omits the country.

### What is the reply's charge against marginal utility theory?

That it explains price by valuations which are themselves formed under the influence of ruling prices, that its want schedules can be adjusted to fit any result and so explain none, and that it takes the property relations and the propertyless labourer as given facts of nature rather than as social products requiring explanation.

These are serious charges, and the subsequent century gave them a long life: versions of all three recur in the methodological literature, in the critique of revealed preference, and in the institutionalist tradition. Their presence in the reply is why the exchange of 1896 and 1904 cannot be scored as a rout in either direction. The critic demolished a derivation; the respondent damaged a methodology; and each man's strongest work was done against the other's foundations rather than against the argument actually in front of him. The asymmetry this article has insisted on is narrower than a verdict on the whole exchange. It concerns one argument, the denial that trading equates the things traded, and one reply's handling of it. On every other front the fighting was real and the honours divided. On that front there was no fighting, only a change of venue, and the change of venue is the fact the grading records.

## Where valuations come from

Hilferding's deepest counter, that marginalism takes preferences as given while historical materialism explains them, deserves a fuller hearing than the reply's own compression gave it, because it identifies the genuine frontier between the schools. Valuations are not born in the valuing mind from nothing. They are formed by income, by custom, by the range of goods a society produces and advertises, by the position of the valuer in the division of labour, by the very prices ruling in the markets where the valuer learned what things cost. A theory that explains prices by valuations and declines to explain valuations has stopped one step short of the social, and the Marxist tradition's insistence on taking the further step is its lasting contribution to the dispute, independent of the labour theory's fate. The Austrian rejoinder, developed by the school's later generations, is that explanation must stop somewhere, that the formation of preferences belongs to psychology and history rather than to economics, and that a theory is not refuted by the existence of questions outside it. Both positions are defensible as demarcations of a science. What neither demarcation licenses is the use of the further question as an answer to the nearer one. That preferences have social origins does not show that exchange embodies an equality; that exchange embodies none does not show that preferences fall from the sky. The two schools' best insights are compatible, and the tragedy of the controversy, visible only when the arguments are graded separately, is how rarely the compatibility was allowed to appear. Each side wielded its genuine insight as a solvent for the other's, and the solvent, in both directions, dissolved only the caricatures.

## The reply assessed: a change of question

Set the reply beside the inequality argument and the gap appears at once. The argument says: exchange does not imply an equality of magnitude between the goods exchanged, so the inference from exchange ratios to a common substance fails. The reply says: the theory of value is not a theory of exchange ratios but an analysis of the social relations of production. Both statements can be true together, and that is precisely the problem. The reply does not deny that exchange fails to imply equality. It does not offer a defence of the inference the opening chapter performs. It withdraws the theory from the ground on which the inference was attacked, and defends the withdrawn position ably. In the vocabulary this article has used throughout, the reply relocates the claim rather than defending it.

The concession inside the relocation deserves to be read slowly, because the whole grading turns on it. To say that value theory is not primarily about exchange ratios is to concede the critique's central description of the opening chapter, for that chapter is about exchange ratios from its first paragraph to its derivation's end. It opens with the proportion in which use values trade. It writes the proportion as an equation. It asks what the equation expresses, and it answers with a substance whose magnitude the proportion measures. If the theory was never primarily about that, then the chapter that founds the theory is an extended exercise on a subject the theory does not treat, and its derivation is a proof of something the theory does not claim. Hilferding cannot have both the chapter and the relocation. Either the derivation matters, in which case the inequality argument must be met on its own ground, or the derivation does not matter, in which case the second and third of the critique's arguments have carried their point about the book's foundations and the theory stands in need of a different foundation than the one its author built.

### Where does the standard reply change the question?

At the point where the critique attacks the inference from ratios to a shared substance, the reply stops discussing ratios and redescribes the theory as an analysis of social relations, a subject on which the inequality argument has no bearing and on which the reply is strong.

What separates relocating a claim from defending it is worth a paragraph of its own, since the distinction grades defences far beyond this dispute. A defence answers the objection on the ground where the original claim was staked: it shows the inference valid, the premise true, the excluded candidates rightly excluded. A relocation keeps the vocabulary of the original claim, its terms and its prestige, while quietly staking the claim on new ground where the objection does not reach. Relocation is not always dishonest; sciences do revise what their theories are about, and a relocation openly announced, with the old claim surrendered in plain words, is ordinary intellectual progress. The difficulty here is that the surrender was never announced. The reply presents itself as a refutation of the critique, and generations of readers filed it as one, while on the decisive argument it had changed the subject. The labour theory continued to be taught as a theory that explains exchange ratios, armed with a reply that defends it as a theory that does not.

Justice to Hilferding requires one qualification, and it is not small. The social reading of value is not an invention of 1904 concocted to escape a critic. The anatomy of the commodity form, of fetishism, of the social validation of private labour, runs through Marx's text from the beginning, and a reader can honestly hold that the derivation was always a compressed presentation of that analysis rather than a freestanding proof. On that reading the reply recovers the theory's real centre rather than relocating it. But the recovery still pays the critique's price. If the opening chapter is a presentation whose warrant lies elsewhere, then as a derivation it fails, which is the second argument's verdict; and the question the inequality argument poses to the presentation remains unanswered, since a presentation of labour's social form still begins, in the text, from an equation between goods and an inference to their common substance. The qualification changes who is blamed for the gap. It does not close the gap.

## Bukharin's rejoinder and the sociology of the margin

The Marxist counter offensive did not end with Hilferding. A generation later Nikolai Bukharin, the Bolshevik theorist, devoted a book to the Austrian school itself, arguing that marginal utility theory was the ideology of a class: the rentier, the coupon clipping consumer detached from production, whose standpoint the theory universalises into human nature. Where Hilferding had answered the critique argument by argument, Bukharin answered the school by its sociology, treating its categories as the self consciousness of a stratum that experiences the economy as a given income to be spent. The book is brilliant, abusive, and instructive for this article's purposes because it exhibits a third defensive manoeuvre beyond answer and relocation: the genetic dismissal, in which a theory's origin is exhibited as a substitute for its refutation. Bukharin's sociology may be accurate as sociology; the Austrian school did flourish among the propertied classes of a decaying empire, and its individualism does mirror the rentier's experience. None of that touches the inequality argument, which is true or false regardless of whose experience it flatters, and the genetic manoeuvre, applied consistently, would dissolve Marxism's own claims in the sociology of its adherents with equal ease. The grading discipline used throughout this article, asking only whether contact was made with the argument stated, exists precisely to refuse all three manoeuvres impartially: the slogan, the relocation, and the genealogy. By that discipline the later Marxist literature, for all its brilliance, leaves the position of 1904 unchanged. The inequality argument had been stated in 1896. It was still awaiting its first engagement.

## The seminar where the opponents sat together

The dispute's most evocative scene belongs to the decade after the reply. Bohm-Bawerk, returned to the University of Vienna after his ministries, conducted a seminar on political economy that became a legend of the republic of letters, because the benches held the future of both sides at once. Among the participants in those years were Ludwig von Mises, who would carry the Austrian argument into the calculation debate; Joseph Schumpeter, who would write the century's most judicious history of the whole controversy; and, from the Marxist camp, Otto Bauer and Rudolf Hilferding himself, the author of the standard reply, sitting in the critic's own classroom. The seminar debated the theory of value and the Marxian system with the principals in the room, and Schumpeter's recollections of it stress the same quality the printed exchange displays: the argument was conducted as though truth, not victory, were the stake, by men who disagreed about nearly everything else.

The scene is more than colour. It explains the standard reply's peculiar authority and its peculiar limits in a single image. Hilferding had not answered a book; he had answered his teacher, in the idiom his teacher's seminar enforced, and the idiom rewarded exactly the skills the reply displays: textual command, systematic construction, the social architecture of a theory. The idiom did not reward, because the seminar's own practice did not require, the pedestrian logical question at the inequality argument's core, whether an exchange, any exchange, embodies an equality at all. A question that belongs to no school's syllabus can wait a century for its hearing even when it is asked in print by the seminar's own master. Schumpeter, who watched both men, delivered the verdict of the best placed witness in his history of the science: he treated Bohm-Bawerk's critique as the most powerful ever mounted against the Marxian economics and Marx himself as an economist of the first rank whose system failed at its foundations, a double judgement that the summary literature, preferring its heroes unmixed, has never quite digested.

## Aggregation: the philosophy of the totals

The first argument's residue, the retreat from individual prices to aggregate identities, raises a question that outruns the dispute and belongs to the philosophy of social science: when is a law about totals a law at all? A total can be illuminating when it constrains its parts, as a conservation law constrains the motions of the particles whose energies it sums. The aggregate identities of the transformation constrain nothing in that sense. Any distribution of individual prices whatever, provided the books are kept as the theory keeps them, returns the same totals, because the totals are the premises from which the average rate is struck. A constraint that every outcome satisfies is not a constraint but a definition, and Bohm-Bawerk's average argument, examined earlier, is the local form of this general point. Defenders have replied that the identities were never meant as constraints but as demonstrations of consistency, proofs that the surface of prices can be generated from the depths of value without remainder. Consistency, however, was the very thing in dispute, and a demonstration that assumes the average rate computed from values in order to derive prices that sum to values demonstrates the consistency of an arithmetic, not of a theory of price. The distinction matters beyond this controversy because aggregative defences have become the standard refuge of theories in trouble across the social sciences, and the critique of 1896 remains the cleanest available lesson in how to audit one: ask what individual state of affairs the total excludes, and if the answer is none, enter the identity in the ledger as a definition and grade the theory on what remains.

## The revisionist use of the critique

The critique's first political effect was felt not among Marx's enemies but inside his movement, where it arrived as evidence in an internal trial. Eduard Bernstein, the executor of Engels's literary estate in exile and the coming theorist of revisionism, was assembling in the later eighteen nineties the case that the movement's doctrine required fundamental revision: the collapse of capitalism was not approaching, the middle classes were not vanishing, and the theory of value, he suggested, had the standing of a hypothesis whose difficulties its defenders understated. The third volume's troubled reception, capped by the Austrian's audit, entered Bernstein's brief as the theoretical count in the indictment. When his Evolutionary Socialism appeared in 1899 and the party congresses condemned his conclusions, the orthodox defence that Kautsky and Luxemburg mounted had to fight on two fronts at once, against the academic critic who said the system was inconsistent and against the comrade who said the inconsistency showed the system needed rebuilding. Hilferding's reply of 1904 belongs to that two front war, and its uncompromising tone, its refusal of every concession, is partly explained by the second front: a reply that conceded the derivation to Bohm-Bawerk would have armed Bernstein. The sociology of the reply, noticed earlier from the Viennese side, thus has a Berlin chapter as well. Arguments in a movement's canon are never only arguments, and the grading of this one has to record that the standard reply was written under a discipline that forbade the very concession, on the inequality argument, that an unencumbered scholar might have made in a paragraph and repaired in a chapter.

## The Russian hearing

Nowhere was the critique received with more seriousness than in the country where Marxism's future was being decided. Russian economists and revolutionaries read German theory as a native literature, and the debate over the third volume ran through the legal Marxist journals of St Petersburg in the eighteen nineties with an intensity the German discussion never matched. Mikhail Tugan Baranovsky, the most gifted economist of that milieu, attempted a synthesis in which the labour theory's social insight would be preserved inside a framework that accepted the marginalist account of price, a project whose very existence concedes the critique's pressure: nobody builds a synthesis with a refuted theory, and nobody needs one with a vindicated doctrine. The Russian discussion also produced the dispute's most interesting defector's trajectory, as several of the legal Marxists migrated from the defence of Marx toward liberal economics and religious philosophy, carrying the critique's lessons with them. For the grading, the Russian hearing matters as a control case. Readers with every motive of allegiance to defend the system entire, and with philosophical training equal to the Viennese, nonetheless treated the value theory as the system's vulnerable member and the critique as its serious examiner. The judgement of the best placed contemporary audiences, in Vienna and in St Petersburg alike, agrees with the ordering this article has defended: the contradiction and the derivation were the contested ground, the reply was a holding action, and the theory's centre of gravity shifted, under the critique's pressure, away from the determination of exchange ratios toward the social analysis the reply had championed.

## Inquiry and presentation: the defence Marx left in his own hand

The strongest shelter ever built for the derivation was built by Marx himself, in the afterword Marx added to the second German edition of the first volume, and the standard reply's deepest move is an application of it. Marx there distinguishes the method of inquiry from the method of presentation. Inquiry appropriates the material in detail, analyses its different forms of development, and traces their inner connection. Presentation, once inquiry has done its work, displays the life of the subject matter as an ideal reflection, and Marx concedes that if the display succeeds it may appear as though the reader faced an a priori construction, a system deduced from concepts rather than discovered in the world. The concession is the shelter. If the opening chapter is presentation rather than inquiry, its derivation is the displayed order of a result established by the whole analysis of capitalist production, and grading it as a freestanding proof mistakes the genre: one does not refute a map by showing that the coastline was not deduced from the compass rose. Hilferding's treatment of the chapter as a concept unfolding itself is this shelter occupied, and Rubin's reading is the shelter rebuilt with better materials.

The shelter's limits are the ones this article has enforced throughout, and they can now be stated in their final form. A presentation that wears the dress of a proof invites grading as a proof, and its author, who revised the chapter repeatedly and prized its dialectical form, cannot complain when a critic takes the dress at face value. More deeply, the shelter protects the chapter by relocating its warrant to the whole work, and the whole work's warrant on the question of exchange ratios is precisely what the other arguments contest: the shelter is a promissory note drawn on the system, offered in defence of the system's entrance. And the inequality argument stands outside the shelter altogether, since it does not grade the derivation's steps but denies the phenomenon the derivation purports to present. A presentation of the equality in exchange has nothing to present if exchange contains no equality, whatever the order of inquiry that produced it. The afterword's distinction is genuine, its use by the defenders is legitimate, and its reach stops, as every defence in this long exchange has stopped, at the door of the third argument.

## The defence that was suppressed

The value form defence, the strongest the tradition produced, has a history that belongs in any honest record of the dispute. Isaak Rubin developed it in Moscow in the nineteen twenties, in essays that treated the opening chapter as the analysis of a social form and abstract labour as a category constituted in exchange, answering the Austrian objections from within a reading of Marx more subtle than the party textbooks allowed. Rubin's reward was destruction. In the consolidation of Soviet orthodoxy his interpretation was condemned as idealist deviation, he was arrested at the decade's turn, and he perished in the purges of the later nineteen thirties, his work suppressed in the country whose doctrine he had spent his life expounding. The essays survived through copies and through the memory of students, reached the West in translation only in the nineteen seventies, and there founded the modern value form school, whose readings now dominate serious Marx scholarship. The episode is not an ornament on the grading. It demonstrates, from the tradition's own history, the grading's central finding: that the defence of the derivation which actually engages its difficulties had to be built outside the standard reply, against the resistance of the institutions that guarded the standard reply, and at a price that measures how threatening the engagement was felt to be. Hilferding, the standard reply's author, perished in his own exile's flight from another terror a few years later. The two ablest defenders of the doctrine against Bohm-Bawerk were both destroyed by the century their dispute helped to shape, and the essay that outlived them all was written by the minister of a vanished empire, in a genre, the courteous demolition, that the century also destroyed.

## Later defences and the road not taken

The standard reply did not remain the only defence, and the alternatives illuminate the grading by showing what engaging the derivation head on would have required. The most important alternative came from the value form school, associated with Isaak Rubin, whose essays on Marx's theory of value appeared in the nineteen twenties. Rubin's reading relocates the opening chapter's centre of gravity from the substance of value to its form: the chapter's real subject, on this account, is why labour's social character shows up as a property of things, and the derivation of abstract labour is a moment in the analysis of the value form, the social process by which private labours are equated in exchange. The equation between commodities, which Bohm-Bawerk read as a false assertion of equality between substances, Rubin reads as the social act by which labours are in fact rendered commensurable, an equalisation performed by the market rather than presupposed by the analyst. This is a genuinely different defence from Hilferding's, and a stronger one against the second argument, since it gives the derivation work to do that does not depend on the sieve's completeness.

Against the third argument, however, the value form reading pays the same toll as the standard reply, in a subtler currency. If the commensurability of goods is a social performance rather than a natural property, then the equality in exchange is something society institutes, and the question pressed by that argument returns in a new dress: what does the instituting, and does the performance require a common substance in the goods, or only a common practice among the exchangers? Money, on the value form account itself, is the institution that performs the equating, and money is not a substance deposited in each commodity in proportion to its labour. The defence thus concedes, once again, that the ratios are not explained by an equality of embodied magnitudes, while enriching the account of what exchange socially means. The pattern is by now familiar enough to name: every serious defence of the opening chapter defends it as something other than a proof that exchange ratios express equal embodied labour, because as that proof it cannot be defended, and the inequality argument is the reason it cannot.

Paul Sweezy's introduction of the debate to English speaking readers in the nineteen forties fixed the canon in the form most students still inherit: Bohm-Bawerk's critique, Bortkiewicz's formalisation, Hilferding's reply, bound together as a single dossier. The canonisation had a grading consequence nobody intended. Bound as a dossier, the exchange reads as a controversy with contributions on both sides and no result, a set piece of the history of thought to be summarised rather than a set of arguments to be scored. Sweezy himself, a Marxist, presented the critique as formidable and the reply as substantially successful, and the pairing of those judgements in a respected textbook taught generations to hold both without asking which arguments each judgement covered. The present article's unfashionable exercise, scoring the four arguments one by one against the one standard reply, is an attempt to recover the result the dossier format dissolved.

## What a successful defence would have to become

Return to the defeater with the later literature in view, and ask what its satisfaction would concretely require. The candidate evidence is the family of empirical studies, running from the nineteen eighties onward, that compute the labour contents of commodities from input and output tables and compare them with market prices, finding correlations that are high by the standards of social science. Defenders cite these studies as the labour theory's empirical vindication. Graded against the defeater, they are necessary and insufficient. They establish a pattern in the ratios, or rather in the levels, that calls for explanation. They do not yet establish that the explanation must run through a magnitude fixed in production rather than through the marginalist machinery, because labour contents are entangled with every other cost: a good that embodies much labour typically embodies much of everything, capital services, materials, energy, and the marginalist account predicts price sensitivity to all costs without singling out labour as a substance. The demonstration the defeater demands would separate the contributions, showing that labour content predicts ratios where rival cost measures, entered as competitors, fail, and that the predictive success has the structure the theory requires rather than the structure of a general cost correlation.

### What evidence would the defeater require in practice?

Studies that separate labour content from the other costs with which it travels, showing that embodied labour predicts exchange ratios where capital services, materials, and energy, entered as rival explanations, do not, and that the pattern follows the form the production side account specifies.

No such demonstration commands the field, and the honest statement of the position is that the defeater stands unsatisfied while remaining, in principle, satisfiable, which is exactly the condition of a live scientific question rather than a closed one. That condition is the strongest available rebuttal to two lazy verdicts: the verdict that the critique of 1896 settled the matter forever, and the verdict that the reply of 1904 did. The inequality argument destroyed a proof. It did not, and by its logical character could not, destroy the possibility that labour time regulates exchange ratios as a matter of empirical fact, discoverable by methods the opening chapter never used. What it destroyed was the shortcut, the claim that the regulation could be read off the form of exchange by deduction. A theory deprived of its shortcut must walk the long way round, through measurement and comparison, and the long way is where the dispute now legitimately lives.

## Competition: the hidden protagonist

Read the two theories side by side and a shared character emerges who does opposite work in each: competition. In Marx's construction, competition is the great falsifier at the surface and the great executor underneath. It falsifies the law of value in every individual price, driving returns to equality across unequal compositions, and it executes the law in the aggregate, transferring the surplus to where the average rate requires and enforcing, through the threat of ruin, the labour discipline and technical change that keep socially necessary times falling. In the Austrian construction, competition is the revealer: it narrows the range within which valuations can settle prices, forces the imputation of product values back onto factors, and disciplines producers toward the margins their customers set. The same mechanism cannot be, in one breath, the solvent of a law and its enforcement, unless the levels at which it works are kept scrupulously apart, and much of the dispute's confusion, in the secondary literature more than in the principals, comes from letting competition change levels mid argument. Bohm-Bawerk saw the level structure clearly, which is why his first argument is framed as a question about which level the first volume's law inhabits. Hilferding saw it clearly too, and his reply is at its best when it assigns each phenomenon its level and refuses to let the critic trade between them. The inequality argument owes its immunity, once again, to standing outside the level structure entirely: it concerns not what competition does to ratios but what an exchange is before competition is mentioned, and no assignment of levels can reach it.

## Gravitation: the metaphor that carried the law

The law of value's characteristic image, inherited from Smith, is gravitational: market prices oscillate around values as planets hold their orbits, disturbed and restored by an attraction that never sleeps. Metaphors in economics are arguments in costume, and this one deserves the audit the critique implicitly performs. Gravitation works as an image because the attracting centre is defined independently of the bodies it attracts: the sun's mass does not depend on the planets' positions. The value centre, in the transformation's economy, is not independent in that way. The average rate of profit that displaces prices from values is computed from the values themselves, so the centre is a function of the system it supposedly attracts, and the image of an external pull misdescribes an internal redistribution. Worse for the metaphor, a planet displaced from its orbit returns; a commodity whose price of production stands permanently above its value by the dictate of its capital's composition is not displaced but relocated, and it will occupy the new position in every period for as long as the composition lasts. Bohm-Bawerk's two averages argument is the prose form of this objection. The gravitational metaphor survived him anyway, because theories keep the images that flatter them, and generations of expositions taught the oscillation of price around value long after the system's own third volume had replaced oscillation with permanent, structured divergence. A reader who has watched the metaphor fail here has acquired a transferable instrument: whenever a theory offers a centre of gravity, ask whether the centre is fixed independently of the bodies, and whether the bodies ever actually return to it.

## The defeater: what a successful reply would have to show

A verdict that cannot state what would overturn it is a prejudice, so this article's verdict on the inequality argument carries its defeater openly. The verdict that the inequality argument stands would be defeated by a demonstration that a systematic regularity in exchange ratios across an economy requires an explanation at the level of production that divergent individual valuations cannot supply. That is the form a successful reply would have to take. It would identify some stable pattern in the ratios themselves, the gravitation of prices around labour costs under competition, say, or the correlation of price movements with changes in technique, and show that the pattern cannot be accounted for by subjective valuations playing against stocks and scarcities, but demands a magnitude fixed in production before exchange begins. Given such a demonstration, the hunt for a common substance would regain its warrant, not through the opening chapter's inference, which would still be invalid as logic, but through the explanatory needs of the phenomenon.

### What would defeat the verdict on the inequality argument?

Evidence that a stable pattern in exchange ratios across a whole economy demands explanation from the side of production, a pattern in how goods trade that divergent individual valuations, stocks, and scarcities cannot account for, leaving a production side magnitude as the needed explanans.

The standard reply does not attempt this demonstration, and the reason is structural rather than accidental. Hilferding's defence proceeds by changing the theory's subject, and a defence that has changed the subject has no use for evidence about ratios. Later defenders have occasionally gestured at the needed demonstration, pointing to the empirical correlation between prices and embodied labours measured in input and output tables, which is high for most economies studied. That gesture is the beginning of the right argument, and it should be recognised as such. But a correlation between prices and labour contents is not yet a demonstration that valuations cannot supply the explanation, since labour contents correlate with costs generally, and costs enter the marginalist account as constraints on supply. The defeater demands the harder step: a pattern that survives the marginalist explanation and still points at production. Until that step is taken, the verdict stands, and stating the defeater is this article's way of keeping the verdict honest rather than comfortable.

## The order the assessment imposes

The four arguments can now be placed in their order, which is the exercise the whole critique has always needed and rarely received. Weakest stands the empirical argument, which borrows all its force from the contradiction and adds only vividness. Next stands the derivation argument, which succeeds against the passage it targets but can be absorbed by demoting the passage, at the price of the theory's announced foundations. Next stands the contradiction itself, the famous one: a genuine structural tension, met by a reply with real content in the aggregate identities, leaving a residue that honest defenders redescribe rather than deny. Strongest stands the inequality argument, alone in meeting no reply, because the defence built against the critique as a whole was built on ground the argument does not occupy. The ordering reverses the emphasis of nearly every summary in circulation, and the reversal is the article's contribution: a critique graded by the fate of its arguments, rather than by the fame of its slogans, turns out to be weakest where it is loudest and strongest where it is quietest.

## Wieser's dissent: the school's third voice

The Austrian camp was never a monolith, and its third founder complicates any simple story of the assault on Marx. Friedrich von Wieser, Menger's other great pupil and Bohm-Bawerk's brother in law, gave the marginal doctrine its enduring technical term and then, in his book on natural value published seven years before the critique of Marx, drew a conclusion his school's partisans have been quietly stepping around ever since. Value in the marginal sense, Wieser argued, is not an artifact of capitalism or of private exchange at all. It would persist in a rationally ordered communist commonwealth, because any society that must economise scarce means among competing ends will impute worth to goods according to their marginal significance, whether or not markets exist to register the imputation. The doctrine his school wielded against the labour theory thus contained, in its most careful statement, an admission that the valuing mechanism it described was regime independent, a feature of scarcity itself rather than of the bourgeois order. Wieser's communists would still need value; they would merely compute it in a planning bureau instead of discovering it in a marketplace.

The admission matters for the grading because it marks the boundary of what the Austrian victory could mean. If marginal valuation operates under any regime, then the refutation of the labour theory's derivation cannot by itself settle the argument about socialism, a point the school's later controversialists, pressing the calculation argument a generation on, had to rewin on different ground, the ground of knowledge and incentives rather than of value's logic. It also casts a retrospective light on the 1896 essay's restraint. Bohm-Bawerk, who knew Wieser's book intimately and shared a faculty and a family with its author, confined his claims to the system he was auditing and declined to deduce the impossibility of the socialist commonwealth from the failure of its founder's economics. The deduction his epigones later attempted required premises the essay does not supply, and the distance between the master's caution and the school's later confidence is one more measure of how polemical inheritance degrades careful work. A grading that restores the essay's actual claims, neither inflated by its friends nor caricatured by its enemies, finds the Austrian school's best mind making a limited argument with unlimited care, while the school's third voice, in the same decade, quietly limited the argument's significance from within.

## The critic's own theory: time preference and roundabout production

Balance requires the audit to change direction. Bohm-Bawerk was not only a critic; he was the builder of the Austrian school's positive theory of value, capital, and interest, and the standing of that construction is part of the record this article must report. His theory of value is the marginal utility doctrine in its most careful form: goods derive value from the wants they satisfy, valued at the margin by the least important satisfaction the stock secures. His theory of interest, the doctrine for which his school is known, explains the premium of present over future goods by three grounds working together: the expectation of being better provided for in the future than in the present, the systematic underestimation of future wants that he called a defect of imagination and will, and the technical superiority of present goods as instruments, since goods in hand now can be set to work in longer, more productive processes while future goods cannot.

The third ground carries the famous concept of roundabout production. A fisherman who catches fish by hand feeds himself poorly; one who first spends weeks building a boat and net, producing nothing edible in the interval, thereafter catches far more per day. Production that detours through the making of instruments takes longer and yields more, and the lengthening of the production period, wisely chosen, raises the product per unit of original input. Capital, on this account, is the stock of intermediate products created by saving and waiting, and interest is the premium that coordinates the waiting with the gain. The construction is elegant, and its account of why a surplus attaches to capital as such was aimed directly at the exploitation theory: the capitalist's return reflects the productivity of time consuming methods and the sacrifice of abstinence, not the unpaid hours of the worker.

### What happened to the theory of roundabout production?

Its measure failed: the average period of production could not be defined independently of the interest rate it was meant to explain, and later capital controversies showed that a technique abandoned as unprofitable could become profitable again as distribution changed, breaking the simple link between roundaboutness and return.

The subsequent fate of the construction is the point the brief for this article insists on, and it is a matter of record rather than of scoring. The average period of production, the theory's central magnitude, resisted definition: the period depends on how inputs at different dates are weighted, the weights depend on the rate of interest, and a magnitude that presupposes the interest rate cannot explain it. Bohm-Bawerk wrestled with the objection from correspondents and critics within his own lifetime and never fully discharged it. In the capital controversies of the twentieth century the difficulty deepened into the phenomena of reswitching and capital reversing, where the same technique can be the profitable choice at two disconnected ranges of the interest rate and unprofitable between them, a result fatal to any simple ordering of methods by their roundaboutness. Mainstream economics quietly set the average period aside, retaining time preference as one ingredient in the theory of interest among several, and the great positive system of the Positive Theory of Capital survives the way its target survives: in fragments, in the training of economists, in concepts absorbed so thoroughly that their origin is forgotten. Neither the labour theory nor its Austrian rival stands where its author built it. The critic's own theory of value fared no better in subsequent economics than the theory he attacked, and recording that symmetry is a requirement of the subject, not a consolation prize awarded to the losing side.

## Fisher's synthesis and the dispersal of the Austrian system

The fate of Bohm-Bawerk's positive theory can be followed one step further, into the hands of the economist who absorbed what was durable in it. Irving Fisher, working in America a generation later, rebuilt the theory of interest on two blades: impatience, the preference for present over future income, and investment opportunity, the productivity of waiting embodied in the menu of techniques. The rebuilding kept the Austrian insight that time is an economic magnitude with a price, and discarded the machinery that had failed, the average period of production as a measurable property of an economy's capital stock. Fisher's agio is Bohm-Bawerk's premium with the metaphysics drained: a market rate at which impatience and opportunity are brought into equality at the margin, no substance of roundaboutness required. The pattern of survival is the one this article has met repeatedly. A system's central magnitude fails; its organising insight migrates into a successor construction that no longer cites it; the founder's name attaches to the critique he wrote of somebody else. Bohm-Bawerk is remembered as Marx's critic. Menger is remembered as a founder. The theorist of roundabout production, who intended to be remembered as the Newton of capital, is remembered as neither, and the symmetry with his target, whose derivation failed while his social analysis migrated into other disciplines, is exact enough to serve as the dispute's epitaph.

## Two theories, two questions: the comparison basis

Comparisons between the labour theory and the marginalist theory usually go wrong at the choice of measuring rod, and the brief governing this article fixes the rod in advance: the theories are to be compared on what each claims to explain. One addresses the determination of exchange ratios, the proportions in which goods trade, and builds its account from valuations, margins, and scarcities. The other, on the standard reply's own description, addresses the social organisation of production, the relations among producers that exchange both expresses and conceals. Compared on the first rod, the marginalist theory is the more complete construction, since it actually derives ratios from its premises, while the labour theory's derivation of ratios is the very thing under attack. Compared on the second rod, the labour theory asks questions the marginalist theory does not pose, about the origin of the property relations and the social form of labour, and silence on a question is not the same as a wrong answer to it.

### What does each theory claim to explain?

The marginalist theory claims to explain exchange ratios and prices from subjective valuations operating under scarcity; the labour theory, on its standard defence, claims to explain the social relations of a producing society, how private labour becomes social and how a surplus arises within formally equal exchange.

Holding both halves of that comparison at once is the discipline this article has tried to practise. It blocks the triumphalist reading of the critique, in which a victory on the ratio question is paraded as a victory over the whole of Marxist economics, and it blocks the defensive reading of the reply, in which the social question's importance is used to pardon a failed derivation on the ratio question. Each theory is strongest where the other is silent, and the history of the dispute is largely the history of partisans grading both theories on a single rod chosen to favour their own. The four arguments, graded individually, resist that flattening: they are arguments about a derivation and a consistency claim, and their success or failure settles those targets and no others.

## The equality premise elsewhere: the proof of surplus value

One further reach of the inequality argument must be charted, because it qualifies a boundary this article drew earlier. The scope section held that the critique does not touch the theory of exploitation, and that remains true of exploitation considered as a relation among classes. But the equality premise does load bearing work in the first volume's proof of surplus value, and the critic's denial of the premise touches the proof's form. Marx's demonstration proceeds by exclusion inside the sphere of circulation. Surplus value cannot arise from buying below value or selling above it, since such gains and losses cancel across the parties and explain no net increment for the class. The demonstration therefore imposes the condition that the solution must be found under exchange of equivalents: the money owner must buy his commodities at their value, sell them at their value, and yet extract more value than he advanced. Labour power is then introduced as the commodity whose use creates the increment within those conditions. The elegance of the construction depends on the equivalents condition being a real feature of exchange rather than a stipulation of the analyst. If exchange as such involves no equality of magnitudes, the exclusion argument loses its framing: the question why the capitalist class as a whole emerges from circulation with more than it advanced cannot be fenced inside the equivalents condition, and explanations that the condition was designed to exclude, including explanations running through the systematic divergence of prices from any embodied magnitudes, re-enter the field. Marxists have answers pitched at production's level that do not depend on the framing, above all the simple fact of the working day's division, which is an observation before it is a theorem. But the proof in its canonical form, the form the movement taught, leans on the premise the inequality argument denies, and the full measure of that argument's reach includes the lean. Precision about reach is the whole business of grading, and the record should show the reach extending one room further than the defenders' summaries admit, while stopping, as the scope section insisted, at the door of the social relation itself.

## The two volumes' two readerships

A sociological footnote explains why the contradiction argument, for all its fame, shocked the movement less than the seminar. The first volume of Capital was the movement's book, read in extracts, taught in party schools, quoted in pamphlets, its derivation reproduced in every popular exposition as the doctrine's foundation stone. The third volume was a specialists' book, read by economists and by the movement's theoretical stratum, its transformation chapters among the least popularised pages Marx ever wrote. The two readerships barely overlapped. A contradiction between the volumes was therefore, for the great majority of adherents, a contradiction between a book they knew and a book they had not opened, and it could be managed by the assurance that the specialists had the matter in hand. The derivation argument had no such shelter. It attacked the foundation stone as the popular expositions presented it, in the form in which hundreds of thousands of readers had learned it, and the inequality argument, had it been popularised with the same energy as the contradiction, would have threatened the same stone from below. That it was not popularised, that the movement's press dwelt on the volumes and their reconciliation while the premise of exchange went unexamined in the pamphlets, is itself evidence for this article's ordering of the arguments by the discomfort they caused. Controversies are remembered in proportion to their manageability. The manageable charge got a literature. The unmanageable one got a silence, and the silence, maintained across a century of summaries, is the loudest verdict the record contains.

## The volume nobody attacked

Between the two volumes the critique prosecutes stands a third that it almost entirely ignores, and the omission is itself a datum. The second volume of Capital, published in 1885, treats the circulation of capital: the metamorphoses of money into productive elements and back, the turnover of advances, and, in its celebrated closing part, the reproduction schemes, in which the economy is divided into two great departments, one making means of production and the other making articles of consumption, and the conditions are derived under which the whole product can be sold and the process repeated, in simple reproduction and in accumulation alike. Bohm-Bawerk's essay touches this material barely at all. His targets are the theory of value at the entrance and the theory of price at the exit; the vast middle machinery of circulation, where Marx is at his most architectural, is left unexamined, whether from judgement that it was peripheral to the system's claims or from the polemical economy that prefers an opponent's weakest fronts.

Posterity reversed the valuation in an instructive way. The reproduction schemes, ignored by the critic and by most of the reply's defenders alike, turned out to be the part of the inheritance with the longest technical future: they stand behind the Marxian tradition's theories of crisis and disproportionality, behind the two sector models of growth theory, and, through readers who carried the scheme's logic into new disciplines, behind the input and output analysis on which the defeater's empirical hopes now rest. A table of interlocking departments, in which each branch's product must find its purchaser in another branch's requirements, is precisely the instrument with which labour contents and prices can be compared across a whole economy. The critic who demolished the derivation thus left untouched the one part of the system that would eventually supply the tools for testing, empirically, whether the demolished derivation's conclusion might nevertheless hold as a matter of measurement. There is no moral in this beyond the one the whole grading has enforced: a system is a bundle, its parts have separate fates, and the part a famous critique ignores may matter more, in the end, than the parts it destroys. The second volume's quiet survival, unread by the controversialists and indispensable to their successors, is the bundle doctrine's most elegant proof.

## What the critique does not touch

Scope discipline cuts both ways, and the limits of Bohm-Bawerk's success should be stated as plainly as its extent. The critique targets a specific derivation in an opening chapter and a specific consistency claim between two volumes. It does not address the theory of exploitation as a social relation, the account of how the working day divides and how the division is enforced by propertylessness rather than by fraud in exchange. It does not address the account of the commodity form and fetishism, the critique of political economy as a critique of categories, or the historical materialist framework within which the economics sits. A reader who concludes from the grading above that Marxist economics as a whole has been refuted has outrun the evidence by a distance the critic himself, to his credit, did not claim to travel: his title announces the close of a system, meaning the value and price construction, and his arguments keep to that announcement.

The wider family of objections to the labour theory, redundancy arguments, empirical challenges, and internal disputes among Marxist economists themselves, is catalogued in the article on [labour theory of value criticisms](/2010/09/01/labour-theory-of-value-criticisms/), and the map of the whole critical literature, of which this essay is one province, is drawn in the [critiques of Marxism complete guide](/2019/08/01/critiques-of-marxism-complete-guide/). Confining the present grading to the 1896 essay and its standard reply is what allows the grading to be exact. Fame had blurred the boundaries of the critique in both directions, crediting it with demolitions it never attempted and saddling it with failures belonging to its epigones. Restoring the boundaries is a service to critics and defenders alike.

## What the critic got wrong

Neutrality in grading is not exhausted by scoring the reply; the critique itself must be audited, and its defects entered with the same plainness. Three stand out. The first is a flattening of Marx's text at the point of the value form. The opening chapter contains, alongside the derivation Bohm-Bawerk grades, an analysis of the form of value, the progression from the simple expression of value in a single equivalent through the expanded and general forms to money, which many readers regard as the chapter's living core. The critique treats the chapter as a syllogism with appendices, and its silence about the form analysis allowed defenders to claim, with some justice, that the real Marx had not been engaged. The claim does not rescue the derivation, which is there in the text and fails as shown. But a critique that grades a chapter's weakest argument while passing its strongest material in silence has chosen its target with a polemicist's economy, and the choice should be registered.

The second defect is the mirror of the first: an understatement of the labour and labour power distinction's force against the exploitation charge in its crude forms. Bohm-Bawerk had engaged that distinction in 1884 and found the exploitation theory circular, since the value of labour power is itself determined within the system it is meant to indict. By 1896 the engagement is assumed rather than renewed, and readers who meet the critique alone may not learn that the distinction answers the vulgar objection that profit is mere cheating in exchange, an objection Marx's theory was specifically built to refuse. The third defect concerns the rhetoric of dropping: the charge that Marx simply drops the assumption of exchange at values in the third volume understates the extent to which the transformation is prepared, announced, and theorised within the system, however inadequate the preparation. Marx did not smuggle prices of production past his readers; he trumpeted them as the solution to the problem his second volume's preface had advertised. A solution can fail without being a sleight, and the critique's occasional slide from the first verdict to the second is its least scrupulous habit. None of the three defects touches the inequality argument, which depends on none of the flattened readings. They are recorded here because a grading that audits only one side is the summary tradition's error wearing new clothes.

## The logical skeleton: three fallacies charged

For readers who like their disputes dissected formally, the critique's logical charges reduce to three named fallacies, and naming them clarifies what a defence would have to repair. The derivation is charged, first, with begging the question in the drawing of its field: restricting the search for a common factor to products of labour assumes the factor's identity in the act of looking for it. It is charged, second, with a false elimination: the survey of candidate properties is incomplete, and an elimination over an incomplete survey certifies nothing, since the true candidate may be among the unexamined. It is charged, third, in the inequality argument, with an ignoratio elenchi at the foundation: the proof, even if its steps were valid, would establish a conclusion beside the phenomenon, because the phenomenon, exchange, does not present the equality the proof sets out to explain. The three charges are of increasing depth. A defender can repair a survey by completing it, at least in principle. A field can be redrawn with the gifts of nature included and the elimination rerun, though the rerun's outcome is not in doubt. The third charge admits no repair of the same kind, because it denies that the explanandum exists. There is no equality in exchange for a corrected derivation to explain. That is the formal skeleton beneath this article's ordering of the four arguments, and it is why the third argument, the briefest in the critic's own pages, carries the verdict that the others, for all their bulk, could not.

## The Grundrisse complication

Twentieth century scholarship complicated the derivation's standing in a way neither party to the original exchange could foresee. Marx's great draft of 1857 and 1858, the Grundrisse, unpublished until the eve of the Second World War, presents the theory of value in a form strikingly different from the opening of Capital: the derivation from the exchange equation is absent, and the argument proceeds from the analysis of money and of labour's social form by another route. Readers who prize the draft have argued that the derivation was a expository device of the published work, a concession to the reader's need for a deductive entrance, rather than the theory's foundation in its author's own workshop. The philological defence cuts in an interesting direction. It strengthens the claim that the theory can survive the derivation's fall, since its author apparently built without the derivation when writing for himself. It weakens, by the same stroke, any claim that the published derivation deserves charitable reconstruction as the theory's heart: if the heart beat elsewhere in the manuscripts, the published passage is a façade, and Bohm-Bawerk's demolition of the façade, however complete, leaves the building's real structure to be assessed on other grounds. The grading in this article is a grading of the published critique and the published reply, the texts the tradition actually fought over, and within that frame the complication changes nothing. As a fact about the larger assessment of Marx, it belongs in the record: the strongest twentieth century defences of the theory have consistently been defences of something other than the passage Bohm-Bawerk attacked, which is the pattern this article has documented from 1904 forward.

## The sympathetic readers and the weakest passage

One misreading, repeated in hostile summaries, holds that defenders of Marx stand unanimously behind the opening chapter's derivation, so that Bohm-Bawerk's second and third arguments strike the whole tradition. The record is otherwise. Sympathetic readers have long treated the derivation with reserve, and many regard it as the weakest passage in the book: a dialectical performance whose conclusion the subsequent analysis must earn by other means. Readers in the value form tradition recast the chapter as a study of the value form rather than a deduction of its substance, which is a sophisticated rescue and also an admission that the passage as written does not do what it appears to do. Others pass over the derivation in expositions altogether, beginning the theory from the analysis of production and mentioning the third thing argument only to set it aside. A critique of a passage that its target's own side handles with tongs is not thereby wasted. It explains why the tongs are needed. But the grading should register the fact: on the derivation, Bohm-Bawerk was pushing at a door that many inside the house had already stopped defending, and the inequality argument's importance is precisely that it shows why the door could not be defended, by attacking the premise the passage shares with every reconstruction of it.

### How do sympathetic readers treat the opening chapter?

With reserve: many treat the derivation as the frailest passage in Capital, reconstruct it as an analysis of the value form rather than a proof of value's substance, or pass over it in exposition and found the theory on the analysis of production instead.

### What does the critique leave standing?

The theory of exploitation as an account of social relations, the anatomy of the commodity form, and the aggregate connection between profit and unpaid labour all stand outside its targets; the critique closes on a derivation and on a consistency claim between volumes, and attempts nothing beyond them.

## The critique in the subsequent literature

The essay's afterlife confirms the grading from an unexpected direction. The contradiction argument generated the largest literature, the transformation debate that ran through the twentieth century in increasingly formal dress, because it posed a problem that could be mathematised. The derivation argument entered the textbooks as a caution about proofs by elimination. The inequality argument, generating no research programme, was absorbed silently into the foundations of the victorious theory: economists trained after the marginalist settlement simply begin from divergent valuations and never meet the premise that exchange implies equality, so the argument that demolished the premise is invisible to them, like a demolished building's empty plot. Hilferding's reply, meanwhile, became the template for a style of defence that later controversies reused, the relocation of a theory's subject under pressure, which makes the paired exchange a case study in the rhetoric of economics as much as in its substance. The critique is still cited wherever the labour theory is taught, usually for the wrong argument, and the correction of that citation habit is the practical purpose the present article serves.

## Does the inequality argument prove too much?

A strong argument should be tested against the suspicion that it proves more than its author wants, and the inequality argument faces one such test that a fair grading must record. If exchange implies no equality and needs no common substance, does the same reasoning dissolve every objective account of price, including the cost of production theories on which Bohm-Bawerk himself relies when he explains why prices gravitate? The critic's system gives production a governing role: roundabout methods, the structure of costs, the productivity of waiting. If the parties' divergent valuations are sovereign, why do ratios not wander wherever bargaining leaves them, and why do they in fact track costs with such fidelity that every merchant's practice assumes the tracking? Pressed to its limit, the inequality argument might seem to saw off the branch its author's own theory sits on.

The test fails, but instructively. The argument's target was never the existence of regularities in ratios, nor the influence of production conditions on them. Its target was a specific inference: from the form of exchange, by way of an equation, to a shared substance, present in equal amount, embodied in the goods. Cost of production theories do not make that inference. They claim that the conditions under which goods can be supplied discipline the bargains parties strike, a causal claim about the constraints on valuation, entirely compatible with the doctrine that exchange itself is moved by divergent estimates. Bohm-Bawerk's own account assigns costs their place inside the marginalist machinery: the value of producer goods is imputed from the value of products, and the costs that seem to govern prices are themselves governed, at one remove, by valuations. Whether that inversion can be sustained is the standing dispute between the schools, but it is a dispute about the direction of determination, not about the existence of determination. The inequality argument is untouched by it, because the argument denies only that the determination must take the form of an embodied common magnitude disclosed by the exchange equation. It proves exactly what it aims at, no more, and the discipline of its aim is a large part of why it has outlasted the grander charges around it.

## Objections a defender should still press

Grading that anticipates no appeal is advocacy in disguise, so the strongest appeals against this article's verdicts belong in the record. Against the treatment of the contradiction, a defender may press the simultaneous interpretation's critics from within Marxist scholarship itself, the temporal readings that claim the aggregate identities hold exactly when the transformation is computed as Marx directed, through time rather than in a static system; the dispute is live, technical, and beyond this article's charter, but its existence qualifies any summary that calls the first argument settled in either direction. Against the treatment of the derivation, a defender may press the philological point that Marx revised the opening chapter between editions and that the French edition, which he supervised and prized, presents the argument in a form less vulnerable to the sieve objection; the point deserves the hearing the critique, fixed on the German text of its day, did not give it. Against the centre of the article, a defender may press the objection that the inequality argument attacks a premise Marx took from Aristotle as a starting intuition rather than as a load bearing axiom, and that the theory's real foundation was always the analysis of production, so that demolishing the intuition leaves the edifice standing on its true supports. That objection, stated carefully, is the most serious of the three, and the answer given throughout these pages applies to it unchanged: a foundation relocated is a foundation conceded to have been misdescribed, and the description stood at the head of the book for a century of readers. The appeals mitigate the verdicts at their edges. None of them reverses the ordering, and the ordering was the article's claim.

## The essay as an artefact of its science

Step back from the arguments and regard the essay itself as evidence about how economics was practised at the moment of its professionalisation. It is short, a hundred pages in translation, built entirely from quotation, arithmetic, and logic, without a single appeal to statistics, archives, or authority. Its author held one of the great offices of a European state and wrote as though office conferred nothing. Its method is the close reading of a canonical text, a practice the discipline was in the process of abandoning for model building, and its endurance suggests the abandonment was premature: the questions it settled were settled by reading carefully what an author had actually argued, a skill no subsequent technique has replaced. The reply shares the character. Hilferding's essay is also a close reading, of the critique and of Marx together, and the pair of texts together form a small monument to a period when theoretical economics was still a branch of scholarship in the humanistic sense, conducted in essays, in journals of political science, by men who expected their opponents to have read the same books.

That shared scholarly character explains a feature of the exchange that puzzles readers formed by later controversies: its courtesy. Bohm-Bawerk praises Marx's genius in the act of dismantling his system, calling the third volume's construction the work of a mind of the first rank even as he pronounces it a failure, and he treats the defenders he answers, Sombart above all, as erring colleagues rather than as propagandists. Hilferding returns the courtesy in kind, acknowledging the critic's eminence and confining his severity to the arguments. The courtesy was not decoration. Both men understood that the dispute was about the credentials of a science, and that credentials are established by the quality of one's engagements with the strongest opposing work. The grading exercise this article has performed, with its insistence on full strength statements and its suspicion of every shortcut, is an attempt to honour the standard the participants set, in a literature that has too often honoured it in the breach.

## What the dispute teaches about theories and their proofs

Draw the threads together into the general lesson, since the series to which this article belongs is concerned with the criticism of Marxism as an intellectual practice and not only with its results. A theory and the proof offered for it are different things, with different fates. The labour theory of value might be a penetrating account of capitalist society and the derivation in its opening chapter might still be invalid; the marginal theory might explain exchange correctly and its author's theory of capital might still collapse on its central magnitude. Criticism that confuses the levels, treating a failed proof as a refuted theory or a surviving theory as a vindicated proof, generates the heat that fills the summary literature. Bohm-Bawerk, at his best, kept the levels separate, which is why his strongest argument is the one aimed with most precision at the level it could actually destroy: not the theory, not the system in every register, but the inference at the theory's gate. Hilferding, at his best, kept them separate too, and his reply's enduring value lies in its demonstration that the theory had other levels at which it could be defended. The tragedy of the exchange, if the word is not too large, is that each man's best work was done at a level the other was not defending, and the literature they founded inherited the cross purposes as a tradition. To read them against each other with the levels distinguished is to recover, from a famous controversy, the unfamous truth it contains: that exchange does not equate the things exchanged, that a great theory was launched by an argument that said it did, and that the theory's defenders have been explaining, ever since, why the launch was not the theory. All three propositions can be held together, and holding them together is what understanding this critique consists in.

## Three generations of relocation

The defences of the labour theory across the century arrange themselves into three generations, and laying them in sequence exposes the pattern this article has tracked from the start. The first generation, Sombart's, relocated the law from fact to thought: value as a logical instrument, immune to prices because it was never a magnitude among them. The second generation, Hilferding's, relocated the theory from ratios to relations: value as the social bond of a producing society, immune to the inequality argument because its subject was the organisation of production rather than the terms of trade. The third generation, the value form school's, relocated the chapter from proof to phenomenology: the derivation as the analysis of how social labour appears as a property of things, immune to the sieve objection because its business was the form of appearance rather than the identification of a substance. Each generation's defence is more sophisticated than the last, and each concedes, in its own vocabulary, the point the generation before had resisted conceding: that the opening chapter does not do what a reader of 1867, or of 1896, would naturally take it to do. The sequence is not a scandal. It is how a research tradition metabolises a successful criticism, by successive reinterpretations that preserve the text's authority while revising its claim. But the metabolising should be visible to the tradition's students, and the summaries' pretence that the reply of 1904 closed the file has hidden the sequence from exactly the readers who most needed it. Grading the critique argument by argument, as this article has done, is simply the sequence viewed from the other end: the critic's charges, sorted by the number of reinterpretations each one forced.

## The blind spot: money

Fairness to the defenders requires one structural criticism of the critique that the grading has so far only touched. Bohm-Bawerk conducts his entire analysis in the barter frame of the derivation itself: two commodities, an equation, a search for their common factor. But the economy Marx theorised is a money economy from the first page, and the opening chapter's own trajectory runs from the simple form of value to the money form, arguing that value necessarily expresses itself in a universal equivalent. A critic who accepts the barter frame for the sake of argument may fairly attack the inference drawn inside it, and the inequality argument does exactly that, legitimately. Yet the frame's acceptance costs the critique its grip on the parts of Marx's construction where money is load bearing: the theory of the value form's necessary expression, the analysis of the circuit of capital, the distinction between the production and the realisation of surplus. Defenders in the value form tradition have built their whole case on this cost, arguing that a critique of barter equations cannot reach a theory whose subject is money as the form of social labour. The argument deserves its weight. It does not reach the inequality argument, which is about exchange as such and holds a fortiori where money mediates, since the parties to a monetary purchase diverge in their valuations exactly as barterers do. But it marks the boundary of the critique's empire with precision: within the theory of the derivation and of price determination, the critique rules; across the monetary and social registers of Marx's work, its writ was always contested, and the contest continues on ground the 1896 essay never surveyed.

## The long shadow forward: from value to calculation

The critique's influence ran forward into the century's largest economic controversy, and a brief tracing of the line belongs to the record, with the boundary of this article's charter kept in view. Bohm-Bawerk's students inherited his conviction that the Marxian construction failed at its foundations, and his seminar's most consequential heir, Mises, radicalised the family argument in 1920: without private property in the means of production there are no prices for them, and without such prices there is no rational calculation, whatever one's theory of value. The calculation argument is a different critique from the one graded here, aimed at a different target, the possibility of socialist planning rather than the consistency of Marx's system, and it has its own article in this series. The line of descent matters because it explains the temperature of the later debates. To the Austrian tradition, 1896 had established that Marxism's economics was a closed system in the worst sense, finished and refuted, and the calculation argument was pressed with the confidence of a school that believed the foundations had already been cleared. To the Marxist tradition, the same confidence read as the repetition of a refutation it believed Hilferding had answered, and the calculation debate opened with each side privately certain the other had been disposed of a generation before. Few spectacles in the history of economics are more instructive than two traditions entering a new controversy each convinced it has already won the old one. The grading of the old one, argument by argument, is the only known cure.

## A short shelf: the texts in their editions

Orientation among editions is a practical kindness to any reader moved to verify the grading, since the dispute's bibliography conceals one trap. The critique first appeared in a festival volume honouring Karl Knies, published at Berlin in 1896, a setting that explains its essay form and its dedication copy politeness. Alice Macdonald's English rendering followed from a London house two years later, with James Bonar's appreciative introduction, and it is that rendering, reprinted across the twentieth century, through which the Anglophone world has mostly read the argument; its renderings of the key passages, including the sentence on equality and equilibrium quoted in these pages, are faithful and have worn well. Hilferding's answer circulated in German periodical form from 1904 and reached English readers in full only decades later, a lag that distorted the Anglophone balance for decades, since the attack could be read entire in an afternoon while the defence had to be taken on trust from summaries. The imbalance was repaired in 1949 when Paul Sweezy edited a single volume binding the critique together with the reply and with Bortkiewicz's formal papers on the transformation, from an American reprint house; that volume, more than any other artefact, fixed the dossier format criticised earlier in this article, three texts frozen as a controversy exhibit. Readers who take up the dossier should read it against its grain, scoring each contribution separately before allowing the binding to suggest a collective verdict. The primary texts are short, public, and translated; nothing in the grading offered here requires apparatus beyond them and the first chapter of Capital itself, which is as it should be for a dispute whose every important move was made in print, in the open, within a single decade.

## A note on reading the principals

Students who go from this article to the texts themselves, as its grading repeatedly invites, deserve three practical directions. Read the critique in the Macdonald translation entire, not in extracts: its force is cumulative, and the extracts in circulation are chosen, by friends and enemies alike, to flatter the contradiction argument at the expense of the fourth chapter, where the derivation and the equality premise receive their treatment. Read the reply entire as well, in the awareness that its English circulation has always lagged its German reputation, and that the summaries of it in textbooks were often written by scholars working from the critique's account of what it said. And read the first chapter of Capital between the two, slowly, with the single question in hand that this article has made its centre: at each step, is the argument describing what the parties to an exchange do, or deducing what a substance inside the goods must be? The chapter reads differently under that question than under any other, and the difference is the whole dispute in miniature. The principals reward the effort because they wrote for readers who would make it: a minister who quoted his opponent at length and a young theorist who answered him page by page, in a republic of letters that assumed the argument, pursued honestly, was the point.

## Two theories of time

Beneath the dispute's technicalities sits a symmetry so neat it would seem invented if the texts did not enforce it: both theories are, at their cores, theories about time. For Marx, time is the substance. Labour time, measured in hours and congealed in products, is what value is made of, and the economy's master magnitude is a duration. For Bohm-Bawerk, time is the price. The interval between present and future goods is what interest measures, and the economy's master problem is the valuation of duration. Each theorist accused the other, in effect, of mishandling time: the Marxist tradition charged the Austrian with dissolving the real process of production, which takes time and labour, into a play of subjective estimates across time; the Austrian charged Marx with mistaking a duration spent for a substance deposited, as though hours spent in making a thing remained inside it like a fluid in a vessel. The metaphor of congelation, Marx's own image of labour crystallised in the product, is the precise point of contact: the critic's whole ontology denies that time can crystallise, while the defender's whole social theory requires something very like crystallisation to make exploitation a measurable transfer rather than a metaphor. Later economics quietly declined both ontologies, treating time as an index rather than a substance or a price in itself, and the dispute's residue in the modern discipline is a pair of techniques, labour accounting on one side and discounting on the other, each used daily by practitioners who have never heard of the war between their inventors. The symmetry is worth a student's contemplation, because it suggests that the deepest theoretical quarrels are often quarrels about which feature of the world a science will take as primitive, and that such quarrels are settled less by refutation than by the slow discovery of which primitives let the science work.

## The ghostly objectivity

Marx had a phrase for the mode of being his derivation assigns to value, and the phrase betrays an awareness that his critic exploited with relish. Value, as the residue of abstraction, is a ghostly objectivity, a phantom like objectivity, the mere jelly of undistinguished human labour. The language is deliberately spectral: value is not a physical property, not a chemical constituent, not anything a laboratory could find in the goods, and Marx insists on the spectrality as a point of theory, the social character of value being precisely its independence of the material body that bears it. Bohm-Bawerk's rejoinder writes itself, and in substance he wrote it: a magnitude that no instrument registers, that is present in goods in proportion to an abstraction the analyst performs, and whose presence is certified only by the exchange ratios it is summoned to explain, stands in danger of being the ratios themselves wearing a metaphysical costume. The defender's answer is that social magnitudes are routinely spectral in this way: the value of money, the authority of an office, the obligation of a debt are none of them physical, and all of them are real in their effects. The answer is strong as far as it goes, and it goes exactly as far as the social reading of the theory extends. What it cannot do is restore the derivation, whose claim was never that value is a social relation with real effects, a claim nobody in the dispute denied, but that a definite magnitude of a definite substance explains why this coat exchanges for that much linen. Ghosts may govern societies. They do not, without further argument, come in quantities that price coats.

## What the respondent built next

The fairest test of a defensive relocation is whether the ground retreated to proves fertile, and Hilferding's subsequent career supplies the test with an unusually clear result. Six years after the reply he published Finance Capital, the study of capitalist development in its most advanced phase, in which the social reading of Marx's economics was applied to the age of banks, cartels, and the fusion of industrial and financial power. The book became the foundation of the Marxist theory of imperialism and remains readable when most of the reply's polemical context has faded. Its method is the reply's method matured: categories treated as social forms, the economy read as a structure of power among classes and institutions, the determination of individual prices left largely to one side. A reader who asks whether the relocation of 1904 was an evasion or a discovery can point to 1910 as evidence for discovery: the ground Hilferding retreated to was real, and he built the tradition's most durable work on it. The grading of the reply must absorb this without surrendering its point. A relocation can be both an evasion of the argument in front of the writer and a discovery of the ground on which the writer's school will flourish; the two verdicts concern different questions, the fate of an argument and the fate of a research programme. This article has graded the argument. The programme's subsequent history, in which the social reading produced Finance Capital while the derivation's defence produced footnotes, is the strongest confirmation the grading could ask for, delivered by the defender's own bibliography.

## The victory that dissolved its victors

The marginalist settlement that followed the dispute had a fate as ironic as any in the record. Within two generations the victorious theory had transformed itself beyond its founders' recognition: utility became ordinal and then behavioural, the valuing subject of the Austrian construction was replaced by the consistency conditions of revealed preference, and the price theory that emerged explained ratios by constraints and equilibrium conditions in which neither a substance of value nor a psychology of valuation survived in its original form. The inequality argument's premise, that exchange is moved by divergent estimates, was absorbed so thoroughly that its descendants state it as a triviality about gains from trade in the first week of instruction, unattributed. Meanwhile the questions the labour theory had kept alive, about the distribution of the surplus among classes and the social form of production, migrated out of price theory into political economy's other provinces, where they continue under other flags. A student surveying the whole arc might conclude that the dispute was won by neither party and settled by the discipline's decision to ask narrower questions, a decision that made the old battlefield unrecognisable to both armies. That conclusion, properly hedged, is the one this article's grading supports. The inequality argument stands, because the narrower questions still begin where it said they must, from parties who value what they receive above what they surrender. The derivation does not stand, because no narrower question has ever needed it. And the social theory that the reply defended stands in the only way social theories do, by the work it continues to generate in hands that have mostly forgotten the reply itself.

## The moral vocabularies audited

Both theories carried a moral vocabulary smuggled inside their technical terms, and the dispute's heat cannot be understood until the smuggling is inspected on both sides. The Austrian construction speaks of abstinence, of sacrifice, of the capitalist's meritorious waiting, a vocabulary in which the return to capital arrives pre justified as the wage of a virtue. Marx had savaged the abstinence doctrine in its earlier, Seniorian form with a satirist's glee, remarking on the convenience of a theory in which the rich man's refusal to consume his fortune counts as labour deserving payment, and the mockery stuck to Bohm-Bawerk's more sophisticated version in the movement's literature, where the three grounds of the agio were routinely summarised as a sermon on thrift. The Marxian vocabulary performs the mirror operation: exploitation, surplus, unpaid labour are technical terms inside the system, defined by the division of the working day, and they arrive pre condemned, so that a reader who accepts the definitions has accepted the verdict before the evidence is called. Bohm-Bawerk's counter, that a science should describe the transfer before baptising it, has the same force against his own school's vocabulary of sacrifice. The grading discipline of this article, which has tried to state every argument in terms its opponents could sign, is the practical refusal of both vocabularies at once. It costs vividness. It buys the only thing grading is for, the ability to say which arguments stand when the adjectives are returned to their owners. Readers who carry one habit from this article into the wider literature of the dispute should carry that one, since the vocabularies, unlike the arguments, have never gone out of print.

## The fate of a word

Disputes of this magnitude leave marks on the language itself, and the career of the word value after 1904 is a compressed history of the whole affair. Before the marginal revolution the word named, in economics, a property that goods were thought to carry within themselves, measurable and comparable across the whole world of commodities, and the classical economists' struggles were struggles to say wherein that property consisted. The Austrian victory redefined the term as a judgement rather than a property: value became something persons confer, in situations, under scarcity, and the noun survived while its grammar changed underneath it, from a substance words take to a relation words report. Professional economics completed the migration by quietly abandoning the noun for most purposes, speaking of prices, of willingness to pay, of equilibrium allocations, and leaving value to the moral and political vocabularies where it had always also lived. Marxist economics retained the older grammar deliberately, as a flag planted on the production side of the economy, and the retention explains a curious feature of the later controversies: the parties often argued past each other at the level of vocabulary before any proposition was reached, since a sentence containing the word value asserted different kinds of things in the two grammars, a measurement in one and a verdict in the other.

The lesson generalises beyond this controversy, and it is the last one this article will draw. Theoretical disputes are frequently won and lost at the level of what a central term will be allowed to mean, and the side that redefines the term wins the territory the term commands without needing to win every argument fought on it. Bohm-Bawerk understood this perfectly, which is why his essay spends its force not on Marx's conclusions but on the concept at the system's gate, the derivation by which value was defined into existence as embodied labour. Hilferding understood it too, which is why his answer spends its force redefining the concept back, as a social relation rather than a magnitude. The century's argument about the labour theory can be read, at this level, as a custody battle over a word, conducted by two parties who agreed about nothing except the word's importance. Readers who learn to watch for the custody battle inside a theoretical dispute possess the master key to this one. They will see why the arguments graded here had the fates they had: the charges that attacked propositions met answers, in proportion to their strength, while the charge that attacked the definition's foundation, the supposed equality inside exchange from which the definition was deduced, met a redefinition instead, because a definition under attack can be defended only by being restated, and a restated definition is a conceded one.

## The symmetry of survival

A century and more after the exchange, the fairest summary of the two constructions is that each survives in the fragments the other could not digest. Marginal utility, the engine of Bohm-Bawerk's alternative, passed into the bloodstream of economics so completely that its origin in the dispute is invisible; students learn demand from schedules of willingness to pay without learning that the schedule was once a weapon aimed at the labour theory. Time preference likewise survives, demoted from the keystone of a system to one determinant among several in the theory of interest, taught without the average period of production that was supposed to give it measure. Of the labour theory, the substance doctrine survives mainly within heterodox schools that maintain it as a research programme, while its social register, the analysis of fetishism, of the value form, of exploitation as a relation rather than a bargain, passed into social theory at large, often in the work of readers who would not defend the derivation for a paragraph. The critique survives entire, but in the distorted form this article set out to correct: remembered for its loudest argument, its contradiction, and forgotten in its strongest, the denial that exchange equates anything.

There is a lesson in the pattern for readers of theoretical disputes generally. Arguments do not survive in proportion to their strength. They survive in proportion to their usability: the contradiction could be formalised, taught, examined, and answered with machinery, so it filled journals; the inequality argument, once absorbed into the victors' foundations, had no further use to anyone, since the winning side no longer needed to state it and the losing side could not afford to. The history of a science, written from its textbooks, systematically overrates the arguments that generated literature and underrates the arguments that ended it. Recovering the underrated argument is not antiquarianism. A student who meets the labour theory through the contradiction debate alone will believe the theory's fate turns on the transformation problem, a technical question with technical answers, and will never learn that the theory's founding proof was dismantled at its first step by an argument about why people trade at all. That student has been given the dispute's noise and denied its signal.

## Grading the graders: how the summaries went wrong

The summary tradition's failure has identifiable mechanics, and naming them may protect the reader against the next famous critique served the same way. The first mechanic is compression by slogan: a book of four arguments becomes a book of one, the one with the shortest statement, and the contradiction between two volumes can be stated in a sentence while the inequality argument needs a paragraph, so the sentence wins. The second mechanic is symmetry of presentation: textbooks allot the critique a paragraph and the reply a paragraph, and the allotment itself testifies that honours were even, whatever the paragraphs say. The third mechanic is the dossier effect described above, the binding of attack and answer into a controversy exhibit, which converts a scored contest into a displayed one. The fourth is deference to the participants' own framing: Bohm-Bawerk led with the contradiction because it was the occasion of his essay, and Hilferding claimed a complete victory because his commission required one, and summaries that follow the participants' framing inherit both distortions at once.

Against these mechanics the only protection is the discipline exercised here: enumerate the arguments, state each at full strength from its own text, state the reply at full strength from its text, and then ask, argument by argument, whether contact was made. The exercise is laborious and unglamorous, and it generalises to every celebrated refutation and every celebrated defence in the history of doctrine. Most famous critiques are bundles. Most famous replies are partial. The interesting question is never whether a side won but which arguments are still standing when the scoring is done honestly, and the answer is frequently, as here, that the standing argument is neither the one the attacker advertised nor the one the defender feared, but a quieter one that both sides had reasons to leave in shadow: the attacker because it was not his occasion, the defender because it was not his answer.

## Errors in the other direction: what the critique's partisans overclaim

The audit has one more side to balance. If the Marxist summaries overclaim for the reply, the Austrian and liberal summaries overclaim for the critique, and their overclaims deserve the same treatment. The first is the claim that Bohm-Bawerk refuted Marx. He refuted, at most, a derivation and damaged a consistency claim; the theory of exploitation read socially, the analysis of the value form, and the historical framework were outside his targets, as the scope section of this article records, and a refutation that leaves its object's most influential parts standing is a victory in a province paraded as a victory in a war. The second overclaim is that the labour theory died in 1896. It died, as an academic orthodoxy, over decades and for many reasons, among them the rise of equilibrium theory's mathematical prestige and the profession's changing questions; within heterodox economics it never died at all, and the empirical literature on price and value correlations is its continuing life, whatever that literature's ultimate verdict. The third overclaim is the subtlest: that because the derivation fails, the questions it was meant to answer are pseudo questions. Why prices gravitate, what anchors the price system, how the surplus is distributed and by what right, are real questions that marginalism answers in its own way and that do not vanish because one answer's proof was invalid. A grading that ends in triumphalism, for either tradition, has failed at the task this article set itself, which was never to award the dispute but to sort its arguments. The sorting leaves the critic with the strongest single argument and the defender with the larger surviving estate, and that untidy distribution is what an honest scoreboard looks like.

## The system as an accusation

The essay's title contains its largest unexamined premise, and examining it is a fitting place to end the grading. To announce the close of a system is to assume that the object under examination is a system: a body of doctrine whose parts support one another so completely that damage at the foundation brings down the whole edifice, and whose author intended exactly that mutual support. Nineteenth century thought was addicted to systems in this strong sense, and Marx, formed by Hegel, courted the description more frankly than most, presenting his work as a totality in which the analysis of the commodity already contained the fate of the capitalist order in embryo. The premise flatters both parties to the dispute. It flatters the critic, whose local victories appreciate in value as the edifice they threaten grows more integrated. It flatters the defender, whose local rescues likewise appreciate, since a system saved at one point is saved entire. And it deforms the judgement of both, by forcing every argument to be graded as a verdict on the whole, when the arguments themselves concern parts: a derivation, a consistency claim, a mechanism, a magnitude.

The method of this article has been a refusal of that premise, and its results can be read as the refusal's justification. Graded as a system, Marxism in 1896 either stood or fell, and the century's polemics are the record of partisans insisting on one verdict or the other. Graded as a bundle of separable claims, the picture that emerges is untidier and truer: a founding derivation that fails as logic and survives as literature; a consistency claim surrendered at one level and defended, inconclusively, at another; a social theory of exploitation and of the commodity form that the critique never engaged and that went on to a long career in other disciplines; a rival construction whose positive system decayed as fast as its target's. Nothing in that picture licenses either camp's traditional summary, and everything in it was available, in principle, to a careful reader of 1904. That the careful reading had to wait for later generations, and then arrived piecemeal, in schools that each preserved one fragment of the whole picture, is the final evidence for the article's governing suspicion: that fame in theoretical disputes attaches to verdicts on systems, while truth attaches to arguments, and the two have rarely coincided. The reader who has followed the grading to this point possesses the less famous and more valuable of the two inheritances.

## The critic's end and the world that ended with him

Biography is not argument, and this article has graded arguments throughout; but the principals' ends cast a backward light that a reader may be permitted, in closing, to notice. Bohm-Bawerk died in August 1914, in the Tyrolean village where he had gone for the summer, within days of the mobilisation that dissolved the world his career had served. The empire whose finances he had thrice administered, whose university had housed the seminar where his opponents sat as students, whose currency and credit his theory of capital had been written to comprehend, ceased to exist within four years, and the German language economics of Vienna scattered into exile, emigration, and memory. He did not live to see the calculation debate his pupils would fight, the triumph of the marginalist framework in the postwar universities, or the strange second life of the doctrine he had audited, enthroned as the official philosophy of states commanding a third of the earth. Hilferding outlived him by twenty seven years and fared worse at history's hands: a minister in the Weimar Republic he had helped to found, an exile after 1933, and finally a prisoner handed over to the Gestapo in Vichy France, dead in custody in 1941, in circumstances never fully clarified. The young man who had answered the minister in the minister's own city was destroyed by the barbarism that consumed the republic of letters both men had inhabited, in which a finance minister could be answered, in print, at length, by a socialist medical student, and the answer could matter. The dispute graded in these pages belongs to that vanished republic, and its courtesy, its textual patience, and its assumption that arguments decide are among the republic's surviving monuments. To grade its arguments carefully, a century on, is one small way of keeping the republic's standards in circulation after its world has gone.

## The verdict restated

The four arguments, scored against the texts and against the one standard reply, stand in an order that reverses their fame. The empirical argument borrows its force and owns none. The derivation argument convicts the opening chapter's proof and is absorbed, at a price, by demoting the proof to a presentation. The contradiction argument establishes a genuine surrender at the level of individual prices and is met, in part, by aggregate identities whose sufficiency remains the technical dispute of the succeeding century. The inequality argument establishes that exchange, the phenomenon from which the whole construction was deduced, does not contain the equality the deduction required, and it is met by no argument in the standard reply at all, only by the relocation of the theory to ground where the argument does not apply, at the price of conceding that the theory is not about the exchange ratios its founding chapter analysed. Its defeater is known and stated: a demonstration that systematic regularities in the ratios require a magnitude fixed in production, of a kind divergent valuations leave unexplained. That demonstration has been gestured at by correlation studies and completed by nobody, and until it is completed the verdict stands, not as a slogan for either camp but as a precise statement about one argument, one reply, and the distance between them. Bohm-Bawerk's critique of Marx survives its century not as the demolition its title promised nor as the failure its opponents reported, but as the cleanest demonstration in the history of economics that a famous refutation and a famous defence can both be genuine, both be formidable, and still pass each other by, leaving the decisive point exactly where the quieter argument put it.

## For students and exam candidates

Students meeting this dispute in an examination should organise their preparation around the grading rather than around the fame. Learn the four arguments as four, with a sentence on each and a verdict on each, and expect the discriminating questions to ask which argument is strongest and why the standard reply leaves it unanswered. The cluster's exam guide works through the answering technique for the critiques cluster as a whole and should be your next stop after the grading here is secure. For structured revision of the primary material, the UPSC previous year question explorer at [ReportMedic](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html) lets you test how often value theory and its critics actually appear and in what forms. A [VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) card built on the four arguments table, one argument per face with its reply and verdict, converts this article into the revision format that survives contact with a timed paper.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The inequality argument is the cleanest available statement of what marginalism claims about exchange, and it teaches better than any textbook paraphrase because it arrives as an attack, with a named opponent and a quoted text. A productive seminar format sets the derivation and the critique's fourth chapter side by side for one session, the reply for a second, and devotes a third to the defeater: students who must specify what evidence would reverse the verdict learn more about both theories than students who must merely summarise them. The four arguments table works as a marking scaffold for essays on the dispute, since it forces the separation of arguments that weak answers merge, and the two senses of equal, magnitude and equivalence, give weaker students a distinction they can actually operate. Expect the strongest resistance from students who have met the dispute as a set piece with a known winner; the grading exercise, in which the winner changes by argument, is the corrective.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Researchers will find the live questions at the defeater's frontier: the empirical separation of labour content from rival cost measures in the prediction of exchange ratios, where the input and output literature continues without a settled result, and the philological questions around the opening chapter's revisions across the editions Marx supervised. The archival situation is favourable and underexploited; the correspondence around the third volume's reception, the seminar records of the Viennese years, and the Russian discussion's journals all reward work that the textbook tradition has not done. Journalists covering the periodic revivals of Marx in public debate should note the pattern this article documents, since it recurs in every revival: the critique is cited for its loudest argument, the reply for its existence rather than its contents, and the inequality argument, the one that bears on how exchange itself is conceived, goes unmentioned by both camps. A paragraph of grading is worth a page of commemoration, and the sources are short, translated, and in the public domain.

## The republic of footnotes

A last feature of the exchange deserves record, because it conditions everything a modern reader can learn from it: both principals argued from a shared library, and said so in their apparatus. The critique's footnotes engage, by name and page, the whole republic of the dispute: Knies, whose objection to the derivation it adopts and credits; Adler, whose earlier examination of Marx's foundations it cites; Lexis and Schmidt and Stiebeling, whose attempted solutions to the profit problem it examines before judging; Sombart, whose apology it honours with a chapter. Nothing is attributed to an opponent that cannot be found at the page cited, and the critic's severest judgements arrive attached to quotations the reader can check. The reply observes the same discipline from the other side, and Marx himself, whatever else is said of his methods, worked in the same republic: his notebooks were vast excerpting machines, his citations exact, his polemics, however savage, anchored in the texts they savaged. The three men disagreed about nearly everything except the rules of evidence, and the rules are why their disagreement remains instructive when the polemics of less disciplined contemporaries, on every side of the century's arguments, have become unreadable.

The republic's rules have a practical consequence for the grading that this article has attempted. Because the principals quoted one another accurately and at length, a later reader can reconstruct the dispute from any one of its texts and can audit any summary, including the summaries embedded in the texts themselves, against the quoted evidence. Few controversies in the social sciences are so auditable, and the audit habit is the inheritance this article most hopes to pass on. When a famous critique is next summarised for you, in a lecture or a textbook or a pamphlet of either church, the republic's question is the one to ask: show me the argument, at the page, in the words, and then show me the answer meeting it. The dispute between the Austrian minister and the young socialist doctor survives that question better than most. Its strongest argument survives it best of all, which is why, a century and more after the republic that housed it perished, the argument is still standing in the open, waiting for the answer its defeater describes.

## Why the grading travels

The exercise performed on this dispute is worth exporting, because the pathology it corrects is not confined to Marxist economics. Every mature field keeps a small museum of famous refutations and famous defences, and the exhibits are almost always mislabelled in the same ways: a bundle of arguments remembered as one argument, a partial reply remembered as a complete one, a verdict on a proof remembered as a verdict on a theory, a verdict on a theory remembered as a verdict on a worldview. The labels persist because they are useful to everyone except the reader: to teachers, who need the set piece to fit the lecture; to partisans, who need the trophy or the martyrdom; to textbook writers, who need the paragraph to end where the page does. Against the museum's labels there is only the unglamorous procedure used here, enumeration and contact: list the arguments, state each from its own text at the strength its author gave it, state the answer likewise, and record, for each pair, whether the two met. The procedure is slow, and it is meant to be. Speed is what produced the labels. A reader who has watched the procedure rescue one quiet argument from a century of mislabelling has acquired something more durable than a verdict on Bohm-Bawerk or on Marx: a way of reading any celebrated controversy as what it actually is, a bundle of separable encounters between arguments and answers, in which the honours are usually divided, the decisive point is usually the quiet one, and the summary you were given is usually the first casualty of an honest count.

## A closing note on the two tempers

Underneath the arguments stand two tempers, and a reader who has come this far may be allowed a word about them, since tempers decide which arguments a thinker can hear. The critic's temper is that of the auditor: patient, exact, a little weary, convinced that most intellectual mischief comes from unexamined words and that a claim's dignity is inversely proportional to its vagueness. The defender's temper is that of the advocate for a cause that is also a hope: systematic, combative, convinced that the questions a theory refuses are as damning as the answers it fumbles, and that precision purchased by narrowing the field is a swindle practised on the powerless. Neither temper is a vice, and the century needed both, the auditor to keep its concepts honest and the advocate to keep its questions large. The misfortune of the exchange is that the tempers never quite engaged, the auditor grading a proof the advocate had already abandoned, the advocate defending a vision the auditor had never contested. Readers inherit both tempers every time they open a theoretical text, and the discipline this article has practised, grading argument by argument while keeping the large questions in view, is simply the two tempers made to share a room. The room is uncomfortable. It is also the only one in which the dispute between Bohm-Bawerk and Marx has ever been honestly settled, in the parts where settlement was possible, and honestly left open, in the parts where it was not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is Bohm-Bawerk's critique of Marx?

Published in 1896, twelve months after Capital's third volume appeared, the critique argues that Marx's completed system fails on four counts: its volumes contradict each other on how commodities exchange, its opening derivation of value is invalid, exchange itself requires no equality between the things traded, and the profit rates the theory predicts do not appear. Its author was the Austrian school's leading theorist, and the essay's method is quotation and close reasoning rather than polemic, which is why the Marxist movement treated it as the attack that had to be answered and commissioned the reply that became standard.

### Q: What is the strongest argument in the critique?

The inequality argument. It holds that an exchange happens only because each party values what is received more than what is surrendered, so no equality between the goods, in any magnitude, exists for a shared substance to measure, and the inference from ratios to embodied labour loses its foundation. The other charges meet replies of varying strength: the contradiction meets the aggregate identities, the derivation charge is absorbed by demoting the passage, and the empirical charge borrows the contradiction's machinery. This one meets no reply in the standard defence, which changes the theory's subject instead of engaging it.

### Q: Is there a contradiction between the volumes of Capital?

On the surface of the texts, yes, in a precise sense. The first volume lays down that goods exchange at their values and calls deviations accidental breaches of the law. The third volume derives prices of production by adding a uniform rate of profit to cost price, which depart from values systematically for every capital whose composition differs from the social average. The defence answers that the law survives in the aggregates, total price equalling total value and total profit equalling total surplus value. Whether that survival suffices is the technical dispute the transformation literature inherited.

### Q: What is the third thing argument?

It is the name given to the reasoning of Capital's opening chapter. Marx writes an exchange as an equation between two commodities and infers that both must be equal to a third thing, a common element present in each in the same magnitude. Setting aside use value, he finds one shared property remaining, being a product of labour, and identifies abstract labour as that third thing, the substance of value. The critique attacks the argument twice: the elimination overlooks other shared properties, and the equation it starts from expresses no equality that would require a third thing at all.

### Q: Is the derivation in the opening chapter of Capital valid?

As a proof, on the grading defended in this article, no. Its survey of common properties is incomplete, since scarcity, being demanded, and being appropriated are properties commodities share no less universally than being produced by labour, and its field excludes priced things that embody no labour, land above all, so the survivor is certified by the boundaries drawn around the search. Many sympathetic readers reach a similar destination by a kinder road, treating the passage as a presentation of conclusions established elsewhere rather than as the demonstration it appears to be.

### Q: What did Hilferding reply?

Writing in 1904, Rudolf Hilferding answered that value belongs among the social categories rather than the technical magnitudes: the labour theory analyses how production is organised among producers who work independently of one another, and how a person's labour becomes part of society's labour only through the sale of its product, and it is not a competing price mechanics to be graded against marginalism. He added that the Austrian theory takes the individual, his preferences, and the property order as given, while historical materialism explains where all three come from. The reply became the standard Marxist defence and is still the framing through which the critique is usually taught.

### Q: Does the reply answer the inequality argument?

No, and the grading turns on the point. The inequality argument attacks an inference about exchange ratios: because goods trade in definite proportions, one element, present in both in the same amount, was said to exist in them. The reply responds that the theory is not chiefly about exchange ratios but about social relations. That response may defend the theory on its new ground, and the ground proved fertile in Hilferding's later work, but it does not defend the inference, which the opening chapter performs in terms of ratios from its first paragraph. The charge and the answer pass without contact.

### Q: Why do labour intensive industries not have higher profit rates?

Because competition among capitals equalises the rate of profit across branches. Capital leaves low return employments and enters high return ones until the differential disappears, so returns settle by the size of the capital advanced rather than by the labour it sets in motion. The fact is the empirical face of the critique's first argument: on the labour theory's premises, surplus arises only from labour, so branches employing more labour should generate more surplus relative to their advances. The theory explains the equalisation through prices of production, which is the very divergence from values the contradiction charge targets.

### Q: How did the critic's own theory of value fare?

No better, in the long run, than the theory he attacked. His marginal utility account of value was absorbed into the mainstream so completely that its origin is forgotten, but his distinctive construction, the theory of interest built on time preference and the productivity of roundabout production, lost its central magnitude, since the average production period resisted every definition that did not presuppose the rate of interest it existed to explain; subsequent controversies over capital deepened the difficulty through reswitching. What survives of the positive system survives in fragments, time preference as one determinant of interest among several, inside successor theories that no longer cite it.

### Q: Is this critique still cited?

Yes, wherever the labour theory of value is taught or contested, though usually in a distorted form. Citations overwhelmingly concern the contradiction between the volumes, the argument that generated the transformation literature, while the inequality argument, the strongest of the four on this article's grading, is seldom mentioned, partly because the marginalist foundations it defended became too familiar to need stating. The reply is likewise cited more often as existing than as read. Scholars of Marx, of the Austrian school, and of the history of economics continue to work on the exchange, and the empirical question its defeater specifies remains open in the input and output literature.

### Q: How does the inequality argument grade the standard reply?

It grades the reply as a relocation rather than an answer. Measured against the other charges, the reply performs respectably: it has content against the contradiction, a genre defence against the derivation charge, and the equalisation mechanism against the empirical charge. Against the claim that exchange embodies no equality, it offers the redescription of the theory as social analysis, which concedes the premise the charge needed, that the theory's founding argument was conducted in terms of exchange ratios and cannot be defended there. A defence that wins on every ground except the ground of the strongest charge has lost the campaign while winning its battles.

### Q: What should a student separate between a theory of exchange ratios and a theory of production?

The questions each answers and the evidence each needs. A theory of exchange ratios must explain why specific amounts of dissimilar goods exchange against one another, and it is tested against the behaviour of prices and the reasoning of the parties who set them. A theory of production and its social relations explains how a society organises its labour, where a surplus arises, and how classes are positioned, and it is tested against the structure of the economy and its history. Confusing the two lets a defence win on one field a charge that was brought on the other, which is the exact manoeuvre the standard reply performs.

### Q: Why does exchange not require the things exchanged to be equal?

Because the motive of each party is a gain that equality would abolish. A trader surrenders a good only when what comes back is worth more to him than what goes, in his own estimation and circumstances, and the other party's estimation runs the opposite way; were the bundles equal in both estimations, neither would bear the trouble, cost, and risk of trading, and the goods would stay where they were. The definite ratio at which trade settles records where the opposed valuations balance under competition. It is an outcome of divergences, not the disclosure of a magnitude the goods shared before the parties met.

### Q: What order does the four arguments table impose on the assessment?

It orders the four by what became of each when the standard reply was brought against it. The empirical argument stands last, a satellite of the contradiction. The derivation argument stands next, potent against the chapter it aims at yet absorbable once that chapter is demoted. The contradiction stands next, a surrender, genuine where individual prices are concerned, partly met by the aggregate identities. The inequality argument stands first and alone, unanswered by the standard reply and answerable only through the demonstration its defeater specifies: that the ratios exhibit a regularity no account built from divergent valuations can supply, short of a magnitude fixed on the side of production.

### Q: What separates relocating a claim from defending the inference the text actually makes?

A defence meets the objection where the claim was first staked, by vindicating the inference, the premise, and the exclusions on their own terms. A relocation keeps the claim's words and standing while quietly restaking it where the objection cannot follow, as when a theory attacked over exchange ratios is upheld as social analysis instead. Relocation can be honest progress when it is declared and the old claim given up in plain words. Here the giving up was never declared, so generations of readers filed a change of subject as a refutation.
