Is Marxism a science? The question is asked constantly and answered carelessly, because the argument about it usually runs on a charge that its own author did not make. The best-known critic of Marxism in the philosophy of science, Karl Popper, did not hold that Marx's theory was never scientific. He held almost the opposite: that the theory began as science, staked itself on predictions that could have failed, and did fail; and that what destroyed its scientific standing was not the failing but the response to it, the reinterpretation of theory and evidence by which its followers rescued it from refutation. Any assessment that starts from the popular version of the charge, in which Marxism is dismissed as unfalsifiable from birth, starts from an error, and the error is committed by defenders as often as by opponents, since a defence aimed at a misstated charge leaves the real one untouched.

![A desk with works on the philosophy of science, a notebook holding the criterion table, and index cards for the failed predictions, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-43.webp)

The second preliminary is that the verdict depends on the criterion, and the criterion most often used in this argument is one that most philosophers of science have abandoned. Falsification, the demand that a scientific theory expose itself to refutation and stand condemned when refuted, describes no actual science for long, because every living theory faces anomalies and every living theory protects itself with auxiliary assumptions while it works out whether the anomaly is fatal. The stronger framework, developed by Imre Lakatos, asks a different question: whether a research programme's modifications predict novel facts that are later confirmed, which he called a progressive problemshift, or whether its modifications merely accommodate what is already known after the event, which he called degenerating. This article applies that framework, and it does not flatter the defence. On the better test, Marxism's record is mixed to poor, and its characteristic response to failed prediction has been accommodation rather than novel prediction. Correcting the charge does not rescue the theory; it relocates the trial to a court where the evidence is harder to evade.

## The question and its criteria: what science is being asked about

The question whether Marxism is a science asks, in its precise form, whether the tradition's central propositions are testable and have survived testing, or whether the tradition has preserved them by adding auxiliary assumptions whenever a prediction failed. That formulation already contains the whole dispute. It distinguishes the propositions from the tradition that carries them, because a proposition can be testable while a tradition behaves untestably, protecting the words by changing what they are allowed to mean. It distinguishes the original theory from its modifications, because the assessment of a living doctrine is an assessment of a sequence, not of a single document. And it leaves open the standard by which survival is judged, which is where the argument's real work lies, since the parties rarely disagree about the historical record as much as they disagree about what the record shows.

Three different questions are habitually folded into the one slogan. The first is logical: are the theory's claims framed so that evidence could count against them at all? The second is historical: when evidence did count against them, what did the tradition do? The third is comparative: whatever standard is applied to Marxism, is it applied with the same severity to the frameworks it competes with? A debate that keeps the three apart can reach a verdict; a debate that slides between them produces only the feeling of one. The logical question is the easiest, and Popper's answer to it is the one this article begins from: the claims were framed to be tested, and were tested. The historical question is where the charge lives. The comparative question is where the defence makes its strongest stand, and it deserves to be heard at full strength before it is answered.

### What is being asked when a doctrine is asked to prove it is a science?

Beneath the wording sits a demand about conduct under pressure: whether the doctrine's claims were staked against evidence in advance, and whether its carriers surrendered or revised honestly when the evidence arrived. The question is not whether the doctrine resembles physics in dress, but whether it behaves as inquiry behaves when inquiry becomes uncomfortable.

What science means in this dispute therefore needs care, because the word carries a prestige that both sides want to borrow. For the opponent, to call Marxism unscientific is to place it with astrology, outside the company of rational belief. For the defender, to call it scientific is to claim the authority of the laboratory for a doctrine of society, and Marx himself encouraged that claim, presenting his work as the discovery of laws of motion of capitalist society comparable in rigour to the laws the natural sciences had found. Both borrowings distort the assessment. The philosophy of science does not distribute a single badge; it offers several criteria, which grade the same body of work differently, and the honest procedure is to set the criteria out, apply each in turn, and report what each yields. That is the procedure of this article, and the criterion table in a later section is its instrument.

The framework at issue throughout is historical materialism, the account of how modes of production structure societies and succeed one another, whose full statement belongs to [historical materialism complete guide](/2010/01/15/historical-materialism-complete-guide/). This article does not re-derive that framework; it puts the framework and its descendants on trial under the criteria, which is a different operation. Nor does it conduct the empirical audit of the economics, which belongs to [testing marxist economics](/2016/08/01/testing-marxist-economics/). The question here is methodological before it is factual: given what the record contains, what does a fair test conclude about the way this tradition has handled its own claims?

One more distinction disciplines everything that follows. A theory, a research programme, and a movement are three different defendants, and they can receive three different verdicts. A theory is a set of propositions; it can be true or false, testable or empty. A research programme is a theory plus the sequence of repairs and extensions its workers produce over decades; it can be progressive or degenerating, which are verdicts about its conduct over time rather than about any single claim. A movement is a body of people and institutions with loyalties, enemies, and a need to survive; its behaviour explains why programmes are defended long after their conduct has become degenerating, but loyalty is not itself a methodological category. Much of the confusion in this debate comes from trying the movement when the indictment names the programme, or acquitting the programme because the theory, at its birth, was framed honourably. This article tries the programme, since the programme is where the evidence of a century and a half accumulates.

## The charge as made: what Popper actually claimed

The charge must be quoted from its author, and its author is Karl Popper, who set it out most fully in *Conjectures and Refutations*, in the chapter that began as an address on science, conjectures and refutations. His account of Marxism there is precise, and its precision is what popular versions lose. Marx's theory of history, he wrote, was a genuinely scientific attempt: it made predictions, about the coming of revolution, about the increasing misery of the working class, about the course of capitalist development, which the future could have confirmed and instead refuted. The failure he charged was not the failure of the predictions. Every science he admired had failed predictions in its past. The failure was that the theory's followers, confronted with refutation, re-interpreted both the theory and the evidence in order to make them agree, and in doing so rescued the theory from refutation at the price of destroying its scientific character. A theory that is reinterpreted until no evidence can touch it has not been vindicated; it has been withdrawn from the jurisdiction of evidence altogether.

### Did Popper say Marxism was never scientific?

no. He held that Marx's theory was originally scientific and made testable predictions, that those predictions failed, and that its adherents then reinterpreted theory and evidence to preserve it. His charge concerns the response to refutation rather than the theory's original character.

The structure of the charge matters more than its verdict, because the structure is what both camps misstate. Popper's complaint is diachronic: it concerns a before and an after. Before, there stood a theory with empirical content, forbidding certain states of affairs and therefore risking itself against them. After, there stood the same vocabulary operated by a method that converted every apparent refutation into a confirmation or an irrelevance. His famous comparison was with the doctrines he took to be pseudosciences from the start, the theories of Freud and Adler, whose adherents could explain any conceivable observation in the theory's own terms and therefore explained nothing. Marxism, on his account, was not born in that condition; it was educated into it. The distinction is not a courtesy. It determines what a defence would have to show: not that the original theory was testable, which the prosecution concedes, but that the tradition's subsequent conduct was the conduct of inquiry rather than of preservation.

Popper returned to Marxism from a second direction in *The Open Society and Its Enemies* and *The Poverty of Historicism*, where the target was historicism, the claim to have discovered laws of historical development that license prophecy about the future course of society. That critique, of the logic of historical prophecy and its political consequences, is a separate argument with its own literature, and it is treated in its own place at [popper critique of marxism](/2012/09/15/popper-critique-of-marxism/). The two charges are often run together, and separating them is part of stating the present one correctly. The historicism charge attacks the kind of claim being made, prophecy dressed as law. The refutation charge attacks the handling of claims after they failed. A defender could in principle concede the second while contesting the first, or the reverse, and an assessment that fuses them can grade neither.

### Why do defenders and opponents misstate the charge in the same way?

Each camp profits from the flattened version. Opponents gain a dismissal that needs no engagement with the theory's content, while defenders gain an accusation they can refute by pointing to any testable claim the tradition ever made. The accurate charge, aimed at the tradition's conduct after refutation, offers neither camp its easy victory.

The misstatement by opponents is the louder one. In the flattened version, Popper is enlisted as having shown that Marxism was never a science at all, a piece of metaphysics masquerading in scientific dress, and the enlistment does polemical work that the genuine charge cannot do, because the genuine charge requires the opponent to discuss the predictions, their failure, and the repairs, which is slow and scholarly labour. The flattened version requires only a label. It also backfires on the opponent who uses it, since any informed defender can produce the passage in which the theory's original scientific character is granted, and the opponent's credibility on the whole subject falls with the single error.

The misstatement by defenders is subtler and does more damage to the defence itself. It runs: Popper called Marxism unfalsifiable; but Marxism plainly makes falsifiable claims; therefore the charge collapses. The middle premise is true and the conclusion does not follow, because the charge was never that the claims, read one by one, lack testable content. The charge is that the tradition, as an institution of belief, arranged its interpretations so that the testable content was never allowed to register a loss. Answering the flattened version leaves that charge standing exactly where it stood, and a defence that spends its force there has defended a position nobody needed to attack. The replies the tradition has made to the standard critiques are collected at [marxist replies to critiques](/2019/12/01/marxist-replies-to-critiques/); the point here is diagnostic, that the most common reply in circulation answers a charge its author did not bring.

There is a further reason the accurate version of the charge deserves a hearing before any verdict on criteria, and it is that the charge identifies a mechanism rather than merely awarding a grade. Reinterpretation is something a tradition does, observably, in texts that can be dated and compared. The prediction appears in one form; the event arrives in another; the commentary then explains that the prediction, properly understood, anticipated the event all along, or that the event is a temporary detour on a road whose destination is unchanged, or that the terms of the prediction referred to something other than what every reader had taken them to refer to. Whether that mechanism operated in this tradition, and how often, and with what exceptions, is an empirical question about intellectual history, and it can be investigated without first settling the philosophy. The philosophy becomes necessary when the defence replies that reinterpretation is what all sciences do, which is true in part and is the strongest card the defence holds. That reply is examined in its own section below; the mechanism it defends is first worth watching in operation on the predictions themselves.

## The predictions at issue: revolution where it was not expected

A theory earns the title of scientific, on any criterion that involves prediction, by staking claims the world can disappoint. Marx's theory staked several, and the three that dominate the assessment are the location of revolution, the trajectory of working-class living standards, and the shape of the class structure. Each was stated by Marx or by the tradition's founding documents in forms definite enough for the following century to grade them, and each received from that century an answer the theory had not offered. The grading must be done claim by claim, because the tradition's later conduct differed by claim: some were quietly dropped, some were reinterpreted, some were relocated, and the differences are the evidence for the verdict on method.

The first prediction concerns where socialist revolution would occur. The theory's logic pointed to the most advanced industrial countries. Capitalism was to prepare its own supersession where it was most developed: where the productive forces had been most fully socialised within the factory, where the working class formed the great majority, where the contradictions between social production and private appropriation stood most naked. England, Germany, France, and the industrial United States were the terrain the argument surveyed. The Manifesto's geography and the analysis in *Capital* agree on the direction of travel: the advanced countries show the less developed the image of their own future. Revolution was to be the act of a mature system against its own maturity.

### Where was revolution expected, and where did it arrive?

The theory's logic assigned revolution to the most advanced industrial societies, where capitalism had most fully developed its contradictions and its successor class. The revolutions that arrived came in the least advanced great power of Europe and then in agrarian societies beyond it, inverting the sequence the argument had projected.

What arrived instead was revolution in Russia, an empire whose industrial working class was a small minority amid a peasant ocean, and after it revolutions or revolutionary regimes across the agrarian world, while the advanced industrial countries produced reform, incorporation, and in the German case a revolution that was defeated and a republic that channelled the working class into parliamentary and trade union forms. The sequence was not merely delayed; it ran backwards. The countries ripest by the theory's own criteria proved the most capable of absorbing their working classes into durable institutions, and the country least ripe produced the seizure of power that the tradition then had to explain. An explanation was duly produced, and its quality as method is the subject of a later section: the theory of the weakest link, and the doctrine of combined and uneven development, recast the anomaly as a discovery, arguing that backwardness itself, under the pressure of a world system, could concentrate contradictions more explosively than maturity. Whether that recasting predicted anything new, or only redescribed what had happened in the theory's vocabulary, is the Lakatosian question, and it will be put precisely.

For the falsificationist grading, the matter is simpler and was the core of Popper's historical claim. A prediction was made, in forms repeated across the tradition's canonical texts; the contrary occurred; and the tradition did not record the prediction as lost. It is worth pausing on how strong the original stake was, because later softening sometimes suggests the theory never committed itself. The commitment was real. Marx expected the English working class to be the lever of the transformation and organised politically around that expectation. Engels, surveying the failure of the English movement to move as predicted, wrote explanations of the delay that already display the pattern this article is tracking: the prediction is retained, and an auxiliary condition, the sharing of the bourgeoisie in the profits of world monopoly and empire, is introduced to explain why the lever has not yet moved. The auxiliary was not absurd; it pointed at something real in the distribution of imperial income. The methodological question is not whether such explanations can be reasonable in the instance. It is what the sequence of such explanations, repeated across a century, amounts to as a pattern of conduct.

## Immiseration: the prediction and its two readings

The second prediction is the immiseration thesis, the claim of the working class's increasing misery, and it is the most instructive of the three because the dispute over what Marx claimed is genuine. On the absolute reading, the claim is that capitalist accumulation drives working-class living standards down: wages pressed toward subsistence, insecurity deepening, the reserve army swelling, pauperism growing as wealth grows. Passages in *Capital* support the reading, including the general law of capitalist accumulation, in which the accumulation of wealth at one pole is presented as the accumulation of misery, agony of toil, and degradation at the other. On the relative reading, the claim concerns shares and relations rather than bread: the worker's portion of the social product he helps create falls relative to the portion commanded by capital, and his position worsens relative to the wealth around him even as his absolute consumption rises. Passages support that reading too, including Marx's insistence that a house may grow while the palace beside it grows faster, leaving the householder relatively worse housed in social terms though his walls are unchanged.

### What did the immiseration claim assert, and how is it read two ways?

On the absolute reading, accumulation presses working-class living standards downward as wealth concentrates. On the relative reading, the claim concerns shares: the worker's portion of the product falls against capital's portion, and his standing worsens relative to surrounding wealth even where his consumption rises. The texts sustain both constructions.

The record of the following century, in the industrial countries whose trajectory the prediction described, runs against the absolute reading. Real wages rose across long periods; the length of the working day fell under legal and collective pressure; mass consumption of goods once confined to the wealthy became ordinary; the pauperised stratum the theory expected to swell became, in the advanced economies, a minority condition rather than the working class's general fate. This article states no figures for wages or inequality, in keeping with the protocol of this cluster; the evidence and its controversies are set out in the companion empirical audit, at [testing marxist economics](/2016/08/01/testing-marxist-economics/), and the direction of the record is not seriously in dispute between the schools, whatever they dispute about its causes and its distribution. What matters for method is the tradition's handling. The absolute claim, in the forms in which generations of the movement's popular literature had broadcast it, was not surrendered. It was exchanged for the relative claim, and the exchange was frequently performed as a clarification, as though the relative reading had been the doctrine all along and only careless readers had thought otherwise.

That exchange is the single cleanest exhibit in the entire case, and it deserves to be weighed without heat. A clarification that narrows a claim after the evidence has turned, presented as the claim's original meaning, is accommodation in its textbook form. The relative thesis may well be the better sociology; relative deprivation is a real mechanism, and a doctrine of shares asks a question about distribution that rising averages cannot answer. Nothing in the methodological verdict denies the relative thesis its content. The verdict concerns the bookkeeping. A research programme that improves its claims under the pressure of evidence is doing what programmes should do; a programme that records the improvement as though no loss had occurred, and teaches the improved claim as the founder's plain meaning, has protected its authority at the expense of its ledger. Lakatos's framework, set out below, grades exactly this difference: modifications are progressive when they buy new content, degenerating when they only re-describe the loss as a gain already possessed.

The defender of the tradition has a further move available here, and fairness requires stating it. The absolute reading, it is said, was always a vulgarisation; Marx's considered texts, on wages especially, describe a value of labour power containing a historical and moral element, rising with civilisation's standards, which is incompatible with a crude law of falling subsistence. The considered texts do contain that element, and the scholarly point is sound. But the methodological question is not only what the founder's best text can be made to say under expert reconstruction. It is what the tradition, in its operative form, predicted and taught. A programme's predictions are the ones its carriers stake in the world, in manifestos, party programmes, and popular expositions, and by that standard the absolute expectation was staked, widely and for decades. Retreating to the founder's most defensible sentence, after the operative version fails, is itself one of the protective manoeuvres the charge describes, and noticing this does not require denying that the defensible sentence exists.

## Polarisation: the two-class prediction and the strata between

The third prediction is the polarisation of society into two great classes. The Manifesto's opening movement describes bourgeois society as simplifying class antagonisms, splitting the social order ever more cleanly into the owners of the means of production and the propertyless who sell their labour. Intermediate formations, the small masters, shopkeepers, artisans, and peasants of the older order, were to sink into the proletariat as capital destroyed their independence, and the modern professions and managers, insofar as the theory noticed them, were to sort themselves to one pole or the other. The prediction gave the theory its political architecture: a society polarised into two camps is a society in which the revolutionary subject is unmistakable and the final conflict is structurally guaranteed.

### What became of the classes between the two poles?

They expanded. Salaried managers, professionals, technicians, administrators, and a persistent small-business stratum grew with the advanced economies rather than dissolving into wage labour of the classical kind, and the occupational structure of the rich societies came to display a broad middle where the prediction had projected a narrowing waist.

The following century expanded the middle instead. Managerial and professional employment grew with the scale of firms and states; technical and administrative occupations multiplied; the self-employed stratum contracted in some sectors and proved stubbornly persistent in others; and the working class itself differentiated by skill, sector, security, and nation into strata whose interests could diverge as sharply as the theory expected the two great classes' interests to diverge. The class structure of the advanced societies, examined in [does the working class still exist](/2011/11/01/does-the-working-class-still-exist/), is not a waist narrowing toward two poles but a distribution with a broad and politically decisive centre. The tradition's responses to this record are again the methodological evidence. One response redefined the proletariat functionally, counting as workers all who sell labour power and own no productive property, which sweeps the manager and the surgeon into the same class as the cleaner and restores the two-class picture by definition. Another relocated polarisation to the global scale, arguing that the middle strata of the rich countries are beneficiaries of a polarisation now enacted between nations, so that the simplification proceeds on the world map though not within any national society. A third conceded the descriptive failure and rebuilt the theory of class around multiple axes of advantage, at the price of the original architecture's clean political conclusion.

Each response has intellectual merits that this article does not need to deny, and each illustrates a different grade of methodological conduct. The definitional response is the weakest: a class concept widened until the prediction cannot fail has purchased consistency at the price of content, and the widened concept then does no predictive work, since no observable distribution of occupations could refute it. The global relocation is the most interesting, because it contains a genuine theoretical idea, that the unit of class formation might be the world system rather than the national society. But as deployed it is an accommodation in form: it was produced after the national record turned, it redescribes that record in the theory's favour, and the new claim it generates, about the political behaviour of the rich countries' salaried strata as beneficiaries of global polarisation, is one the tradition has been content to leave untested in any risky form. The reconstructive response, rebuilding class analysis on plural foundations, is the most honest and the least protective, and it is correspondingly the one that travels under names increasingly distant from the original programme. The pattern across the three predictions can now be stated. In each case the original stake was definite; in each case the record turned; in each case the tradition's dominant response preserved the doctrine's authority by reinterpretation, relocation, or redefinition rather than by recording a loss and buying new content. That pattern is the prosecution's case under any criterion. The remaining questions are whether the criterion most used to convict is a fair one, and whether a fairer one acquits.

## The response to refutation: reinterpretation as the charge's centre

Before the criteria are examined, the mechanism at the charge's centre deserves a section of its own, because it is the observable behaviour that every criterion is ultimately grading. Reinterpretation, as the charge describes it, is not ordinary theoretical development. All inquiry revises. The mark of the protective reinterpretation is its direction of fit: the revision is chosen not because it opens new questions but because it closes an old wound, and its content is exhausted by the service it performs for the doctrine's survival. Three forms recur in this tradition's history, and they have been visible in the sections above. The first is semantic descent, in which a term's meaning is narrowed or shifted after the event, as when increasing misery became relative misery. The second is relocation, in which the claim is transferred to a terrain where the refuting evidence does not reach, as when polarisation moved from the national society to the globe. The third is postponement, in which the prediction's horizon is extended without limit, so that failure at any date becomes prematurity rather than error, and the theory's timetable, once its proudest precision, dissolves into an unspecified future that no calendar can embarrass.

### What does rescuing a theory from refutation look like in practice?

It looks like a sequence of small, locally reasonable adjustments: a term clarified after its plain sense fails, a claim relocated to a scale where the evidence cannot follow, a deadline allowed to lapse into an open future. Each step can be defended in isolation; the rescue is visible only in the sequence.

Postponement deserves a word of its own, because it is the form least visible to the tradition's own members. A prediction with no date cannot be refuted by any date, and Marx himself was often careful with dates, sometimes burned by them, as when the crisis he expected in a given year arrived on schedule in form but not in consequence. The movement he founded was less careful, and its century is littered with imminent expectations whose imminence quietly renewed itself. The methodological point is not that long horizons are illegitimate in social theory; geological and demographic theories work in long horizons honourably. It is that a horizon extended after each disappointment, without the extension itself generating new content, converts a prediction into a posture. The posture can still inspire, organise, and console, which is the territory of the faith framing examined later. What it can no longer do is claim the epistemic standing of a forecast that risked itself and survived.

Set against the mechanism, the counter-evidence within the tradition should be recorded now, because the verdict at the end of this article is mixed rather than total, and its mixed character is earned in details like these. The tradition contained, from early on, internal critics who named the protective manoeuvres as they occurred. Rosa Luxemburg warned against the substitution of organisational loyalty for analysis. Karl Kautsky, in his later polemics, accused the Bolshevik tradition of converting Marxism into a state creed immune to evidence, a charge whose source inside the tradition gives it a standing no external critic could claim. Eduard Bernstein, earliest and most consequential of the internal revisionists, looked at the record on immiseration and polarisation in the eighteen nineties and concluded, in print, that the predictions had failed and the theory must be revised to match the facts. Bernstein's treatment by the orthodox is itself evidence in the case: his revision was answered not as a contribution to the programme's ledger but as heresy, and the word revisionist entered the tradition's vocabulary as an accusation. A programme confident of its method can afford to grade its losses; the institutional reflex that met Bernstein suggests a movement protecting something other than a method, and the faith sections below return to what that something might be.

None of this yet settles the assessment, because the defence has a principled reply that must now be given its full weight. The reply is that the behaviour described, auxiliary repair under the pressure of anomaly, is not the pathology of a creed but the normal metabolism of science itself, and that the criterion which convicts Marxism of it, naive falsification, would convict every science that ever survived its first decade. If that reply is sound, the charge collapses into a truism about all theorising, and the prosecution must find a better criterion or withdraw. The next two sections take the reply seriously: first by conceding what is true in it, that falsification is the wrong test, and then by setting out the better test the philosophy of science actually produced, on which the case can be tried again with cleaner instruments.

## Why falsification is the wrong test

The defence begins from solid ground, and the ground should be conceded without reservation before anything is built on it. Falsification, in the simple form in which it circulates in this debate, is not the operative criterion of any mature science, and most philosophers of science have rejected it as a description of how theories actually live and die. The reasons are structural, not charitable. A theory never meets evidence alone. Between the theory and the observation stands a scaffolding of auxiliary assumptions: about instruments, about background conditions, about the interpretation of measurements, about which of the theory's several consequences is being tested in a given case. When a prediction fails, logic alone cannot say whether the theory or one of the auxiliaries is at fault. This is the Duhem problem, named for the physicist and philosopher Pierre Duhem, and it is not a loophole invented for Marxism's benefit; it is a permanent feature of testing, as familiar in astronomy as in economics.

### Why do anomalies not falsify a theory on their own?

Because a prediction is drawn from a theory joined to auxiliary assumptions about conditions and measurement, and a failed prediction indicts the whole conjunction without identifying the guilty member. Judgement, not logic, must apportion the blame, and judgement can honestly apportion it to an auxiliary, sometimes for years.

The history of the sciences Popper admired is a history of anomalies absorbed, deferred, and occasionally dissolved, by exactly the manoeuvres the charge calls rescue. Newtonian mechanics faced the anomalous motion of Uranus, and its carriers did not surrender the theory; they posited an unseen planet, and found Neptune, converting an anomaly into the theory's most celebrated triumph. The same mechanics faced the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion, posited no planet that observation would ratify, and carried the anomaly for decades until a successor theory explained it from deeper principles. Nothing in the logic of the two cases distinguishes them in advance. In one, the auxiliary repair was genius; in the other, it was a dead end honestly carried. What distinguishes healthy from pathological repair is therefore not the repairing but what the repairing yields over time: new content confirmed, or only the wound dressed. That distinction is the seed of the better criterion, and it shows why the simple falsificationist court cannot hear this case. In that court, Newton stands convicted beside Marx, and a criterion that convicts Newton of pseudoscience has indicted itself.

### What work do auxiliary assumptions do inside a theory?

They connect the theory's abstract claims to the concrete situations where evidence is gathered, specifying conditions, instruments, and initial states. They are indispensable and therefore permanently available for abuse, since any failure can be charged to them; the discipline lies in whether the charge, once made, generates consequences of its own that evidence can grade.

Popper himself, it should be recorded, knew the simple version of his criterion was a caricature of his position. His actual methodology contained rules against the conventionalist stratagems, the ad hoc repairs by which a theory might be immunised, and he distinguished repairs that increase a theory's testable content, which he welcomed, from repairs that decrease it, which he forbade. That distinction already points beyond falsification toward the criterion of content growth that Lakatos would formalise. But the concession cuts against the defence as much as for it. If even the falsificationist's own mature position grades repairs by whether they add content, then the defence cannot rest in the observation that repair is universal. It must show that this tradition's repairs added content of the risky kind, predictions of facts not already known, subsequently confirmed. That is a showing the defence is entitled to attempt and that this article's verdict explicitly invites, in a form stated precisely in a later section. What the defence cannot do is treat the universality of repair as an acquittal, because no serious criterion, including Popper's own, ever proposed to convict repair as such.

There is a second, quieter reason falsification fails as the test for a doctrine like Marxism, and it concerns the kind of claim a social theory makes. Many of the tradition's central propositions are tendency claims: they assert that a mechanism operates, other things equal, within systems that also contain counteracting mechanisms the theory itself names. The falling tendency of the rate of profit was stated by Marx alongside its counteracting influences in the same volume. A tendency claim is not refuted by an outcome in which the counteracting influences prevailed, and grading it requires reconstructing the mechanism's operation rather than reading a scoreboard. This is a genuine complication, and honest assessment must honour it; tendency claims are legitimate instruments across the sciences, from evolutionary biology to macroeconomics. But the complication has a price the defence rarely pays willingly. A theory whose claims are all tendencies with named counter-tendencies must specify, in advance, what the world would look like if the mechanism were absent or overwhelmed permanently, or it has withdrawn its claims from grading altogether. The tradition's practice, across the predictions examined above, was too often to claim the precision of law when forecasting and the elasticity of tendency when graded. A criterion adequate to this case must be able to price that oscillation, which is a further reason to move from the falsificationist's single question, was it refuted, to the programme historian's question, what did its sequence of versions yield.

## The criterion table: four tests and what each yields

The criteria can now be set side by side. The criterion table below is this article's findable artifact, and it is built to enforce the discipline the debate usually lacks: each criterion is stated by what it asks, then applied to Marxism, then applied to comparable frameworks in the social sciences, so that no verdict in the third column is purchased by a severity the fourth column escapes. The rows are ordered from the crudest test to the most discriminating. Falsification asks the least and has been rejected as a general standard. The progressive research programme criterion asks the most and is this article's centre. Between them sit explanatory adequacy, on which Marxism performs best, and the raw predictive record, on which the grading is severest. Reading the table vertically is the article in miniature: the answer to the title's question is not a word but a profile, and the profile's shape, strong where the tests are loose and weak where they are exacting, is itself the finding.

Two warnings govern the table's use. The first is that the rows are not interchangeable currencies. A strong showing on explanatory adequacy does not compensate a weak showing on the programme criterion, because the two answer different questions: a framework can illuminate everything and risk nothing, which is the profile of a metaphysics of genius rather than of a science. The second warning is that the fourth column is not decoration. Every judgement in the third column was formed by asking whether the same evidence, produced by a rival programme, would draw the same grade, and the sections that follow conduct that comparison in the open, first for the programme criterion that matters most and later, at length, for the field as a whole.

## The better criterion: Lakatos and the research programme

The framework this article adopts was set out by Imre Lakatos in his essay on falsification and the methodology of scientific research programmes, published in the volume *Criticism and the Growth of Knowledge* that he edited with Alan Musgrave. Lakatos had been Popper's student and colleague, and his project was to save what was true in falsification, its insistence that growth requires risk, while discarding what was false in it, the picture of theories dying by single refutations. His unit of appraisal is not the single theory but the research programme: a sequence of theories bound by a hard core of assumptions its practitioners refuse to abandon, protected by a belt of auxiliary hypotheses that may be revised freely, and guided by a positive heuristic, a plan for building models and digesting anomalies, and a negative heuristic, the instruction not to aim criticism at the core. Within that structure, revision is not a scandal but the programme's ordinary life. The scandal, and the glory, lie in the revision's yield.

### What makes a problemshift progressive rather than degenerating?

A shift is progressive when the revised theory predicts some novel fact, a fact improbable in the light of the programme's rivals or previously unknown, and at least some of its novel predictions are corroborated. A shift is degenerating when the revision only explains, after the event, the anomaly that provoked it.

The notion of the novel fact carries the framework's weight, and it needs precision, because the defence of any programme will ransack its history for predictions that came true. A novel fact is not any fact the theory can be made to fit. It is a fact the theory's construction was not designed to accommodate and that competing accounts did not lead one to expect: the return of a comet on a schedule computed from the theory, the bending of light where the older mechanics required straightness, the discovery of a planet at the coordinates an anomaly demanded. Lakatos's student Elie Zahar sharpened the notion in a way later writers largely accepted: a fact counts as novel relative to a theory if it was not used in the theory's construction, even if it was known beforehand. A known fact pressed into service as the theory was being built is accommodation wearing prediction's clothes. The distinction is exacting, and deliberately so; it is the line between a programme that learns from the world and a programme that learns only how to describe the world it has already seen in its own vocabulary.

Lakatos applied his own criterion to Marxism, and his application belongs in this article in full view, because it deprives the defence of the suspicion that the criterion was chosen to convict. Writing as a methodologist grading programmes across the sciences, he asked what novel fact Marxism had successfully predicted, and he answered with a list of the programme's famous failures: that the first socialist revolution would occur in the most industrially developed society, that the working class would suffer absolute impoverishment, that socialist societies would know no revolutionary conflicts among themselves. Each failed, and each failure, he observed, was followed not by a revision that bought new content but by auxiliary explanations constructed after the event: the theory of imperialism to explain the delay of revolution in the advanced countries, the theory of the labour aristocracy, and others of the same pattern. His verdict was that the programme's problemshifts had been degenerating. The verdict is not sacred; Lakatos's grading of particular episodes can be contested, and parts of it are contested below. But its existence disciplines the debate. The criterion that convicts here was built by a philosopher trying to describe the best sciences accurately, applied by him to this programme among others, and its standards are the standards on which programmes he admired were acquitted. The defence must therefore engage the criterion itself, or produce the novel facts, and the sections that follow examine how much of each the record allows.

## Applying the criterion: the accommodations

The criterion now has to be worked, not merely stated, and the honest way to work it is to take the programme's major modifications one by one and ask of each the Lakatosian question: what novel fact did this revision predict, and was the prediction corroborated? The modifications that matter are the ones already encountered as responses to the three great disappointments. The reinterpretation of immiseration from an absolute to a relative claim. The transfer of polarisation from national societies to the world system. The recasting of revolution's geography through the weakest link and combined and uneven development. And, standing behind them, the theory of imperialism, the programme's largest single construction of the twentieth century, which underwrote the other three. Each will be graded by the same rule, and the grades will not be uniform, because the record is not uniform; but the pattern that emerges is the pattern the verdict will report.

### How was the immiseration claim rebuilt after the record turned against it?

It was rebuilt by migration from the absolute register to the relative one: from falling living standards to a falling share of the social product, a claim the rising record could not touch. The rebuilt claim retained content about distribution, but the migration predicted nothing that was not already visible when it was performed.

Begin with immiseration, the cleanest case. The modification's chronology is the first fact about it: the relative reading rose to dominance inside the tradition as the absolute record worsened, not before. Its content is the second fact: a claim about shares, unlike a claim about living standards, is compatible with every trajectory of working-class consumption, including indefinite improvement, so the modification purchased safety by surrendering exposure. Its yield is the third fact: the relative thesis generated no prediction of a novel fact that the tradition then staked and saw confirmed. It did not forecast a measurable movement of shares under specified conditions and stand by the forecast; it redescribed the distributional question in terms that the disappointing record could not embarrass. On Lakatos's grading this is a degenerating problemshift in near-perfect form, theoretically degenerating because the revision has no excess empirical content over its predecessor, and empirically degenerating because no novel fact was corroborated in its support. The point is not that relative deprivation is unreal or unimportant. It is that the programme, at this joint, behaved as the charge describes: it preserved the doctrine's sentence by changing the sentence's meaning, and recorded the change as fidelity.

The relocation of polarisation grades the same way, with one complication that fairness must register. The complication is that the world-systems reconstruction, in the hands of Immanuel Wallerstein and the dependency writers, was a substantial theoretical construction with its own architecture of core, semiperiphery, and periphery, and it organised a great deal of historical material that national frameworks handled badly. A construction can be illuminating and still be, in the programme's ledger, an accommodation: the question is not whether it organises the known but whether it risked the unknown. The relocation was undertaken because the national class structures of the rich countries refused to polarise; its central political implication, that the salaried strata of the core are bound to the system by their share of its global surplus, was available the moment the construction was finished, since it was the anomaly's own explanation restated as a theorem. What the construction did not do was stake a forecast its rivals denied and the future then ratified. Its defenders point to its anticipation of the core's deindustrialisation and the rise of manufacturing in parts of the periphery, and the anticipation is real as description of tendencies its authors noticed early. But noticing a tendency already under way and projecting it is extrapolation, and extrapolation of the visible is precisely what the novelty criterion excludes from the honours of prediction, since the construction of the theory used the very facts later cited as its confirmations.

The recasting of revolution's geography is the modification where the defence's case is strongest, and it is taken separately below, because the theory of imperialism and the doctrine of the weakest link contain elements that were staked in advance of the events they are credited with foreseeing, and an honest ledger must weigh them in their own section rather than dissolve them into the pattern. What can be said here is the general shape of the problem. A modification produced in 1916 to explain why revolution had not come where it was expected, and why war had come instead, begins under the heaviest suspicion of accommodation, because its construction materials are the anomalies themselves. To escape the grade of degenerating, it must show novel content beyond those materials, predictions its rivals would have denied, made in time and confirmed in fact. The next sections weigh the two best candidates the tradition possesses, this one and the credits that belong to the original programme's components, before the invitation is issued in its precise form.

## Applying the criterion: the credits

The ledger has another side, and the article's neutrality depends on entering it with the same care. Some components of the programme generated predictions that were risky when made and confirmed in the event, and the two the brief for this assessment directs attention to are the concentration analysis and the account of the wage and employment cycle. They share a feature that distinguishes them from the accommodations: they belong to the theory's original construction rather than to its later repairs, and they were stated as mechanisms with observable consequences before those consequences were the commonplaces they have since become. Their existence is why the honest verdict is mixed to poor rather than simply poor, and why the falsificationist picture of a theory empty from birth is as false to the record as the defence's picture of unbroken progress.

### Which parts of the programme produced predictions that were confirmed?

The analysis of concentration, which projected the growing scale and centralisation of capital when the economy of small producers was still extensive, and the analysis of the wage and employment cycle, which tied the expansion and contraction of the reserve of labour to the rhythm of accumulation itself.

The concentration analysis stands in the first volume of *Capital*, in the account of accumulation's historical tendency. Marx argued that accumulation carries within it the centralisation of capital: capitals grow by absorbing capitals, competition itself becomes a weapon by which the larger destroy or swallow the smaller, and the social form of production concentrates in ever fewer and larger units even as the labour it organises becomes ever more collective. When the argument was written, the economy of independent producers and small firms remained extensive across the industrialising world, and the joint-stock corporation was a young and contested form. The century and a half that followed ran in the prediction's direction on a scale its author could illustrate only in embryo: the rise of the great corporation, the merger movement, the trust and the multinational, the concentration of banking, and the recurring public anxiety, across otherwise hostile schools of economics, about the market power of the largest firms. Rival frameworks did not lead their holders to expect this trajectory with anything like the same confidence; the marginalist mainstream long treated the competitive firm of small scale as the economy's normal atom. Here, then, is a prediction with genuine novelty in Zahar's sense: owing nothing to the materials the theory was built from, denied or unseen by rivals, and corroborated by the subsequent record. It belongs to the credit side without qualification, and its existence refutes any version of the charge that denies the original theory empirical content.

The cycle analysis is the second credit. In the chapter expounding accumulation's general law, Marx bound the demand for labour to the pace and composition of accumulation, arguing that the system generates a relative surplus population, an industrial reserve army, whose expansion and contraction regulate wages and discipline the employed. The mechanism implied a recurring pattern: accumulation accelerates, the reserve drains, wages rise and profits come under pressure, accumulation falters or mechanises, the reserve refills. The pattern was stated before the systematic study of business cycles existed as a field, and the subsequent literature on cycles, including work far outside the tradition, repeatedly rediscovered the linkage between labour-market tightness, wage movement, and the cycle's turning points, in forms from the Phillips curve's original wage observations to later models of profit squeeze and of reserve-army dynamics built explicitly on Marx's mechanism by economists working inside the programme's descendant schools. The corroboration here is of a mechanism rather than of a dated forecast, and tendency claims, as an earlier section warned, must pay for their elasticity. But the mechanism was specified in advance, its observable signature was identified, and the signature has been found in the record by investigators with no loyalty to the programme. That is what a confirmed component looks like, and the ledger is dishonest without it.

Two disciplines govern the use of these credits in the verdict. The first is that they credit the founding theory's construction, not the programme's subsequent conduct. Lakatos's question is about problemshifts, about what the sequence of modifications yielded, and a programme does not become progressive because its founding document contained insights, any more than it becomes degenerating because its founding document contained errors. The credits show the programme began from a theory with real empirical content and real predictive successes to its name, which is exactly Popper's concession restated from the other side. The second discipline is that the credits are components, and components are not the whole. The tradition's centre of gravity, its claims about history's direction, the fate of the working class, and the destination of the system, lies in the propositions that failed and were accommodated. A verdict that averaged the components into a single mild grade would conceal the distribution that matters: strength in the analysis of capital's internal dynamics, weakness in the prophecies on which the doctrine's world-historical claim, and its demand for allegiance, were built. The faith framing, when it arrives, will be found to attach to precisely the weak components, which is not a coincidence.

## The defence's favourite exhibit: the world market passages

No assessment of the programme's predictive record is complete until it has faced the exhibit the defence produces first, the Manifesto's celebrated pages on the bourgeoisie's transformation of the world. Written in 1848, those pages describe capital driving across the whole surface of the globe, battering down national walls, drawing raw materials and markets into a single web, making production and consumption cosmopolitan, and dissolving local and national self-sufficiency into universal interdependence. Readers encountering them after a century and a half of accelerating integration routinely experience them as prophecy fulfilled, and the defence rests much of its popular case on that experience: here, it is said, is a novel fact predicted a hundred and fifty years in advance, the globalisation that rival doctrines never foresaw.

The exhibit deserves its fame and fails the criterion, and both halves of that sentence need their reasons. It fails, first, on the novelty condition in its sharpened form. The expansion of the world market was not an unknown fact pressed on a reluctant world in 1848; it was the most visible economic process of the preceding decades, the age of free trade agitation, of steam shipping and the first submarine cables, of an industrial exporter seeking markets on every continent. Marx and Engels were describing, with unmatched rhetorical power, a tendency fully under way and widely observed, and projecting it forward. Projection of a visible tendency is not nothing; most contemporaries projected it timidly, and the Manifesto's confidence about its destination, a genuinely universal interdependence, outran the evidence of its day and was vindicated. But the criterion's honour is reserved for facts the theory's construction did not use and rivals did not expect, and the world market's growth was used in the construction, as the very engine of the bourgeois epoch the document exists to announce. The exhibit is extrapolation of genius, and extrapolation is not the currency in which progressive problemshifts are paid.

It fails, second, on a condition specific to this trial. The verdict under examination concerns the programme's modifications, the sequence of revisions by which the tradition answered its anomalies. The world market passages belong to the founding stratum of 1848, decades before the anomalies accumulated. They therefore credit the founders' insight, which no party to this dispute denies, and they cannot acquit the successors' conduct, which is what the charge arraigns. A defence that answers an indictment of the repairs by exhibiting the foundations has changed the subject, and the change is the more tempting because the foundations here are genuinely magnificent. The magnificence is not in question. Its relevance is.

## Imperialism and the weakest link: the hardest case

The strongest candidate for a progressive problemshift in the programme's history is the construction built during the First World War: Lenin's theory of imperialism, with its companion doctrines of the weakest link and, in Trotsky's hands, of combined and uneven development. It was built to digest the programme's two most dangerous anomalies, the failure of revolution in the advanced countries and the collapse of working-class internationalism into national war in 1914. From the materials of those anomalies it constructed an account of capitalism's monopoly stage, in which the export of capital, the division of the world among the great powers, and the bribery of a labour aristocracy from imperial surplus together explained both the war and the delay, and implied that revolution would come not where capitalism was ripest but where the chain of the world system was weakest, in the backward country where foreign capital, a concentrated modern proletariat in a few great plants, and a peasant majority in ferment were combined by the unevenness of development itself.

Grade the construction by its chronology first, because the chronology is the defence's best evidence. The theory was substantially formed by 1916, and the event it is credited with foreseeing, revolution in Russia, arrived in 1917. Trotsky's version of the underlying idea was older still, reaching back to the aftermath of 1905, and it did stake, in advance, the claim that the Russian revolution would not halt at its bourgeois stage but would be driven by its own dynamics toward the expropriation of capital, a claim the orthodox stage theory of the period denied and events in their sequence bore out. Here, if anywhere in the programme's record, stands a modification that appears to satisfy the criterion's form: constructed partly in advance of its confirming event, denied by the rival orthodoxies of its day, and corroborated in the theatre it named. An honest ledger enters this, and the entry is why this article's verdict says mixed to poor rather than poor without qualification.

Then grade it by its construction, because the criterion asks a second question. The theory of imperialism was built from the anomalies it explained: the war it redescribed was already burning, the delay it explained was already decades old, and its central mechanism, the labour aristocracy sustained by imperial surplus, was the auxiliary Engels had already reached for when the English lever failed to move. Its novel content beyond the materials of its construction was thinner than its architecture suggests. The weakest-link expectation, moreover, was confirmed in the instance that inspired it and then generalised into a doctrine of revolution's geography that the subsequent record treated unkindly in both directions: revolutions arrived in several weak links and congealed into regimes the theory's own categories struggled to describe, while the advanced countries, whose eventual maturation the construction postponed rather than cancelled, never supplied the confirming instance the original theory had promised. And the construction's own risky implications fared badly where they were definite: the expectation that the imperialist stage was capitalism's final one, beyond which no further development of the system's productive or geographic scope was possible, sits awkwardly against the century of expansion, decolonisation without collapse, and integration that followed. The balanced grade is that the imperialism construction was the programme's most impressive accommodation, containing one genuinely anticipatory element in the Russian expectation, and that a single arguable element, contested in its novelty and inseparable from the anomaly that provoked it, does not reverse the direction of a ledger built from a century of shifts. It does, however, mark the precise ground on which a successful defence would have to fight, and the invitation section states the terms.

## The verdict defeater: what would reverse this assessment

An assessment that cannot state what would overturn it has committed the sin it arraigns, so this article states its defeater in the open, in the form the brief for this cluster requires and the method itself demands. The verdict that the programme has been largely degenerating would be defeated by the identification of a modification made in response to anomaly that subsequently predicted a novel fact later confirmed. Each clause of that sentence is a condition, and the defence's exhibit would have to satisfy them all. The exhibit must be a modification, a change made to the theory in its career, not a passage of the founding texts, since the founders' insight is not on trial. It must have been made in response to anomaly, since that is the circumstance whose handling is being graded. It must have predicted, in advance and in a form its rivals had reason to deny, a fact novel in the sharpened sense, not used in the modification's construction. And the fact must subsequently have been confirmed, by evidence independent of the tradition's own bookkeeping.

### What single exhibit would overturn the verdict delivered here?

A named modification, produced under the pressure of a failed prediction, that staked a novel fact in advance and saw it confirmed: one such exhibit, surviving scrutiny of its chronology and construction, would break the pattern on which the verdict rests and require the ledger to be reopened.

The invitation is not rhetorical, and its terms are not rigged. The Russian expectation examined in the previous section was admitted to the ledger and weighed, and it moved the verdict from poor toward mixed; a second exhibit of equal strength, or a first exhibit stronger, would move it further. Nor is the standard exotic. It is the standard by which the programme's admirers already grade the sciences they admire, and the standard the founders' own best work meets: the concentration analysis, had it been a modification rather than a foundation, would pass every clause. What the invitation exposes is the shape of the programme's actual history, in which the modifications cluster overwhelmingly at the accommodative pole. The tradition's scholars command archives and literatures no single article can exhaust, and it remains possible that the exhibit exists unrecognised in some subfield's working papers. Until it is produced and survives the scrutiny of its chronology, the verdict stands, and the burden's location is itself a finding: after a century and a half, the defence's difficulty is not that the court is hostile but that the exhibit is missing.

One clarification protects the invitation from a misunderstanding the tradition's rhetoric encourages. The defeater is not a demand for a dated prophecy of a singular event, the crude test the defence likes to attribute to its critics before declining it. Novel facts in the sciences are rarely singular events; they are patterns, signatures, and distributions, the perihelion's advance and the comet's return, the concentration curve and the cycle's rhythm. The programme deals in exactly such materials, and its credits in this ledger are of exactly that kind. The demand is only that a modification, somewhere in the long sequence of repairs, should have earned its keep the way the foundations earned theirs: by telling the world something it did not already know, in time for the world to agree. A tradition confident of its record should welcome the invitation's precision, since precision about what would count as success is the first casualty in every degenerating programme's internal discourse, replaced by celebrations of survival, of relevance, of the founder's inexhaustible richness, all of which may be true and none of which is the question.

## The defence at full strength

The defence must now be stated as its ablest respondents state it, at full strength and without caricature, because the verdict that follows is worth only what the objection it survives is worth. The defence, attributed to named respondents across the philosophy of science and the methodology of economics, runs as follows. Every research programme in the social sciences uses auxiliary assumptions, and none makes precise predictions that survive testing in the manner of the physical sciences. Thomas Kuhn's account of normal science showed that the protection of a paradigm's core against anomaly is not a deviation from scientific practice but its ordinary constitution: scientists do not abandon a paradigm at the first counterinstance, or the tenth; they work within it, articulating it against the recalcitrant cases, and they are right to, since a paradigm abandoned at every anomaly would never develop the precision that makes deep testing possible. Paul Feyerabend pressed the point into a general scepticism about method: the episodes the falsificationists celebrate, examined closely, show scientists retaining theories in the teeth of refuting evidence and prospering by the retention, so that the rules invoked against Marxism describe no science that ever existed. And the methodologists of economics, grading their own field, reached conclusions that cut the ground from any selective severity: Mark Blaug, applying Popperian and Lakatosian standards to mainstream economics with more sympathy for those standards than any Marxist needed to show, concluded that economists preach falsification and practise immunisation, defending their core with the same auxiliary dexterity, and that whole research programmes within the prestige core of the discipline could be graded degenerating by the same ledger used here.

From these premises the defence draws its conclusion. Applying to Marxism a standard that would eliminate most of economics and sociology is not an assessment but a disqualification, a verdict reached by choosing the court. The social world differs from the physical in ways that bear directly on prediction: its systems are open, its agents read the theories made about them and alter their conduct, its experiments cannot be rerun under controlled conditions, and its mechanisms operate through meanings that shift under the investigator's hands. A criterion formed on the history of physics, however refined by Lakatos, imports expectations the subject matter cannot meet, and the demand for novel facts, in a domain where the facts are singular configurations of institutions and events, asks for a kind of repetition the world does not supply. On this view, Marxism's record of accommodation is the record of every serious attempt to theorise society at scale, and its singling out says more about the politics of its judges than about its method. The defence adds, finally, that the programme's longevity and fertility are themselves evidence no merely degenerating enterprise could produce: a hundred and fifty years of research across history, sociology, economics, and geography has drawn problems, concepts, and findings from the framework, and dead programmes do not colonise living disciplines.

This is a strong defence, and parts of it are true. Kuhn's description of normal science is accurate as sociology of knowledge, and it does follow that protective conduct, as such, cannot distinguish Marxism from science in general. Blaug's findings about economics are a permanent embarrassment to any grader who would apply the criterion selectively, and this article has no intention of applying it selectively: the next section applies it across the field and reports what it yields there, including the fields where the yield is unflattering to Marxism's rivals. The openness of social systems and the reflexivity of their agents are real features of the domain, and they do lower the ceiling on what prediction can mean there. What the defence does not establish is its conclusion. For the conclusion to follow, the criterion would have to acquit every programme or convict every programme, grading nothing; and the criterion does neither, because programmes within the same difficult domain, facing the same openness and reflexivity, differ measurably in their conduct and their yield. Some social-scientific programmes have produced modifications that predicted novel facts and were confirmed; others have produced only the protective arts. The defence's premises show that the bar must be set where the domain allows it to be cleared. They do not show that this programme cleared it, and the programme's own apologists, when they celebrate its confirmed anticipations, concede the bar's legitimacy by claiming to have cleared it. A defence cannot both dismiss the test as foreign to social science and boast of the occasions the tradition passed it. The next section therefore takes the defence's strongest premise as the article's own rule: one criterion, applied symmetrically, to Marxism and to the comparable frameworks alike, with the grades published for all.

## The criterion applied symmetrically: the field graded whole

Apply the programme criterion across the social sciences, then, with the severity constant. The exercise concedes at the outset what the defence insists on: the criterion is demanding for the whole field, and no major social-scientific programme emerges from the grading with the lustre of a mature physical science. What emerges instead is a distribution, and distributions are what assessments are made of. At one end stand programmes whose modifications have repeatedly bought confirmed novel content. At the other stand programmes that survive by the protective arts alone. Marxism's place in that distribution, neither at its foot nor near its head, is the honest comparative finding, and it is less comfortable for the defence than the tu quoque it replaces, because the tu quoque promised exoneration by company and the distribution delivers grading by company instead.

### How do comparable programmes fare under the same criterion?

Unevenly, which is the point. Some macroeconomic and psychological programmes contain genuinely progressive episodes in which modifications predicted facts their rivals had not expected; others, including prestigious ones, grade as degenerating across long stretches. The criterion discriminates within the field, and Marxism's record sits below the field's stronger performers.

Consider economics, the defence's chosen comparison, in the spirit of Blaug's own audit. The neoclassical programme's hard core, maximising agents and equilibrium, has been protected by auxiliaries as dexterously as any doctrine in this article, and long stretches of its refinement, the successive repairs of general equilibrium theory against its own impossibility results, grade as degenerating by strict Lakatosian lights: content-preserving mathematics elaborated in response to internal anomalies, yielding elegance rather than novel facts. Yet the same broad field contains episodes of unmistakable progressiveness. The rational expectations modification of macroeconomics, whatever its ultimate fate, was a problemshift that predicted a novel fact its Keynesian rivals denied: that systematic monetary policy would lose its purchase on real output as agents incorporated the policy rule into their expectations, an implication staked in advance and borne out closely enough in the stagflation era to transform the discipline's practice. Behavioural economics, in its founding decades, repeatedly predicted anomalies of choice, in framing, in endowment, in time preference, that the standard model excluded and experiments then produced on demand. A field that can generate such episodes cannot plead the domain's impossibility when its other programmes fail to. The bar is clearable in social science. It has been cleared, in public, by programmes working on the same recalcitrant human material.

Psychoanalysis, Popper's original companion exhibit, grades at the distribution's lower end by near-universal consent of methodologists, its modifications a museum of accommodation, and its survival a phenomenon for the sociology of belief rather than the history of discovery. Modernisation theory, the mid-century programme that projected a single path of development for the decolonising world, degenerated through the same sequence this article has tracked in Marxism: failed expectations, auxiliary distinctions between traditional and transitional cases multiplied after each failure, and a final dissolution into vocabularies that describe everything and stake nothing. Demography's transition theory shows the mixed middle: a framework that organised its domain powerfully, accommodated its anomalies with auxiliary stages, and yet generated enough disciplined expectation about mortality and fertility sequences to earn partial credit. Sociology's grand programmes divide similarly, some research traditions within it, in network analysis and in the study of collective action, having produced modifications with confirmed novel content, while its more prophetic schools replicate the pattern of protection.

Against this field, Marxism's comparative position can be stated without either of the debate's customary distortions. It is not uniquely exposed to the criterion; the criterion wounds programmes across the field, including the discipline that most loudly claims scientific status. It is not at the field's floor; its founding credits and its fertility exceed psychoanalysis and modernisation theory. And it is not among the field's stronger performers on the dimension the criterion measures. Its modifications, across the longest run any social programme has had to compile a record, are predominantly accommodations, and its confirmed novel predictions belong overwhelmingly to its founding stratum rather than to its repairs. The concession the defence demanded has been made in full: the standard is severe for every programme in the field, and this article has published the field's failures beside the defendant's. The finding that survives the concession is the narrower and harder one. Demandingness is not disqualification where others have met the demand, and on the evidence of its own sequence, this programme's record on the better criterion is not among the stronger ones. The defence asked for symmetry. Symmetry is what it has received, and symmetry does not acquit.

## The standard the tradition set itself

One question shadows the whole proceeding and should be answered before the faith framing is reached: by what right is this doctrine graded as a science at all, rather than excused as a philosophy, a politics, or a vision of the good society, none of which owe the criterion anything? The answer is that the tradition claimed the title itself, at its foundation, in terms that leave no room for the excuse. Engels, presenting the doctrine to the public in the pamphlet drawn from his polemic against Eugen Dühring and published as *Socialism: Utopian and Scientific*, defined the tradition's difference from its socialist predecessors precisely by its scientific character: where the utopians had designed societies from ideals, Marx had discovered the laws of the existing society's motion and of its necessary transformation, converting socialism from an aspiration into a finding. The pamphlet was the movement's most widely read introduction for generations, and its title became the tradition's self-description. Marx's own self-understanding ran the same way. He presented the analysis of capital as the exposure of an economic law of motion, accepted with pride the comparison of his work's significance to Darwin's, and treated the prediction-bearing parts of the theory, the tendencies of accumulation and their political consequences, as among its principal claims on belief.

The point is not biographical. It fixes the jurisdiction. A doctrine that asks to be believed as ethics may decline the laboratory's standards with honour; a doctrine that presents itself as the discovered science of society's laws, and that organised parties and states on the authority of that claim, has submitted itself to grading by the standards of the sciences it invoked. When the grading turns unfavourable and the defence replies that the standards are foreign to social inquiry, the reply must reckon with the founders' own insistence that the standards were met. Nor can the tradition take refuge in the youth of the social sciences at its founding, since the claim was renewed in every generation, in the textbooks of parties and the constitutions of states that proclaimed their rule scientific in character. The criterion applied in this article is therefore not an external imposition. It is the tradition's own promissory note, presented for payment after a hundred and fifty years, and the interest of the case lies in the ledger the payment discloses.

There is a corollary that defenders and opponents both miss. Because the tradition claimed scientific status for the whole doctrine, its adherents experienced every empirical disappointment as a crisis of the whole, and defended every joint as though the entire structure depended on it. A research programme with a more modest self-understanding can shed a failed prediction the way a ship sheds a boat, recording the loss and sailing on; programmes in economics do this routinely, and their cores survive because their claims were never total. The Marxist programme's totalising claim, one science embracing history, economics, politics, and philosophy, made each anomaly a threat to the whole and therefore made accommodation, rather than amputation, the institutional reflex. The protective pattern this article has documented is thus not an accident of personnel or of Russian conditions. It follows from the doctrine's architecture of belief, from the fusion of its empirical claims with its title to rule the future. That fusion is also the bridge to the faith framing, for a body of propositions that must not be allowed to fail has crossed the line that separates a programme's hard core, which scientists also protect, from a creed's articles, which are protected by sanctions. The difference lies in what happens to the member who records the failure aloud, and the tradition's treatment of its Bernsteins has already supplied one answer.

## A fourth stake: the state that was to wither

The three predictions graded so far concern capitalism's development. A fourth stake, less often entered in this ledger because it concerns the successor order, completes the record and connects the methodological case to the political one examined elsewhere in this cluster. The theory held that the state, as an apparatus of class rule standing above society, would lose its function once classes were abolished, and would wither away in Engels's phrase, replaced by the administration of things. The expectation was not decorative. It distinguished the tradition's promised order from every previous despotism in principle: the coercive apparatus was to be the first casualty of its own victory, and the dictatorship of the proletariat was theorised as a transitional form whose defining property was that it abolished itself.

The record of the states built under the doctrine's banner ran in the opposite direction with a uniformity that no other item in this ledger matches. In each case the apparatus grew: in scope, in personnel, in the reach of its supervision over economic and cultural life, and in the permanence of its emergency provisions, which renewed themselves across generations. The tradition's explanations for the growth form a complete catalogue of the protective arts in a single case. Encirclement explained the first decade and was extended, as a standing condition, across seven. Backwardness explained the need for a tutelary apparatus and survived the backwardness it was meant to remedy. The survival of hostile classes explained the apparatus's targets until the classes were declared abolished, after which their survival was rediscovered in new forms, wreckers, speculators, cosmopolitans, each generation's category fitted to the apparatus's requirements. And the withering itself was postponed into a future whose distance from the present increased as the present accumulated. Whatever grade the other predictions receive, this one exhibits the pattern in its purest available form, because the stake was definite, the contrary was total, and the protective sequence can be read, dated stage by dated stage, in the tradition's own official literature. The cluster's Soviet articles carry the narrative; its place here is as the ledger's most legible page.

## The internal reckoning: analytical Marxism

Late in the programme's history there occurred an episode that complicates the pattern in an honourable direction, and the ledger must record it, because it shows what the tradition's conduct looked like when its ablest members applied the standards this article applies. From the late nineteen seventies, a group of philosophers and social scientists working inside the tradition, associated with G. A. Cohen's reconstruction of historical materialism and gathering in what became known as the September Group, set out to restate Marxist claims with analytic precision and to surrender whatever could not survive restatement. Cohen defended the theory of history as a functional explanation, in which the relations of production are explained by their tendency to develop the productive forces. Jon Elster, from within the same circle, subjected that defence and much else to a methodological audit of exceptional severity, demanding microfoundations, rejecting functional explanation without mechanisms, and concluding that large parts of the inherited doctrine, its dialectics, its holistic economics, its confident laws, failed the standards of ordinary social science and should be abandoned as false or empty.

The episode grades mixed on the criterion, and its mixedness is instructive. As a problemshift, the analytical reconstruction predicted few novel facts; its yield was clarification, not forecast. But its conduct broke the pattern this article has documented in the most important respect: losses were recorded as losses. Elster's audit did not redescribe failure as fidelity; it amputated, in print, under the tradition's own imprint, and its author accepted the consequence that what remained was a smaller doctrine than the one he had inherited. Other members of the circle rebuilt empirical programmes, on class, exploitation, and the state, with formal models whose implications could be and were tested, and some of those subprogrammes earned credits of the ordinary scientific kind. The episode therefore stands in the ledger as evidence that the programme's degenerating pattern was a property of its dominant institutions rather than of its propositions as such: where the sanctions of orthodoxy were absent and the standards of the surrounding disciplines governed, Marxist researchers behaved as researchers behave, grading their inheritance and discarding what failed. The defence is entitled to cite the episode as the programme's capacity for self-correction. The prosecution is entitled to note where the episode occurred, at the tradition's academic margin, in its declining decades, against the resistance of its official carriers, and that its most rigorous product was a verdict on the doctrine not far from the one this article reaches by the programme's longer road.

## The paradigm defence and its limit

The Kuhnian strand of the defence deserves a separate weighing, because it is the strand most often treated as decisive and it contains a limit its users rarely mention. Kuhn's *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* described how a paradigm, a governing framework of concepts and exemplary solutions, organises normal science: anomalies are treated as puzzles for the paradigm's articulation, failures are charged to the practitioner rather than the framework, and the framework itself is not in jeopardy in the daily work of its community. All of that is true of Marxist scholarship at its best, and it licenses a great deal of the protective conduct the charge arraigns. A Marxist historian refining the account of a transition, a Marxist economist articulating the theory of crisis against an awkward decade, are doing what normal scientists do, and the falsificationist who would convict them of bad faith for refusing to abandon their framework at each anomaly has understood neither Kuhn nor science.

But Kuhn's account has a second half. Paradigms accumulate anomalies until the articulation fails faster than it succeeds, confidence drains, and the field enters crisis, from which it exits, if it exits, by a revolution that installs a successor. The protection Kuhn licenses is therefore temporary and conditional: it is the protection a framework enjoys while its articulation is fertile, and it expires when the puzzles multiply unsolved and the community's ablest members begin to defect. Measured against that fuller standard, the defence's citation of Kuhn proves less than its users hope. The Marxist programme's anomaly load, across the predictions graded here, is not the ordinary friction of normal science; it is the accumulation, over more than a century, of failures at the framework's prophetic centre, met by a community whose institutional carriers, the parties and states, forbade the crisis to be declared. Where no state enforced them, the defections Kuhn's model predicts duly occurred: the ablest members of successive generations exited the framework or rebuilt it past recognition, and the analytical episode described above is one such exit conducted from inside. Kuhn, read whole, describes Marxism's trajectory with uncomfortable fidelity: a paradigm of immense fertility, protected past the term of its fertility by institutions that could not afford its crisis. The defence that cites him for the protection must also accept him for the expiry.

## The long run: postponement as the last auxiliary

A final defensive argument remains, the most venerable in the tradition's repertoire, and it must be faced because it underlies all the others. The programme, it is said, is young by the standards of history; capitalism itself is a few centuries old; the tendencies the theory names operate across epochs, and a grading conducted after a mere century and a half mistakes the timescale of the claims. Newton's mechanics reigned for two centuries before its anomalies ripened into a successor. The ledger, on this view, is not unfavourable but merely open, and the verdict of degeneration is a judgement pronounced at half time.

The argument fails on the criterion's own terms, and its failure is diagnostic. Lakatos's grading does not require a programme to have completed its claims; it grades the direction of the sequence to date, the ratio of content bought to content protected, and it expressly permits a degenerating programme to revive, since the ledger is always open to the exhibit the invitation section requests. What the long-run argument asks for is different: not more time in which to produce novel facts, but exemption from grading until the facts arrive, which converts the programme's every present failure into a promissory note on an unspecified future. That conversion is postponement, the third of the protective forms catalogued earlier, promoted from a tactic to a philosophy of history. Its tell is that it specifies no observation, at any date, that would count against it: a century of contrary record is youth, two centuries would be adolescence, and the theory's timetable, once the source of its scientific pride, has become the one quantity about which nothing definite may be said. Programmes in the genuine long sciences do not argue this way. Geology's timescales are vast, and its claims were graded, fiercely and continuously, on the evidence of each decade, because its mechanisms generated expectations about the present's rocks as well as the past's. A doctrine confident that time is on its side should be the readiest to be graded by the time elapsed. The tradition's reluctance, at this last redoubt, to accept any grading at all is the point at which the methodological case shades into the territory of the next sections, where the question is no longer how the doctrine handles evidence but what kind of allegiance it commands and why that allegiance survives the handling.

## The faith framing: the secular eschatology argument

A separate line of criticism now enters, and it must be kept separate, because it argues a different thesis by different means and its confusion with the methodological case has damaged both. The eschatology argument does not claim that Marxism's propositions failed their tests. It claims that the doctrine's deep structure reproduces the structure of a salvation religion, and that this structural kinship, not its evidence, explains the allegiance it commands. Karl Löwith gave the argument its scholarly form in *Meaning in History*, contending that the modern philosophy of history, Marx's included, is eschatology secularised: the biblical pattern of a creation, a fall, a chosen bearer of redemption, a final crisis, and a kingdom beyond history, translated into the vocabulary of economics, with the direction of the whole guaranteed by the pattern rather than established by the evidence. The pattern, once seen, is difficult to unsee, and the argument's influence on how the tradition is read has been out of all proportion to its length.

### What pattern does the eschatology argument claim to find?

A directed history moving through suffering toward a redeemed final condition: an original unity lost, a chosen collective agent whose tribulations carry the meaning of the whole, a climactic rupture in which the old order is judged, and a reconciled world beyond conflict, guaranteed by the story's shape.

Map the correspondences as the argument draws them. History has a direction and a destination, as sacred history does: the succession of modes of production moves, through contradiction and crisis, toward a terminus that is not merely later but better in a sense that redeems the journey. Humanity's story has a fall: the dissolution of primitive community into property, class, and alienation, from which the whole subsequent drama is the recovery. The drama has a chosen agent, a collective whose suffering is universal in significance and whose victory is everyone's: the proletariat, the class with radical chains, whose emancipation contains the emancipation of all, a formulation Marx himself cast in terms that invite the comparison. The story has its climactic rupture, the revolution, in which the old world is judged and the accounts of history are settled. And it has its final condition, the classless society, in which the conflicts that drove history cease, administration replaces rule, and humanity's prehistory ends, a condition whose features are specified almost entirely by negation of the present's evils, as the kingdom's traditionally are. Around the structure gather the supporting furniture: a canon of texts, prophets and interpreters, an account of evil that locates it in a system rather than in souls, and a promise that the meaning of present suffering is stored and will be honoured at the end.

The argument's force is real, and the sections after next will weigh what it establishes. Its force lies first in explanation: it accounts for features of the tradition that the methodological case leaves mysterious. It explains the doctrine's emotional architecture, its capacity to convert political defeat into a chapter of a guaranteed story, its production of consolation under persecution and of certainty under refutation. It explains why the protective conduct documented above feels, from inside, not like evasion but like faithfulness: a participant in a salvation story does not experience an anomaly as evidence against the story, because the story has a place prepared for suffering and delay, and the delay of the redemption is among the oldest of its themes. And it explains the tradition's characteristic literary forms, the sacred history retold in each generation's textbooks, the founders' lives arranged as gospel and acts, the heresies named and anathematised in a vocabulary the tradition borrowed without embarrassment. What the argument does not do, and what its careless users claim for it, is refute a single proposition. A doctrine may wear the structure of a religion and still contain truths; the germ theory of disease has no eschatology and astrology has a rich one, but the grading of either is conducted on its claims and their evidence, not on its narrative furniture. The eschatology argument is an account of the tradition's form and appeal. The trial of its propositions belongs to the criteria already applied, and conflating the two proceedings is the error this article's remaining faith sections are written to prevent.

## Aron and the inverted phrase

The eschatology argument's most famous single stroke was delivered by the French sociologist Raymond Aron in his study of 1955, *The Opium of the Intellectuals*. Marx, in a celebrated sentence of his early work, had called religion the opium of the people, the consolation that reconciles the suffering to their condition and dulls the impulse to change it. Aron inverted the phrase against the tradition that wielded it. Marxism itself, he argued, had become the opium of the intellectuals of his country and his generation: the doctrine that reconciled them to the realities of the regimes built in its name, dulled their scrutiny of those regimes' crimes, and supplied, in the guaranteed future, the consolation for every present horror that the facts could not supply. The inversion was polemical and was meant to be. But its target was precise, and it was not the doctrine's propositions. It was the function the doctrine performed for a specific class of believers, the intellectuals who administered its meaning, and the observable deformation of their judgement wherever the doctrine's prestige and the record collided.

### What phrase did the French study invert, and against whom?

It took Marx's description of religion as the opium of the people and returned it upon his own doctrine, as the opium of the intellectuals: the consolation that reconciled educated believers to the conduct of regimes ruling in the doctrine's name, and dulled their scrutiny of that conduct.

Aron's study repays attention beyond its title because its method anticipates the discipline this article has tried to practise. He did not argue that the doctrine's adherents were stupid or insincere; he argued that a structure of belief had captured their critical faculties at exactly the points where the belief was engaged, leaving those faculties intact, even brilliant, elsewhere. The intellectual who could dissect the ideology of any other power accepted, for the one power that claimed his doctrine, standards of evidence he would have mocked in a student. That asymmetry, documented in the Parisian debates of the period, is the faith framing's best empirical exhibit, because it is a phenomenon of conduct rather than of structure: believers grading evidence by its friendliness to the belief. It is also, notice, the same asymmetry the methodological sections documented at the level of the programme, the criterion applied leniently to one's own and stringently to rivals, now observed at the level of persons. The two cases, the programme's ledger and the believer's judgement, are one phenomenon at two scales, and the faith framing earns its place in this article by naming the mechanism that connects them: allegiance that precedes evidence and survives it, organised by a story in which the allegiance itself is a virtue and doubt a form of betrayal.

## The critical history: the pattern traced at length

Where Aron wrote a polemic of a season, the former Marxist philosopher Leszek Kołakowski built the framing's monument. His *Main Currents of Marxism*, published in three volumes in the later nineteen seventies, traced the doctrine from its founders through its golden age to its breakdown, and its author brought to the task a qualification no external critic could match: he had been a Marxist philosopher of standing inside a Marxist state, had revised his allegiance under the pressure of the record, and had been expelled from his party and driven from his country for the revision. The history's governing perception is the one the eschatology argument predicts. Again and again, across a century of schools and schisms, Kołakowski finds the doctrine's adherents preserving the eschatological core, the guaranteed destination and the chosen agent, while surrendering, qualifying, or reinterpreting every definite claim the core had generated, until in the ruling parties of his century the doctrine survived as a liturgy of legitimation whose content had been entirely replaced by its function. His conclusion about the founders themselves is more sorrowful than Aron's polemic: that the fantasy of a society beyond conflict and alienation, installed at the doctrine's heart as its scientific destination, carried within it the warrant for the regimes that claimed to be building it, since a destination that redeems all suffering licenses any road.

The history's authority in this debate is double-edged, and both edges should be acknowledged. Its author knew the tradition from inside, in its texts and its institutions, and his account of the degeneration of a science into a creed is the record of a witness. But a witness who has lost a faith writes with the convert's zeal in reverse, and Kołakowski's reading of the founders, in which the catastrophe is present from the first page, has been contested by scholars who find in the same texts the resources for the self-criticism the regimes suppressed. For this article's purposes the contest is secondary. What the critical history establishes beyond serious dispute is the pattern's scale: the protective sequence documented in this article's ledger is not an artefact of a few famous predictions but the tradition's characteristic motion across its whole career, visible in its philosophy, its economics, and its politics alike, and visible most clearly to the historian who had once performed the motion himself. When such a history is written by a tradition's own philosopher, the faith framing ceases to be an opponent's metaphor and becomes part of the tradition's self-knowledge, which is perhaps why it is the framing's version that believers find hardest to answer and most necessary to ignore.

## Canon, commentary, and heresy: the institutional resemblances

Between the eschatology argument's structure and Aron's believers stands an institutional layer that the faith framing also illuminates, the tradition's organisation of its own texts and loyalties. The resemblances to a church are not superficial decorations; they are functional solutions to the problem every doctrine faces when its founder dies and its predictions begin to fail, and cataloguing them explains how the protective conduct of the earlier sections was organised and enforced. There is a canon: a bounded set of founding texts whose authority settles disputes, surrounded by a penumbra of commentary whose industry is exegesis, the demonstration that the canon, rightly read, anticipated the present difficulty. There is an orthodoxy: a custodial institution, the party and its theoretical journals, licensed to declare the canon's meaning and to police departures. There is a vocabulary of heresy with sanctions attached, of which revisionist, deviationist, and renegade are the working terms, each marking not an error to be corrected but a loyalty to be expelled. And there is a practice of retrospective prophecy, in which the canon's authority is renewed by discovering in it the foreknowledge of whatever has occurred, so that the founder is never found unprepared by history, only unread by his followers.

Each resemblance has its methodological price, and the prices sum to the ledger already presented. Exegesis directed at showing the canon anticipated the anomaly is accommodation with a literary method: the text is ransacked for the sentence that can bear the required meaning, and the founder's least defensible operative claims are retired in favour of his most elastic ones. The policing of heresy raises the cost of recording a loss above the cost of protecting it, reversing the incentive structure of inquiry, in which the member who finds the error gains standing. Retrospective prophecy destroys the ledger itself, since a tradition that rewrites its past expectations to match outcomes can no longer know what it predicted, and a programme that cannot know what it predicted cannot be graded even by its friends. These are not metaphors imposed from outside. The tradition's own internal critics, from Bernstein's expulsion from orthodoxy to the analytical school's marginality, encountered them as working conditions, and the sociology of the doctrine's institutions belongs in any adequate answer to the question of the title, because it explains the supply of the conduct the criteria grade.

## What the faith framing does and does not establish

The faith framing can now be given its exact weight, which is considerable in one direction and nil in another, and this article's fairness depends on stating both without blur. What it establishes is an explanation of adherence. It accounts for the doctrine's survival through a century of disappointed prediction with its allegiance not merely intact but periodically intensified; for the asymmetry of scrutiny its believers apply; for the institutional forms its custodians built; for the emotional register of its literature, in which doubt is treason and endurance is sanctity; and for the otherwise puzzling fact that the collapse of the regimes ruling in its name diminished the doctrine's prestige without dissolving the allegiance of many who had no regime left to defend. A theory of why people hold a belief is not a small thing, and the eschatology framing, with Aron's study and the critical history as its monuments, is the most powerful such theory this tradition has attracted. Where it is used for that purpose, it is a contribution to knowledge.

### Does a resemblance to religion settle whether propositions are true?

It settles nothing about them. A belief's psychological and institutional resemblance to faith explains why it is held and how it survives disappointment; the truth of its claims is a separate question, graded on evidence. Doctrines with religious structures have contained truths, and doctrines without them have been false.

What the framing does not establish is the falsity of anything. To infer from the structure of a belief to the falsehood of its content is the genetic fallacy in ceremonial dress, and the inference is as invalid against Marxism as it would be against the doctrines of its critics, several of which, market providentialism among them, exhibit their own eschatologies of a final harmonious order and their own protected cores. Nor does the framing grade the components separately, as the criterion does: the analysis of concentration does not become less confirmed because the doctrine that houses it also houses a myth of redemption, and the prophecy of polarisation does not become more false because it was believed with religious intensity. The framing's proper office in this article is explanatory and diagnostic. It explains why the programme's degenerating conduct met so little internal resistance, and it diagnoses what the methodological verdict cannot: that the stakes of the grading were never merely intellectual for the graded, since what stood under examination was not a hypothesis but a home. Readers who use the faith framing as a refutation have not understood it; readers who dismiss it on the ground that it refutes nothing have missed its office just as completely. It is the answer to the question the criteria leave behind, why a programme graded as this one has been graded continued to command the allegiance of intelligent and honourable people, and that question is real, and its answer is the framing's achievement.

## The founder's own practice: revisions Marx actually made

A distinction the whole case turns on, and which the tradition's custodians had institutional reasons to blur, is between the founder's conduct as an investigator and the tradition's conduct as his church. The founder's conduct was frequently better than the church's. Marx revised positions under the pressure of evidence in ways his followers later found embarrassing enough to soften or suppress, and the episodes belong in the ledger because they show the programme's degenerating pattern to be a property of its institutions rather than of its originating mind. Late in his life, questioned by Russian revolutionaries on whether their country was condemned to pass through capitalism before socialism, Marx refused the universal timetable his own earlier rhetoric had encouraged. In the drafts of his reply to Vera Zasulich, and in the preface he and Engels gave the Russian edition of the Manifesto, he allowed that the Russian rural commune, if it survived and if revolution in the West complemented it, might serve as the starting point of a different path. The concession was conditional, hedged, and unpublished in his lifetime in its fullest form, but it was a genuine revision: the sequence his system had seemed to mandate was declared, by its author, to be a sketch drawn from Western European experience rather than a fate imposed on every nation.

His Irish revision cuts the same way and was made in public. Marx had long counted on England's workers as the lever of transformation, with Ireland's fate to follow England's emancipation. By the end of the eighteen sixties he had reversed the order in his correspondence and his political work: England's working class would accomplish nothing decisive until Ireland was separated, because the antagonism between English and Irish workers, artificially sustained, was the secret of the English class's impotence despite its organisation. The reversal matters methodologically. It was driven by observation of a mechanism his earlier expectation had missed, it was stated as a correction of his own previous view, and it reorganised his practical priorities. Nothing in the episode resembles the protective pattern: a prediction embedded in his strategy failed, and he changed the strategy's premise and said so. The tradition that canonised him preserved the texts of these revisions and declined to imitate the conduct they displayed, preferring the founder's certainties to the founder's example. When the charge speaks of the response to refutation, its true target stands revealed by the contrast: not the man who wrote the theory, who could write "I have changed my opinion" in substance and act on it, but the institution that could not, because its title to obedience was the founder's infallibility rather than his method.

## The charge translated: Popper in Lakatosian terms

The two critics whose frameworks organise this article are usually presented as rivals, Popper the falsificationist against Lakatos the historian of programmes, and the defence exploits the rivalry by defeating each with the other. It is worth showing that on this case they are the same critic at two resolutions, because the demonstration closes the defence's favourite escape route. Popper's complaint was that the theory's followers re-interpreted theory and evidence to make them agree, rescuing the theory from refutation. Translate the complaint into Lakatos's vocabulary and it reads: the programme's problemshifts were degenerating, its modifications accommodating anomalies without excess content, its protective belt absorbing every impact while its hard core issued no new instructions to the world. Lakatos's own application of his criterion to Marxism, with its list of failed forecasts and post hoc auxiliaries, is Popper's narrative with the bookkeeping made explicit. The continuity is not accidental. Lakatos built his framework to explain how the conduct Popper condemned could be distinguished, inside real sciences, from the conduct Popper admired, and his verdict on this programme is the destination toward which Popper's anecdotes were already pointing.

The translation also disciplines Popper's side of the case. In the falsificationist telling, the sin is reinterpretation, and the telling tempts its users into the flattened charge this article began by correcting, as though any reinterpretation were the fall. In the Lakatosian telling, reinterpretation is the universal solvent of inquiry, innocent in itself, and the sin is specified with a precision that protects the innocent: reinterpretation that buys nothing. A defence that has learned to shrug at Popper, on the ground that all theories reinterpret, has not thereby answered Lakatos, for whom the shrug is the starting point of the inquiry rather than its end. Conversely, a prosecution that rests on Popper's rhetoric of rescue has not earned its verdict until it has done Lakatos's work, exhibit by exhibit, which is the work the middle sections of this article attempted. The two critics converge, then, on a single demand, stated once in the language of logic and once in the language of history: show us what the revisions bought. The tradition's difficulty is that the demand, in either language, is one it has spent a century and a half not quite meeting, and the convergence of its two ablest critics on the same unanswered question is itself evidence about where the case's weight lies.

## The company of the charge: what Marxism was not accused of being

Popper's demarcation problem began, by his own account, with the contrast between Einstein's theory and the doctrines of Freud, Adler, and the Marxism of his Viennese youth, and the contrast illuminates the charge by showing its boundaries. What struck the young Popper about the psychological theories was their explanatory omnipotence: every conceivable human action, and its opposite, could be derived from them with equal facility, so that their adherents experienced the world as an endless stream of confirmations. A theory that is confirmed by everything is confirmed by nothing, and the feeling of explanatory power it generates is the signature of its emptiness. Einstein's general relativity, by contrast, staked itself on a measurement, the bending of light at the sun's edge, that could have destroyed it, and its author's willingness to abandon the theory if the measurement failed was, for Popper, the scientific attitude incarnate.

Marxism entered Popper's list from the other side of that contrast, and the entry is the biographical root of the charge's precision. The Marxism of his youth had impressed him precisely by its difference from the psychological doctrines: it made claims about the world, about crises and revolutions, that events could and did disappoint, and his break with it came not from discovering that it risked nothing but from watching its adherents, after the failures of the postwar revolutions in central Europe, explain the failures in ways that converted them into evidence of the theory's depth. The charge of this article's concern is thus the report of a witness to a transition: a doctrine crossing, within one observer's lifetime, from the Einstein side of the line toward the Adler side. The crossing is the whole indictment, and it is why the flattened version of the charge, which places Marxism on the Adler side from birth, is not a harsher version of Popper's view but a different and false one. It is also why the defence's textual labours, demonstrating the theory's original testability, answer a question the prosecution never asked. The prosecution's question is about the crossing, and the crossing is a historical event in the tradition's conduct, datable to the decades in which its predictions first failed at scale and its institutions first chose protection over revision.

## The textual defence: were the predictions conditional all along?

The most scholarly form of the defence does not deny the record but re-reads the stakes. Marx's claims, it argues, were never the unconditional forecasts the charge arraigns. They were tendency statements embedded in a method that stressed contingency, the openness of historical outcomes to political action, and the dependence of every trajectory on conditions the theory itself enumerated. The letter to Zasulich and the Irish revision, exhibited above, are produced as the founder's own demonstration that his sequences were sketches of a logic, not timetables of a fate. On this reading, the charge convicts the vulgar Marxism of the parties, a doctrine of iron necessity manufactured by popularisers and regimes, while the theory in its considered form staked only what a science of tendencies may honourably stake, and the failures belong to the vulgarisation's ledger rather than the theory's.

The reading has genuine textual support, and this article has itself relied on the founder's revisions as evidence of his scientific conduct. But as a defence of the programme it proves too much and too little. Too much, because a theory whose every claim is conditional on conditions that can be enumerated only after the event has purchased immunity at the price the criterion exists to detect: the conditions must be specified in advance, with the observations that would show them absent, or the conditional form is postponement wearing a scholar's gown. Too little, because the programme that was actually carried through history, the doctrine of the parties, the textbooks, and the states, was the unconditional version, preached as necessity and enforced as orthodoxy; and a research programme is graded on the sequence of theories its carriers actually produced and defended, not on the most defensible reconstruction its scholars can assemble after the grading is announced. There is a real distinction between Marx and Marxism, and the distinction acquits the man of much that the movement must answer for. But the title of this article names the movement's doctrine, and the movement cannot spend a century trading on the founder's authority and then, at the audit, disown the trades as vulgarisations. The vulgarisation was the programme, in the only sense in which programmes exist in history: as what their institutions taught, staked, and protected.

## A third credit: crises as the system's normal weather

The credits section entered two components on the programme's favourable side, and completeness requires a third, smaller and of a different kind. Marx's theory treated crises not as accidents befalling capitalism from outside, invasions, harvest failures, policy errors, but as the system's own periodic product, generated by the same accumulation that generated its growth, and destined to recur with its development. In the decades when the theory was formed, the respectable economics of the day largely treated commercial crises as disturbances of a natural harmony, explicable by monetary mismanagement or by shocks external to the economy's logic, and a settled doctrine associated with Say denied the possibility of general gluts altogether. Against that background, the insistence that overproduction crises were endogenous, recurrent, and inseparable from the system's success was a risky commitment: it staked the theory on the future's weather. The future supplied the weather. Crises recurred across the following century and a half with a regularity that no school now disputes, and the study of the business cycle, an industry the classical framework had no theoretical room for, became a permanent department of economics. The credit is real but must be entered at its correct value. Recurrence is a pattern claim, and pattern claims are cheaper than the dated, mechanism-specific forecasts the criterion honours most; several rival accounts, from underconsumptionist and monetary traditions outside Marxism, also expected recurrence. What the programme can claim is priority and integration: it expected the storms before the meteorological service existed, and it derived them from the same mechanism that explained the calm. The entry stands as a component credit, consistent with the ledger's general shape, in which the founding analysis of capital's dynamics repeatedly outperforms the tradition's later conduct and its prophecies of destination.

## Grading a tendency: the protocol fairness requires

The defence's most reasonable methodological demand, running through several sections above, is that tendency claims be graded as tendencies, not as the dated forecasts they never were. The demand should be granted, and granting it properly requires a protocol, because a tendency claim graded loosely is a claim graded never. The protocol this article has applied, stated now in the open, has four rules. First, the mechanism must be specified independently of the outcome: the theory must say what process generates the tendency, in terms that could be examined in cases where the tendency is not in dispute. Second, the counteracting influences must be enumerated in advance, as Marx himself enumerated the countertendencies to the falling rate of profit, and not discovered in the wreckage of each failure. Third, the tendency must have a signature: some observable pattern, in the distribution or the sequence of events, whose absence over a stated run of cases would count as the mechanism's defeat rather than its concealment. Fourth, the ledger must be kept in public: the tradition's own texts, dated, are the record of what was expected, and reconstructions of those expectations after the event are inadmissible as evidence of foresight.

Under this protocol the tradition's tendency claims divide instructively. The concentration tendency passes: its mechanism is specified, its signature, the rising scale and centralisation of capitals, is observable, and its absence, a durable renaissance of small production, would have counted against it and did not occur. The cyclical tendency passes in the manner described. The immiseration tendency, in its absolute form, fails the third rule's test across the long run of the advanced countries, and its relative replacement fails the fourth, since the replacement was entered in the ledger as though it had always been the claim. The polarisation tendency fails the third: the signature, a narrowing class structure, was absent across the very societies and decades the theory surveyed, and the global relocation fails the second, its counteracting machinery having been assembled after the national record was in. The protocol thus delivers the same verdict as the cruder scoreboard, but it delivers it in a form the defence's reasonable demand cannot refuse, because the rules are the rules any tendency science, geology, demography, evolutionary biology, accepts for itself. Fairness to tendencies is not a lower standard. It is the same standard, stated at the level where tendencies live, and the tradition's difficulty survives the translation intact.

## A worked case: the falling rate of profit and the Okishio challenge

Abstract grading persuades less than a single case followed through the whole machinery, and the debate over the falling rate of profit is the case where the programme's conduct can be watched at closest range, because the claim is precise, the challenge was precise, and the responses are all in print. Marx's law of the tendency of the rate of profit to fall, stated in the third volume of *Capital* with its counteracting influences enumerated in the same breath, holds that accumulation raises the proportion of capital laid out on means of production relative to that laid out on labour, and that since labour is the source of surplus, the rate of profit on the whole capital tends downward across the system's development, crises being the violent restorations of the conditions of profitability. The claim is a tendency claim in the protocol's sense: mechanism specified, countertendencies listed in advance by its author, signature in principle observable in the long movement of profit rates.

In the nineteen sixties the Japanese economist Nobuo Okishio proved a theorem that struck at the mechanism itself. Given the theory's own assumptions about how capitalists choose techniques, he showed that any cost-reducing innovation a capitalist would actually adopt raises, rather than lowers, the equilibrium rate of profit when real wages are constant, so that a falling rate requires rising real wages to explain it, which reverses the tradition's preferred direction of causation. The theorem was not an external polemic; it was constructed inside the programme's own value categories by an economist working in the tradition's Japanese school, and it presented the programme with the purest form of anomaly a theory can meet, an internal result contradicting a central law. The programme's responses across the following decades exhibit, in one debate, the entire range of conduct this article has catalogued. Some responses were amputative and honourable: economists in the tradition accepted the theorem's force, rebuilt the crisis theory on other foundations, profit squeeze, disproportionality, financial mechanisms, and recorded the falling rate law as surrendered or demoted. Some were reconstructive in the promising sense: the temporal single-system interpretation rebuilt the valuation of inputs and outputs on a reading of Marx's texts that dissolved the theorem's premises, a textual and logical labour whose merits specialists still contest, and which at least engaged the anomaly at its own level. And some were protective in the textbook form: the theorem was charged to the bourgeois character of its assumptions, its author's school was impugned, and the law was reasserted in forms whose elasticity had quietly grown, until the tendency's signature was no longer specified tightly enough for any movement of measured profit rates to embarrass it. The measurement disputes that engulfed the empirical branch of the debate, over the valuation of capital stocks and the treatment of unproductive labour, are documented in this series' empirical audit of the economics; what matters here is the distribution of the responses. A programme in health produces the first two kinds in dominance. The historical record of this debate shows all three in proportions the reader may grade, with the protective kind enjoying the sponsorship of the tradition's official institutions and the amputative kind proceeding, as with the analytical school, at its margins. The case is the ledger in miniature, and it is why this article's verdict is a distribution rather than a slogan.

## The demarcation problem's own fate

Honesty about the title's question requires a final piece of context about the question itself. The problem of demarcation, of drawing the line between science and non-science by a single criterion, has not fared well in the philosophy of science that produced it. Larry Laudan, surveying the wreckage in an essay on the demise of the demarcation problem, argued that two centuries of attempts had failed to produce a criterion that included the obvious sciences and excluded the obvious non-sciences, and that the honorific use of the word science, as a badge of epistemic authority to be awarded or withheld in cultural combat, should be abandoned in favour of the tractable questions underneath: which claims are well tested, which are speculative, which research traditions are progressing and which are stalling. The verdict of this article is written in Laudan's spirit, and its form obeys his moral. It does not award or withhold a badge. It grades a programme's conduct on the criterion that best survives the philosophy's own scrutiny, publishes the grades of the comparable programmes beside it, and states the exhibit that would change the grade. Readers who came for a one-word answer, science or faith, are offered instead the profile the evidence supports, and are warned that the demand for the one word is itself part of the phenomenon under examination, since the word's prestige, not its content, is what the combat over it has always been about.

That warning deserves its paragraph, because it explains the debate's otherwise inexplicable temperature. If the question were really about method, its resolution would be a technical matter for specialists, and its literature would be as calm as the literature on the demarcation of geology. The question is instead a proxy in a political conflict, in which science functions as the title of legitimacy for a doctrine that claims to govern the future, and faith functions as the title of contempt for the same claim. Each camp therefore needs the verdict in its crude form, and each camp's literature is organised to prevent the graded form from being heard: the opponents, because the graded form concedes the founding theory's genuine content and credits; the defenders, because the graded form records the ledger of the repairs. An assessment that both camps find partially uncongenial has not thereby proved itself impartial, but an assessment that either camp could quote entire, without remainder, in its own pamphlets would have failed the record in a way this article has tried at every joint to avoid.

## Explanatory adequacy: the row of the table where the defence wins

The criterion table gave Marxism its strongest grade on explanatory adequacy, and the grade was not a consolation prize; it records the property that explains the tradition's intellectual survival better than the faith framing alone can do. The framework renders an extraordinary range of phenomena intelligible within a single scheme. The commodity form, analysed at the opening of *Capital*, generates an account of how social relations between people acquire the appearance of relations between things, and that account ramified into a theory of ideology, of how the surface forms of a society systematically misrepresent its structure to its participants, which remains a working instrument in fields that have discarded every other part of the inheritance. The analysis of class connects the distribution of income to the organisation of production by a mechanism rather than by a metaphor. The theory of accumulation binds investment, employment, wages, and crisis into one dynamic. A reader who asks why generations of historians, sociologists, geographers, and literary scholars, most of them no more orthodox than their disciplines required, kept returning to this framework for instruments, finds the answer here: it explains, in the sense of making mechanisms visible, more of its domain with fewer disconnected parts than any rival scheme of comparable ambition.

Explanatory power of this order is a genuine epistemic good, and the verdict of this article does not confiscate it. What the programme criterion adds is the observation that explanatory power is also the property most easily counterfeited by a degenerating programme, because a scheme flexible enough to explain everything that happens has usually achieved the flexibility by the accommodations the criterion grades. The two rows of the table must therefore be read together and in the right order. Explanation that precedes the fact, that tells the investigator where to look and what signature to expect, is the engine of progressive science, and the founding Marxism did this often enough to earn its credits. Explanation that follows the fact, however brilliant, is the programme talking to itself in a mirror, and the tradition's later career is rich in mirrors of the first quality. The distinction is not between Marxism and science. It is the distinction, inside every ambitious framework, between its youth and its old age, and the tragedy the ledger records is of a framework whose explanatory genius survived its predictive discipline and was used, by its custodians, to make the survival feel like vindication.

## The fertility argument weighed

The defence's closing argument, that a merely degenerating programme could not have colonised the disciplines as this one has, deserves a more careful answer than dismissal, because fertility is a real property and Lakatos himself treated a programme's heuristic power, its capacity to generate problems and partial solutions, as a component of its appraisal. The fact stands: concepts forged in this tradition, class, ideology, alienation, commodity fetishism, primitive accumulation, hegemony in its Gramscian descendant, circulate as working tools in history, sociology, anthropology, geography, and cultural studies, and research traditions with no allegiance to the doctrine's politics use them daily. A programme that had contributed nothing but protective verbiage would not have stocked so many other people's workshops.

The weighing turns on what fertility evidences. It evidences the explanatory adequacy already credited, and the heuristic richness of the founding construction, whose problems were so well chosen that a century of workers could mine them. It does not evidence progressiveness in the criterion's sense, because the fertility is overwhelmingly the fertility of the foundations rather than of the repairs: the concepts the disciplines borrowed were coined in the programme's first generations, and the modifications graded in this ledger, the reinterpretations and relocations, exported nothing comparable. Astrology's long dominion over medicine and astronomy is the standing warning against inferring truth from fertility, and the warning should be applied gently here, since the comparison is of logical form rather than of content: a framework can organise inquiry for centuries around problems it cannot solve, and its very organisation of the problems can be its lasting contribution. That, on the evidence assembled, is close to this programme's actual historical role. It taught the social sciences which questions about capitalism were worth asking, and equipped them to ask those questions better than the programme's own custodians, in the end, were willing to. The disciplines took the questions and left the prophecies, which is the market's verdict on the components, delivered over a century, and it agrees with the table's.

## The great relocation: Western Marxism's turn from prediction to interpretation

The programme's geography contains a confession that its maps rarely acknowledge. After revolution failed to arrive in the West and the Soviet regime had consolidated, the tradition's most original minds in Western Europe, Georg Lukács, Karl Korsch, Antonio Gramsci, and later the Frankfurt School, progressively relocated the programme's centre of gravity from the terrain where its claims had failed, political economy and the forecast of revolution, to the terrain of culture, consciousness, and philosophy, where claims are graded by illumination rather than by event. The relocation produced work of enduring value. The theory of hegemony, forged by Gramsci in a prison cell to explain why the Western working class consented to its order, is a genuine theoretical construction, and the Frankfurt analysis of mass culture opened a field. But in the programme's ledger the relocation must be entered for what it was: a strategic withdrawal from the falsifiable frontier, undertaken in direct response to the frontier's collapse, which preserved the tradition's intellectual life by moving it to ground where the criterion of novel prediction does not operate. A programme may honourably become a philosophy; many have. What it may not do is become a philosophy in practice while continuing to claim, in its self-description, the standing of the science it was. The Western tradition's best minds were often candid about the transformation; its institutions, citing their work as the programme's continuing vitality, were not. The episode also refines the verdict's distribution, since the relocated programme's products are not failed science but successful interpretation, and grading them as science at all is the category error the demarcation discussion warned against. They belong in the ledger as evidence about the programme's response to refutation, which is where this article has placed them: the most intelligent accommodation ever devised, the conversion of a prophetic doctrine into a critical one, at the price of the prophecy.

## Symmetry completed: the critics' frameworks graded

The symmetric application promised earlier has one more obligation to discharge before the verdict, because the frameworks that organised the opposition to Marxism were themselves research programmes with predictions, and a grading that exempted them would commit the asymmetry this article exists to refuse. The totalitarianism framework, the mid-century construction that grouped the Soviet and fascist regimes under a single syndrome and organised Western analysis of the communist world, made its own definite expectations: that such regimes were essentially unchangeable from within, that their ideology was a fixed operating system rather than a decaying legitimation, and that their trajectory was permanence or expansion rather than internal dissolution. The end of the European regimes falsified the expectation of permanence in the most public manner available to social science, and the framework's carriers responded with manoeuvres this article's readers will recognise: the collapse was redescribed as the framework's vindication in general while its specific expectations of stability were quietly retired, and auxiliary distinctions between hard and soft variants of the syndrome, assembled as the regimes liberalised, absorbed the anomalies of the intervening decades. Convergence theory, the rival expectation that the two systems would grow alike, failed in the opposite direction and was accommodated with equal dexterity. Modernisation theory's grading has already been entered. The point of the exercise is not to acquit Marxism by its company, a move this article has refused at every stage. It is to establish the calibration of the field honestly: the social sciences' grand frameworks, of every political colouring, show the same distribution of conduct, with accommodation the statistical norm and progressive episodes the honourable exception. Within that calibration, the verdict on this programme stands as graded, neither the unique disgrace of the opponents' pamphlets nor the misunderstood excellence of the defence's, but a record poorer than the field's best and richer than its worst, compiled over a longer run than any rival was granted. Length of run is itself part of the finding. Programmes granted a century and a half, states, and the allegiance of millions accumulate more failures than programmes granted a seminar decade, and they also accumulate more chances to have produced the exhibit that would reverse the grade. The exhibit's absence, across that expanse of opportunity, is the datum the defence must finally answer.

## What a progressive problemshift would have looked like

The invitation issued earlier can be made more useful by sketching, concretely, the shape of the exhibit it requests, since defenders sometimes speak as though the criterion demanded the impossible from a theory of society. It does not. Imagine the tradition, confronting in the nineteen fifties the anomaly of the expanding salaried middle strata, responding not by redefining the proletariat to absorb them or relocating polarisation to the globe, but by a modification that staked itself: a revised class theory specifying, in advance, which segments of the salaried strata would be proletarianised in function and income as routine white-collar work was mechanised, which would consolidate as a distinct stratum with its own political signature, and what observable movements in earnings, autonomy, and allegiance would distinguish the two paths over the following generation. Such a modification would have had excess content; parts of it would have failed and parts might have been confirmed by the subsequent automation of clerical labour and the political behaviour that accompanied it; and the ledger entry, win or lose, would have been a scientific event. Or imagine the crisis theory, after the long postwar boom embarrassed its expectations, responding with a modification that specified the conditions under which the boom's own mechanisms, rising real wages, expanding credit, the internationalisation of production, would generate a crisis of a stated form at a stated level of the system, rather than with the assurance that the boom was a deferral whose bill would arrive in an unspecified currency. The elements for such shifts existed inside the tradition; its best economists produced fragments of them, usually at the margins, usually against the resistance of the custodians, and usually without the institutional follow-through that turns a fragment into a programme's problemshift. The counterfactual is not a taunt. It is the measure of the distance between the tradition's talent and its conduct, and it is the reason the verdict in this article is delivered in sorrow rather than in triumph by anyone who has read the founding texts with attention. The capacity was there. The institutions spent it on protection.

## The exemption claim: a different logic

One defence remains that has not yet been faced in its own terms, because it does not dispute the grading but the court's jurisdiction. In the tradition's official philosophy, dialectical materialism, the claim was made that the doctrine's method is not the formal, test-bound logic of the sciences arraigned in this article but a higher logic of contradiction and development, to which the categories of prediction and refutation apply only in a subordinate way. On this view, to grade Marxism by Lakatos is to grade a symphony by the rules of accountancy: the instrument is real, the application is a category error, and the doctrine's truths, being dialectical, announce themselves in the movement of the whole rather than in the fate of any stakeable claim.

The claim must be answered plainly, because it is the one defence that, if accepted, ends the trial rather than losing it. A method that exempts its holder from the grading applied to every rival account of the same world has not discovered a higher logic; it has resigned from the conversation in which accounts compete. The resignation would be honourable if it were complete. A doctrine that presented itself as a wisdom, a vision, or a faith, and asked for allegiance on those terms, could decline the laboratory's jurisdiction with dignity, as religions and philosophies do. The exemption claim does the opposite: it retains the title of science, the claim to have discovered the laws of society's motion, the authority over policy and education that the title was used to justify, while declining the obligations the title carries whenever the obligations fall due. Nor is the exemption even faithful to the founders, whose practice, as this article has shown, was to stake claims, revise them in public, and submit their work to the judgement of a future they expected to grade it. The dialectical exemption was manufactured by the custodians, in the decades when the grading turned hostile, and its function in the tradition's economy is exactly the function of the protective belt raised to the level of logic itself: the final auxiliary, behind which no anomaly can follow. A criterion that could not see through it would be useless; the programme criterion sees through it by asking the only question that cannot be dialectised away, what the revisions bought, and the exemption claim, examined as a revision, bought the programme fifty years of immunity in the territories where it was enforced. Immunity is not content. The court declines the exemption.

## The historical sciences: Darwin's programme as the control case

The defence that Marxism is a historical science, and must be graded as one, is the last of the serious defences, and it is best tested against the historical science Marx himself took as his model. Darwin's programme dealt in deep time, singular sequences, and mechanisms invisible in operation, everything the defence pleads for its own client. It also accumulated anomalies of exactly the kinds that afflict historical theories: the fossil record's gaps, the apparent suddenness of new forms, the problem of inheritance's mechanism, which Darwin got wrong. And its subsequent history contains the episodes by which the criterion recognises health. The programme's defenders predicted, from the theory's logic, the existence of transitional forms in strata of stated age and character, and such forms were found, from the early bird-reptile fossils unearthed within two years of the *Origin* to the fish-tetrapod intermediates located in our own era in rocks chosen because the theory said the transition should be recorded there. The mechanism of inheritance, the programme's greatest gap, was supplied by a modification, the union with genetics in the modern synthesis, which did not merely protect the core but multiplied its content, generating the population thinking from which novel predictions about variation and selection continue to flow. Darwinism's carriers also protected, evaded, and excommunicated in their day; the programme's history is not spotless, and its social applications disgraced it in places. The contrast is not between a pure science and a corrupt doctrine. It is between a historical programme whose modifications, across a comparable span of years, repeatedly bought confirmed novel content, and one whose modifications repeatedly bought safety. Marx welcomed the comparison with Darwin in his lifetime. The comparison, conducted on the criterion both programmes can be graded by, is the most respectful form the verdict can take, and it is not the verdict the tradition's custodians have taught their readers to expect.

## What success would have looked like

The positive standard implicit in the whole assessment should be stated once, affirmatively, so that the verdict is measured against a possibility rather than against a vacuum. A scientifically successful Marxism, by the middle of the twentieth century, would have looked like this. Its theory of capitalist development would have generated, from its modifications, a sequence of staked expectations about the system's evolution, about the geography of its crises, the composition of its classes, the trajectory of its distribution, of which a substantial proportion had been confirmed against the expectations of rivals, and its failures would have been recorded in its own textbooks as losses that taught the programme something, the way the sciences teach their dead ends as honoured ancestors. Its account of the successor order would have been revised in the light of the first experiments with the same openness, so that the second generation of theory described the states actually being built, their novel pathologies included, rather than explaining why those states were not the theory's responsibility. Its institutions would have rewarded the member who corrected the founder, as the founder had corrected himself, and its internal critics would have been its ornaments rather than its heretics. Nothing in this picture requires the doctrine to have been true in its destination claims; a programme can be progressive and wrong, as the history of science attests on every shelf, and can earn the title of science by its conduct on the way to a refutation it finally accepts. The picture's every element was available to this tradition, whose founder modelled several of them. That the actual history diverges from it at almost every point is the finding, and the divergence, not the destination claims alone, is what the words degenerating programme exist to describe.

## The afterlife: the programme after its carriers fell

The collapse of the European regimes at the end of the nineteen eighties, and of the Soviet Union shortly after, was the largest single anomaly in the tradition's history, and its handling is the programme's most recent entry in the ledger. The event had not been predicted by the tradition's mainstream, whose analyses of the Soviet system, whether apologetic or Trotskyist or social democratic, had converged on its permanence in their different vocabularies; the dissident and state-capitalist schools that had diagnosed its brittleness took a deserved credit, small in the ledger's total but real. The mainstream's response followed the classical sequence at continental scale. The regimes were redescribed as having never embodied the doctrine, a claim with genuine textual support that had nonetheless not been the doctrine's operative position while the regimes were available as evidence of its vitality. The collapse was attributed to betrayals, encirclement, and the deficiencies of particular leaders, the circumstantial auxiliaries of an earlier section promoted to explain the largest event of the century. And the doctrine's centre of institutional gravity completed its migration from parties and states to universities, where the incentives of the host discipline, novelty of interpretation prized above all, rewarded exactly the fertility without ledger-keeping that the fertility section described.

One further episode of the afterlife requires grading because the defence cites it constantly. The financial crisis of the century's first decade revived popular interest in Marx's analysis of capitalism, sent readers to *Capital* in numbers its publishers had not seen for a generation, and was widely celebrated in the tradition's press as the doctrine's vindication. The ledger's entry is narrower. The programme had predicted, as a founding credit, that crises are the system's normal weather, and the storm's arrival confirms the credit already entered; it does not add a new one, because no modification of the programme had staked the crisis's form, timing, or mechanism in advance against rival accounts, and the accounts produced after the event, however illuminating, are accommodations of a fact the whole world already knew. Retrospective prophecy, the institutional habit named in the faith sections, is the precise term for the celebration, and its prevalence in the afterlife's literature is the continuity datum: the conduct survived the carriers. A programme whose states have fallen and whose parties have dissolved, and whose scholarly remnant still reaches first for the vindication rather than the ledger, has demonstrated that the pattern this article documents was never principally about the states at all. It was about the structure of the allegiance, which is where the faith framing began.

## The politics of the label: why the crude question will not die

It remains to explain why the question continues to be asked in its crude form, science or faith, when the materials for the graded answer have been available for half a century. The explanation is that the crude form serves every party to the quarrel except the truth. For the doctrine's opponents in political combat, the crude verdict of pseudoscience performs the excommunication that argument would have to earn slowly, and it spares them the graded form's concessions, the founding credits, the comparative embarrassments of their own frameworks, the concession that the charge's author granted the theory its scientific birth. For the doctrine's custodians, the crude charge is the ideal opponent, refutable by any schoolboy with the Popper passage open, and its perpetual circulation spares them the graded form's ledger, in which the refutations do not appear and the accommodations do. For the publishing and teaching industries that live off the quarrel, the crude form is a renewable resource, generating the same book and the same seminar every decade, while the graded answer, being an answer, threatens to end the franchise. And for the wider culture, the word science retains the aura of the laboratory at the exact historical moment when the laboratory's own philosophers have retired the badge, so that the combat over the word is a combat over an aura, conducted by parties who would mostly be unable to state the criterion they are fighting under.

Against this economy of interests, an assessment article can do only one thing, which is to make the graded answer so available, so plainly stated, and so scrupulously fair to the losing sides that the crude form's users are put to the embarrassment of their shortcuts. That has been the ambition of the preceding sections. The verdict that follows is therefore deliberately unquotable in the pamphlets of either camp without the qualifications that surround it, and its unquotability is offered, at the end of a long argument, as a small piece of evidence that it might be true.

## Grading when the facts are contested: a note on method

A reader who has followed the ledger may object that its entries depend on facts, the movement of wages, the shape of class structures, the rates of profit, that are themselves contested terrain, measured differently by rival schools with rival instruments, so that the grading smuggles in the grader's economics. The objection identifies a real feature of this field, and the article's defence against it should be explicit. The ledger grades conduct before it grades outcomes. Its central entries do not require the reader to accept any school's measurement of anything; they require only the tradition's own dated texts and the tradition's own subsequent texts, set side by side. That the immiseration claim was restated in a relative form as the absolute record worsened is a fact about the literature, verifiable in its pages, whatever the true movement of wages was. That polarisation was relocated to the globe after the national structures refused to narrow is a fact about the sequence of theories, not about the true shape of any class structure. That the withering of the state was postponed by successive auxiliaries is written in the official record of the states concerned. Where the ledger does enter credits, concentration and the cycle, it relies on directions of evidence that the rival schools' own literatures concede in substance, and it has routed every genuinely contested measurement to the empirical audit rather than building on it. A grading of conduct is not immune to dispute, but it is immune to the specific charge of resting on contested facts, and it is the appropriate grading for a question about method, since method is conduct. The tradition is not here convicted of having been wrong about the world, though on the great prophecies it was. It is convicted, on its own paper trail, of having responded to being wrong in the manner the criteria exist to detect.

## The retreat to critique: was it ever a science at all?

A quieter defence, heard more often in the seminar room than in the pamphlet, holds that the whole proceeding misconceives its object: Marx's work announces itself on its title pages as a critique of political economy, and critique, the immanent exposure of a society's categories and contradictions, is a genre with its own standards, to which prediction is as foreign as it is to a work of moral philosophy. On this account the prophecies were the movement's embroidery on a fabric of analysis, and the analysis stands or falls by its illumination, which the explanatory row of the table has already graded generously.

The defence describes something real about the founding work, whose subtitle is exactly what it says, and whose greatest pages are critique in the stated sense. It fails as an apologia for the programme for the reason the textual defence failed: the programme in history was not content to be a critique. Its founder prophesied in the same volumes that he analysed, drew political conclusions from the prophecies, and organised an international movement on their authority; his collaborator codified the whole as the science of society's laws; and the movement's parties staked their strategies, and later their states, on the prophecies' truth. A tradition may not spend a century and a half drawing cheques on the prophetic account and then, at the audit, announce that it was always a work of criticism. The honest version of the seminar defence is available and has been taken by the tradition's best modern representatives: to concede that the doctrine was two things, an analysis that endures and a prophecy that failed, and to rebuild the inheritance on the analysis alone, surrendering the title of science for the destination claims while retaining it, in the modest programme sense, for the components that earned it. That is, in substance, the verdict this article reaches by the longer road, and the seminar defence is best understood not as an objection to it but as the verdict's academic form, arrived at independently. Where the two differ is that the verdict keeps the ledger attached, and the ledger is the part the seminar prefers to forget.

## Why the failures cluster: mechanisms of things and prophecies of agents

The ledger's most interesting pattern has been noted in passing and deserves its own examination before the verdict, because it suggests the deepest available explanation of the programme's divided record. The credits cluster in one region and the failures in another, and the boundary between the regions is not random. Where the theory analysed the mechanics of capital, accumulation, concentration, the cycle, the reserve of labour, it repeatedly saw further than its rivals. Where it prophesied the conduct and destiny of collective agents, the location of revolution, the simplification of classes, the destination of history, it repeatedly failed. The first region concerns processes that proceed whether or not anyone understands them, driven by competition's compulsion on agents who must accumulate or perish. The second concerns the future choices of classes and states, agents who read, organise, adapt, and, not least, read the prophecies made about them.

The asymmetry has a name in the philosophy of social science: reflexivity. A prediction about a planetary orbit leaves the orbit unmoved; a prediction about a class's behaviour enters the deliberations of that class and of its enemies, and alters the behaviour predicted. Governments that had read the immiseration thesis built welfare states partly to refute it; employers and states that had read the polarisation thesis learned to cultivate the middle strata the thesis said would vanish; parties that had read the revolution thesis organised to prevent or to force the event, and the event's geography was scrambled by the organising. Marx himself understood the mechanism in its political form, insisting that men make their own history though not in circumstances of their choosing, a sentence whose first clause is the permanent solvent of his system's prophecies and whose second clause is the permanent defence of its analyses. A theory that contains, in its own foundations, the reason its forecasts of agents will be unreliable has a special obligation to hold those forecasts lightly. The tradition held them with both hands, because they were the warrant of its authority, and the asymmetry in the ledger is the price. The finding generalises beyond this case, and it is the closest this article comes to a law of its own: programmes of social theory earn their scientific standing in proportion as they analyse mechanisms and forfeit it in proportion as they prophesy destinations, and the proportion in this programme was set, tragically for its scientific standing, by the uses its carriers made of it.

## Two more programmes graded: Weber and Keynes

The symmetric audit can be completed with two brief gradings that fix the scale's middle region, where most serious programmes live. The Weberian programme, organised around the rationalisation of the modern world, generated no dated prophecies and protected its core with ideal types whose elasticity its critics have always noted; yet its modifications repeatedly bought content, in the comparative sociology of religion's economic ethics, in the analysis of bureaucracy whose expectations about administration's growth and character the century ratified in detail its author specified in advance, and in the research traditions on authority and legitimacy that continue to produce findings their founder's scheme made visible. Its grade is the mirror of this article's subject: weak on prophecy by design, since it largely refused prophecy, and progressive in episodes where its concepts were sharpened into instruments. The Keynesian programme shows the full Lakatosian biography within a single lifetime of a field: a founding modification of the classical core that predicted, against the orthodoxy of its day, the persistence of mass unemployment without wage collapse and the efficacy of demand management, implications confirmed closely enough in the war and postwar decades to count as a progressive shift of the first rank; then a long articulation in which the programme's belt absorbed anomaly after anomaly, the Phillips curve's successive repairs being the type case, until the stagflation anomaly and the rational expectations counterattack forced the crisis Kuhn's model predicts. The two gradings matter here because they show the criterion doing discriminating work on programmes no one arraigns as faiths, awarding honours and recording degeneration within the same careers. Marxism's career, set between them, is longer than both and shows the same phases, fertility, articulation, protection, crisis, in a slower and grander cycle, with the difference that its crisis, when it came, was forbidden by its carriers from doing the work crises do in Kuhn's account, which is to clear the ground for a successor. The programme's tragedy in the criterion's terms is not that it degenerated. It is that degeneration, elsewhere a phase, was made in this case a permanent condition by institutions that could survive anything except the admission.

## The critic's concessions: what Popper granted Marx

Fairness to the charge requires fairness to its author's whole position, which the pamphlet versions on both sides truncate. Popper's writings on Marx contain, beside the indictment, a sustained acknowledgement that embarrasses the flattened charge more thoroughly than any defender's pamphlet. He treated Marx as a major figure in the history of social thought whose critique of the capitalism of his day was animated by a moral seriousness he honoured, and he credited the Marxian analysis with a permanent methodological acquisition: the demonstration that social phenomena must be explained through institutions and their unintended consequences rather than through the psychology of individuals, a point on which he ranked Marx's insight above the psychologism of most of his contemporaries. He praised the analysis of the economic process in *Capital* as a genuine, if flawed, attempt at institutional explanation, and he directed his fire with explicit care at the historicist superstructure, the claim to laws of historical destiny, rather than at the whole edifice. He even recorded that Marx's prophecy had been, in his judgement, a false prophecy honestly made, and that its falsity had done the world the service of goading the democracies into the reforms that refuted it, an irony he savoured: the prediction helped create the conditions of its own failure, which is reflexivity acknowledged by the prosecution itself.

These concessions matter to the assessment for two reasons. First, they confirm from the prosecution's side the distribution this article has reached by its own road: a founding analysis of real power and partial predictive success, a prophetic superstructure that failed, and a tradition that protected the superstructure at the expense of the analysis. When prosecutor and ledger agree on the shape of the record, the shape is probably the record's. Second, the concessions model the conduct the whole case is about. Popper revised his judgements in print across his career, softened formulations, acknowledged the force of objections from Lakatos and others, and rebuilt his own position in successive editions and replies, which is to say that the falsificationist, in his practice, behaved as Lakatos said all scientists behave, protecting a core by fertile modification. His programme's modifications, the sophisticated falsificationism that answered Duhem and the conventionalist stratagems, bought content: they generated the very criterion by which this article has graded his target. The trial's deepest symmetry is that judge and defendant were in the same business, and the judge's business prospered by the rules the defendant's custodians broke.

## The mirror: the eschatology of the doctrine's rivals

The faith framing, before it is left, must pass the symmetric test this article has applied to every other instrument, because an instrument that only wounds one side is a weapon rather than a measure. The eschatological structure, a directed history, a chosen agent, a redemptive passage, a final condition beyond conflict, is not a Marxist monopoly. The liberal doctrine of progress in its confident centuries carried the same furniture in different dress: history as the expansion of commerce and reason, the bourgeoisie or the entrepreneur as chosen agent, the final condition a perpetual peace of trading nations. Its twentieth-century heirs produced, at the moment of the Soviet collapse, a proclamation of history's end in the universalisation of the market order, a text whose structure, a long pilgrimage of ideological forms terminating in the author's own dispensation, Löwith would have recognised at a glance, and whose subsequent embarrassments were absorbed by its adherents with auxiliaries indistinguishable in form from those catalogued here. The market's providential versions, in which an invisible hand guarantees the harmony of interests in a final state that history approaches, replicate the pattern in economics' own house. None of this grades any proposition of any of these doctrines, which is precisely the point the faith sections established. The eschatological shape is the shape of modern historical hope as such, the secular inheritance of a religious civilisation, and every doctrine that promises a destination will wear it. Marxism's distinction is not that it wears the shape but that it wore it while claiming, with unique explicitness, to have replaced hope with knowledge, and while building institutions that punished the observation of the difference. The framing convicts the tradition of a pretension, not of a pattern, and assessments that wield it as a general refutation of the doctrine's content have mistaken the instrument, as this article has been at pains to show.

## Use-novelty applied: three exhibits re-examined

The criterion's sharpest instrument, Zahar's refinement of the novel fact, has been used throughout in summary form, and a section applying it slowly to the three exhibits that dominate the popular argument will show both its power and its fairness. The test asks of each alleged prediction a single question: was the fact used in the construction of the theory or modification that is credited with predicting it? If it was used, the confirmation is the theory agreeing with its own ingredients, and the honour of prediction is withheld however impressive the agreement feels. If it was not used, and the fact was improbable on rival accounts, the honour is paid in full.

The world market passages fail the test, as an earlier section held, because the expansion they describe was the construction material of the Manifesto's whole argument, the observed engine of the bourgeois epoch, projected forward with genius. The concentration analysis passes it. The tendency toward the centralisation of capital was not a datum the theory was built to accommodate; the economy of Marx's day displayed concentration in embryo and competition among multitudes in fact, and the theory's construction ran from the logic of accumulation to a destination the contemporary scene did not display and the dominant economics did not expect. The subsequent century's ratification is therefore a payment the criterion honours without discount. The Russian expectation of the weakest-link construction divides under the test, which is why it has been graded as the ledger's most arguable entry. The revolution's occurrence in a backward country was not used in the construction of orthodox Marxism; it was the anomaly the construction was built to digest, and the construction did assert, before the event, that the chain would break at its weakest link rather than its strongest. But the construction's materials were the war and the collapse of the old expectation, and its assertion was inseparable from the anomaly it answered, so that scholars of the criterion divide on whether the confirmation was novel or was the anomaly's own face turned toward the future. The division is instructive in itself. A criterion that can locate, with this precision, the exact joint at which an exhibit's status is contestable has earned its place as the trial's instrument, whatever grade the exhibit finally receives, and the invitation to the defence is correspondingly precise: produce an exhibit that does not divide the judges at the joint.

## Prediction and prophecy: the grammar of the future

Running beneath the whole case is a distinction of grammar that the tradition's rhetoric systematically blurred, and stating it cleanly may be the most useful service this article performs for a general reader. A prediction, in the grammar of science, is a conditional sentence whose conditions are stated and whose failure is informative: given these mechanisms and these circumstances, this signature will appear, and if it does not, something in the premises is wrong. A prophecy, in the grammar of the traditions from which the eschatology argument descends, is an unconditional sentence about a destination, whose failure is never informative because the destination's arrival is guaranteed by the authority that announces it, and delay is a category internal to the announcement. The two grammars can wear identical words. "The system will produce its own gravediggers" is a prediction if it is shorthand for a mechanism whose operation can be traced and whose blockage can be specified; it is a prophecy if its truth is carried by the story's shape and its every postponement is absorbed as the story's depth.

The tradition's career can be written as the slow migration of its central sentences from the first grammar to the second. In the founding texts the predictive grammar dominates often enough to earn the credits this ledger has paid: mechanisms are named, signatures implied, revisions made when the signatures failed to appear. In the custodial texts the prophetic grammar takes over: the destination sentences are repeated without their mechanisms, the conditions are dropped as pedantry, and the sentences' function shifts from staking the theory to rallying the faithful, until in the official literature of the ruling parties the future is spoken of entirely in the prophetic register, as a possession rather than a hypothesis. The migration explains a datum the methodological sections left scattered, which is that the tradition's scientific quality varies inversely with its political power. In opposition, poor and persecuted, it analysed; in power, endowed and enforced, it prophesied, because prophecy is the grammar of legitimation and analysis is the grammar of inquiry, and institutions choose the grammar their survival requires. The title's question, science or faith, is thus finally a question about which grammar a body of sentences is being used in, and the same sentence can change its answer as it changes its custodians. That is why this article has graded a programme's conduct rather than a doctrine's essence, and why its verdict, though severe, is addressed to the custodians and their incentives more than to the texts they mishandled.

## The ledger at earlier dates: degeneration is a verdict on a run

A possible misunderstanding of the programme criterion should be removed before the verdict, because it affects how the verdict may be used. Lakatos's grades are not essences; they are judgements on a sequence up to a date, and they are revisable in both directions, since a degenerating programme can be revived by a fertile modification and a progressive one can coast into protection. It is therefore worth asking how this programme's ledger would have read at earlier closings, because the answer disciplines any triumphalism in the present grade. At the end of the nineteenth century, the ledger was genuinely promising: the concentration analysis was being ratified by the trust movement before the world's eyes, the crisis theory organised a growing literature, the programme's historical works were transforming the study of the recent past, and its failures, the English lever, the pace of immiseration, could still be charged to a young theory's auxiliaries with a clear conscience. Bernstein's audit of the eighteen nineties was alarming to the orthodox precisely because the ledger was still arguable, and a programme in health would have answered it with revisions rather than anathemas. By the century's midpoint the ledger had darkened: the great prophecies had failed in their first full trials, the protective machinery was built and staffed, yet the programme's academic fertility was at its height and its historical school was producing work of permanent value. A grader closing the books then might have written mixed, and meant it as a warning. The grade of mixed to poor belongs to the full run, in which the warning's half century was spent as it was spent. The purpose of the exercise is not to soften the verdict but to locate it in time, as the criterion requires. Programmes are not condemned by nature; they are graded on their conduct, and conduct can change, which is why the invitation to produce the reversing exhibit is a real invitation and not a formality. The tradition's remaining carriers are free, at any moment, to begin the sequence that would reopen this ledger. Nothing in the record suggests the court's door is locked from the inside.

## The popular charge's kernel: why the flattened version survives

The article began by correcting the popular version of Popper's charge, and intellectual honesty requires a closing word on why the false version flourishes, because its flourishing is not mere ignorance. The flattened charge, that Marxism is unfalsifiable, is wrong as attribution and wrong as history, but it is the vernacular expression of a perception that the accurate charge confirms: that in the mouths of its custodians the doctrine could not lose. A layperson who has argued with a believer and watched every fact, high wages and low, boom and slump, revolution and reaction, emerge from the argument as confirmation of the same doctrine has experienced the phenomenon Popper analysed, in its social form, and reaches for the word unfalsifiable to name the experience. The experience is real even though the word misfires, since the doctrine's texts contain falsifiable claims in abundance; what the layperson has met is not the texts but the practice, the protective belt operated as a conversational method. Scientists who repeat the flattened charge are often reporting the same encounter from the other side of the seminar table. The accurate charge is therefore not a refutation of the popular one but its explanation and its discipline: it locates the unfalsifiability where it actually lives, in the conduct of a tradition rather than in the logic of a theory, and it prescribes the remedy the popular version cannot, which is not to dismiss the theory unread but to grade the tradition's sequence and publish the ledger. Articles that merely correct the attribution, and stop, leave their readers with the impression that the perception was baseless, and the impression is false. The perception was the beginning of the case. It took a philosopher of Popper's quality to state what the perception was a perception of, and it has taken this article's length to grade it.

## The limiting case: doctrine protected by state power

The institutional argument running through this article has a limiting case that fixes its meaning by exaggeration, and it should be stated briefly and exactly. In the Soviet Union of the nineteen thirties and forties, the state's protection of doctrine extended beyond the social theory that legitimised it into the natural sciences themselves, most notoriously in the ascendancy of Trofim Lysenko, whose Lamarckian biology was installed as orthodoxy because it harmonised with the official philosophy's preferences, while genetics, the progressive programme in its field, was suppressed as bourgeois and its practitioners destroyed. The episode is not an entry in Marxism's ledger as a theory of society, and this article does not enter it as one. Its relevance is to the mechanism. Lysenkoism shows what a research programme's protective belt becomes when it is armed: criticism reclassified as sabotage, anomaly reclassified as treason, the ledger itself abolished by decree. Between the seminar's gentle accommodations and that terminus there is a continuum, and the continuum is institutional, not logical. Every step along it is a step in the same direction, the raising of the price of recording a loss, and the tradition's social theory travelled further along it than any comparable programme in the modern sciences precisely because it alone, among the programmes graded here, acquired states. The moral for the criterion is that degeneration is not only an intellectual event. It is what happens to inquiry when the inquirers' institution cannot afford the inquiry's results, and the Marxist programme is the type case not because its ideas were uniquely protective but because its institutions were uniquely unable to afford their failure. Any future carrier of any doctrine, reading this ledger, should find its warning addressed to themselves.

## The last carrier: the university and its incentives

Since the fall of the regimes, the programme's principal carrier has been the university, and the change of carrier has changed the pattern of its conduct in ways the ledger should record, because they explain the afterlife's peculiar combination of vitality and sterility. The party rewarded protection and punished revision; the university rewards novelty and punishes nothing. Under party discipline, the programme's characteristic sin was the auxiliary that saved the doctrine. Under academic incentives, its characteristic sin is the reinterpretation that generates a publishable reading, the founder's texts mined for anticipations of each new theoretical fashion, so that Marx has been successively reconstructed as a theorist of whatever the surrounding disciplines happen to prize. The two sins are cousins. Both substitute the carrier's needs for the world's evidence; both produce literatures of great ingenuity whose relation to the programme's ledger is decorative. Yet the academic carrier has one virtue the party lacked: it permits the amputations. The analytical school's audit, the historians' revisions, the economists' surrenders of untenable laws, all proceeded under university shelter, unpunished if also unrewarded with influence over the tradition's self-image. The programme's best modern work has therefore been done in a condition of institutional indifference, by scholars free to grade the inheritance because no institution's survival depended on the grade. If the tradition has a scientific future, the afterlife suggests its unlikely address: not a restored church but a dispersed republic of letters in which the name Marx functions as the name of a set of problems rather than of a creed, and in which the ledger this article has kept is kept by the heirs themselves, as a matter of professional honour. The founders, who revised in public and asked to be graded by the future, would have recognised the address as the one they left in their will.

## The historians' exception: a subprogramme that behaved

One region of the programme's empire behaved, across long stretches, as the criterion wishes every region to behave, and its exceptionality sharpens the general verdict by showing that the verdict was not foreordained by the doctrine's content. The Marxist historical school, the generations of historians formed by the tradition in Britain, France, and elsewhere, took the framework's concepts into the archives and staked them on the evidence of particular centuries. The results included genuine discoveries: the reconstruction of the English revolution's social depths, the recovery of the crowd and the common people as historical agents with their own rationality, the mapping of how customary economies carried moral expectations that market society violated at its peril. E. P. Thompson's making of the English working class and his anatomy of the moral economy, Christopher Hill's reordering of the seventeenth century, Eric Hobsbawm's syntheses of the long nineteenth century, whatever their individual controversies, were works in which a framework generated questions the archives then answered in ways that surprised the framework's friends as often as its enemies, which is the signature of inquiry rather than of illustration.

The school's internal debates supply the criterion's clearest positive exhibit inside the tradition. The transition debate, opened by Maurice Dobb's studies and Paul Sweezy's challenge, and renewed a generation later in the Brenner debate, turned on a question the framework itself had posed: what mechanism dissolved feudalism and installed capitalism? Positions were staked with archival consequences attached; Robert Brenner's argument, that the divergent development of Eastern and Western Europe turned on the balance of class forces in the countryside rather than on demography or trade, directed researchers to records whose testimony discriminated between the rival accounts, and the debate's participants, including its losers, treated the outcomes as verdicts rather than as occasions for reinterpretation. Here, in miniature and at the tradition's scholarly margin, is a problemshift sequence with the progressive form: a modification that organised new evidence, rivals that could state what would change their minds, and a literature that kept its ledger in public. The exception proves the article's central contention by contrast. The doctrine's concepts were never the obstacle to scientific conduct. The obstacle was the institutional demand, wherever the doctrine ruled or hoped to rule, that the conduct stop short of the destination claims. The historians could afford their honesty because nothing was staked on their conclusions except the truth; the parties could not, because everything was. A reader who wishes to see what this tradition was capable of, as science, should read its historians; whoever wishes to see what its institutions did to that capability should read the rest of this article.

## A note on the word faith

The title's second term has been used throughout in its structural sense, and a brief note on its meanings will prevent a final misunderstanding. Faith names at least three things that the debate confuses. It can mean belief held without evidence, or against it, which is the sense the opponents intend and the sense the methodological sections have, in effect, been grading under another name. It can mean trust, the reliance on a person or a tradition that all inquiry also requires, since no scientist verifies everything and every programme is carried by a confidence that outruns its proofs; in this sense every research programme runs partly on faith, and Kuhn's normal science is unintelligible without it. And it can mean fidelity, the loyalty that binds a member to a community and its story, which is the sense the eschatology sections analysed. The verdict of this article can now be translated into the title's vocabulary with the senses distinguished. As trust, the faith invested in the founding analysis was substantially honoured by the evidence. As belief maintained against the ledger, the faith demanded by the custodians for the destination claims was not honoured, and the demand is the degenerating pattern's human form. As fidelity, the faith of the tradition's adherents was a real and often honourable thing, expended on a story whose scientific warrant had lapsed, which is the precise configuration that Aron watched in Paris and Kołakowski recorded from inside. Science or faith is therefore the wrong disjunction not because the answer is somehow both in a blur, but because the doctrine's different components, and its carriers' different relations to them, fall on different sides of the line at different times, and the graded profile is the only form in which the truth of the matter can be stated without lying.

## Components, not a single grade: the distribution stated

The assessment has been building toward a distribution, and the distribution should now be stated in one place, because the commonest misreading of any graded verdict is to compress it back into the single word it replaced. The tradition is not one defendant but a portfolio, and its holdings have performed differently under every criterion in the table. The analysis of capital's dynamics, accumulation, concentration, the cycle, the reserve of labour, stands as the portfolio's strength: founded in mechanisms, confirmed in patterns, and still generating instruments other programmes borrow. The doctrine of history as a succession of modes, driven by the growth of the productive forces, stands in the middle distance: a scheme of immense organising power whose definite expectations, about sequences and about the correspondence between forces and relations, have been confirmed in some theatres and embarrassed in others, and whose ablest modern defenders maintain it as a framework of questions rather than of laws. The prophecies of destination, revolution where capitalism was ripest, polarisation, immiseration in its preached form, the withering of the state, stand as the portfolio's ruin: staked plainly, disappointed plainly, and protected by the full apparatus of accommodation until the protection, not the disappointment, became the tradition's defining intellectual act. And the political doctrine built on the prophecies, the claim of a party or a state to rule in the name of a known future, stands outside the grading of science altogether, in the territory where the faith framing and the cluster's political articles conduct their darker examinations.

### Why does the tradition not receive one grade for all its parts?

Because its parts were staked differently, tested differently, and defended differently. The analysis of accumulation earned confirmations its custodians did not need to manufacture; the prophecies of destination earned disappointments its custodians could not afford to record. A single grade would average the portfolio into a fiction that describes no component accurately.

The distribution also explains the debate's otherwise puzzling sociology, in which intelligent partisans talk past each other for decades without either side simply lying. The defender exhibits the portfolio's strength, the analysis, the historians, the concepts the disciplines borrowed, and reports truthfully that the inheritance is alive and fertile. The opponent exhibits the portfolio's ruin, the prophecies and their protection, and reports truthfully that the doctrine's central promises failed and were fudged. Each has chosen the component that suits the verdict required, and the choice, not dishonesty, is the mechanism of the quarrel's permanence. The criterion table's discipline exists to defeat exactly this selection: it forces the components onto one page, under criteria applied in one severity, and forbids the verdict to be carried by the strongest or the weakest holding. What survives that discipline is the profile this article has drawn, and the profile's asymmetry, strength in analysis and ruin in prophecy, is not a compromise manufactured for neutrality's sake. It is the shape of the evidence, visible alike to the prosecutor who conceded the analysis and to the internal auditors who amputated the prophecy, and an assessment that flattened it in either direction would be refuted by the record it claimed to summarise.

## The verdict: the right criterion and the honest result

The article's namable claim can now be delivered as its verdict, with the whole ledger behind it. The right criterion is not falsification: falsification is the wrong test, rejected by the philosophy of science for reasons that have nothing to do with charity toward this defendant, and its rejection does not rescue Marxism. On the better test, whether the programme's modifications have predicted novel facts that were subsequently confirmed, the record is mixed to poor. The mixture is real: the founding analyses of concentration and of the labour market's cycle generated confirmed predictions; the imperialism construction contains one arguable anticipation; the historical school conducted genuinely progressive episodes; the analytical school modelled honest amputation. The poverty is also real, and it is located at the programme's centre: the modifications with which the tradition met its great disappointments, on immiseration, on polarisation, on the geography of revolution, on the withering of the state, were accommodations, reinterpretations and relocations and postponements that bought safety and no content, and the tradition's characteristic response to failed prediction, across fifteen decades and under every variety of its institutions, has been to absorb the record rather than to anticipate it. That is the honest result, and it is stated against the defence's interest as the correction of Popper's charge was stated against the prosecution's, because an assessment that corrects only one side is a brief, and this article was commissioned as an assessment.

### What is the honest result when the better test is applied?

That the programme's founding analyses earned genuine confirmations while its characteristic repairs earned none: the modifications by which it survived its great disappointments accommodated the record instead of anticipating it, leaving a record mixed in its components and poor at its prophetic centre.

Two riders complete the verdict and bind its future use. The first is the defeater, repeated here because a verdict that buries its defeater has learned nothing from its subject: the grade of largely degenerating falls if anyone can identify a revision, made under an anomaly's pressure, that went on to predict some novel fact the future then confirmed, and the invitation to produce that exhibit stands open to the tradition's scholars on the terms stated in its own section. The second rider concerns the faith framing's place in the final account. The eschatology argument explains the allegiance, Aron documented its deformation of judgement, and the critical history traced the pattern across the tradition's whole career, and none of it enters the grade, because grades are for propositions and their handling, and the framing is for the believers and theirs. The complete answer to the title's question is therefore double, and its doubleness is its accuracy. As a science, the programme is graded mixed to poor on the criterion that matters, with its honours in analysis and its ruin in prophecy. As a faith, in the structural sense, it functioned for millions as the secular eschatology its critics described, and that functioning, not any single proposition's failure, is why the grading was resisted for so long by people fully capable of reading a ledger. The doctrine asked to be judged as the science of history's laws. It is so judged. What the judgement finds is a great analysis yoked to a failed prophecy, and a tradition that, when the two came into conflict, chose the prophecy and protected it, and thereby chose, in the only sense the title's disjunction finally permits, faith over science at exactly the points where science would have required surrender.

## What the verdict does not say

Verdicts are misused in proportion to their quotability, and this one is issued with its limits printed on its face. It does not say that the founding theory was empty, a fraud, or a religion from birth; the record refutes each of those, and the prosecutor's own concessions refute them again. It does not say that the tradition's concepts are worthless; the disciplines that borrowed them are the standing refutation. It is equally silent in the opposite direction: rival programmes are nowhere pronounced innocent, and the symmetric audit recorded degeneration across the field, including in the frameworks of this doctrine's prosecutors, and the verdict's severity is calibrated to a field in which severity is generally due. It does not say that the doctrine's moral energies, its indictment of exploitation and its solidarity with the insulted and injured of the earth, are touched by the grading at all; a programme's epistemic conduct and its ethical force are different properties, and some of the century's most necessary moral witnesses spoke in this tradition's vocabulary while some of its criminals ruled in the same vocabulary, neither fact grading the other. Finally, the ledger is nowhere pronounced closed. The criterion under which the verdict was reached is a historian's criterion, its grades are revisable, and the exhibit that would reopen this one has been specified with a precision that is itself the article's final act of fairness. What the verdict says is narrower and, being narrower, harder to evade: that on the best available test of scientific conduct, applied as evenly as the author could apply it, this programme's long career divides into an analysis that earned its standing and a prophecy that was protected out of its standing, and that the protection, repeated across generations and institutions, is the fact about Marxism that any honest answer to the old question must begin from.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The single most reliable improvement to classroom argument on this topic is the correction about the charge. Students arrive with Popper as the man who proved Marxism was never a science, and the seminar that begins by reading the actual passage, in which the theory's scientific birth is granted and the indictment is aimed at the response to refutation, changes the quality of everything that follows, because the class must thereafter argue about conduct and evidence rather than about labels. A productive exercise is to set the ledger work directly: assign each student one modification from the tradition's history, the labour aristocracy, the weakest link, the relative reading of immiseration, the global relocation of polarisation, and require a one-page grading under the novel fact criterion, with the chronology and construction of the modification documented from primary texts. The exercise teaches the criterion by use, teaches the tradition's history in its most consequential joints, and reliably produces the discovery that the grades are arguable at exactly the joints this article identified, which is the moment the topic stops being a recitation. Pair the exercise with Aron's study for the sociology of the seminar itself, since students grading believers should be invited to notice the asymmetries in the room.

For researchers, the live frontier of this question is not the verdict but the ledger's unfinished entries: the subprogrammes, in agrarian history, in the economics of innovation, in the comparative study of labour regimes, where Marxist modifications continue to be produced and can be graded prospectively rather than in retrospect, and where the invitation issued in this article might yet be answered by work already in progress. For historians of the tradition, the open problem is the institutional one this article pressed: a comparative account of how the programme's carriers, party, state, and university in succession, priced the recording of losses, which would convert the faith framing from an essayist's instrument into a research programme of its own, and not the least interesting one its subject has generated. For journalists, the practical residue is a discipline of quotation: never report the charge without its concession, never report a crisis as a doctrine's vindication without asking what was staked in advance, and treat every claim that a thinker foresaw the present as a request to see the dated text. The tradition's custodians and its enemies both trade in the flattened versions because the flattened versions are portable; the researcher's and the journalist's shared duty is to make them slightly less portable, one accurate paragraph at a time.

## The programme's own criterion: the standpoint answer

The tradition was not without its own answer to the demarcation problem, and the assessment is incomplete until that answer has been stated and graded like the others. The doctrine of ideology, present from the founders onward, held that thought is conditioned by the thinker's position in the relations of production, and that the ruling ideas of an age are the ideas of its ruling class. Karl Mannheim's *Ideology and Utopia* turned the instrument into a general sociology of knowledge and posed the question it could not avoid: if all thought is situationally bound, what exempts the doctrine that says so? The tradition's most sophisticated answer was the standpoint theory, given classical form in Lukács's *History and Class Consciousness*. The proletariat's standpoint, on this account, is epistemically privileged not by the virtue of its holders but by its position: the class that is itself a commodity, experiencing its own reification, can know society as a totality because its emancipation requires that knowledge, whereas the bourgeoisie's standpoint is structurally barred from the whole by its interest in the parts. The claim to science is thus re-founded as a claim about who can see.

The answer deserves respect as philosophy and grading as method. As philosophy, it identifies a real phenomenon, that social position shapes cognition and that some positions disclose what others conceal, a phenomenon every sociology of knowledge since has had to accommodate. As method, it fails the criterion's elementary demand, because it supplies no procedure by which a disputed claim can be graded independently of the standpoint of its maker. When the empirical workers of the actual proletariat declined to see what the standpoint said they saw, the theory's custodians drew the distinction that completed the immunisation: between the class's empirical consciousness and its imputed consciousness, the awareness it would have if it understood its position, which the party, as the standpoint's trustee, was licensed to supply on the class's behalf. With that distinction in place, no observation of any actual worker could count against the doctrine, since the observation merely measured the distance between empirical and imputed consciousness, a distance the doctrine existed to explain. The standpoint criterion thus ends where the exemption claim ended, by a more distinguished road: it relocates the test of truth from the world's behaviour to the believer's location, and a test that cannot be failed by the trustee of the standpoint is not a test. The episode belongs in the ledger as the programme's most intelligent protective construction, and its descendants in later standpoint theories, which generally retain the insight about position and cognition while surrendering the trustee and the guarantee, show by their amendments exactly where the original construction's flaw was located.

## The jurisdiction over nature: the dialectics of nature episode

The programme's claim to science extended, in its official form, beyond society to nature itself, and the fate of that extension is a compact ledger entry that the assessment should not omit. Engels's manuscripts on the dialectics of nature, published posthumously by the Soviet institute in the nineteen twenties, proposed that the same dialectical laws governing history, the transformation of quantity into quality, the interpenetration of opposites, the negation of the negation, operate throughout the natural world, and the official philosophy erected the proposal into a doctrine binding on the sciences within its jurisdiction. The episode's grading is swift. The dialectics of nature generated no novel facts for any natural science; its laws were illustrated from the sciences' results after the fact, in the manner of accommodation, and its practical effect within its jurisdiction was licensing and interference, the philosophy's custodians certifying which scientific theories were dialectical and which were idealist, with consequences that reached their terminus in the Lysenko affair examined earlier. Outside the jurisdiction, the doctrine was simply ignored by the sciences it claimed to govern, and the tradition's own ablest philosophers in the West, from Lukács onward, quietly disowned or bracketed it, Lukács indeed having denied, in the work of his maturity's preface, that the dialectic could be extended to nature at all, since nature knows no subject to stand over against it. A component of the official programme was thus surrendered in practice by its best minds, retained in liturgy by its institutions, and never missed by the sciences it purported to found. The pattern is by now familiar enough to need no emphasis: it is the whole case in one province, the claim of universal jurisdiction, the absence of novel content, the protection by institution, and the quiet amputation at the margins.

## The general crisis: postponement given a periodisation

The tradition's official doctrine about capitalism's trajectory in the twentieth century supplies the ledger's most systematic exhibit of postponement, because it was postponement organised as a periodisation. The doctrine of the general crisis of capitalism, codified in the Soviet textbooks, held that the system had entered its final epoch with the First World War and the Russian revolution, and that its subsequent history was the deepening of that crisis toward the inevitable end. When the system declined to end, the doctrine did not retire; it subdivided. The general crisis acquired stages, a first stage covering the war and revolution, a second stage after the next war, a third stage announced as the postwar boom's contradictions matured, each stage's proclamation absorbing the previous stage's disappointment and redescribing survival as a deeper form of the terminal condition. The boom decades were entered as the crisis's temporary stabilisation, a category inherited from the interwar debates, whose temporary character renewed itself for a generation. No observation available to the twentieth century could have embarrassed the doctrine, since expansion proved the crisis was being deferred at rising cost, contraction proved it was deepening, and stability proved it was gathering force. A periodisation that fits every period has ceased to describe any of them, and the doctrine's career is the cleanest available demonstration that the protective pattern was not a series of individual lapses by embarrassed commentators. It was the official science of a state, taught in its academies, printed in its textbooks, and graded, by the criterion this article applies, as degenerating in its purest institutional form.

## The transformation problem: a second worked case

The profit rate debate was offered as a worked case of the programme's conduct, and a briefer second case confirms that the distribution of conduct found there was not an accident of that debate. The transformation problem, the question of how the labour values of *Capital*'s first volume are transformed into the prices of production of the third, was shown within a decade of the third volume's publication to contain an incompleteness: Marx transformed outputs into prices while leaving inputs at values, and Ladislaus von Bortkiewicz, in the first decade of the twentieth century, supplied the simultaneous correction and demonstrated that the two aggregate equalities Marx had asserted, total value equals total price and total surplus value equals total profit, could not in general hold together. The problem was internal, technical, and undeniable in its narrow form, and the programme's century of responses divides along the now familiar lines. One line surrendered the claim in its original form and rebuilt what could be saved, on probability, on single-system temporal readings, on monetary interpretations of the value form, each reconstruction a genuine intellectual labour whose effect was to preserve a smaller and more defensible doctrine. Another line treated the problem's existence as a misunderstanding to be dissolved by exegesis, and a third, in the official literature, ignored it for decades while continuing to teach the third volume's constructions as settled science. The empirical branch of the question, whether labour values track market prices closely enough for the theory's purposes, produced findings that partisans of several reconstructions cite, and it belongs with the empirical audit, not here. For the ledger of conduct, the case adds one more distribution of the same shape: an anomaly met, at the tradition's margins, by its ablest minds with revision and surrender, and at its custodial centre by exegesis and silence. Two worked cases do not prove a pattern, but the pattern was established by the great predictions before the cases were opened, and the cases show the pattern reproducing itself at the scale of a technicality, where the stakes were smallest and honesty was cheapest. That reproduction at low stakes is among the most damning entries in the ledger, because it excludes the excuse that protection was the price of political survival. On the transformation problem, nothing political depended on the answer for half a century, and the centre protected anyway. Protection had become the programme's habit, and habits, unlike strategies, persist when their reasons lapse.

## The charge's political career: Popper in the Cold War

The assessment has graded a doctrine; a final act of fairness requires noticing what was done with the grading instruments themselves, because the charge examined in this article had a political career that its author neither controlled nor wholly welcomed. *The Open Society and Its Enemies*, written in exile during the war against fascism, became after the war a canonical text of the liberal West in its contest with the Soviet Union, circulated, translated, and taught as an arsenal book, and its author's critique of historicism was conscripted into a struggle in which the graded subtleties of his position, his concessions to Marx, his insistence that the theory had been born scientific, were the first casualties. Popper himself resisted the conscription in the ways available to him, maintaining his social democratic commitments, criticising the market doctrines of his own admirers, and repeating in print the distinctions his Cold War readers skipped. The episode matters to this article for a methodological reason beyond biographical justice. It demonstrates that the fate of an assessment in the world is independent of its accuracy, and that a grading can be simultaneously correct in its distinctions and serviceable, in its flattened form, to a camp. The defence is therefore wrong to dismiss the charge as a Cold War weapon, since its author forged it before the Cold War and defended its distinctions against the warriors; and the prosecution's pamphleteers are wrong to wield it as one, since the weapon's maker blunted every edge they rely on. The graded verdict offered here is issued in the knowledge that it will be flattened by its readers in both camps if it is read at all, and in the conviction, which the whole exercise presupposes, that the unflattened version is worth printing anyway.

## The rivals' debts: what the opposition learned

The fertility finding can be strengthened from an unexpected direction, the testimony of the programme's rivals, whose debts to it are a matter of their own record. Joseph Schumpeter, no friend of the doctrine's politics, opened his most famous work by treating Marx as a master whose analysis of the capitalist process he proposed to extend, and his own celebrated mechanism, the creative destruction by which capitalism revolutionises its structure from within, is a descendant of the Marxian dynamics of accumulation with the destination amputated and the engine retained. The business cycle literature that grew outside the tradition worked problems the tradition had posed. The economics of industrial organisation inherited its questions about concentration from the debates this programme started. Keynes's circle absorbed, through intermediaries and antagonists alike, the underconsumptionist and monetary threads that ran through the Marxian literature on crisis. Even the totalitarianism framework, graded earlier for its own accommodations, was built partly from the tradition's internal critics, its Djilas and its dissidents, whose categories the opposition adopted because the tradition's defectors understood the regimes as no external observer could. A programme's influence on its enemies is not a criterion of its truth, and no such entry is made for it here. It is entered as the final calibration of that row of the table devoted to explanatory power: frameworks are borrowed from when they see something real, and the scale and duration of the borrowing, across a century of hostile disciplines, is evidence about the founding analysis that no verdict on the prophecies can confiscate. The tradition's tragedy, stated for the last time in its baldest form, is that what its rivals borrowed was its science, and what its custodians protected was its faith.

## The demography of belief: exits, conversions, and what they measure

Kuhn's model of crisis predicts a demography: as a paradigm's articulation fails, its ablest younger members defect first, its institutions age, and its recruitment narrows to those for whom the framework is an identity rather than an instrument. The tradition's twentieth century exhibits the predicted demography with unusual completeness, and the exits constitute a data series the assessment may legitimately read. The pattern of departure is itself graded evidence. Bernstein left by audit, publishing the record and inviting the movement to revise, and was answered by anathema. The generation of 1956 left by trauma, after the secret speech and the Hungarian rising supplied anomalies that could not be accommodated by men and women whose allegiance had been moral before it was theoretical, and the parties of the West haemorrhaged their intellectuals in a single season. Kołakowski left by philosophy, working through the doctrine's foundations until the eschatological core stood exposed, and was expelled for the working. The analytical school left by method, surrendering claims in print until what remained was unrecognisable to the custodians. What the exits measure is not the doctrine's truth, since men and women leave true doctrines and die for false ones. They measure the programme's capacity to retain its most evidence-sensitive members through its anomalies, which is a property of its institutions and its protective conduct rather than of its propositions. A programme in progressive health loses members to boredom and to rival excitements. This programme lost them, in wave after dated wave, to the record, and each wave's memoir testifies to the same discovery: that the losses could not be recorded inside. The demography is the faith framing's confirmation from the census rather than from the pulpit, and it belongs in the full answer to the title's question as the human form of the ledger's arithmetic.

## Who pays for revision: the economics of the protective belt

One analytic thread remains to be drawn tight, because it converts the article's institutional observations from a narrative into a mechanism. Revisions inside a research programme are not free, and their price is not distributed evenly. A revision that surrenders a claim costs the reviser the standing that claim conferred, and costs the institution the authority it had lent against the claim's security; a revision that accommodates costs nothing in the present, its price being charged to the programme's future credibility, an account no living member is responsible for balancing. Every institution therefore faces a standing incentive to accommodate and a standing disincentive to amputate, and the difference between programmes is the difference between the counterweights their environments supply. The competitive sciences supply a brutal counterweight: a rival programme stands ready to publish your anomaly as its novel fact, and the cost of protection is exacted promptly by competitors who profit from your embarrassment. The Marxist programme's environments progressively removed the counterweight. In the parties, the rival was the class enemy, whose criticisms were discounted in advance by the doctrine of ideology. In the states, the rival was suppressed. In the universities, the rival programmes in neighbouring offices had their own belts to protect and no jurisdiction over the tradition's internal grading, which proceeded by the standards of interpretation rather than of the ledger. At each stage the price of revision rose and the price of protection fell, and the conduct this article has documented follows from the prices with an almost economic necessity, which is a conclusion the tradition's own method, explaining ideas by the material interests of their carriers, should find congenial. The programme was not refuted by its enemies. It was priced out of honesty by its institutions, and the explanation, being materialist, is the most Marxian sentence in this article.

## The strongest exhibit reconsidered: the peasant modification

Intellectual honesty requires returning, before the assessment closes, to the one modification in the programme's history that presses hardest against the verdict, harder than the weakest-link construction with which it is entangled. The orthodox theory assigned revolutionary agency to the industrial proletariat and expected the peasantry to dissolve or to follow. The Chinese application of the doctrine, after the urban insurrections of the nineteen twenties were drowned in blood, rebuilt the strategy on the opposite assignment: the peasantry as the revolution's main force, the countryside encircling the cities, a protracted war from rural base areas in which a party of a new type would construct, in the liberated zones, the embryo of the successor order. The modification was made in direct response to anomaly, the catastrophic failure of the orthodox strategy in the country concerned. It was staked in advance, on the record of dated texts, against the ridicule of the orthodox custodians, who denounced it as a peasant deviation from Marxism. And its central expectation was confirmed in the event: the strategy took power in the largest country on earth, on roughly the schedule and by roughly the road its author had mapped.

The ledger must enter this, and the entry is the largest single credit on the modification side of the account. Yet the grading, conducted with the criterion's precision, shows why the exhibit does not reverse the verdict. The novel fact it predicted was the success of a strategy, a fact about the modification's own political engineering, rather than a fact about the world the theory described: it confirmed that a peasant war could be organised to victory, a discovery in the art of revolution, and it left unconfirmed, indeed it quietly interred, the theory's claims about which class history had appointed and why. The modification purchased its triumph by surrendering the hard core's sociology, the proletariat as universal agent, while retaining the destination and the party form, and its author's own theoretical writings performed the surrender as a development, the sinification of the doctrine, in the classical accommodative grammar. Lakatos's framework has a category for such episodes: a modification may be empirically progressive in one dimension while the programme's theoretical core contracts, and the historian's task is to record both movements without allowing either to swallow the other. Recorded so, the peasant modification stands as the proof that the programme's carriers were capable, under sufficient pressure and at sufficient distance from the custodial centre, of the conduct the criterion honours: a stake laid in advance, a risk accepted, an outcome that could have destroyed the staker. That the episode's lesson was then canonised rather than generalised, its method converted into a new orthodoxy whose own custodians protected it in their turn, is the afterlife's familiar pattern, and it is why one magnificent exhibit, honestly entered, moves the grade from poor toward mixed without overturning the distribution the century compiled.

## Monopoly capital: the stagnation modification

A second modification of genuine substance deserves its entry, because it shows the programme's American school attempting exactly the operation the criterion rewards. Paul Baran and Paul Sweezy, confronting the anomaly of the long postwar boom in the most advanced capitalist economy, rebuilt the theory's crisis account in *Monopoly Capital*: the system's problem was no longer the falling rate of profit in its classical form but the rising surplus and its absorption, since monopoly pricing generated a growing economic surplus that investment outlets could not automatically employ, so that stagnation was the system's normal state and its actual motion depended on counteracting expenditures, military spending, the sales effort, and the financial superstructure. The modification had excess content of a recognisable kind. It directed attention to magnitudes its rivals treated as peripheral, the share of resources absorbed by advertising and distribution, the weight of military expenditure in sustaining demand, the hypertrophy of finance, and it implied a definite expectation, that the system's dynamism was borrowed from these absorbers and would falter as they faltered. Parts of the expectation aged well; the subsequent literature on financialisation, from many directions, rediscovered the terrain the modification had mapped, and the stagnation question returned to mainstream economics in later decades in forms its new authors did not always trace to this source. Parts aged badly; the boom the modification was built to explain outlasted its explanations, and the modification's own custodians extended its categories to cover the continuation with the same elasticity the ledger has recorded elsewhere. The balanced entry is of a problemshift theoretically progressive in its construction and unevenly corroborated in its career, the strongest such entry the programme's economics can show after its founding generation. Its existence, like the historians' exception and the peasant modification, is a component of the word mixed in the verdict, and its isolation, one such construction across the postwar decades of the world's largest Marxist literature, is what justifies the word poor.

## The dependency ledger: a subprogramme graded

The dependency school, the programme's most influential construction in the postwar study of development, offers a complete subprogramme for grading, with a founding stake, an anomaly, and a modification whose conduct can be followed in a single generation. The founding claim, in the work of André Gunder Frank and the writers of the Latin American school, held that development and underdevelopment are two faces of one process, that the metropolis develops by underdeveloping its satellites, and that the periphery's integration into the world market was therefore the mechanism of its poverty rather than the road out of it. The claim had genuine content and genuine novelty: it inverted the modernisation school's expectation, it organised the economic history of whole continents into an intelligible pattern, and it staked the periphery's future on continued immiseration under integration. The anomaly arrived from the quarter no one had assigned it: parts of the periphery, above all in East Asia, industrialised at speed inside the world market, raising incomes and transforming their class structures within a generation, while the Latin American heartlands of the theory stagnated in patterns the theory had not distinguished in advance. The school's responses divide in the ledger's familiar proportions. Its ablest figure, Fernando Henrique Cardoso, had already rebuilt the claim from inside, replacing the stagnationist core with the subtler doctrine of associated dependent development, in which dependency and industrialisation combine, a modification that surrendered the founding prediction and bought a framework that organised the Brazilian reality its predecessor could not. The custodial response, in the school's more doctrinaire reaches, absorbed the East Asian cases as exceptions purchased by geopolitics, an auxiliary whose truth in part does not alter its function in the sequence. The subprogramme's grade is thus the programme's grade in miniature, and its inclusion here serves the assessment's recurring purpose: to show that the pattern under examination is not a judgement on a few famous texts but a distribution of conduct that reproduces at every scale of the tradition, from the world system to the seminar, wherever absorbing a disappointment cost less than recording it.

## The concentration tendency in a new domain: the digital test

A founding credit can appreciate as well as decay, and the ledger should record the appreciation the concentration analysis has earned in a domain its author never saw. The economy of digital platforms, built on networks whose value grows with their users, has concentrated at a speed and to a degree that the industrial age's trust movements, the original corroboration, did not match: in search, in social communication, in electronic commerce, in the infrastructure of computation itself, a handful of firms occupy positions that the economics of the marginalist mainstream, with its equilibrium of many small competitors, did not lead it to expect, and that the Marxian mechanism, competition as the war in which the larger capitals devour the smaller and the socialisation of production outruns its private appropriation, describes as its home terrain. The entry must be made with the criterion's discipline. The corroboration belongs to the founding analysis, not to any modification, since the tradition's contemporary writers applied the concentration concept to the platforms after their dominance was visible, which is extrapolation's easier cousin, application. What the episode confirms is the durability of the founding mechanism's reach: a tendency specified from the logic of accumulation in the eighteen sixties continues to organise the most novel sector of the twenty-first century's economy, a fact about the analysis which no verdict upon the prophecies can reach. The ledger's asymmetry stands once more in its clearest form. Where the founder analysed a mechanism, his work keeps passing tests set by centuries he did not live to see. Where his custodians prophesied a destination, the centuries keep returning the same grade.

## The metabolic rift: a recent modification examined

The most discussed modification of the programme's recent decades, its ecological reconstruction, provides a final and usefully contemporary grading exercise, because its materials are fresh and its partisans claim for it exactly the honour the criterion adjudicates. The reconstruction, associated above all with John Bellamy Foster's recovery of Marx's concept of metabolic rift, holds that the founding analysis contained an ecological critique of the first rank: capital's organisation of agriculture and industry ruptures the metabolic relation between humanity and the earth, shipping nutrients from soil to city and returning waste where fertility was removed, so that the system's ecological destructiveness is a theorem of its dynamics rather than an accident of its greed. The textual recovery is genuine scholarship; the passages exist, they were neglected, and their restoration has enriched the reading of the founder. As a problemshift, the grading divides. The reconstruction's yield has been overwhelmingly interpretive: it has reorganised the reading of the canon and generated a literature applying the rift concept to successive ecological crises, each application an accommodation of a known emergency in the recovered vocabulary. Its anticipations run backward, the founder shown to have foreseen what the present suffers, which is retrospective prophecy in its scholarly form. Against this, the reconstruction has issued in at least the beginnings of prospective work, research programmes in ecological economics that use the rift as a measurable relation rather than as a metaphor, and if that work matures into staked and confirmed findings about material flows, the ledger will record a progressive episode in the programme's late period, which the criterion, being a historian's instrument, is built to welcome whenever it occurs. For the present the entry stands as the pattern's mildest modern instance: a real discovery about the founder, celebrated as a discovery about the world, in a tradition whose oldest habit is to confuse the two.

## The specialists' standing: how the literature treats the case

A reader may finally wish to know whether the verdict delivered here is an eccentricity or a position within a recognisable scholarly distribution, and the question can be answered without appeal to authority, as a matter of the record. In the philosophy of science's own literature, the case has served for two generations as a standard exhibit, the example through which textbooks introduce the difference between a theory and a programme and the grading of problemshifts, and the standard treatment, following Lakatos, records the programme as the type case of degeneration arrested by fertility. In the methodological literature of economics, the case is cited by the discipline's own auditors as the warning against which their programmes are measured, which is why Blaug's audit carries its sting. The dissenting literature is real and should be characterised fairly: it consists of defences of particular components, reconstructions of particular claims, and arguments, several of them weighed in this article, that the criterion itself is unjust to historical and social inquiry. What the dissenting literature has not produced, in the half century since the criterion was published, is the exhibit the criterion requests, a modification's novel fact produced and defended under scrutiny, and the absence has a sociology of its own that this article has already examined: the tradition's ablest defenders have preferred to contest the court, to re-read the founder, or to rebuild components, rather than to fight on the ledger, because the ledger is where the case is weakest. The verdict of this article therefore stands within the scholarly distribution at the point the evidence assigns it, severe on the programme's conduct, generous to its components, and open, on stated terms, to reversal. It is not the last word, because the criterion forbids last words about programmes still capable of surprising their historians. It is the current state of a ledger that anyone may inspect and that its subject's heirs are invited, in the only way that would settle the matter, to amend.

## The laboratory that was disowned: the Soviet case as evidence

The programme's conduct toward the Soviet experiment is the single most instructive episode in the entire ledger for a student of method, because it exhibits, in one case and across one lifetime, the full cycle of confirmation claimed and confirmation disowned. For decades the tradition's mainstream cited the Soviet Union as the doctrine's experimental proof: here was the theory enacted, its planning vindicated against the market's anarchy, its social achievements exhibited as the destination's first instalment, and sceptics were answered not with arguments but with the existence of the place, an argument from incarnation that no seminar could match. When the experiment's own record darkened beyond the reach of the auxiliaries, and finally when the state dissolved, the mainstream's position migrated, by stages the faith sections would recognise, to the claim that the experiment had never been an instance of the doctrine at all, that its deformities disqualified it as evidence, and that the theory stood untested by a case it had merely resembled. Each position, held at its date, was arguable. The sequence is the offence. A case cannot be a programme's laboratory while it succeeds and a foreign country when it fails; evidence law in every serious discipline forbids the retrospective disqualification of the experiment one has oneself designed and celebrated. The correct conduct was available throughout and was practised by the tradition's honourable minorities, the Trotskyists who graded the experiment against the doctrine and recorded a degeneration, the state-capitalists who recorded a refutation of the identification, the dissidents who recorded the facts at the price of their liberty. The mainstream's refusal of every such grading, in favour first of incarnation and then of disownership, is the protective pattern at the scale of a civilisation, and it is the episode a student should master if only one is to be mastered, because every element of the case, the stake, the anomaly, the auxiliary, the postponement, and the final rewriting of the ledger's past, appears in it in letters too large to misread.

## The labour share: the relative reading's late evidence

The relative reading of immiseration, graded earlier as an accommodation in its manner of arrival, has acquired in recent decades a body of evidence that fairness requires the ledger to notice, because the phenomenon it names has moved from the tradition's pamphlets into the mainstream's own measurements. Economists with no allegiance to the programme, working in its central institutions, have documented a long decline in labour's share of income across many countries and industries, a distributional drift in the direction the relative thesis describes, driven by mechanisms, technology, globalisation, and the weakening of labour's bargaining institutions, that the literature continues to contest. No figures are stated here, in keeping with this cluster's protocol; the direction and its documentation in the mainstream literature are the entry. The grading of the entry is the instructive part. The phenomenon confirms that the distributional question the relative thesis asked was a real question, and that the tradition's instinct, to watch the shares and not only the averages, was methodologically fruitful when the mainstream was watching neither. It does not convert the relative reading into a novel prediction retroactively, because the reading as restated specified neither the period, nor the mechanism, nor the signature by which its confirmation could be distinguished from its refutation, and a claim compatible with every movement of the shares cannot be confirmed by one of them. The episode is the criterion's distinction in its purest contemporary form: the difference between having asked the right question, for which the tradition deserves lasting credit, and having staked the right answer, which is the honour the ledger reserves and cannot award. Programmes are remembered for their questions more often than for their answers, and there are worse fates; but the title of science is paid for in answers, and the tradition's habit of spending its questions' credit as though it were answers' credit is the entire case compressed into one transaction.

## The Erfurt settlement: the claim enters the programme of a party

The doctrine's scientific self-understanding was not a private opinion of its founders; it was codified, at the tradition's first great organisational settlement, into the programme of the largest socialist party in the world, and the codification explains how the claim acquired its institutional weight. The Erfurt Programme of the German Social Democrats, adopted in the eighteen nineties with a theoretical part drafted under Engels's eye and a practical part of immediate demands, opened with the doctrine's destination claims stated as the necessary outcome of economic development: the means of production's concentration, the proletariat's growth, the system's crises, presented not as hopes but as the tendencies of a process the party merely recognised and would administer into its conclusion. Kautsky's commentary on the programme, the era's authoritative exposition, taught a generation of organisers that the party's confidence rested on knowledge, that history's direction was a finding and not a wager. The settlement's consequence for method was profound and mostly malign. A claim written into a party's programme ceases to be a hypothesis and becomes a loyalty, because the organisation's cohesion, recruitment, and morale are mortgaged to it; the German party's theorists spent the following decades defending propositions whose defence was a condition of their offices, and Bernstein's audit detonated as it did precisely because it was delivered against a programme rather than against a paper. Every subsequent carrier repeated the settlement in its own form, the claim migrating from programme to textbook to constitution, and at each migration the price of revision rose. The assessment of the programme's conduct is incomplete without this organisational fact, because it identifies the exact joint where a research programme becomes a creed: not when its claims are believed, but when they are enacted, when surrendering them would dissolve the body that carries them. Sciences have programmes too, in the other sense, and surrender theirs routinely, because no laboratory's charter names a destination of history. The tradition's misfortune was to be carried by bodies whose charters did.

## What remains standing: an inventory after the audit

An assessment that ends in demolition alone has usually graded carelessly, and this one closes its ledger with an inventory of what the audit leaves standing, since the standing items are the tradition's bequest to readers who owe it no allegiance. There stands, first, a set of mechanisms: accumulation's drive to concentrate, the cycle's generation of a reserve of labour, the commodity form's power to disguise social relations as relations between things, each specified by its founder with a precision that later sciences have repeatedly found usable. There stands, second, a set of questions that the surrounding disciplines did not know they needed until this programme forced them: who owns, who labours, whose interest a common sense serves, what a crisis is for, why a society's account of itself diverges from its structure. There stands, third, a historiography: the demonstration, in works that survive their framework, that the past looks different from below and that the difference is evidence. There stands, fourth, a permanent methodological exhibit: the most fully documented case in social thought's history of how a programme degenerates, instructive to every successor programme precisely because the talent wasted in the degeneration was so great. And there stands, finally, a warning with an address on it, the founders' own practice of staking, revising, and surrendering, preserved in their texts like a conscience the tradition could neither obey nor destroy. What does not stand is the guarantee: the destination, the appointed agent, the science of history's end. The inventory is not a consolation assembled for politeness. It is the accurate residue of a hundred and fifty years of the most ambitious social theory ever attempted, and a reader who masters the standing items possesses instruments that much of the modern social sciences quietly runs on, while a reader who demands the guarantee as the price of the instruments has understood neither the audit nor the age that made it necessary.

## The question turned inward: the critics' own predictions graded

Symmetry has one more debt to pay, and paying it is the assessment's final proof of good faith. The critics of Marxism also organised programmes, and their flagship claims were also staked against the century. The calculation argument, in the strong form in which Ludwig von Mises first delivered it, held that rational economic allocation under common ownership of the means of production was impossible in principle, since without market prices for capital goods no calculus exists by which alternatives can be compared. The Soviet economy then existed for seven decades, allocating badly, wastefully, and at monstrous human cost, but allocating, industrialising a continent and sustaining a superpower's arsenal, which is not what impossibility in principle looks like. The strong claim's carriers responded as carriers do: the impossibility was redescribed as an impossibility of efficiency, then of optimality, then of matching a counterfactual market's performance, each restatement truer and weaker than the last, until the surviving claim, that planning allocates worse than markets in the general case, was one its original author would have found a disappointing residue of his theorem. The episode is entered here without triumph, because the weaker surviving claim is substantially correct and the Soviet record is its evidence. It is entered because the pattern is the pattern: a stake, an anomaly, a sequence of accommodations by which a proud impossibility was preserved as a defensible tendency. The critics of this programme were also a programme, and their ledger, graded by the same criterion, shows the same diseases in milder form, milder because their institutions never held states and their claims never had to legitimate a rule. The conclusion the inward turn forces is the one the whole article has approached: degeneration is the default disease of doctrines that organise allegiance, and the Marxist case is distinguished not by the disease's presence but by its scale, its duration, and the greatness of what it consumed.

## The emigrants: the programme's content under other flags

The last entry in the ledger belongs to a class of evidence that is easy to overlook because its members changed their colours. Across the twentieth century, scholars formed inside the tradition carried its instruments into the mainstream disciplines and there produced work that the home programme, in its custodial form, could neither have commissioned nor survived unchanged. Economic historians trained in the Marxist school rebuilt the study of industrialisation around class and property relations and then followed their evidence into conclusions the orthodoxy had not budgeted for. Development economists formed in the dependency debates staffed the institutions of the postcolonial world and revised the doctrine against the record of their own ministries. Sociologists who began with the framework's totalising ambitions ended by building the middle-range theories, of fields, of networks, of organisations, whose cumulative findings are the discipline's actual capital, and more than one of them recorded that the journey began when a Marxist question survived the collapse of the Marxist answer. The emigration is the programme's strangest legacy and its most hopeful datum. A doctrine's content, it turns out, can be progressive in exile while degenerating at home, because the emigrants worked under institutions that priced revision honestly, rewarded the recorded loss, and asked of a concept only whether it found anything. The concepts, tested in that air, often did. Nothing in the phenomenon rehabilitates the custodial programme, whose conduct is the subject of this article's verdict; but it completes the explanation of the tradition's uncanny persistence in the intellectual world, which is not only the persistence of a faith, as the eschatology sections described, but the persistence of a toolkit, detached piece by piece from the doctrine that forged it and proved, piece by piece, in workshops the doctrine never controlled. The founders promised that their theory would be a guide to action. Its verified afterlife is as a guide to inquiry, in the hands of scholars who surrendered the destination and kept the instruments, which is perhaps the most scientific fate available to a nineteenth-century system in a world that declined to end on schedule.

## The sequence and its suppressed anomaly: the Asiatic mode affair

The theory of history at the programme's core contained, in its founder's own writings, an anomaly that its custodians handled by the method this article has taught its readers to recognise at sight, and the episode deserves a section because the suppression is documented to the year. Marx's scheme of successive modes of production, ancient, feudal, and bourgeois in the famous preface's list, sat alongside his references to an Asiatic mode, a formation of self-sustaining villages beneath a centralised state that appropriated the surplus, which fitted no place in the sequence and implied that history's road was not single. The category was an embarrassment in direct proportion to its interest: it suggested that the succession of modes was a Western European sketch rather than a universal timetable, a suggestion the founder's late Russian correspondence, exhibited earlier in this article, shows him entertaining in his own case. In the nineteen twenties the category was debated in earnest by Soviet historians and orientalists, because it bore on the classification of China, of India, and, dangerously, of any state that had absorbed society's surplus through a central apparatus. The debate was closed by authority at the start of the nineteen thirties, the Asiatic mode was struck from the permissible scheme, and the founder's scattered remarks were retired from the operative canon, after which the five-stage sequence was codified in the official short course as the doctrine of history itself. The episode's methodological anatomy is complete in miniature. An internal anomaly, raised by the tradition's own scholars from the founder's own texts, threatened a classification with political implications; the institution resolved the anomaly not by revising the scheme but by forbidding the category, and the scheme's subsequent career, taught as science to generations, rested on a censorship that its students were not permitted to know about. No entry in this ledger shows more clearly the difference between the founder's practice and the custodians', since the anomaly they suppressed was one he had created and left open, as investigators leave doors open behind them. The sequence component of the portfolio was graded, in the components section, as standing in the middle distance between the analysis and the prophecies. The Asiatic affair is the record of how it was kept there: not by the evidence of the archives, which its own historians were ready to supply, but by the requirements of the state that had made the sequence its charter.

## What revival would require: a note to the heirs

The criterion's grades are revisable, and the article owes its readers, in closing, a plain statement of what a revision in the favourable direction would concretely require of the tradition's remaining heirs, since an invitation without instructions is only a taunt. It would require, first, a published ledger: a canonical accounting, maintained by the tradition's own scholars, of what its successive versions staked and what the world returned, with the losses printed in the same type as the gains. It would require, second, dated stakes: new modifications issued with their signatures and horizons stated in advance, in venues where rivals can witness the staking, so that the next generation's grading is an act of reading rather than of archaeology. It would require, third, honours for the auditors: a tradition that wishes to be believed as a science must make its Bernsteins and its Elsters into its ornaments, cited at its conferences and taught in its schools, since an institution's treatment of its internal critics is the one statistic about it that cannot be counterfeited. And it would require, fourth, the surrender of the guarantee: the destination claims retired from the category of findings to the category of hopes, where they may be honoured as hopes without corrupting the findings that remain. None of this is impossible, and fragments of all four exist somewhere in the tradition's dispersed present. What has never existed is their combination in a carrier with the standing to make them the tradition's official conduct. Until it exists, the verdict of this article stands as the ledger's current balance, and the heirs are reminded, in the founder's own spirit, that he asked for no better treatment than to be graded by a future he trusted to be more rigorous than his followers. The grading is the compliment. The protection was the insult.

## The reader's part: how to hold a graded verdict

A word, finally, on the use a general reader should make of a graded verdict, since the article has refused the portable simplicities both camps sell. The refusal is not a demand for perpetual suspension of judgement. It is a demand that judgement be held in the form the evidence takes: by components, with the ledger attached, and with the defeater in view. Practically, this means that the next time the reader meets the claim that Marxism has been refuted, the useful response is a question about which component, on which criterion, with what concessions to the prosecutor's own text; and the next time the reader meets a crisis, a bestseller, or a movement's revival offered as the doctrine's entire vindication, the useful response is a request for the dated stake. These two questions, asked in both directions with equal courtesy, are the whole method of this article reduced to a habit, and a reader who acquires the habit has acquired the thing the title's question was always really asking for: not a verdict to repeat, but a way of telling, in any doctrine's career and not only this one's, the difference between a programme that is learning and a faith that is lasting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Is Marxism a science?

The answer is a profile rather than a word. The founding theory was framed as science and earned genuine confirmations, above all in its analysis of concentration and of the employment cycle. The programme that carried it, graded on the strongest available criterion, whether its modifications predicted novel facts, records a history dominated by accommodation, in which failed predictions were reinterpreted rather than surrendered. Its explanatory power remains formidable and its prophecies of destination failed and were protected. A single label would have to average those findings into a fiction, which is why this article grades the components and publishes the ledger instead of awarding or withholding the badge.

### Q: Did Popper say Marxism was never scientific?

He said nearly the opposite, and the precision matters because both camps profit from the error. In Popper's account the theory began as a genuine science: it made predictions definite enough to be tested, and the tests went against it. His charge was aimed at what followed, the joint reworking of doctrine and data by which the theory's followers rescued it from refutation and thereby destroyed the scientific character it had been born with. The indictment is therefore about conduct after failure, not about the theory's birth. A defence that proves the original claims were testable answers a charge nobody brought, and an attack that calls the doctrine unfalsifiable from the start misquotes its own prosecutor.

### Q: Which Marxist predictions failed?

Three great forecasts dominate the ledger. Revolution was expected in the most advanced industrial countries and arrived in Europe's least developed great power and then in agrarian societies, inverting the projected sequence. The prophesied increase of working-class misery, in the absolute form in which the movement preached it, ran against the long record of the industrial countries, whatever the disputes between absolute and relative readings. And society was to polarise into two great classes while the intermediate strata, managers, professionals, technicians, and administrators, expanded instead. A fourth stake completes the record: the state in the successor order was to wither away, and in every state built under the doctrine's banner it grew. Each failure was met by reinterpretation, which is the fact the assessment grades.

### Q: What is a degenerating research programme?

In Lakatos's framework, a research programme is a family of theories held together by an inviolable core and revised through a protective belt of auxiliary assumptions. A programme degenerates when its revisions do no more than absorb the already known: each modification is built after an anomaly arrives, explains that anomaly in the programme's vocabulary, and predicts nothing new that the world then confirms. The contrast is a progressive programme, whose modifications have excess content, staking facts not used in their construction and improbable on rival accounts, some of which are corroborated. Degeneration is thus a verdict on a sequence's conduct over time, not on any single theory's truth, and a degenerating programme may still contain true and fertile components, as this one does.

### Q: Is falsification the right test?

No, and the concession is the beginning of the assessment rather than its end. Falsification in its simple form describes no durable science, because theories meet evidence only through auxiliary assumptions, and a failed prediction cannot by logic alone identify which member of the conjunction is guilty; Newtonian astronomy absorbed its anomalies for centuries by the same repairs the simple test would condemn. Most philosophers of science have therefore retired the test as a general criterion. But retiring it does not acquit any particular programme, because the stronger criterion that replaced it, the progress or degeneration of a programme's modifications, grades the conduct falsification gestured at with far greater precision, and on that criterion the verdict in this case is mixed to poor.

### Q: What is the secular eschatology argument?

It is the claim that Marxism reproduces, in secular vocabulary, the architecture of a salvation faith: a directed history, an original unity lost through a fall into property and alienation, a chosen collective agent whose suffering carries universal meaning, a final crisis that sits in judgement on the old order, and a reconciled condition beyond conflict in which history's wounds are healed. Karl Löwith gave the argument scholarly form, arguing that the modern philosophy of history is biblical eschatology translated into economics. The framing illuminates the tradition's rhetoric, its emotional architecture, and the loyalty it commands through disappointment. It establishes nothing about the truth of the doctrine's propositions, which must be graded on evidence, and using it as a refutation commits the genetic fallacy.

### Q: Does the criterion apply to other social sciences?

Yes, and this article has applied it to them in the open, because a criterion aimed at a single defendant is an instrument of prosecution, not of judgement. The results are uncomfortable across the field. Stretches of mainstream economics grade as degenerating under the same ledger, as Mark Blaug's audit concluded from inside the discipline; psychoanalysis stands near the field's floor; modernisation theory dissolved through the same protective sequence traced here. But the field also contains progressive episodes, in macroeconomic modifications that staked and won, in behavioural research programmes that predicted anomalies on demand, which shows the bar can be cleared in social science. Marxism's comparative position is neither the field's disgrace nor its honour roll: poorer than its strongest performers, richer than its weakest, over the longest run any of them has had.

### Q: Has any modification predicted a novel fact?

That is the precise question on which the verdict turns, and the honest answer is that the candidates are few and contested. The strongest is the weakest-link construction of the war years, which asserted in advance that revolution would snap the world system's chain at its backward link rather than its advanced centre, and which Russia's revolution then appeared to confirm; yet the construction was built from the anomaly it answered, and specialists divide over whether the confirmation was a novelty or merely the anomaly wearing a future tense. The founding analyses of concentration and of the cycle predicted genuinely novel facts, but they are foundations rather than modifications. The invitation stands open: one modification, made under anomaly's pressure, that staked a novel fact and saw it confirmed, would reopen the ledger.

### Q: Is Marxism a religion?

Not in the literal sense: it has no deity, no worship, and no doctrine of the supernatural, and its founder's materialism is a rejection of the religious worldview in terms. The serious version of the claim is structural rather than literal. In its eschatological architecture, its canon and commentary, its orthodoxies and heresies, and above all in the fidelity it demanded and received through a century of disappointed expectation, the tradition functioned for many adherents as a faith functions, supplying meaning, consolation, and a guaranteed destination. Functioning as a faith is a finding about the tradition's sociology and about the allegiance it organised. It is not a finding about the truth of its analyses, several of which survive the grading that its prophecies failed.

### Q: What would count as scientific success here?

A sequence of modifications that bought knowledge the programme did not already have. Concretely: revisions of the theory, made under the pressure of failed expectations, that staked in advance some pattern the rivals denied, in the movement of classes, the geography of crises, the trajectory of distribution, and were then borne out by testimony the tradition did not itself keep; failures recorded in the tradition's own textbooks as losses that instructed it; internal critics rewarded rather than anathematised. A programme may progress for a time and still end wrong, earning the standing of science by its conduct on the way. Success was never defined as the destination arriving. It was defined as the programme learning from the world faster than it learned to protect itself, and by that standard the materials for success existed and were, at the decisive joints, declined.

### Q: How does the right criterion grade the defence as well as the charge?

Symmetrically, which is its virtue. The same ledger that records this programme's accommodations records the accommodations of its rivals: the repairs of equilibrium economics against its own impossibility results, the protective sequence by which the totalitarianism framework absorbed the collapse it had pronounced impossible, the dissolutions of modernisation theory. The defence is graded by it too, in the strict sense that the defence's own exhibits are admitted and weighed: the world market passages, the weakest-link expectation, the founding credits. A criterion that grades everyone cannot be dismissed as an instrument built for one conviction, and its judgements against the defence's client carry the more weight because its judgements against the prosecution's frameworks are printed in the same article.

### Q: What should a student separate between rejecting a test and passing a better one?

Everything, because the commonest losing move in examinations on this question is to treat the two as one. Rejecting falsification is a preliminary: it shows that the crude court lacks jurisdiction, and it is worth marks when it is done with the Duhem problem and the history of anomaly management in real sciences. Passing a better test is the trial itself: it requires the candidate to apply the programme criterion to named modifications, with chronology and construction examined, and to report the result even where it wounds the side the candidate favours. A script that performs only the preliminary has refuted a criterion and assessed nothing. Markers are trained to notice the substitution, and the distinction, stated explicitly in an answer's opening, signals that the candidate will not commit it.

### Q: Why is falsification the wrong test for a social science programme?

For reasons that apply to every science and bite hardest in the social ones. A prediction issues from a theory joined to auxiliaries about conditions and measurement, so failure cannot identify the culprit; social systems are open, so the conditions cannot be held constant; and the agents studied read the predictions and alter their conduct, so the target moves under the instrument. A test that would convict Newtonian astronomy for absorbing the anomaly of Uranus, and every living social programme alongside it, is not a test but a slogan. The remedy is not to abandon grading but to grade the sequence: whether a programme's successive revisions bought confirmed novel content or only restated its losses as its depths, which is a question social programmes can be made to answer.

### Q: How does the criterion table order the frameworks?

The table's rows ascend from the crudest test toward the most discriminating, and its columns force the comparison the debate avoids. Falsification comes first because it is the test in popular circulation and the first to be retired. Explanatory adequacy follows, the row on which this programme scores highest and where several rival frameworks also do well, which is why it cannot settle the question alone. The predictive record states the raw balance of stakes and outcomes. The programme criterion closes the table because it subsumes the others' insights into a judgement on conduct over time: not whether a claim was framed boldly, nor whether the framework illuminates, but whether its career of revision learned from the world or learned to evade it. Read vertically, the columns show each framework's profile across all four tests, and the profiles, not any single cell, are the findings.

### Q: What separates accommodating an anomaly from predicting a novel fact?

Chronology and construction. An accommodation is built after the anomaly is known, from the anomaly's own materials, and its success consists in redescribing the troubling fact in the programme's vocabulary so that the doctrine emerges unscathed; the relative reading of immiseration, produced once the absolute record had turned, is the type case. A novel prediction is staked before its fact is known, or at the least formed no part of the materials from which the claim that entails it was built, and it risks the programme on territory the rivals expect to be otherwise; the concentration tendency, asserted when small production still dominated the landscape, is the type case. The same sentence can migrate between the categories as its history is examined, which is why the grading of a programme is archival work and not impression, and why the tradition's retrospective discoveries of its own foresight must always be met with a request for the dated page.
