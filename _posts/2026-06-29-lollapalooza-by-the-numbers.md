## The ranged-scale rule, and why it beats invented precision

The organizing idea of this page has a name, and the name is the ranged-scale rule. It states that Lollapalooza is best quantified in stable ranges rather than fixed points, because the exact figures shift every edition while the ranges hold, so a durable range tells the truth where a precise figure only pretends to. A handful of stages, not exactly eight forever. A large multi-act lineup, not precisely one hundred and seventy every time. A major downtown park footprint measured in the hundreds of acres, not a surveyed figure that changes with each site plan. A total attendance in the hundreds of thousands across the four days, not a turnstile count nobody outside the production office can confirm. The rule is simple to state and surprisingly hard to follow, because the pull toward false exactness is strong, and most pages give in to it.

Why does the rule matter so much for a festival specifically? Because a festival is a moving target by design. The stage plan gets redrawn to fit a new lineup. The act count rises or falls with how the bill is built that year. The footprint flexes as the production team reworks the site for weather, for construction in the park, for a new activation or a relocated entrance. Attendance is capped and sold, but the total that actually walks through the gates across four days depends on weather, on single-day versus four-day mix, on no-shows and re-entries. A number that was exactly right for one edition is quietly wrong for the next, and a page that published it as timeless truth is now a source of misinformation it will never correct. A range dodges that fate. It was true last edition, it is true this edition, and it will almost certainly be true next edition, which is precisely what a durable reference is supposed to guarantee.

There is a second reason the rule matters, and it goes to the whole point of this series. The wager the series makes is that accuracy over precision-theater wins the trust that precision-theater only borrows.
