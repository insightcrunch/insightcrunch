Every homebuyer with a delayed flat, a defective flat or a builder who has stopped answering the telephone faces the RERA vs consumer protection choice before facing the builder. The grievance is usually simple to state: possession promised and not delivered, money taken against a project that stands half built, a flat handed over with defects the brochure never mentioned, or an escalation demand with no basis in the agreement. The hard part is not stating the grievance. The hard part is that Indian law offers the same buyer two different doors for the same complaint, the Authority created by the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 and the consumer commissions created by the consumer protection statute, and the two doors lead to different rooms. What can be ordered differs. How the order is collected differs. What the builder must deposit before he can even appeal differs. A buyer who chooses on the basis of which queue looks shorter is choosing on the one dimension that matters least, because the practical question is which forum can give the relief the buyer actually wants and then make the builder pay it.

![RERA vs consumer protection for homebuyers, comparing the real estate Authority and the consumer commissions as rival forums for builder delay and defects - Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-81.webp)

The choice is real because the courts have held that it exists. The real estate statute does not close the consumer door, and the consumer statute does not yield to the specialist regulator, so an allottee may elect between them. The choice is also consequential because it is made once. A buyer cannot recover the same refund twice, cannot run the same claim through both systems to a double satisfaction, and having obtained an order in one forum cannot re-litigate the identical claim in the other. This article compares the two routes across the dimensions that decide outcomes in practice, states which forum each dimension favours, and defends a verdict. The verdict is that the forums should be chosen by remedy and enforcement, not by speed: the specialist Authority can direct a refund with interest at a prescribed rate and recover the money as arrears of land revenue, while a consumer commission can award compensation of a kind the Authority's own machinery handles only through a separate adjudication, and can back its order with a coercive process against the person of the defaulter. The right question is therefore not which forum is faster but which one can grant the relief wanted and collect it. There is also one circumstance in which the entire comparison collapses, and a buyer whose builder is sliding towards insolvency needs to understand it before choosing anything: once a corporate insolvency resolution process is admitted against the promoter, the moratorium halts proceedings under both statutes, and the buyer's remedy becomes a claim inside the insolvency process rather than a case in either forum.

## The decision the buyer actually faces

Strip the problem to its structure and the buyer faces three questions in sequence, of which the forum question is only the second. The first question is what the buyer wants at the end of the case: the flat, finished and handed over; or the money back with interest; or the money back with interest and compensation for the years of rent paid elsewhere, the interest carried on a home loan against a flat that did not exist, and the mental agony of a decade of broken promises. Different forums are built to deliver different items on that list, and a buyer who has not decided what he wants cannot choose rationally between them. The second question is which forum can deliver the wanted item against this builder, in this State, on the state of the project as it stands: registered or unregistered, nearly complete or abandoned at the plinth, solvent or circling insolvency. The third question is what happens after the order, because an order against a builder is a piece of paper until it is executed, and the two systems execute their orders by entirely different machinery, one through the district revenue administration and the other through the coercive powers of a court-like body with a penal provision behind it.

Two features of the buyer's position sharpen the decision. The first is asymmetry of staying power. The builder litigates with the buyers' own money, collected in instalments and deployed across projects, while the buyer litigates out of savings already depleted by the purchase itself. Procedural choices that look neutral on paper, the deposit a builder must make before an appeal is heard, the number of appellate tiers he can climb, the executing machinery he can obstruct, translate directly into years and into settlement pressure. The second feature is that the buyer's evidence is unusually good by the standards of civil litigation. The agreement for sale, the payment receipts, the allotment letter and the builder's own advertised possession date usually establish the core of the case without oral evidence, which is why both forums can and do proceed summarily, and why the realistic contest in most cases is not about liability but about remedy, quantum and collection.

One discipline underlies the choice and is enforced by both forums against the buyer himself. The allottee who stops paying instalments in protest at the delay, without the protection of an order or a lawful ground, puts himself in default: the real estate statute obliges the allottee to make timely payments and charges him interest at the prescribed rate for his own delays, and consumer commissions, for their part, discount or refuse relief to buyers whose grievance is entangled with their own unexplained failure to pay what the agreement required. The buyer contemplating either forum should therefore keep his own payment record clean or be ready to explain every departure from it, because the builder's first factual defence in each system is that the default began on the buyer's side of the ledger.

It follows that the comparison has to be run on dimensions that track the buyer's real exposure: what relief each forum can grant, whether the compensation a buyer most wants is inside or outside the forum's easy reach, what it costs the builder to appeal, how the order is enforced, and what clocks are running against the buyer in each system. Speed enters the comparison, because delay is the original injury in most of these cases, but speed is one dimension among eight, and it is the dimension on which public discussion of the two forums is most often anecdotal and least reliable.

## Two statutes built for different jobs

The deepest difference between the routes is not procedural at all. It is that the two statutes were built to do different jobs, and each forum's strengths in a homebuyer's case are the strengths of its design.

The real estate statute of 2016 is a regulatory statute with an adjudicatory arm. Before any dispute arises, it disciplines the market: a promoter may not advertise, market or sell a project that is required to be registered until it is registered with the State Authority; the promoter must deposit seventy per cent of the amounts realised from allottees into a separate account, to be withdrawn only for the cost of construction and the cost of the land, against certificates of engineers, architects and chartered accountants; the promoter must disclose the project layout, the approvals obtained, the timeline for completion and the quarterly progress on the Authority's website; and the promoter's freedom to alter plans, to take advances above ten per cent of the cost before a registered agreement for sale, and to transfer the project to a third party without the allottees' written consent is restricted by the Act itself. The full design of that statute, its registration, escrow and disclosure machinery and the sanctions behind it, is set out in the guide to [the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016](/2018/12/01/rera-act-2016-explained/), and it is not repeated here. What matters for the comparison is the consequence of the design: when a buyer complains to the Authority, the Authority already holds the project's file. It has the sanctioned plans, the registration declarations, the quarterly updates and the withdrawal certificates. The buyer does not have to prove the project's status to a forum that starts from ignorance, because the forum is the project's regulator and the record is its own.

The approvals a project needs before a single flat can lawfully be sold sit in yet another legal world, the world of planning permissions, commencement certificates and municipal sanctions administered under State town planning and municipal law, and the Authority's registration process is built on top of those approvals rather than in place of them; the structure of that local layer is mapped in the guide to [local government law in India](/2018/10/01/local-government-law-india-guide/). A buyer comparing forums should register what this layering means in practice. A complaint to the Authority can draw on a regulatory file that records whether the approvals existed and whether the disclosures matched them, while a complaint to a consumer commission treats approvals as evidence to be produced and proved like any other fact in the case.

The consumer statute is an adjudicatory statute and nothing else. It does not register builders, does not supervise projects, does not hold escrow accounts and does not audit disclosures. It creates a three-tier system of commissions, District, State and National, whose entire function is to hear complaints by consumers against traders and service providers and to order relief. Its unit of concern is not the project but the transaction: a person who buys goods or hires services for consideration is a consumer, and construction of a house is a service, a position the Supreme Court settled in Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta, where the Court treated housing activity by a development authority as a service within the consumer statute and the allottee of a flat as a consumer entitled to complain of deficiency in it. Parliament's definition of service was itself amended in the early nineteen nineties to name housing construction expressly. Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, which received the President's assent on 9 August 2019 and whose principal provisions, including the chapter on consumer disputes redressal commissions, were brought into force on 20 July 2020, with the provisions on the Central Consumer Protection Authority following on 24 July 2020, the same structure continues: the consumer who hires a service for consideration, expressly including construction, may complain of deficiency in that service to the commission that has jurisdiction. The 1986 statute, which the 2019 Act repealed and replaced subject to savings for pending matters, governed the entire first phase of the overlap with the real estate statute, and much of the case law on homebuyer complaints, including the decision that settled the concurrency question, was decided under it. A buyer reading older orders should therefore expect to meet the 1986 Act's vocabulary of fora and its different pecuniary test, both of which are dealt with below, because the difference decides real cases about complaints filed in that period.

One more design difference runs through everything that follows. The real estate statute is State-administered: there is an Authority for each State and Union territory, applying the central Act but with State rules prescribing matters such as the interest rate payable to allottees, and the quality, staffing and pace of Authorities vary from State to State in ways every practitioner in this field can describe. The consumer commissions are likewise State and district bodies under a central Act, with their own variations in bench strength and backlog. Neither forum is a single national institution with a uniform record, and any comparison that speaks of the two routes as if each had one consistent disposal pattern is describing a country that does not exist. The comparison in this article is therefore run on the design of the two systems and on the reported pattern of their working, and the decision rule at the end is framed to be applied to the buyer's own State and the buyer's own project.

The document at the centre of both routes deserves a sentence of its own. In each forum the case turns on the agreement for sale, and a buyer should understand what that document is in law: a contract for the sale of immovable property does not, of itself, create any interest in or charge on the property, a distinction between contract and conveyance that the [guide to the Transfer of Property Act](/2014/01/01/transfer-of-property-act-india/) develops in full. The agreement fixes the price, the possession date and the builder's promises, and both the Authority and the commissions treat the builder's failure against his own agreement as the core of the wrong; the Supreme Court's insistence, in the line of cases running through Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Limited v. Govindan Raghavan, that one-sided clauses imposed by builders on buyers who have no real choice cannot be enforced as written, was worked out in consumer proceedings on precisely such agreements.

## Before the second door: the consumer-only era and why it was not enough

For the first three decades of the modern housing market, the homebuyer had exactly one specialist forum, and it was the consumer forum. The Consumer Protection Act, 1986 had been built for defective goods and deficient services generally, and after Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta established that housing construction is a service and the allottee a consumer, the commissions became, by default, the homebuyer's court. They did the work as well as an adjudicatory body can: they ordered refunds with interest, compensation for mental agony, and in egregious cases the kind of strictures on development authorities and builders that fill the law reports of the nineteen nineties and the two thousands. The case law that still governs builder liability, on one-sided agreements, on the duty to hand over what was promised, on compensation for the years of waiting, was made there, because there was nowhere else to make it.

What the consumer route could never do was prevent the default, and by the second decade of this century the limits of a purely adjudicatory answer were plain for anyone to see. A commission could compensate the buyer of a failed project; it could not stop the next project of the same builder from being sold off a brochure, could not ring-fence the buyers' instalments against diversion to other ventures, could not compel disclosure of the approvals the project did or did not have, and could not supervise completion. The pattern of the market was that each failed project generated its own crop of complaints, each decided on its own record, while the builder's other projects repeated the cycle. The demand for a regulator grew out of that structural gap, fed by parliamentary committee scrutiny of the housing market and by the sheer scale of stalled projects in the metropolitan regions, and the real estate statute of 2016 was Parliament's answer: registration before marketing, escrow of seventy per cent of receipts, continuing disclosure, and an Authority armed with inquiry and direction powers, with adjudication attached. The consumer commissions were not displaced by that answer, because the older statute was never repealed by it and its own remedies clause said the opposite; but the centre of gravity of homebuyer law shifted, and the argument of the next four years, until Imperia Structures closed it, was about whether the shift had been a replacement or an addition.

The consumer statute was then itself rebuilt. The 2019 Act repealed the 1986 Act, in force for the redressal commissions from 20 July 2020, and the rebuilding is directly relevant to the comparison, because the consumer route a buyer faces is the 2019 version, not the forum of the older case law. The new Act kept the three-tier commissions and the complaint remedy, and changed the working details this article has already flagged: pecuniary jurisdiction measured by consideration paid, appeal deposits of fifty per cent with the old monetary caps removed, and non-compliance with orders made a more serious offence. It added machinery the 1986 Act lacked: a Central Consumer Protection Authority with power to act for consumers as a class against unfair trade practices and misleading advertisements, a mediation chapter under which commissions may refer a complaint to mediation at the admission stage or later, electronic filing, and the complainant's right to file where he resides or personally works for gain rather than only where the builder carries on business. A buyer comparing forums is therefore not choosing between a Victorian remedy and a modern regulator. He is choosing between two modern statutes, one rebuilt around regulation in 2016 and one rebuilt around adjudication in 2019, each drafted with the other in view, and each expressly additional to the other. That is the setting in which the concurrency question arose and was answered.

## RERA vs consumer protection: why both doors stay open

The first question the comparison had to survive was whether there is a comparison to make. Builders argued, when the real estate statute commenced, that a homebuyer's complaint belonged exclusively to the new Authority: the Act created a specialist forum, barred the jurisdiction of civil courts over matters within the Authority's competence, and contained a provision giving its provisions effect notwithstanding anything inconsistent in any other law. If that argument had succeeded, this article would be a page long, and buyers whose projects predated registration, or whose grievances sat awkwardly with the Act's scheme, would have had one door and one door only.

The argument failed on the text of the real estate statute itself. Section 79 bars the jurisdiction of a civil court in respect of any matter which the Authority, the adjudicating officer or the Appellate Tribunal is empowered to determine, and consumer commissions are not civil courts, a distinction the Supreme Court had drawn before in Malay Kumar Ganguli v. Dr. Sukumar Mukherjee when it held that the commissions under the consumer statute are quasi-judicial authorities and not courts for such purposes. Section 88 then provides that the provisions of the real estate statute shall be in addition to, and not in derogation of, the provisions of any other law for the time being in force, which is the standard legislative formula for a cumulative remedy. Section 18 itself, the refund provision, opens by preserving the allottee's other remedies: the right to withdraw from the project and claim refund with interest is expressed to be without prejudice to any other remedy available. The consumer statute carried the mirror-image provision, Section 3 of the 1986 Act, reproduced as Section 100 of the 2019 Act, stating that its provisions are in addition to and not in derogation of any other law.

The Supreme Court put the matter beyond argument in Imperia Structures Limited v. Anil Patni, decided on 2 November 2020, where a group of allottees of a Gurugram project had complained to the National Commission of delay in possession and the builder contended that registration of the project under the real estate statute ousted the Commission's jurisdiction and that the buyers were in any event not consumers. The Court, in a bench of Uday Umesh Lalit and Vineet Saran, JJ., held that the bar in Section 79 operates against civil courts and not against consumer fora, that Section 88 makes the remedies additional, and that an allottee is entitled to pursue the consumer remedy for deficiency in service including delay in handing over possession. The remedies under the two statutes are therefore concurrent in the strict sense: both forums are open, each can be approached first, and the buyer's grievance does not have to be recharacterised to fit through either door.

Concurrency of jurisdiction must not be confused with duplication of recovery, and the distinction matters enough to state at the outset rather than in the forum-shopping discussion where it also belongs. Two proceedings about the same delay, asking for the same refund and the same compensation, are not two chances at the same money; they are an abuse the forums themselves police. What concurrency gives the buyer is a genuine election at the start, and in some configurations proceedings of genuinely different characters: a complaint to the Authority invoking its regulatory supervision of the project is a different act from a consumer complaint seeking compensation for deficiency, and buyers have on occasion used the regulatory track for the project and the adjudicatory track for their personal loss. The election that the law protects is the election of the forum that will decide the claim for relief, and it is that election the rest of this article is about.

### Does an arbitration clause in the builder-buyer agreement close either door?

No. The Supreme Court held in Emaar MGF Land Limited v. Aftab Singh that an arbitration agreement between buyer and builder does not oust the jurisdiction of the consumer fora, whose remedies are statutory and additional; and the Authority's jurisdiction flows from the real estate statute itself, not from the parties' contract, so the clause displaces neither forum.

Builders pressed arbitration clauses hard in the first years of the overlap, because an arbitration is private, slower to constitute than buyers expect, and fought on terrain where the builder is a repeat player. The failure of that argument in the consumer context is one of the quiet structural facts of this field: the homebuyer's forums are creatures of statute, their jurisdiction is conferred by Parliament rather than by the parties' bargain, and a standard-form clause drafted by the builder cannot contract the buyer out of them. The same logic answers a family of related clauses that appear in builder-buyer agreements, clauses purporting to confine the buyer to a named city's courts, to make the builder's own determination of delay final, or to cap the buyer's compensation at a nominal figure. The commissions have treated such clauses through the doctrine of unfair trade practice, and in Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Limited v. Govindan Raghavan the Supreme Court refused to bind a buyer to a one-sided clause that entitled the builder to charge interest on the buyer's delayed instalments at a steep rate while paying the buyer a token sum for its own years of delay, holding the incorporation of such one-sided terms in a standard-form contract to be an unfair trade practice. A buyer choosing a forum should know that the consumer route carries this doctrine in its working equipment, because it was developed there, while the Authority approaches the same clauses through its power over the agreement for sale's conformity with the model agreement and the Act.

## The pecuniary question: the price paid decides the room

Inside the consumer route there is a second choice, and it is not a choice at all: the value of the transaction assigns the buyer to a tier. The District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission divide original complaints by pecuniary jurisdiction, and filing in the wrong tier wastes months. This is the point on which published guidance is most often out of date, because the test and the thresholds have both changed, and the change is the single most consequential technical difference between the consumer statute as it stood when the real estate statute commenced and the consumer statute in its present form.

Under the Consumer Protection Act, 1986, the test was the value of the goods or services together with the compensation claimed. Section 11 gave the District Forum complaints where that combined value did not exceed twenty lakh rupees; Section 17 gave the State Commission complaints above twenty lakh rupees and up to one crore rupees; Section 21 gave the National Commission complaints above one crore rupees. The compensation claimed was thus part of the jurisdictional arithmetic, and a buyer could reach a higher tier by claiming more, which produced predictable inflation of claims and a body of case law about whether the compensation claimed was genuine or a device to select a forum.

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 changed both the measure and the amounts. The measure is the value of the goods or services paid as consideration, alone. What the buyer paid, or agreed in the statutory sense to have paid, for the flat fixes the tier; the compensation sought for delay, mental agony or anything else is irrelevant to the forum. As enacted, Section 34 gave the District Commission complaints where the consideration paid did not exceed one crore rupees, Section 47 gave the State Commission complaints above one crore rupees and up to ten crore rupees, and Section 58 gave the National Commission complaints above ten crore rupees. The Act armed the Central Government to prescribe different figures by rules, and by the Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021, notified on 30 December 2021, the tiers were reset: the District Commission takes complaints where the consideration paid does not exceed fifty lakh rupees, the State Commission where it exceeds fifty lakh rupees but does not exceed two crore rupees, and the National Commission where it exceeds two crore rupees. Guidance that still quotes one crore and ten crore is describing the Act as enacted and not the rules that govern filing; guidance that computes the tier on the compensation claimed is describing the repealed statute.

The constitutional challenge to the new measure failed. In Rutu Mihir Panchal v. Union of India, decided on 29 April 2025, a bench of P. S. Narasimha and Manoj Misra, JJ. upheld Sections 34, 47 and 58 against the argument that a consideration-based test violates the equality guarantee of Article 14 by forcing a claimant who paid a small price for goods but suffered a catastrophic loss into the lowest tier. The Court held that the classification of claims by the value of goods and services paid as consideration has a direct nexus with the object of creating a hierarchical structure of remedies, that Parliament is competent to prescribe pecuniary limits, and that the consumer loses no remedy by the allocation, since the quantum that may be claimed and awarded is untouched by the tier to which the complaint is assigned; the Court coupled its conclusion with a direction that the consumer protection councils and the Central Authority keep the working of the statute under review. For the homebuyer the practical effect of the test and the 2021 thresholds together is that the ordinary individual purchase, a flat bought for a consideration within fifty lakh rupees, begins in the District Commission for the buyer's own district, which is a materially different litigation experience from beginning before a State or National bench, and that a buyer cannot buy his way into a higher forum by the size of his grievance.

A worked example fixes the rule better than the statement of it. A buyer who paid forty-eight lakh rupees for a flat and claims, on top of refund, twelve lakh rupees as compensation for rent and mental agony files, under the rules in force, in the District Commission, because the consideration paid is forty-eight lakh rupees and the twelve lakh of compensation is simply not counted; under the repealed statute the same complaint, valued at sixty lakh rupees on the combined measure, would have been filed in the State Commission. A buyer who paid two crore and forty lakh rupees files in the National Commission however small his compensation claim, because the consideration alone crosses the two crore line. Group complaints raise the one refinement the cases supply: under the 1986 Act, the National Commission held in Ambrish Kumar Shukla v. Ferrous Infrastructure Private Limited that where numerous consumers with the same interest join in one complaint, the pecuniary value is the aggregate of the claims, a rule that let associations of modest buyers reach the higher tiers together; under the 2019 Act's consideration measure, the application of the test to a joined complaint is a matter associations must settle at the filing counter on the complaint as framed, because a wrong tier costs the group its place in the queue and the objection, once taken by the builder, is not cured by the strength of the underlying grievance. The buyer who reads that his grievance is worth a crore in compensation and concludes that his forum is the National Commission has made the commonest filing error in the field, and it is an error the older law invited and the present law punishes with a return of the complaint.

Against this intricate tiering, the real estate statute has nothing to set, and the absence is itself a point of comparison. The Authority has no pecuniary jurisdiction at all. A complaint under Section 31 lies to the Authority of the State where the project is located whether the flat cost thirty lakh rupees or thirty crore rupees, because the Authority's competence is project-based and regulatory rather than claim-based. The buyer never faces a jurisdictional objection about the size of his claim inside the real estate route, and never has to choose a district; the forum is given by the project's registration. The corresponding cost of the design is that the buyer also cannot choose: a project in a State whose Authority is slow, understaffed or functioning through a single member takes the buyer with it, and there is no neighbouring tier to escape into.

## The clocks: limitation in one statute, silence in the other

A buyer's choice between the forums is constrained at the threshold by time, and here the statutes differ in kind. The consumer statute has an express limitation period and the real estate statute has none, and the practical consequences run in both directions.

Section 69 of the 2019 Act provides that a commission shall not admit a complaint unless it is filed within two years from the date on which the cause of action has arisen, and permits a complaint filed later to be entertained where the complainant satisfies the commission that there was sufficient cause for the delay, with the reasons for condonation recorded. The 1986 Act had the same two-year period in Section 24A. Two years is short against the timescale of a housing project: a buyer who books in one year, is promised possession in the third, accepts assurances through the fourth and fifth, and reaches a lawyer in the sixth is, on a crude reading of the provision, already out of time, and builders have taken the limitation objection in case after case on exactly those facts.

The objection usually fails on the law of continuing cause of action, and a buyer should understand why, because it determines when the clock in fact starts. Where the wrong is the failure to deliver possession on the promised date and the builder remains in default, the cause of action is treated as continuing from day to day until possession is actually offered; in Fortune Infrastructure v. Trevor D'Lima the Supreme Court proceeded on the basis that a purchaser cannot be compelled to wait indefinitely for possession and that the failure to hand over the flat gives rise to a recurring cause of action, and in Samruddhi Co-operative Housing Society Limited v. Mumbai Mahalaxmi Construction Private Limited the Court again treated the continuing failure to obtain the occupancy certificate and hand over possession as a continuing wrong for limitation purposes. The buyer who waits while the default continues is therefore generally within time, and the dangerous case is a different one: the buyer who accepted a refund cheque, signed a settlement, took possession with defects and then slept on the defect claim, or whose grievance crystallised into a single completed event years before filing. There the two-year period, subject only to condonation on sufficient cause, is a real bar, and it is the consumer route's sharpest procedural trap.

### When does the two-year clock start against a builder who keeps promising possession?

Not on the possession date, in the ordinary case. Because the failure to hand over possession is a continuing default, the cause of action arises afresh as the default continues, and the Supreme Court has treated buyers who waited on a builder's promises as within time when they filed while still out of possession.

The clock becomes dangerous when the wrong is complete and single, a settled refund, a defect accepted at handover, a payment extracted and not repeated, and then the two years of Section 69 run in earnest.

The real estate statute prescribes no period of limitation for a complaint under Section 31, and the omission was deliberate in structure if not in statement: a regulatory complaint about a continuing state of affairs, an uncompleted project, an unregistered sale, a disclosure not made, is not naturally governed by a limitation clock at all. Authorities have in practice entertained complaints about projects delayed by many years, and the age of the grievance goes to discretion and to the computation of interest rather than to maintainability. Two qualifications keep the picture honest. First, the absence of a period in the Act is not the presence of a promise: a buyer who files a regulatory complaint about a transaction that closed a decade ago, on a project long completed and conveyed, will meet the objection that the Act's scheme is directed at ongoing regulation, and some authorities and tribunals have borrowed limitation principles by analogy in individual cases, so the position is silence administered case by case rather than a settled entitlement. Second, the clocks of the two systems interact: a buyer who spends years inside the real estate route and loses may find the consumer route's two years argued against him on the facts of his particular grievance, relieved only by the continuing-cause doctrine or by condonation. The general position described here is information about how the two systems are built; it does not substitute for advice on an individual case, and limitation is the point on which that caution bites hardest, because limitation periods and forum rules are unforgiving and a month of delay can close a door that merit would have opened.

## Reliefs: what each forum can actually order

The centre of the comparison is relief, and it is here that the two systems diverge in ways a buyer can price before filing. Begin with the refund case, the commonest endgame: the project is years late, the buyer has lost confidence or lost the need, and wants out with the money back.

Under Section 18 of the real estate statute, if the promoter fails to complete or is unable to give possession in accordance with the agreement for sale, or discontinues his business, the allottee may withdraw from the project and is entitled, without prejudice to any other remedy, to the return of the amount received by the promoter with interest at the rate prescribed by the State rules, and the Supreme Court in Newtech Promoters and Developers Private Limited v. State of Uttar Pradesh, decided on 11 November 2021, held that the allottee's right under Section 18 is unqualified: where the promoter has failed to give possession by the date in the agreement, the allottee may seek refund with interest, and the promoter cannot force the allottee to wait or to accept a revised timeline. The interest is prescribed rather than assessed, which carries a double consequence. The buyer does not have to prove loss to earn it; the rate in the State rules applies by computation. And the buyer cannot argue it upwards on the facts of his own suffering; the prescribed rate is the measure whether his actual losses were smaller or far larger.

Compensation under the real estate statute travels by a different internal road. Section 71 commits claims for compensation under Sections 12, 14, 18 and 19 to an adjudicating officer, a judicial officer appointed in consultation with the appropriate government, who assesses compensation on the factors in Section 72, which include the amount of disproportionate gain or unfair advantage made by the defaulting party, the amount of loss caused, the effect of repeated default and the factor of mens rea in the statutory sense of whether the default was intentional. The Authority itself orders the refund and the interest and the regulatory directions; the compensation for the loss as such is carved out to the adjudicating officer's separate proceeding. A buyer who wants refund, interest and compensation out of the real estate route may therefore be pursuing two proceedings before two different functionaries of the same system, sequential or parallel depending on the State's practice, each with its own appeal.

The consumer commission's menu is set out in one place and can be awarded in one proceeding. Section 39 of the 2019 Act empowers the commission, on the allegations about services being proved, to direct the opposite party to remove the deficiency in the services, to return the price or charges paid together with such interest as the commission decides, to pay compensation for loss or injury suffered due to the negligence of the opposite party, with an express power to grant punitive damages in circumstances the commission deems fit, to discontinue an unfair trade practice and not repeat it, and to provide for adequate costs to the parties. Behind that text stands four decades of consumer jurisprudence in which compensation for mental agony and harassment is a standard, separately assessed head in builder cases: commissions have treated the years of waiting, the rent paid for substitute accommodation, the interest carried on a loan for a non-existent flat and the distress of the experience as compensable injury, assessed on the facts rather than by formula, and the Supreme Court's own awards in this line, as in DLF Homes Panchkula Private Limited v. D. S. Dhanda, combined interest on the refund with compensation while cautioning against compensating the same default twice over under overlapping heads. The buyer who wants to be made whole for what the delay actually cost him, rather than to receive the prescribed rate and stop, is asking for something the consumer commission is designed to give and the real estate Authority is designed to split off into a second proceeding.

Between the exit case and the possession case stands the buyer who will neither leave nor be bought out: the allottee who stays in the project and wants the delay paid for month by month. Section 18 provides for him expressly in its proviso: where an allottee does not intend to withdraw from the project, he shall be paid by the promoter interest for every month of delay till the handing over of possession, at the prescribed rate, and the courts have treated that entitlement as an unqualified right that accrues from the failure to hand over possession by the agreement date and is not defeated by the buyer's later acceptance of possession when it is finally offered. The prescribed rate is set by State rules and in the larger States is commonly fixed by reference to the State Bank of India's highest marginal cost of lending rate plus two per cent, the formula Maharashtra's rules adopt, which ties the buyer's compensation for delay to a published lending benchmark rather than to proof of his individual loss. The staying buyer in the consumer route has no equivalent formula: his compensation for the same months is assessed by the commission on his facts, rent paid and interest borne and distress proved, which for a buyer with heavy documented outgoings may exceed the prescribed rate comfortably and for a buyer without documents may fall below it. The formula-versus-assessment contrast is the reliefs comparison in miniature, and it recurs in the table.

The possession case cuts the other way. A buyer who wants the flat, finished, rather than an exit, finds the real estate statute holding instruments the consumer statute does not possess. The Authority supervises the project as a going concern: it can direct the promoter to complete in accordance with the registration declarations, act on lapse or revocation of registration under Section 7, and under Section 8, where registration lapses or is revoked, facilitate the remaining development work through the association of allottees or another agency. Its orders engage the promoter as a regulated entity with a registration to lose. A consumer commission, by contrast, decides the complaint before it: it can direct the builder to deliver possession and remove deficiencies, and routinely does, but it commands no register, no escrow account and no completion machinery, and its direction to hand over possession depends for execution on the same enforcement process as its money orders. Neither forum, it should be said plainly, can conjure a finished building out of an insolvent or absconding promoter; the difference is that the Authority has statutory tools aimed at the project's completion as an outcome, while the commission has tools aimed at the buyer's loss as a liability.

### Can the consumer commission make the builder finish the flat instead of refunding the price?

Yes, within limits. Section 39 empowers a commission to direct the removal of deficiency in service, and commissions have directed builders to complete construction, obtain the occupancy certificate and hand over possession with compensation for the delay, where the buyer elects to stay in the project rather than take a refund.

What the commission cannot do is run the project: it has no escrow account to release, no registration to suspend and no power to substitute another developer, so a completion order against a builder who cannot or will not build is only as good as the enforcement behind it.

Punitive damages deserve a distinguishing sentence, because they are the relief most often advertised and least often decisive. The power exists in Section 39 and is exercised sparingly, in cases of contumacious or repeated conduct, and at figures that supplement rather than transform the award. The realistic centre of gravity in a consumer case is refund with interest as decided, compensation assessed on proved loss and mental agony, and costs; the realistic centre of gravity in a real estate case is refund with prescribed interest, regulatory pressure on the project, and a separate adjudication for anything beyond. A buyer whose losses are documented and large, rent receipts across years, a loan carried at a high rate, a wedding or a retirement displaced, will usually find the consumer menu the one that maps onto his loss. A buyer whose object is the money back at a known rate with the least contest about quantum, or whose object is the project's completion under supervision, is looking at the Authority's side of the table.

## The real estate route in practice: one complaint, two functionaries

A complaint to the Authority is made under Section 31, by any aggrieved person, in the form and with the fee the State rules prescribe; in the larger States the fee is a modest fixed sum, of the order of one thousand rupees, and the filing is increasingly electronic through the Authority's own portal. The complaint is then distributed inside the system according to what it asks for. So much of it as seeks the Act's regulatory and refund outcomes, directions to the promoter, refund and interest under Section 18, penalties for contravention, goes to the Authority, which in many States hears complaints through single-member benches under the delegation the Act permits, a practice the Supreme Court sustained in Newtech Promoters against the argument that complaints had to be heard by the Authority as a whole. So much of it as seeks compensation for loss goes, under Section 71, to the adjudicating officer. A buyer drafting a single complaint that asks for everything should expect it to be worked on at two desks, and should not mistake an order on the refund for a decision on the compensation, or the reverse.

The Authority's procedure is inquisitorial in a way the commissions' is not. Under Section 35 the Authority may inquire into the promoter's affairs, call for information and explanations, and require the attendance of persons and the production of documents; under Section 36 it may issue interim orders restraining a promoter from a course of conduct; under Section 37 it may issue directions to promoters, allottees and agents as it considers necessary; and disobedience of its orders carries a penalty under Section 63 that runs for every day of default up to a cumulative five per cent of the estimated cost of the project, with disobedience of the Appellate Tribunal's orders exposing a promoter to imprisonment extending to three years or a further fine extending to ten per cent of the estimated cost, or both. Several Authorities have also built conciliation forums at which a large share of complaints, particularly those about possession timelines and interest computation, are settled by a recorded compromise before adjudication, a facility that suits the buyer whose object is a workable outcome on this project rather than a precedent. The Authority route in practice is thus less like a trial than like taking a grievance to the industry's referee, who holds the file, the whistle and, in the penalty provisions, a set of sanctions that operate on the builder's licence to do business at all.

## The consumer route in practice: one complaint, one proceeding, every head of loss

A consumer complaint is filed under Section 35 of the 2019 Act by the consumer, by a recognised consumer association on his behalf, by one or more consumers with the permission of the commission where numerous consumers share the same interest, or by the Central Authority or a government in the cases the Act specifies; the fee prescribed by the Consumer Protection (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions) Rules, 2020 is scaled to the consideration paid and is nominal by the standards of civil litigation, nothing at all where the consideration does not exceed five lakh rupees, two hundred rupees in the band above it, rising by slabs to seven thousand five hundred rupees at the National Commission. Filing may be electronic, and the territorial rule of the 2019 Act lets the complainant file in the commission within whose limits he resides or personally works for gain, or where the builder resides or carries on business, or where the cause of action arises, a spread of choices the 1986 Act did not give in the same form and one that matters to buyers who booked in one city and live in another.

The procedure is the summary procedure of Section 38: notice to the builder, his version within the period the Act prescribes, evidence ordinarily on affidavit, and a decision on the documents, which is why builder cases, turning as they do on the agreement and the payment record, fit the forum's method. The 2019 Act adds two features a buyer should price in. The first is mediation: Section 37 empowers the District Commission, at the stage of admission or thereafter, where it appears there exist elements of a settlement acceptable to the parties, to refer the complaint for mediation with the parties' consent, and a settlement recorded in mediation ends the case on agreed terms, often faster than adjudication and sometimes with a possession schedule the builder actually keeps because he drafted it. The second is the class dimension. The association complaint and the same-interest complaint let the buyers of a tower or a project proceed together, sharing cost and presenting the builder's default as the pattern it usually is rather than as twenty isolated accidents; the leading homebuyer cases in the National Commission, including the line that produced Imperia Structures itself, were group complaints of exactly this kind, and the procedural economy they create is one of the consumer route's genuine advantages over a system in which each allottee's complaint, whatever its merits, is formally his own.

Against these advantages the buyer must set the forum's known condition: the commissions carry heavy dockets inherited from the 1986 Act's three decades, benches in several States sit short of strength for long stretches, and the summary design is a direction of travel rather than a description of every commission's list. None of that is a reason to avoid the forum; it is a reason to file a complaint that a busy bench can decide on the papers, with the agreement, the receipts, the possession clause and the computation of the claim set out in the order the bench will want them, which is advice that applies identically, it should be said, before an Authority deciding complaints in batches.

## Eight dimensions and a decision

The comparison can be drawn together. The table that follows states each dimension, what the real estate route offers on it, what the consumer route offers on it, and which forum the dimension favours and why, and the decision rule after the table converts the dimensions into instructions a buyer can apply to his own case.

The decision rule the table supports is short enough to state in one breath. Decide first what you want: the flat, the money, or the money plus full compensation. If the object is the project completed or a refund at the prescribed rate with the regulator's file and pressure behind it, go to the Authority. If the object is compensation that matches what the delay actually cost, including mental agony and rent and loan interest, assessed in a single proceeding and backed by an offence provision, go to the commission. If the builder shows signs of insolvency, stop and take advice about the insolvency process before filing anywhere, because that process, once admitted, overrides the comparison altogether.

## Appeals and deposits: what it costs the builder to fight on

The first order in a homebuyer's case is rarely the end of the matter, and the appellate design of each statute decides how much of the buyer's victory is real money in hand while the builder fights on. Both systems learned the same lesson from civil litigation, that an appeal without a deposit is a free option on delay, and both make the builder pay to appeal; they set the price differently.

Under Section 43(5) of the real estate statute, any person aggrieved by a direction, decision or order of the Authority or the adjudicating officer may appeal to the Real Estate Appellate Tribunal, but where the appellant is the promoter, the appeal shall not be entertained unless the promoter has first deposited with the Tribunal at least thirty per cent of the penalty imposed, or such higher percentage as the Tribunal determines, or the total amount to be paid to the allottee including interest and compensation imposed, or both, as the case may be. The text repays reading, because its two limbs bite differently: against a penalty, the deposit is a percentage, at least thirty per cent and up to the whole as the Tribunal directs; against an order to pay the allottee, the deposit is of the total amount payable, which the Supreme Court's later reading of the proviso in the compensation context has confirmed to mean the whole of what the order directs to be paid to the allottee, including interest and compensation, before the appeal is heard. In Newtech Promoters the Court upheld the constitutional validity of this pre-deposit condition against the promoters' challenge, treating allottees and promoters as distinct classes and the deposit as a legitimate condition designed to discourage frivolous appeals and to secure the allottee's recovery. An allottee's own appeal, by contrast, carries no deposit condition at all, since the proviso speaks only of the promoter. A further appeal lies to the High Court under Section 58 on a substantial question of law, within sixty days.

The consumer statute sets a flat rate and applies it symmetrically to whoever has been ordered to pay. Under the second proviso to Section 41, an appeal from the District Commission to the State Commission by a person required to pay an amount under the order is not entertained unless fifty per cent of that amount is deposited; the second proviso to Section 51 does the same for appeals from the State Commission to the National Commission, and the second proviso to Section 67 for appeals from the National Commission to the Supreme Court. The uniformity is the point of comparison. The 1986 Act had capped each deposit, at twenty-five thousand rupees at the first tier, thirty-five thousand at the second and fifty thousand at the third, taking the lesser of fifty per cent or the cap, so that on any substantial homebuyer award the builder's price of appealing was a trivial fixed sum. The 2019 Act removed the caps entirely: fifty per cent of a crore-scale award is real money, deposited before the appeal is even entertained, and the Supreme Court has since confirmed that the deposit is a condition of entertainment and not a bargaining chip, while holding that an appellate commission may, for reasons recorded, require deposit of a higher proportion or the whole as a condition of staying the order under appeal.

For the buyer's decision the deposit architecture matters in two concrete ways. It discounts the builder's threat to appeal, because the appeal begins with a substantial payment into the forum rather than with a notice, and builders who would litigate a free appeal indefinitely settle cases they would otherwise spin out. And it determines the buyer's interim position: money deposited in the appellate forum is money identified and held against the outcome, which is a different practical situation from a decree against a builder whose assets are being moved between projects while the appeal meanders. On this dimension the consumer route's flat fifty per cent is the more demanding condition at the first appeal, while the real estate route's requirement of the total amount payable to the allottee is, on refund and compensation orders, more demanding still; what the two systems share, and what distinguishes both from the civil court the buyer avoided, is that the era of the costless builder appeal is over in each of them.

## Enforcement: where the routes diverge most

Ask practitioners who work in both systems where the comparison is in practice decided and the answer, more often than not, is execution. The two statutes enforce their orders through different arms of the State, and the difference is not technical: it is the difference between asking the district revenue administration to collect the money as if it were land revenue, and putting the defaulter in peril of imprisonment if he does not pay.

Section 40 of the real estate statute provides that if a promoter, allottee or real estate agent fails to pay an amount of interest, penalty or compensation imposed on him, it shall be recoverable from that person as arrears of land revenue, and in Newtech Promoters the Supreme Court read the provision with the purpose of the Act to hold that the refundable amount determined under Section 18 is itself recoverable within Section 40, notwithstanding an infelicity in the provision's drafting. The mechanism is a recovery certificate transmitted to the Collector, who then proceeds as for the recovery of land revenue arrears: attachment and sale of property, in the classical revenue manner. The design has a real virtue, which is that the buyer does not have to institute a separate execution proceeding or pay further court fees to set the State's collection machinery in motion. Its weakness is equally structural. Revenue recovery is one demand among the many a district administration carries, it proceeds at administrative rather than litigative pace, it can be slowed by the promoter's objections, stays obtained elsewhere and transfers of assets, and the buyer has no direct control over its tempo. Why so many recovery certificates issued under the real estate statute remain unexecuted for long periods, and what the State-level record shows about the district machinery's response, is examined in the account of [homebuyer remedies under the real estate statute](/2018/12/15/rera-homebuyer-remedies-india/), which owns that record; for the comparison, the point is that the buyer's order converts into a claim on a queue administered by officials who were not parties to his case.

The consumer statute enforces by two instruments held together. Section 71 of the 2019 Act provides that every order of a District, State or National Commission shall be enforced in the same manner as if it were a decree made by a court in a suit, with the provisions of Order XXI of the Code of Civil Procedure applying as far as may be, which brings with it the decree-holder's ordinary armoury: attachment and sale of the judgment-debtor's property, and arrest and detention in the manner the Code permits. Section 72 then criminalises the default itself: whoever fails to comply with an order of a commission is punishable with imprisonment for a term not less than one month and extending to three years, or with a fine not less than twenty-five thousand rupees and extending to one lakh rupees, or with both, and the commission itself holds the powers of a Judicial Magistrate of the first class to try the offence summarily. The 1986 Act's corresponding provision, Section 27, carried a lower fine band, and the 2019 Act's raising of it is of a piece with its removal of the appeal-deposit caps: the statute's enforcement philosophy is coercion of the person. Directors and officers of a defaulting builder company find that a consumer order is not a letter to be filed but a process that can reach their liberty, and compliance rates respond to that difference in kind, not merely in degree. A buyer comparing forums should weigh this dimension with the seriousness the table gives it, because against a solvent but unwilling builder the offence provision is the single most effective collection instrument either statute contains, while against a builder with no assets and no fear of the process because the company is already a shell, neither instrument collects anything, which is the point at which the insolvency discussion below takes over.

### Can the Authority act against a builder beyond the single complaint before it?

Yes, and this is the regulatory surplus the consumer route cannot match. The Authority registers the project, can inquire into the promoter's affairs on complaint or on its own motion, can call for information and direct compliance, can impose the Act's penalties for contraventions, and can revoke a project's registration for the defaults Section 7 specifies.

A buyer's complaint therefore reaches a forum that can act on the project as a whole, not only on the buyer's claim. A consumer commission decides the case before it; the class-level regulator on that side, the Central Authority, protects consumers as a class and is not a forum for the individual buyer's refund.

There is a fair counterweight, and honesty requires it in a comparison that has so far favoured the commission on enforcement. The offence provision's strength is also its friction: a prosecution for non-compliance is a proceeding the commission must conduct, the builder can contest whether the order was capable of compliance and whether the default was wilful, and commissions vary in their appetite for committing defaulters. Conversely, the revenue route, for all its torpor, operates without any further adjudication and attaches to land, and builders with land banks in the district have reason to fear it. The enforcement verdict is therefore a buyer's verdict about his particular builder: against a trading builder with assets, directors in the jurisdiction and a reputation to protect, the consumer route's coercion is the sharper instrument; against a builder whose one asset is the project land itself, the Authority's certificate may reach the asset more directly. What neither buyer should do is choose the forum on the relief menu alone and discover the enforcement position afterwards.

## The point at which the comparison collapses: insolvency

Every statement made so far assumes a builder who can be made to pay or to build. The assumption fails in the case that has generated the largest homebuyer losses of the period, the developer who enters the insolvency process, and the buyer choosing a forum while his builder approaches that point needs the legal position stated without varnish.

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 changed the homebuyer's world in two steps. First, Parliament amended the Code in 2018 to explain that an amount raised from an allottee of a real estate project has the commercial effect of a borrowing, which placed allottees within the class of financial creditors, and the Supreme Court upheld that amendment and the allottees' status as financial creditors in Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Limited v. Union of India, decided in 2019, rejecting the builders' challenge that homebuyers were being artificially dressed as lenders. The Jaypee Infratech proceedings, which reached the Supreme Court in Chitra Sharma v. Union of India, had already shown the Court protecting homebuyers within an ongoing insolvency process before the amendment settled their status. A later amendment in 2020 added a threshold for allottees acting together: an application by allottees against a real estate developer must be filed jointly by not less than one hundred allottees of the same project or not less than ten per cent of the total number of allottees, whichever is less. Second, and decisive for the comparison: on the admission of a corporate insolvency resolution process against the promoter, the moratorium declared under Section 14 of the Code, whose operation is traced in the guide to [the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016](/2012/04/01/insolvency-bankruptcy-code-2016-guide/), prohibits the institution or continuation of suits and proceedings against the corporate debtor and stays the execution of judgments, decrees and orders against it. Proceedings before the Authority and before the consumer commissions are proceedings against the debtor for this purpose, and orders already made in the buyer's favour cannot be executed during the moratorium. The buyer's remedy converts, by operation of law, into a claim to be filed and verified in the resolution process, where he votes as part of the committee of creditors through an authorised representative and takes what the approved resolution plan gives financial creditors of his class, frequently a completed flat under a new developer's plan, sometimes a fraction of the money, delivered on the plan's timetable rather than the forum's.

Inside the process, the homebuyer's position has a shape of its own that forum thinking does not prepare him for. His claim is filed with the interim resolution professional and verified against the builder's records, which is where the completeness of his agreement and payment documentation stops being good practice and becomes the measure of his admitted claim. He is represented in the committee of creditors by an authorised representative for the class of allottees, who votes the class's share on the strength of the allottees' instructions canvassed under the Code's machinery, so the organised project, with an association that can aggregate preferences, negotiates in the plan process in a way the scattered buyer cannot. Resolution plans for real estate debtors characteristically propose completion of the project by the incoming developer or by the allottees' own arrangements, with the flats delivered under the plan and the money claims of those who want out compromised within it, which means the insolvency route silently answers the exit-versus-completion question at the level of the class rather than the individual. The appellate tribunal for the Code even experimented, in the Umang Realtech proceedings, with confining the resolution process to the single project in default rather than the developer's whole portfolio, an approach the practitioners call reverse resolution, whose career in the case law reflects how badly the standard process fits an industry in which each project is economically a separate venture wearing a common corporate shell. None of this machinery belongs to either forum in the comparison; it displaces both, and it rewards exactly the assets, documents, organisation and a liquidated claim, that the diligent buyer in the earlier sections was building for a different contest.

The practical teaching is uncomfortable and simple. A buyer who smells insolvency, construction long stopped, lenders circling, other projects of the group in proceedings, should not ask only which of the two forums is better, because both answers expire on admission. What preserves value at that point is the quality of the claim: the agreement, the payment record and any order already obtained, which fixes and liquidates the buyer's claim for the process and improves his documentary position within it, and the organisation of allottees, since the process rewards classes that act together and meet its thresholds. Conversely, a buyer deciding between forums against a builder who is merely late, illiquid in the ordinary way of developers, and still selling and building, is in the world of the earlier sections, where the comparison holds in full. The collapse of the comparison is not a reason to abandon it; it is a reason to run it early, while it still governs.

## Interim protection while the case runs

A comparison of final reliefs skips the question anxious buyers ask first, which is what protects them while the case is pending. A builder who learns of a complaint can create facts: sell the booked flat to another purchaser, raise a fresh demand under a disputed clause, or move collections out of the project's reach. Both statutes answer the anxiety, in characteristically different styles.

The real estate statute's answer is the stronger on paper. Section 36 empowers the Authority, during an inquiry, to issue interim orders restraining any promoter, allottee or agent from acting in a manner detrimental to the interests of the parties, without a prior hearing where the circumstances require it, and behind that power stands the regulatory architecture itself: the seventy per cent separate account operates from the day of registration and not from the day of the complaint, so the project's receipts are partially ring-fenced before any buyer files anything, and a direction to the promoter about the project binds a regulated entity whose registration is hostage to its conduct. The consumer statute's answer in the 2019 Act is Section 38(8), which empowers the commission, during the pendency of a proceeding, to pass such interim order as it considers just and proper on an application made for the purpose; commissions use it to restrain builders from alienating the booked unit, from cancelling the allotment for the very defaults in dispute, and from coercive recovery of contested demands while the complaint is heard. The 1986 Act's commissions had worked with a narrower interim toolkit for much of their history, and the 2019 Act's express provision closed a gap builders had exploited by treating the pendency of a complaint as a licence to pre-empt it.

In practice the interim dimension rarely decides the forum on its own, because the catastrophic interim risks in this field, diversion of the project's funds and sale of the unit, are addressed by both systems in some form. It decides cases at the margin: the buyer who discovers that his flat is being marketed to others while he negotiates belongs before the forum that can restrain the sale on an urgent application, and the buyer whose project is mid-construction and haemorrhaging collections belongs in the system whose escrow and direction powers are already wrapped around the project. Interim protection is therefore best treated as a tie-breaker inside the decision rule rather than a dimension of its own: run the verdict on relief and enforcement first, and let the interim position settle the cases the verdict leaves close.

## Forum shopping, honestly

Concurrency invites the charge of forum shopping, and the charge deserves a straight answer rather than a defensive one. A legal system that deliberately creates two forums for the same grievance, and says in both statutes that the remedies are additional, has itself authorised the choice; exercising a statutory election between forums Parliament left open is not an abuse, and Imperia Structures treats it as the allottee's entitlement. The buyer who selects the commission because its compensation menu matches his loss, or the Authority because the project's completion is what he wants, is doing exactly what the design invites.

The abuse begins at a different line, and buyers cross it at their peril. The same relief cannot be obtained twice: a refund recovered in one forum is a refund recovered, and a second proceeding demanding it again will fail and may draw costs and adverse findings on the buyer's credibility that poison his legitimate claims. Parallel proceedings on the same claim, run simultaneously in the hope that one forum will move first, are liable to be stayed or dismissed when the duplication surfaces, and builders' counsel look for them; the buyer who has filed in one forum and dislikes its pace should take advice about withdrawal and election rather than quietly opening a second front. And an order obtained in one forum is, on the claim it decides, final between the parties in the other: the principle that a matter once adjudicated cannot be re-agitated is not a technicality but the foundation on which both systems' finality provisions rest. The honest summary is that the law gives the buyer one claim and two doors, and expects him to pick a door and walk through it.

### Does a buyer have to send a legal notice before filing in either forum?

No statute makes a pre-filing notice a condition of the complaint. Neither Section 31 of the real estate statute nor the complaint provisions of the consumer statute require the buyer to serve a demand notice before filing, and a complaint is not defective for want of one.

A notice remains practically valuable in both routes: it fixes the builder with a dated demand, often starts the interest narrative, and sometimes produces a settlement or a possession offer that narrows the case. But it is a matter of tactics and evidence, not a jurisdictional step, and a buyer whose limitation position is tight under the consumer statute should file first and not spend the period on correspondence.

## The doors not in the comparison

Two other doors are sometimes pointed to, and a complete comparison should say why they are not in the table. The first is the civil court. Section 79 bars a civil court from entertaining a suit in respect of any matter the Authority, the adjudicating officer or the Appellate Tribunal is empowered to determine, which covers the run of homebuyer claims under the real estate statute, and the consumer statute was designed from 1986 as the summary alternative that makes an ordinary civil suit for deficiency in service unnecessary for a buyer who qualifies as a consumer. Civil proceedings retain a residual place for disputes that are not, in substance, allottee grievances at all: contests over title between private parties, claims by persons who are not allottees or consumers in the statutory senses, and commercial disputes between the builder and his contractors, lenders and landowners that merely form the background to the buyer's case. For the buyer's own claim for possession, refund or compensation, the civil court is not a third option but the forum both statutes were built to retire, slower, court-fee-bearing on ad valorem scales the commissions deliberately undercut, and procedurally committed to a full trial in cases that the two specialist systems decide on affidavit evidence.

The second door is the criminal complaint, typically for cheating, and it is not a remedy door at all. A prosecution, if it proceeds, punishes; it does not refund, and neither statute treats the pendency of a criminal case as any obstacle to the buyer's civil recovery in his chosen forum, nor the civil recovery as any obstacle to the prosecution. Buyers reach for it because the builder's conduct often feels like fraud and sometimes is, and because the prospect of a criminal process concentrates a builder's attention in negotiations. Its honest place in the comparison is as leverage adjacent to a remedy claim, pursued or threatened in parallel by some buyers, valuable exactly insofar as it moves a settlement in the forum where the money is actually decided, and dangerous when it substitutes for that claim, because a buyer who spends his limitation period as a complainant in a police station may return to the consumer commission to find the two years of Section 69 argued against him on a grievance that had crystallised long before. The table compares the two forums that can decide the buyer's claim; the criminal process decides the builder's guilt, which is a different question, and confusing the two has cost more buyers their remedies than any error of forum selection between the Authority and the commissions.

## The verdict: choose by remedy and enforcement, not by speed

The comparison permits the defended answer the framework requires. The deciding factor is the pairing of relief with enforcement: what the forum can order, matched against how its order is collected. On that pairing the consumer route is the stronger general answer for the individual buyer whose case is a money case, and the real estate route is the stronger answer where the buyer's object is the project itself or a refund at a known rate with the regulator engaged. The reasoning, compressed, is this. Most homebuyer grievances that reach a forum are, in substance, claims for money against a builder who has had the use of the buyer's funds for years: the refund, the rent paid elsewhere, the loan interest, the distress. For that claim the commission offers the fuller instrument in a single proceeding: refund with interest at a rate the commission sets on the facts, compensation for loss and for mental agony assessed in the same case, punitive damages in the fit case, and costs; an appeal that begins only on deposit of half the amount ordered; and an order enforceable as a decree and backed by an offence provision that reaches the defaulting builder's directors personally. The Authority matches the refund, fixes its interest by rule rather than by assessment, splits compensation away to an adjudicating officer, and enforces through the district revenue queue. Where the buyer's loss exceeds the prescribed interest, as it does in most long-delay cases with documented rent and borrowing costs, the commission's arithmetic is simply the larger and more accurate one, and its collection machinery is the sharper of the two.

The verdict reverses where its premises fail, and the exceptions are as much a part of the answer as the rule. Where the buyer wants the flat and the project is capable of completion, the Authority's supervision, its hold over the escrow account and its Section 8 machinery for completing the development through the allottees' association address the object directly, and a commission's money award is a consolation the buyer did not seek. Where the buyer's State Authority is functional and fast and its consumer commissions are drowning, the design verdict yields to the local record, which is why the decision rule directs attention to the buyer's own State rather than to a national average that does not exist. Where the grievance is regulatory in character, false disclosures, diversion of escrowed funds, sales in breach of registration, the Authority is not merely an option but the only forum whose powers answer the complaint, since a commission can compensate the buyer before it but cannot discipline the project. And where insolvency impends, both verdicts are overtaken, for the reasons given above.

There is a textual footnote to the speed question that both camps in the anecdote war omit. The consumer statute writes its aspiration into the Act: Section 38 directs that every complaint be disposed of as expeditiously as possible and that endeavour be made to decide it within three months of the builder's receipt of notice where the complaint does not require analysis or testing of goods, a description that fits the standard builder case, which turns on documents. The real estate statute writes no disposal period into the Act at all; the pace of an Authority is set by its benches, its rules and its list, and varies accordingly. An endeavour clause is not a guarantee and no buyer should treat three months as a promise; but a comparison that never mentions that one statute commits its forums to a stated endeavour and the other leaves pace wholly to administration is not comparing the texts, only the stories.

What the verdict rejects is the way the choice is usually made. Buyers choose forums on stories about speed, a neighbour's case that finished in a year, a lawyer's anecdote about a slow commission, and speed is the dimension on which the two systems are least distinguishable by design and most variable by place. Both were built as summary, document-driven alternatives to the civil court; both carry dockets that reflect the scale of builder default in the housing market; both are faster in some States and benches than in others. A choice made on relief and enforcement survives contact with either queue, because the buyer ends in the forum that can give him what he came for and collect it; a choice made on promised speed delivers him, at whatever pace, to a forum that may be unable to award the compensation he lost years proving.

## The decision rule applied: three buyers

Abstract verdicts earn their keep only when they survive contact with cases, so take three buyers whose facts differ in the ways that matter and run the rule on each.

The first buyer paid forty-two lakh rupees for a flat promised in the fourth year after booking and is in the ninth, living in rented accommodation in the same city, paying rent that has risen across the period and servicing a home loan on the undelivered flat. The project is registered, construction continues slowly, and the builder is a going concern with other projects selling. Her documented losses, rent and loan interest together, run well beyond what the prescribed interest rate would yield on her instalments. On the decision rule her case is a money case with assessed losses larger than formula losses: the consumer route, in the District Commission of her own district since the consideration paid is below fifty lakh rupees, claiming refund or possession with compensation assessed head by head, is where her arithmetic is largest and where enforcement, if the builder defies the order, carries the offence provision behind it. If she elects instead to stay in the project, the same analysis holds for her compensation claim, with the Authority's monthly interest as the fallback she can compute to the rupee before choosing.

The second buyer paid one crore and eighty lakh rupees for a flat in a project that stopped at the fourteenth floor when the builder's registration was entangled in a dispute with the landowner, and the association of allottees is organised and wants the building finished, not refunds that a half-built tower cannot fund. His object is completion. No commission can give it to him in the form he needs: a money award against a stalled project is a claim on an empty treasury, while the Authority holds the levers the case actually turns on, the registration, the escrow account, the power under Section 8 to facilitate completion of the remaining development through the association or another agency, and continuing supervision of whoever builds. His forum is the Authority, and his complaint is really an application to set the completion machinery in motion, with his personal money claims riding behind the project's revival rather than ahead of it.

The third buyer's facts carry the warning flag. She paid sixty-five lakh rupees to a builder whose other projects have entered insolvency proceedings, whose site office has closed, and whose lenders are enforcing securities. Both forums remain formally open to her, and a quick order from either would fix her claim; but if a resolution process is admitted against her builder, every proceeding freezes under the moratorium and her remedy becomes a claim in that process, where her recovery will be decided by a resolution plan negotiated among creditors and where organised allottees with verified claims fare better than isolated claimants. Her correct sequence is to perfect and document her claim at once, to organise with the project's other allottees towards the thresholds the insolvency statute sets for allottees acting together, and to treat the forum choice as secondary to the insolvency advice she needs before, not after, admission. She is the buyer for whom the comparison collapses, and the collapse is the reason the decision rule puts the solvency check first in time even though it comes last in the table.

## The recommendation by reader type

For the homebuyer, the working sequence is to settle the object before the forum. Write down, before any filing, whether the case is an exit case or a completion case, and what the delay has cost in figures that can be documented: instalments paid, rent paid, interest paid on the home loan, the period of both. Keep the agreement, the payment receipts, the allotment letter and the builder's communications in one ordered set; buyers who assemble that record early, using whatever office file viewer opens their Excel payment schedules, Word documents and PowerPoint project presentations in a single place, such as [an office file viewer for Excel, Word and PowerPoint files](https://reportmedic.org/tools/office-file-viewer-excel-docx-pptx.html), find that both forums reward the same documentary completeness, because both decide largely on papers. Then apply the decision rule: completion or prescribed-rate refund with the regulator engaged, to the Authority; full compensation in one proceeding with the coercive enforcement behind it, to the commission of the tier the consideration paid assigns. Check the builder's solvency signals before either filing.

For the advocate, the comparison's pressure points are the election and the clocks. The election must be made cleanly and recorded, because the builder's first line of defence in the second forum is the first proceeding; the consumer clock of two years must be diarised from the crystallisation of the grievance and not merely from the possession date, with the continuing-cause doctrine pleaded properly where it is relied on; and the deposit architecture should shape settlement advice from the first hearing, since a builder facing a fifty per cent deposit on appeal, or the total amount payable under the real estate proviso, negotiates in a different register from a builder facing a costless appeal. The quantified claim should be built to the forum: prescribed interest needs no proof of loss but caps the recovery; assessed compensation needs the loss proved head by head, with the discipline the Supreme Court imposed in Dhanda against double counting between interest and compensation for the same period.

For the researcher, the two statutes are a natural experiment in institutional design for the same harm. One legislature answered builder default by building a regulator with an adjudicatory arm, project-level information and administrative enforcement; the other, older answer was a hierarchy of specialist courts with compensatory relief and penal enforcement, refurbished in 2019 with a regulator of its own for class-level wrongs. The questions the experiment poses are empirical and unanswered in any settled way: whether prescribed interest under-compensates systematically against assessed compensation; whether revenue recovery or penal execution collects more, faster, against trading builders; whether the Appellate Tribunal's total-amount deposit or the commissions' fifty per cent deposit settles more cases. The published record permits the structural comparison this article has made; the outcome comparison awaits data neither system yet publishes in usable form, and assertions about it should be treated with the suspicion this article has applied to claims about speed.

The forums will coexist for as long as both statutes stand, and the coexistence is not a defect to be lamented but a design the courts have confirmed and the buyer is entitled to use. The buyer's task is narrower than the system's: one claim, two doors, and a decision that should be made on the relief at the end of the corridor and the machinery that will collect it, and on nothing else.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can a homebuyer choose between RERA and a consumer commission?

Yes. The Supreme Court held in Imperia Structures Limited v. Anil Patni that the real estate statute does not bar a complaint before a consumer commission: Section 79 of that Act bars civil courts, and consumer commissions are not civil courts, while Section 88 provides that the Act is in addition to and not in derogation of any other law. The consumer statute carries the matching provision in Section 100 of the 2019 Act. The allottee therefore has a genuine election between the Authority of the State where the project is registered and the consumer commission assigned by the consideration paid, and may approach either first. What the buyer cannot do is recover the same relief twice, so the election, once carried to an order, is final for that claim.

### Q: Which consumer commission hears a claim of a given size?

The tier is fixed by the value of the goods or services paid as consideration, not by the compensation claimed. Under the Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021, notified on 30 December 2021, the District Commission hears complaints where the consideration paid does not exceed fifty lakh rupees, the State Commission where it exceeds fifty lakh rupees but does not exceed two crore rupees, and the National Commission where it exceeds two crore rupees. As enacted in 2019 the figures were one crore and ten crore rupees, and guidance quoting them is out of date. The Supreme Court upheld the consideration-based test in Rutu Mihir Panchal v. Union of India. Under the repealed 1986 Act the measure was the value of the service together with the compensation claimed, with tiers at twenty lakh rupees and one crore rupees.

### Q: What relief does each forum give a homebuyer?

The Authority can order refund of the amount paid with interest at the rate prescribed by the State rules where the promoter fails to give possession, direct compliance and completion, and impose penalties; compensation for loss is assessed separately by an adjudicating officer under Section 71. A consumer commission can, in one proceeding under Section 39 of the 2019 Act, direct removal of the deficiency, refund with such interest as it decides, compensation for loss or injury including mental agony, punitive damages in fit circumstances, discontinuance of an unfair trade practice, and costs. The practical difference is that the consumer menu prices the buyer's actual loss in a single case, while the real estate route pays the refund and prescribed interest through the Authority and sends compensation down a second track.

### Q: Which route is faster for a homebuyer?

Neither, reliably, and speed is the weakest ground on which to choose. Both systems were designed as summary, document-driven alternatives to the civil court, and the consumer statute directs in Section 38 that endeavour be made to decide a complaint within three months of the builder receiving notice where no testing or analysis is required. Actual disposal depends on the State, the bench strength of the particular Authority or commission, and the docket each carries, and it varies more within each system than between them. A buyer who chooses on relief and enforcement gets the forum that can award and collect what he came for at whatever pace his State delivers; a buyer who chooses on promised speed may arrive quickly at a forum unable to grant the compensation he spent years accumulating.

### Q: What deposit does an appeal require in each forum?

In the consumer route, a person ordered to pay who wishes to appeal must deposit fifty per cent of the amount ordered before the appeal is entertained, at each tier: District to State under Section 41, State to National under Section 51, and National to the Supreme Court under Section 67 of the 2019 Act, which removed the monetary caps the 1986 Act had set. In the real estate route, a promoter's appeal to the Appellate Tribunal under Section 43(5) is not entertained unless he deposits at least thirty per cent of any penalty, or such higher percentage as the Tribunal directs, or the total amount to be paid to the allottee including interest and compensation, before the appeal is heard; an allottee's own appeal carries no such condition. The Supreme Court upheld the real estate pre-deposit in Newtech Promoters.

### Q: How is an order enforced in each forum?

An order of the Authority for interest, penalty, compensation or refund is recovered as arrears of land revenue under Section 40: the Authority issues a recovery certificate and the district administration proceeds against the promoter's property in the revenue manner, without the buyer filing a separate execution case, but at administrative pace. A consumer commission's order is enforced under Section 71 of the 2019 Act in the same manner as a decree of a civil court, with the execution provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure applying, and Section 72 makes failure to comply an offence punishable with imprisonment for not less than one month and extending to three years, or a fine between twenty-five thousand rupees and one lakh rupees, or both, triable summarily by the commission itself. The first route collects through the revenue queue; the second coerces the person.

### Q: Can a homebuyer pursue both RERA and consumer remedies at once?

The forums are concurrent but the recovery is single. A buyer may invoke the Authority's regulatory supervision of the project and pursue his personal claim in one chosen forum, and the statutes' additional-remedies clauses keep both doors open at the threshold. What he may not do is run the same claim for the same refund and compensation to orders in both systems and recover twice, or re-litigate in the second forum a claim the first has decided. Parallel proceedings on the identical claim are liable to be stayed or dismissed when the duplication appears. The safe course is to elect the forum that matches the relief wanted, record the election, and use the other statute, if at all, only for a purpose the first proceeding does not cover.

### Q: Which route is better when the builder is insolvent?

Neither, once the insolvency process is admitted, and that is the point at which the comparison collapses. On admission of a corporate insolvency resolution process against the promoter, the moratorium under Section 14 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 halts proceedings before the Authority and the commissions and stays execution of orders already obtained. The buyer's remedy becomes a claim in the resolution process, where allottees rank as financial creditors, a status the Supreme Court upheld in Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Limited v. Union of India, and where recovery depends on the resolution plan. Before admission, the better course is to perfect the claim, assemble the agreement and payment record, and organise with fellow allottees, since the Code sets thresholds for allottees acting together and rewards a documented, verified claim over a forum victory that the moratorium freezes.

### Q: Where does an appeal lie in each route after the first order is passed?

In the real estate route, an appeal from the Authority or the adjudicating officer lies to the Real Estate Appellate Tribunal of the State, and from the Tribunal to the High Court under Section 58 on a substantial question of law, within sixty days. In the consumer route, appeals climb the commission hierarchy: from the District Commission to the State Commission under Section 41, from the State Commission to the National Commission under Section 51, where the appeal from an original order lies on a substantial question of law, and from the National Commission to the Supreme Court under Section 67. Each system also gives its commissions and tribunals powers of review and revision in defined cases. The consumer ladder has more rungs; the real estate ladder reaches a constitutional court one step sooner.

### Q: Can a buyer who purchased a flat for resale or a commercial purpose use either forum?

The consumer route may be closed to him, and the real estate route is not. The definition of consumer in Section 2(7) of the 2019 Act excludes a person who obtains services for a commercial purpose, subject to the exception for services used exclusively for earning a livelihood by self-employment, and builders routinely contest the status of investors who bought multiple units or bought to resell; the question is fact-specific, turning on the dominant purpose of the purchase. The real estate statute's allottee carries no such exclusion: the Act protects the allottee of a unit in a registered project whatever his motive for buying, because its concern is the regulated transaction and the project, not the buyer's purpose. A speculative purchaser is therefore a consumer-route litigant with a threshold fight on his hands and a real estate complainant without one.

### Q: Can a consumer complaint filed after two years still be heard?

Yes, if the commission condones the delay. Section 69(2) of the 2019 Act permits a complaint filed after the two-year period to be entertained where the complainant satisfies the commission that there was sufficient cause for not filing within time, and the commission must record its reasons for condoning the delay. Sufficient cause is judged on the facts: a builder's documented assurances that possession or a refund was imminent, correspondence that kept the buyer negotiating, illness, or the pendency of another proceeding pursued in good faith have all the makings of such a case, while mere inattention after the grievance had crystallised has not. Condonation is a discretion, not an entitlement, and it is exercised complaint by complaint; the buyer's protection is to plead the cause for the delay in the complaint itself, with the correspondence exhibited, rather than to treat the two years as a formality the forum will waive on request.

### Q: Why is compensation decided separately from a refund under the real estate statute?

Because the Act divides the work between two functionaries. The Authority, which registers and supervises the project, orders the refund and the interest under Section 18 and gives the regulatory directions, while Section 71 commits the adjudication of compensation, the assessment of what the buyer actually lost, to an adjudicating officer, who weighs the factors Section 72 lists: the disproportionate gain or unfair advantage the default produced, the amount of loss caused, the effect of repeated default, and whether the default was intentional. The design treats compensation as a judicial assessment requiring its own inquiry, distinct from the regulator's computation of refund and prescribed interest. The buyer's cost is procedural: one grievance, two proceedings, two orders, two appeals. A consumer commission suffers no such split, awarding refund, interest and compensation in a single Section 39 order.

### Q: Can a buyer file the complaint where she lives, or must she go where the project is located?

It depends on the forum, and the consumer statute changed the answer in the buyer's favour. Section 34(2) of the 2019 Act lets a complaint be instituted where the complainant resides or personally works for gain, as well as where the opposite party resides or carries on business or where the cause of action arises, so a buyer who booked in the city she has since left can file from home. The real estate route fixes the forum by the project: the complaint lies to the Authority of the State in which the project is registered, because that Authority holds the project's file, and there is no corresponding complainant-side choice. Buyers who have moved States after booking therefore find the consumer route geographically kinder, though the commission's orders still have to be executed where the builder and his assets are.

### Q: What happens to a complaint filed while the Consumer Protection Act 1986 was in force?

It continues under the old Act. The 2019 Act repealed the 1986 Act by Section 107 but with the standard savings: proceedings instituted under the repealed statute are not invalidated, complaints pending before the commissions continue and are decided under the law under which they were filed, and the change of statute does not reopen concluded matters. The practical consequences are real, because the two statutes differ in the respects this comparison turns on: a complaint filed in the 1986 period carries the old pecuniary test of value plus compensation claimed, the old capped appeal deposits, and the lower fine band for non-compliance under the old Section 27. Buyers and builders with long-running cases should identify which statute governs the complaint before arguing about thresholds, deposits or enforcement, since much published guidance silently assumes the 2019 text.

### Q: Can a buyer claim the rent paid for another home while waiting for possession?

Yes, and the two forums price it differently. Rent paid for substitute accommodation during the delay is a recognised head of loss in consumer proceedings: commissions assess it as part of the compensation for the builder's deficiency, on proof of the tenancy and the payments, together with interest the buyer carried on a loan for the undelivered flat and compensation for mental agony, subject to the discipline against compensating the same default twice under overlapping heads. In the real estate route, the Authority's own order gives refund or delay interest at the prescribed rate, which is computed without reference to the buyer's rent; the rent as such is compensation, and must be taken to the adjudicating officer under Section 71 to be assessed on the Section 72 factors. A buyer with years of documented rent will usually recover more of it through the commission's single assessment.

### Q: What fee does a buyer pay to file a complaint in each forum?

Both systems price entry low, deliberately. In the real estate route the complaint fee is prescribed by State rules and in the larger States is a modest fixed sum, commonly one thousand rupees, payable through the Authority's portal. In the consumer route the fee under the Consumer Protection (Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions) Rules, 2020 is scaled to the consideration paid: nothing where the consideration does not exceed five lakh rupees, two hundred rupees in the band above that, and rising slabs that reach seven thousand five hundred rupees for a complaint before the National Commission. Neither fee bears any relation to the court fee an ad valorem civil suit would demand on the same claim, which is part of the design of both statutes: the price of the door was set so that the buyer already out of pocket for the flat is not priced out of the remedy.

### Q: Can a group of buyers from the same project file a single complaint in each forum?

Yes, in both, by different doors. Section 35 of the 2019 Act permits a complaint by one or more consumers on behalf of numerous consumers having the same interest, with the permission of the commission, and recognised consumer associations may also complain; the leading National Commission cases on builder delay, including the group complaint behind Imperia Structures, were association and same-interest complaints of this kind. In the real estate route, a complaint under Section 31 may be filed by any aggrieved person, and associations of allottees, which the Act itself recognises and in places requires the promoter to facilitate, complain for their members as a matter of routine practice, quite apart from the association's separate statutory roles on transfer of the project and on completion after revocation. Group filing shares cost, presents the default as a pattern, and in the insolvency setting becomes essential rather than merely convenient.

### Q: Who can continue a buyer's complaint if the buyer dies before it is decided?

His legal heir or legal representative. The 2019 Act's definition of a complainant in Section 2(5) expressly includes, where the consumer is dead, his legal heir or legal representative, who may file the complaint or be brought on record in a pending one, and the commissions proceed on that footing without the claim abating. In the real estate route, the allottee's interest under the agreement for sale is itself a heritable and transferable interest, and the Authority entertains the complaint of the person who stands in the allottee's place on proof of succession or assignment, the promoter being in any event obliged to deal with the recorded allottee's successor for possession and conveyance. Families should place the death certificate and the succession or nomination papers with the case record at the first opportunity, since an unopposed substitution early is a formality and the same application late is an argument.

### Q: How is the interest on a refund worked out in each forum?

In the real estate route the rate is prescribed, not negotiated: the State rules fix it, in the larger States commonly by reference to the State Bank of India's highest marginal cost of lending rate plus two per cent, and it runs on the amount the promoter received, for the refund case from the payments and for the staying allottee month by month until possession is handed over. The buyer need prove no loss to earn it and cannot argue it upward. In the consumer route the Act fixes no rate; the commission decides the interest on the refund as part of its Section 39 order, on the facts of the case, and the Supreme Court's own awards in the builder line have settled at figures such as nine per cent a year in the cases it decided. Formula in one forum, assessment in the other: predictable and capped in the first, argued and variable in the second.

### Q: Can a buyer ask for the flat and, in the alternative, a refund in the same complaint?

Yes. Buyers routinely plead in the alternative, possession with compensation for the delay or, if the builder cannot or does not deliver within the time the forum fixes, refund of the amount paid with interest and compensation, and both the commissions and the Authority mould the final order to the position reached at the hearing rather than holding the buyer to an election made in ignorance at the filing. Framing the alternative matters, because an unqualified prayer for refund reads as an exit the builder may accept with relief, while an unqualified prayer for possession abandons the exit if the project then collapses. What the forum will not permit is both satisfactions at once: the flat and the price of the flat are alternatives in substance as much as in pleading, and the order will give one or the other, with compensation for the delay capable of accompanying either.
