Agriculture legislation is the cluster in which the civil services examination most reliably separates the candidate who understands the Constitution from the candidate who has memorised a controversy. The questions arrive dressed as economy questions, carrying the vocabulary of prices, procurement and markets, and the candidate who answers them in that vocabulary alone writes a competent newspaper paragraph and scores in the middle of the field. The candidate who scores is the one who sees that nearly every agriculture question is, underneath the dressing, a question about who holds the power to act: which legislature may enact the rule, which entry of the Seventh Schedule authorises it, which institution administers it, and which level of government pays for it. Agriculture is a state subject that the Union influences constantly and commands rarely, and the examination is built to detect whether the candidate knows the difference.

![Agriculture legislation study guide for UPSC, mapping the Seventh Schedule entries to the statute set and the answer method - Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-116.webp)

That single recognition reorganises the whole subject. The statute list stops being a pile of Acts to memorise and becomes the working record of a constitutional division of labour: marketing law belongs to the states because the entries say so, food distribution law belongs to both levels because a different entry says so, and the support price belongs to neither legislature in statutory form because no legislature has enacted it. An answer built on that record reads as analysis. An answer built on the merits of a policy, however well informed, reads as opinion, and opinion is the one currency the mains examination does not accept. This guide works through the cluster the way a teacher would build it for a serious aspirant: the syllabus map first, then the constitutional entries that generate the questions, then the statute set with its names and years fixed accurately, then the four moments that organise the field in memory, then the institutions candidates confuse, the support price correction, the confusions that cost marks, the shapes in which questions are framed, a worked approach to a typical demand, the technique for writing on the farm laws episode without either partisanship or evasion, the evidence discipline, and a revision structure that holds the whole cluster together.

## What agriculture legislation questions are actually testing

The syllabus mapping is the first thing to get right, because agriculture legislation sits across two papers and is examined differently in each. In the polity and governance paper, the subject enters through the federal division of powers, through the working of welfare entitlements, and through the institutions of economic governance. In the economy paper, it enters through agricultural marketing, price support, the public distribution system, food security, land and credit questions, and the economics of subsidies. The same statute can therefore be examined twice in two registers: the National Food Security Act as a governance question about rights, identification of beneficiaries and grievance machinery, and the same Act as an economy question about fiscal cost, targeting and leakage. The candidate who prepares the statute once, in one register, is prepared for only half of its examination life.

The preliminary examination tests the cluster at the level of fact and identity. The demand there is recognition: which entry of which list covers a subject, which body performs which function, which statute is in force and which has been repealed, which commission recommends and which corporation executes. Errors at this level are unforgiving because they are usually errors of identity, and the cluster is unusually rich in identities that resemble each other: an advisory commission and a procurement corporation, a model Act and an enacted Act, a statute and an executive arrangement that shares its vocabulary. The mains examination tests the same material at the level of judgement. The demand there is structured evaluation: a reform proposal to be assessed against the division of powers, an institution to be appraised against its mandate, an episode to be explained through its process rather than its slogans. The candidate who prepares only the factual layer meets the mains paper with a memorised list and no method; the candidate who prepares only opinions meets the preliminary paper with attitudes and no anchors.

Three consequences follow for preparation. First, the entries come before the statutes, because the entries explain why the statutes exist in the form they do and predict where a reform proposal will run into constitutional resistance. Secondly, every statute must be learned with its status attached, in force, amended or repealed, because the cluster contains a complete ordinance-to-repeal arc within a span of seventeen months and preparation material that treats repealed law as living law is a documented source of error. Thirdly, the politically charged material must be prepared as process rather than as allegiance: the examinable content of the farm laws episode is the ordinance route, the competence claim, the stay, the committee and the repeal, and a candidate who can narrate that sequence with both cases stated fairly owns the episode in a way that a candidate with a strong view and a weak chronology does not.

One further feature of the map deserves emphasis before the entries themselves. The cluster's statutes are old, and the examination knows it: the control statute predates the Republic's second decade, the seed statute belongs to the 1960s, and the marketing settlement is older than most of the candidates. Antiquity is not a defect in the material; it is the material, since the paper tests whether the candidate can read a field whose law was laid down for scarcity and is being asked, in question after question, to serve an economy of surplus and movement.

## The constitutional spine: cite the entry, not the slogan

The namable claim of this guide, and the spine of every strong answer in the cluster, is that agriculture questions are marked on whether the candidate identifies who has the power to act. The examiner is testing whether the candidate understands that most agricultural reform in India is a state matter which the Union can influence but not command. An answer that names the entry and reasons from it outscores an answer that recites the merits of the policy, because the entry is where the marks are and the merits are where every candidate sounds alike.

The entries themselves must be held cold. Entry 14 of the State List gives the states agriculture, including agricultural education and research, protection against pests and prevention of plant diseases. Entry 28 of the State List gives the states markets and fairs, which is the constitutional home of the regulated market yard and of every state marketing statute. Entry 18 of the State List covers land, land tenures and the transfer of agricultural land, which is why tenancy and land reform are state territory. Entry 30 of the State List covers money-lending and the relief of agricultural indebtedness, which is why agricultural credit has a state law dimension alongside the banking system. Entries 26 and 27 of the State List give the states trade and commerce within the state and the production, supply and distribution of goods, but both are expressly made subject to Entry 33 of the Concurrent List, and that subordination is one of the most examinable textual details in the whole cluster.

Entry 33 of the Concurrent List is the Union's principal door into the field. It covers trade and commerce in, and the production, supply and distribution of, foodstuffs including edible oilseeds and oils, cattle fodder, raw cotton and raw jute, together with the products of industries whose control the Union has declared expedient in the public interest. Entry 34 of the Concurrent List adds price control. These two entries are the basis of the essential commodities power, and Entry 33 was the claimed basis of the Union's 2020 trade legislation, a claim the courts were never required to decide. Entry 42 of the Union List gives Parliament inter-state trade and commerce, which supports the argument that produce moving across state lines is a legitimate Union concern even where the market in which it is sold is a state institution. Article 246 distributes the field between the lists, Article 254 resolves repugnancy when Union and state laws collide in the concurrent field, with the state law able to prevail within that state if it has been reserved for and received the assent of the President, and Article 252 allows Parliament to legislate on a state subject for states that consent by resolution. The full treatment of the lists themselves belongs to the series' dedicated study of the Seventh Schedule entries, and the candidate should read this cluster alongside [the Seventh Schedule lists explained](/2018/09/15/seventh-schedule-lists-explained/), because the entries are the ground on which every statute in the cluster stands.

### Can Parliament repeal a state agriculture produce marketing Act?

No. A marketing Act is state legislation on state entries, and Parliament cannot repeal it. Parliament can legislate over that ground where a Concurrent or Union entry reaches it, persuade states to adopt a model law, attach conditions to schemes, or use the consent route in Article 252, but the state Act falls to the state legislature alone.

The remaining routes deserve a paragraph each in the candidate's notes, because questions are set on them. Article 249 lets the Rajya Sabha, by a resolution supported by two-thirds of members present and voting, declare it necessary in the national interest that Parliament legislate on a state subject, for a year at a time; it has never been the vehicle of agricultural marketing reform, and its examinable feature is precisely that disuse, since it shows how heavy the constitutional machinery for overriding the state field is designed to be. Article 252 is the consent route, Parliament legislating for states whose legislatures resolve to invite it, the route by which the water pollution statute entered a state field in the following cluster of this series; in agriculture it stands as the unused alternative whose existence indicts or excuses the ordinance route of 2020, depending on the answer's argument. Article 254 is the collision rule rather than a route: where a state law on a concurrent subject conflicts with a Union law, the Union law prevails, but a state law reserved for the consideration of the President and assented to prevails in that state, which is why assent, sought and withheld, became a battlefield after 2020 when Punjab and other states legislated against the Union farm laws from within their own field. The candidate who holds all four provisions can answer any federalism question the cluster can generate, because every such question is, in the end, a question about which of these doors the Union used, tried to use, or declined to use.

The distinction in that answer, between legislating over a subject and repealing the law that governs it, is exactly the distinction the 2020 episode tested. The Union legislated on trade areas and contract farming by resting on the Concurrent and Union entries; it did not and could not amend the state marketing Acts, which continued to govern the market yards throughout. States responded inside the same constitutional grammar: Punjab passed its own amendment Bills to blunt the Union laws within the state and sought the assent of the President under the repugnancy route, and the assent was not forthcoming, which is itself a complete examination point about how federal disagreement is processed in this field. A candidate who can move between the entry, the statute and the assent mechanism in a single paragraph is writing at the level the paper rewards.

## The statute set: names and years to hold cold

The statute set is short enough to be learned exactly and important enough that approximations are penalised. The candidate should be able to produce, from memory, the name, the year, the level of government, and the status of each statute in the set, and should treat any preparation material that cannot do the same as unreliable.

The Essential Commodities Act, 1955, Act 10 of 1955, is the Union's standing instrument of control over production, supply and distribution, resting on the Concurrent entries described above. Its operative heart is the power to issue control orders regulating or prohibiting the production, supply and distribution of essential commodities, and its schedule of commodities was recast in the liberalisation era: an amendment of 2006 placed the declaration of essential commodities on a statutory footing, and the schedule that emerged was a fraction of the long lists administered in the control era. The Act matters in the examination less for its detail than for its function, as the standing evidence that the Union's foodstuffs power is concurrent, exercisable, and routinely used for stock limits and movement questions.

State marketing law is the second element, and its defining feature is the absence of a central Act. Agricultural marketing is governed by state statutes enacted under the markets and fairs entry, of which the Maharashtra Act of the early 1960s, the Karnataka Act of 1966 and the Punjab marketing framework are the worked examples in this series. Over that state settlement the Centre has laid successive model laws, the model marketing Act of 2003 and the model agricultural produce and livestock marketing law released in April 2017, which states may adopt, adapt or ignore, and the electronic national agriculture market launched in April 2016, which links existing market yards without displacing the state statutes beneath them. The examinable point is the pattern itself: in marketing, the Union proposes and the states dispose, and a candidate who writes of the APMC Act as though it were a single central statute has misunderstood the field at its foundation.

The three enactments of 2020 and their repeal form the third element, and they must be learned as a closed episode with its status settled. The ordinances came first, in June 2020. Parliament then enacted the Farmers (Empowerment and Protection) Agreement on Price Assurance and Farm Services Act, 2020, Act 20 of 2020, on contract farming; the Farmers' Produce Trade and Commerce (Promotion and Facilitation) Act, 2020, Act 21 of 2020, on trade outside the regulated yards; and the Essential Commodities (Amendment) Act, 2020, Act 22 of 2020, which confined stock limits on the principal foodstuffs to extraordinary circumstances. The two principal Acts received the assent of the President on 24 September 2020 and the amendment Act on 26 September 2020, with publication following on 27 September. In January 2021 the Supreme Court stayed their implementation. In November 2021 the government announced repeal, and the Farm Laws Repeal Act, 2021, Act 40 of 2021, which received assent on 30 November 2021, repealed all three and removed the 2020 insertion from the essential commodities statute. The detailed passage record is carried in [the farm laws 2020 explained article](/2014/04/01/farm-laws-2020-explained/) and the protest and process critique in [the farmers' protest legislative lessons article](/2014/05/01/farmers-protest-legislative-lessons/), and this guide deliberately teaches the episode at the level of what an answer needs rather than retelling the whole story.

### Are the three farm laws of 2020 still in force?

No. All three were repealed by the Farm Laws Repeal Act, 2021, passed that November after the government announced the decision, without a substantive debate on the merits. The essential commodities amendment fell with them, so the parent Act of 1955 stands in its earlier form. Any answer discussing the three laws as living law is out of date.

The National Food Security Act, 2013, Act 20 of 2013, is the fourth element and the cluster's one great entitlement statute. It received assent on 10 September 2013 and was deemed to be in force from 5 July 2013, the date of the ordinance it replaced. It converted the public distribution system from an administrative scheme into a legal entitlement covering up to seventy-five per cent of the rural population and fifty per cent of the urban population, with grain entitlements per person for priority households, a larger household entitlement for the poorest Antyodaya households, issue prices set in its first schedule, and the eldest woman of the household named as its head for the ration card. Around the grain entitlement it placed nutritional entitlements for children and pregnant and lactating mothers, a food security allowance when the entitlement is not supplied, and grievance machinery running from district officers to state food commissions.

A second rank of instruments surrounds the core set and should be recognised when named, even if the candidate would not volunteer them. The Food Corporations Act, 1964, already met among the institutions, is a statute of the set in its own right. The Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act, 2007 carries the warehouse receipt. The Agricultural Produce (Grading and Marking) Act, 1937, the colonial-era statute behind the AGMARK grade, survives as the quality mark's legal root and appears in the paper as a recognition item. The control orders issued under the essential commodities statute, on sugarcane, on fertiliser movement and pricing, on seed after the Seed (Control) Order of 1983 brought seed within the essential commodities framework, form a layer of delegated legislation beneath the Acts whose existence the candidate should know even where its detail is left to the specialist articles. And the state Acts on tenancy, ceilings and money-lending, too various to name as a set, complete the field on the land and credit side. The discipline for the second rank is recognition without rehearsal: know what each instrument is and whose it is, and spend the saved effort on the core six.

The seed statutes complete the set. The Seeds Act, 1966, Act 54 of 1966, is the quality statute: it works through notification of kinds and varieties, minimum limits of germination and purity, labelling of seed offered for sale, and a certification machinery of agencies and laboratories, with penalties that belong unmistakably to its decade. The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001, Act 53 of 2001, is the rights statute: it created a registry and an Authority for plant varieties, conferred exclusive rights on breeders for a defined term, and placed alongside them a set of farmers' rights, including the farmer's privilege to save, use, sow, exchange and sell farm produce of a protected variety while being barred from selling it as branded seed, and a compensation route where a registered variety fails to perform as claimed. The two statutes teach opposite lessons about the state's posture toward the seed, control of quality in the first and allocation of rights in the second, and examiners use them accordingly.

The set should be practised as a recitation with status attached, in a fixed order that mirrors the field's logic rather than its chronology: the control statute of 1955, the marketing settlement of the states with its models of 2003 and 2017, the closed episode of 2020 to 2021, the entitlement of 2013, and the seed pair of 1966 and 2001. Recited in that order, the set tells the field's story by itself: a Union power held in reserve, a state field the Union may only persuade, an attempt to break that limit and its reversal, a right built where the entries allowed one, and a technology governed by splitting quality from property. A candidate who can recite it in that order, with a year and a status for every member, has the cluster's facts in their final form; everything else in this guide is the method for spending them.

## The statutes in answer form

Knowing the set is the recognition layer. The mains layer is knowing what to say about each statute when it appears inside a larger question, and the formulations below are the ones a teacher would drill.

On the Essential Commodities Act, the answer-ready formulation is that it is a power held in reserve. The Act does not administer prices in ordinary times; it authorises the Union and, by delegation, the states to intervene in production, supply, distribution and trade of scheduled commodities when the government forms the opinion that intervention is necessary in the public interest, through control orders that can fix prices, compel sale to the state, licence dealers, limit stocks and restrict movement. The candidate's points are the breadth of the delegation, the contested history of stock limits as an anti-hoarding instrument that can also deter the storage a modern supply chain needs, the use of the Act against onions, pulses and edible oils in price spikes, and the significance of the 2020 amendment's attempt to confine stock limits on the principal foodstuffs to war, famine, extraordinary price rise and grave natural calamity, an attempt the repeal withdrew. The Act is the cluster's standing lesson that a statute can matter most in the years it is barely used, because its existence disciplines the market it might enter.

On state marketing law, the formulation is that the yard is a legal monopoly with a service attached. The state Acts made the notified market yard the compulsory first point of sale for listed produce, licensed the intermediaries who operate there, and financed the system by a fee on the transaction, to which commission and other charges are added in the high-levy states until the stack takes a significant share of the price the farmer receives. The answer-ready analysis names the monopoly's three faces: its protective face, an open auction, standard weighing and a payment obligation in a place the farmer can reach; its extractive face, the licensed intermediary's rent and the political economy of the market committee; and its fiscal face, the dependence of procurement operations on the yard's infrastructure, which is why the Union's procurement in the surplus states runs through the very institution its reform documents criticise. The Bihar experiment of 2006, repealing the state Act outright, and the incomplete spread of the single licence, the private market and the direct purchase centre under the model laws, supply the evidence section, with the assessments kept in their contested state.

On the National Food Security Act, the formulation is entitlement with an implementation shadow. The candidate states the entitlement exactly: coverage up to seventy-five per cent of the rural and fifty per cent of the urban population, five kilograms of grain per person per month for priority households at the scheduled prices of three, two and one rupees for rice, wheat and coarse grains, thirty-five kilograms per household for Antyodaya households, nutritional entitlements and a maternity benefit for women, the eldest woman as head of household, the food security allowance when the grain is not supplied, and the commissions and grievance officers who police the whole. Then the shadow: identification of households from census data that ages, states that built the machinery at different speeds, the portability problem of the migrant whose ration card belonged to a village he had left, answered in part by the one nation one ration card portability built on the computerised delivery system, and the conversion of the entitlement to free grain in the period after the pandemic, which changed the fiscal arithmetic and the politics of the Act without amending its text. An answer that pairs the exact entitlement with the honest shadow is complete in a way that neither a celebration nor a lament is.

On the seed statutes, the formulation is the two halves of a settlement. The Seeds Act of 1966 governs the seed as goods: notified kinds and varieties, minimum germination and purity, truthful labelling, certification for those who seek it, inspectors who may sample and analysts who may test, and penalties mild by modern standards. Its examinable weakness is the gap between certified seed, which passes through the certification machinery, and truthfully labelled seed, which the producer vouches for himself, since most seed sold moves in the second channel and the Act's quality promise is correspondingly uneven. The plant varieties Act of 2001 governs the seed as property: registration on criteria of novelty, distinctiveness, uniformity and stability, the breeder's exclusive right for a term of years, the researcher's exemption, the farmer's privilege and compensation claim, benefit sharing into a national gene fund, and compulsory licensing when the public need for seed is unmet. The PepsiCo episode belongs here in an answer as the working illustration, and the stronger answers add the international frame in one line: the Act is India's sui generis answer to the obligation to protect plant varieties, chosen over the international union model precisely to keep the farmer's half of the settlement inside the statute.

## Four moments that organise the field

A chronology of the cluster runs to dozens of dates and cannot be carried into an examination hall. Four moments can. The candidate who holds them can place any statute, episode or institution in the cluster without a timeline, because each moment explains the layer of law it created.

The first moment is the middle of the 1960s, when the procurement system was built in a few years of scarcity-driven legislation and institution-making. The setting matters for the answer: two successive drought years, a food economy dependent on imported grain arriving under concessional arrangements from the United States, and a political class that had concluded that dependence on foreign harvests was a strategic vulnerability. The response was a package rather than a policy: new seed varieties and the agronomy that went with them, a price announced in advance to make planting the new varieties rational, a corporation to buy and hold the grain the price elicited, state market yards to organise the sale, and a seed law to police the quality of the input on which the whole design rested. The Green Revolution is the name the package acquired, and the candidate should notice that its legislative skeleton is the first moment of this guide: every institution the cluster examines was drawn in those years, and later law amended the drawing without replacing it. The Agricultural Prices Commission was constituted in January 1965 to advise on price support, and the Food Corporation of India began functioning in the same month under its Act of 1964, giving the Union a buyer, a storekeeper and a distributor of grain. State marketing statutes spread across the same decade, creating the regulated yards through which the procured grain would move. The Seeds Act of 1966 belongs to this moment too, as the legal companion of the new seed varieties whose spread the decade demanded. The support price, the corporation, the market yard and the seed law are one settlement, and an answer that treats them as one settlement, born of shortage and built for procurement, has understood the cluster's foundation.

The second moment is the liberalisation turn from 1991 onward, when the control apparatus built in scarcity was progressively pruned. Its agricultural expression was the contraction of the essential commodities regime: commodities were removed from control in waves, and the recasting of the schedule by the amendment of 2006 fixed a short statutory list where a sprawling administrative one had stood. The examinable point is direction rather than detail. The decades after 1991 did not repeal the control statute; they narrowed its reach while leaving the power intact, which is why the Act could be reactivated for stock limits whenever prices rose, and why the argument in 2020 was about confining its use rather than abolishing it.

The third moment is 2013, when food became a statutory entitlement. The significance of the National Food Security Act for examination purposes is not its grain quantities but its legal form: a benefit that had been delivered as policy became a right that could be demanded, with named beneficiaries, named quantities, a named price, an allowance when the state failed to supply, and commissions to hear complaints. That change of form is what connects the Act to the right to food litigation that preceded it and to the governance questions that follow it, and a candidate who writes about the Act as a bigger subsidy has missed the point the examiner is marking.

The third moment carries a procedural detail worth isolating, because it foreshadows the fourth. The Act of 2013 began as an ordinance in July of that year, and its statutory commencement was backdated to the ordinance's date, so that the cluster's two most consequential modern statutes both entered the world by the ordinance door, one to be ratified into permanence and one to be repealed within seventeen months. The comparison is a ready-made examination point on the ordinance power itself: the same instrument, used for a welfare entitlement with a decade of litigation and committee work behind it and for a market redesign with neither, and the candidate who can run that comparison has connected the agriculture cluster to the process articles of the series without leaving the evidence.

The fourth moment is the arc from 2020 to 2021, the ordinance-to-repeal sequence that dominates the cluster's politics. Its examinable content is process: legislation by ordinance on a subject the states regarded as theirs, a competence claim resting on the Concurrent and Union entries that was never judicially tested, passage in the upper House by voice vote amid a disputed demand for division, a year of encampment at the capital's borders, a judicial stay, and a repeal carried through Parliament with the same speed and the same absence of debate as the enactment. The candidate who holds the arc as a process story can answer questions on ordinances, on federalism, on parliamentary procedure and on judicial review from a single prepared episode, which is why this moment earns its place among the four.

## The judicial anchors: three cases and what each is for

The case load of this cluster is deliberately small, and the discipline the brief of any good teacher imposes is the one the examiners reward: in these papers a case is an illustration of a principle, and the candidate who states the principle and names the case scores, while the candidate who narrates facts at length does not. Three cases carry the cluster, and each has a defined job.

The right to food litigation is the first. The People's Union for Civil Liberties petition, begun in 2001 in the setting of drought and starvation deaths alongside overflowing public granaries, asked the Supreme Court to enforce the right to food as an aspect of the right to life in Article 21. The Court never delivered a single concluding judgment that closed the matter; instead it governed by interim orders over years, converting the government's own food and employment schemes into enforceable entitlements, directing that cooked midday meals be provided in primary schools, appointing commissioners to report on compliance, and keeping the case alive as a continuing supervision. Its examination use is precise: it is the authority for the proposition that a welfare scheme can harden into a legal entitlement under judicial pressure, and it is the background against which the Act of 2013 was later written as legislation doing deliberately what the orders had done provisionally. An answer on food security that opens with the litigation and arrives at the statute has its genealogy right; an answer that treats the Act of 2013 as an uncaused act of generosity has started the story in the middle.

The stay of the farm laws is the second anchor, and its job is to illustrate the difference between judicial review entertained and judicial review completed. The challenges to the three Acts of 2020 raised the competence question in its purest modern form: whether the trade and contract farming laws fell within the Concurrent and Union entries the Union claimed, or whether they trenched on the state field of agriculture and markets. The Supreme Court's order of 12 January 2021 in the Rakesh Vaishnav proceedings stayed the laws and constituted a committee, and the repeal of the laws in November of that year ended the litigation without a decision. The principle the case illustrates is therefore double: the extraordinary character of staying enacted legislation, and the structural fact that repeal can moot a constitutional question that the political process could not settle, leaving the law of the entries exactly where it stood before the episode began. Candidates should be able to say both halves, because the paper tests the second as often as the first.

The plant variety litigation is the third anchor, and its job is to make the breeder-farmer tension concrete. The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act gives the breeder an exclusive right and gives the farmer a protected privilege over the same seed, and the PepsiCo litigation tested the boundary in public: the company's Indian arm sued Gujarati farmers over its registered potato variety FL 2027, withdrew the suits in 2019 after political and public pressure, and then lost the registration itself when the Authority revoked it in December 2021 on grounds that included defects in the application. The Delhi High Court's intervention in the years that followed, setting aside the revocation at the division bench stage in 2024, and the Supreme Court's refusal to interfere with that course in 2026, left the episode where most real litigation ends, in procedural restoration rather than in a grand ruling. For the examination the principle is the one the statute's design announces: India built a sui generis system under its international obligations that refuses to choose between breeder and farmer, and the litigation is the working demonstration of the friction that refusal guarantees.

## The institutions candidates confuse

No confusion in the cluster costs more marks than the confusion of institutions, because the mains paper asks institutional questions directly and the preliminary paper asks them by identity. Four bodies must be kept apart with complete clarity.

The Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices is an advisory body attached to the agriculture ministry. It began in January 1965 as the Agricultural Prices Commission and took its present name in 1985. It is not a statutory body: no Act creates it, no Act empowers it, and its function is to recommend support prices after studying costs of production, demand and supply, price trends and the terms of trade between agriculture and the rest of the economy. It recommends; it does not decide, announce or buy. The decision on a recommended price rests with the government, which is free to accept, raise or decline it, and that gap between recommendation and decision is itself an examinable feature of the arrangement.

The Food Corporation of India is the opposite kind of body: a statutory corporation created by the Food Corporations Act, 1964, and functioning from January 1965. It is the Union's procurement and distribution arm, the agency that buys grain at the support price through the state machinery, holds the central pool stocks, moves grain across the country and issues it to states for the distribution system. Where the Commission advises on price, the Corporation acts on price; where the Commission has no field presence at all, the Corporation's operations run through state agencies, market yards and storage depots in every procuring state. An answer that sends a recommendation to the Corporation or a purchase order to the Commission has failed an identity test that the examiner set deliberately.

### What is the difference between the Food Corporation of India and the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices?

The Commission is a non-statutory advisory body that recommends support prices on costs and markets. The Corporation is a statutory body under a 1964 Act that procures, stores and distributes grain at the prices the government decides. One advises on the price, the other trades at it, and confusing the two is the commonest institutional error in the cluster.

Two further institutions complete the map and appear in better answers as supporting detail. The state food commissions created by the Act of 2013 are the entitlement's own watchdog inside each state, charged with monitoring the Act's working and hearing appeals against the district grievance officers, and the unevenness with which states constituted them and filled their benches is the standard evidence for the implementation shadow that follows the Act in every serious assessment. On the marketing side, the Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority, created by the Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act, 2007, administers the negotiable warehouse receipt, the instrument meant to let a farmer store produce, borrow against the receipt and sell when the price suits rather than at the harvest's distress moment; its electronic receipts belong in marketing answers as the storage half of the reform design, the half that attacks the forced sale rather than the place of sale.

The agriculture ministry itself is the third actor, and its role is distinct from both: it owns the policy, carries the legislation, answers in Parliament and administers the schemes that neither the Commission nor the Corporation owns, from the price support scheme for crops the Corporation does not handle to the deficiency payment and private procurement pilots run under the price support umbrella. Below the Union level sit the state institutions that the state statutes created: the agricultural produce market committees that run the regulated yards and collect the market fee, the state marketing boards above them, and the state food and civil supplies machinery that lifts grain from the central pool and runs the fair price shops. The plant varieties Authority under the Act of 2001 and the seed certification agencies under the Act of 1966 complete the map on the technology side. A candidate who can walk a quintal of wheat through this institutional chain, from a recommended price to a decided price to a purchased, stored and issued stock, owns the descriptive half of the cluster.

## The support price question, taught properly

The support price is the single most examinable correction in the whole subject, and the candidate should treat it as such. Preparation material across the market describes the minimum support price as though it were a statutory entitlement, a price guaranteed by law that a farmer can claim by legal right. It is not. No statute creates the support price, no statute fixes it, and no farmer can sue for it. It is an executive arrangement: an advisory commission studies costs and recommends, the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs decides, and the government announces a price at which its agencies stand ready to buy specified produce of fair average quality. Its legal character is that of a policy commitment backed by procurement machinery, and everything examinable about it flows from that character.

The mechanics an answer needs are few and precise. Support prices are announced for twenty-two mandated crops, with a separate fair and remunerative price for sugarcane that is legally distinct and rests on the sugar control framework rather than on the announcement system. The Commission builds its recommendation on cost concepts that the candidate should know by name and relation: the paid-out costs of cultivation, those costs together with the imputed value of family labour, and the comprehensive cost that adds the rental value of owned land and interest on owned capital. Which of those concepts the announced price is measured against is the substance of the longest-running argument in the field. The National Commission on Farmers, chaired by M. S. Swaminathan, recommended in its reports of 2004 to 2006 that the support price be fixed at least fifty per cent above the comprehensive cost. The government announced in the budget of 2018 a price of one and a half times the cost of production, and the Cabinet Committee approved kharif prices on that basis in July 2018, measuring cost on the narrower concept that counts paid-out costs and family labour but not the rental value of land. Swaminathan himself welcomed the step while recording that it fell below the level his commission had recommended. An answer that can state both positions, name the cost concept each rests on, and identify the difference as a difference of definition rather than of arithmetic is an answer that has mastered the topic.

The demand for a legal guarantee is the live form of the question, and it must be answered with the same precision. The protesting unions demanded that the support price be converted into a statutory entitlement, and after the repeal of the farm laws the government constituted a committee on the subject by a notification of July 2022, chaired by the former agriculture secretary Sanjay Agrawal, to examine the support price system and related questions; the Samyukt Kisan Morcha declined to nominate members to it, and no legislation giving the price a statutory form followed. The full analysis of what a guarantee would have to decide belongs to [the MSP and procurement legal framework article](/2014/07/01/msp-procurement-legal-framework-india/), which owns the price question in this series; the study point for the aspirant is narrower. The examiner who asks about a statutory support price is asking whether the candidate can see that the proposal changes the legal species of the thing, from an announced price supported by a standing offer to buy, into a right whose breach would need a remedy, a defendant and a fiscal bearer, and that most answers fail because they debate the generosity of the price instead of the character of the instrument.

Sugarcane stands apart inside the price system and earns its own paragraph in preparation, because it is the standing counterexample that proves the support price analysis. The cane price is not announced into existence as a policy offer. The fair and remunerative price is fixed by the Union government under the Sugarcane (Control) Order of 1966, an instrument of delegated legislation issued under the control order power of the essential commodities statute, and sugar mills are legally obliged to pay it for the cane they purchase, with interest consequences for delay and a recovery machinery behind the obligation. Several cane-growing states have historically announced their own higher state advised prices above the Union floor, which mills in those states must then pay, adding a federal layer to the price itself. The Rangarajan committee on the sugar sector recommended moving the arrangement toward a revenue-sharing formula between farmers and mills, and the recommendation's partial career is a tidy study in why price design and price level are different arguments. For the examination the point is the contrast in legal species: put cane beside wheat in an answer and the candidate can show, from inside the same sector, the difference between a price the state must enforce because law commands it and a price the state supports because policy announces it, which is the distinction the whole support price topic exists to teach.

One paragraph of a support price answer does more work than the rest, and it is worth constructing deliberately. It is the paragraph in which the candidate converts the topic from prices to instruments. It runs, in substance, as follows in any competent answer: the support price system protects the farmer not by commanding the market but by entering it, since the state's promise is a standing offer to buy at the announced price through its procurement agencies, effective only where and when procurement actually operates, which is why the same announced price is a floor under wheat in Punjab and a rumour in a state without procurement centres. From that single observation the answer can generate its analysis unaided: the geographic concentration of benefit, the cropping distortion toward the procured cereals, the fiscal exposure when offtake lags procurement, and the reason the guarantee demand arises precisely where the standing offer does not reach. A candidate who can write that paragraph has understood that the support price is a market intervention wearing the clothes of a price announcement, and the examiner reading it knows the difference at once.

## The fiscal dimension: reading the subsidy argument without figures

A large share of mains questions in the cluster are, beneath their agricultural surface, public finance questions, and the candidate needs the structure of the fiscal argument even where the figures themselves are left as categories. The food subsidy that the Union budget carries is not the support price under another name. It is the difference between the economic cost of grain to the Food Corporation and the price at which the grain is issued, together with the carrying cost of the stocks held between purchase and issue. The economic cost itself is a composite: the price paid to the farmer, the incidentals of procurement including the statutory levies and commissions of the marketing system, and the costs of storage, movement and distribution. Each element of that composite answers to a different institution and a different examination point, and an answer that can decompose the subsidy in this way can discuss its reform intelligently, because every serious reform proposal, from the Shanta Kumar committee's recommendations on the Corporation to the case for cash transfers in place of grain, is a proposal about one element of the composite rather than about the whole.

Two fiscal mechanisms deserve named places in the candidate's preparation. Decentralised procurement, under which some states procure, store and distribute grain for the central system within their own borders instead of the Corporation doing so, is the fiscal face of the federal argument: it shortens the movement chain, ties procurement to the state's own distribution, and appears in answers as the working example of cooperative operation inside a central entitlement. The gap between procurement and offtake, between what the system buys at the announced price and what entitled households lift at the issue end, is the second: it is where buffer stocks accumulate beyond their norms, where carrying costs grow, and where the argument about open-ended procurement of rice and wheat in a handful of surplus states acquires its fiscal edge. The examiner does not need the candidate's numbers for any of this. The examiner needs to see that the candidate knows which quantities exist, what drives them, and which committee, survey or audit is the natural source for each, which is the evidence discipline of this guide applied to the finance ministry's side of the field.

## The confusions that cost marks

Beyond the institutional confusion, the cluster carries a stable set of errors that preparation material itself transmits, and a teacher preparing candidates for this subject spends as much time removing them as adding knowledge. The candidate should be able to recognise each error on sight.

The first is the statutory support price, dealt with above: the claim that the minimum support price is guaranteed by law. It appears in coaching notes, in popular writing and in the demands of protest movements phrased as though the guarantee existed and had been breached, and it is wrong in a way that reorganises an answer once corrected. The second is the single central marketing Act: the assumption that one APMC Act governs India's markets. There is no such Act. There are state Acts of different decades and designs, two central model laws and a digital platform above them, and the difference between those instruments is the difference between command and persuasion in a federal system. The third is the living farm law: preparation cycles are slow, and material printed during the seventeen months when the three Acts of 2020 were on the statute book continued to circulate after their repeal, so that candidates describe trade areas, farming agreements and the amended stock limit regime as current law. The repeal is itself part of the syllabus of the episode, and an answer that omits it advertises its own staleness.

The fourth error is the survivor amendment: the belief that the essential commodities amendment of 2020, having been enacted, remains available to describe. It does not; the repeal Act removed its operative insertion from the parent Act, and the stock limit power stands where it stood before 2020. The fifth error is the price-charge assumption in food security: the first schedule of the Act of 2013 states issue prices, and candidates recite them as the prices beneficiaries pay, without the later decision, recorded in this series' food security articles, to supply the Act's grain free of cost for a defined period beginning in January 2023, a change in the delivered reality of the entitlement that a governance answer is expected to register. The sixth error is the undated figure: leakage percentages, procurement shares and subsidy totals quoted without a source, a period or a method, in a field where the estimates are famously method-dependent and rival studies place the same quantity far apart. The seventh error is the collapsed institution, the treatment of the Commission, the Corporation, the ministry and the market committee as interchangeable names for the government, when each has a distinct legal character and the paper tests exactly those distinctions.

Three more errors complete the standard set. The eighth is the model Act mistaken for law: the candidate who writes that the model marketing Act of 2003 reformed India's markets has credited a proposal with the force of an enactment, when the model bound no one until a state legislature adopted its provisions, which states did unevenly and on their own timetable. The ninth is the sugarcane conflation: the fair and remunerative price for sugarcane is not the support price of a twenty-third crop but a distinct statutory price under the sugar control framework, binding on sugar mills through the control order issued under the essential commodities statute, and examiners set the distinction deliberately because it tests whether the candidate separates an announced price from a legally enforceable one. The tenth is the invented judgment: the statement that the Supreme Court struck down, or upheld, the farm laws, when the Court did neither, having stayed them and been overtaken by their repeal. It is the most serious of the ten because it fabricates the record rather than simplifying it, and it is the one a law-trained examiner will penalise without sympathy.

Each of these errors has the same shape: a simplification that was convenient for a summary and fatal in an answer. The correction in every case is the same discipline this guide teaches, to attach to every claim its level, its instrument and its date: which legislature, under which entry, in what form, and whether the form survives.

## How questions in this area are framed: type and demand

No specimen questions are reproduced here, deliberately, because the transferable skill is recognising the demand, not memorising a paper. Questions in this cluster recur in a small number of shapes, and each shape rewards a different preparation.

The competence shape asks, in substance, who should or who can act: whether marketing reform is for the Union or the states, whether Parliament may legislate on a subject the entries give to state legislatures, whether a proposed national framework respects the federal distribution. This shape is marked on the entries, and it is answered by naming the entry, identifying the route the Union actually has, model law, incentive, consent or concurrent legislation, and concluding on the route rather than on the policy. The evaluation shape presents a reform, a committee recommendation or a scheme and asks for its assessment. It is marked on structure: the design of the instrument, the evidence category that bears on it, the strongest objection, and a position that follows from the analysis rather than preceding it. The institutional shape asks about a body, the Commission, the Corporation, a market committee, a food commission, and is marked on mandate, legal character and the gap between the two. The episode shape asks about an event with a legislative history, above all the arc from 2020 to 2021, and is marked on process: sequence, procedure and the constitutional questions the episode raised and left open. The entitlement shape asks about food security and is marked on legal form: right against scheme, entitlement against delivery, statute against implementation record. The data shape asks the candidate to reason about a trend or a fiscal burden and is marked on the discipline of the evidence, the naming of categories and periods rather than the recitation of figures.

The mark scheme of the mains paper shapes how all of this should be deployed, and the candidate should internalise the arithmetic. In the general studies pattern the cluster's questions arrive in the standard denominations, the shorter answer expected in the region of one hundred and fifty words and the longer in the region of two hundred and fifty, which means the method of this guide is also a rationing device: an entry-first opening is not elegance but economy, since the placement sentence earns its marks in the first two lines and frees the remaining space for the instrument map and the evidence. A candidate who spends the first half of a short answer describing the distress of the farmer has spent the marks on the part every candidate can write, and reaches the part only the prepared candidate can write with the space exhausted. The worked approaches in this guide are therefore built to the shorter denomination deliberately; an approach that fits the short answer expands gracefully into the long one by widening the evidence paragraph, while an approach built long cannot be compressed in the hall without losing its spine.

The cluster also lives in the optional papers, and the aspirant with law, economics, agriculture or political science as an optional meets it there at greater depth and in a different idiom: the law optional examines the same statutes as doctrine, the economics optional the same prices as theory, the agriculture optional the same institutions as field practice. The preparation transfers because the record is the same, but the candidate should notice the change of marking currency: in the optionals the principle must be stated with its authorities, case and committee named and accurately carried, where the general studies paper rewards the principle stated with the case named as illustration. One file, two registers, and the discipline of knowing which register one is writing in is the last refinement the cluster teaches.

Within the preliminary paper the same cluster appears in recognition form: a body matched to its function, a statute matched to its year and status, an entry matched to its list, a statement about the support price whose falsity lies in the word legal. Within the mains paper the demands are carried by the familiar verbs, and the candidate should read them as instructions about structure. A demand to discuss invites the map of positions. A demand to examine invites the mechanism. A demand to critically evaluate invites the strongest case on each side and a reasoned conclusion, and it is in this shape that the farm laws and support price questions usually arrive. Reading the verb before writing the first line is the cheapest mark-earning habit in the cluster, because a discussion answer to an evaluation demand fails however much it knows.

## Procurement geography: the map of purchase is the map of the policy

No single fact organises more mains answers in the cluster than the geography of procurement, and it can be stated without a single contested figure. The state's standing offer to buy operates at full strength where the machinery of purchase, the market yards, the state agencies, the milling and storage chain, was built earliest and densest, which is the wheat belt of Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh and a set of rice-surplus states that joined the system later and on different terms. In much of the rest of the country the announced price exists as information rather than as a floor, because the purchase centres, the working capital and the agency presence that convert an announcement into a transaction are thin or absent in the harvest window when the farmer must sell. The consequences write the standard mains analysis by themselves: cropping patterns in the procuring states locked onto the two procured cereals, with the water table paying for the rice half of the rotation; market prices in the non-procuring states settling below the announced price in glut years, which is the factual root of the guarantee demand; and a distribution system whose grain flows run from a few surplus states to the whole Union, making food security a logistics of the surplus belt. The decentralised procurement states, which run purchase and distribution within their own borders for the central system, sit in the analysis as the federal middle term between full central operation and absence. A candidate who draws this map in an answer, in words, before reaching for any statistic has already answered the question's real demand, which is to explain why a national price is a regional experience.

## Prelims: the recognition layer

The preliminary paper deserves a section of its own because its demands are different in kind and its errors are different in cost. A mains answer can survive a wobbly date; a preliminary answer cannot, because the preliminary format converts every question into a test of whether an identity is exactly right. Preparation for this layer is therefore identification training, and it has three components.

The first is the matching discipline: bodies to functions, statutes to years, entries to lists, instruments to levels of government. The candidate should be able to complete, instantly and in either direction, the pairs this cluster generates: the Commission recommends and the government decides; the Corporation procures and the Commission never touches grain; Entry 28 is state and Entry 33 is concurrent; the model Act persuades and the state Act commands; the Act of 2013 entitles and the arrangement of 1965 announces. The second component is the status trap, which the preliminary paper sets with particular care in this cluster because the cluster supplies ready-made false statements: that the farm laws remain in force, that the support price is statutory, that a central APMC Act exists, that the essential commodities amendment of 2020 survives. Each false statement is built from true materials assembled wrongly, and the defence is the habit of checking every statement for its level, its instrument and its date before judging its substance. The third component is the negative question, the format that asks which statement is not correct, in which the candidate's preparation must include the errors themselves as objects of knowledge: the commonest error list in this guide is, among its other uses, a preliminary syllabus.

## A worked approach to a typical demand

Take the commonest mains demand in the cluster, an invitation to evaluate agricultural marketing reform in the light of the federal division of powers. The approach generalises, and the candidate should rehearse it until the sequence is automatic.

The opening move is constitutional, not descriptive. The answer begins by placing the subject: markets and fairs sit in Entry 28 of the State List and agriculture in Entry 14, so the regulated marketing system is a state creation, and any reform of it is in the first instance state legislation. That single placement, made in the first two lines, announces to the examiner that the answer will be built on the record. The second move maps the instruments by their legal character: the state Acts that constitute the yards, the central model laws of 2003 and 2017 that proposed their reform without commanding it, the electronic market platform that linked yards without unseating the Acts beneath them, and the Union legislation of 2020 that created a parallel trade area outside the yards by resting on the Concurrent and Union entries, together with its repeal in 2021. The third move turns to evidence by category: the persistence of the first-sale bottleneck in the yard, the layering of fee and commission that the marketing articles in this series document for the high-levy states, the contested record of the one large state experiment in abolishing the regulated market altogether, in Bihar from 2006, where the assessments of research bodies and later studies point in different directions and the honest answer says so. The fourth move states the counter-argument at strength: that the yard, for all its rents, is where price discovery, weighing, payment assurance and the state's procurement operations physically happen, and that reform which weakens the yard before alternatives mature transfers bargaining power rather than removing it. The closing move concludes on the tractable question, which is the route of reform: that durable change in a state field travels by state legislation, central models and incentives, and that the 2020 attempt stands as the demonstration of what the unilateral route costs.

### What should the first two lines of an agriculture answer do?

They should place the subject in the Constitution before describing it: name the entry and the list governing it, and state the legal character of the instrument in issue, statute, model law, executive scheme or announced policy. An entry-first opening tells the examiner the answer is built on the record, and every later paragraph inherits that credit.

Notice what the worked approach never does. It never opens with the merits of reform, never asserts a figure it cannot source, never describes a repealed statute in the present tense of living law, and never treats the Union and the states as interchangeable authors of the same law. It also never runs the episode and the evaluation together: the 2020 legislation appears as one instrument among several, with its status stated, rather than as the emotional centre of the answer. The method transfers directly. An entitlement question opens with the legal form of the food security Act and only then reaches delivery and leakage. A support price question opens with the non-statutory character of the price and only then reaches cost concepts and the guarantee demand. The order of moves is the method, and it is why the scaffold table later in this guide is organised by question type rather than by statute.

## The national market argument: the Union's strongest card

One argument inside the marketing debate deserves isolation because it is the Union's strongest constitutional card and the candidate will meet it in every formulation of the question. It runs from Entry 42 of the Union List, inter-state trade and commerce, together with the foreign trade entry beside it: agricultural produce does not respect state boundaries, a wheat surplus in one state feeds a deficit in another, exportable produce reaches a port through several jurisdictions, and a trade fragmented into dozens of state market regimes, each with its own fee, licence and check on movement, is a trade taxed by geography at every border. From that premise the reform case argues that the national market is not a slogan but a constitutional subject in its own right, on which Parliament's competence is original rather than borrowed, and that the electronic national market, the rationalisation of the single licence, and the trade area experiment of 2020 were three attempts, of increasing boldness and decreasing consent, to give the premise institutional form.

The counter-argument is equally constitutional and must be given its strength: the inter-state entry governs trade between states, but the first sale of produce by a farmer to a trader inside his own state's yard is trade within the state on a state entry, and the producing states answer that a national market built by overriding the first sale does not unify trade so much as relocate its rents from the licensed yard to the unlicensed platform. The examiner who sets this question is setting exactly that collision, between Entry 42 read widely and Entry 28 read strictly, and the answer that scores is the one that stages the collision as a collision of entries before it evaluates either side's economics. It is the namable claim of this guide in its purest form: the candidate who writes the marketing question as a contest between two entries has answered the paper's actual question, and the candidate who writes it as a contest between reform and obstruction has answered the editorial page's.

A complete answer on the national market question also registers the forum that was not used. The Constitution supplies machinery for exactly this class of disagreement, the Inter-State Council contemplated by Article 263 and the deliberative bodies of the federation, and the reform decade's habit was to proceed without it: models circulated by ministry letters, platforms built by administrative action, and in 2020 legislation carried by ordinance while the councils of the federation stood unconsulted. The point is not ceremonial. Cooperative federalism in this field is a technology with a track record the candidate can cite, since the goods and services tax, whatever its other controversies, was carried through a constitutional council in which the states sat as members, and the contrast between that route and the agriculture route is the readiest comparative paragraph the cluster offers on process. An answer that ends the national market discussion there, with the machinery named and its disuse recorded, has concluded on evidence rather than on temperament.

## A second worked demand: the entitlement question

The method transfers, and a second rehearsal in a different register fixes it better than a second paragraph of advice. Take the entitlement shape: an invitation to assess whether the legal right to food created in 2013 has changed what the Indian state owes the hungry.

The opening placement is again legal before it is descriptive: the Act of 2013 converted a distribution scheme run under the Concurrent foodstuffs entry into a statutory entitlement with identified beneficiaries, quantified grain, a scheduled price, an allowance on failure to supply, and commissions to enforce it, and the question is what that conversion changed. The instrument map follows: the ration card and its head of household, the fair price shop and its dealer, the district grievance officer, the state food commission, the vigilance committees, and behind them the judicial ancestry of the right in the People's Union for Civil Liberties litigation, where interim orders had already been running schemes as entitlements for a decade before Parliament legislated. The evidence move names the contested territory without settling it falsely: the leakage estimates and their rival methods, the exclusion errors that follow from identification by dated census data, the improvement claims made for computerised delivery and portability of the ration card across states, and the fiscal weight of supplying grain free of cost under the decision that followed the pandemic period. The counter-argument is stated at strength: that an entitlement on paper coexisted for years with states slow to constitute commissions, identify households or pay the allowance, so that the right's practical content lagged its legal form, which is the standard gap between enactment and implementation that this series tracks across subjects. The conclusion lands on the tractable question, the legal form: that the conversion mattered independently of its delivery record, because it changed who bears the burden of failure, moving it from the citizen who must plead for a benefit to the state that must answer for an entitlement. A candidate who has rehearsed this sequence owns the food security topic in both papers, in the governance register and the economy register alike, and owns it in a form that survives any verb the examiner attaches to it.

## Writing on the farm laws without either flag or fog

The anxiety aspirants feel about this episode is the practical problem this article exists to solve, and it deserves a direct answer. Candidates fear that a critical answer will be marked down as partisan and that a supportive answer will be marked down as propagandist, and many resolve the fear by writing answers that describe everything and conclude nothing. That resolution is the one genuine failure mode. An answer that refuses to conclude reads as evasion, and examiners mark evasion as absence of judgement, which is the quality the mains paper exists to test. The honest position is that the examination rewards structure and evidence, not direction: a well-evidenced answer that sets out both cases and reaches a reasoned position is a strong answer whichever side its reasoning favours, and both a defence mounted as loyalty and a denunciation mounted as virtue are weak answers, because each substitutes allegiance for analysis.

The technique is to choose the question within the question on which a conclusion is analytically available. On the merits of freeing agricultural trade, reasonable economists divided and the empirical record was cut short by the repeal, so a conclusion there is a wager. On the process, a conclusion is available on evidence: legislation on a state-sensitive subject was carried by ordinance in a pandemic session, the competence claim rested on entries whose reach over trade areas was arguable and was never judicially resolved, the upper House passed the principal Bills by voice vote after a demand for division was not accepted, no select committee examined the Bills, and the repeal a year later was passed by the same compressed method that had enacted them. A candidate can conclude, firmly and defensibly, that the episode damaged the conventions by which contested economic legislation is legitimised, while recording that the substantive direction of the trade law had serious support. That is not fence-sitting. It is the discrimination of the answerable question from the unanswerable one, and it is the highest-value technique this guide teaches.

Both cases must be stated at the strength their holders gave them. The government's case, made by the agriculture minister Narendra Singh Tomar in Parliament and by the Prime Minister in public defence of the laws, was that farmers were confined to regulated yards and licensed intermediaries, that trade should be free of the yard's fees and geography, that contract farming would bring investment and assured prices, and that the support price and the mandis would continue untouched. The economist Ashok Gulati supported the direction of the trade reforms as long-delayed liberalisation of a controlled market. The case against, made by the Samyukt Kisan Morcha, the coalition of farm unions that held the encampments at Delhi's borders from November 2020, was that the trade area would hollow out the yards on which price discovery and support price procurement depended, that contract farming pitted the smallest bargainers in the economy against its largest firms, that the laws were made without consultation on a subject belonging to the states, and that only a statutory guarantee of the support price would answer the insecurity the laws exposed. Critics of the process in Parliament, among them the opposition members suspended after the voice vote protests in the Rajya Sabha, objected to the method of passage itself. Each of those positions belongs in an answer stated as its holders stated it, attributed and unparaphrased into caricature, before the candidate's own reasoning begins.

### Did the Supreme Court decide whether the farm laws were constitutional?

No. The Court stayed the implementation of the three laws by its order of 12 January 2021 in Rakesh Vaishnav and appointed a committee to hear stakeholders, but the competence and rights challenges were never adjudicated, since their repeal later that year left the petitions without a living subject. The constitutional questions the episode raised remain formally open.

What of the episode should be memorised can be specified exactly, and the specification relieves much of the anxiety. Memorise the sequence, ordinance, passage, protest, stay, announcement, repeal, with the year attached to each stage and the assent dates of the enactments held to the day, because the preliminary paper can ask for any of them and the mains paper assumes all of them. Memorise the competence claim and its textual basis, the Concurrent foodstuffs entry with the inter-state trade entry in support, and the objection that agriculture and markets are state entries, because that exchange is the episode's whole constitutional content. Memorise the institutions of the episode: the committee the Court appointed and what became of its report, the coalition that held the borders and its two demands, the ministry that carried the Bills and the office that announced the repeal. Do not memorise the provisions of the repealed Acts beyond their architecture, the trade area, the farming agreement, the stock limit confinement, because repealed detail earns nothing and dates its owner. The provisions were the politics of the episode; the process is its examination content, and confusing the two is the error the anxious candidate makes under pressure.

The stay deserves a paragraph of its own in any complete treatment, because it is examinable from two directions. As a remedy it was unusual: the operation of statutes duly enacted was suspended while their validity remained undecided, and the committee the Court appointed, whose members included the agricultural economist Ashok Gulati and the farm leader Anil Ghanwat and from which Bhupinder Singh Mann recused himself within days, reported to the Court in March 2021 without its report being made public by the Court; Ghanwat released it himself in March 2022. As a study point it teaches the candidate to distinguish three judicial acts that popular writing merges: entertaining a challenge, staying a law, and deciding a challenge. Only the first two happened. The candidate who writes that the Court struck down, upheld, or approved the farm laws has invented a judgment, and in a law-optional or polity answer that invention is disqualifying in miniature.

## Portability and the migrant: an entitlement learns to travel

One development inside the food security system deserves treatment as a teaching case in its own right, because it shows an entitlement changing under administrative and judicial pressure without a line of its statute being amended. The Act of 2013 tied the ration card to a place: the household drew its grain from the fair price shop of its area, an arrangement built for a settled population in a country whose workforce moves. The migrant worker in a city was, in the system's design, fed at a village he had left. The portability programme known as One Nation One Ration Card attacked the defect from inside the entitlement, using the computerisation of the distribution system and the seeding of ration cards to let a beneficiary draw grain from any fair price shop in the country, and the Supreme Court's suo motu proceedings on the miseries of migrant labourers during the pandemic converted the programme from a rollout schedule into a judicially pressed obligation, directing in 2021 that portability be implemented across the states and that dry rations reach migrant workers whom the Act's identified households did not cover.

For the examination, the case teaches three transferable lessons. It teaches that implementation technology can amend a statute's practical meaning as surely as a legislature amends its text, and that answers on delivery systems should be written in that awareness. It teaches the continuing role of the courts in the food entitlement after 2013, since the right to food litigation's method, interim directions under a retained case, survived the statute it helped to produce and reappeared in the migrant proceedings. And it teaches the candidate to follow an entitlement to the person it has difficulty reaching, because that is where the examiners' questions concentrate: the tenant without a record, the migrant without a local card, the household identified from an ageing census. The cluster's hard cases are all versions of the same person, the citizen the record cannot see, and the candidate who names that pattern once can deploy it in the credit section, the food section and the land section of three different answers.

## The price support umbrella: the schemes behind the announcement

The announcement of the support price is the visible tip of a scheme structure the mains paper expects the candidate to know in outline, because questions on making the price effective are answered from it. The standing instrument is the price support scheme, under which designated central nodal agencies, with the Food Corporation at their head for the foodgrains, undertake procurement operations when market prices fall to the announced level, sharing losses with the states in the operation's design. Around it the umbrella programme announced in 2018 under the name PM-AASHA gathered three components whose names and logic should be held distinctly. The price support scheme proper is the physical procurement limb. The price deficiency payment scheme, piloted earlier in Madhya Pradesh under the name Bhavantar, is the limb that pays the farmer the difference between the announced price and the realised market price without the state taking the grain, converting a purchase operation into a transfer and raising its own questions of registration, verification and moral hazard. The private procurement and stockist pilot is the limb that enrols private buyers as the state's purchasing hand in selected districts. Each limb is a different theory of how an announced price becomes an effective one, by purchase, by compensation, or by delegation, and the examination value of the umbrella is that it lets the candidate discuss the guarantee demand concretely: the demand asks, in effect, for the first limb's certainty at national scale, and the fiscal and administrative record of all three limbs is the evidence on which that ask is judged. Article 109's record, which this guide follows, notes the Madhya Pradesh deficiency experiment of 2017 across its eight kharif crops as the pilot experience on which the national design drew, with the modal price mechanism at its centre; the candidate should present it, as the owner article does, as evidence with limits rather than as verdict.

## The biotechnology frontier: where the cluster meets the science paper

Agriculture legislation has a frontier with the science and technology paper, and the candidate should know its outline because questions cross it in both directions. The regulation of genetically modified crops in India runs not through an agriculture statute but through the rules of 1989 framed under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, governing hazardous micro-organisms and genetically engineered organisms, administered through the Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee in the environment ministry, whose approval a modified crop needs before environmental release. The system's two examinable episodes are the approval of Bt cotton in 2002, which remains the one genetically modified crop in commercial cultivation, and the moratorium imposed in 2010 on Bt brinjal after the regulator's clearance, which taught the system that a scientific approval and a political permission are different instruments. The plant varieties Act intersects the frontier on the property side, since the variety a breeder registers may be a transgenic one, and the farmers' rights in the Act of 2001 were written with the seed economy of exactly such varieties in view. An answer that can place the appraisal committee, the environment statute and the varieties Act in their correct relation to one another has the frontier mapped; an answer that attributes the approval of modified crops to the agriculture ministry or to the Seeds Act has failed the identity test at the cluster's edge, which is where identity tests are most often set.

## The land question underneath the cluster

Underneath the marketing, price and food questions sits the land question, which the economy paper examines directly and the polity paper through the entries, and which connects this cluster to the acquisition and tenancy material elsewhere in the series. The candidate needs its outline in examination form. Land, rights in or over land and land tenures are Entry 18 of the State List, and the great post-independence reforms were state legislation built on it: the abolition of the zamindari intermediaries, the imposition of ceilings on holdings, the protection and in places the conferment of ownership on tenants. The first of those reforms succeeded as legislation and is treated in answers as the Republic's one completed land reform; the second and third are the standing study in the gap between enactment and record, because ceilings were defeated by transfers and exemptions that the revenue record absorbed, and tenancy was driven underground by laws that made the recorded tenant a claimant to the owner's land. Land records are the administrative thread that ties the cluster together: the same record that fails the tenant in a credit application fails the state in a ceiling case and fails the family in an acquisition, and the digitisation of records is examined as the unglamorous reform on which the glamorous ones depend.

For the answer, the discipline is the familiar one of this guide. Place the subject in Entry 18 before discussing any reform. Distinguish the three classical reforms from one another, because examiners treat success in abolition and failure in ceilings as different findings about the state's capacity rather than as one verdict on land reform. And carry the tenant's invisibility from this section into the credit and procurement discussions, where it does its real analytical work, since the cultivator whom the record does not name is the point at which the land question, the credit question and the food question are the same question.

## Evidence discipline: cite the category, not the number

The cluster is full of numbers that move, and the implementation articles in this series establish the discipline that an aspirant should carry into the examination hall: in this field, an unsourced figure is a liability and a sourced category is an asset. Leakage from the distribution system is the standing example. Estimates of the share of grain that fails to reach entitled households have been produced by the Planning Commission's programme evaluation organisation, by academic studies built on national sample survey data, and by later researchers working with improved delivery records, and they differ across periods and methods by margins large enough to reverse an argument. The Shanta Kumar committee of 2015, appointed to restructure the Food Corporation, worked with a figure in the region of half the grain leaking in the period it examined, and researchers Jean Drèze and Reetika Khera disputed both the figure and the trend it implied, pointing to measurable improvement in several states. A candidate who asserts one number has chosen a side in a methodological quarrel without knowing it; a candidate who writes that leakage estimates vary by study, period and state, that the direction of reform-era evidence points to improvement where delivery was computerised and portability introduced, and that the fiscal and exclusion costs are the live questions, has said something true, durable and markable.

Buffer stocks supply the second standing exercise in the same discipline. The Union holds grain against norms fixed for operational need and strategic reserve at set points of the year, and the stocks in the central pool have run above those norms for long stretches, which every side of the argument uses: critics of open-ended procurement cite the excess as proof the state buys what it cannot use, defenders cite the same granaries as the reserve that carried the distribution system through the pandemic's free grain years without a purchase crisis. Both readings are honestly available from the same fact, and the candidate's task is to hold the fact at the level of pattern, stocks persistently above norms in the surplus years, drawn down when distribution expanded, and to attach the valuation of that pattern to the argument being made rather than pretending the tonnage settles it. It is the purest instance in the cluster of the guide's rule that evidence is cited by category and direction, and that the number, where it is not certain, is the one thing the answer must refuse to counterfeit.

The discipline generalises into a set of habits. Name the evidence category before the proposition: the Economic Survey's treatment of the food subsidy, the reports of the Comptroller and Auditor General on offtake and storage, the findings of a named committee, the consumption data of the national sample surveys. Give every figure that must be used a period and a provenance, or convert the claim into the pattern the figures jointly support. Treat procurement shares, subsidy totals and buffer stock levels as period facts that date quickly, and write them in the past tense of a stated period rather than in the eternal present of the summary note. And where the record is genuinely contested, as it is on the effects of Bihar's repeal of its marketing Act and on the reach of the distribution system after computerisation, present the contest as the finding: the examiner is testing whether the candidate knows that the evidence is disputed, and an answer that reports a dispute accurately scores above an answer that settles it falsely.

## Credit, tenancy and distress: the quiet third of the cluster

The scaffold gives agricultural credit and distress a row of its own, and the candidate should understand why a subject with no single famous statute earns a permanent place in the paper. The answer lies in the entries. Money-lending and the relief of agricultural indebtedness are Entry 30 of the State List; land and land tenures are Entry 18; incorporation and regulation of cooperative societies, the institutional skeleton of rural credit, are Entry 32 of the same list. The consequence is that the legal field of agricultural distress is state law layered under Union fiscal and banking instruments, and questions on distress are federalism questions in exactly the sense this guide has taught from its first section.

The teaching points are three. The first is the tenant's invisibility: tenancy in much of Indian agriculture is oral and concealed, because state tenancy laws made recorded tenancy dangerous for the landowner, so the cultivator who actually farms the land often cannot prove the fact to a bank, a procurement agency or a compensation scheme, and every instrument that keys its benefit to the record, credit, procurement registration, crop loss relief, passes the actual cultivator by. The second is the design response the system produced: the effort to build a model land leasing framework at the Centre's instance, through the expert committee whose work NITI Aayog carried in 2016, proposing that states legalise and record leasing so that the tenant becomes visible to credit and relief without the owner losing title, an episode that reproduces the marketing pattern exactly, a state field, a central model, and adoption left to the states. The third is the distinction between debt relief as event and credit structure as condition: loan waivers are state fiscal events announced against Entry 30 territory, while the structure that reproduces indebtedness, the informal lender's persistence, the cooperative system's weaknesses, the cost of cultivation rising against the announced price, belongs to analysis, and examiners reward the candidate who separates the waiver as politics from the structure as political economy. A candidate who can run the tenant's invisibility through credit, procurement and relief in one paragraph has the distress topic in its examinable form.

## What not to memorise: the negative syllabus

A study guide earns its keep as much by what it releases the candidate from as by what it assigns, and this cluster has a clear negative syllabus. Do not memorise the provisions of the repealed Acts of 2020 beyond their architecture; the trade area, the farming agreement and the stock limit confinement as designs, not as sections. Do not memorise procurement, leakage, subsidy or stock figures as bare numbers; memorise instead which body publishes each, in what period the commonly quoted figure sits, and which rival figure it must be stated beside. Do not memorise the membership of committees beyond the chair and the one or two names the episode made significant; the prices Commission is an institution, not a dramatis personae. Do not memorise state-by-state schedules of market fees, ceiling limits or tenancy provisions, which differ in detail that no paper rewards; memorise the pattern, that the fee stacks highest in the procurement belt, that ceilings were everywhere evaded through the record, that tenancy protection produced concealment, and one worked state example for each, which the marketing and land sections of this guide supply. Do not memorise debate speeches, protest slogans or editorial verdicts on the 2020 episode; memorise the sequence and the two cases in the form their holders' most serious representatives gave them. And do not memorise this guide's formulations as substitute knowledge: the scaffold and the model paragraphs are instruments for organising the record under pressure, and an examiner can tell within two sentences whether the record is underneath them. The negative syllabus is, in the end, the evidence discipline applied to the candidate's own memory: what cannot be carried accurately should be carried as a category, and what cannot be carried at all should be honourably dropped before the hall, not invented inside it.

## A revision structure that survives the calendar

The cluster is best revised as one file with four layers, built once and revisited in shrinking passes. The first layer is the anchor layer: the entries, written out by list and number, and the statute table with name, year, level and status for every instrument in the set. This layer is pure recognition material for the preliminary paper and should be revised until it can be reproduced without prompting. The second layer is the moment layer: the four organising moments of this guide, each held as a short prose account of what was built and why. The third layer is the dispute layer: for each question type in the scaffold, the strongest case on each side with its holders named, the government's and the unions' on the farm laws, the prices Commission's cost concepts and the Swaminathan recommendation on the support price, the evaluation studies and their critics on leakage. The fourth layer is the worked layer: a small number of answers written in full under time, marked against the sequence of the worked approach, opening placement, instrument map, evidence by category, counter-argument, conclusion on the tractable question.

Two practical habits complete the structure. Previous-year question work should be organised by shape rather than by year, so that the candidate meets the cluster as the examiner builds it; converting the scaffold and the worked answers into a slide-form revision deck and running it through [the ReportMedic presentation viewer for agriculture revision decks](https://reportmedic.org/tools/ppt-viewer.html) is a serviceable way to rehearse the rows under time pressure. And every figure encountered in revision should be interrogated before it is admitted to the file: its source, its period, its method, and the rival figure it must coexist with. A figure that survives that interrogation can be used; a figure that cannot should be converted into the category claim it supports. Revised in this form, the cluster is finite. The entries do not change, the statute set changes slowly and announces its changes in the Gazette, the disputes are stable in their personnel and their arguments, and the method, once learned, does not need to be relearned for each new question the paper invents.

The method of this guide is not proprietary to agriculture, and the candidate who has built it here owns it elsewhere. The same sequence, placement in the Constitution, instrument map, evidence by category, counter-argument at strength, conclusion on the tractable question, is the method taught in this series for the internal security cluster in [the internal security laws UPSC mains guide](/2011/08/15/internal-security-laws-upsc-mains/) and for the fiscal cluster in [the tax laws UPSC economy guide](/2012/11/15/tax-laws-upsc-economy-guide/), and a revision calendar that rotates the three clusters through the same four layers multiplies the value of each. Teachers report the transfer working in the other direction as well: a candidate drilled to open a tax answer with the entry in the Seventh Schedule that authorises the levy stops opening agriculture answers with a lament about the farmer, and starts opening them with the law.

A boundary belongs here rather than in a footnote. This guide is examination preparation and legal information in general form; it is not advice on any individual matter, academic or legal, and a candidate facing a question of personal eligibility, employment or litigation should take advice on the facts of that case rather than reasoning from a study article.

## The cluster beyond the written papers: essay and interview

The cluster's examination life does not end with the two written papers, and a complete preparation registers its two further habitats in durable terms. The essay paper draws its topics from the same public questions the cluster organises, food security as a test of the welfare state, the farmer's place in a liberalising economy, cooperative federalism strained by economic legislation, and the essay rewards exactly the competences this guide builds at larger scale: both cases at strength, the constitutional placement made early, the conclusion earned on a tractable question rather than performed on a slogan. The candidate who has internalised the method writes the essay as a long answer, and the candidate who has memorised positions discovers that an essay has no use for them.

The interview is the third habitat, and its demand is different again: not structure on paper but judgement in conversation, most often through the candidate's own background, a home state's agriculture, its marketing arrangements, its procurement presence or absence, held up against the national record. The preparation that serves there is the same file built for the written papers, entries, statutes, moments, disputes, with one addition: the candidate should be able to speak about the agriculture of a particular state, its principal crops, its marketing law's character, its place inside or outside the procurement system, at the level of informed description, because the board's questions travel from the national record to the candidate's own ground and back. Nothing in this habitat rewards a view. Everything in it rewards the candidate who can hold the record steady while being pressed, concede what the evidence concedes, and decline the invitations, which will come, to denounce or to defend on command.

## A note for teachers: running this cluster in a classroom

The brief of this guide names the teacher as a reader, and the classroom problem deserves its own treatment because it is the aspirant's problem in institutional form. A teacher preparing a cohort on this cluster must teach the most politically charged episode in recent Indian legislative history to students who hold strong views about it, in a room where the examination's neutrality and the students' convictions pull in opposite directions. The resolution that works is the resolution this guide teaches the candidate: separate the process from the politics and mark only the process. A class can be required to state the government's case and the unions' case each to the satisfaction of a classmate who holds it, to reconstruct the passage sequence from the record without adjectives, and to write the conclusion on the competence and procedure questions where conclusions are earnable. What a class cannot be required to do is agree about the laws, and a teacher who demands agreement teaches the students to perform agreement, which is the one skill the examination will detect and punish.

Three confusions should be pre-empted before the episode is taught at all, because they harden if the narrative arrives first: the statutory support price, the central marketing Act, and the living farm law. Each is best corrected by the primary material itself, the text of the repeal Act read aloud in class beside a coaching summary that still lists the repealed laws, the schedule of the essential commodities statute beside the claim that nothing in the field is controlled, the entries read from the Seventh Schedule rather than paraphrased. The cluster is generous to the teacher in one respect: its errors are all checkable against documents short enough to put in a student's hands, and a cohort that has once watched a confident summary fail against a statutory text carries the evidence discipline into every other subject it studies.

## Verdict: the record is what is marked

The series thesis holds in the examination register with unusual literalness. The statutes of Indian agriculture are the text, and the division of competence that decides who may enact them is the record, and it is the record that the answer is marked on. A candidate may hold any view of the farm laws, of the support price, of the entitlement model of food security, and the paper will not reward or punish the view. What it rewards, reliably and across papers, is the candidate who knows that marketing is a state field the Union must approach by model, money and persuasion; that the foodstuffs entries make distribution a shared field in which the Union could and did build an entitlement; that the support price is an executive instrument whose proposed conversion into a right is the real question; and that the seed statutes divide the state between controlling quality and allocating rights. Learn the entries, fix the statutes by name, year and status, state both cases with their holders named, cite evidence by category, and conclude on the question that admits a conclusion. That is the whole method, and it is enough.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How is agriculture legislation tested in the civil services examination?

It is tested in two registers across two papers, and the candidate should prepare for both consciously. The preliminary examination tests recognition: entries matched to lists, bodies matched to functions, statutes matched to years and to their status as living or repealed law. The mains examination tests judgement in the polity and governance paper, where the cluster appears as federalism, welfare entitlement and institutional design, and in the economy paper, where it appears as marketing, price support, distribution and subsidy. The same statute therefore has two examination lives, and the National Food Security Act can be set as a governance question about rights and grievance machinery or as an economy question about fiscal cost and targeting. Across all of it, the constant demand is the identification of who holds the power to act, and preparation that begins from the constitutional entries serves every register at once.

### Q: Which agriculture statutes must an aspirant know by name and year?

The core set is six instruments, each to be known with its year, its level of government and its status. The Essential Commodities Act, 1955 is the Union's control statute. The state marketing Acts, of which the candidate should know the pattern and one or two examples such as the Karnataka Act of 1966, stand with the Centre's model Acts of 2003 and 2017, which are proposals rather than law. The three farm Acts of 2020, numbered 20, 21 and 22 of that year, must be known together with the Farm Laws Repeal Act, 2021 that annulled them. The National Food Security Act, 2013 is the entitlement statute. The Seeds Act, 1966 and the Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 complete the set on the technology side. Around them sit a second rank for recognition: the Food Corporations Act, 1964, the Warehousing Act of 2007, and the grading and marking statute of 1937 behind the AGMARK mark.

### Q: How do you write about the farm laws without taking a side?

By taking a position on the questions that admit one and declining the ones that do not. State both cases at the strength their holders gave them, the government's case for free trade and investment made by its ministers, and the unions' case on the yards, the support price and the absence of consultation, each attributed and neither caricatured. Then conclude where a conclusion is earnable: on the competence claim that was never judicially decided, on the ordinance route and the voice vote by which the laws passed, on the repeal that repeated the method of the enactment. Those are process findings supported by the record, and an examiner can mark them without asking what the candidate believes. What fails is the answer that performs allegiance in either direction, and the answer that hides behind description and concludes nothing, which reads as evasion rather than neutrality.

### Q: Which entries of the Seventh Schedule govern agriculture?

The governing entries are divided across all three lists, which is the root fact of the subject. In the State List, Entry 14 covers agriculture itself, Entry 18 covers land and land tenures, Entry 28 covers markets and fairs and is the home of the marketing statutes, Entries 26 and 27 cover trade within the state and the production and distribution of goods subject to the Concurrent List, and Entry 30 covers money-lending and the relief of agricultural indebtedness. In the Concurrent List, Entry 33 covers trade and commerce in and the production, supply and distribution of foodstuffs, cattle fodder, raw cotton and raw jute, and Entry 34 adds price control; those two entries carry the essential commodities power and the food security entitlement. In the Union List, Entry 42 covers inter-state trade and commerce. Articles 246, 252 and 254 then distribute, lend and arbitrate the field those entries create.

### Q: How should support price policy be explained in an answer?

Begin with its legal character, because that is what separates a prepared answer from a summary. The support price is not created by any statute: the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices, a non-statutory advisory body, recommends after studying costs, the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs decides, and the government announces a price at which its agencies stand ready to buy. Then explain the benchmark dispute precisely, the National Commission on Farmers' recommendation of fifty per cent above the comprehensive cost against the government's announced one and a half times the narrower cost that excludes the rental value of land, naming the cost concept in each case. Close with the operating truth that the price bites only where procurement machinery exists, which is why the same announcement is a floor in the surplus states and a reference point elsewhere, and why the guarantee demand arises where the machinery does not reach.

### Q: Which food security facts are most often asked?

The questions turn on a compact set of facts about the National Food Security Act, 2013, and the candidate should hold them exactly. The Act is Act 20 of 2013, assented to on 10 September 2013 and deemed in force from 5 July 2013, the date of the ordinance it replaced. Its coverage runs up to seventy-five per cent of the rural population and fifty per cent of the urban population. Priority households are entitled to five kilograms of grain per person per month and Antyodaya households to thirty-five kilograms per household, at issue prices of three, two and one rupees for rice, wheat and coarse grains set out in the first schedule. The eldest woman of the household is its head for the ration card. The Act adds nutritional entitlements, a maternity benefit, a food security allowance when the entitlement is not supplied, and grievance machinery rising to state food commissions, and the questions test the delivery record, portability and identification against exactly that text.

### Q: What is the commonest error in agriculture law questions?

The commonest error is the description of the minimum support price as a statutory guarantee. It appears throughout preparation material and in answers, and it is false: no Act creates the support price, no farmer can sue for it, and it operates as an announced price supported by a standing offer to buy where procurement machinery exists. The error matters beyond itself because it inverts the analysis of every question it touches, turning the guarantee demand, which is a proposal to change the law, into a complaint that the law has been breached. Behind it stand the cluster's other stable errors: treating the state marketing Acts as one central APMC Act, discussing the repealed farm laws of 2020 as living law, confusing the advisory Commission with the procuring Corporation, and quoting leakage or procurement figures without a source, a period or a method. Each is a simplification that an answer built on level, instrument and date avoids automatically.

### Q: How do agriculture topics overlap with federalism?

The overlap is not incidental; the subject is one of the principal theatres in which Indian federalism is worked out. The entries divide the field before any policy begins, marketing and land to the states, foodstuffs and price control to the concurrent sphere, inter-state trade to the Union, so that almost every agricultural controversy is simultaneously a controversy about the division of powers. The instruments of the overlap are equally concrete: the Centre legislates in the shared field and the state law yields unless it carries the assent of the President under Article 254, a provision that turned from doctrine into battlefield when states legislated against the farm laws and the assent was withheld; the Centre persuades in the state field through model Acts, platforms and scheme conditions; and the Centre pays, through procurement and subsidy, for policies whose legal home is a state statute. A question on agricultural reform that does not reach the entries has not reached its subject.

### Q: In what order should the agriculture statutes be revised for the examination?

Revise them in the order of the field's logic rather than the order of their years, and revise the entries before any of them. Begin with the Seventh Schedule entries, since they explain why each statute exists in its form. Then take the control statute of 1955, the state marketing settlement with its central models of 2003 and 2017, the closed episode of the three Acts of 2020 and their repeal in 2021, the entitlement Act of 2013, and the seed pair of 1966 and 2001. In that sequence each statute comments on the one before it: reserve power, persuasion in a state field, the limit broken and restored, a right built where the entries allowed, and a technology divided between quality and property. Attach to every statute its year and its status as the recitation proceeds, and finish each pass through the set by writing one timed answer from the scaffold, because revision that never becomes writing does not survive contact with the paper.

### Q: How much of the 2020 farm law episode should an aspirant memorise?

Memorise the process in full and the provisions only in outline. The sequence must be exact: ordinances in June 2020, enactment that September with assent on 24 September for the two principal Acts and 26 September for the essential commodities amendment, the year of protest at the capital's borders, the Supreme Court's stay of 12 January 2021 and its committee, the announcement of repeal on 19 November 2021 and the repealing Act's assent on 30 November 2021. The competence exchange must be quotable: the Union's reliance on the Concurrent foodstuffs entry and inter-state trade against the states' claim on agriculture and markets. The institutions must be named correctly, the coalition that protested, the ministers who carried the Bills, the committee members the Court appointed. The provisions of the repealed Acts need only their architecture, a trade area outside the yards, a framework for farming agreements, a confinement of stock limits, because repealed detail earns nothing and dates the candidate who recites it.

### Q: Should an aspirant quote figures in agriculture answers, and how?

Sparingly, sourced, and never as the load-bearing member of an argument. The field's famous quantities, leakage from the distribution system, the fiscal weight of the food subsidy, the share of farmers reached by procurement, are produced by rival studies using different periods and methods, and they differ enough to reverse conclusions. The safe technique is to cite the category and the custodian: the estimates of the programme evaluation organisation and of named academic studies for leakage, the Economic Survey and the audit reports for subsidy structure and stocks, the findings of a named committee where a committee has reported. Where a figure is unavoidable, give it its period and its author in the same sentence, and state the direction the body of evidence supports rather than the point estimate. An examiner reads an unsourced percentage as a coaching artefact; an examiner reads an honestly framed range, with its dispute acknowledged, as scholarship under time pressure.

### Q: How do agriculture questions in the preliminary examination differ from those in the mains?

They differ in the kind of knowledge they can detect. The preliminary paper is a recognition instrument with no room for argument: it asks which entry sits in which list, which body recommends and which procures, which statute is in force and which was repealed, and it builds its false options out of the cluster's standing confusions, the statutory support price, the single central marketing Act, the surviving amendment of 2020. A single identity error is fatal to the question and cannot be recovered by reasoning. The mains paper is an evaluation instrument: it offers a reform, an episode or an institution and asks for structured judgement, where partial knowledge can still score if the placement is constitutional, the cases are fairly stated and the conclusion is earned. Preparation should therefore run on two tracks that meet in one file: identification drills, exact and repeated, for the preliminary layer, and timed written answers built on the scaffold for the mains layer, with the errors list serving both as a trap catalogue for the first and a conclusion checklist for the second.

### Q: What is the safest structure for an answer on the demand for a statutory support price guarantee?

Open by stating the present legal position exactly: the support price is an executive arrangement, recommended by an advisory Commission and announced by the government, and the demand is therefore a proposal to change the law, not to enforce it. Then set out what a guarantee would have to decide before it could operate, since those decisions are the analytical content of the question: which crops and which farmers it would cover, against whom the right would run when no buyer appears, what remedy and what forum a breach would carry, how the price would be set once the setter is bound by it, and which level of government would bear the open-ended fiscal commitment. Give the demand its strongest voice, the unions that made it the centre of their case and the committee constituted in July 2022 under Sanjay Agrawal to examine the support price system, from which the protesting coalition withheld its nominees. Conclude on the design question, where a reasoned position is available, rather than on the generosity of any particular price.

### Q: Which commissions and committees are worth naming in agriculture answers?

Five names carry the cluster's committee landscape, and each is worth naming for a distinct purpose. The Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices is the standing institution, advisory and non-statutory, whose cost concepts organise the price debate. The National Commission on Farmers under M. S. Swaminathan is the benchmark authority, named for its recommendation of a support price fifty per cent above the comprehensive cost. The Shanta Kumar committee of 2015 is the restructuring authority, named on the Food Corporation and the distribution system and for the leakage figure its critics disputed. The committee the Supreme Court appointed in January 2021, whose report the Court did not publish and which Anil Ghanwat released in March 2022, is the episode authority on the farm laws. The committee notified in July 2022 under the former agriculture secretary Sanjay Agrawal is the guarantee authority, named on the support price demand and the coalition that declined to join it. Naming any of them with the wrong errand is worse than naming none.

### Q: How should the right to food case be used in a food security answer?

Use it as a principle with a name attached, not as a story. The proposition it carries is that welfare schemes can harden into enforceable entitlements under judicial supervision: in the People's Union for Civil Liberties litigation begun in 2001, the Supreme Court's interim orders converted the government's food and employment schemes into entitlements the states could be held to, directed cooked midday meals in primary schools, and kept the matter under continuing oversight through its commissioners. In an answer, that proposition does two jobs. It supplies the genealogy of the National Food Security Act, which legislated in 2013 the entitlement the orders had built provisionally, and it frames the governance question that follows, whether statutory form has delivered what judicial form began. Two or three sentences carry the whole value of the case. The candidate who narrates drought, deaths and hearings at paragraph length has spent the answer's scarcest resource on the part of the material the examiner is not marking.

### Q: What should a candidate do when a question assumes the support price is already a legal entitlement?

Correct the premise in the opening, briefly and without triumph, and then answer the question the examiner intended. The correction takes one sentence of placement: the support price is an announced executive price, not a statutory entitlement, and the analysis proceeds on that footing. The candidate can strengthen the correction with the cluster's built-in contrast, the sugarcane price, which is fixed under a control order issued under the essential commodities statute and is legally payable by mills, so that the difference between an enforceable price and an announced one is shown rather than merely asserted. From there the intended question is answered in full: the working of the procurement system, the case for and against converting the announcement into a right, and the fiscal and design questions the conversion raises. Handled this way, the false premise becomes an asset, since the correction itself demonstrates the precision the paper is testing, while the candidate who accepts the premise builds the entire answer on an error the examiner planted deliberately.

### Q: How should state marketing amendments and the model Acts of the Centre be used in a marketing reform answer?

Use them as the evidence of how reform actually travels in a state field. The model Acts of 2003 and 2017 show the Union's method where it cannot command: a drafted template proposing the single licence, the private market, direct purchase from the farmer and the delisting of perishables from the yard's monopoly, offered to states that remained free to adopt, adapt or ignore it. The state amendments show the receiving end of that method: states that adopted fragments across two decades, high-levy states that protected the fee and commission structure on which their procurement and their politics rested, and Bihar, which in 2006 repealed its marketing Act altogether and supplied the debate with its most contested case study. In the answer, the pair does analytical work no summary can: the gap between the model's design and the states' adoption is the measure of the federal constraint, and the candidate who quantifies nothing but demonstrates that gap from the instruments themselves has answered the federalism half of the question with primary material.

### Q: How can four organising moments replace a chronology in revision?

Because the cluster's dozens of dates collapse, without loss, into the four settlements that produced its law. The middle 1960s carry the support price arrangement, the Food Corporation, the state marketing statutes and the Seeds Act as one scarcity-built package. The liberalisation decades from 1991 carry the contraction of the essential commodities regime to its short statutory schedule. The year 2013 carries the conversion of food distribution into a statutory entitlement. The arc from 2020 to 2021 carries the ordinance-to-repeal episode and every process question it generated. A statute encountered in revision is filed under its moment, an institution under the moment that created it, and a question under the moment it draws on, so that the candidate retrieves a structured account where a chronology would offer only a list. The method has a second virtue under examination conditions: a moment can be narrated from understanding when a date is forgotten, and an answer built on the four settlements degrades gracefully, while an answer built on a memorised timeline collapses at its first gap.

### Q: Where does the plant varieties statute fit in an answer: economy, polity, or science and technology?

It fits in all three, and the candidate should be able to rotate it deliberately. In an economy answer the Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 is seed industry law: registration, the breeder's exclusive right for a term, and the incentive structure for private and public breeding. In a polity answer it is a rights settlement: the same statute that creates the breeder's property writes the farmer's privilege to save, sow, exchange and sell farm produce of a protected variety into the text, with a compensation route when a variety fails to perform as claimed, which makes it the cluster's clearest specimen of a statute that refuses to choose between its two constituencies. In a science and technology answer it is India's sui generis response to the international obligation to protect plant varieties, chosen over the international union model, and it stands next to, but must never be confused with, the environmental clearance system for genetically modified crops run under the rules of 1989 and the appraisal committee. The PepsiCo litigation illustrates whichever face the question turns toward the candidate.

### Q: How should an aspirant handle a question that invites a personal opinion on the farm laws?

Treat the invitation as a demand for judgement, not for allegiance, and answer it with a concluded analysis whose reasoning is visible. The examiner is not collecting opinions and has no machinery for rewarding one politics over another; what the marking rewards is the discrimination between the questions bundled inside the episode. So give the episode's cases first, the government's and the unions', attributed and at strength, and then offer the opinion where an opinion can be defended on the record: on the use of the ordinance for contested economic legislation in a state field, on passage by voice vote without committee scrutiny, on a repeal conducted by the method it condemned. A position taken there is a professional judgement about legislative process, and stating it plainly is the opposite of taking a side in the partisan sense. The two failures to avoid are symmetrical: the opinion performed as loyalty or as denunciation, which the examiner reads as the absence of analysis, and the refusal to conclude at all, which reads as the absence of the very quality the mains examination exists to certify.
