Class in itself and class for itself may be the most confidently quoted pair of terms in the whole vocabulary of Marxism, and the confidence is the problem. Open almost any revision guide, lecture slide, or dictionary of sociology and the two phrases arrive together, set in quotation marks or in the solemn type reserved for a thinker's own coinage, arranged as the two stages of a single journey: a class first exists objectively, sharing a position in production without knowing it, and then awakens, organises, and becomes a class for itself. The picture is neat, memorable, and almost entirely unsupported by the texts it claims to summarise. Marx never wrote the phrase class in itself. He wrote, once, in a book of 1847, that a mass had become a class as against capital but not yet for itself, and that single subordinate clause, buried in a polemic against Proudhon, is the entire textual foundation on which the symmetrical pair and its stage theory were later built. Everything else, the balanced terminology, the developmental ladder, the implication that the second stage ripens out of the first the way a plant ripens out of a seed, was supplied by expositors who borrowed Hegel's German vocabulary and fitted Marx's clause into it.

That is not a pedantic complaint about citation form, and this article is not an exercise in pedantry. The misattribution matters because the borrowed vocabulary smuggles in a philosophy. To call the first stage a class in itself is to describe a collectivity as something that already fully is what it is, with its interests fixed and its identity complete, merely waiting for the veil of unawareness to lift. The historical record of how collectivities actually form, in factories and villages and party offices, is messier than that, and Marx's own concrete writing about peasants, artisans, and industrial workers is messier too. A reader who knows which half of the pair is genuine, where the genuine half occurs, and what work it was doing in its single appearance is protected against a whole family of errors: quoting words Marx never wrote, teaching a doctrine he never stated, and assuming a guaranteed direction of travel that the evidence of class formation does not supply. Precision of this kind costs nothing and pays every time the terms are used in an essay, a seminar, or an editorial meeting.

## What class in itself is supposed to mean

Begin with the pair as the textbooks present it, stated as fairly as its defenders would wish, because the correction only has force if the corrected version is the real one and not a caricature. In the standard presentation, a class in itself is a category of people who occupy the same position in the relations of production, share the same relation to the means of production, and therefore share material interests, whether or not they recognise any of this. The mill hands of a cotton town, on this account, are a class in itself the moment the mills employ them on common terms: same dependence on a wage, same exposure to the same employers, same interest in the length of the working day and the level of pay, all of it true of them as a matter of objective fact before a single one of them has thought a political thought. Membership is assigned by position, not by opinion. A worker who votes against every union proposal and despises collective organisation belongs to the class in itself exactly as much as the shop steward does, because belonging is a fact about where a person stands in production, not about what a person believes.

A class for itself, in the same presentation, is what that category becomes when it recognises itself. The shared position is now matched by shared consciousness and shared organisation: trade unions to defend the common interest in wages and hours, political parties to carry the interest into the state, newspapers and meeting halls and strike funds to make the collectivity durable. The class no longer merely is a class in the analyst's ledger; it acts as one in history. Its members describe themselves in its name, sacrifice for it, and are recognised by opponents as a collective force to be bargained with, repressed, or bought off. The movement from the first term to the second is then described as the central drama of modern politics, the process by which an economic category becomes a historical subject.

Stated that way, the pair does real work, which is why it survives. It separates two questions that lazy analysis merges: whether a set of people share a material position, and whether they have organised around it. Those questions plainly can come apart. Populations that share everything on paper, the small farmers of a province or the casual labourers of a port, may never combine at all, while populations divided by trade, language, and confession sometimes combine with startling speed under the pressure of a war, a lockout, or a charismatic campaign. Any vocabulary that keeps position and organisation distinct earns its keep, and the pair's endurance in sociology long after its textual credentials were questioned reflects that usefulness. The argument of this article is not that the distinction is worthless. The argument is that its pedigree is forged, its symmetry is invented, and its implied direction of travel, from in itself upward to for itself as if by maturation, is a philosophical commitment that entered through the terminology rather than through any argument Marx ever made.

Notice, too, what the standard presentation quietly assumes about interests. For the first stage to be describable at all, the analyst must be able to say what the interests of the position are independently of what its occupants say they are. The mill hands have an interest in shorter hours even if every mill hand interviewed demands more overtime. That assumption, that a position carries objective interests which can be specified from outside and may diverge from every expressed preference, is load-bearing throughout the pair's career, and it will need a section of its own below. It is also the point where the vocabulary stops being a neutral description and starts being a theory, because assigning interests to people who disavow them is a strong claim that requires defence, not a definition that can be slipped past a reader in a glossary entry.

## The wrong definitions that circulate

Around the standard presentation a penumbra of looser errors has gathered, and a glossary article should name them individually, because each one distorts a different part of the subject. The first and commonest is the quotation error itself: the pair printed inside quotation marks and followed by the name Marx, as though both phrases could be looked up in his works. They cannot. Only the second half exists, in a form examined closely below, and a student who quotes the first half in an examination script announces to a knowledgeable marker that the knowledge came from a summary rather than from the texts. Attribution errors of this size are not trivia. They are the points at which a whole chain of secondary literature reveals that it has been copying itself.

The second error turns the first stage into a state of mind. In this version a class in itself is a class that exists but does not know it, a sleeping collectivity whose members suffer from a kind of collective unawareness, and a class for itself is the same collectivity awake. The psychological reading feels natural because the vocabulary of selfhood invites it, yet it misstates both halves. The in itself stage, even in the expositors' reconstruction, is a claim about position and not about awareness: the occupants may be exquisitely aware of their grievances, articulate about their employers, and rich in local solidarities, and the category applies to them unchanged, because it never described their mental state in the first place. Conversely, awareness without organisation, a workforce full of resentment and empty of unions, parties, or funds, does not satisfy anyone's definition of the second stage. What the pair tracks, insofar as it tracks anything, is the difference between a position held in common and a collectivity organised in common, and both sides of that difference are social facts rather than feelings.

The third error is the ladder reading, in which the two terms name stages that every class passes through in sequence, as a caterpillar passes through a chrysalis. Nothing in the single source passage supports a universal sequence, and the record refuses to supply one. Collectivities have organised around a position and then dissolved back into competition among their members; organisations have been built first and gone looking for a constituency afterwards; and whole strata, the French small-holding peasantry being Marx's own great example, have persisted for generations in the condition the pair calls preliminary without showing any tendency to graduate. A stage theory predicts a direction and implies that history grades the results. The evidence shows outcomes, some durable and some reversed, and a vocabulary that cannot register reversal without embarrassment is a poor instrument for describing them.

The fourth error collapses the pair into its famous neighbour and treats class in itself and class for itself as early and late names for class consciousness, low consciousness and high consciousness on a single scale of awareness. The neighbouring concept has its own article in this series, and the difference matters enough to deserve a full section below; briefly, consciousness names something held in minds, which can be partial, divided, borrowed, or false, while the pair, even on the most sympathetic reading, names a relation between position and organisation. Confusing the two produces examination answers in which a workforce that reads the right newspapers is declared a class for itself while possessing no organisation whatsoever, an answer that fails on the pair's own textbook terms before any subtler criticism begins.

The fifth error is one of scale: the pair presented as a major doctrine of Marxism, a load-bearing element of the system alongside surplus value or the falling rate of profit, meriting a chapter in every exposition. Its actual textual footprint is one clause in one early work, never repeated, never developed, never mentioned in the three volumes of Capital or in the Manifesto's much longer discussions of class struggle. Doctrines in Marx have addresses; they can be traced through drafts, repetitions, and defences against critics. This formulation has a single address and no forwarding information. Treating it as a pillar flatters the expositors who built the pillar and misleads every reader about where the weight of Marx's class theory actually rests, which is on the definition of class by relation to the means of production and on the analysis of particular struggles, both of which stand perfectly well without the pair.

## The single occurrence in The Poverty of Philosophy

The Poverty of Philosophy appeared in 1847, written in French, as a sustained attack on Pierre-Joseph Proudhon and his attempt to apply Hegelian categories to political economy while freezing every economic relation into an eternal idea. It is a young man's polemic, brilliant and unfair in roughly equal measure, and it is not a book in which Marx is building systematic doctrine. He is dismantling an opponent's method. That context matters for everything that follows, because the famous clause occurs inside an argument about how combinations of workers arise, offered against Proudhon's habit of treating economic categories as timeless. Marx is insisting that the categories themselves have histories, that large-scale industry gathers workers together, that competition among capitals and among workers first divides them, and that the maintenance of wages becomes, through struggle, a common interest defended by combination. The passage is about association under pressure, not about stages of being.

Read closely, the argument runs in a sequence that the textbook pair then rearranges. Economic conditions, Marx says, first transformed the mass of the population into workers, and the domination of capital created for that mass a common situation and common interests. So far the movement is entirely on the side of position: a mass has been made into something by conditions it did not choose. Then comes the clause on which everything hangs. This mass, he writes, is "already a class as against capital, but not yet for itself" (eleven words of quotation, and the only ones from this source that any careful treatment needs). The sentence continues into the struggle itself: in the struggle, the mass unites, constitutes itself as a class for itself, and the interests it defends become class interests. Notice the grammar. The for itself condition is reached in struggle, inside the activity of uniting and defending, not by a prior awakening of consciousness and not by the passage of time. Marx's own sentence makes organisation in conflict the mechanism, a point the later doctrine kept forgetting and the formation objection, examined below, would eventually recover from an unexpected direction.

Three features of the passage deserve to be fixed in a reader's memory. First, the operative contrast in the sentence is not between in itself and for itself at all. It is between a class as against capital, defined relationally by its opponent, and a class for itself, defined by its own self-constitution in struggle. The phrase as against does the work that later writers reassigned to in itself, and it does different work: it describes a relation between two parties, not a state of internal completeness awaiting recognition. Second, the subject of the sentence is this mass, the workers being gathered by large-scale industry in a specific argument about combination, not class in general and not every stratum in every period. Generalising a remark about one mass in one polemic into a law of all classes is the expositors' second move, as unlicensed as the first. Third, the passage never returns. Marx did not take the formulation up in the Manifesto the following year, did not use it in his journalism on France, did not build it into Capital, and did not defend it against anyone. Writers protect their load-bearing formulations. This one was left where it fell, in a book its author later assessed with notable coolness.

### Did Marx use the phrase class in itself?

Not in those words. In The Poverty of Philosophy he describes a mass that has become a class as against capital but not yet for itself. The paired phrase class in itself is a later Hegelian reconstruction by expositors, not a Marx quotation.

The honest answer to the snippet question, then, is that the pair is a half-quotation: one genuine limb and one prosthetic. Later scholarship has searched the corpus, including the notebooks and the correspondence, and the standard finding is consistent: the German pair Klasse an sich and Klasse fur sich, presented as a matched set, is not Marx's usage. Translations multiply the confusion, because a translator rendering the 1847 sentence into the vocabulary the tradition later standardised can make the passage look as though it contains both halves, and readers of translations inherit a tidiness the French original does not have. Anyone citing the pair should therefore cite it as what it is, a traditional formula built around a genuine fragment, in the same way that careful writers handle other famous attributions examined in this series' guide to [misattributed Marx quotes](/2013/08/15/misattributed-marx-quotes/), where the pattern of a real sentence acquiring invented companions is traced across several cases.

## The attribution table

The corrections above can be compressed into a single findable artifact, the attribution table, which separates the four items that discussions of this topic habitually blur: the two halves of the famous pair, the German vocabulary behind them, and the peasant image from the Eighteenth Brumaire that does the pair's analytical work without using its words.

| Formulation | Who actually wrote it | Where | In what words | How it is usually misquoted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| class for itself | Marx | The Poverty of Philosophy, final chapter, 1847 | a mass not yet for itself, becoming so in struggle | printed as one half of a matched pair Marx coined |

| an sich and fur sich | Hegel | the Logic and the Phenomenology, as general dialectical vocabulary | in itself and for itself, marking potential and self-related actuality | treated as terminology Marx invented for class analysis |
| the sack of potatoes | Marx | The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte, on the French peasantry | peasants forming a collectivity as potatoes in a sack form one | quoted as a sneer at peasants rather than an analysis of representation |

A table of this kind earns its place because each row corrects a different citation habit. The first row protects a genuine fragment from being discarded along with its invented partner; scepticism about the pair sometimes overshoots, and students conclude that Marx wrote neither phrase, which is false and loses them the real passage. The second row isolates the actual error, which is narrow and specific: not the use of a helpful distinction, but the attribution of its first half. The third row restores the vocabulary to its original owner, whose philosophical system gives the terms their developmental charge. The fourth row relocates the analytical weight to the text where Marx actually performed the analysis the pair gestures at, among the French peasants of the mid nineteenth century, and that text receives a full section below. Readers who keep the four rows distinct will find that most published confusion on this topic dissolves into one row or another.

## The German vocabulary behind the pair

To see what the expositors imported, a reader has to visit the vocabulary's first home. An sich and fur sich are Hegel's terms, part of the working machinery of his Logic and his Phenomenology, and they belong to a general account of how anything at all develops from implicit being into explicit self-relation. A thing is an sich, in itself, insofar as what it is remains internal, undeveloped, and unposed against the world; the standard illustrations are organic. The seed is the plant in itself, containing the whole structure of the mature organism as unrealised determination, and the child is the rational adult in itself, possessing reason as capacity long before reason possesses itself as thought. Fur sich, for itself, names the further condition in which that implicit content has become explicit for the thing itself: the plant unfolded, the mind aware of its own activity, being that has returned to itself out of its otherness and knows the return as its own.

Two properties of this vocabulary matter for the class question, and both travelled with it when the expositors borrowed it. The first is that the pair, in Hegel, marks moments of a single process rather than two kinds of object. Nothing in Hegel is simply an sich as a permanent classification; the in itself is defined by its destination, by what it is on the way to becoming, and it is intelligible only in light of the for itself that completes it. The second property follows: the movement from one moment to the other is, in Hegel's system, necessary in structure even where it is contingent in timing. The seed that never germinates is a failure of the process, an arrested case, not evidence that germination is optional. Development in this idiom is the unfolding of a content already present, which is why the vocabulary fits a philosophy in which spirit comes to know what it always was.

Now observe what happens when terminology with that charge is laid over Marx's 1847 clause. The clause said that a mass, made a class as against capital by shared conditions, becomes a class for itself in struggle. It did not name the earlier condition. Supplying the label in itself for that earlier condition does far more than fill a gap in a table. It recasts the mass as a seed: complete in its determinations, possessed of fixed interests and a fixed identity, needing only to unfold. Organisation, on that picture, becomes recognition rather than construction, the moment when the collectivity notices what it already is. Every contingency of actual organising, the choices of which workers to unite, which demands to raise, which allies to accept, which boundaries to draw, fades into a maturation story in which the outcome was contained in the starting point. The expositors who performed this reconstruction were not forgers in any dramatic sense. They reached for the most prestigious vocabulary available in the tradition, the master's own philosophical mother tongue, and used it to systematise a fragment. Systematisation is what expositor's manuals exist to do. But the price of the system was a metaphysics, and readers are entitled to know the price was paid and by whom.

### What does an sich mean in Hegel's vocabulary?

In Hegel's vocabulary an sich names what a thing is implicitly, in its undeveloped inner nature, before that nature becomes explicit to the thing itself. A seed is the plant in itself: everything is present as potential, nothing yet unfolded into the self-aware form that fur sich will name.

Marx's own relationship to Hegel's vocabulary was intimate and increasingly ironic, and the fuller comparison of the two dialectics belongs to this series' treatment of [Hegel's dialectic against Marx's](/2012/08/15/hegel-dialectic-vs-marx-dialectic/), where the borrowed machinery and the points of rupture are laid out in detail. For the present purpose one observation suffices. In the writings of the 1840s Marx uses Hegelian idioms constantly, playfully, and often against Hegel, and nobody would build a doctrine on every idiom a twenty-nine-year-old polemicist tried out in a single book. The Poverty of Philosophy is saturated with the language of its target's master because Proudhon claimed that language for himself. To extract a permanent technical term from a text written partly in parody of the terminology is a scholarly error before it is anything else, and it compounds when the extracted term is then quoted back as though it were the text's own coin.

## The half-quotation

The namable claim of this article can now be stated in its sharpest form. The half-quotation: only for itself is Marx's wording; in itself is a Hegelian back-formation supplied by later expositors; and citing the pair as a Marx quotation is the single most common attribution error in undergraduate work on class. Each element of the claim can be checked by any reader with a library card, which is what distinguishes it from the looser assertions that fill quotation websites. The genuine fragment stands in the final chapter of The Poverty of Philosophy, in the passage on combination. The absence of its partner can be confirmed against the standard editions and the collected works. The back-formation can be watched happening across the history of the manuals, as the balanced pair gradually replaces the unbalanced sentence in one exposition after another until the balanced version is the only one most readers ever meet.

Why does an error of this modest size deserve the superlative? Because of where it sits. Attribution mistakes about class cluster at exactly the point where students, journalists, and new lecturers first meet the theory, and first impressions of scholarly standards are durable. A reader whose first Marx quotation turns out to be half-invented learns, or fails to learn, a lesson about the whole secondary literature: that it recycles. The same recycling produces the Manifesto lines Marx never wrote, the predictions he never made, and the doctrines, like the two-stage journey of classes, that no text states. Catching the half-quotation early trains the reflex that catches the rest. Editors notice the error for the same reason markers do. It is a shibboleth, a small fact whose possession signals that a writer has read primary texts and whose absence signals the opposite, fairly or not.

There is also a defensive use for the claim, worth stating because students often meet the pair first as a weapon in argument. When a critic dismisses Marxism by refuting the stage theory, pointing out with justice that classes do not mature like organisms, the reply that the stage theory is an expositor's construction and not Marx's doctrine is not evasion. It is the correct allocation of the refutation. The critic has damaged the manuals. What remains standing, the definition of class by position, the analysis of combination and representation in the concrete works, the problem of how shared conditions do or do not become shared action, still requires engagement on its own terms. Precision about attribution is thus not a retreat from argument. It is how a reader keeps argument aimed at claims somebody actually made.

## The Eighteenth Brumaire and the sack of potatoes

Five years after the polemic against Proudhon, Marx wrote the analysis that the pair is always used to summarise, and he wrote it without the pair. The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte, composed in the immediate aftermath of the coup of December 1851, asks how a republic born of revolution delivered itself to an adventurer, and its most famous analytical passage concerns the small-holding peasants of France, the largest single population in the country and the social base on which Louis Bonaparte's power rested. The passage performs, in concrete historical material, exactly the distinction between shared position and collective self-organisation that the textbook pair gestures at, and it performs it better, because it specifies the mechanisms on both sides: what the peasants shared, and precisely what prevented the sharing from becoming combination.

What they shared was nearly everything the first stage of the pair requires. Millions of families lived under economic conditions that separated their mode of life, their interests, and their culture from those of other strata, placing them in opposition to those strata: small plots worked by family labour, debt to the usurer and the mortgage, the tax demands of the state, hostility to the creditor, the notary, and the town. Insofar as a local connection existed among them and the similarity of their interests produced no community, no national bond, and no political organisation among them, Marx concludes, they did not form a class at all in the politically operative sense. And insofar as the identity of their interests produced none of those bonds, the shared condition remained a mere juxtaposition. His image for that juxtaposition is the one every student remembers: the great mass of the French nation was formed by simple addition, he wrote, "much as potatoes in a sack form a sack of potatoes" (eleven words), a sack being a container whose contents are identical, adjacent, and wholly unconnected to one another.

The analytical payload of the passage is the sentence that follows, and it is the sentence the textbook tradition consistently underreads. The peasants, Marx argues, are consequently incapable of asserting their class interest in their own name, whether through a parliament or through a convention; they cannot represent themselves, and they must be represented. Representation is the hinge. Where the industrial workers of the 1847 passage constitute themselves through combination, the peasants of 1852 are constituted, politically, by another: by the representative who appears simultaneously as their master, as an authority over them, as unlimited governmental power that protects them from the other classes and sends them rain and sunshine from above. That is the soil in which Bonapartism grows, and the analysis explains the otherwise baffling spectacle of the most numerous population in France delivering the state to a man who governed above society in their name. Nothing in this analysis resembles a stage awaiting maturation. The peasants are not seedlings of a class for itself. They are a population whose mode of production, scattered holdings, local horizons, suspicion of combination, actively obstructs the organisational forms that self-representation would require, and into that obstruction steps a power that represents them against their own political emergence.

### Why does the sack of potatoes image matter?

The image matters because it is the distinction working without its labels. The French small-holding peasants share conditions, soil, and interest, so they form a class; yet their isolation blocks the organisation that would make them act as one, so they must be represented by another.

Readers who want the peasantry passage in its full narrative setting, with the coup's chronology and the strange career of Louis Bonaparte laid out chapter by chapter, will find it in this series' [reader's guide to the Eighteenth Brumaire](/2013/07/15/eighteenth-brumaire-readers-guide/), which follows the text's argument in sequence. The point for the glossary is narrower. When Marx needed the distinction the pair names, in the most searching piece of class analysis he ever wrote, he reached not for Hegelian labels but for a sociology of connection: identity of conditions on one side, intercourse, community, and organisation on the other, and a theory of representation to explain what fills the gap when the second side is missing. A student who can reproduce that analysis owns the substance. A student who can only recite the pair owns the label of the substance, and the difference between those two possessions is the difference this article exists to mark.

One further feature of the Brumaire passage deserves emphasis, because it quietly refutes the ladder reading from inside Marx's own practice. The peasants are not described as an early stage of the industrial workers, and their condition is not presented as a phase any population passes through on a schedule. The difference between the two populations is a difference of material organisation: concentration in factories and towns against dispersion across plots, daily cooperation imposed by the production process against isolation imposed by it. Change the conditions and the possibilities change, which is a claim about circumstances, not about maturation. Where the Hegelian label implies that time and self-recognition do the work, the Brumaire's analysis assigns the work to railways, markets, conscription, debt, and the parish pump, the unglamorous machinery by which separate households either acquire a common life or fail to. That materialism of connection is the mature Marx's actual contribution to the question the pair fumbles, and it survives every objection raised against the pair, because it never depended on the pair in the first place.

## The vocabulary that is absent everywhere else

Negative evidence carries weight when the absences are systematic, and here they are. The Communist Manifesto, published months after The Poverty of Philosophy and devoted more directly to class struggle than any other short work Marx wrote, describes bourgeoisie and proletariat, recounts the organisation of the proletarians into a class and consequently into a political party, and watches that organisation being continually upset by competition among the workers themselves and continually re-formed on a wider scale. The concepts the pair claims to name are all in play: shared position, combination, dissolution, recomposition. The terminology of the pair is nowhere in play. The Manifesto's own language for the transition is organisational and political, a vocabulary of unions, parties, and battles, and its dynamic runs in both directions, formation and disruption alternating, which the ladder model cannot represent.

Capital extends the silence across three volumes and three decades. The first volume analyses the working day, cooperation, machinery, and accumulation with constant attention to how workers are gathered, divided, and set against one another; the analysis of the reserve army in this series' earlier glossary entry shows how much organisational consequence Marx draws from the forms of surplus population without once reaching for the 1847 clause. The chapter on classes that was to close the third volume breaks off after little more than a page, in the famous unfinished fragment where Marx asks what makes a class and finds the obvious answers, identity of revenues and revenue sources, immediately inadequate. Generations of commentators have mined that fragment, and whatever else it shows, it shows its author still wrestling with the definition of class by criteria of position and relation at the end of his life, not deploying a settled two-stage doctrine from his youth. The doctrine's absence from the fragment is as telling as its absence from the Manifesto: at the one place where a summary of the theory of class was explicitly begun, the pair does not appear.

The correspondence and the journalism tell the same story by omission. Marx wrote about combinations, strikes, the International, the Paris Commune, and the peasantry for forty years after 1847, in venues where a handy formula for the maturation of classes would have been rhetorically priceless, and the formula does not recur. Set beside this record, the scholarly habit of citing the pair as Marx's theory of class formation inverts the actual proportions of his work. His theory of class, insofar as a single theory exists, is the definition by relation to the means of production together with the concrete analyses of struggle, and the fullest statement of the definitional side is given in this series' [guide to the Marxist theory of class](/2011/09/01/marxist-theory-of-class-guide/), which traces the criteria of position, revenue, and authority across the texts. The pair is a footnote that escaped its page and ate the chapter.

## Objective interests and what hangs on them

Beneath the attribution question sits a harder one that would survive the correction of every quotation mark, and an honest glossary must dig it out. The first half of the pair, however labelled, asserts that a position in production carries interests that can be specified objectively, in advance of and if necessary against the stated preferences of the position's occupants. Wage labourers share an interest in higher wages and shorter hours; small producers share an interest in cheap credit and protection from foreclosure; and these interests are said to be facts about the positions, discoverable by analysis, in the same way that the load-bearing properties of a beam are facts about the beam. Everything in the pair's career depends on whether that assertion can be sustained, because a class in itself with no objective interests is merely a statistical category, a set of people an analyst has grouped by a criterion of the analyst's choosing, and the journey to for itself becomes a journey from the analyst's notebook into the world, which no population has ever taken.

The strongest case for objective interests is structural and begins modestly. Positions expose their occupants to characteristic pressures and close off characteristic options, whatever the occupants think about it. A household dependent on a wage must sell labour power continuously or fall back on savings, kin, or relief, and that necessity generates recurrent conflicts with the buyers of labour power over price, duration, and intensity, conflicts that erupt in workplaces whose occupants have never heard a word of theory. The recurrence is the evidence. Strikes over speed and staffing break out among workers with every variety of political opinion and none, because the pressure is built into the position, and an interest, on this restrained definition, is simply the name for what the pressure pushes toward: the side of each recurrent conflict on which the occupants' material situation places them. Defined that way, objective interests are neither mysterious nor sinister. They are the predictable lines of fracture in a structure, and denying them leaves the recurrence of the same conflicts in the same places an unexplained coincidence.

The difficulties begin when the modest definition is asked to carry more. Recurrent conflicts over wages and hours are one thing; a complete schedule of interests, extending to the form of the state, the fate of other strata, and the desirable organisation of society, is another, and the distance between them is where the pair quietly expanded its claims. From the fact that a position generates pressure on a handful of immediate questions, nothing follows about which wider programme serves the occupants, because wider programmes bundle the immediate questions with alliances, sequences, and sacrifices on which reasonable occupants disagree and on which the position itself is silent. A foundry worker's material situation speaks clearly about the foundry's shift rota and obscurely, if at all, about monetary policy, yet political organisation requires positions on both. The slide from fracture lines to full programmes is the slide from analysis to imputation, and it is the point at which the objective interests claim stops describing the world and starts licensing the describer.

The licensing problem deserves plain statement, since it governed much of the pair's political afterlife. If interests are objective and knowable by analysis, then an organisation claiming the analysis can claim to know a population's interests better than the population does, and disagreement between the population and the organisation can be reclassified as the population's failure to recognise itself, the precise condition the in itself label was built to describe. The label and the licence fit together with dangerous neatness: the first stage names the epistemic deficiency, the second names its cure, and the curer appoints himself. None of this refutes the modest structural claim, and none of it is an argument against organisation, without which shared positions remain politically mute, as the Brumaire's peasants demonstrate from the other side. It is an argument for discipline in the concept's use: objective interests asserted at the level where positions genuinely generate them, programme-level claims owned as arguments to be won rather than facts to be recognised, and the occupants' disagreement treated as evidence about the programme rather than as a symptom in the occupants. Held to that discipline, the concept of objective interests remains a tool. Released from it, the concept becomes a permission slip, and the history of the pair is in large part the history of the permission slip being used.

## How a passing phrase became a doctrine

Doctrines are rarely born in the texts they cite. They are assembled by intermediaries with institutions to run, and the pair's assembly can be dated with unusual precision to the generation that turned Marxism into a taught system. The decisive figure is Georg Lukacs, whose History and Class Consciousness, published in 1923, gave the in itself and for itself contrast its philosophical depth and its second stage its mechanism. Lukacs confronted a problem the nineteenth century had left open: if consciousness is shaped by position, why does the consciousness of actual workers lag so far behind the consciousness their position seems to assign them? His answer distinguished the thoughts men actually hold in a situation from the thoughts that would be appropriate to that situation grasped as a whole, the awareness that could be imputed to a class on the basis of its objective place in production. Imputed consciousness, zugerechnetes Bewusstsein in his German, supplied what the 1847 clause lacked: a bridge between position and awareness, built of theory, across which an organisation could claim to carry the class's real consciousness to a class not yet in possession of it.

### What did Lukacs add to the pair?

Lukacs supplied the missing machinery. In History and Class Consciousness he distinguished the consciousness workers actually hold from the consciousness imputed to their position, the awareness they would reach if they grasped their situation whole. That imputation, not the single phrase of 1847, built the doctrine's second stage.

The power of Lukacs's construction should be acknowledged before its costs are counted. It explained, within the theory's own terms, the gap between revolutionary expectation and working-class moderation that the collapse of the Second International and the failures after 1918 had made undeniable, and it did so without abandoning the claim that position matters. It also restored philosophy to the centre of Marxist discussion after decades of economic summary, which is why the book's influence ran so far beyond its organisational moment. But the costs were structural. Imputation is unfalsifiable in the hands of its holder: no expression of opinion by actual workers can count against the consciousness imputed to them, since the theory predicts the divergence it then explains. And the party that performs the imputation acquires, by the same move, an authority that no vote of the imputed can withdraw. Lukacs himself later repudiated parts of the book under pressure, and critics inside and outside the tradition have treated its central construction as everything from a profound recovery of Marx's method to the philosophical charter of substitutionism. For the glossary, the essential point is simpler: the doctrine that bears the pair's name is substantially his, and citing it to Marx misplaces two generations of argument.

Around the philosophical construction grew the pedagogical one. The parties and movements of the twentieth century required teachable systems: manuals for cadre schools, dictionaries of terms, examination syllabuses for the converted. A symmetrical pair with a direction of travel is supremely teachable. It fits a blackboard, it generates examination questions with model answers, it gives lecturers a before and an after around which to organise any historical episode, and it flatters the teaching organisation by casting it as the midwife of the second stage. Through those channels the pair passed into the general culture of the left and out into the universities, shedding its provenance at every step, until sociology textbooks with no party allegiance at all were presenting it as Marx's own summary of his theory. Provenance-shedding of that kind is the normal fate of successful formulas, and it is why the correction offered here has to be repeated in every generation: the formula's success guarantees that the error will be reprinted faster than the footnote that repairs it.

## The stage theory dissected

It is worth slowing down over the textbook model itself, because its defects are instructive beyond this topic: the model is a small museum of the ways summary literature deforms the theories it summarises. In its standard form the model runs as follows. Stage one: a class exists in itself, an objective category unified by position, its members unaware of their unity and their interests. Transition: through experience of struggle, education, and organisation, awareness spreads. Stage two: the class exists for itself, conscious, organised, and capable of historical action, its interests now pursued as a programme. Diagrams draw two boxes and an arrow. Timelines assign dates. Examination rubrics award marks for identifying which stage a given population had reached at a given moment, as though the question had an answer key.

Each element of that construction fails on inspection. The unaware first stage fails because awareness was never the criterion: the populations assigned to stage one in the standard examples, handloom weavers, Silesian textile workers, the miners of a dozen coalfields, were frequently formidably aware of their situation, literate in grievance, and organised in everything from friendly societies to illegal combinations, while remaining, in the model's terms, ungraduated because their organisation did not take the approved political form. The transition fails because its mechanisms are named so generally, experience, education, struggle, that they explain every outcome and therefore none: populations with abundant struggle experience have disorganised as often as they have organised, and the model has no vocabulary for the difference except to declare the experience insufficient, which is the imputation move again in pedagogical dress. The second stage fails because its supposed unity dissolves on contact with any actual organised movement, which contains factions imputing different consciousnesses to the same class and excommunicating one another in the name of the class's real interests. A model whose stages cannot be identified without the model's own theory doing the identifying is not a description of history. It is a sorting device for sermons.

### How did textbooks freeze the two-stage reading?

Textbooks need portable formulas, and a symmetrical pair with arrows between its halves fits a slide, a revision card, and an examination rubric. Repeated across manuals, party schools, and dictionaries of Marxism, the pair acquired the authority of a quotation through sheer circulation, long after the single source passage stopped being checked.

Yet the model persists, and persistence on this scale always has causes beyond inertia. The stage form flatters every party to the transaction. It flatters teachers, who gain a narrative arc for a syllabus. It flatters organisations, which appear in the story as history's appointed tutors. It flatters students, who can master in an afternoon a formula that seems to unlock two centuries of politics. And it flatters the theory itself, presenting as a finished doctrine what its author left as a polemical aside, thereby making Marxism look more systematic and more predictive than its texts support. Against that coalition of conveniences, the correction has only the texts on its side, which is why the correction must be made exactly, with the passage quoted, the date given, and the absent partner named, rather than waved at as a general caution about secondary sources.

## Class consciousness, the near neighbour

No glossary entry on this pair can avoid its famous neighbour, because the two are confused in print weekly, and the confusion is understandable: both concern the gap between what a population's position is and what its members make of it. The full treatment of the neighbour belongs to this series' account of [class consciousness explained](/2011/09/15/class-consciousness-explained/), which traces that concept from Lukacs through the empirical literature on working-class attitudes. The boundary between the concepts can nevertheless be drawn sharply here, and drawing it dissolves a large share of the pair's apparent content.

Class consciousness names a state of awareness: the beliefs, identities, and interpretations through which members of a class understand their situation and their opponents. It is therefore gradable, divisible, and empirical. A population can hold a vivid consciousness of grievance against foremen and none about the state; can combine fierce solidarity inside a trade with indifference to every other trade; can hold, as study after study of working-class Conservatism in Britain demonstrated, an outlook in which deference, sectional pride, and resentment of employers coexist in proportions no stage model predicts. Consciousness in this sense is something researchers can interview people about, and its findings habitually embarrass tidy theory. The pair, by contrast, names a relation between position and organisation, and organisation is not a state of awareness at all. A disciplined union with a strike fund and a rulebook is a fact of the world whether its members are fervent, cynical, or asleep, and a crowd burning with shared conviction and possessing no organisation beyond the afternoon is a different fact of the world, moving in the opposite column.

### Why is class consciousness not the same distinction?

Class consciousness names an awareness, something held in minds and open to degrees, dispute, and false versions. The pair names a relation between a shared position and collective self-organisation. A group can hold strong shared beliefs without organisation, and organise effectively while its members disagree about ultimate aims.

Once the boundary is drawn, a deflationary possibility comes into view, and a precision article should state it. Much of what the pair is used to say decomposes, without residue, into the two plainer claims that positions can be shared without being organised around, and that organisation is a separate achievement with its own conditions. Those claims need no Hegelian vocabulary, no stages, and no imputation, and they are compatible with everything solid in the tradition's concrete work. What the pair adds beyond them is exactly the questionable surplus: the implication of a subject slumbering inside the category, the germ of the later self inside the earlier condition, waiting for its appointed recognition. Readers who strip that surplus away keep the analysis and lose the metaphysics, which is the trade this article recommends in every section and formalises in its verdict below.

## The teleology inside the vocabulary

Teleology is the explanation of things by the ends toward which they are supposedly moving, and the charge against the pair is that its borrowed grammar is teleological in its bones. The charge should be made precisely, because teleology is a word critics spend cheaply. The claim is not that users of the pair believe in a cosmic purpose or a guaranteed revolution. The claim is narrower and harder to escape: the in itself and for itself contrast, in its Hegelian source, describes a content whose later form is already the truth of its earlier form, so that the earlier condition is understood retrospectively from the later one and is deficient exactly insofar as it has not yet become what it is. Import the grammar and the import holds even for users who disavow the philosophy. A population described as a class in itself is being described from the standpoint of its own future, as the not-yet of an organised collectivity, and every feature of its actual life that does not point toward that future becomes, in the description, immaturity rather than substance.

The distorting effects are visible wherever the vocabulary governs research. Questions get selected by their relation to the destination: historians hunt for the first stirrings, the embryonic unions, the proto-political strikes, and write the rest of a population's life, its religion, its kinship strategies, its migrations, its fierce local and sectional loyalties, as background noise or as obstacles. Loyalties that competed successfully with class loyalty for generations are treated as delays rather than as outcomes, as though history had missed an appointment. The possibility that the destination itself was one contingent product among several, assembled by particular organisers under particular pressures and quite capable of not existing, cannot be formulated inside the grammar, because the grammar has already decided what the population really was all along. That is the deepest service the formation objection performs: it gives researchers back the ability to describe a population's life as its own, rather than as a draft of something else.

Fairness requires noting the defence that sophisticated users of the pair mount at this point, because it is the strongest available and the verdict below depends on judging it. The defence runs that all structural description is retrospective in this mild sense: to call a gathering a market, a crowd a public, or a settlement a city is to classify by criteria that later developments make salient, and nobody supposes the classification guarantees the development. The pair, on this reading, is a heuristic pair of spectacles, one lens for position and one for organisation, and teleology enters only when careless users mistake the spectacles for a prophecy. The defence succeeds for the distinction and fails for the terminology, which is the pattern of this whole article. Nothing in the defence requires the Hegelian words, the stage arrows, or the attribution to Marx, and everything questionable in the usage comes bundled with precisely those ornaments. A heuristic that must be perpetually guarded against its own vocabulary is a heuristic that should change its vocabulary.

## The formation objection at full strength

The strongest modern challenge to the pair does not quarrel with its quotations. It denies the picture of class formation that the pair presupposes, and it does so on historical evidence. The challenge is associated above all with Adam Przeworski, whose work of the mid 1980s, above all Capitalism and Social Democracy of 1985, together with his earlier studies of class formation, subjected the whole tradition of thinking about classes as pre-formed subjects to a single corrosive question: what if the collectivities that appear in politics are not the maturation of positions but the products of organisations, assembled by parties, unions, and movements that select which of the many divisions in a society will be made politically real? On that account, class formation is a contingent political achievement, reversible and competitive, and a class does not become for itself. It is organised into existence, by actors who could have organised different collectivities instead, and often did.

Stated at full strength, as Part Six discipline requires, the objection has three blades. The first is compositional: any actual population is cross-cut by divisions of trade, skill, region, language, confession, citizenship, and gender, so the raw material contains many possible collectivities, and which one becomes politically operative depends on the strategies, resources, and accidents of organisation, not on the position alone. Electoral socialism's own history supplies the evidence Przeworski pressed hardest: socialist parties, needing majorities, repeatedly chose to address voters as the people, the nation, or the producers rather than as a single class, thereby constituting the very constituency they claimed merely to represent, and diluting the class whose maturation they awaited. The second blade is temporal: organisation precedes identity as often as it follows it. Parties found newspapers that create readerships, unions create the solidarity they are supposed to express, and the shared consciousness celebrated as stage two is frequently the artefact of the institutions assigned to stage one's remedy. The third blade is the charge of unfalsifiability already met in the discussion of imputation: a theory that reads every failure of formation as immaturity and every success as maturation has insulated itself against the record, and the record, given a fair hearing, shows formation succeeding and failing for reasons, resources, repression, prosperity, war, organisational skill, that the maturation picture cannot rank.

### What does the formation objection actually claim?

The objection claims that classes are made, not matured. Parties, unions, newspapers, and struggles draw the boundaries of a collectivity and could have drawn them otherwise, along lines of trade, region, religion, or nation. Nothing in a shared position guarantees which collectivity, if any, political organisation will succeed in constituting.

The historical scholarship of class formation runs in the same channel from an independent source. E. P. Thompson's The Making of the English Working Class, published in 1963, had already insisted, a generation before Przeworski's formulation, that class is a historical phenomenon, something that happens in human relationships when people, out of common experiences inherited or shared, feel and articulate the identity of their interests as between themselves and against others whose interests differ. Thompson's famous summary, that class is "a relationship, and not a thing" (eight words, and among the most quoted in social history), was aimed at exactly the reification the pair encourages: the treatment of a class as an object that exists fully formed in the economy and then steps, like an actor changing costume, onto the political stage. Thompson's England shows formation as a cultural and organisational labour spread over fifty years, in chapels and corresponding societies and illegal unions, producing a collectivity that had to be made in every locality and could be unmade by repression, prosperity, or schism. His account and the political scientist's converge from different archives on the same conclusion: the interesting question is never whether a class has matured, but who did the making, with what materials, against which rival makings, and why the making held or failed.

## The verdict on the objection

The verdict of this article is that the formation objection succeeds against the doctrine and fails against the fragment, and that keeping those two targets separate is the whole discipline the topic requires. Against the doctrine, the pair as a stage theory with a Hegelian first term and an imputed destination, the objection is decisive on every blade. Collectivities are constituted by organisation under conditions of competition among possible loyalties; the direction of travel is not guaranteed and is frequently reversed; and the analyst who begins from a position must still explain, with evidence about institutions and choices, how any particular collectivity came to exist. A theory of class that cannot be wrong about a population's future is not a theory of that population. The stage model can be wrong about nothing, and so explains nothing, and its continued presence in textbooks is a pedagogical scandal of a mild but real kind.

Against the fragment, the genuine 1847 sentence, the objection largely misses, and this is the concession the brief for honest treatment requires in the other direction. Marx's sentence does not describe a mass ripening. It describes a mass uniting in struggle, constituting itself as a class for itself in the act of defending interests that combination has already made collective, which is a formation claim in embryo: no organisation, no second condition; organisation as the mechanism, not the midwife, of the transition. The Brumaire analysis strengthens the acquittal. Its peasants are not an immature version of anything; their non-formation is explained by the material organisation of their production, and the political consequence, representation by another, is drawn without a whisper of developmental embarrassment. Where Marx worked concretely, he worked the way the formation theorists recommend, attending to connection, communication, and the institutions that manufacture or prevent a common life. The distance between Marx's practice and the doctrine attributed to him is the measure of the expositors' intervention, and conceding the objection substantially, as this article does, turns out to be a way of recovering Marx from his systematists rather than surrendering him to his critics.

What, then, may a careful writer still do with the pair? Three uses survive audit. As a loose shorthand for the gap between shared position and shared organisation, the pair is serviceable in contexts where its provenance is not at issue, provided the shorthand is not dressed as quotation. As a teaching exhibit, the pair is genuinely valuable, because its career exhibits in miniature how traditions manufacture doctrines, and a seminar that traces the half-quotation learns more about Marxism's transmission than one that memorises the formula. As a historical term, finally, the pair is indispensable for reading the twentieth century's own literature, in which parties, programmes, and polemics deploy it constantly and cannot be understood without it. What does not survive is the pair's use as a predictive instrument or a licence: no population owes history its maturation, no organisation inherits authority from a stage it claims to administer, and no examination answer should present the ladder as Marx's account of anything. Used within those limits, the distinction the pair gestures at remains what it was in 1847, a sharp observation about combination, waiting inside a clause for readers willing to quote it exactly.

## Historical cases read through the distinction

Cases discipline vocabulary, and four episodes, read in sequence, show both what the distinction can illuminate and where its stage version breaks. France between 1848 and 1851 remains the type case because Marx analysed it without the terminology, and its lesson is negative capability: an enormous population shared position, grievance, and even a common enemy in the creditor and the tax collector, yet produced no self-representation, and instead supplied the electoral mass and the social alibi for a Caesar. Any vocabulary that files that outcome under not yet has confused a description with a promise, since nothing in the subsequent century of French rural history delivered the maturation the file label implies; the smallholding structure dissolved through markets and migration, not through its occupants graduating into a class for itself.

Chartism in Britain shows the opposite configuration, formation running ahead of the position the textbooks assign it. The movement of the late 1830s and 1840s assembled handloom weavers being destroyed by machinery, factory operatives, miners, artisans, and shopkeepers into a single national political collectivity with a charter, a newspaper, mass petitions, and a name its members gave themselves. By every organisational criterion the pair's second stage was reached, yet the collectivity's material positions were heterogeneous and in part pre-industrial, and when the movement broke, its components dispersed into trade unionism, Liberal politics, emigration, and respectability. A stage model must either deny the achievement or deny the dispersal; a formation account records both without strain, as the success and then the failure of a particular act of political assembly. The episode is the standing refutation of the idea that organisation is position's natural blossom. It was a construction, built by identifiable organisers out of mixed materials, and it lasted exactly as long as the construction held.

German social democracy before 1914 supplies the case the doctrine loves most and understands least. Here stood the largest socialist party in the world, with its unions, press, choral societies, and a membership culture so complete that historians describe a society within the society, apparently the very portrait of a class for itself. Yet the party's own strategists spent those decades managing the gap between the collectivity they had built and the class in whose name they built it: the membership was disproportionately skilled, male, and Protestant; the electoral appeal reached far beyond wage labour; and in August 1914 the organisation's parliamentarians voted the war credits of the state the movement existed to oppose. None of that makes the achievement unreal. It makes it an achievement of organisation with its own logic and limits, not the maturation of a position into its truth, and Przeworski's analysis of the electoral dilemmas behind that history remains the sharpest account of how the party's choices about whom to constitute as its constituency shaped what the German working class politically was.

The British labour movement's long twentieth century adds the lesson of reversal, the phenomenon the ladder cannot draw. A collectivity assembled by unions and a party across two generations, dense with institutions and identity, entered a period, after the defeats and industrial restructuring of the 1980s, in which union density collapsed, the industries that had concentrated its membership vanished or dispersed, and the shared identity thinned to a voting habit and then, in many districts, not even that. Nothing about the episode resembles a stage regressed, because stages in the textbook sense do not regress; organisms that have matured do not unmature. Formations dissolve when their material supports and organisations fail, and a vocabulary adequate to class must be able to say so plainly. The formation vocabulary says it in a sentence. That is the practical test between the two accounts, and on case after case the formation account passes it while the pair's doctrine requires another footnote about false consciousness to survive.

## Before the clause: class in the writings of the 1840s

The clause of 1847 did not arrive from nowhere, and its prehistory explains both its phrasing and its limits. In the manuscripts and polemics of the preceding years Marx was working out, against the Young Hegelians on one side and the utopian socialists on the other, an account in which human collectivities are defined by their place in production and their ruling ideas are the ideas of their material relations. The German Ideology, drafted in 1845 and 1846 and left unpublished in his lifetime, contains the period's most sustained treatment: individuals form a class insofar as they share a common position against another class, the class achieves an independent existence over against the individuals composing it, and its average members are subordinated to conditions that confront them as an alien power. There is rich material there about position, domination, and the illusion of communal interest, and there is no trace of a two-stage vocabulary. The emphasis falls on what a shared position does to people, the subordination it imposes and the false universality it generates, not on a maturation those people are scheduled to undergo.

The Holy Family and the Theses on Feuerbach, from the same crowded years, supply the philosophical pressure that the clause's wording quietly carries. The eleventh thesis, philosophers have interpreted the world while the point is to change it, is the famous distillate of a broader insistence that consciousness follows activity and organisation rather than preceding them like a tutor. Read against that insistence, the 1847 sentence's grammar, in which the mass becomes for itself in the struggle and not before it, is exactly what one would expect from its author at that moment: the self-constitution is an act, not an awakening. The expositors' reconstruction reversed the order of the original philosophy, placing the completed identity first, in the in itself, and treating action as its expression. Tracing the clause back into its decade therefore does double duty. It shows the sentence to be characteristic of the young Marx at his most materialist, and it shows the doctrine built on the sentence to be, in the strictest sense, a Hegelianisation of a passage that was already a materialist correction of Hegel.

One more document of the decade deserves a place in the record, because students regularly assume the pair must be hiding in it: the letter to Weydemeyer of March 1852, in which Marx summarised his own contribution to the theory of class for a correspondent in America. What he claimed as his novelty there was threefold: that the existence of classes is bound up with particular historical phases of production, that class struggle necessarily leads to a transitional political form, and that this transition itself leads to the abolition of classes. Whatever one makes of those claims, the letter is the closest thing Marx ever wrote to a syllabus of his class theory, composed five years after the clause and in full awareness of the Brumaire he had just finished, and the pair plays no part in it. Scholars building the doctrine later had to pass over the author's own syllabus in silence to keep the pair at the centre. Noticing that silence is among the cheapest and most reliable tests a reader can apply to any exposition: where the pair dominates and the letter is absent, the exposition is describing the tradition, not the texts.

## The inverted case: organisation without a shared position

Every distinction casts a shadow question, and the pair's shadow is its inverse: can a collectivity be for itself without being in itself, organised and self-conscious without the shared position that is supposed to ground it? The textbook model cannot ask the question, since its stages are sequential, but history asks it constantly, and the answers illuminate the original distinction better than another tour of the standard examples. Political organisation is promiscuous in its materials. It has built durable, self-conscious collectivities out of confession, language, region, and resentment of a capital city; it has built them out of consumers, veterans, and motorists; and within the class vocabulary itself, it has repeatedly built movements whose members' positions diverged so widely that the shared interest had to be manufactured by the programme rather than discovered beneath it. The peasant leagues, artisan movements, and cross-class national movements of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries were not failed versions of a class awakening. They were successful formations of a different kind, and their success is data.

Marx's own great case of the inversion is Bonapartism read from the other side. The regime of Louis Bonaparte presented itself as the embodiment of the peasantry, the nation's largest class, and organised that class's political existence from above: its identity, its enemies, its festivals, its plebiscites. Here a class was, in a sense the textbooks cannot process, represented into collective self-consciousness without any self-organisation at all, its for itself condition supplied by a state that needed the class as a constituency and manufactured the constituency it needed. The Eighteenth Brumaire's analysis of that manufacture, the representative who is simultaneously master and protector, remains the sharpest account available of organisation substituting for position, and it is no accident that later theorists of populism keep returning to the text. The pair, which assumes that self-organisation is the authentic form and representation its absence, has no shelf for a case in which representation is the very mechanism of collective existence. The formation framework shelves it without effort: one more way, neither the purest nor the rarest, in which a collectivity gets made.

The inverted case also disciplines the concept of objective interests from a fresh angle. If organisation can constitute a collectivity around interests its members did not previously share, then the direction of causation between position and interest is not one-way, and the analyst's confidence in reading interests off positions should fall accordingly. Interests, on the evidence of the inverted cases, are partly found and partly founded: found in the recurrent pressures a position imposes, founded in the programmes that teach a population which of its pressures to name, blame, and organise around. A glossary that leaves its readers with that two-part formula has given them something the pair never could, an account of class formation in which both the materialist anchor and the political labour are visible, and in which neither is reduced to the other's shadow.

## The distinction in sociology after Marx

Sociology inherited the pair along with the rest of the Marxist vocabulary, and its career inside the discipline is a fair test of what in the pair was substance and what was packaging, because sociologists kept what they could use and quietly relabelled the rest. The most explicit inheritance is Ralf Dahrendorf's, whose Class and Class Conflict in an Industrial Society of 1959 rebuilt the theory of conflict on foundations he openly derived from Marx while rejecting the stage philosophy outright. Dahrendorf's quasi-groups, aggregates of position-holders with latent interests arising from authority relations in associations, are the in itself category with the Hegelian label filed off and the mechanism specified: positions carry role expectations, role expectations generate latent interests, and under specifiable conditions, communication, recruitment, a charter, a leadership, the quasi-group becomes an interest group and then a conflict group. The architecture is the pair's, translated into a research programme in which every transition the textbook model assumed is converted into a variable to be investigated. That Dahrendorf, a liberal critic of Marxism, found the structure worth rebuilding is among the strongest evidence that the underlying distinction between position and organisation answers a real question.

The empirical traditions that followed were less kind to the pair's stronger claims. Erik Olin Wright's decades of comparative survey research on class structure and consciousness found the links between position and outlook real but modest, mediated by household, trajectory, and the contradictory locations, managers, supervisors, credentialed employees, that a two-box scheme cannot place; his work retained class as a map of positions while treating consciousness as a separate, stubbornly variable outcome, which is the deflationary settlement this article recommends, arrived at by questionnaire rather than by quotation. The British tradition of community and occupational studies told a similar story from below: mining villages and docklands displayed solidarity of a density no theorist needed to impute, organised through chapels, clubs, and kin as much as through unions, while the affluent worker studies of the 1960s found instrumentally minded employees whose union membership coexisted with privatised lives and Conservative votes, a combination the stage model's sorting could only process as immaturity. Across these literatures the pair survives, where it survives, as a pair of questions: what do these people share, and through what, if anything, do they act together? Stripped to those questions, it is good sociology. Dressed as a doctrine with stages and a destination, it has been a generation's cautionary tale.

Textbooks in the discipline, it must be said, have been slower to absorb the settlement than the research literature, and the undergraduate who meets the pair in a sociology course will usually meet the unreconstructed version, quotation marks included. The reasons are the pedagogical ones already examined: the pair is short, symmetrical, and examinable, and a discipline that teaches hundreds of thousands of beginners will always prefer a formula that fits a lecture slide to a controversy that fills a term. Readers of this article who go on to teach should expect to correct the formula in their own students with the same patience its correction requires here, and should take comfort from the fact that the correction is interesting in a way the formula is not: a class of students that has just learned its favourite quotation is half-invented has learned something about scholarship itself, which is more than the formula ever taught anyone.

## The pair in translation

A word about language, since the pair's career is partly a history of translation decisions. Marx wrote the source passage in French, in a book whose full title announces a reply to Proudhon's Philosophy of Poverty, and the French sentence carries its contrast in a form that maps awkwardly onto the later German terminology: the mass is already a class vis-a-vis capital, en face du capital in the sentence's economy, but not yet pour elle-meme, for itself. The pour elle-meme is unmistakably personal, a reflexive idiom of selfhood, and translators into German and English faced a choice between rendering the sentence's actual words and rendering the doctrine that later grew around it. Editions prepared under the influence of the doctrine tend to smooth the sentence toward the pair, supplying in the first limb a balance the original lacks, and readers comparing translations across the decades can watch the back-formation being ratified by the apparatus of scholarship itself. German discussions compounded the effect by debating the pair in Hegel's own tongue, where an sich and fur sich sound native and inevitable, although the native speaker who coined the sentence wrote it in French and used neither word.

The practical consequence for citation is straightforward and worth engraving. Anyone quoting the passage should quote from a translation that prints the sentence's real asymmetry, should cite The Poverty of Philosophy by its final chapter rather than by the pair, and should treat any edition or anthology that presents the two phrases as a matched quotation as a source to be checked against the original before further use. Translation is never a neutral pipe, and this topic, in which a whole doctrine balances on the rendering of one clause, is the standing classroom demonstration of that truth.

## What the distinction gets right

A correction that leaves its subject in ruins has usually overshot, and this article's corrections should not be read as a demolition. Strip the pair of its forged pedigree, its stage arrows, and its imputed destinations, and a residue remains that no serious account of class can do without. The residue has three components. First, position is real independently of recognition: the pressures a shared situation imposes operate on the organised and the unorganised alike, and an analysis that waits for a population to describe itself before granting it a location in the structure will miss most of what the structure does. Second, organisation is a separate and contingent achievement: it has conditions, costs, entrepreneurs, and failure modes of its own, and no quantity of shared position substitutes for it, as every sack-of-potatoes population in the record attests. Third, the gap between the two is where politics happens: the space in which parties, unions, states, and movements compete to define what a shared position means and whether it will mean anything collectively at all. Those three claims, stated without a single German word, contain everything defensible in the pair and impose none of its metaphysics.

It is worth adding that the residue does positive work in fields far from the seminar room. Union organisers reason with it when they distinguish a workforce's grievances, which are position's voice, from its organisation, which must be built meeting by meeting. Electoral analysts reason with it when they separate a demographic's structural situation from the parties competing to give that situation a political name. Historians reason with it when they refuse to backdate a movement's identity into the decades before its institutions existed. In each case the user is employing the distinction as a pair of questions, what is shared and what has been built, and the questions retain their edge precisely because they promise no answer in advance. The pair began, in its one genuine half, as an observation about combination under pressure. Restored to that modesty, it can go on being useful for another century and a half, and its invented half can retire to the only place it ever belonged: the history of how doctrines are made.

## The passage read whole: combination, not maturation

Because so much turns on one passage, the passage deserves to be read whole rather than mined for its famous clause, and reading it whole is the surest antidote to the doctrine. The closing pages of The Poverty of Philosophy concern strikes and combinations of workers, a topic Marx takes up because Proudhon had condemned combinations as economically harmful while accepting the wage relation that provokes them. Marx's reply builds the combination from the conditions up. Large-scale industry, he observes, concentrates great masses of people in single places and under single capitals, people unknown to one another and thrown into competition. Competition is the first social fact of the passage, and it divides the mass against itself: workers compete for employment, and that competition is the bourgeoisie's chief weapon, a point the Manifesto would soon compress into its observation that organisation is continually upset by the competition among the workers themselves. Against that solvent, the maintenance of wages, the one interest every competitor shares, becomes the rallying ground, and combination begins as defence of the wage.

The passage then traces combination's own education, and its sequence is institutional rather than psychological. Combinations begin locally and partially, a single factory, a single trade, a single demand. They acquire permanence, funds, and rules. They confront the law, which in that period criminalised them in both England and France, and the confrontation teaches the combination that its enemy is not only the individual employer but the state that polices the labour market, at which point the struggle, Marx argues, takes on a political character. Observe what is absent from this sequence: no awakening, no veil lifting, no moment at which the mass recognises its essence. There are funds and fines, court cases and deputations, defeats and re-formations. The class for itself of the famous clause is the name the passage gives to the end of that institutional road, a mass that has united in struggle and defends its interests as class interests because its organisations have made those interests collective property. Everything the formation theorists would later insist upon, the priority of organisation, the constructed character of unity, the ever-present counter-current of competition, is already present in the text the doctrine claims as its charter. The doctrine did not develop the passage. It replaced it.

Reading whole also restores the passage's polemical temperature, which the textbook version cools into neutrality. Marx is arguing, against a rival for the leadership of French socialism, that combinations and strikes are not regrettable frictions in an otherwise harmonious economy but the very process by which workers become a political force, and that a socialism which deplores the process while praising the goal has understood neither. The clause about the class for itself is a weapon in that argument: it tells Proudhon's readers that the mass they pity as an inert category is already in motion against capital and needs association, not tutelage, to complete the motion. There is a rich irony in the afterlife. A sentence written against tutelage, against the posture of the enlightened theorist toward the unformed mass, was rebuilt by later tutelary organisations into the charter of their tutelage, complete with a first stage defined by the mass's deficiency. The history of the pair is not only a citation error. It is a demonstration that doctrines can invert the politics of the texts they cite while keeping the citation.

## The strongest defence of the pair, answered

Intellectual honesty requires the pair's best defence to be heard in its strongest form, not the textbook form already dismantled. That defence, made by careful scholars rather than by manuals, runs as follows. Granted that the exact phrase class in itself is absent, the distinction it names is present in Marx's practice everywhere, and arguably in the 1847 sentence itself: a class as against capital is precisely a class defined by its objective relation to an opponent, which is what in itself was coined to say, and the later terminology merely baptises a contrast Marx drew in other words. On this view the back-formation is a legitimate clarification, of the kind every living tradition performs on its founders, and objecting to it confuses philology with thought. The defence adds that the stage implication, where it appears, is the fault of vulgarisers, and that serious users of the pair, from Lukacs onward, employed it to pose the problem of organisation, not to prophesy its solution.

The defence deserves a divided verdict. Its first limb is largely sound, and this article has relied on it: the distinction between position and organisation is genuinely Marx's, visible in 1847, in the Brumaire, and across the mature work, and no correction offered here touches it. Terminology that names a real distinction in a founder's thought is a normal scholarly instrument, and the pair, used as such an instrument with its provenance acknowledged, is no worse than a dozen other standard labels Marx never used, beginning with several under which his own doctrines are routinely taught. The defence fails at its second limb, because the provenance is not in practice acknowledged: the pair circulates in quotation marks, in examination rubrics, and in the mouths of lecturers as Marx's own formulation, and a clarification that requires a footnote it never receives has become, in transmission, a misattribution. The remedy is therefore not to ban the terms but to demote them, from quotation to label, from doctrine to distinction, and to attach the founder's name only to the half he wrote. Traditions survive such demotions easily; the distinction loses nothing by being introduced as the tradition's shorthand for a contrast Marx drew in a single clause of 1847, and it gains the one thing the textbook version cannot supply, which is the truth about itself.

There is a final consideration that tips the balance toward demotion, and it concerns the direction of scholarly travel. Fields mature by replacing inherited labels with specified mechanisms: where an older literature said maturation, the newer says organisation, communication, brokerage, and resource mobilisation, each term naming something that can be observed, counted, and compared. The pair's Hegelian dress belongs to the older stratum, and clinging to it costs more than nostalgia. It costs the questions the newer vocabulary asks, which are invariably the questions on which real explanations depend: which organisers, which networks, which funds, which defeats. A glossary exists to move readers from the vocabulary they arrived with toward the vocabulary that does the work, and on this term the itinerary is clear: arrive with the pair, leave with the distinction, the passage, and the questions.

## The pair and the middle strata

One more analytical test exposes the pair's limits from an unexpected side: the populations that fit neither box. The pair presupposes, in its textbook use, a society sorting itself into two great camps whose positions are legible, and its stages are then assigned camp by camp. The strata that have grown fastest in the century and a half since 1847 decline the assignment. Salaried managers exercise authority over other people's labour while selling their own; credentialed professionals hold scarcity rents in their qualifications alongside a wage relation to their employers; small proprietors work beside their single employee and share the employee's exposure to the bank. Erik Olin Wright's term for such cases, contradictory locations within class relations, names the difficulty exactly: the position itself is double, so no single set of objective interests can be read from it, and the in itself stage, which requires a legible position as its entry ticket, cannot even be stamped. What such strata politically become has historically depended, with unusual nakedness, on which organisations courted them and which identity they were offered, which is the formation thesis operating in the open. The pair's defenders sometimes reply that intermediate strata are transitional and will polarise into the great camps in time. That reply, now well over a century old, is itself the teleology objection wearing a demographic disguise: a prediction perpetually deferred by the populations it describes, who persist in organising, when they organise, around the cross-cutting identities the model assures them are secondary.

## The pair in party debates: substitution and its critics

The doctrine's political career deserves a section of its own, because the stakes of the vocabulary stop being academic the moment an organisation claims to administer the passage between the stages. Within the socialist movements of the early twentieth century, the question of who brings consciousness and organisation to a class defined as not yet possessing them was fought out with a directness the textbooks mute. Lenin's What Is To Be Done? of 1902 argued, in its most contested pages, that the working class by its own effort arrives only at trade-union consciousness, the politics of the wage bargain, and that socialist theory, elaborated by intellectuals from the material of the sciences, must be brought to the movement from outside the immediate economic struggle. The pamphlet quoted the Austrian leader Karl Kautsky approvingly on exactly that point, and whatever qualifications its author later attached to the formulation, the political architecture it sketched, a class in itself awaiting a consciousness delivered by its organised vanguard, became the operational version of the pair for the century's largest Marxist movements. The in itself stage had acquired an institutional remedy, and the remedy had acquired an address.

The critics inside the movement saw the danger early and named it with a precision that later history made famous. Rosa Luxemburg, reviewing the organisational dispute in 1904, warned against an ultra-centralism that would convert the party's central committee into the sole thinking element and the class into an instrument to be manoeuvred, insisting that the errors of a genuinely movementist class politics were historically more fruitful than the decrees of the best central committee. Leon Trotsky, in the same controversy and from a position he would later abandon in practice, coined the period's most corrosive formula for the logic he opposed: substitutionism, the sequence by which the party substitutes itself for the class, the central committee for the party, and finally a single leader for the committee. Neither critic used the glossary pair, and neither needed to; they were arguing with the doctrine the pair would come to label, the proposition that a collectivity's real consciousness and interests can be held in trust by its self-appointed organ. Their warnings belong in any full account of the pair because they identify, from within the tradition and in advance, the exact point at which a vocabulary of stages becomes a constitution for tutelage. The subsequent record of parties that ruled in the name of classes they had classified as immature is the long footnote to those warnings, and a precision article records the connection without needing to exaggerate it: the vocabulary did not cause the record, but it gave the record a theoretical language in which every act of tutelage could describe itself as education.

## Why the error survives: the sociology of a misquotation

Misquotations are usually treated as accidents, pebbles in the shoe of scholarship, but a misquotation that survives a century of correction is better treated as an institution, with causes that can be enumerated. The first cause is anthology economics: editors assemble readers and sourcebooks under deadline, take formulations from previous anthologies, and reproduce the apparatus of those volumes along with the texts, so that a balanced pair printed in one successful reader enters the next dozen without passing a primary text on the way. The second cause is the lecture hall's demand for symmetry, already examined: a pair with two matching halves is teachable in a way a lopsided clause is not, and teaching materials optimise for the classroom hour rather than for the archive. The third cause is translation ratification, the process traced above in which editions prepared inside the doctrine smooth the source sentence toward the formula, so that even a student who does consult the text in translation meets the balanced version and reasonably concludes that the balance is authentic. Each cause is mundane, and together they form a copying machine that no single correction can switch off, which is why the correction must be institutional too: glossaries that print the attribution table, syllabuses that set the passage beside the formula, examiners who reward the distinction between them.

The fourth cause is the most interesting, because it implicates the formula's critics as well as its friends. The pair is useful to opponents of Marxism in exactly the form its friends invented: a stage theory with a guaranteed destination is far easier to refute than a clause about combination, and generations of critics have aimed at the doctrine while believing they were aiming at Marx. Both camps thus acquired an interest in the strong version's survival, one as charter and one as target, and the modest original, in which a mass unites in struggle and thereby constitutes itself, served nobody's polemic and was left unread. There is a general lesson here that extends well beyond this term, and it is the lesson this series' reception articles return to from several directions: the version of a thinker that circulates is the version that is useful to the circulators, and the labour of checking is never finished because the incentives that produced the error do not retire when the error is exposed. A glossary entry is a small intervention against a large machine, and it should be written, as this one has been, without illusion about the machine and without despair about the intervention, since students meet the formula one classroom at a time and can be inoculated one classroom at a time.

## Using the terms correctly

The closing summary of correct usage can be given as a set of working rules for writers, each one earned by a section above. Cite the second half of the pair to Marx and the first half to the tradition: a sentence that says Marx wrote of a class not yet for itself, while later expositors supplied the matching in itself, is accurate, quotable, and short. Quote the 1847 passage from its final chapter, in its real asymmetry, and let the asymmetry carry the argument. Use the distinction freely and the stage theory never: position and organisation are separate variables, movement between them runs in both directions, and any sentence implying a population is overdue for its maturation should be struck in revision. Treat objective interests as a claim about recurrent pressures, defensible at the level of wages, hours, and security, and as an argument to be won at the level of programmes, never as a fact that disagreement refutes itself against. Prefer, wherever the analysis is doing real work, the mechanisms the newer literature names: combination, communication, funds, charters, brokerage, repression, and the rival loyalties that organisation must defeat or absorb. And keep the attribution table beside the keyboard. A reader building a set of study notes on this cluster could do worse than to [save the passage, the table, and the verdict together on VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html), where the correction can sit permanently next to the formula it corrects, which is where corrections have to live if they are to outlast the copying machine described above.

None of this diminishes the genuine fragment. On the contrary: a clause that survived being turned into a doctrine, a ladder, and a licence, and still reads, in its own words, as a sharp observation about how a mass becomes an actor by uniting in struggle, has earned its place in the small set of Marx's formulations that repay exact quotation. The glossary's whole duty to such a clause is to hand it on unbroken, with its date, its context, and its missing half honestly marked. That duty discharged, the pair can be left where this article leaves it: the distinction retained, the doctrine discharged, the quotation restored to its single, sufficient half.

## For students and exam candidates

This term is one of the cheapest differentiators available to you in an examination, because almost every competing script will present the pair as a Marx quotation with two stages, and yours does not have to. Open with the correction, stated flatly: Marx used only the second phrase, once, in The Poverty of Philosophy of 1847, writing of a mass that had become a class as against capital but not yet for itself. Then show that you know what the single passage argues, combination arising from competition and struggle rather than awareness ripening on a schedule, and reach for the Eighteenth Brumaire's peasantry whenever an example is required, since the sack of potatoes analysis gives you position without organisation and representation filling the gap, all in Marx's own practice. Handle objective interests with the discipline this article models: assert them at the level of recurrent pressures on wages and hours, and treat programme-level claims as arguments. If the question invites evaluation, the formation objection is your strongest material, stated through Przeworski's claim that collectivities are organised into existence and Thompson's insistence that class is a relationship rather than a thing. Close by returning the pair to its proper shelf, as the tradition's shorthand for a distinction Marx drew once, and you will have written the answer the rubric's authors, if they know the literature, were hoping somebody would write.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The one-slide fix is the attribution table: four rows, five minutes, and a class that arrived reciting a doctrine leaves knowing a distinction and a caution. Build the session around the collision between the formula and the passage. Set the textbook pair on one side and the 1847 sentence on the other, and let students find the missing half themselves; the discovery teaches more about secondary literature than any lecture on method. Follow with the Brumaire peasantry as the worked example, asking the seminar to specify, in concrete terms, what the peasants shared and what they lacked, which forces the position and organisation variables apart in students' own words. The liveliest discussion reliably comes from the inverted case: ask who in the room's reading has been represented into existence as a constituency, and the abstraction dissolves. Assess with a citation exercise rather than an essay at least once: hand out three published uses of the pair, one from a textbook, one from a newspaper, one from a party manual, and require a verdict on each with the passage as the standard.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Cite the formulation as a tradition, not as a text: the pair in itself and for itself, standardised by expositors after Lukacs, around a genuine fragment in the final chapter of The Poverty of Philosophy. When the analysis itself is your subject, work from the mechanisms, combination, communication, funds, repression, rival loyalty, and treat maturation language in your sources as evidence about the source's doctrine rather than about the population described. Historians will find the formation literature, Thompson on England and Przeworski on electoral socialism, the productive frame, because it converts the pair's assumptions into researchable questions about organisers and institutions. Journalists face the pair in a specific temptation: the sentence that says a workforce, a region, or a demographic has become a class for itself, or has failed to, imports a stage theory into a news report under cover of a familiar phrase. The accurate sentences are less elegant and more informative: name the organisation, the membership, the funds, the action, or record their absence. Editors who keep the attribution table in the style file will catch the quotation error at the same pass, and will earn the gratitude of every reader who has ever checked.

## What counts as organisation

The pair's second condition is usually left at the level of a gesture, unions and parties are named, and the analysis moves on, yet everything in the distinction depends on where the threshold of organisation lies, and a precision article should pace the boundary. At the lowest level stand the ephemeral forms: the crowd that assembles over a price or an eviction, the workshop that walks out together and returns together, the network of kin and neighbours that hides a fugitive organiser or feeds a striker's family. Such forms express solidarity and can win real victories, and the tradition that reserves the second stage for durable institutions tends to underrate them; the food riots and machine-breaking campaigns of early industrial England, which Thompson taught historians to read as disciplined actions governed by a moral economy rather than as spasms, show coordinated collective action of great sophistication leaving almost no institutional trace. At the middle level stand the durable mutual forms: friendly societies, burial clubs, cooperatives, and trade unions, bodies with funds, rules, and officers, which convert episodic solidarity into a standing capacity and survive their founders. At the highest level stand the political forms, parties and parliamentary fractions, which claim to speak for the collectivity in the state and to bind its local organisations into a single will.

The boundary matters because the pair's users smuggle their conclusions through it. A population with dense middle-level organisation and no party of its own, the classic case being the British working class of the later nineteenth century, strong in unions and cooperatives while voting in large numbers for Liberal candidates, can be filed under either stage depending on where the analyst sets the threshold, and the filing then masquerades as a finding. A population with a party claiming its name and almost no workplace organisation beneath, a configuration familiar from many national movements, passes the threshold on paper while failing it in the factory. The honest procedure is to report the levels separately and let the reader see the profile: episodic capacity, durable mutual organisation, political representation, each present or absent on its own evidence. Profiles of that kind are what the formation literature actually produces, and they make the pair's single threshold look like what it is, a relic of the stage picture, in which all the levels were assumed to arrive together as symptoms of one underlying maturation. They rarely arrive together. The history of class formation is largely the history of their unevenness, and an analyst equipped with the profile can describe a population's real situation in a paragraph where the stage vocabulary can only assign it a grade.

Spontaneity deserves its own paragraph in this survey, because the pair's doctrine made a bogey of it. In the tutelary version, action without the authorised organisation is by definition immature, the first stage thrashing, and organisers are licensed to distrust every initiative they did not schedule. The record supports a cooler view. Spontaneous action is information: it reveals where the pressures of position have become intolerable and where the existing organisations have failed to register them, and the movements that reshaped the twentieth century repeatedly began as unscheduled irruptions, strikes no union called, occupations no party planned, which organisations then raced to house before they dispersed. A framework that grades such irruptions as deficiencies of consciousness will misread its own movement's history at every turning point. The formation framework reads them as the raw events of class formation itself, the moments at which new collectivities announce that the existing ones no longer contain them.

## Lukacs, reification, and the pair's philosophical second life

The doctrine's most sophisticated version deserves a fuller hearing than the manual tradition gave it, because Lukacs's construction was an attempt to solve real problems and its failure is instructive at a higher level. History and Class Consciousness placed the pair inside a general theory of reification, the process by which social relations take on the character of things, governed by laws that confront people as a second nature. Within that theory, the distinction between a class's position and its consciousness is not a pair of stages but a single contradiction: the worker experiences the labour market as an objective order of things, prices, wages, dismissals, while occupying the one position in the social structure from which that order can be grasped as a human product. The for itself condition, on this account, is the moment the commodity labour power becomes conscious of itself as a commodity, and thereby cracks the reified surface of the whole. Whatever its fortunes as philosophy, the construction explains why the pair acquired its aura of depth: in Lukacs's hands the second stage is not a club membership but an epistemological event, the point at which the structure becomes visible from inside.

The construction's difficulties are the obverse of its elegance. The epistemological privilege assigned to one position is asserted from the structure of the theory rather than demonstrated from the experience of the position's occupants, whose actual recorded consciousness, in every archive historians have opened, remains obstinately plural, sectional, and mixed with loyalties the theory must classify as reified. The imputation bridge then does the work the evidence declines to do, and the organisation performing the imputation inherits the epistemological privilege along with the political one, a double inheritance that the critics of substitutionism had already warned against in cruder language. Lukacs's later self-criticism, delivered under institutional pressure and therefore hard to weigh, conceded the book's tendency to treat consciousness as the subject of history in a manner he came to regard as idealist. Readers approaching the pair through Lukacs should take both the concession and its circumstances seriously, and should notice what the whole episode demonstrates about the term's career: at every level of sophistication, the pair tends to relocate the hard empirical questions, who organises, who follows, who pays, who defects, into a philosophy of consciousness where they cannot be answered, and the relocation is the tell that the vocabulary has stopped serving the analysis and started replacing it.

## The peasantry beyond France

Marx's potato sack was French, but the analytical problem it names is general, and following it across other countrysides tests whether the distinction, shorn of its stages, really does travel. The Russian peasantry of the later nineteenth century lived under the commune, the mir, which periodically redistributed land among households and bound the village into collective obligations of tax and recruitment. Here was a population with more communal institution than the French smallholders possessed, yet the commune's solidarity faced inward, organising the village against the tax collector and the neighbouring village alike, and the same institution that created connection at one scale obstructed it at every larger one. Russian revolutionaries of successive generations broke themselves on that ambiguity, some romanticising the commune as a ready-made collectivism, others dismissing the peasantry as a sack of potatoes with a longer spoon, and the countryside's actual political behaviour, immense jacqueries alternating with sullen loyalty to the throne, fits neither romance nor dismissal. It fits a profile: dense local organisation, no durable national connection, representation contested between throne, party, and village meeting. The profile describes; the pair only grades.

The Chinese countryside of the early twentieth century supplied the complementary lesson, in which an outside organisation treated the peasantry's non-formation not as a verdict but as a task. Communist organisers in the Jiangxi and Yan'an periods built, village by village, the connective tissue the smallholding structure lacked: peasant associations, women's associations, militia, schools, and a land policy calibrated to give the associations something material to defend. Critics of the pair will recognise the procedure as formation theory in practice, whatever vocabulary the organisers themselves used, and its success under conditions of war does not rescue the stage model; it refutes it, since the stages were traversed, where they were traversed, by deliberate institutional construction under specific pressures, and remained untraversed in regions the construction never reached, among populations whose positions were identical. Identical positions, divergent outcomes, the difference made by organisation and war: that is the formation thesis in a single sentence, and the peasantries of three continents keep supplying the evidence.

## Rival collectivities: nation, confession, and craft

A population is never a blank sheet on which one identity waits to be inscribed, and the pair's deepest empirical weakness is its silence about the competition. Every stratum the textbooks assign to the first stage already belongs to going concerns: nations with flags and armies and school systems devoted to their reproduction, confessions with weekly rituals and welfare networks older than any union, crafts with apprenticeship lineages and fierce internal honour, regions, races, and kin networks, each with its own account of who the population is and who its enemies are. Class formation, where it occurs, is a victory in a crowded field, won against rivals that do not retire when defeated but persist inside the victorious collectivity, available for reactivation whenever the class organisation falters. August 1914 remains the canonical demonstration. The most completely organised working classes in Europe, possessed of parties, unions, press, and a half-century of internationalist profession, chose within days to constitute themselves as nations in arms, and their organisations, far from resisting the choice, administered it. A stage model can only record the catastrophe as a mass relapse into stage one, a description that explains nothing and insults the evidence. A formation account records that the national collectivity, built by states with conscription, schooling, and tariffs over the same decades, proved the stronger construction when the two were forced into competition, and that the international class collectivity had never possessed institutions of comparable depth. The comparison of institutional depths, unglamorous as it is, explains the outcome; the comparison of stages merely mourns it.

Craft and sectional loyalty supply the quieter, everyday version of the same rivalry. The labour aristocracy debates of British historiography turned on whether skilled strata, organised earlier and better than the unskilled, constituted the vanguard of a class or a interest bloc against it, and the honest answer the archives support is that they constituted whichever their institutions were doing that decade: leading general movements in one period, defending differentials against the unskilled in another, sometimes both within a single union's rulebook. Sectionalism of that kind is not a failure of a class to become itself. It is what collectivities built from real positions look like, since positions are sectional before they are general and every general organisation is a negotiated federation of sections whose unity must be continually re-won. The pair's image of a single subject coming to self-knowledge dissolves, under this evidence, into a politics of federation and brokerage, which is less inspiring and more true, and which returns the analyst to the questions the formation framework asks: who federated whom, on what terms, with what side payments, and how long the bargain held.

## A reader's protocol for checking an attribution

Because this article's correction is one instance of a general problem, it should leave readers with the method as well as the instance, a short protocol for testing any famous formulation before repeating it. Begin with the exact words: write down the phrase as quoted, including its punctuation and capitalisation, and refuse paraphrase until the wording is fixed, since drift in wording is where back-formations breed. Then search the primary corpus in a reliable edition, in the language of composition where possible, and record not only whether the phrase appears but how its nearest relatives read, because the gap between the genuine sentence and the quoted formula is the finding. Next, date the formula's own first appearances: a phrase that cannot be found in the author but appears uniformly in expositions after a certain decade belongs to that decade, and naming its true period is usually possible within a seminar's work. Then check the translations in both directions, since a formula ratified by translators will appear to be confirmed by every edition the checker opens. Finally, ask who benefits: formulations survive because institutions use them, and identifying the use, the syllabus, the party school, the examination rubric, explains the survival better than any hypothesis about carelessness. A reader who runs that protocol on the pair will reproduce this article's findings from the sources in an afternoon, which is exactly the position a glossary should put its readers in: not trusting the correction, but equipped to audit it.

The protocol generalises, and the habit it builds is the real deliverable. The same afternoon's work will test the other furniture of the tradition, the letters and speeches and deathbed sayings that circulate with a great name attached, and will find the same distribution of results: a core of genuine fragments, a penumbra of paraphrases promoted to quotations, and a few outright inventions whose careers map the needs of the institutions that repeated them. Scholarship on any politically charged thinker labours under this weather, and the defence is never a better memory for the genuine fragments alone. The defence is the reflex of checking, applied before the quotation leaves one's own page, since every writer who repeats an unchecked formula becomes one more cause of its survival for the next reader.

## Combination and the law: the passage's hidden context

The 1847 passage was written against a legal background that modern readers, accustomed to lawful unions, easily miss, and restoring that background sharpens everything the clause says. In France, the Le Chapelier law of 1791, passed in the name of revolutionary liberty, had abolished the guilds and forbidden workers' coalitions as offences against the freedom of labour, and its prohibitions, carried into the penal code, made the combination a crime through the whole period of Marx's youth. In England, the Combination Acts of 1799 and 1800 criminalised union activity outright; their repeal in 1824, engineered by Francis Place and Joseph Hume through a parliamentary committee, was followed within a year by fresh restrictions, and master and servant law continued to imprison workers for breach of contract in circumstances where an employer's identical breach was a civil matter, a asymmetry that persisted in thousands of prosecutions a year into the 1860s. Combination, in the world the passage addresses, was therefore not the natural efflorescence of a maturing class. It was a conspiracy in the eyes of the law, organised in public houses and friendly society rooms under constant threat of informers, transportation, and the blacklist, and its survival required funds held in secret, officers willing to be prosecuted, and rituals of loyalty borrowed from the friendly societies precisely because those societies were lawful.

That context explains two features of the passage that the textbook reading flattens. The first is its insistence that combination is driven by the defence of wages against competition among the workers themselves, a mechanism stated without romance: the enemy named first is not the employer but the worker's own competitor, and unity is an achievement against the grain of the labour market, purchased by discipline. The second is its attention to association's self-education through illegality. A combination that must survive prosecution learns to distinguish its economic demands from the political order that criminalises them, and the passage's claim that class struggle becomes political is, in context, a report on what the law taught: that the wage question could not be separated from the question of who governed, because the governor's statute book stood inside every wage dispute. Historians of the British and French movements have documented that education in detail, in trial records and union minute books, and their findings uniformly support the passage read as formation history. The class for itself was not awaited. It was assembled, under prosecution, by named people in named rooms, and it dissolved wherever the assembly was broken up faster than it could be rebuilt.

The legal history also supplies the cleanest available test between the two readings of the pair, because law is a variable the stage model cannot process and the formation model predicts from. Where combination was decriminalised and unions acquired legal personality, funds, and the right to picket, durable organisation followed within a generation, among populations whose positions a stage theorist would have had to describe as unchanged. Where repression intensified, organisation collapsed among populations whose supposed maturation should, on the stage reading, have been irreversible. Positions constant, laws variable, organisation tracking the laws: the experiment has been run repeatedly by history itself, in the repeal years in Britain, in the legalisation of French unions in 1884, in the Wagner Act's transformation of American organising in 1935, and in the anti-union statutes that reversed so much of it in later decades. A vocabulary that cannot register the independent variable in that experiment should not be trusted with the experiment's results, and the pair, which contains no word for law, cannot register it. The passage of 1847, written by a man who followed the trials of combinations in the press, contains more sociology of organisation in its margins than the doctrine built upon it contains in its whole frame.

## Partial formation: the normal condition

Between the sack of potatoes and the disciplined party stretches the territory where almost every real population lives, and a glossary that has criticised the binary owes its readers a map of the middle ground. Partial formation is not a stage on the way to completeness; it is the normal, stable condition of collectivities, and it comes in recognisable varieties. There is the population organised densely in one locality and absent in the next, the miners of one coalfield a byword for solidarity while the miners of another, identically positioned, remain unorganised for a century, a contrast that sends the analyst to local history, to the ownership pattern of the pits and the memory of a single lockout, rather than to any general maturation. There is the population organised around everything except its position: churched, schooled, and regimented by associations that give it a powerful collective life whose content is confessional or national, so that the organisational capacity exists in abundance and is spent, from the class analyst's viewpoint, in the wrong currency. There is the population whose organisation is real but mortgaged, unions recognised by employers precisely on condition of political quiet, parties financed by interests their programmes oppose, forms that deliver the shell of the second stage as a technology of containment. And there is the population that forms brilliantly for a single campaign, a strike wave, an election, a rising, and then subsides into its households for a generation, leaving memoirs and a street name as the whole residue.

Each variety defeats the binary in a different way, and enumerating them is more than taxonomy. The local variety shows that formation is an event in places, not a property of positions. The confessional variety shows that organisational capacity and class content are independent variables, so that a population can be saturated with association and untouched by class politics in the same decade. The mortgaged variety shows that the presence of the second stage's institutions proves nothing about whose interests they serve, a point the objective interests discussion already prepared. The episodic variety shows that formation has no ratchet: it is not stored, and a later generation inherits the memory of organisation rather than the organisation, which it must build again at full price. A serious vocabulary for class would have drawers for all four, and the newer literature, speaking of uneven development, segmented mobilisation, incorporation, and demobilisation, has been building those drawers for decades. The pair has two drawers, one of them mislabelled with a forged signature. Retiring the pair in favour of the fuller cabinet is not a loss of theoretical ambition. It is what theoretical ambition looks like when it is audited against the record.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What does class in itself and class for itself mean?

In the standard textbook presentation, the first term names a set of people who share the same position in the relations of production, the same relation to the means of production, and therefore the same material interests, whether or not they recognise any of it. The second term names that same set once it has become conscious of its shared position and organised to act on it, through unions, parties, and a common programme. The movement between the two is then described as the passage from an objective category to a historical actor. That presentation is useful as a first approximation, and this article has used it as the starting point. Its defect is one of pedigree rather than of sense: only the second phrase is Marx's wording, the first is a later Hegelian back-formation, and the tidy two-stage journey the pair suggests is an expositor's construction rather than a doctrine stated in any text. Used with that warning attached, the pair remains a serviceable shorthand for the gap between sharing a position and organising around it.

### Q: Did Marx actually use the phrase class in itself?

No, not in those words, and the point is checkable in an afternoon. In the final chapter of The Poverty of Philosophy, published in 1847, Marx writes of a mass that the domination of capital has given common conditions and common interests, and he says that this mass is a class as against capital but not yet for itself, becoming for itself in the struggle as it unites and defends its interests. The paired first phrase appears nowhere in his writings as a matched term. It was supplied later, by expositors who reached for Hegel's vocabulary of an sich and fur sich to give the passage a symmetrical terminology its author had not given it. Translations prepared inside that later tradition sometimes smooth the sentence toward the pair, which is why the check should be made against the sentence's real asymmetry: a class as against capital on one side, not yet for itself on the other, with no first label supplied. Citing the pair as a quotation therefore repeats the single most common attribution error in writing on class.

### Q: Where does the in itself and for itself distinction come from?

The vocabulary comes from Hegel, in whose Logic and Phenomenology an sich, in itself, names what a thing is implicitly and undeveloped, and fur sich, for itself, names that same content become explicit and self-related: the seed is the plant in itself, the child the rational adult in itself. Marx, writing The Poverty of Philosophy in French in 1847, used only the second limb, in a sentence about a mass becoming a class for itself through struggle. Later expositors, above all in the tradition descending from Lukacs's History and Class Consciousness of 1923, fitted the Hegelian pair over Marx's clause, supplying the missing first term and, with it, the developmental structure the German words carry in their home system. The distinction as a piece of class analysis is therefore a hybrid: a Marxian observation about combination, dressed in Hegelian terminology by other hands, and then circulated as though the whole garment were the founder's own. Knowing the garment's two tailors is the beginning of using it carefully.

### Q: What is the sack of potatoes passage about?

The passage stands in The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte, Marx's analysis of how the French republic of 1848 delivered itself to a coup. Its subject is the small-holding peasantry, millions of families working similar plots under similar pressures of debt, tax, and the moneylender, sharing conditions and interests as completely as any population in the country. What they lacked was connection: scattered holdings, local horizons, and no national bond or political organisation among them, so that their collectivity was formed by simple addition, much as potatoes in a sack form a sack of potatoes, identical, adjacent, and unjoined. Because they could not assert their interest in their own name, Marx argues, they had to be represented, and their representative, appearing as master and protector at once, was Louis Bonaparte. The passage is the distinction between shared position and shared organisation performed in concrete history, without the pair's terminology, and it adds the element the terminology misses: an account of what fills the gap, representation by another, when a population cannot represent itself.

### Q: How does a class move from in itself to for itself?

On the textbook model, the movement happens through experience: struggle teaches, organisation spreads, consciousness rises, and the category becomes an actor. Marx's single passage gives a more mechanical and more credible sequence. Large-scale industry gathers workers who begin in competition with one another; the defence of wages, the one interest every competitor shares, produces combination; combination acquires funds, rules, and permanence; the law's hostility teaches the combination that its opponent includes the state; and the mass, uniting in that struggle, constitutes itself as a class for itself, with the interests it defends becoming class interests. Nothing in the sequence is guaranteed, and the record shows it running backward as readily as forward, when repression breaks the funds, prosperity dissolves the grievance, or rival loyalties outbid the combination. Later formation scholarship, from Thompson to Przeworski, restates the sequence without the stage language: collectivities are organised into existence by identifiable actors under identifiable conditions, and the honest answer to the question is a list of mechanisms, combination, communication, institutions, and their enemies, rather than a law of maturation.

### Q: Is the in itself and for itself distinction teleological?

In its textbook form, yes, and the charge can be made precisely. The Hegelian vocabulary from which the first term was borrowed describes a content whose later form is the truth of its earlier form, so a population labelled a class in itself is being described from the standpoint of its supposed future, as the not-yet of an organised collectivity, with every feature of its life that does not point toward that future classified as immaturity. The consequences for research are visible wherever the vocabulary governs, in histories that hunt for stirrings and embryonic unions while treating a population's religion, kinship, and sectional loyalties as obstacles. The defence that the pair is only a heuristic, one lens for position and one for organisation, succeeds for the bare distinction and fails for the terminology, since nothing in the heuristic requires the Hegelian words or the arrow between stages. Marx's own concrete analyses, above all the Brumaire, are not teleological: the peasants' non-formation is explained by material circumstances, not recorded as a missed appointment with destiny.

### Q: What are objective class interests?

Objective class interests are the interests an analyst attributes to a position in production independently of whether its occupants state them, on the ground that the position exposes whoever holds it to characteristic pressures. A household dependent on selling its labour power must find buyers, and that necessity generates recurrent conflicts over wages, hours, and intensity with the buyers, so an interest in the terms of sale can be read from the structure of the situation. At that modest level the concept is defensible and does real explanatory work. The concept becomes hazardous when it is extended from those recurrent pressures to a complete political programme, because a position that speaks clearly about shift rotas is silent about alliances and constitutions, and the extension is performed by whoever claims the analysis. That is the point at which objective interests turn into imputation, and at which disagreement can be reclassified as the occupants' failure to recognise themselves. The discipline this article recommends is to assert interests only where positions generate them, and to own programme-level claims as arguments.

### Q: Can a class be for itself without being in itself?

The textbook model cannot ask it, since its stages are sequential, but history asks it constantly, and the answer is that organised, self-conscious collectivities are routinely built on materials other than a shared position. Confession, language, region, and nation have supplied the basis for formations with charters, funds, and martyrs, and within the class field itself, movements have united strata whose positions diverged so widely that the shared interest had to be manufactured by the programme rather than discovered beneath it. Marx's own case of the inversion is Bonapartism: the regime of Louis Bonaparte organised the French peasantry's political existence from above, supplying the identity, the enemies, and the ceremonies of a class constituency that had not organised, so that representation did not fill a gap but manufactured the collectivity it claimed to express. Organisation does not only express an existing unity of position; it can create the unity it expresses, which is the formation thesis stated from the other side, and a reason to distrust any vocabulary that treats the second stage as the first stage's natural blossom.

### Q: Which historical examples show a class for itself?

The strongest examples are those where organisation can be pointed to rather than inferred. Chartism in Britain assembled weavers, factory operatives, miners, and artisans into a national political collectivity with a charter, a press, and mass petitions within a few years, and its dispersal after defeat shows the achievement's constructed character. German social democracy before 1914 built a party, unions, newspapers, and a whole associational world that historians describe as a society within the society, though its parliamentary conduct in August 1914 exposed the distance between the organisation and the class in whose name it spoke. The French peasantry of the Brumaire stands as the great negative example, a population sharing everything except connection. In each case the instructive question is the formation question, which organisers built what, from which materials, against which rivals, and the examples reward that question with evidence. They punish the stage question, which asks only how far along a population had travelled, with the discovery that the populations kept travelling in directions the stage map does not contain, including backward.

### Q: Is the distinction still used in sociology?

It is, in two different conditions. In the research literature the pair survives in translated form: Dahrendorf rebuilt it as the passage from quasi-groups with latent interests to organised conflict groups, specifying the conditions, communication, recruitment, a charter, that the textbook model assumed; Wright's survey programme kept class as a map of positions while treating consciousness as a separate and stubbornly variable outcome; and the class formation literature descending from Thompson asks the pair's questions with the stages removed. In undergraduate teaching the unreconstructed version persists, quotation marks included, because a symmetrical formula fits a lecture slide and an examination rubric better than a controversy does. The distinction's sociological afterlife illustrates its content rather neatly: the same formula leads one existence in the seminar room, where its provenance is examined, and another in the textbook, where it is reproduced by copying. A reader meeting the pair in a sociology course should ask which of the two existences is on the page, and will usually be able to tell from whether the 1847 passage is quoted or merely invoked.

### Q: Why did later writers turn a single phrase into a stage theory?

Because stage theories are useful to the institutions that teach and recruit, and the materials for this one lay ready to hand. Marx's clause of 1847 offered a fragment about a mass becoming a class for itself in struggle. Hegel's vocabulary offered a symmetrical terminology in the tradition's mother tongue. Lukacs, confronting the gap between workers' consciousness and the consciousness their position seemed to assign them, supplied the mechanism of imputed consciousness, which converted the fragment into a philosophy of class formation. The parties and cadre schools of the twentieth century required teachable systems, manuals, dictionaries, and syllabuses, and a two-stage formula with a direction of travel is teachable: it fits a blackboard, generates model answers, and casts the teaching organisation as midwife of the second stage. Each step was intelligible, and none required bad faith. What the sequence produced was a doctrine attributed to a founder who had written one clause and never repeated it, which is why the history of the pair is read as a case study in how traditions manufacture the doctrines they need.

### Q: What should a student avoid claiming about the origin of the pair?

Avoid claiming that Marx coined both phrases, that the pair appears in the Communist Manifesto or in Capital, that it states a two-stage law of class development, or that a class in itself is an unaware class waiting to wake. Each of those claims is in print in respectable places, and each is false or unsupported. The defensible claims are narrower and, being narrower, stronger: Marx wrote once, in The Poverty of Philosophy of 1847, of a mass become a class as against capital but not yet for itself; the matching first phrase is a later Hegelian reconstruction; the distinction between shared position and shared organisation is at work across Marx's analyses, above all the Eighteenth Brumaire; and the stage theory built on the pair belongs to the tradition of Lukacs and the manuals. A student who writes four such sentences has the attribution right, and markers who know the literature recognise the signature. The broader rule is the one this article has applied throughout: quote only what can be produced, and let the produced text carry the argument.

### Q: How does the French peasantry example limit the in itself idea?

The peasantry limits the idea by showing a population that satisfies every condition of the stage and exhibits no tendency to leave it. The small-holding families shared a mode of life, a relation to the means of production, enemies in the creditor and the tax collector, and interests any analyst could read. If the first stage carried any momentum toward the second, this is where it should have shown. Instead the material organisation that defined their position, scattered plots, household production, horizons bounded by the commune, obstructed the intercourse and combination that self-organisation required, and the expression of their condition arrived from outside, in a representative who governed above them in their name. The example converts the first stage from a beginning into a permanent condition, and it relocates the explanatory weight from the position to the circumstances of connection: railways, markets, conscription, debt, and the institutions that either manufacture a common life or prevent one. A stage theory can only file the peasants under not yet. Marx's analysis, which never uses the label, explains them.

### Q: What would count as organisation turning shared position into shared action?

Evidence, rather than assertion: durable bodies with funds, rules, and officers that survive their founders; communication that reaches across the localities a position is scattered through, a press, a federation, a shared name its bearers give themselves; coordinated action maintained under pressure, a strike held through a lockout, a boycott observed in districts that gain nothing from it; and representation that the represented can instruct, replace, and disown. The levels should be reported separately, since they rarely arrive together: episodic capacity in crowds and walkouts, durable organisation in unions and societies, political representation in parties, and a population may be rich in one and empty in another for generations. The British working class of the later nineteenth century, dense in unions and cooperatives while voting largely Liberal, is the example of an uneven profile, and uneven profiles are what the historical record contains. Where those marks are present, shared position has been turned into shared action by identifiable labour. Where they are absent, no quantity of shared position, and no stage label, can substitute for them in an honest account.

### Q: How does Thompson's account of the making of a class retell the same transition historically?

E. P. Thompson's The Making of the English Working Class, published in 1963, retells it as fifty years of cultural and organisational labour rather than as a maturation. His working class is present at its creation: artisans, weavers, and factory workers, out of common experiences, come to articulate the identity of their interests against the interests of their rulers, through corresponding societies, chapels, illegal unions, and a press. His summary, that class is a relationship and not a thing, was aimed at exactly the reification the pair encourages, the treatment of a class as an object formed in the economy that steps onto the political stage. In Thompson's England nothing ripens; things are built, at price, locality by locality, and unbuilt by repression, prosperity, and schism, which is why his account converges with the formation objection raised by political scientists from different archives. For the glossary the moral is plain: the transition the pair compresses into an arrow is, in historical writing, a research programme, and the arrow is the one part of the inheritance a reader should decline.
