Few questions in the study of a single thinker carry as much hidden weight as the dispute over the young Marx and the mature Marx. Stated plainly, the proposition in dispute is this: somewhere in the middle of the eighteen forties, Karl Marx abandoned the philosophical humanism of his early writings, broke with the vocabulary of alienation and species-being, and founded a science of history and political economy on entirely different foundations, so that the author of the Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts and the author of Capital are best read as two different thinkers who happen to share a name. One side of the argument accepts that proposition and builds a periodisation upon it. The other side denies it, insisting that the concerns of the early writings persist, transformed but recognisable, through the whole of the later work. Both sides can quote texts. Both sides can point to dates. The argument has lasted for decades because the evidence genuinely pulls in two directions, and because a great deal more than chronology rides on the answer.

![The young Marx and the mature Marx, the break thesis, Althusser and the Grundrisse evidence - Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-41.webp)

The stakes become visible once a reader asks what the verdict controls. If the break is real and deep, then alienation is a youthful category that the mature theory discarded, and any reading of Capital that leans on alienation is importing a notion its author had rejected. If continuity holds, then the critique of political economy is the working out, by other means, of a critique of estranged labour that Marx formed in his twenties, and the economic writings lose their deepest motive when they are severed from the early manuscripts. Teachers decide which Marx their students meet first. Editors decide which texts belong in a selection and in what order. Political traditions decide which Marx they claim as an ancestor, the humanist philosopher of freedom or the scientist of capital. A dispute about periodisation is therefore also a dispute about inheritance, and it deserves to be settled by evidence rather than by allegiance. The evidence, handled carefully, does settle more of it than the polemics on either side suggest.

## Why the young Marx question matters

A reader who has never heard of the epistemological break can still feel its effects, because the break thesis quietly governs how Marx is packaged. Anthologies that open with the manuscripts of 1844 and treat Capital as the fulfilment of a humanist project tell one story. Anthologies that begin with the Theses on Feuerbach, skip to the critique of political economy, and treat the early writings as juvenilia tell another. Survey courses inherit the same fork. The student who meets the philosopher of alienation first will read the fetishism chapter of Capital as a late flowering of an early insight. The student who meets the economist first will read the same chapter as an analytical result about commodity form, and may regard the manuscripts as a biographical curiosity. Neither student has been given false texts. Each has been given a selection, and the selection was made by someone who had already answered, explicitly or by habit, the question this article examines.

The dispute also matters because it is a model case of a general problem in intellectual history: how to tell the difference between a thinker changing his mind and a thinker changing his instruments. Vocabularies alter for many reasons. A writer may abandon a word because the idea behind it has been rejected, or because the word has been captured by opponents, or because a new analytical framework expresses the same idea with greater precision, or because the writer has moved to a different genre with different conventions. Only the first of those reasons counts as a break in thought. The others are changes of equipment. Much of the young Marx literature consists, at bottom, of an argument about which of these explanations fits the disappearance and reappearance of a handful of German words across a forty year writing life. That is why the debate repays close attention even from readers whose interests lie elsewhere: the method used to resolve it, ordering works by composition, testing vocabulary by function, and separating a writer's self description from his practice, transfers to the study of any thinker whose papers were published out of sequence.

There is a further reason the question refuses to die. Marx never wrote an autobiography of his own development, and the one retrospective sketch he did publish, the preface to the Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy of 1859, describes a path from law and philosophy through journalism to political economy in terms that both camps can claim. The break camp reads the preface as the author's own announcement that he had settled accounts with his earlier philosophical conscience. The continuity camp reads the same pages as a compressed itinerary that names turning points in employment and study, not repudiations of earlier concerns. Because the author left no verdict, the texts themselves must supply one, and the texts were not published in an order that makes the task straightforward. The chronology of publication, as later sections will show, distorted the debate for half a century, and correcting for that distortion is the single most clarifying move a reader can make.

### What did Marx actually write before the supposed break?

Before 1845 Marx wrote journalism, a critique of Hegel's philosophy of right, an introduction attacking religion as consolation, studies of political economy excerpting Smith, Say and Mill, and the manuscripts of 1844 with their account of estranged labour. The writing is philosophical in idiom, saturated with Feuerbach, and organised around human essence and its loss.

That direct answer conceals how mixed the early corpus already is. The young Marx of the legend is a pure philosopher who discovered economics late, but the manuscripts of 1844 are notebooks on political economy, filled with excerpts from the classical economists and with attempts to restate their categories. Wages, profit and rent are already the organising triad. What the young writer lacked was not contact with economics but a critique of its categories: he took over the economists' terms and pressed moral and philosophical charges against the system they described. Conversely, the mature Marx of the legend is a pure economist who left philosophy behind, but the first chapter of Capital contains the most concentrated piece of philosophical argument in his entire output, the analysis of the commodity form and its fetish character. The legends of a philosophical youth and an economic maturity survive because each camp exhibits the texts that flatter its periodisation and shelves the rest. An honest map has to include the economics in the early work and the philosophy in the late work from the start, which already tells against any wall built between them.

The early writings also contain the seed of a difficulty that runs through the whole debate: Marx's relation to Ludwig Feuerbach. The manuscripts of 1844 work with Feuerbach's notion of species-being, the idea that human beings possess a communal essence from which capitalist labour estranges them. The Theses on Feuerbach, eleven compressed paragraphs written in the spring of 1845, turn against Feuerbach himself, charging that his materialism contemplates individuals in isolation and misses practice, the sensuous human activity through which people make and remake their world. Whatever else the theses do, they mark a genuine change of philosophical allegiance and method. The break camp treats them as the hinge of the century. The continuity camp replies that rejecting Feuerbach's contemplative method is not the same as rejecting the account of estranged labour, which can survive translation into a theory of practice. Both readings find support in the eleven theses, because the theses really do break with a master while retaining problems the master had posed. A hinge, in other words, is not necessarily a wall: a door and the room beyond it belong to the same house even when the door swings.

## The case for the break, stated at full strength

The break thesis reached its sharpest form in the work of Louis Althusser, above all in the essays collected as For Marx and in Reading Capital, both published in 1965. Althusser proposed that Marx's work divides into periods separated by what he called, borrowing a phrase from the history of science, an epistemological break. In his periodisation, the early works run to 1844 and remain ideological, governed by a humanist problematic inherited from Feuerbach and, behind him, from the young Hegelians. The works of the break itself are concentrated in 1845: the Theses on Feuerbach and The German Ideology, where Marx and Engels settle accounts with their former philosophical conscience and found the materialist conception of history. A transitional period follows, running through the late eighteen forties and most of the fifties, in which the new science is still clearing its ground. The mature works begin around 1857, with the Grundrisse as a still imperfect threshold and Capital as the achieved science of the capitalist mode of production. Readers who want the philosopher in his own right can consult the intellectual biography of [Louis Althusser](/2015/12/15/louis-althusser-intellectual-biography/), which sets out this scheme in its original polemical setting.

The vocabulary of the scheme matters. Althusser did not say merely that Marx developed, or matured, or changed his emphases. Every serious reader grants development. The claim was stronger: that the questions themselves changed. In the early works, on this reading, history is explained by an essence of man that is lost and will be recovered, so that communism figures as the solution of the riddle of history, the restoration of an estranged humanity to itself. In the mature works, explanation runs the other way: there is no essence doing the work, only structures, relations of production, and the concepts proper to them, such as mode of production, productive forces, surplus value and labour power, none of which has a counterpart in the manuscripts of 1844. A problematic, in Althusser's technical sense, is the invisible framework that decides which questions can be asked and what counts as an answer. The break is a change of problematic, and within a new problematic the old words, when they survive at all, survive with new meanings assigned by their place in a different conceptual system. That is the strongest form of the thesis, and any verdict that ignores it will have answered a weaker claim than the one actually made.

Supporting evidence for the break is not hard to find, and the continuity side does itself no favours by pretending otherwise. The German Ideology, written in 1845 and 1846, mocks the philosophical vocabulary of the young Hegelians with a ferocity that extends to the idiom of essence and alienation itself; its authors announce that they are setting out from real individuals and their material conditions, not from what men say or imagine themselves to be. Anyone tracing the alleged rupture should read the guide to [The German Ideology](/2010/03/01/german-ideology-readers-guide/) alongside the manuscripts, because the change of tone is unmistakable. Then there is the pattern of Marx's own publication choices. He published the Poverty of Philosophy in 1847, an attack on Proudhon written in the new polemical manner. He never published the manuscripts of 1844, never returned to them for material, and allowed The German Ideology, in his own later phrase, to be abandoned to the gnawing criticism of the mice. A writer who believed the early manuscripts contained the heart of his project might be expected to have rescued at least some of them. The break camp also points to the architecture of Capital, whose central categories are derived from the commodity, not from human essence, and whose argument about exploitation rests on the distinction between labour and labour power, a distinction the young Marx did not possess. On the strongest version of the case, the young writings explain capitalism as the loss of man's essence, whereas the mature writings explain it as a system of relations with its own laws of motion, and these are different theories that happen to reach adjacent conclusions.

One more consideration gives the break thesis its plausibility, and it concerns what a science is. Althusser wrote in a French intellectual culture where the history of science, especially the work of Gaston Bachelard, had made the idea of epistemological rupture familiar: sciences are born by breaking with the ideological notions that precede them, as chemistry broke with alchemy. Transferred to Marx, the model yields a satisfying narrative in which philosophy plays the role of the pre scientific stage, honoured for its aspirations and discarded for its method. The narrative also served an evident purpose inside the French Communist Party, where Althusser was a member and an internal critic: against a party humanism that quoted the young Marx to soften doctrine after 1956, the break thesis defended the rigour of the mature theory and demoted the manuscripts to a document of prehistory. A thesis can be both an instrument in a party argument and a serious contribution to scholarship, and this one was plainly both. Its defenders would say that the polemical occasion explains the timing of the intervention, not its truth, and they would be right to insist on the distinction. The truth of the periodisation has to be tested against the manuscripts themselves, which is where the case begins to strain.

## The case for continuity, stated at full strength

The continuity case begins with an inventory, and the inventory is embarrassing for any strong version of the break. Alienation is not a word Marx used in his twenties and then dropped. The manuscripts of 1844 analyse estranged labour under four aspects: the worker is estranged from the product, from the activity of production, from species-being, and from other people. A full guide to [Marx on alienation](/2011/04/01/marx-on-alienation-complete-guide/) traces how each aspect is specified, and the striking fact for the periodisation question is how much of that specification survives translation into the later work. The Grundrisse, drafted in the winter of 1857 and 1858 as the preparatory manuscript for the critique of political economy, speaks repeatedly of labour confronting the worker as an alien power, of the conditions of production standing over against living labour as objectified labour belonging to another, and of the worker's own accumulated past activity returning as capital that dominates him. Capital itself, in the fetishism section of its first chapter, describes a world in which the social relations of producers assume the fantastic form of relations between things, which is the estrangement of the manuscripts restated as a doctrine of form. The chapter on machinery describes the workshop as an automaton in which the worker serves the system of machinery rather than using his tools, an inversion of subject and object that any reader of 1844 will recognise on sight.

The continuity defenders built their case into a literature. Erich Fromm's Marx's Concept of Man, published in 1961 with the first English translation of the manuscripts at its heart, presented the young Marx as the key to the whole and helped ignite the humanist reading in the English speaking world. Istvan Meszaros, in Marx's Theory of Alienation of 1970, produced the most systematic statement: alienation, he argued, is not a detachable youthful theme but the critical core that the economic categories of Capital presuppose, since without it the extraction of surplus value would register as a neutral technical fact rather than as a relation of domination. Bertell Ollman's Alienation, published in 1971, reconstructed the concept with philosophical precision and showed how a theory of internal relations connects the early formulations to the mature method. Behind these worked a longer tradition of readers, from the editors who first published the manuscripts in 1932 to the dissident Marxists of Eastern Europe, for whom the early writings supplied a Marx who criticised bureaucratic domination as readily as market domination. The tradition that organised itself around these texts is surveyed in the account of [Marxist humanism](/2011/07/15/marxist-humanism-explained/), and its scholarly wing made a claim that is independent of its politics: that the textual record, read whole, shows one project developing rather than two projects colliding.

Continuity also has an argument from the logic of the later theory, and it may be the strongest card in the continuity hand. Capital explains how surplus value is extracted, but the explanation leaves a question hanging: why should the reader regard the extraction as a wrong, or even as a fact of more than accounting interest? The mature Marx rarely pauses to say. The early writings supply the normative architecture that makes the later critique a critique rather than a description: labour is the activity through which human beings objectify themselves and recognise themselves in a transformed world, and a system that turns that activity into a torment, its product into a hostile power, and other people into competitors or instruments, is indicted by standards the young Marx articulated before he knew how the system worked. On the continuity reading, the development after 1845 is the discovery of mechanisms adequate to an indictment already formed. The young Marx knew what was wrong and lacked the science of it; the mature Marx built the science and trusted the reader to remember what was wrong. A comparison with the anthropology at the centre of the early work, examined in the guide to [Marx's species-being](/2011/05/15/marx-species-being-explained/), shows how much of the later critique of the working day, of machinery and of accumulation quietly presupposes that account of what labour is for a human being.

There is biographical evidence as well, of a modest but cumulative kind. Marx returned repeatedly, across the decades, to themes announced in the early writings: the critique of religion as inverted consciousness gives way to the critique of fetishism, which is its economic generalisation; the early attacks on the state as an illusory community anticipate the later analysis of political forms; the Jewish Question essay of 1844, whatever its other difficulties, already distinguishes political emancipation from human emancipation and already treats the rights of man as the rights of egoistic man in civil society, an analysis whose structure reappears wherever the mature work separates the citizen from the bourgeois. None of these parallels is identity, and the continuity side weakens its case when it claims identity. What the parallels establish is a lineage of problems. The questions persist while the instruments for answering them are rebuilt, in some cases rebuilt so thoroughly that the answers look like new theories. Whether persistence of questions with transformation of instruments amounts to continuity or to break is exactly the point on which the two camps divide, and it cannot be settled by counting words. It can, however, be settled in part by a text that neither camp had properly absorbed when the lines of battle were drawn.

### Why does the Grundrisse matter more than any other single text here?

The Grundrisse was drafted in 1857 and 1858, squarely inside the years the break thesis calls mature, yet its pages return constantly to alienation, objectification and the inversion of subject and object. A break dated 1845 cannot explain why the economist of 1857 still thinks in the vocabulary he supposedly discarded twelve years earlier.

The point deserves expansion, because it is the fulcrum of the whole article. The Grundrisse is not a marginal notebook or a stray letter. It is a manuscript of roughly eight hundred pages in the standard English edition, written at white heat during the economic crisis of 1857, in which Marx worked out for the first time the full architecture of his critique: money, capital, surplus value, circulation, the forms preceding capitalist production, and the famous fragment on machines in which automation points beyond labour time as the measure of wealth. This is the workshop of Capital, the place where the mature theory was actually assembled. If the epistemological break had done what the strong thesis claims, this is the last place where the old problematic should be found presiding. Yet the manuscript's governing critical figure is precisely the one the young Marx made his own: objectified labour, dead labour, confronting the living worker as an alien and hostile power, appropriating his activity and returning it to him as the domination of capital. The language is not decorative. It does analytical work, marking the difference between labour's product as the worker's own objectification and the same product accumulated as another's property. A reader who wants to test this claim against the manuscript's own structure can follow the guide to [the Grundrisse](/2013/08/01/grundrisse-readers-guide/), which maps the passages where the alienation vocabulary carries the argument about machinery and about precapitalist forms.

The circumstances of the manuscript's composition sharpen the point in a way readers should not miss. Marx wrote the Grundrisse in a state of expectation, convinced that the commercial crisis breaking in 1857 might be the overture to revolution, and determined to have the theoretical foundations of his critique set down before events outran him. The notebooks were therefore no leisure exercise in reminiscence; they were the most urgent writing of his life to that date, the place where everything he had learned in a decade of study was being forced into a single architecture under time pressure. Material written under that pressure reveals what a thinker actually relies on, as distinct from what he professes in calmer methodological statements, and what Marx relied on, page after page, was the figure of labour estranged from its conditions and its products, now carried by the new machinery of value analysis. A conversion completed twelve years earlier should have left that figure behind precisely here, in the forced draft where habits of thought declare themselves. Its centrality here, of all places, is the biographical form of the textual argument, and it converges with the textual form from an independent direction.

Nor is the Grundrisse an isolated survival, a fossil bed sealed off from the published theory. Between the manuscript and Capital stands a chain of drafts: the Contribution of 1859, the economic manuscripts of 1861 to 1863 with their enormous historical excavation of earlier theories of surplus value, and the draft chapter known as the Results of the Immediate Process of Production, written in the middle eighteen sixties as a bridge into Capital and then withheld from the published volume. The Results speaks the language of formal and real subsumption, of labour subsumed under capital first in its inherited form and then in a form revolutionised by capital itself, and it describes the product and the conditions of labour confronting the producer as alien powers with a directness that recalls 1844 in everything except systematic finish. When Capital finally appeared in 1867, the fetishism section condensed the same insight into the doctrine that social relations between men assume the form of relations between things. The thread from the manuscripts through the workshop drafts to the published masterpiece is unbroken. What changes along the thread is the analytical machinery wrapped around the insight: value form analysis, the labour and labour power distinction, the theory of surplus value. What does not change is the insight itself, that under this mode of production the worker's own activity and its products stand against him as powers of an alien subject, capital. That is the textual situation the break thesis has to explain, and explaining it away as mere verbal survival concedes the debate's central question, since the survival is structural as well as verbal.

## The publication lag argument

Here the article's namable claim can be stated in full. The publication lag argument holds that the break thesis was built on a canon whose composition dates and publication dates diverge by up to ninety years, and that once the works are ordered by when they were written rather than by when readers could obtain them, the vocabulary and the problem of alienation run continuously through Marx's career in a way the strong break thesis cannot accommodate. The divergence is startling when it is laid out. The manuscripts of 1844 were written in Paris and left unpublished at Marx's death; they appeared in print only in 1932, in the early volumes of the collected works then being edited in Moscow and Berlin. The German Ideology, composed in 1845 and 1846, waited until the same collected works for its full publication, eighty six years after it was drafted, apart from fragments. The Theses on Feuerbach were published by Engels in 1888, more than four decades after their composition, as an appendix to his own pamphlet on Feuerbach. The Grundrisse, drafted in 1857 and 1858, was published in Moscow in 1939 and 1941, on the eve and in the middle of a world war that guaranteed it few readers, and became generally available only with the Berlin edition of 1953; the complete English translation arrived in 1973. The Results of the Immediate Process waited until 1933 for a Russian publication and far longer for wide circulation. Even Capital was a staggered revelation, its second and third volumes edited by Engels from manuscripts and published in 1885 and 1894, years after the author's death.

Now consider the sequence in which the twentieth century actually met these texts, because the debate was formed by that sequence and not by the order of composition. Readers of the early twentieth century knew a Marx assembled from Capital, the Manifesto, the polemics and the historical journalism. The manuscripts of 1932 landed in that settled picture like a dispatch from an unknown country, and the first effect was astonishment: here was a philosophical Marx, Hegelian in idiom, preoccupied with estrangement, who seemed to belong to a different intellectual world from the economist. The sense of two Marxes was born in that moment of reception, and it is intelligible as a product of publication history rather than of the writing itself. A corpus that arrives out of order manufactures discontinuities. When the Grundrisse then surfaced into general circulation in the nineteen fifties and sixties, it should have complicated the picture at once, since here was the mature economist thinking in the young philosopher's terms. The complication was registered, and scholars of several camps, including David McLellan in his studies of the young Marx and the Grundrisse translation project, pressed it. But by the time the Grundrisse was widely read in English, in the nineteen seventies, the break thesis had already been formulated, institutionalised and translated into the vocabularies of several disciplines. Althusser's intervention of 1965 was made when the Grundrisse was newly circulating in French intellectual life yet before its evidence had been digested by the scholarship at large. The strong break was, in that precise sense, a thesis of the interval between two publications: framed after the manuscripts had made the young Marx vivid, before the Grundrisse had made the mature Marx strange.

The remedy the publication lag argument prescribes is a discipline of reading. Order the works by composition. Then ask of each period not which words it uses but what work its central categories perform. Students and researchers who keep their own manuscripts, excerpts and periodisation tables in order will find that [saving notes and building a citation-linked reading list on VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) makes this discipline practical, since a reading list sorted by date of writing rather than date of publication is the single most useful artefact a student of this debate can possess. Under that discipline the picture that emerges is neither of the legend's two Marxes. It is the picture of a writer whose earliest critique fastened on estrangement as the experienced form of a system he could not yet analyse, who spent two decades building the analysis, and whose workshop drafts show the early figure of thought still load bearing inside the finished structure. The lag argument does not deny change. It denies that the change has the shape the strong thesis requires, a shape that looks suspiciously like the shadow cast by the publication schedule onto the writing life.

### How did the order of publication shape what readers could believe?

The early writings reached print in 1932, the Grundrisse only decades later, so generations of readers met the philosophical Marx as a revelation and the economist's workshop as an afterthought. Judgements formed on that sequence mistook the history of editing for the history of thought, and periodisations hardened before the decisive evidence was in general circulation.

To see how deep the distortion runs, consider what a periodisation is worth when it is drawn across a partially invisible corpus. Althusser's scheme assigned the Grundrisse a transitional status, a work still on the near side of full maturity, and that assignment did a great deal of quiet work, because it allowed the manuscript's alienation language to be filed as residue rather than as evidence. The filing was possible because few of the scheme's first readers could check the manuscript for themselves. Translation compounded the lag: the French and English receptions of Marx ran years behind the German texts and decades behind the manuscripts, so that debates in Paris and London were conducted over different subsets of the corpus and then mistaken for debates over the whole. Editorial framing added a further twist, since the 1932 editors presented the manuscripts within an official collected works whose institutional sponsors had their own reasons for managing the young Marx carefully. Every stage of transmission selected, and every selection nudged interpretation. None of this makes the break thesis a mere artefact of editing; the changes of 1845 are real, as the next sections grant in full. It does mean that the confidence with which a clean rupture was asserted exceeded what the available evidence licensed, and that the subsequent arrival of the workshop drafts should have lowered that confidence more than it did. Scholarship, like the markets Marx analysed, is path dependent: an interpretation entrenched in syllabuses and translations acquires defences that later evidence must besiege rather than simply address.

A classification that its two most important workshop texts disobey is not thereby refuted in every particular, since classifications can survive exceptions, but the burden shifts. The break defender must now argue that the disobedient vocabulary is inert, that the words survive while the concept has been replaced, and that argument, the strongest available to the break camp, deserves a section of its own.

## Vocabulary is not concept: the strongest objection

Fairness requires the objection in its most formidable form, and it is formidable. Continuity of vocabulary, the objection runs, is not continuity of concept. A word can persist while the theoretical system that gives it meaning is dismantled and rebuilt, and in the rebuilt system the old word performs different work. When the Grundrisse speaks of alienation, it speaks inside an analysis of value, money and capital that the young Marx did not possess; estrangement there names a relation internal to the capital relation itself, derived from the forms of exchange and production, not a falling away from a human essence posited in advance. The young Marx explained capitalism by an anthropology. The mature Marx, even when he borrows the anthropology's words, explains by structures in which no essence appears as a premise. Pointing at the recurring word therefore proves nothing about the theory, and the continuity case, insofar as it rests on the word, rests on a philological illusion. Reinforcements for the objection are at hand. Thinkers routinely retain terms across genuine revolutions in their thought, as the word substance survives from scholasticism into Spinoza with its meaning inverted, and as Marx himself retains value, labour and capital from the economists while redefining each. If redefinition is compatible with break everywhere else, why should alienation be the exception?

### What separates a change of method from a change of mind?

A change of mind discards the earlier problem as misconceived, while a change of method keeps the problem and rebuilds the instruments for solving it. Marx after 1845 keeps the problem of labour dominated by its own products and rebuilds every instrument, which is why the development reads as transformation rather than repudiation.

The answer to the objection must therefore shift its ground, from vocabulary to function, and on that ground the continuity case can be rebuilt in a form the objection does not touch. Ask what the category of alienation does in each period. In 1844 it does three things. It names the experienced structure of work under private property, in which activity is coerced, product is expropriated and fellow producers appear as competitors. It supplies the critical standard by which that structure is judged, the standard of a labour that would be free, social and self directed. And it frames the historical claim that the system producing estrangement also produces the class whose interest lies in abolishing it. Now ask what the corresponding figures do in the mature work. The fetishism analysis names the experienced structure of a society in which producers are governed by the movement of their own products. The critique of the working day and of machinery supplies the standard in negative form, measuring the working life against what the development of the productive forces makes possible. The theory of accumulation frames the historical claim about the class the system produces. Function by function, the architecture corresponds. What has vanished is the word species-being as a load bearing premise, and what has replaced the anthropology is a theory of forms and relations that derives the estrangement from the structure of commodity production instead of asserting it as the loss of an essence. That is a change in the foundations of the explanation. It is not a change in what is being explained, nor in the judgement passed on it, nor in the historical wager attached to it.

Concession is due in the other direction as well, and the verdict of this article depends on making it cleanly. The strongest continuity claims overreach. A reading that treats Capital as the manuscripts with statistics, or that finds the whole later theory already present in embryo in 1844, dissolves a real intellectual labour of twenty years into a mere unfolding, and cannot explain why the labour and labour power distinction, the analysis of the value form, or the theory of surplus value required the long detour through the British Museum. Nor can crude continuity explain Marx's own polemical violence against the idiom of his youth in 1845 and 1846, which was more than a change of stationery. The break camp is right that something ended around 1845: the Feuerbachian framework, the philosophy of essence and return, the confidence that criticism of religion supplied the model for criticism of society. Something ended, and something else demonstrably continued, and a periodisation honest about both will describe the joint between them as a transformation with a datable hinge rather than as either a wall or a mirage.

### What would count as proof of a genuine rupture?

A genuine rupture would be proved by mature texts that repudiate the early problem itself, by categories in Capital that cannot be stated without contradicting the manuscripts, or by an explicit retrospective disowning of the estrangement critique. None of the three exists, and the workshop drafts point the other way, which is why the strong thesis fails.

## Why the debate became politically charged

Periodisation disputes inside a scholarly field rarely generate heat on their own; the heat in this case came from politics, and the political history explains both the timing of the break thesis and the passion of its opponents. The manuscripts of 1844, once published in 1932, were taken up slowly, and their wide influence dates from after 1956, when the secret speech in Moscow and the crushing of the Hungarian rising drove Marxists inside and outside the communist parties to search for a Marx who could criticise the regimes ruling in his name. The young Marx answered that need almost perfectly. His subject was domination in work itself, the reduction of human activity to a means of survival, and his standard was a rich human emancipation that no existing state could plausibly claim to have achieved. Humanist Marxisms flourished on that basis across both halves of a divided Europe: in the Yugoslav Praxis school, in the Budapest school around the legacy of Lukacs, in the work of dissident philosophers in Poland and Czechoslovakia, and in the Western movements of the nineteen sixties for whom the early writings licensed a Marxism of liberation rather than of administration. To defend continuity, in those decades, was often to defend that political possibility. To assert the break was, correspondingly, to defend an orthodoxy of the mature doctrine against dilution, whether the orthodoxy was a party's or a seminar's.

Althusser's own intervention belongs inside that charged field and cannot be understood outside it. Writing as a philosopher within the French Communist Party, he confronted a party leadership that had embraced a language of humanism after 1956 and read the young Marx as its charter. His anti-humanism was a counter intervention: by relocating the scientific Marx on the far side of an epistemological break, he made the manuscripts inadmissible as evidence about the mature theory and restored Capital to the centre of doctrine. The move was directed at least as much against his own party's official humanism as against any academic opponent, and its stakes were the orientation of a mass organisation, not merely the reading list of a faculty. Opponents replied in kind. Roger Garaudy, for a time the French party's leading philosopher before his expulsion, defended the humanist reading from within the same institution, and the argument between the camps was conducted with an intensity that only makes sense when a tradition's identity is felt to be at issue. Similar charges ran through the Eastern European debates, where the young Marx was a resource of reform communists and a target of their censors, and where the question of which Marx was authentic could decide whether a book was published, a chair kept, or a journal closed.

Two cautions belong to any honest account of this political history. The first is that political motivation does not settle scholarly questions in either direction. The humanists had reasons of state and conscience for wanting continuity, and Althusser had reasons of party strategy for wanting a break, and none of those reasons shows which reading the manuscripts support; the texts remain the tribunal, and this article has tried to keep them so. The second caution is that the political charge explains the debate's exaggerations. Where a periodisation carries the fate of movements, each side is tempted to state its case absolutely: a total break with nothing carried over, or a seamless continuity with nothing genuinely new. The textual record, as the preceding sections have shown, supports neither absolute. It supports a hinge and a transformation, and the tragedy of the debate's political decades is that the middle position, which the evidence favours, was the one position neither camp could use. A scholarly generation freer of those stakes can afford to occupy it, and the best recent work on Marx's development, attentive to the drafts and the notebooks, increasingly does.

## Misreadings the debate attracts

Five misreadings recur so reliably that naming them is a service in itself. The first presents the break as a settled fact of scholarship, a date learned like the date of a battle, when in truth the strongest living scholarship on the manuscripts and the drafts treats strong rupture as a position to be argued, not a premise to be assumed. The second divides the corpus into a philosophical youth and an economic maturity, when the early notebooks are steeped in political economy and the mature masterpiece opens with a philosophical analysis of form; the division mistakes the binding of the books for the character of the thought. The third credits Althusser with inventing the idea of a break, when intuitions of a divide between the Hegelian apprentice and the economist run back through earlier commentary and even through the editorial framing of the 1932 publication itself; Althusser's contribution was to sharpen the intuition into a doctrine with a date, a mechanism and a philosophical pedigree, which is a different and more interesting achievement. The fourth assumes that accepting continuity enlists the reader in Marxist humanism as a politics, when the textual claim and the political programme are separable: a scholar can hold that alienation persists as a category in Capital while rejecting every political conclusion the humanists drew, and many do. The fifth, and the most consequential for general readers, treats the debate as a merely academic quarrel about shelving, when its outcome decides whether the most famous critique of capitalism in the modern world rests on a theory of human flourishing or expressly refuses to, and therefore decides what kind of argument a reader of Capital is actually being given.

## The verdict: continuity of concern, discontinuity of method

The evidence now permits a verdict, and the verdict is a defended middle: continuity of concern with discontinuity of method. The concern that organises Marx's work from 1844 to the end is the domination of producers by the accumulated products and organised forms of their own labour, and the possibility of a society in which that domination is abolished. The method by which the concern is pursued changes decisively around 1845 and keeps changing thereafter: from a Feuerbachian anthropology of essence and estrangement, through the materialist conception of history announced in 1845 and 1846, to the critique of political economy whose value form analysis derives the phenomena of estrangement from the structure of commodity production itself. The publication lag argument explains why the change of method was misread as a change of mind: the corpus arrived out of order, the young philosopher was met as a stranger in 1932, the workshop that joins him to the economist circulated too late to prevent the stranger from being mistaken for a second author. Ordered by composition, the career shows a hinge, and a hinge is the right image: two panels, one movement, a real joint that bears weight precisely because the panels it joins are solid on either side.

### Is there a break between the young and the mature Marx?

There is a real change in method and vocabulary around 1845, but not a clean break. The Grundrisse, written in the mature period, uses alienation language throughout, and Capital retains recognisable descendants of the early categories. Continuity of concern with discontinuity of method is the defensible position.

One clarification protects the verdict against a predictable misreading of its own. Continuity of concern does not mean that the young Marx was right about everything the concern touches, nor that the mature work endorses the manuscripts' every judgement; the essay on the Jewish question, the crude communism passage and the reliance on Feuerbach's anthropology all contain positions the later Marx abandoned, revised or would have had to disown, and the verdict's continuity is located in the problem and the standard, not in a blanket vindication of the early texts. Discontinuity of method, likewise, does not mean that the method of 1844 was worthless; the inversion of Hegel's state and civil society and the critique of merely political emancipation were methodological achievements of the first rank, achieved with the early instruments. The conjunction is exact because the record is exact in both directions, and a reader who carries the two clauses together, refusing to let either swallow the other, holds the position that the whole of the evidence, ledgers and exhibits and chronology alike, was assembled to support.

The deciding consideration, if one must be named, is the function test applied to the workshop drafts. A thesis about what a thinker abandoned must survive contact with the texts in which the later work was actually composed, and the Grundrisse and the Results are those texts. Their estrangement figures are not quotations from the author's youth or ornaments upon an alien structure; they organise the analysis of machinery, of subsumption, of the inversion of subject and object that the published Capital condenses into fetishism. What would overturn the verdict is equally specifiable: a mature text in which Marx identifies the estrangement problematic itself as an error, or a demonstration that the fetishism analysis can be fully restated without any equivalent of the inversion the early work described. Neither has been produced. Until one is, the strong break remains a brilliant overstatement, valuable for the rigour with which it forced readers to specify what changed in 1845, and false in its central claim that what changed was the problem itself.

## For students and exam candidates

This debate is a standard postgraduate essay question, and the periodisation table above is the structure of a strong answer. Begin by stating the break thesis precisely, with its dates: ideological works to 1844, the break in 1845, transition, maturity from the late eighteen fifties. Credit Althusser by name and give his reason, the change of problematic, rather than a vague claim that Marx grew up. Then deploy the Grundrisse as the counterweight, noting when it was written and when it could be read, since the lag between those dates is the most original point available to you. Close with the function test: vocabulary may migrate while concepts change, so compare what alienation does in 1844 with what fetishism does in 1867. Examiners reward that sequence because it states a position, tests it against the hardest text, and concedes exactly what the other side gets right about method. The commonest failure is the survey that reports both camps and concludes that the truth lies somewhere between, without saying where or on what evidence.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The most reliable seminar on this material runs the debate twice, once with the chronology withheld and once with it revealed. In the first session, distribute extracts from the manuscripts of 1844 and from the fetishism section of Capital without dates, and let the group argue from style and vocabulary alone; most groups will construct some version of the break unaided, which teaches them that the thesis answers to something real in the texts. In the second session, hand out the periodisation table, composition dates against publication dates, and watch the argument reorganise itself around the Grundrisse. Assign the three positions in advance, break, continuity and transformation, and require each presenter to state the opposing case before defending their own. The exercise leaves students with a transferable skill: suspicion of any intellectual biography whose turning points align too neatly with the history of publishing. It also enlivens a topic that can die in summary, because students defend positions they have watched collapse and rebuild within a single afternoon.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

For researchers, the lasting yield of this debate is a method: composition dates against publication dates as a general instrument for reading any thinker's development. Corpora that arrive out of order manufacture false ruptures and false anticipations alike, and the Marx case is the best documented example of the mechanism, complete with editors, translators and political sponsors at every stage of transmission. The working rules follow directly. Cite manuscripts by date of writing, and treat first publication as an event in reception history rather than in the author's life. Distrust periodisations proposed during intervals when key texts were unavailable, and retest them when the corpus changes, as this one did with the Grundrisse and again with the complete historical critical edition of Marx and Engels, whose volumes keep altering what can be claimed about drafts and notebooks. Journalists face a narrower version of the same discipline. The two Marxes headline is a gift to editors and a trap for accuracy: the defensible short form is that scholars dispute how far the economist of Capital remained the philosopher of alienation, that the strongest single text in the argument is a manuscript published ninety years after it was written, and that the answer most specialists would now defend distinguishes continuity of concern from discontinuity of method. Anything shorter is caricature, and anything more confident is advocacy wearing the dress of reportage.

## What remains open

A verdict can be defended without the file being closed, and three questions remain genuinely open. A fourth deserves mention alongside them, because it concerns the terms in which the whole debate has been conducted. The opposition of young and mature is itself a biographical metaphor, borrowed from the lives of artists, and it smuggles in the suggestion that the later work's superiority, where it exists, is the superiority of age over youth, of experience over ardour. The documentary record resists the metaphor at both ends. The manuscripts of 1844 contain analysis of a precision that many older writers never reach, and the work of the eighteen sixties contains ardour, invective and speculative daring in undiminished supply. What actually divides the corpus is not the author's age but his equipment: the presence or absence of a theory of value, of the labour and labour power distinction, of an account of the forms in which social relations appear. Periodisations stated in terms of equipment, rather than of birthdays, lose the drama of the two statues and gain the ability to say exactly what changed and when, which is the exchange this article has recommended throughout. Programmes of research that adopt the equipment vocabulary find the old debate quietly reformatted beneath them: the question becomes which instruments each text commands, what work each instrument does, and whether the problems addressed persist across the changes of tooling. That is a question with answers, and the answers converge on the verdict defended here from several independent directions.

The convergence is worth one further remark, because it bears on how much confidence the verdict deserves. The publication lag argument approaches the question from the history of the book. The function test approaches it from the internal structure of the concepts. The Lukacs episode approaches it from the reception history, as a natural experiment in what the published mature work contains. The late Marx material approaches it from the biography's far end, where the pattern of serial transformation repeats. Four methods, four bodies of evidence, one shape in the result: continuity of concern, discontinuity of method, a hinge at 1845 and further hinges after. Verdicts in the humanities rarely enjoy that kind of triangulation, and where they do, the remaining disputes can be left to the specialists with a clear conscience. The reader who came to this article for a side to take has been given something better and harder: a distinction to apply, a table to consult, a reading order to follow, and a standing instruction to check the dates of writing before believing any story about a thinker's development, in this corpus or in any other where the archive arrived out of order. The first concerns the late Marx: the notebooks on anthropology, natural science and non Western societies from the final years complicate every periodisation drawn across the earlier decades, and a scheme built for 1844 against 1867 may need a third panel for the Marx of the Zasulich correspondence and the Russian commune. The second concerns the status of the anthropology itself: whether the mature work's silence about human essence is a rejection, a presupposition too deep to state, or a genuine replacement by the theory of forms is a question the function test narrows without eliminating. The third is editorial and unfinished: as the complete edition publishes the notebooks in full, the map of what Marx read and excerpted in each period will keep shifting, and with it the fine grain of any account of his development. What will not shift is the discipline this debate teaches. Read the works in the order they were written. Ask what each category does, not merely which words recur. And when a thinker appears to be two people, check the publication history before believing it, because the gap between a writing life and its arrival in print has manufactured more than one phantom author, and in this case it very nearly succeeded.

## The early writings, text by text

A periodisation is only as good as its acquaintance with the texts it sorts, and the early corpus rewards a slower walk than the legends allow. Begin with the doctoral dissertation of 1841, on the difference between the Democritean and Epicurean philosophies of nature, a work of classical scholarship in which the young Marx already prefers the philosopher of self consciousness and swerve to the philosopher of mechanical necessity. Nothing in it predicts the critique of political economy, yet its temper is prophetic: the interest falls on the moment where a system of atoms admits deviation, freedom and history. The journalism for the Rheinische Zeitung in 1842 and 1843 then forced the philosopher into contact with material interests: debates on the law on thefts of wood, where customary rights of the poor collided with the property claims of forest owners, and on the distress of the Moselle wine farmers, where bureaucratic rationality met peasant ruin. Marx himself later identified those embarrassments, a philosopher obliged to pronounce on material questions he did not understand, as the spur to his study of political economy. The continuity camp draws attention to the sequence because it complicates the portrait of a pure philosopher surprised by economics: the economics entered through the courthouse and the vineyard, through law and administration, before it entered through the textbooks.

The Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of Right, drafted in 1843 at Kreuznach, performs the famous inversion: where Hegel made the state the subject and civil society its predicate, Marx reverses the terms, treating family and civil society as the premises of which the state is the product. The manuscript's companion introduction, published in the Deutsch-Franzosische Jahrbucher in 1844, contains the description of religion as the opium of the people, a phrase whose full sentence is more sympathetic than its afterlife suggests, since religion there is the sigh of the oppressed creature and the heart of a heartless world before it is a consolation to be criticised. The same introduction assigns the proletariat its world historic role for the first time in Marx's writing, as the class whose emancipation contains the emancipation of humanity because its chains are radical and its suffering universal. Alongside it in the same journal appeared the essay on the Jewish question, a reply to Bruno Bauer arguing that political emancipation, the granting of rights by a secular state, falls short of human emancipation because the rights granted are those of egoistic man separated from his fellows. Whatever judgement a reader passes on that essay's treatment of its ostensible subject, and the judgements are rightly severe in places, its analytical skeleton, the split between citizen and bourgeois, state and civil society, is the skeleton on which much of the later critique of political forms is built. The break camp must treat these texts as juvenilia governed by a Feuerbachian problematic. The continuity camp replies that the problems posed in them, the inversion of subject and predicate, the critique of merely political emancipation, the search for a class whose interest is general, are the problems of the whole career in their first dress.

The manuscripts of 1844 themselves deserve description rather than slogans, because their actual structure is stranger and more interesting than either camp's emblem. They are three unfinished notebooks, written in Paris between April and August of that year, combining excerpts from Smith, Ricardo's followers, Say, Mill and others with sustained passages of original argument. The celebrated section on estranged labour works through its four aspects with a rigour that belies the label of lyric philosophy. The product of labour confronts the worker as an alien object because the more he produces, the less he possesses and the mightier the world of things arrayed against him grows. The activity of production is estranged because work is experienced as mortification, a sacrifice of life rather than its expression, fled from when no compulsion binds. Species-being is estranged because the free, conscious, universal character of human production, man's capacity to form things in accordance with the laws of beauty and to contemplate himself in a world he has made, is degraded into a mere means of survival. And man is estranged from man because the product and the activity, alienated from the producer, must belong to another, the capitalist, whose relation to the worker is then deduced from the structure of estrangement itself. The manuscripts go on to sketch communism as the positive transcendence of private property and hence of self estrangement, and to criticise the crude communism of levelling envy as a negation still trapped in the categories it opposes. They also contain, less often quoted, a sustained engagement with Hegel's Phenomenology, praised for grasping labour as the self creation of man and criticised for knowing only abstractly mental labour. A reader who works through the notebooks entire finds not a hymn but an argument in progress, half political economy and half philosophical anthropology, whose unfinished state is itself evidence: this was a workshop, and the question of the debate is what became of the tools forged in it.

## The pivot texts and the hinge years

If the break has a datable home, it is the spring and winter of 1845 and 1846, and the two texts of those months repay the closest reading in the whole dispute. The Theses on Feuerbach are eleven short paragraphs, jotted in a notebook, never prepared for publication by their author, and later described by Engels as the first document in which the germ of the new world outlook was deposited. Their content is a compressed revolution in method. The first thesis charges all previous materialism with conceiving reality only as an object of contemplation, not as sensuous human activity and practice, and charges idealism with developing the active side only abstractly. The sixth thesis dissolves the human essence into the ensemble of social relations, the sentence the break camp quotes most often and the continuity camp most labours to qualify. The eighth thesis declares that social life is essentially practical and that the mysteries of theory find their rational solution in human practice. The eleventh, the most famous sentence Marx ever wrote, opposes interpretation of the world to changing it. Read as the break camp reads them, the theses liquidate the anthropology: no essence, no estrangement from essence, only practice and relations. Read as the continuity camp reads them, the theses change the ground on which estrangement is to be understood, from a fall out of essence into a deformation of practice, and a critique of deformed practice is exactly what the manuscripts had attempted with poorer tools. The notes are short enough that both readings can be maintained with textual decency, which is one reason the debate has lasted: its pivot document is an aphorism set, and aphorisms license reconstruction.

The German Ideology is the opposite problem, an enormous, disorderly, unfinished manuscript in which Marx and Engels attack the young Hegelian milieu from which they had emerged. Its positive doctrine, the materialist conception of history, is stated with a clarity that still startles: the first premise of human history is the production of the means to satisfy needs, the second is the production of new needs, and from production flow the division of labour, forms of intercourse, property relations and eventually the state and the ideologies that present the ruling ideas of an age as universal. Its polemical bulk, hundreds of pages against Stirner alone, is a settling of accounts conducted with savage comedy. For the break thesis the book is the foundation charter of the science, and its contempt for philosophical phrase making is Exhibit A against the manuscripts. For continuity the same book carries surprises. Its account of the division of labour describes individuals fixed in exclusive spheres of activity, hunted by an alien power arising from their own cooperation, a social force that confronts them as external because they have not subdued it to themselves. That is the structure of estrangement restated in the vocabulary of production and intercourse, inside the founding text of the supposed rupture. The authors even preserve the horizon of the manuscripts in their famous sketch of a society in which nobody has an exclusive sphere, the passage about hunting in the morning and criticising after dinner that has been quoted with embarrassment by admirers and with glee by detractors. A break text that retains the early utopia's furniture and the early critique's central figure is a strange sort of break, and the strangeness is data. It suggests that 1845 and 1846 mark a change in what Marx thought his critique rested on, from anthropology to the study of production, without marking a change in what the critique was of or what it was for.

The years immediately following complicate tidy schemes further. The Poverty of Philosophy of 1847, Marx's first published book in the new manner, attacks Proudhon's attempt to eternalise economic categories and insists that categories are as transient as the relations they express, a methodological principle that governs the later critique. The Communist Manifesto of 1848, written with Engels for a league of working men, narrates history as class struggle and ends by summoning the workers of all countries to unite; its rhetoric of emancipation is continuous with the introduction of 1844 even as its engine is the new conception of history. Wage Labour and Capital, delivered as lectures in 1847 and published in 1849, explains wages through the sale of labour power in a form Marx later corrected, since the distinction between labour and labour power, on which the mature theory of surplus value depends, was not yet drawn. That last fact is the break camp's cleanest exhibit from these years: the central concept of Capital is absent, and its absence is theoretical, not verbal. The continuity camp must grant it, and the verdict of this article does grant it, while noting that a missing mechanism is not a discarded problem. The lectures already ask why the product of the workers returns to them as capital commanding their labour; they lack the instrument that will eventually answer the question, and the instrument took another decade to forge.

## The break thesis under fire

Althusser's construction attracted criticism from inside Marxism as fierce as any it attracted from outside, and the criticisms map the weak points that a balanced verdict has to register. The most theatrical assault came from the English historian E. P. Thompson, whose The Poverty of Theory of 1978 denounced Althusserian Marxism as an ahistorical structuralism that abolished human agency, moral argument and the discipline of evidence in favour of a self certifying theoreticism. Thompson wrote as a defender of the humanist and moral Marx, and his polemic, whatever its excesses, identified a real cost in the break doctrine: if the early writings are ideology and the mature science forbids moral vocabulary, then the tradition loses the language in which its own adherents actually protest, a loss Thompson documented from the practice of working class movements themselves. From a different quarter, the analytical and critical Marxisms of the English speaking world pressed the periodisation on scholarly grounds. Norman Geras, in a careful review of the literature on Marx and human nature, argued that a theory of human nature persists in the mature Marx, visible in the distinction between human nature in general and its modification in each epoch that Capital itself draws, and that the denial of any such continuity rests on selective quotation. John Plamenatz and others, writing earlier and from liberal scholarship, had already treated the corpus as one developing whole whose parts illuminate each other, and David McLellan's biographical and textual studies supplied generations of students with the composition chronology that makes the publication lag argument possible.

French debate supplied its own corrections. Within Althusser's circle, the collective project of Reading Capital contained tensions that its participants later acknowledged, and Etienne Balibar's subsequent work moved toward a more supple account of Marx's development, treating the texts as sites of an unfinished transformation rather than as provinces divided by a wall. Althusser himself, in later self criticism, conceded that his earlier formulations had been theoreticist, had exaggerated the science and ideology contrast, and had treated the break with a rigidity his own method should have forbidden. Such concessions do not refute a thesis, since a thesis stands on its evidence and not on its author's subsequent moods, but they weaken the dogmatic form in which the break was transmitted to syllabuses, where a heuristic periodisation hardened into a sorting rule for admissible quotations. The distinction between Althusser's argued position and Althusserianism as a school doctrine matters for fairness, and the same distinction applies on the other side, where a humanist reading attentive to texts degenerated in some hands into a devotional cult of the manuscripts in which every later category was found prefigured in 1844 and the twenty years of economic labour were treated as a long footnote. The debate's polemical decades damaged both inheritances, and one task of a later account is to recover the arguable core of each from the school doctrines that encased it.

A further line of criticism deserves record because it changes the frame rather than the score. Several scholars have questioned whether the humanism and anti-humanism that organise the debate are themselves stable categories. The young Marx's humanism is not the humanism of the Renaissance or of liberal individualism; it is a doctrine of species activity formed inside German philosophy. Althusser's theoretical anti-humanism is not a denial that human beings suffer or act; it is a claim about what can serve as a premise in a science of history. Combatants who ignore those specifications end up refuting positions nobody holds, a pattern visible whenever the break is summarised as the claim that the later Marx stopped caring about workers, or continuity as the claim that nothing changed. The specifications restore the debate to its genuine difficulty, which is considerable enough: how a critique grounded in what human activity is can be rebuilt as a critique grounded in what social forms do, without surrendering the standard by which the forms are judged wanting. That difficulty is not an embarrassment to be dissolved by choosing a camp. It is the intellectual substance of Marx's middle decades, and the periodisation question is best understood as the question of how he worked through it.

## The humanist Marxisms and what they built

The continuity side of the debate was never only a thesis about texts. It was carried by schools, journals and movements whose work constitutes a large part of twentieth century Marxist culture, and a fair account should record what they built with the young Marx as their charter. The Yugoslav Praxis group, gathered around the journal of that name from 1964, developed a philosophy of praxis in which alienation under bureaucratic socialism was criticised with the same instruments the manuscripts applied to capitalism, and paid for the criticism with suppression of the journal and removal of professors. The Budapest school, formed by the pupils of Georg Lukacs, among them Agnes Heller and Gyorgy Markus, worked out a theory of needs and of everyday life in which the early writings supplied the standard of a rich human development against both market and command economies; Markus's study of Marx's concept of human essence remains one of the most exact reconstructions of the anthropology on any side of the debate. In France, Henri Lefebvre had introduced the manuscripts to a wide readership well before the nineteen sixties and built from them a critique of everyday life that influenced the movements of 1968. In the United States, Fromm's edition and the circle around the journal Studies on the Left carried the humanist Marx into the New Left, for which the early writings offered a socialism of liberation distinct from the administered societies of the East. Herbert Marcuse, whose Reason and Revolution of 1941 had already treated the manuscripts' themes through the Hegelian Marx, gave the tradition its most ambitious philosophical synthesis in the following decades.

Against this record the break camp entered a legitimate demurrer, which the continuity side weakens itself by ignoring. A politics built on the manuscripts could slide into moralism, denouncing alienation in general while lacking the economic analysis that identifies mechanisms and levers; and the regimes criticised in the manuscripts' name were not refuted by quotation, since their own ideologies also claimed humanist descent. The demurrer generalises into a principle that this article's verdict respects: the value of a periodisation is tested by what it lets a reader do. The break thesis, whatever its textual overreach, forced readers back to the economic work and to the specificity of its categories, and scholarship is better for that compulsion. The continuity thesis, whatever its occasional sentimentality, preserved the normative core without which the economics is a mechanism in search of a purpose, and the tradition is better for that preservation. A verdict that ranks one contribution as truth and the other as error misses how the debate functioned as a division of labour. The transformation position can honour both functions because it assigns each camp the part of the record it read best: the break camp read 1845, the continuity camp read the drafts, and the whole record is larger than either reading.

## Editors, editions and the manufacture of two Marxes

Behind the publication lag stands a cast of editors whose decisions shaped what could be thought, and their story belongs in any complete account. The first collected edition of Marx and Engels, undertaken in Moscow under David Ryazanov at the Marx-Engels Institute, was a work of genuine scholarship conducted inside an institution with doctrinal obligations; Ryazanov's arrest in 1931 and the edition's interruption belong to the political history of the texts as much as to their bibliography. It was that edition which first printed the manuscripts of 1844 and The German Ideology, in 1932, presenting readers with a young Marx unknown to the tradition that had fought over his legacy for fifty years. A rival German edition of the manuscripts, prepared by Siegfried Landshut and Jacob Peter Mayer in the same year, framed them for a social democratic readership and drew different lessons. The posthumous construction had begun even earlier, with Engels himself, whose editing of the second and third volumes of Capital from manuscripts of varying finish was an act of intellectual executorship that later scholarship, armed with the original drafts, has learned to distinguish from the author's hand. Engels also framed the Theses on Feuerbach for their first readers in 1888, describing them in terms that made them a charter of the new outlook, which is to say that the break interpretation has an editorial pedigree reaching back to the surviving partner of the firm.

The postwar decades added the great translation projects. The fifty volume Collected Works in English, published between 1975 and 2004, made the corpus available to the Anglophone world on a scale that transformed teaching, while the second historical critical edition, the MEGA, resumed in the nineteen seventies and continued after the collapse of the states that sponsored it, began printing the notebooks, excerpts and drafts in full, including the economic manuscripts of the early eighteen sixties and the ethnological notebooks of the final years. Each enlargement of the visible corpus has had the same double effect: old periodisations are embarrassed by new evidence, and new refinements become possible. The ethnological notebooks, published in transcription by Lawrence Krader in 1972, showed a Marx in his last years studying Morgan, Maine and Kovalevsky and annotating communal forms with an intensity that fits neither the humanist youth nor the economist of the legend, which is one reason the late Marx has become a third panel in the periodisation picture. Kevin Anderson's Marx at the Margins assembled the evidence for that later development, tracing the movement from the journalism of the eighteen fifties on India and China to the letters on Ireland and the drafts on the Russian commune in which Marx entertained paths to socialism that bypassed a full capitalist stage. A writer still revising his views on the largest questions in his final decade is a poor candidate for a biography that freezes him into two statues facing each other across 1845. The editorial moral is general and worth stating as a rule: never build a periodisation on an incomplete corpus without marking it provisional, because the archive has a long record of punishing confidence.

## The function test applied, concept by concept

General claims about continuity survive or fall on particular concepts, and the honest way to close the argument is to run the function test down the list of the early work's central categories and record, for each, what became of it. Take species-being first, the most vulnerable of the early notions and the one the break camp exhibits as pure Feuerbach. In 1844 the term names man's life activity as free, conscious and universal, the standard against which estranged labour is measured. In the mature work the term itself all but vanishes, and no honest continuity argument should pretend otherwise. Yet the standard it carried does not vanish. Capital's chapter on the labour process distinguishes human production by the architect and the bee, the result existing in the imagination before it exists in reality, which is the universality and consciousness of species activity restated as a definition of the labour process in general. The Grundrisse speaks of the rich individuality and the free development of human powers as the aim beyond a society organised by labour time, and the third volume of Capital locates the realm of freedom beyond the realm of necessity in the shortening of the working day. The word is gone; the measuring rod remains, naturalised into the theory's account of what labour is and what its liberation would mean. Score that case as transformation, and note that transformation is precisely the verdict this article defends, so the exhibit supports the middle position against both absolutes.

Take estranged labour itself, and the case strengthens. The four aspects of 1844 map onto mature doctrines with a fidelity that coincidence cannot explain. Estrangement from the product becomes the analysis of accumulation, in which the worker's past labour, objectified as capital, returns to command his present labour and to set the terms of his employment. Estrangement from the activity becomes the theory of the working day and of the labour process under capital, with its struggles over length, intensity and the despotism of the workshop. Estrangement from species-being becomes, by the route traced above, the contrast between labour as self activity and labour as mere means of survival that structures the critique from beginning to end. Estrangement from other men becomes the analysis of competition among workers, of the reserve army that sets the employed against the unemployed, and of the fetish forms in which relations between people are mediated by relations between things. In each mapping the mature version is more exact, more historical and more fertile than its ancestor, and in each the ancestor is recognisable. A theory may be transformed in all its instruments and continuous in its architecture, and this fourfold mapping is what that combination looks like when it is demonstrated rather than asserted.

Take the critique of religion, the earliest of Marx's critical exercises, and the pattern repeats in a form the break camp itself often concedes. The introduction of 1844 argued that the criticism of religion is the premise of all criticism because man makes religion and the criticism of heaven must become the criticism of earth. The mature work generalises exactly that move: the critique of political economy is presented as unmasking the religious reflex of the real world in the fetishism of commodities, and the first chapter of Capital speaks of finding the rational kernel within the mystical shell in a passage about method that openly recalls the early inversion of Hegel. The object of unmasking changes from theology to the value form; the operation, dissolving a power that men made and then obey as alien, is the same operation, now performed on the central institution of the society rather than on its creed. Ideology critique in the later tradition descends from that operation, and its genealogy runs through 1844 as plainly as through 1846.

Finally, take the distinction between political and human emancipation, drawn in the essay on the Jewish question and dismissed by strict break readers as youthful moralising. Its mature descendants are the analyses of the state as a form separated from civil society, of formal equality in exchange concealing substantive domination in production, and of the sphere of circulation as a realm of freedom and equality whose promises the hidden abode of production betrays. The famous transition in Capital from the noisy sphere of exchange, where freedom, equality, property and Bentham rule, into the workshop where the money owner strides in front and the labourer follows as one who brings his own hide to market, is the 1844 distinction between the citizen's heaven and the bourgeois's earth rewritten as economic theatre. A reader who cannot see the lineage has been trained not to see it, and the training is the periodisation. Set the four mappings side by side and the shape of the whole development comes into focus: a single critical project, first stated in the language of philosophical anthropology, then rebuilt over twenty years in the language of forms, relations and laws of motion, with its standards, its targets and its historical wager carried across the rebuilding. That is the strongest statement of the continuity case, and it is fully compatible with every genuine novelty the break camp has catalogued, which is why the transformation verdict, and not either absolute, is where the evidence leaves a careful reader.

## The late Marx and the third panel

Every two panel periodisation is embarrassed by the writing of Marx's final decade, and the embarrassment is instructive. The Marx of the eighteen seventies was not resting on the system of Capital. He was studying Russian, following the debates of the Russian populists, excerpting Morgan's Ancient Society and the works of Maine, Lubbock and Kovalevsky into notebooks dense with commentary, and corresponding about whether the Russian rural commune might serve as a point of support for a social regeneration that avoided the full capitalist dissolution he had analysed for Western Europe. The letter to Vera Zasulich of 1881, preceded by four laborious drafts, answers that the historical inevitability sketched in Capital was expressly restricted to the countries of Western Europe, a qualification whose existence alone rebukes any portrait of the mature theory as a closed system indifferent to historical variety. Meanwhile the Critique of the Gotha Programme of 1875 shows the old polemicist attacking his own movement's programme with undiminished ferocity, and incidentally distributing remarks on distribution, labour and the phases of a future society that have fuelled arguments about his normative commitments ever since. The drafts and letters on Ireland from the late eighteen sixties had already revised the expectations of the Manifesto period, relocating the lever of English working class emancipation to the national question of the neighbouring island.

What does the third panel do to the debate? It dissolves the premise both camps shared, that development, if it occurred, ran once and in one direction, from philosophy to science or from promise to fulfilment. The late notebooks show a thinker still absorbing new evidence, still revising the geography and the anthropology of his theory, and entirely capable of surprising the custodians of his system, as he surprised the Russian readers who turned his supposed doctrine against his own conclusions. Teodor Shanin's collection on the late Marx and the Russian road, and Haruki Wada's studies from Japanese scholarship, built a literature on this material that neither the humanist nor the Althusserian scheme of the nineteen sixties had anticipated. For the young Marx question the implication is direct. If the career contains a third phase irreducible to both the first and the second, then the temptation to narrate the whole as a single crossing, whether fall or ascent, should be abandoned for a picture of serial transformations, each with its own hinge, its own gains and its own losses. The 1845 hinge remains the deepest, because it concerns method itself. It is not the only one, and recognising the plurality of hinges is the final correction the chronology administers to the legends.

## Which Marx first: a reading order

Practical readers ask a simpler question than the scholars, and the debate gives it a sharper answer than any syllabus admits: in what order should the works be read? The break prescription is to begin with the mature theory and treat the early writings as an optional preface, on the ground that reading the manuscripts first installs a humanist lens through which Capital will be systematically misread. The continuity prescription reverses the order, on the ground that the economics is unintelligible as a critique unless its standard of judgement has been absorbed from the early work. The transformation verdict implies a third order, and it can be defended on the evidence assembled here. Read the manuscripts of 1844 first, but briefly and with the questions they pose written down rather than their vocabulary memorised. Then read the Theses and the selections from The German Ideology, watching the instruments change while the questions are restated in the new idiom. Then read the Grundrisse selections on alienation and on machinery beside the first chapter and the machinery chapter of Capital, and observe the function test being passed before your eyes, the same figures doing the same work inside a transformed analysis. A reader who has performed that sequence owns the debate rather than inheriting a side in it, and owns it in the only form in which it deserves to be owned, as a judgement formed on the primary evidence in its compositional order. The reading takes weeks rather than years, and it converts the young Marx question from a piece of scholarly gossip into what it should be for every serious reader of Capital: a decision about what kind of book they are holding, made knowingly instead of by editorial accident.

## What the verdict changes in practice

Verdicts in intellectual history are worth defending only if they alter practice, and this one alters several. For the writing of intellectual biography, it prescribes suspicion toward any narrative whose turning points coincide with publication events, and it supplies the remedy of compositional ordering that the publication lag argument formalises. For the editing and teaching of texts, it argues against selections that quarantine the early writings in a separate volume labelled juvenilia, and against the opposite quarantine that prints the manuscripts as the secret truth of the economics; the evidence supports anthologies that interleave the workshop drafts with the published work and let readers watch categories migrate. For political argument conducted in Marx's name, it withdraws two licences: the licence to cite the young Marx against the economics, as if the critique of estrangement authorised ignoring the analysis of value, and the licence to cite the science against the standard, as if the analysis of value rendered the critique of estrangement obsolete. Both licences were exercised freely during the charged decades, and both depend on the strong form of one camp's thesis. For the study of other thinkers, finally, the debate bequeaths its method entire. Darwin's notebooks, Freud's abandoned projects, Wittgenstein's two philosophies, Keynes before and after the General Theory: in each case a corpus, a contested hinge, a vocabulary that migrates, and a reception distorted by the order of publication. The student who has learned to ask, of any such case, whether the problem persisted while the instruments were rebuilt, and to test the answer against the drafts in which the later work was composed, has acquired from the young Marx debate something more durable than a position on Marx. That transferability is the final reason the question, so parochial in appearance, has earned the length of the present treatment.

## Marx's own testimony reconsidered

Both camps appeal to the author's authority, and the author left exactly one extended retrospective, the preface to the Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy of 1859, which is why its paragraphs have been parsed like scripture. In it Marx recounts his studies in law and philosophy, his editorship and its collision with the authorities over material questions, his withdrawal to the study, and the first fruit of that withdrawal, the critique of Hegel's philosophy of law, whose conclusion he summarises: that legal relations and forms of state are rooted in the material conditions of life, whose anatomy is political economy. He then states the guiding thread of his studies, the famous summary of the materialist conception in which the mode of production conditions social, political and intellectual life, and he recalls the Brussels and Manchester years, the settling of accounts with German philosophy in a manuscript abandoned to the mice, and the conviction, reached with Engels, that the task was to set out from production rather than from consciousness. The break camp reads the document as a curriculum vitae of rupture: philosophy behind him, science before him, accounts settled. The continuity camp reads the same paragraphs as an itinerary of problems, noting that the conclusion of the Hegel critique, with its inversion of state and civil society, is presented without embarrassment as the doorway to everything after, and that a writer repudiating his youth does not usually date his guiding thread from the youthful work.

The honest assessment is that the preface will not arbitrate, because it was written for a purpose other than arbitration. It is a public introduction to a work of economics, composed by an author managing a reputation among readers who knew the journalist and the pamphleteer, and it compresses two decades into a page with the ruthlessness such documents require. Other testimony cuts both ways with similar inconclusiveness. Marx's letters show him, in 1868, recommending that a correspondent who wanted to understand his method read the first chapter of Capital with care, and they show him, in the same decades, rereading Hegel and even planning a short exposition of the dialectic, projects that suit a philosopher's afterlife as readily as a scientist's. Engels, the witness closest to the work and the editor of its remains, praised the manuscripts indirectly by publishing the Theses as a founding document while never arranging for the manuscripts of 1844 themselves to appear, an editorial pattern that both camps have plundered. The conclusion the preface actually supports is the modest one this article has drawn from the corpus as a whole: Marx knew that 1845 and 1846 had changed his standpoint, said so in the language of settled accounts, and continued for forty years to work the seam the early critique had opened. Authors are witnesses to their own development, not judges of it, and the deference owed to testimony ends where the texts begin to testify for themselves.

## The witness who had not read the manuscripts: Lukacs

One episode in the reception history functions almost like a controlled experiment, and no account of the evidence is complete without it. In 1923 Georg Lukacs published History and Class Consciousness, a work written in ignorance of the manuscripts of 1844, which still slept in the archives, and of the Grundrisse, which slept with them. Working from Capital, from the published polemics and from the Hegelian tradition, Lukacs reconstructed a theory of reification: the commodity form, become universal, stamps the consciousness of the producer, who contemplates his own activity as a thing governed by laws alien to him, and stands in a contemplative posture before a second nature of his own making. When the manuscripts surfaced nine years later, readers discovered that Lukacs, proceeding from the mature work alone, had arrived at a doctrine recognisably cognate with the estranged labour of 1844, down to the contemplative stance that the first Thesis on Feuerbach had attacked in another vocabulary. Lukacs himself, in a later self criticism, treated the convergence with a mixture of pride and alarm, but for the periodisation question its meaning is plain. If the categories of the young Marx were truly absent from the mature work, a reader of genius working only from the mature work should not have been able to reinvent them. The experiment can be run in the opposite direction with the same result: readers who begin from the manuscripts alone do not reinvent the theory of surplus value, which is the break camp's symmetric and legitimate point. Taken together the two failures of reinvention describe the corpus exactly as the transformation verdict describes it: the critical architecture survived the rebuilding of the machinery, and the machinery had genuinely to be built.

## Attempts to dissolve the dispute

Between the camps there have always been positions that try to dissolve the dispute rather than win it, and they deserve a hearing before the verdict's implications are drawn. One such position treats the quarrel as a confusion of levels: the young and mature writings, it says, belong to different genres and answer different questions, philosophy in one case and economics in the other, so that asking whether they agree is like asking whether a man's letters agree with his ledger. The dissolution fails on the evidence of the workshop drafts, where the genres mix on the same page and the questions are continuous, but it usefully rebukes readers who demand of every text in a corpus that it contain the whole doctrine in miniature. A second dissolution, associated with readers of a pragmatist temper, holds that the question of the real Marx is unanswerable and uninteresting, since texts mean what their uses make them mean and each generation will manufacture the Marx it needs. That position explains the debate's political decades with uncomfortable accuracy and then forfeits the right to correct any reading whatever, including readings the texts plainly forbid, which is too high a price for peace. A third, more promising dissolution distinguishes respects: continuous in its object, the critique of a society in which producers are ruled by their products; discontinuous in its theory of that object; continuous in its normative standard, variously grounded; discontinuous in its philosophical anthropology. Stated so, the dispute largely evaporates, and what remains is the work of specifying each respect exactly, which is the work this article's function test attempts. The residue that does not evaporate is small but real: whether the standard by which Capital judges can be grounded in the theory of forms alone, or whether it silently draws on the anthropology it discarded. On that residue the camps still divide, now with precision, and a reader who has followed the argument this far is equipped to take a side in the narrow quarrel that survives the wide one's dissolution.

## The debate in three national mirrors

The geography of the debate adds a last layer of explanation, because the break and continuity theses each had national strongholds whose intellectual institutions shaped their fortunes. In Germany the question was filtered through the editorial politics of the collected editions and through a philosophical culture for which the young Marx arrived entangled with the Hegel renaissance and with the memory of a Marxism that had been a state doctrine in one part of the country and a proscribed creed in the other; the Frankfurt tradition, from Horkheimer's early programme to Habermas's reconstruction of historical materialism, worked the seam between the early critique and the mature theory as a standing problem rather than a settled partition. In France the debate was at its most doctrinal, because a mass party, a prestigious philosophical field and the heritage of structuralism converged on it, and because Althusser's intervention was aimed at targets inside his own camp with a precision foreign accounts often miss. In the English speaking world the debate arrived translated and delayed, mediated by Fromm's edition of the manuscripts, by the New Left's search for a usable Marx, and by university syllabuses that taught the early writings in philosophy departments and the economics in others, an institutional partition that reproduced the two Marxes in the curriculum long after scholars had complicated them in print. Each mirror distorted, and each preserved something the others lost: Germany kept the philology, France kept the rigour about concepts, the Anglophone world kept the question of what the theory is for. A synthesis that draws on all three traditions, as this article has tried to do, is itself an argument that the age of the camps is ending, since the materials for the transformation verdict were assembled by the camps' own labours and could not have been assembled without them.

## Inside the Grundrisse: three exhibits

Because the Grundrisse carries so much of this article's verdict, its evidence should be exhibited rather than summarised, and three exhibits will serve. The first is the treatment of the forms of production that preceded capitalism, where Marx traces how the dissolution of communal property and of the worker's ownership of his conditions of labour produced the free labourer of the modern world. The analysis is historical and economic, a genuine achievement of the mature method, and its organising pathos is unmistakably the one of 1844: a long process in which the producer is separated from the conditions of his activity and those conditions accumulate opposite him as an alien world. Primitive accumulation in Capital tells the same story with the English enclosures as its evidence, and the line from the manuscripts' question, how did labour come to confront its own conditions as another's property, to the mature histories is direct and documented. The second exhibit is the celebrated fragment on machines, where Marx imagines automation advancing to the point at which direct labour time ceases to be the measure of production and the worker steps to the side of the process as watchman and regulator. The passage looks beyond a society founded on the theft of alien labour time toward one in which disposable time becomes the measure of wealth, and its standard of judgement is the free development of human capacities that the manuscripts had named as the content of emancipation. Nothing in the break scheme predicts that the most visionary pages of the mature workshop would be written in the early work's normative register; on the transformation account the register's survival is exactly what one expects.

The third exhibit is quieter and for that reason perhaps the most telling. Throughout the Grundrisse the analysis of money returns to the theme that the individual carries his social power in his pocket, that in the money form the social bond itself confronts people as a thing external to them, an observation the young Marx had made in his excerpts on Mill in 1844, where money is described as the alienated capacity of mankind and the confounding and compounding of human qualities. Between the Mill excerpts and the Grundrisse lie thirteen years, the whole supposed rupture, and a complete reconstruction of method; the observation survives the transit intact, acquires a theory around itself, and reappears in Capital as the fetishism of the money form. Exhibits of this kind cannot be multiplied indefinitely without tedium, but their pattern is the argument: again and again, a figure of thought formed in the Feuerbachian workshop is found, in the drafts of the eighteen fifties and sixties, load bearing inside the new construction, modified in its grounding and unmodified in its office. Philology of this sort is slow work, and the debate's impatience with it explains many of its errors. Camps are formed on manifestos. Verdicts are formed on exhibits.

## The evidence each side must explain away

A fair way to close the evidential part of the case is to list, in prose, the exhibits each camp finds inconvenient, since a position is best measured by the weight of what it must carry as exception. The break camp must explain away the alienation saturation of the Grundrisse, drafted in the heart of the mature period. The Results of the Immediate Process must be filed as well, with their account of labour subsumed under capital and confronting its conditions as alien powers, written as the intended bridge into Capital. Filed likewise are the fetishism chapter, the architect and the bee, the realm of freedom passage in the third volume, and the transition from the sphere of exchange into the hidden abode of production, each of which restates an early figure in a mature register. Lukacs, too, demands a filing: he reinvented reification from the published mature work without access to the manuscripts the category supposedly died with. And the author's own habit of returning, in letters and plans, to the philosophical sources of his youth without a word of repudiation for the manuscripts' central concern completes the accumulation. None of these is inexplicable on the break account; each has its filing as residue, as metaphor, as survival. But the filings accumulate, and a scheme that must file the workshop drafts, the masterpiece's most famous chapter and the theory's normative horizon as exceptions has begun to describe its exceptions better than its rule.

The continuity camp's ledger is shorter but not empty, and honesty requires it in the same detail. It must explain away the polemical violence of The German Ideology against the idiom of essence and estrangement, written by the manuscripts' own author within two years of their composition. It must explain away the absence, before the eighteen fifties, of the labour and labour power distinction, without which surplus value cannot be theorised and with which the whole explanation of exploitation changes character. The disappearance of species-being as a premise demands its filing too, with the corresponding change in what the critique claims to rest on, from an anthropology of man's essence to an analysis of forms whose normative standard is carried tacitly. So does the author's decision never to publish the manuscripts and never, in print, to rest an argument on them again. And it must explain the plain fact that the mature work contains whole continents, the theory of rent, of circulation, of crisis tendencies, the analysis of the working day as a terrain of class struggle, for which the early writings offer no anticipation whatever. These filings also accumulate, and a continuity that treats twenty years of conceptual revolution as the unfolding of a seed has described its rule at the cost of the record's most conspicuous fact, which is how hard the later work was to make.

Set the two ledgers side by side and the verdict of this article stops looking like a compromise and starts looking like the only description that carries both sets of exhibits without filing fees. The break ledger is longer, and heavier in the decisive place, the workshop drafts, which is why the strong break fails. The continuity ledger is real, and heaviest in the decisive place of method, which is why strong continuity fails with it. What remains is the hinge: a thinker who discarded a philosophy and kept its problem, rebuilt his science and kept its standard, abandoned a vocabulary and kept the architecture it had framed. Readers who require a single word for that pattern have one available in the title the evidence suggests. Transformation is not the refusal of a verdict. It is the verdict, and it is more precise than either of the absolutes it replaces, because it says where the continuity lies and where the discontinuity lies and what evidence would move the boundary. A debate conducted for half a century by serious people over real texts deserves an ending of that kind, in which the losing positions are not mocked but pensioned, honoured for the exhibits they forced into the record and retired from the office of describing the whole.

## The wider philosophical afterlife of the manuscripts

No account of the debate is complete without the readers who took the young Marx into philosophies of their own, because their uses of the manuscripts form part of the evidence about what the early work contains. Jean Paul Sartre, in the Critique of Dialectical Reason of 1960, rebuilt a Marxist philosophy of history around scarcity, practico inert matter and the alienation of praxis in its own products, a construction unmistakably descended from 1844 and offered as the existential completion of the mature theory's missing anthropology; whether the completion was needed is precisely the debate's question, posed from the opposite side. Karel Kosik, in Dialectics of the Concrete, published in Czech in 1963 and suppressed after the Prague Spring, made the early critique of fetishised everydayness the centre of a philosophy of praxis that remains the Praxis tradition's most original achievement. Maurice Merleau-Ponty, a generation earlier, had already taken the young Marx as the philosopher of history's ambiguity and broken with orthodox dialectics partly on that ground. In the German speaking world the manuscripts nourished the critical theory of the Frankfurt School from its beginnings, in Horkheimer's programme and in Marcuse's essays of the nineteen thirties on the philosophical foundations of the economic concept of labour, which read the manuscripts, hot from their first publication, as the philosophical charter of the whole critique. Erich Fromm carried the same texts into a humanist psychology and a popular readership numbered in millions.

The afterlife matters to the periodisation question in a way that is easy to miss. Each of these readers treated the early work not as a relic but as a live instrument, and the instruments worked: they generated analyses of bureaucracy, of consumption, of everyday life and of culture that the orthodox Marxisms of the same decades conspicuously lacked. A category discarded by its author in 1845 and inert thereafter would be an unlikely source of that much later fertility. The break camp has a reply ready, that fertility in readers proves suggestiveness rather than doctrinal continuity, and the reply has force wherever the readers' constructions depart from Marx's own later arguments, as Sartre's freely do. But the pattern across so many independent traditions, Czech, French, German, American, each finding in the manuscripts the standard by which administered societies stand condemned, is at least evidence that what the manuscripts contain is not a discarded scaffolding but the statement of the problem in its most exposed form. Scaffolding is removed when the building stands. The problem statement is consulted for as long as the problem lasts, and the problem of labour dominated by its own objectifications has lasted.

## How the weighing was done: a note on method

Readers are entitled to know the scales on which a defended verdict was reached, and the method used here can be stated briefly enough to be checked. Texts were weighted by proximity to the composition of the disputed works: drafts and notebooks in which the mature theory was actually assembled count for more than programmatic statements about it, because a writer's practice in the workshop is better evidence of his categories than his announcements in the preface. Composition dates were treated as the ordering that matters and publication dates as evidence about reception only. Vocabulary was never counted as proof by itself; every claim of continuity was required to pass the function test, showing the same office performed in the later system, and every claim of novelty was required to name a category or a derivation that the early work could not have produced. Testimony by the author and his partner was admitted as evidence and discounted as verdict. Where the record supported both camps in different respects, the respects were distinguished rather than averaged, which is how the verdict came to be a conjunction, continuity of concern with discontinuity of method, instead of a midpoint. A method that can be stated can be disputed at the level of its rules, and a reader who rejects the rules, who holds, for example, that programmatic statements should outweigh drafts, is invited to rerun the weighing and see whether the strong break recovers. The prediction of this article is that no assignment of weights consistent with the drafts' existence will recover it, and that the argument, at its deepest, is about whether the Grundrisse and the Results are to count as Marx's mature thought or as anomalies within it. On that question, at least, the chronology leaves no room at all: they are the mature thought in the act of being thought, and a periodisation that must quarantine them has already delivered its own refutation.

## A short coda on the two statues

Popular memory prefers statues, and the debate's longevity owes something to the convenience of a pair: the bearded economist of the British Museum and the young firebrand of Paris, each available for separate commemoration. The texts, read in the order they were written, refuse to sit for either portrait. They show a student of Roman law turned journalist, embarrassed by wood thefts and wine growers into economics; a philosopher who read the economists until their categories broke in his hands; a revolutionary who spent the nineteen fifties of his own life in a reading room rebuilding those categories into a theory of forms; a draftsman whose notebooks think in the old figures while the new machinery is assembled around them; and an old man annotating Morgan and entertaining Russian populists with a flexibility his disciples on both sides found inconvenient. It is a better story than the statues, because it is the story of work. The young Marx question, pursued honestly, ends by dissolving its own title: there were never two Marxes, and there was never only one, if one means by that a mind that finished changing in 1845 or in 1867. There was a writing life, with a hinge where the method turned and the problem held, and the task of the reader is neither to worship the hinge nor to deny it, but to understand what swung on it and what, through all the swinging, stayed fixed to the frame.

## Two scenes of writing: Paris and London

The whole debate can be held in the mind as a contrast between two scenes of writing, and the contrast is worth dwelling on because it disciplines the imagination against both legends. Paris, in the spring and summer of 1844: a twenty six year old exile, lately married, his first child newborn, expelled in prospect from every country he settles in, fills notebooks at a furious pace with excerpts from the economists interleaved with argument. He has no publisher for the notebooks and no audience beyond a circle of exiles; the work is a young man thinking at full stretch in the conviction that he has found the riddle of the modern world in the estrangement of labour. The idiom is Feuerbach's, the ambition is total, the economics is excerpted and second hand, and the standard of judgement, a humanity realising itself in free social production, is asserted with the confidence of a philosophy that has not yet been made to pay for its assertions in the currency of mechanism. London, in the winter of 1857 and 1858: a thirty nine year old exile, his circumstances straitened, his health damaged, the reading room of the British Museum behind him and a commercial crisis breaking over Europe, writes through the nights to finish a manuscript of political economy before the crisis peaks, filling eight hundred pages with the categories of money and capital worked out at last. The idiom is now his own, forged in the interval; the economics is original and formidable; and yet the pages keep returning to the figure the Paris notebooks made central, the worker's own objectified labour standing over him as an alien power, as if the older insight were the compass by which the new machinery was being aligned. Between the scenes lies everything the break camp rightly celebrates: the settling of accounts with philosophy, the materialist conception of history, the decade of study, the invention of the concepts that made the critique a science. Across the scenes runs everything the continuity camp rightly defends: the problem, the standard, the wager on the class the system itself produces. A biography that honours both the interval and the thread is the biography the evidence writes, and it is neither a conversion narrative nor a story of a seed unfolding, but the record of a problem pursued through the rebuilding of every instrument brought to bear on it.

## Objections from outside the tradition

The debate is usually conducted within Marxism, but its sharpest external objection deserves admission, because it targets the continuity method itself. Why, the objection asks, should a thinker be bound by, or credited with, manuscripts he chose not to publish? A writer's considered work is the work he issued to the world; the drawer manuscripts are drafts, and drafts are where discarded ideas live. On this view the whole continuity literature commits an archival fallacy, promoting into doctrine what its author demoted into the drawer, and the Grundrisse, a notebook never prepared for any reader, is the weakest possible foundation for claims about the real Marx. The objection is serious and its premise is half sound: Marx's failure to publish the manuscripts of 1844 across four decades is a fact requiring explanation, and the continuity ledger above recorded it. But the inference from drawer to discard fails against the workshop evidence. The Grundrisse was not a discarded draft of a doctrine abandoned; it was the quarry from which the published Contribution and Capital were cut, revised into print by the author's own hand within two years of its composition, which makes it evidence of the published work's meaning in the strongest sense scholarship recognises, the sense in which the drafts of any writer illuminate the choices frozen in the final text. The manuscripts of 1844 stand differently, as an early quarry rather than the masterpiece's own, and the verdict of this article has never claimed for them the authority of doctrine. It claims only that the problem they posed organised the enquiries that followed, a claim about the direction of a life's work for which drafts, letters, notebooks and published volumes are all competent witnesses. A jurisprudence of texts that excluded everything unpublished would not merely settle this debate; it would abolish the study of how any major work came to be, and with it most of what the humanities know about their central figures. The external objection is therefore answered where it is strongest and declined where it overreaches, in the same pattern as the internal objections before it.

There is a second external objection, quieter and in some ways deeper, aimed at the stakes rather than the method. Periodisation disputes, it is said, are the scholasticism of traditions in decline, a way of arguing about a founder when the world the founder addressed has moved on, and the energy spent deciding which Marx is real would be better spent deciding whether either Marx is right. The point has force as sociology and none as refutation, since the question of what a theory claims is prior to the question of whether the claim is true, and the young Marx debate is precisely a dispute about what the theory claims: whether its centre is a science of structures that happens to have moral consequences, or a critique of estrangement that happens to have acquired a science. Those are different theories, with different tests, different vulnerabilities and different descendants, and a reader cannot assess Marxism at all until the dispute between them has been at least provisionally settled. That the settlement offered here is provisional, open to the notebooks still emerging from the archive, is not a weakness borrowed from caution. It is the form that honest verdicts take when the evidence is a corpus still in the process of being published, a hundred and forty years after its author's death, out of order to the very end.

## The science and ideology distinction, examined

One load bearing element of the break thesis has so far been taken on its own terms, and it should be examined before the file closes, because its weakness propagates through the whole scheme. The distinction between science and ideology, as Althusser wielded it, treats the two as kinds of discourse separable by their objects and their methods, with a datable frontier between them in a thinker's life. The model came from the history of the exact sciences, where breaks of that kind do occur: a chemistry after Lavoisier does not merely extend the phlogiston theory, it replaces the entities in terms of which combustion is discussed. Transferred to a body of social thought, the model strains at three points. The entities of the earlier discourse, alienation, species-being, human essence, are not discarded in the later work in the manner of phlogiston; they are, as the function test showed, partially absorbed, partially transformed and partially retired, a pattern without analogue in the chemical case. The frontier is not datable in the manner of a publication or a discovery, since the works of the alleged break are themselves transitional mixtures, as The German Ideology's retention of the estrangement figure demonstrates. And the criterion by which the later discourse is certified as science is internal to the scheme in a way the chemical case escapes, since nobody has produced, for the critique of political economy, the independent experimental tribunal that certified chemistry. None of this shows that the later work is not more rigorous than the earlier; it plainly is, in its derivations, its handling of evidence and its architecture. It shows that the metaphor of a scientific revolution, with its clean frontier and its discarded entities, describes the development worse than the homelier metaphors of rebuilding and transformation that the documentary record suggests. A thesis can be illuminating as a provocation and misleading as a map, and the break thesis's career exhibits both properties in the highest degree: it taught a generation to read Marx's development as a problem instead of a pageant, and it taught the same generation a solution that the drafts refute. Honouring the provocation while correcting the map is the exact measure of what a later verdict owes to a great error, and the break thesis, on the evidence assembled in this article, earns that description without insult: the most productive mistake in the modern study of Marx, productive because it forced the continuity case to become exact, and a mistake because the continuity, once exact, is what the record shows.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Is there a break between the young and the mature Marx?

There is a real change of method and vocabulary around 1845, but the evidence does not support a clean rupture. The Feuerbachian framework of essence and estrangement is abandoned, yet the problem it framed, producers dominated by their own objectified labour, persists through the Grundrisse and into Capital, where fetishism restates it as a doctrine of form. The defensible verdict distinguishes continuity of concern from discontinuity of method. That position concedes to the break camp everything true in its account of 1845 while refusing the stronger claim that Marx discarded the early problem itself, a claim the workshop drafts contradict in detail.

### Q: What is the epistemological break?

The epistemological break is Althusser's name for the supposed rupture in 1845 separating Marx's ideological early works from his scientific maturity. Borrowed from the history of science, the term claims a change of problematic: before the break, history is explained by a human essence lost and to be recovered; after it, explanation proceeds by structures and relations with no essence as a premise. The Theses on Feuerbach and The German Ideology are cast as the works of the break itself. The notion is stronger than development or maturation, since it asserts that the questions changed, not merely the answers, which is why accepting or rejecting it reorganises the whole reading of Marx.

### Q: Which works belong to the young Marx?

In the standard periodisation the young Marx comprises the writings to 1844: the critique of Hegel's philosophy of right and its introduction, the essay on the Jewish question, the letters and articles of the early eighteen forties, and above all the Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844, where alienation and species-being receive their classic statement. The Theses on Feuerbach and The German Ideology of 1845 and 1846 are then assigned to the break itself, and everything from the Poverty of Philosophy onward counts as transitional or mature. The classification is Althusser's scaffold, and it should be used as one: convenient for arranging a syllabus, contested as a claim about the thought itself.

### Q: Did the Grundrisse settle the young Marx debate?

Not by acclamation, but it changed what the break thesis has to explain. A manuscript of 1857 and 1858, at the heart of the mature period, thinks persistently in terms of alienation and the inversion of subject and object, so defenders of a rupture in 1845 must argue that the surviving vocabulary is conceptually inert. That argument can be made, and making it forced the debate onto the better ground of function rather than word counts. The Grundrisse therefore settled the debate against the strong break for most readers of the drafts, while leaving a refined dispute about how far transformed concepts count as continuous. Settlement in scholarship usually looks like that: a burden shifted, not a vote taken.

### Q: Why did this periodisation debate become politically charged?

Because each periodisation licensed a politics. After 1956, Marxists seeking distance from the regimes ruling in Marx's name found in the young philosopher of alienation a standard those regimes could not meet, and humanist readings flourished across Eastern and Western Europe. Althusser's anti-humanism answered from inside the French Communist Party, defending the scientific standing of the mature doctrine against the party's own turn to humanist language. Where the authenticity of a tradition's founder decides the fate of journals, chairs and party lines, a scholarly question about dates acquires the intensity of a succession dispute. The heat was real, and it explains why both camps stated their cases absolutely when the texts support a hinge.

### Q: Does Capital still use the language of alienation?

The word itself appears sparingly, but the structure it named is present at the theory's centre. The fetishism section describes social relations between producers assuming the form of relations between things, and the machinery chapter describes the worker serving an automaton that confronts him as an alien power, while the analysis of the working day measures a life consumed by a process the worker does not govern. Whether this counts as the language of alienation depends on the test applied. By vocabulary, the term has receded. By function, the estrangement of producer from product, activity and fellow producers is restated with a precision the manuscripts could not achieve, which is the continuity case in miniature.

### Q: Who argued for continuity across Marx's career?

The continuity case was built by several generations. The editors who published the manuscripts in 1932 opened the question; Erich Fromm's Marx's Concept of Man carried the humanist reading to a wide public in 1961; Istvan Meszaros gave it systematic form in Marx's Theory of Alienation, arguing that the category is the critical core the economics presupposes; and Bertell Ollman's Alienation reconstructed the concept philosophically and tied it to the mature method. Behind the books stood the Praxis school, the Budapest school and other dissident traditions for whom continuity was a scholarly finding before it was a banner. Their shared claim is modest at its best: one project, developing, whose early problem survives its changes of instrument.

### Q: When exactly is the break supposed to happen?

Althusser dates the break to 1845, the year of the Theses on Feuerbach and the beginning of The German Ideology, written with Engels through 1846. On his scheme the works before that year are ideological, the two named works perform the rupture, a transitional decade follows, and maturity proper arrives around 1857 with the Grundrisse and the work toward Capital. The precision of the date is part of the thesis's appeal and part of its vulnerability. A single year must bear the weight of the manuscripts on one side and the drafts of the eighteen fifties on the other, and the Grundrisse, sitting inside the mature period while speaking the early language, is the exhibit that makes the single year hard to defend.

### Q: Does the answer change how you read Capital?

Decisively. A reader persuaded of the strong break approaches Capital as a self sufficient science and treats the fetishism chapter as a piece of value form analysis whose philosophical resonance is accidental. A reader persuaded of continuity approaches the same chapter as the mature statement of the estrangement problematic, and reads the economics as the mechanism beneath a critique whose standard was set in 1844. The difference shows in what each reader takes the book to be for: refutation of rival economists, or indictment of a form of life. The transformation verdict defended in this article licenses the second reading while insisting on the first reading's rigour about categories, which is why the periodisation question is worth an essay rather than a footnote.

### Q: Is the young Marx debate still live?

Its political heat has faded with the movements that supplied it, but the scholarly question remains open in refined forms. The publication of the complete historical critical edition keeps revising the map of the drafts and notebooks, the late Marx of the ethnological notebooks has complicated every two panel periodisation, and the function of the early categories inside the critique of political economy is still argued wherever Capital is read closely. What has died is the absolute form of both camps: few specialists now defend a total rupture or a seamless unfolding. A debate that ends by refining its question has not been wasted, and by that standard this one is alive in the only way that matters.

### Q: What evidence would settle the break debate rather than prolong it?

Evidence addressed to the right test. The break would be proved by a mature text repudiating the estrangement problem itself, by an explicit retrospective disowning of the manuscripts' critique, or by a demonstration that fetishism can be restated without any equivalent of the inversion of subject and object. Continuity in the strong sense would be proved by showing that the anthropology of species-being functions as a premise inside the derivations of Capital, which no one has shown. Since neither proof has appeared, the debate is settled by the drafts that do exist, and they support transformation: same problem, rebuilt instruments. New notebook publications could shift the balance at the margins, and the openness to that possibility is what separates a verdict from a dogma.

### Q: How should a teacher present Althusser's break without caricaturing the humanists?

By presenting both interventions in their settings and at their strongest. Give Althusser his technical claim, the change of problematic, his Bachelardian model of science, and his situation inside a party argument about humanism after 1956. Give the humanists their textual claim, that the category of alienation persists and does work in the mature writings, and their situation among readers seeking a Marx able to criticise existing socialist states. Withhold the Grundrisse chronology until the positions are stated, then reveal it and let the class watch the argument move. Caricature enters when either side is rendered as mere politics or mere pedantry; each was a serious reading of real texts, and students learn the debate by feeling the force of both before judging.

### Q: Why does the publication date of a manuscript distort judgements about its influence?

Because readers naturally treat the order in which texts reach them as the order in which the author produced them, and build narratives of development on that false sequence. A manuscript published ninety years late arrives as a revelation or an embarrassment rather than as a stage, and its vocabulary is judged against a canon from which it was absent when the canon formed. Influence is distorted twice over: the text is denied the influence it would have had if published when written, and it is granted a spurious influence as the supposed key or scandal of the corpus. Marx's manuscripts are the extreme case, but any archive edited posthumously, from Nietzsche's notebooks to Kafka's papers, exhibits the same mechanism in smaller measure.

### Q: Which continuity argument does a journalist most often oversimplify?

The claim that Marx never gave up on alienation. Compressed into a sentence, it suggests that the economist of 1867 still held the philosophy of 1844 unchanged, which no careful continuity scholar asserts and which the transformation of the categories refutes. The oversimplification usually travels with its mirror image, the claim that Althusser proved there were two Marxes, and a report that carries both has replaced a dispute about method with a soap opera about identity. The accurate short form names the distinction itself: continuity of concern, discontinuity of method, with the Grundrisse as the text any summary must mention. A journalist who keeps those three elements has conveyed the debate; one who drops them has conveyed only its noise.

### Q: How does the break thesis change the standing of the alienation concept?

If the strong break holds, alienation is demoted to a biographical fact about the young Marx: an inherited Feuerbachian notion that the scientific theory discarded, of interest to historians of philosophy and inadmissible in readings of Capital. Teaching would properly begin in 1845, and appeals to estrangement in criticism of working life would need another foundation. If the transformation verdict holds, alienation survives as the problem the mature categories analyse, with fetishism and subsumption as its developed forms, and the concept keeps its standing as the critical core of the whole project. The standing of a single concept thus carries the entire periodisation on its back, which is why a dispute that looks like an argument about dates is, at bottom, an argument about what Marxism is.
