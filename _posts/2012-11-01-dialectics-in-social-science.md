Dialectics has an odd afterlife in the social sciences, because the term itself has nearly vanished from the places where its habits are most faithfully practised. A student teacher on a training course in almost any country will be taught the zone of proximal development as a settled finding of developmental psychology, will be examined on it, and will plan lessons around it, without once being told that the psychologist who built the concept constructed his whole method out of Marx's writings and said so in print. A sociologist trained in network analysis will learn to treat relations as prior to the things related, will cite a famous manifesto for that commitment, and will rarely learn that the manifesto's central distinction was worked out a century earlier inside Marxist philosophy of science. A biologist who insists that organism and environment make each other will be working a seam opened by two Harvard scientists who announced their Marxism on the first page of their book. The pattern repeats often enough, across enough separate disciplines, that it deserves a name and an audit rather than a shrug.

![Dialectics in social science, relational methods and systems thinking that carry its habits without its name - Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-78.webp)

The name this article gives the pattern is the unnamed inheritance, and the claim behind the name needs stating precisely at the outset, because it is easy to inflate and easy to dismiss. The claim is not that every relational or process oriented method in the social sciences is secretly Marxist, and it is not that researchers who use these categories owe a political allegiance they have failed to declare. The claim is narrower and more checkable. A small set of analytical categories, worked out most systematically in the Marxist tradition under the heading of dialectics, can be found doing identifiable work inside at least five living research programmes. In some of those programmes the descent is documented in the founders' own statements and citations. In others the connection is a resemblance that honest reporting has to label as resemblance. And the near total disappearance of the word dialectics from the programmes that inherited the categories is itself a historical fact with historical causes, chiefly the professional cost of the association during the decades when these fields were forming. Each part of that claim can be tested, and the later sections of this article state, lineage by lineage, what evidence would count against it.

An audit of that kind has to begin by separating three things that polemics on both sides habitually fuse. There is the vocabulary, the words dialectic, contradiction, negation, sublation, which most of the inheriting fields have discarded or never used. There is the machinery, the actual analytical moves the vocabulary once labelled, which is where survival, if it happened, will be found. And there is the allegiance, the political and doctrinal commitments of the tradition that carried the machinery, which the inheriting fields mostly do not share and are under no obligation to share. Survival of machinery without vocabulary or allegiance is a familiar event in the history of science. Astronomy kept the observational records of astrology while discarding its claims. Chemistry kept the laboratory techniques of alchemy. Nobody supposes that a chemist who uses a retort has thereby endorsed transmutation, and nobody should suppose that a psychologist who uses a dialectical category has thereby endorsed a party programme. The interesting question is never whether the inheritors are crypto anything. The interesting question is whether the machinery still does work that would be lost if it were removed, and whether knowing where it came from helps a researcher use it well or judge where it stops working.

## Where dialectics survives without its name

The survey that follows visits five fields in turn, and the order is deliberate, running from the most documented lineage to the most contestable one. Developmental psychology in the Vygotskian tradition comes first because the founder's debt is explicit, voluminous, and still visible in the canonical texts that teacher education assigns. Activity theory comes second because it is the direct institutional descendant of that psychology, a field that formalised contradiction into a research instrument and then watched the instrument travel into organisation studies, education research, and human computer interaction, shedding its genealogy at each border crossing. Relational sociology comes third because its founding manifesto restated, in the discipline's leading journal, a distinction between substantialist and relational thinking that reproduces the internal relations argument at the heart of dialectical method, with a documented though partial line of descent. Dialectical biology comes fourth because its two founders wore the lineage openly and built an entire research stance out of it, which makes it the control case against which the quieter fields can be measured. Organisational paradox research comes fifth and last because it is the hardest case, a thriving literature that treats constitutive contradiction as its founding insight while drawing its ancestry from many sources, of which the Marxist one is neither the loudest nor, in most of its citations, present at all.

### Is dialectics still used in social science?

Yes, largely under other names. Vygotskian developmental psychology, activity theory, relational sociology, dialectical biology, and organisational paradox research all use categories of internal relation and constitutive contradiction, usually without acknowledging the Marxist lineage from which they derive, and each field has renamed the categories in a vocabulary of its own, which is why the survival is so easy to miss.

That answer, compressed into a paragraph, contains the whole article in embryo, and the rest of the piece is the slow unpacking of its clauses. The five fields named are not the only candidates. Process sociology in the tradition of Norbert Elias, the field theory associated with Pierre Bourdieu, the critical realist philosophy of science associated with Roy Bhaskar, and the systems traditions discussed later all have claims on the reader's attention, and several of them enter the argument where they belong, as comparisons and as boundary cases. The five chosen are the ones where the categories can be watched doing specific, describable work: generating a research design, licensing a unit of analysis, explaining a failure of prediction, or organising an intervention. Where a category does no such work, where it functions as decoration or as a ritual genuflection in an introduction, this article does not count it as survival. Survival means the category is load bearing. Remove it and a finding, a method, or an explanation collapses. That standard is strict, and it is meant to be, because a looser standard would let the unnamed inheritance claim expand until it covered everything and explained nothing, which is precisely the fate the objection considered later in this article predicts for it.

One more preliminary distinction protects everything that follows. A lineage claim and a resemblance claim are different kinds of statement and they demand different evidence. A lineage claim says that a category travelled, through identifiable texts, teachers, translations, and institutions, from the dialectical tradition into a later field, and it is proved or refuted in archives, bibliographies, and the founders' own acknowledgements. A resemblance claim says only that a later field, for its own reasons, rebuilt a similar category, and it is assessed by comparing the machinery, not the mail routes. Confusing the two is the characteristic error of both camps in this argument. Enthusiasts upgrade resemblances into lineages because a bigger inheritance flatters the tradition. Sceptics downgrade documented lineages into resemblances because a smaller one flatters their sense that serious science owes the tradition nothing. The audit below grades each field separately and refuses both upgrades and downgrades. Vygotsky is lineage, attested by the man himself. Activity theory is lineage once removed, attested by its founders and its institutional history. Relational sociology is lineage in part and convergence in part, and the proportions matter. Dialectical biology is lineage worn openly. Paradox theory is, in the main, resemblance with a few documented threads, and complexity theory, treated later as a boundary case, is resemblance almost entire. An inheritance map drawn with those gradations marked is less dramatic than either camp prefers, and considerably more useful.

## The categories that travel: relation, contradiction, process

Before visiting the fields, the machinery itself needs an inventory, because the word dialectics has been used to label several different toolkits and the travelling one is specific. The toolkit that survives is not the famous triad of thesis, antithesis, and synthesis, which the inheriting fields do not use and which, as the wider series shows, Marx himself never used as a formula. Nor is it the codified laws of dialectical materialism as the Soviet textbooks froze them, a list the inheriting fields mostly never encountered and would not have taken if they had. The travelling toolkit has four tools, and each can be recognised in a host field by the work it does rather than by any label attached to it.

The first tool is the priority of internal relations. A relation is internal when it enters into what the related things are, so that neither term can be specified independently of the other. Capital and wage labour are the tradition's standard example, each unintelligible without the other, but the tool is general. Teacher and learner, organism and environment, landlord and tenant, coloniser and colonised: in each pair, the dialectical habit is to refuse to define the terms first and relate them afterwards, and to insist instead that the relation is constitutive of the terms. The contrast case is the external relation, where two independently constituted things interact, as billiard balls do, each fully itself before and after the collision. Most quantitative social science is built, usually without discussion, on the external model, entering variables into a design as though each carried its meaning into the analysis from outside. The internal relations tool produces a different research instinct: define the unit by its relations, expect the unit to change when its relations change, and treat any attempt to hold one term fixed while varying the other as a modelling decision with costs, not as a neutral default.

The second tool is contradiction treated as constitutive rather than as error. In ordinary research practice a contradiction is a defect: inconsistent findings, a mis-specified model, a respondent who has misunderstood the question. The dialectical habit treats some contradictions as data about the object rather than as failures of the observer. A system can be so built that it generates opposed demands by its normal functioning, and the opposed demands are then among the most informative facts about it, because they show where the system's own requirements pull against each other and where change, when it comes, is most likely to start. The school that requires teachers to raise measured attainment and to attend to each child's developmental pace, the firm that requires units to compete and to share knowledge, the economy that requires households to save and to consume: in each case the opposed requirements are not mistakes in an otherwise coherent design but products of the design itself. The tool does not license calling every tension a contradiction, and the fields that inherited it have had to build taxonomies to separate structural contradiction from mere friction, trade off, and dilemma. But the founding move, promoting contradiction from noise to signal, is recognisably the same move in each of them.

The third tool is process taken as prior to product. The dialectical habit assumes that the objects of social science are stabilised processes, snapshots of movement, and that explanation runs backwards from the snapshot to the movement that produced and maintains it. A test score is the frozen end of a learning history. An occupational structure is the frozen end of decades of hiring, training, migration, and closure. A personality trait, in the Vygotskian handling, is the fossil of a social relation that has been internalised. The tool changes what counts as a satisfactory answer. Asked why a child cannot perform a task, the process habit refuses the static inventory of what the child lacks and asks instead what sequence of interactions built the present performance and what altered sequence could rebuild it. Asked why an industry has the skill profile it has, the habit asks after the training regimes, credentialing bodies, and labour market signals that continuously reproduce that profile. Static description is not forbidden by the tool; it is demoted, treated as a preliminary to the genetic account that does the explaining.

The fourth tool is the historicity of categories themselves. The tradition insists that its own concepts have birthdays, that categories like value, class, and even childhood fit the social formations that produced them and fit other formations badly or not at all. This is the tool the inheriting fields use most quietly and perhaps most valuably, because it functions as a permanent warning against exporting a construct beyond the conditions that made it measurable. A developmental stage scheme built on schooled populations, a class schema built on industrial employment, a management theory built on the mid century corporation: the historicity tool instructs the researcher to ask, of any inherited instrument, what world it was calibrated in and whether that world is the one under study. The tool is also the one that turns back on the dialectical tradition itself, and later sections apply it there, asking whether categories forged in nineteenth century political economy still fit the formations in which the inheriting fields work. An inheritance that cannot survive its own fourth tool is not worth defending, and the strongest version of the argument for the unnamed inheritance is the one that submits to the audit it recommends.

With the toolkit inventoried, the source doctrine should be placed before the survey begins, because the inheriting fields drew on different strata of it. Readers who want the doctrine stated whole, its Engelsian systematisation, its Soviet codification, and the long argument about whether Marx held it at all, will find that work done in [the complete guide to dialectical materialism](/2012/07/01/dialectical-materialism-complete-guide/), and this article deliberately does not repeat it. What matters for the survey is only that the four tools above were all present, in worked form, in the tradition's classical texts, in Marx's method of rising from the abstract to the concrete, in his treatment of the capital relation as internally constitutive, in his analysis of the contradictions a mode of production generates by functioning, and in the historical indexing of his categories. The fields visited below took the tools from different shelves of that workshop, at different times, by different routes, and one of the pleasures of the survey is watching how selective each act of borrowing was. None of the five fields took the whole toolkit. Each took the tool its own problems required, left the rest, and usually renamed what it took. Selective, renamed, unacknowledged: that is what inheritance looks like when the estate is contested and the heirs have reasons of their own for discretion.

## Vygotsky: the lineage that begins in the open

Lev Vygotsky died in 1934, aged thirty seven, leaving a body of work that was suppressed in his own country within two years of his death and recovered, piece by piece, over the following half century. The recovery is the reason his case opens the survey. With most claimed inheritances, the historian must argue from resemblances and from the sociology of citation. With Vygotsky, the argument is conducted in the founder's own sentences. He told his readers which tradition his method belonged to, named the texts he was working from, and built his central concepts as deliberate applications of that method to psychological material. A survey of dialectical survival that could not establish its first case from documents would be in trouble. This one can, and the documents are not obscure. They sit in the opening chapters of the books that teacher education programmes assign.

Vygotsky's formation explains the directness. He came to psychology in the Soviet Union of the 1920s, when Marxism was not an oppositional doctrine but the official philosophy of the state that employed him, and when the urgent institutional question was whether a Marxist psychology was possible at all and what it would look like. His answer, elaborated in the manuscript posthumously known as The Historical Meaning of the Crisis in Psychology, was that psychology's fragmentation into warring schools was a methodological crisis, and that the way through it was the method Marx had used in Capital: not the importation of conclusions about society into the study of mind, but the importation of a way of building concepts. He was explicit that phrases borrowed from Marx without that methodological labour were worthless, and he mocked colleagues who assembled psychologies out of quotations. What he took was the toolkit. Internal relations became the relation between the social and the individual planes of development. Contradiction became the engine of developmental change, the means by which a child's existing organisation of mind generates the tensions its next organisation resolves by restructuring. Process became the genetic method itself, the insistence that a psychological phenomenon is understood only when the history of its formation has been reconstructed. Historicity became his doctrine that the higher psychological functions are products of a specific cultural history and are therefore different in kind, not merely in content, across formations with different tools, languages, and institutions.

### What did Vygotsky take from Marx's method?

He took a way of building concepts rather than a set of conclusions. From the method of Capital he took the search for the simplest relation that generates a whole domain, the treatment of mind as formed through social relations later internalised, and the rule that a phenomenon is explained when the history of its formation has been reconstructed.

The internalisation thesis is where the first tool does its most consequential work. Vygotsky's general genetic law of cultural development holds that every higher function appears twice, first between people as a social relation and then within the child as a psychological function. Attention, memory, reasoning: each begins as something done jointly, distributed across a child and the adults and signs around the child, and each becomes individual by being reconstructed on the inner plane. The claim is unintelligible on the external relations model, where a mind is a self contained processor that social influences impinge upon from outside. On that model, the social is a variable added to a constituted individual. On Vygotsky's model, the individual function is the social relation, transformed. The relation is internal to what the function is. This is the priority of internal relations stated in developmental vocabulary, and it is the reason Vygotskian research designs look so different from the designs of the psychology that surrounded them. The unit of analysis is never the isolated child. It is the child in joint activity, the child with tools, the child within a historically specific institution of childhood. Change the relations and you have not changed the context of development. You have changed the thing developing.

The zone of proximal development, the concept through which most of the world meets Vygotsky, is the second and third tools fused into an instrument. The zone is defined by the distance between what a child can do unaided and what the same child can do in collaboration with more capable others, and its theoretical bite is that it relocates the object of developmental assessment from product to process. A test of unaided performance photographs a completed development, the fossil record of functions already formed. The zone, by contrast, exposes functions in the course of formation, buds rather than fruits, in his metaphor, and therefore tells the teacher or the researcher something about where development is heading rather than only where it has been. Two children with identical unaided scores can have zones of entirely different widths, and the width predicts responsiveness to instruction better than the score does. The dialectical ancestry of the instrument is not decorative. It is the contradiction tool in working dress: the gap between independent and assisted performance is a tension internal to the child's present organisation, and instruction that works within the zone converts that tension into the motor of the next organisation. It is also the process tool as a measurement philosophy: assess the becoming, not only the become. Teacher education absorbed the instrument so completely that it now circulates as common sense in staffrooms that have never heard the name dialectics, and its route into those staffrooms, through translated texts, textbooks, and training syllabuses, is traceable in the ordinary bibliographic way. That traceability is what a documented lineage looks like, and it is worth pausing on the evidentiary standard it sets. Nothing in the later, quieter cases should be called lineage on weaker evidence than this without the weakening being announced.

Two cautions belong inside the Vygotsky case itself, because the brief for honest reporting binds here as everywhere. The first is that Vygotsky's Marxism, though explicit, was neither orthodox nor static. He quarrelled with the reflexologists and the official philosophers of his own milieu, drew freely on Hegel, Spinoza, and the Gestalt psychologists, and left positions in visible motion at his death. Scholars still argue about how far his later work was moving beyond the framework his earlier work had announced, and the honest summary is that the lineage runs to a restless, revisionary Marxism of method, not to a catechism. The second caution concerns the scale of his explicitness. His methodological statements are concentrated in particular texts, some of them unpublished in his lifetime and all of them filtered through editorial histories that scholars continue to dispute. What cannot be disputed, because it is distributed across the published and posthumous work alike, is the direction of the borrowing: he described his own project, repeatedly and in his own voice, as the construction of a psychology on the methodological foundations Marx had laid. The sceptical reading of this case therefore has to work hard. It must treat the founder's repeated self description as a costume adopted for institutional safety, and it must do so for the one case where the costume theory is least plausible, since the texts that declare the method most openly include manuscripts he never published and therefore never needed as protective colouring. That is the shape a serious counter argument would have to take, and noticing its shape matters for the general audit, because a claim that survives only by explaining away its best evidence is a claim in retreat.

The downstream career of the Vygotskian inheritance completes the picture of how a lineage goes quiet. His works were banned in the Soviet Union from 1936 until the thaw, and reached Western readers in substantial form only from the 1960s, in translations that arrived edited, abridged, and framed by introducers who presented him as a cognitive psychologist of genius whose philosophical commitments were an accident of his address. The framing worked because it served everyone. Cold War psychology could absorb the instruments without the ancestry. Teacher education could absorb the zone without the method. Soviet and post Soviet scholarship could reclaim a national genius without reopening the question of what his method implied. By the time the fuller texts and the scholarly reconstruction of his intellectual biography became available, the instruments were already naturalised in their host fields, and naturalised instruments do not get re examined for passports. The pattern, absorption under a neutral description followed by amnesia about the route, recurs in every subsequent case in this survey, with the single difference that in those cases the absorption was quieter because the founders were less explicit or the politics less charged. Readers who want the education side of the story told whole, the uses and misuses of Vygotsky in schooling debates and the Marxist theory of education that surrounds his reception, will find it in [the guide to Marxism and education theory](/2021/05/01/marxism-and-education-theory/), which owns that territory; the concern here is narrower, the survival of a method inside a science that mostly believes it has no methodological debts of this kind.

## Activity theory: contradiction promoted to an instrument

If Vygotsky is the case where the lineage declares itself, activity theory is the case where the lineage builds an institution and then watches the institution's products travel under a trade name. The field descends in a documented line from Vygotsky's circle. Alexei Leontiev, his collaborator and successor, reformulated the inheritance around the concept of activity, arguing that the unit of psychological analysis should be neither the isolated response nor the abstract function but the object oriented, socially organised activity through which people transform their world and themselves. The move preserved the toolkit while shifting its centre of gravity. Internal relations became the structure of the activity itself, subject, object, tools, community, rules, and division of labour forming a system whose elements define one another. Contradiction, which in Vygotsky had been chiefly a developmental engine, became in the hands of the third generation of the school something more operational: a diagnostic category for locating where an activity system is generating its own disturbances and therefore where its transformation is likely to begin.

The third generation is associated above all with Yrjo Engestrom, whose Learning by Expanding, published in 1987, rebuilt the inheritance for the study of work and learning in organisations. Engestrom's model of the activity system, the triangle of subject, object, mediating tools, community, rules, and division of labour that now appears in thousands of studies, is usually taught as a piece of systems modelling, and its presentation in the literature is telling for this survey. The diagram travels. The genealogy often does not. Yet the genealogy is neither hidden nor deniable. Engestrom's theoretical writings discuss the Marxian and Vygotskian sources at length, trace the concept of contradiction through the tradition, and build the field's central method, the analysis of contradictions within and between activity systems, as a conscious development of that inheritance. What the field did was not to conceal its ancestry but to make the ancestry optional reading. A researcher can be trained in the method, can run a Change Laboratory intervention in a hospital or a school, can publish the results in a respected journal, and can do all of it while treating the theoretical chapters as background that a busy practitioner may skip. The inheritance survives as machinery with the manual's first chapter detachable.

### How does activity theory turn contradiction into data?

It grades the disturbances an activity system produces and traces them to structural tensions inside the system or between neighbouring systems. A complaint, a workaround, a recurring breakdown is treated not as noise but as the visible symptom of an opposed requirement built into the work, and the analysis follows the symptom back to the requirement.

The taxonomy Engestrom built for this work deserves a moment's precision, because it shows the contradiction tool being sharpened rather than merely repeated. Primary contradictions operate inside a single element of a system, as when the object of medical work is simultaneously a patient to be healed and a unit of cost to be contained. Secondary contradictions operate between elements, as when a new tool demands skills the division of labour does not supply. Tertiary contradictions arise between the established form of an activity and a culturally more advanced form pressing against it, and quaternary contradictions run between an activity and its neighbours, as when a school's outputs meet an employer's incompatible expectations. A sceptic is entitled to ask whether this is anything more than a vocabulary for organisational friction, and the field's best answer is methodological rather than rhetorical. The taxonomy directs fieldwork. It tells the researcher which disturbances to collect, how to sort them, and what kind of intervention each sort licenses, culminating in the method of expansive learning, in which the collective that owns the activity is led to model its contradictions and design a way through them. A vocabulary that changes what data get gathered and what interventions get built is doing more than decorating an introduction. It is functioning exactly as the survival standard set at the start of this article requires: remove the category and the research design collapses.

The field's wanderings since then illustrate the second half of the unnamed inheritance claim, the shedding of the name at border crossings. Activity theory entered human computer interaction as a corrective to laboratory cognitivism, entered information systems research as a way of studying technology in organisational context, entered health services research and the study of professional learning, and in each host discipline the citations foregrounded the triangle and the intervention method while the theoretical ancestry receded to a footnote or vanished. Citation studies of the kind the sociology of knowledge specialises in can track the recession, and the pattern is not mysterious. A method that arrives carrying a Marxist genealogy pays a toll at the gate of fields whose gatekeepers associate the genealogy with ideology rather than with rigour, while the same method arriving as a neutral modelling framework pays nothing. Researchers are adaptive creatures. They cite what the host field rewards. Nothing in this requires bad faith, and the honest version of the unnamed inheritance claim does not allege any. It alleges something about academic history: that during the decades when these migrations occurred, the professional cost of foregrounding the lineage was real, unevenly distributed, and sufficient to produce the pattern of detachable first chapters that the literature displays. The claim is about incentives and their sediment in citation practice, which is why the disciplinary history assembled in [the account of Marxism and the sociology of knowledge](/2021/07/01/marxism-sociology-of-knowledge/) is the necessary companion to this survey, supplying the institutional story this article can only sketch.

A boundary should be marked before leaving this field, because activity theory also shows where the inheritance claim must stop. The field's empirical substance, its findings about learning in workplaces, its intervention successes and failures, stands or falls on its own evidence and would not be impugned by any revision of the genealogy. Nor does the genealogy confer authority on the findings. A contradiction analysis is not better because Marx analysed contradictions, and a Change Laboratory is not worse because its founder read him. Lineage is a fact about where tools came from, and the tools are thereafter the property of whoever can make them work. Keeping that boundary bright is what separates an audit from an annexation, and the objection examined later in this article is best understood as the fear that the boundary will not be kept.

## Relational sociology: the manifesto and the internal relations argument

Sociology's version of the story begins with a manifesto. In 1997 Mustafa Emirbayer published in the American Journal of Sociology a programmatic essay, Manifesto for a Relational Sociology, which sorted social theory into two camps and urged the discipline to change sides. On one side stood substantialism, in its two familiar forms: the self action of atomistic individuals pursuing given interests, and the inter action of already constituted entities colliding like the billiard balls of the earlier discussion. On the other side stood the relational alternative, in which the terms of every analysis are constituted by the transactions among them, so that the proper units of social science are not things but configurations of ties, not entities but processes of relating. The manifesto named its ancestors generously, drawing on pragmatism, on Simmel, on Elias, on network structuralism, and it has been absorbed into the discipline's self understanding to the point where relational is now among the most common honorifics a sociological argument can claim.

The relevance to this survey lies in how exactly the manifesto's central distinction reproduces the first tool of the dialectical toolkit. Substantialism is the doctrine of external relations stated as a disciplinary programme: constitute the units first, then study their interactions. Relationalism, in the manifesto's strong form, is the doctrine of internal relations stated in the same register: the units are precipitates of the relations, and an analysis that begins from pre constituted units has built its conclusion into its starting point. The argumentative work the distinction does is also the same. It is deployed against variable centred quantitative practice, against methodological individualism, and against any scheme in which a property like class, status, or identity is treated as an attribute a person carries into situations rather than as a position within a structure of relations that situations express. Readers who have followed the earlier articles of this cluster will recognise the shape immediately, because it is the shape of the argument Marx made against the economists who treated the categories of bourgeois society as natural attributes of things. The question the audit must answer is whether the resemblance is descent, convergence, or a mixture, and here the documentary record supports a mixture with the proportions honestly statable. Emirbayer's essay works from sources the dialectical tradition also worked from, above all Hegel and the pragmatist recovery of process, and the relational current in sociology has tributaries, in network analysis and in the Annales influenced historical sociology, that owe Marxism little. At the same time, the broader relational turn absorbed, through the historical sociology of the 1970s and 1980s, a generation of scholars formed by Marxist debates about class formation, and the traffic in concepts between that scholarship and the relational programme is visible in the citations. The verdict this survey records is the mixed one: relational sociology is a convergence that a partial lineage fed, not a colony the tradition founded and not a coincidence it can claim no part in.

Field theory sharpens the same point from a neighbouring direction, and it earns its place in the survey because the brief's query ladder keeps meeting it. The concept of a field, developed most influentially by Pierre Bourdieu and independently in organisational sociology, treats a domain of practice as a structured space of positions defined wholly by their relations to one another, in which what an agent can do, and what an agent is, follows from position within the space rather than from intrinsic endowment. Bourdieu's version adds the machinery of capital in its several forms and of habitus, the internalised history that lets a position become a disposition. The internal relations tool is not a resemblance here so much as the explicit design principle: a field is nothing but a system of internal relations, methodically purged of substantialist residue. Bourdieu's own relation to Marx was lifelong, argumentative, and unconcealed; he plundered the tradition, quarrelled with its orthodoxies, and said both things in print. Field theory is therefore better classified as an acknowledged quarrel than as an unnamed inheritance, and its inclusion in this survey is as a calibration case. It shows what the spectrum's middle looks like: a field that neither hid the lineage nor accepted the label, and whose resistance to the label is itself instructive, since Bourdieu's objection was never to the machinery but to the school loyalties and political tests he associated with the name.

### What does a field add that a variable cannot?

A variable records an attribute a case carries from one context to another. A field records the structure of relations that gives the attribute its value in the first place, so the same endowment counts differently in different fields. The addition is positional explanation: why identical resources yield unlike outcomes when the surrounding configuration of positions changes.

The quantitative question, which the FAQ below takes up in compact form, deserves a fuller airing inside the relational case because this is the field where the supposed incompatibility is most often asserted. Relational claims are sometimes heard as a veto on measurement, as though treating relations as constitutive forbade counting them. The record shows the opposite. Network analysis, the most thoroughly quantified branch of relational research, measures relations directly and has built its mathematics on the priority of ties over attributes. Sequence analysis reconstructs careers and biographies as processes rather than as bundles of variables. Event structure analysis and the comparative historical methods associated with scholars like Andrew Abbott rebuilt explanation around the ordering of occurrences, on the stated ground that the variable centred model misrepresents how social processes actually unfold. What the relational commitment vetoes is not number but a particular metaphysics of number, the assumption that a measurement means the same thing in every configuration in which it appears. That veto is the fourth tool, the historicity of categories, operationalised as a warning about construct validity across contexts. A dialectical inheritance that disciplines measurement rather than forbidding it is a more interesting survival than the mystical enemy of science that polemics describe, and it is the survival the record supports. The procedure question, how a researcher actually conducts an inquiry on these commitments, belongs to [the guide to the dialectical method in research](/2012/10/15/dialectical-method-in-research/), which this article cross refers rather than duplicates; the survey's task is to establish that the commitments are alive in working programmes, and on the relational evidence they are.

## Dialectical biology: the control case

Every audit benefits from a control, a case where the variable under study is present at full strength and openly, so that the quieter cases can be read against it. Dialectical biology is this survey's control. In 1985 Richard Levins and Richard Lewontin, two senior Harvard biologists, published The Dialectical Biologist, a collection whose framing chapters announce the commitment in the title and argue it without embarrassment. Levins was a population geneticist and ecologist who had worked on the modelling of complex systems and on agriculture in Cuba. Lewontin was among the most prominent evolutionary geneticists of his generation, a central figure in the molecular measurement of genetic variation and a relentless critic of genetic determinism. Neither man treated the dialectical commitment as a private philosophy bolted onto an unrelated science. Both argued, in technical venues as well as in the collection, that the commitment did work inside the science: it decided what counted as a satisfactory explanation, which models were built, and which questions were asked.

The organism environment relation is where the toolkit's first tool appears in its purest contemporary form. The standard evolutionary picture treats the environment as a pre existing set of problems, an examination paper set by nature, to which organisms adapt or fail. Levins and Lewontin argued that this picture misdescribes the causal structure. Organisms construct their environments as surely as environments select organisms: they modify the chemistry, temperature, and structure of their surroundings, they choose and alter their niches, they determine which features of the external world are relevant variables in their lives at all. The environment of a burrowing animal is partly the animal's own product; the atmosphere every terrestrial organism breathes is the accumulated waste of ancient photosynthesis. Neither term of the pair can be specified independently of the other, which is the definition of an internal relation, and the research programme that grew from the argument, later consolidated under the name niche construction, carried the point into mainstream evolutionary theory, generally with the genealogy cited less often than the finding. The second and third tools are equally visible in the control case. The collection insists that wholes constrain and enable their parts while being nothing over and above them, that change is driven by the interpenetration of opposed processes, and that the static categories of a snapshot biology, gene, trait, fitness, are abstractions from processes whose reconstruction is the actual explanatory task. Their critique of reductionism followed from the toolkit rather than preceding it: a science that begins by isolating parts with externally defined properties has, on this view, decided its conclusions in its first move.

The connection to the punctuated equilibrium debate places the control case inside one of the famous scientific controversies of the late twentieth century, and the placement needs care. The theory of punctuated equilibrium, proposed by Niles Eldredge and Stephen Jay Gould in 1972, held that the fossil record's pattern of long stasis interrupted by rapid change was a genuine signal rather than an artefact of missing strata. Gould's intellectual formation included a serious engagement with the dialectical tradition, Lewontin was part of the same Harvard milieu of argument, and the theory's defenders and enemies alike noticed the family resemblance between its pattern of gradual accumulation issuing in rapid reorganisation and the dialectical grammar of quantitative change passing into qualitative change. The honest audit grades this as the survey grades relational sociology, only more so: a milieu of documented engagement, a genuine resonance of pattern, and no warrant for claiming the palaeontology as a dialectical product, since its evidential base was stratigraphy and its arguments were conducted in the discipline's own terms. What the episode shows is the permeability of a research culture at a particular place and time to habits of thought circulating in it, which is a weaker and more interesting claim than descent, and one the sociology of science makes about many traditions besides this one.

The control case earns its name by supplying the comparison the quieter cases need. Here is what the inheritance looks like when nobody pays a toll: the founders name the tradition in the title of the book, argue the categories in the open, absorb the professional consequences, which in their case were real but survivable from tenured chairs at an elite institution, and produce a body of technical work whose quality is judged by the usual standards. Set beside this, the silence of the other fields becomes legible as a dependent variable rather than a mystery. Where the founders were junior, or the host field more policed, or the political weather worse, the same inheritance arrived with the name filed off. The comparison also disciplines the enthusiasm of this survey's own side. Lewontin spent a considerable part of his polemical energy attacking the misuse of dialectical vocabulary by its devotees, insisting that the categories earned their place by generating biology and not by being intoned. The control case thus contains its own internal sceptic, and a survey that quotes the lineage with approval should quote the discipline with equal approval: the inheritance is worth claiming only where, as in the best of this field, it can be shown at work on material that pushes back.

The natural science boundary of the whole question lies nearby and should be acknowledged without being crossed. Whether nature itself, independent of the sciences that study it, is dialectical in structure was the burden of Engels's unfinished project and of the long quarrel that followed it, and that quarrel has its own article in this series, [the guide to Engels and the dialectics of nature](/2012/09/01/engels-dialectics-of-nature-guide/), where the manuscript evidence and the scientific audit are set out. The present survey makes no claim on that territory. Its claim concerns the social and life sciences as human practices: which categories their practitioners use, where those categories came from, and whether the categories work. A category can be a good instrument in a science without being a law of the universe, and most of the inheriting fields need nothing stronger than that.

## Paradox theory: contradiction in the management school

The fifth field is the survey's hardest case, and it is included because an inheritance audit that visits only friendly territory proves nothing. Organisational paradox theory consolidated as a named research programme in the management literature around the start of the 2010s, with the 2011 Academy of Management Review article by Wendy Smith and Marianne Lewis supplying its most cited formalisation. The programme's founding claim is that organisations face persistent, interdependent, contradictory demands that cannot be resolved by choosing one pole and cannot be wished away as temporary dysfunctions. Exploration competes with exploitation, global integration with local responsiveness, the control that hierarchy supplies with the autonomy that initiative requires. Earlier management thought treated such tensions as problems, trade offs to be optimised or dilemmas to be decided. The paradox programme treats them as constitutive features of organising itself, to be worked with through acceptance, differentiation, and integration rather than eliminated. Any reader of the earlier sections will feel the recognition stir. Constitutive contradiction, internal to the system's normal functioning, generating the agenda for its change: the machinery is the second tool of the dialectical toolkit, installed at the centre of a field published by the flagship journals of business scholarship.

### Why did the fields stop naming the source?

In the documented cases, because naming carried a professional price that silence avoided: appointments, journals, and funders in the decades when these fields formed often treated the Marxist association as evidence of ideology rather than of method. Silence cost the founders nothing they valued more, so the citations thinned while the categories stayed at work.

Recognition, however, is where the audit must apply its brakes, because the paradox programme's own account of its ancestry is plural and mostly runs elsewhere. Its citations reach back to the organisational literature on competing values, to systems thinking, to Eastern philosophy as filtered through management writers, to the psychology of cognitive frames, and to a long indigenous tradition of observing that managerial demands conflict. Direct engagement with Marxist dialectics appears at the margins, in a minority of papers that draw on critical management studies, a subfield where the lineage is openly kept. The evidentiary verdict is therefore the weakest in the survey, and it should be recorded without flinching: paradox theory is principally a resemblance, an independent rebuilding of the contradiction tool under the pressure of similar phenomena, with a few documented threads of descent running through its critical wing. The resemblance is itself informative. When a field with no institutional reason to flatter the dialectical tradition, and every commercial reason to prefer its own brand names, rebuilds the category of constitutive contradiction because the phenomena of organisational life will not submit to trade off logic, the rebuilding testifies to something about the phenomena. Contradiction as signal keeps being rediscovered by researchers who trip over it in the field. That testimony supports the utility of the category. It does nothing to enlarge the inheritance, and this survey does not pretend otherwise.

The management school setting adds one more datum to the professional cost argument, from the opposite side. Inside a business school, an explicit Marxist genealogy for a research programme would not merely be impolite; it would reposition the programme within the institution's internal politics, associating it with the critical management enclave and raising the price of its concepts in the mainstream journals where Smith and Lewis chose to compete. The programme's founders built their synthesis from sources the mainstream honours, and the synthesis travelled accordingly, into doctoral syllabuses and consulting practice, at a speed the critical wing's openly genealogical work has never matched. A cynic reads this as proof that packaging is everything. A historian reads it as the recurring mechanism of the unnamed inheritance operating in a new habitat: the category survives at the price of its passport, and the price is set by the host institution's politics, not by the category's merits. Both readings can be true at once, and the survey's business is with the mechanism, which the paradox case displays in its clearest form precisely because the descent there is thinnest. Where there is almost no lineage to hide, the incentives that would hide one stand out in relief.

## Systems thinking and complexity: the tempting overclaim

No discussion of dialectical survival escapes the question of systems thinking and complexity, because the family resemblance is loud and the temptation to annex is strong on both sides. The systems tradition, from Ludwig von Bertalanffy's general system theory through cybernetics to the complexity sciences of the Santa Fe generation, speaks a language of wholes, feedback, emergence, and self organisation that sounds, to an ear trained on the dialectical literature, like a translation. Emergence in particular gets claimed as a rediscovery: the appearance of qualitatively new properties at higher levels of organisation was a stock theme of the dialectical tradition, complete with Engels's examples, long before complexity science made it a research programme with mathematics attached. The claim is heard constantly in the tradition's own seminars, and the FAQ below answers its short form. The long form requires the audit's full apparatus, and the apparatus returns a verdict of resemblance with episodes of contact, stated as plainly as the paradox verdict and for similar reasons.

The contact episodes are real. Bertalanffy worked in a Vienna saturated with the philosophy of science debates in which dialectical materialism was a participant, and the systems tradition's founding generation argued with Marxist interlocutors as a matter of course. Complexity's historians have noted the migration of individual researchers and the shared ancestry in nineteenth century developmental thinking. But the machinery differs at the decisive points, and the differences are the audit's real subject. Systems theory's founding gesture was the search for formal isomorphisms across domains, laws of organisation indifferent to the material organised. The dialectical tradition's founding gesture, its fourth tool, is the opposite suspicion: that categories are welded to the historical material in which they were formed, and that an isomorphism purchased by abstracting from that material may be an artefact of the abstraction. Complexity science explains emergence through specified mechanisms, nonlinear interaction, feedback, adaptation, and tests the explanation in simulation. The dialectical tradition gestures at the pattern of emergence with far greater confidence than mechanism. Where the two meet, it is usually the complexity side that has supplied the rigour and the dialectical side that has supplied, at most, a prior sense that the pattern was there to be found. Priority in sensing a pattern is a genuine historical fact and a modest intellectual credit. It is not a lineage of method, and an inheritance claim that annexed complexity science would destroy the credibility the graded verdicts elsewhere in this survey have taken such care to build.

The honest summary of the boundary cases can now be drawn. Across the six programmes examined, one founder declared lineage, one field institutionalised it with detachable attribution, one major programme shows a fed convergence, one control case wore it openly, and two thriving literatures display resemblance with threads. That distribution, not any single case, is the survey's finding, and it is robust to the removal of any one row. Strike paradox theory and complexity entirely, as a hostile auditor might, and the documented core, Vygotsky, activity theory, dialectical biology, and the partial relational descent, still stands. Strike the documented core, as a hostile auditor cannot without explaining away founders' own statements, and the resemblances alone would support only a claim about the recurrence of the categories, not about inheritance. The two claims are different, both are worth making, and the whole discipline of this article has been to keep them in separate columns.

## The lineage table

The survey's findings compress into the brief's findable artifact, the lineage table, which sets one row per field against the category it uses, the name it gives that category, the state of the acknowledgement, the work the category does, and the test of whether the field could state its central claim without it. The table is a map, not a verdict machine, and its last column is the column that matters, because it applies the survival standard uniformly: a category counts as inherited in the strong sense only where the field's characteristic claims would have to be reformulated, at real cost, if the category were withdrawn.

Read down the acknowledgement column and the article's thesis is visible at a glance. Read down the final column and its limits are equally visible. The two columns do not move together, which is the single most important fact in this survey. The fields where the category is most load bearing include both the most explicit acknowledgement, in the biological case, and a near total silence, in the downstream uses of activity theory. Acknowledgement tracks the politics of the host institution and the biography of the founder. Load bearing tracks the fit between the category and the field's material. Any theory of the survival that confuses those two variables, that reads silence as absence or noise as presence, will misread every row of the table.

## Why the name went missing

The causes of the silence deserve their own section, because the unnamed inheritance claim stands or falls on whether the silence has innocent explanations sufficient to account for it. Four causes recur in the historical record, and they are cumulative rather than competing. The first is state violence and its shadow. Vygotsky's works were suppressed in his own country; the school that carried his name was dismantled; scholars across Eastern Europe learned across the middle decades of the century that the dialectical label attached their work to a state doctrine whose custodians could end careers and, in the worst years, lives. A category associated in professional memory with denunciation meetings does not need to be forbidden to be avoided. The second cause is the mirror image in the West, where the association ran the other way: in the American academy of the early Cold War, a declared Marxist method invited scrutiny of a kind that cost appointments and grants, and the generation of scholars who rebuilt fields after that period had been trained, many of them, to translate their commitments into the neutral idiom of method. Translation, practised for a decade, becomes a native language. The third cause is the condition of the inheritance itself. The Soviet codification of dialectical materialism into a catechism of laws and stock examples did real damage to the toolkit's reputation among working scientists, who encountered the categories most often in their most rigid form, wielded by philosophers claiming jurisdiction over laboratory results. The Lysenko catastrophe, in which a state backed doctrine destroyed a national genetics community, attached itself to the word dialectics in the memory of biologists worldwide, and it is one of the ironies this survey records that Levins and Lewontin had to spend argumentative capital separating their Marxism from that memory before their biology could be heard. The fourth cause is the ordinary machinery of disciplinary formation. New fields legitimate themselves by naming founders, founding journals, and founding problems, and the legitimation is cheapened, in the eyes of the gatekeepers every new field must persuade, by a genealogy that runs through a politically marked tradition. So the genealogy is pruned to the respectable branches. Pragmatism may be named, since it is a philosophy with university chairs. Hegel may be named at a distance of two centuries. The nineteenth and twentieth century tradition that actually carried the categories into psychology, biology, and the social sciences is summarised, where it is mentioned at all, as context.

None of these causes requires a conspiracy, and the claim requires none. It requires only that the price of the name was positive and the price of silence was zero, sustained over the decades in which citation habits ossify into disciplinary common sense. That is a claim about academic history of exactly the kind the sociology of knowledge exists to test, with appointment records, journal archives, translation histories, and citation curves as its evidence. It also generates a prediction by which it can be checked: where the price fell, acknowledgement should recover, and the record broadly obliges. The republication of Vygotsky's fuller texts, the scholarly reconstruction of his Marxist formation, the open self description of critical realist and activity theoretical schools, and the willingness of a younger generation of relational scholars to list their debts in full all date from the decades in which the Cold War price collapsed. A silence with causes that lapse, followed by speech when they lapse, is what a priced silence looks like. A silence that meant absence would have nothing to recover from.

## Two further cases at the boundary: critical realism and process sociology

The table's seven rows are the survey's core, but two further programmes belong in the record because they calibrate the scale at points the core leaves empty. Critical realism, the philosophy of science associated with Roy Bhaskar, whose A Realist Theory of Science appeared in 1975, is the case of acknowledgement at full volume in the present tense. Bhaskar's system, with its stratified ontology, its distinction between the real, the actual, and the empirical, and its insistence that social structures are both the condition and the outcome of the activity they govern, descends from the Marxist philosophy of science by a route its author never disguised, and the school that formed around it conducts its arguments with the genealogy on the table. The case matters to the audit because it isolates the variable of price. Here is a programme that kept the name and the lineage, built its institutions anyway, in journals and conferences of its own, and paid the corresponding price in mainstream uptake, where its concepts circulate far less widely than the concepts of the quieter programmes surveyed above. The comparison does not show that silence was cowardice. It shows that the toll was real, since the programme that declined to pay in silence paid in reach instead.

Process sociology, the tradition descending from Norbert Elias, calibrates the scale at the opposite point, a genuine and deep resemblance with a genealogy that runs almost entirely elsewhere. Elias's master concept of figuration, a web of interdependent people whose balances of power constitute both the web and the persons in it, is the internal relations tool in all but name, and his account of long term processes, above all the civilising process, is the process tool deployed at civilisational scale. Yet Elias's formation was in the Heidelberg of Weber's circle and in argument with Mannheim, his Marxism was that of an attentive reader rather than an adherent, and the tradition that claims him has its own sufficient ancestry. The resemblance here teaches the audit its most useful negative lesson. Internal relations thinking was reinvented, in the same decades and sometimes in the same universities, by scholars working from Weberian and pragmatist sources, because the phenomena of interdependence pressed the category on anyone who looked at them steadily. A world in which the category can be independently reinvented is a world in which resemblance alone proves nothing, which is why this article has graded every case and why the graded map, with Elias placed outside the inheritance and Emirbayer placed half inside it, is worth more than either the maximal claim or the dismissal.

The two boundary cases also frame the question the tradition's defenders least like to hear, which the next sections take up directly: if the categories can be reinvented by scholars who never read the tradition, and can be rebuilt by fields that owe it nothing, what exactly does the inheritance add? The answer cannot be the categories themselves, since those demonstrably circulate without it. The answer, when the objection has been given its full force and answered, turns out to concern the difference between possessing a tool and possessing the workshop notes: the record of what the tool was built for, where it has broken before, and which of its uses its makers learned to distrust. Whether that difference is worth the vocabulary that carries it is the honest form of the question with which this article began.

## The strongest objection: ancestor-hunting and the annexation charge

The objection to everything claimed so far must now be stated at the strength its best proponents would insist on, because the survey's graded verdicts were designed in anticipation of it and mean little until it has been faced. The objection has three layers, and the deepest is the one named in this article's commission: the charge of unfalsifiable ancestor-hunting.

The first layer is deflationary. Every field surveyed states its claims perfectly well in its own vocabulary. Developmental psychologists do not need the word dialectics to use the zone of proximal development, and saying that the zone is dialectical adds no prediction, no measurement, and no intervention to anything they do. Activity theorists run their laboratories with a taxonomy of contradictions that functions whether or not anyone reads its theoretical chapters. Relational sociologists have a manifesto with its own sources, cited in its own pages. On this layer the objection concludes that the genealogy is a hobby for historians, harmless in a footnote and inflationary in a thesis, and that calling five research programmes an inheritance of Marxism is a way of borrowing their prestige for a tradition that did not earn it in their laboratories.

The second layer is methodological, and it cuts at the category judgements on which the whole survey rests. Resemblance between conceptual schemes is cheap. Any sufficiently abstract description of a method, relations matter, tensions drive change, things are processes, can be matched to any sufficiently abstract description of another, and the matcher controls the level of abstraction. Set the description coarse enough and dialectics will be found in Heraclitus, in the Book of Changes, in Darwin, in thermodynamics, and in the management of a football team. Set it fine enough and nothing will match anything, since every field's categories are welded to its own material. The ancestor-hunter's art consists in tuning the abstraction until the desired ancestors appear, and the tuning is invisible in the finished product, which presents the match as a discovery. On this layer the objection concludes that lineage talk of the resemblance kind is not false but worse, a genre of writing whose conclusions are fixed by its method before the evidence is consulted.

The third layer is the falsifiability charge in its pure form. Ask what the unnamed inheritance claim forbids. If a field uses relational categories and cites Marx, that is lineage. If it uses them and does not cite him, that is the unnamed inheritance, the silence itself recruited as evidence. If it uses them and cites other ancestors, that is convergence that a partial lineage fed. If it does not use them at all, it has simply not been surveyed. Every possible state of the literature confirms the thesis, and a thesis that every state of the evidence confirms is not a thesis but a posture. The claim that the name went missing because naming was costly is, on this layer, the perfect immunising device, since it converts the absence of the expected evidence, citations, acknowledgements, a living vocabulary, into the strongest confirmation available. Historians of science have a name for arguments with this shape in other domains, and the name is not complimentary. If the survey cannot say what the world would look like if its thesis were false, the survey has produced an edifying narrative, not a finding.

That is the objection at full strength, and parts of it are simply correct. The deflationary layer is correct about the present tense: no working researcher in the five fields needs this article, or the vocabulary it discusses, to do the next piece of work. The methodological layer is correct as a warning about the genre, which is why the survey fixed its survival standard before visiting any field, load bearing or nothing, and why it recorded two verdicts of resemblance against its own side's interest. A defence that begins by conceding what the objection gets right is in a stronger position than one that treats the objection as a misunderstanding, and the reply that follows is built on those concessions.

## What would count against the claim

The falsifiability charge is answered not by protesting that the claim is empirical but by specifying, lineage by lineage, the evidence that would defeat it, and by checking that the defeating evidence is the kind that could actually have turned up. A claim is falsifiable when its defeaters are describable in advance and discoverable in the record. The unnamed inheritance claim, stated as a graded map rather than as a slogan, has defeaters at every grade.

For the Vygotsky lineage, the defeater would be documentary: evidence that his methodological declarations were insincere or imposed, drafts showing the Marxian apparatus added by editors, correspondence disavowing the method his books announce, or a demonstrated absence of the categories from his working concepts as distinct from his framing chapters. Such evidence would be findable if it existed; archives have been open and hostile readers motivated for decades. What the archives have instead yielded, in the work of scholars who reconstructed his notebooks and his reading, is the opposite movement, a formation more saturated in the method, not less, than the edited texts suggested. For activity theory, the defeater would be a demonstration that the contradiction taxonomy is decorative: studies in the field succeeding with the taxonomy removed, interventions whose outcomes are independent of the contradiction analysis that supposedly directs them, or a founding record showing the category imported as rhetoric after the method was built from other materials. The field's internal critics have in fact pressed versions of this charge, arguing that contradiction is invoked more often than it is operationalised, and where that charge sticks, in particular studies, the survey's verdict for those studies should fall to resemblance or below. The claim survives by being divisible, each row defeasible on its own evidence.

For the relational convergence, the defeater is subtler and therefore more instructive. The mixed verdict asserts that a partial lineage fed a convergence. It would be defeated from one side by a citation and formation history showing the relational programme's makers innocent of any contact with Marxist scholarship, which would reduce the case to pure convergence, and from the other side by evidence that the manifesto's distinction was taken whole from a single Marxist source, which would promote it to lineage. The actual record, plural sources with a documented Marxist tributary in historical sociology, is what a mixed verdict looks like when it is true, and the fact that both promotions and demotions are describable shows the verdict is a finding rather than a mood. For dialectical biology the defeater would be the demonstration that the founders' dialectical writings are detachable from their biology, that the organism environment work follows from models they built on other grounds. Their technical critics have had every incentive to show exactly this, and the standing difficulty of the attempt, the way the mutual constitution thesis is wired into the models themselves, is the control case doing its work. For paradox theory and complexity, finally, the survey has already entered the defeater's verdict: resemblance, with the inheritance claim withdrawn. A thesis that withdraws its claim over two of its six fields, on stated evidential grounds, is not a thesis that every state of the evidence confirms. It is a thesis with a shape, and the shape is the finding.

One further specification protects the professional cost argument from the immunisation charge. That argument asserts that acknowledgement was priced during the fields' formation. It would be defeated by evidence that acknowledgement was costless or rewarded in the relevant times and places, by appointment and publication records showing declared Marxist methodologists advancing as fast as their silent peers, or by the failure of the predicted recovery, fields continuing to suppress a lineage after the price collapsed and the archives opened. The recovery is imperfect and uneven, as recoveries are, but its direction is the one the argument predicts, and the argument stands exposed to the archive in the ordinary way. The central category around which so many of these judgements turn, contradiction and its grades, receives its own full treatment in [the article on contradiction in Marxist method](/2012/08/01/contradiction-in-marxist-method/), and readers who want the category itself audited, its distinction from dilemma, trade off, and tension, should go there; the present point is only that a survey which grades, divides, and withdraws has answered the falsifiability charge in the only way such charges can be answered, by exhibiting the observations that would have changed its mind.

### What would make a lineage claim false?

Three discoveries would do it in any given case: documents showing the founder's declared sources lay elsewhere and the matching passages were editorial additions, a demonstration that the category can be removed from the field's method without loss, or citation evidence that the supposed channel of transmission did not exist when the borrowing is dated.

## The fields that walked away

A survival survey is incomplete without the negative space around it, the disciplines and programmes where the categories were once present, or were offered, and were refused or abandoned. The negative space disciplines the positive claim in two ways. It shows that inheritance was never automatic, that fields with every opportunity to take up the toolkit declined it when the toolkit did not fit their material or their institutions. And it identifies the conditions under which the categories die, which is evidence about what keeps them alive elsewhere.

Economics is the largest territory in the negative space, and the most instructive, because the discipline began as the tradition's home ground. Marx's categories were forged as a critique of political economy from inside its own literature, and for two generations the argument between Marxist and mainstream economics was an argument within a shared textual world. The marginal revolution and the subsequent mathematisation of the discipline changed the terms of admission. As economics rebuilt itself around equilibrium modelling and the maximising individual, the internal relations structure of Marx's categories, in which the individual agent is constituted by class position, became not so much refuted as untranslatable into the discipline's working language. Marxist economics survived as a heterodox enclave, with its own journals and its own unresolved internal problems, above all the transformation of values into prices, but the mainstream's trajectory is a genuine abandonment: a field that possessed the categories, in the sense that its founding arguments were conducted against them, and organised its progress as their exclusion. The lesson the audit draws is that categories die when the host field's standards of what counts as a solution change underneath them, and no amount of genealogical piety revives them. Survival elsewhere in this survey happened where the host field's problems kept regenerating the phenomena the categories describe.

The abandonment inside psychology's mainstream is a second territory, and a closer one, since it borders the survey's strongest case. The Vygotskian inheritance survived in developmental and educational research, but the discipline's centre of gravity moved through behaviourism and then through the cognitive revolution toward models of mind as information processing, in which the social constitution of functions is not denied so much as deferred, treated as a topic for a later chapter that the research programme never schedules. Within that mainstream, dialectical categories are not suppressed; they are unintelligible as moves in the game being played, because the game's unit is the isolated processor and its properties. The contrast between the two psychologies is the cleanest demonstration in the survey that survival is a property of problem situations, not of arguments. Where the problem is learning in institutions, the categories live. Where the problem is the architecture of a processor, they have no purchase, and their absence there is no scandal and no loss that the field itself can be made to feel.

Formal and quantitative sociology supplies a third, partial abandonment, partial because the same discipline houses the relational programme surveyed above. The variable centred mainstream of the discipline, organised around survey instruments and regression based explanation, was the explicit target of the relational manifesto, and the manifesto's target has not surrendered; it remains the discipline's centre of methodological gravity, training most of its students and filling most of its journals. The two programmes coexist with a working border and occasional raids. For the audit, the coexistence is the datum. A single discipline can simultaneously house the inheritance in working order and organise its mainstream around the assumptions the inheritance rejects, which is further evidence that survival tracks problem situations and institutional niches rather than the verdict of any discipline wide referendum on the tradition. The categories live where they work. The survey has no more triumphalist conclusion to offer than that, and the fields that walked away are entitled to the same respect as the fields that stayed: each made a judgement about its own material, and the judgements are revisable in both directions, as the relational programme's growth inside a quantitative discipline already shows.

## Three more habitats: geography, the workplace, the developing system

The core table carried seven rows, and the boundary discussion added two programmes, but a survey that stopped there would understate the range of habitats in which the categories work, and would miss three cases that each calibrate a point the core leaves unmarked. They are presented briefly and in the audit's graded language: one lineage openly kept inside a mainstream social science, one lineage kept openly inside a subfield enclave, and one resemblance operating inside developmental science itself, in the same discipline as the survey's strongest case.

Human geography is the openly kept lineage, and its openness is the datum. Henri Lefebvre, a philosopher formed inside French Marxism, published The Production of Space in 1974, arguing that space is not a neutral container in which social relations occur but a product of those relations and in turn a condition of them, an internal relation stated for the geographer's central object. David Harvey, the discipline's most influential living theorist in the decades since, rebuilt his geography on Marxist foundations in the open, beginning with Social Justice and the City in 1973, and devoted a full chapter of Justice, Nature and the Geography of Difference in 1996 to dialectical reasoning as such, defending the priority of relations and process against the substantialist habits of spatial science in terms that announce their ancestry on the page. Geography therefore stands as the survey's existence proof that a social science can carry the lineage without the name going missing, and the conditions of the exception are as instructive as the exception. The discipline's object, produced space, resists substantialist treatment so stubbornly that the relational toolkit was a competitive advantage rather than a liability, and the field's institutional structure, with its tolerance for theoretical pluralism and its distance from policy clienteles, set the price of acknowledgement lower than psychology or management ever did. Where the price is low, the name survives. The correlation across the survey's cases is not perfect, but it runs the right way too consistently to be accidental, and it converts the professional cost argument from a story about fear into a claim about prices that different habitats set.

The sociology of work is the enclave case. Harry Braverman's Labor and Monopoly Capital, published in 1974, reopened the study of the labour process with an argument drawn directly from Marx's analysis of machinery and the degradation of work, and the research tradition it founded, labour process theory, conducted its arguments for two decades with the genealogy on the table: the contradiction between capital's drive to control the labour process and workers' resistance to that control was the field's organising tension, stated as a constitutive feature of the employment relation rather than as a malfunction within it. Michael Burawoy's Manufacturing Consent in 1979 carried the same inheritance into ethnography, and his later methodological writings on the extended case method argued explicitly that a Marxist science of the workplace proceeds by extending out from the case to the structures that constitute it, internal relations as a fieldwork discipline. Inside the enclave, acknowledgement cost little, because the enclave's journals and conferences were the relevant market and the lineage was a credential there. Outside it, in mainstream organisation studies, the same findings about control and resistance circulated widely with the genealogy thinned to a citation of the finding rather than of the framework. The enclave case thus refines the cost argument once more: the price of the name is set locally, by subfield, and a single discipline can contain a habitat where the name is an asset adjoining a habitat where it is a toll. Any future historian who tests the unnamed inheritance claim should test it at that resolution, subfield by subfield, because the aggregate citation counts of whole disciplines will wash the pattern out.

Dynamic systems theory in developmental science is the resemblance case inside the survey's home discipline, and it is the most delicate judgement in this article. From the 1990s, a programme associated with Esther Thelen and Linda Smith rebuilt the study of infant development around the claim that behaviour emerges from the real time interaction of many components, neural, bodily, environmental, none of which contains the developmental outcome in advance, and that categories like stage and milestone are stabilisations of a process rather than its causes. Paul van Geert's work on dynamic growth modelling gave the programme mathematics. The family likeness to the third tool of the dialectical toolkit, process prior to product, and to the Vygotskian suspicion of static assessment is unmistakable, and the programmes share intellectual ancestors in the organismic tradition of developmental theory. Yet the dynamic systems literature's working sources are synergetics, ecological psychology, and the motor control research of Nikolai Bernstein, and its encounters with Vygotsky, though real, are those of a neighbouring programme rather than a parent. The audit records the case as resemblance with contact, and records also why the case matters out of proportion to its size. Two programmes in one discipline, one descended and one convergent, arrived at process first accounts of development within living memory of each other. That is the strongest evidence available that the categories track something in the material itself, since convergence by independent routes is how science ordinarily certifies that a pattern is in the phenomena rather than in the observer. A tradition confident in its tools should welcome the convergence cases rather than annexing them, because convergence is the compliment that independent evidence pays.

## The texts in transit: how the inheritance travelled physically

Lineages travel in objects, and a section on the physical carriers is owed, because the unnamed inheritance was transmitted as much by editions, translations, and textbook abridgements as by arguments. The Vygotsky who reached Western psychology arrived in stages, each stage an editorial artefact. The 1962 English Thought and Language was cut to a fraction of the Russian text, with the methodological and philosophical material disproportionately removed, so that the first Anglophone generation met a cognitive psychologist with the dialectics edited out, not by censors but by publishers estimating a market. The 1978 Mind in Society was a compilation assembled by editors from manuscripts of different dates, invaluable and formative, and not a book Vygotsky wrote. The fuller scholarly editions and the biographical reconstructions came later still. A lineage transmitted through such carriers does not merely risk being forgotten; it is transmitted in a form in which the ancestry is structurally unlikely to be noticed, because the passages that announce it are the passages editors of a scientific classic learn to trim. The same pattern, in milder form, marks the other fields. Engestrom's triangle reproduces beautifully in a slide deck; the theoretical chapters that carry its genealogy do not. Emirbayer's manifesto is assigned whole, but it is assigned as a statement about method in the present tense, and its footnotes, where the ancestors live, are the part of a manifesto nobody examines students on. Textbook culture is a machine for converting lineage into technique, and it operates on every tradition impartially. The unnamed inheritance is partly a product of that machine working on a tradition whose name was, for the additional reasons already given, unusually easy to leave on the cutting room floor.

For the working researcher the practical consequence is a reading discipline rather than a lament. The sources of a method are found in the texts the textbooks summarised, and a researcher who wants to know what a category was built to do has to go to the founding documents in their fullest available editions, in the way this article has tried to model: Vygotsky's methodological writings and not only the zone's textbook paragraph, Engestrom's theoretical chapters and not only the triangle, the manifesto's own source list weighed rather than assumed. Graduate training rarely schedules that reading, which is why the teacher persona section below assigns it a specific place. The point generalises beyond this tradition. Every mature field runs on categories whose founding arguments its practitioners have not read, and the cost of the omission is the same everywhere: the category is used at the strength of its textbook paraphrase, its limits unknown because the arguments that marked the limits were in the chapters that got cut.

## Does the dialectical vocabulary earn its keep?

Strip the argument to its honest remainder and a single question is left. The categories circulate without the vocabulary, the fields that use them prosper without the genealogy, and independent programmes reinvent the machinery when their material demands it. What, then, does the vocabulary add, and for whom? The deflationary layer of the objection says the answer is nothing, and the survey has conceded that answer for the purpose of doing the next piece of research inside any of the five fields. But research is not the only activity the vocabulary might serve, and the remainder of the case for it rests on three functions that are not the function of generating findings.

The first function is calibration. A tool's founding arguments record what the tool was built to do and, more valuably, what its makers learned it could not do. The dialectical literature contains, alongside its grander claims, a long internal record of failure and correction: arguments about when contradiction language degenerates into a rubber stamp that explains everything and nothing, warnings against mistaking a verbal resolution for a real one, disputes about whether the categories of one formation may be carried into another. A field that inherits the category without the record inherits the tool without the workshop notes, and must rediscover the failure modes at its own expense. Paradox theory's internal debates about whether every tension is being inflated into a paradox, and activity theory's debates about whether contradiction is being operationalised or merely invoked, are recognisably the old failure modes arriving on schedule in fields that did not inherit the notes. The vocabulary, or at least the literature it indexes, is the cheapest available subscription to that record. This is a modest function, librarianship rather than revelation, and the survey states it at that strength deliberately.

The second function is connective. A researcher who knows that the contradiction taxonomy in an education journal, the relational manifesto in a sociology journal, and the mutual constitution thesis in a biology journal are dialects of one language can move findings across the borders, and can notice when one field has solved a problem that is blocking another. The paradox literature's techniques for working with persistent tensions, for instance, are a developed practical repertoire that activity theory's interventionists and the relational programme's empirical researchers have barely sampled, and the sampling is unlikely to happen by accident, because literatures do not cite across gaps they cannot see. Genealogical knowledge is the map of which gaps are bridges in disguise. The third function is critical, and it is the one the tradition itself would put first. A category that arrives with its history visible can be argued with at the level of its assumptions. A category that arrives naturalised, as common sense about zones and tensions and relations, can only be applied or ignored. The unnamed inheritance, for all the vitality it demonstrates, is also a loss of arguability: the social sciences are full of dialectical commitments that their holders cannot defend because they do not know they hold them, and cannot revise because revision requires knowing what the commitment replaced. Making the inheritance visible returns those commitments to the jurisdiction of argument. Whether the vocabulary that does this work must be the old vocabulary is a fair question, and the answer is probably no; any accurate map of the descent would serve. But the map has to be drawn from the record, and the record is kept in the tradition's texts, which is why the vocabulary keeps being needed as an index even by scholars who would never use it as a banner.

## The audit turned homeward

The fourth tool of the toolkit, the historicity of categories, was promised an application to the tradition itself, and the debt falls due here. If the categories survive because they fit the problem situations of the fields that host them, the same test applies to the tradition's own formulations, and some of them do not survive it unchanged. The contradiction concept the inheriting fields use is noticeably more disciplined than the one the classical literature often deployed: graded, taxonomised, tied to observable disturbances, hedged about by distinctions between contradiction, dilemma, trade off, and tension that the classical texts draw inconsistently at best. The process concept has likewise been sharpened by contact with fields that had to measure it; sequence analysis and dynamic modelling know things about process that the tradition's programmatic statements only assert. Even the internal relations doctrine emerges from the survey modified, since the relational programme's plural sources showed the doctrine being reached by routes that owed nothing to Hegel, which loosens the tradition's claim that its particular derivation is the load bearing part. A defender of the inheritance should therefore state the final position exactly. What survives is a toolkit, improved in transit, whose best current versions are distributed across the inheriting fields rather than concentrated in the tradition's own keeping. The tradition's remaining distinction is archival and critical: it holds the founding arguments, the record of failures, and the standing invitation to audit any category, including its own, by asking what work it does and what would count against it. The fields audited in this survey have, on the evidence, kept the tools in better repair than the house they came from sometimes managed. That is not a defeat for the house. It is what a successful inheritance looks like from the executor's side of the table, and it sets the terms on which the name might again be worth using: not as a claim of ownership over other people's findings, but as the title of the archive where the workshop notes are kept.

## Working rules for naming a method dialectical

The survey closes its argument with the evidentiary discipline it has applied, stated as rules for any reader who meets a survival claim in the wild, including the claims of this article. A survival claim should name the category precisely enough that its absence is imaginable; a claim about a general spirit of holism or process fails this rule at once. It should specify the evidence type, founder's declaration, institutional descent, citation channel, or machinery comparison, and accept the grade that evidence type supports, neither upgrading resemblance on enthusiasm nor downgrading declaration on distaste. It should state the defeater, the discovery that would withdraw the claim, before it states the claim's rhetorical uses. It should keep the three questions separate that polemic fuses: whether the category is present, whether it is descended, and whether it is any good, since a category can be present without descent, descended without merit, and meritorious without either. And it should apply the price test to silences: before reading a missing citation as a missing influence, ask what the citation would have cost in that field in that decade, and before reading it as proof of hidden influence, ask whether the silence is equally well explained by the ordinary amnesia of textbook culture, which forgets every tradition's founders with perfect impartiality. Claims that survive these rules are worth the argument they provoke. Claims that cannot survive them should be returned to the genre they belong to, which is commemoration, a respectable genre with different standards and no need to borrow the audit's.

## How a contradiction gets observed

Sceptics about the contradiction tool usually grant the rhetoric and deny the observation. A tension, they say, is in the eye of the analyst; one researcher's structural contradiction is another's ordinary friction, and a category that cannot be tied to a repeatable observation procedure is a licence for impressionism. The strongest reply the inheriting fields have produced is a procedure, and it comes from activity theory's methodological wing, in the work of Engestrom and Annalisa Sannino on the discursive manifestations of contradictions. Their proposal is that contradictions, which are structural and therefore not directly visible, announce themselves in talk and text in identifiable forms: dilemmas, in which a speaker oscillates between incompatible evaluations; conflicts, in which participants argue and criticise from opposed positions within the work; critical conflicts, in which a person faces inner doubt generated by contradictory motives in the activity itself; and double binds, in which a participant confronts equally unacceptable alternatives that the system imposes and cannot escape by individual effort. Each form has linguistic markers, characteristic connectives, hesitations, and shifts of footing, so that a transcript can be coded for manifestations and the coding checked between analysts. The structural claim is then an inference, disciplined by the requirement that manifestations cluster and recur around the same systemic node rather than scattering as the random static of organisational life.

The procedure matters to this survey beyond its home field, because it converts the falsifiability discussion from principle into practice. A contradiction analysis conducted this way can be wrong in findable ways. The manifestations may fail to cluster. The node they point to may fail to predict where the next disturbance appears. An intervention aimed at the node may leave the manifestations untouched while a rival intervention, aimed at training or resourcing, dissolves them, which is evidence that the tension was a skill gap or a shortage wearing structural dress. Paradox research has developed a parallel observational discipline from its own side, tracking how managers' language about a tension shifts between either or framings and both and framings across a decision episode, and treating the shift, rather than the tension's mere presence, as the predictor of outcome. Two fields that inherited or rebuilt the contradiction tool have independently concluded that the tool survives only as an observation procedure with defeaters attached. That convergence on discipline is the inheritance's most encouraging feature, worth more than any single finding, because it shows the category being held to standards its classical users stated more often than they met.

## The historical sociology tributary, examined

The mixed verdict on relational sociology asserted a Marxist tributary entering through historical sociology, and the assertion should be cashed out in names and texts rather than left as atmosphere. The clearest single document is E. P. Thompson's The Making of the English Working Class, published in 1963, whose preface contains the most quoted statement of the internal relations doctrine in the whole literature of social history: class, Thompson wrote in substance, is not a thing or a stratum but a historical relationship, something that happens in human relationships when people, through common experience, come to identify their interests against others, and it follows that class can be defined by people in their own struggles rather than read off from a position in a table. The book then performed the doctrine for nine hundred pages, treating its object as a process of formation across half a century rather than as a census category, and it formed a generation. Charles Tilly's work on collective action, whatever its later distance from Marxism, was built in argument with that formation, and his relational realism, set out programmatically near the end of his career, restated the priority of transactions and ties in terms the manifesto tradition could recognise as kin. Theda Skocpol's structural account of revolutions carried a different part of the inheritance, the insistence that states and class relations constitute one another, into the centre of comparative politics. None of this makes relational sociology a Marxist product. It makes the tributary real, documented in prefaces, acknowledgements, and a shared problem, the explanation of class formation, that the manifesto's generation inherited from the historians. When the relational programme later presented its distinction between substantialism and relation as a discovery about social theory in general, it was generalising a lesson its tributary had already taught in one domain: that the most important objects of social science are relationships with histories, and that an analysis which freezes them into attributes has discarded the phenomenon it meant to explain.

## Analytical dualism argues back

The boundary cases earn a further entry because the most instructive criticism of the process inheritance has come from inside the house of its friends. Margaret Archer, working within the critical realist orbit, charged that the tradition's handling of structure and agency commits a fallacy she named central conflation: structure and agency are so thoroughly interpenetrated in the theory that neither can be examined acting on the other over time, and the price of the fusion is explanatory, since real causal questions ask what the structure did to the agents and what the agents then did to the structure, in sequence. Her morphogenetic alternative insists on analytical dualism, separating structure and agency for analytical purposes precisely so that their interplay across time can be studied, with structural conditioning, social interaction, and structural elaboration as the phases of a cycle. The argument is a family quarrel of the best kind, because both parties claim the same ancestors and the same phenomena, and it demonstrates something the survey needs on record: the inheritance is not a orthodoxy that fields receive but a set of positions that competent heirs dispute, down to the question of whether internal relations should be asserted ontologically or handled as an analytical convenience. Archer's cycle is itself a process instrument, and her quarrel with conflation is conducted with the toolkit's own demand that categories earn their keep. A tradition whose heirs argue this productively about its central doctrine is alive in the only sense that finally matters to a survey of survival. The dead version of a doctrine is the one recited identically by everyone who mentions it.

## One phenomenon, two analyses

Abstract grading benefits from a single worked contrast, and the contrast is best drawn on material the inheriting fields have actually worked: the chronic problem of a hospital discharging patients into community care that cannot receive them, a case type that activity theoretical studies of care pathways have examined in detail. The substantialist analysis of the problem is competent and familiar. It inventories the actors as pre constituted units, hospital, general practice, district nursing, family, each with attributes, capacities, waiting lists, budgets. It models the discharge as a handoff between units and locates the failure in the interfaces: information lost, incentives misaligned, one unit's output arriving in a form the next unit cannot process. Its remedies follow from its ontology: better protocols at the interface, shared information systems, liaison roles, targets for handoff times. Many of these remedies help, and the analysis is not foolish. It is, however, systematically blind to a class of fact its units cannot express. The patient who is the object of the hospital's activity is a case to be treated and discharged; the patient who is the object of the community service's activity is a person to be supported in living at home; and these are not two descriptions of one object but two objects, constituted differently by two activity systems whose rules, divisions of labour, and success criteria were built for different purposes. The discharge failure is then not a friction between well formed units but a contradiction between the objects themselves, which is why interface repairs so often relocate the failure rather than removing it: the boundary is crossed more smoothly by an object that still changes its identity in the crossing.

The relational and activity based analysis begins where the substantialist one cannot. It takes the care pathway, not the institution, as its unit, treats the patient as constituted jointly by the systems that successively claim the case, and reads the recurring disturbances, the readmissions, the complaints, the workarounds invented by nurses and families, as manifestations pointing to the contradiction between the two objects and to secondary contradictions between the new shared instruments and the old divisions of labour. Its remedy is correspondingly different in kind: not a better handoff but a renegotiation of the object itself, conducted with the parties who own the systems, in the direction of a shared object, the patient's trajectory of living with an illness, under which both systems' work can be redesigned. The two analyses can be compared on evidence, which is what saves the contrast from being a parable. Where interface repairs succeed and stay successful, the substantialist account was sufficient and the contradiction language was ornament. Where failures migrate across repaired interfaces and cluster at the same systemic node, and where object renegotiation succeeds after protocol repair has failed, the relational account has earned its ontology in the only currency this survey recognises. The example generalises uncomfortably for the substantialist default. A great many stubborn problems in education, welfare, and organisational life have the migrating failure signature, and the signature is the empirical footprint of internally related systems whose objects do not match. Recognising the footprint is the practical skill the inheritance transmits, under whatever name the transmitting field prefers.

## The codification detour and the channels of recovery

One channel question remains, because the fields surveyed formed during decades when the direct route from the classical texts was, for most Western scholars, neither the shortest nor the safest path to the toolkit. The categories often arrived mediated, and the mediation has a history that begins with a freezing. In the Soviet Union of the 1930s the dialectical tradition was codified into an official doctrine, dialectical materialism as a catechism of universal laws illustrated by stock examples, teachable, examinable, and enforceable. Codification did two kinds of damage at once. It made the categories rigid where the classical use had been investigative, converting instruments for posing questions into a machine for certifying answers. And it exported the rigid version as the tradition's public face, so that scientists and philosophers outside encountered dialectics most often as the catechism, and formed their estimate of the toolkit from its worst edition. The recovery of the toolkit in the West ran through the counter tradition that historians call Western Marxism: through Lukacs's essays of 1923, with their doctrine of reification and their recovery of the category of totality, through the Frankfurt School's long exile and return, through the postwar French and Italian readings, and through the patient scholarly work, from the 1960s onward, of retranslating and re editing the classical texts themselves. Several of the survey's fields drank from these mediated streams rather than from the source. The relational generation in sociology absorbed totality and internal relations partly through the Lukacsian and historical sociological channels. Critical realism's formation is unintelligible without the mediated recovery. Even the Anglophone Vygotsky arrived through a channel cut by editors working in the recovery's climate.

The detour matters to the audit for a reason beyond completeness. Mediation explains the selectivity of the inheritance better than direct descent does. A field that receives a toolkit through a philosophical counter tradition receives the parts that tradition had itself selected and sharpened: relation, totality, contradiction, the critique of reification, and it does not receive the parts the channel dropped, notably the classical tradition's own empirical ambitions and its arguments about value and exploitation, which belong to other articles in this series. The inheritance is therefore not a smaller copy of the whole. It is a specific subset, filtered twice, once by the channel and once by the host field's problems, and its coherence as a subset, the way the four tools hang together in field after field, is explained by the channel's coherence rather than by any coordination among the hosts. This is the kind of unglamorous causal story, editions and exiles and syllabuses, that the sociology of knowledge exists to tell, and it is fitting that the survey of the unnamed inheritance should end its historical work there: not with a spirit moving through the disciplines, but with books, in particular translations, on particular desks, in decades when the name on their spines was best not mentioned in a grant application.

## The revisionist challenge to the strongest case

The survey's strongest case has attracted the survey's most interesting attack, and honesty requires giving the attack its own section rather than a footnote. Over the past two decades a revisionist current in Vygotsky scholarship, associated with Anton Yasnitsky and with the archival turn in the history of psychology, has argued that the received Vygotsky, the heroic founder whose method this article has described, is partly a construction of his later editors, translators, and celebrants. The revisionists point to the textual record: works published under his name in edited forms he never approved, a canon assembled posthumously, quotations that circulate in forms their sources do not support, and a biographical legend polished by a school with institutional reasons to polish it. Some revisionist claims go further and question the depth of the Marxist formation itself, suggesting that the methodological declarations served the institutional politics of a Soviet psychologist negotiating a dangerous decade.

The audit's response should be exact about what this attack does and does not reach. It reaches the legend, and where the legend exceeds the documents, the survey's phrasing should be, and has been, correspondingly careful: the lineage was graded on the founder's published and posthumous methodological statements, which the revisionists dispute in emphasis and editorial status rather than in existence. It does not reach the machinery. The genetic law of cultural development, the zone, the unit of analysis arguments are present in the work however the canon was assembled, and their descent from the method of Capital is a fact about the concepts' structure and stated rationale, not only about the man's soul. It also does not reach the transmission, since the channels through which the inheritance reached teacher education are the very editions whose construction the revisionists document; a lineage transmitted by constructed texts is still a lineage, though the historian must describe the construction as part of the route. What the attack genuinely damages is any version of the Vygotsky case that rests on the founder's personal authority or on the image of a single heroic transmission, and it thereby improves the survey it attacks, forcing the case onto its defensible ground: documents, concepts, channels. The episode should stand in this article as the model of how the other rows ought to be treated if comparable archival challenges mature against them. A lineage claim that cannot specify which of its parts an archive could embarrass is not yet a historical claim at all.

## Where the name was compulsory

The professional cost argument has a mirror case, and the mirror is worth a section because it excludes a lazy reading of the whole survey, the reading in which naming is simply good and silence simply loss. In the Soviet Union the name was not costly but compulsory, and the consequences for the categories were as distorting as silence was elsewhere. A method that every dissertation must declare and every textbook must apply becomes, under compulsion, a loyalty formula: the declaration is audited, the application is not. Soviet scholarship of the middle decades contains oceans of formally dialectical prose in which the categories perform no analytical work whatsoever, prefaces in which contradiction and development are invoked over research of the most static and substantialist design. The 1936 decree against pedology, the discipline through which Vygotsky's school had worked, showed the complementary face of compulsion: the state that mandated the vocabulary also reserved the right to abolish a field by resolution when its findings displeased. Under such conditions the rational scholar's strategy is the mirror of the Cold War Western one. There, silence about the lineage protected the work. Here, fluent declaration of the lineage protected the worker, and the categories paid the price in both systems, drained of content in the one case by ritual use and in the other by ritual avoidance.

The mirror case sharpens the survey's thesis into its final form. The survival of the categories and the survival of the name are independent variables, and neither tracks the health of the other. The name thrived, as a formula, exactly where the machinery was most often idle. The machinery thrived, in several Western fields, exactly where the name was unusable. What tracks the machinery's health, across every case in this survey, is neither declaration nor silence but the presence of problems that punish substantialist habits and of institutions that let researchers follow the punishment where it leads. That is a conclusion about the sociology of method with no partisan residue in it, and it is the conclusion the evidence, East and West, jointly supports. It also quietly answers the reader who has followed the argument this far and wants to know what should be done about the unnamed inheritance. Nothing needs to be done about the name. The work to be done is the work the fields are already doing where they are healthy: stating categories precisely, building observation procedures for them, writing down their defeaters, and keeping the founding arguments in print and in argument, under whatever title page the local price of honesty permits.

## Open problems the inheriting fields now share

A survey of survival should end its analytical work by looking at what the inheritors face jointly, because shared open problems are the practical form the inheritance takes in the present. Three such problems recur across the rows of the table, and they are the points at which a reader of this article can watch the categories being tested rather than commemorated.

The first is the problem of causal inference among internally related units. The statistical machinery of causal inference was built on an assumption that one unit's treatment does not disturb another unit's outcome, an assumption that internal relations deny in principle for most social material. The methodological frontier of the past generation, interference effects, network exposure, spillover designs, is the discipline of causal inference discovering, in its own technical language, that its units are related internally and that the discovery costs it the clean identification it prized. No lineage is claimed for this frontier; its sources are statistics and epidemiology. But its existence means that the first tool of the old toolkit is now a live technical problem inside the most formalised research practice in the social sciences, and the conceptual resources for thinking about it, what a unit is when its relations constitute it, were stockpiled by the tradition this survey has followed. Whether the frontier's practitioners draw on that stockpile or rebuild it is a question the next decade's citation record will answer, and either outcome is consistent with the survey's thesis, since the thesis concerns the categories' fitness for the material, not their owners' reading habits.

The second shared problem is prediction. Constitutive contradiction is a powerful retrospective instrument; it explains why a system generated the disturbance it did. Its prospective use, forecasting where a system will generate strain and of what kind, is weaker everywhere it has been tried, and the fields know it. Activity theory's taxonomy grades contradictions partly by their developmental promise, paradox research distinguishes productive from vicious cycles of tension, and both are attempts to convert a diagnostic category into a forecasting one without pretending the conversion is complete. The honest position, which this survey adopts, is that the tool earns its keep in diagnosis, intervention design, and the criticism of static explanation, and that its predictive credentials are a research programme rather than a result. The third shared problem is scale. The categories were forged for, and work most naturally at, the scale of institutions, pathways, classrooms, wards, firms. Their behaviour at the scale of whole societies and long durations, the scale of the classical tradition's own ambitions, is exactly where their record is thinnest and their rhetoric has historically been thickest. The inheriting fields have mostly been wise enough to work at the scale where the observation procedures hold. A reader who wants the tradition tested at the grand scale should treat the small scale successes as a reason to build the instruments, not as a promissory note that the grand claims have been redeemed.

These open problems return the survey to its opening image with the terms clarified. The student teacher meeting the zone of proximal development is meeting a tool that works, at classroom scale, with an observation discipline attached, descended by a documented route from a tradition whose name her syllabus omits. Whether the name is restored to her syllabus is a question for her institution's politics. Whether she is taught the tool's founding argument, its limits, and its defeaters is a question for her teachers, and it matters more. The inheritance, named or unnamed, is finally worth exactly what can be done with it under audit, and the audit, as this article has tried to practise it, is open to every reader who cares to run it again on better evidence.

## Dynamic assessment: the zone becomes a practice

The Vygotskian lineage has one branch in which the machinery changed an industry rather than a literature, and it deserves recording because it shows the process tool operating as a measurement technology. Dynamic assessment is the name for a family of testing practices that reject the static examination's founding assumption, that a valid measure of ability must record unaided performance under standardised conditions. In its place the dynamic examiner tests, intervenes, and retests within the assessment itself, offering graduated assistance and measuring the examinee's responsiveness to it: how much help was needed, of what kind, and how far the assisted performance transfers when the help is withdrawn. The intellectual descent is direct and acknowledged. Reuven Feuerstein's theory of mediated learning and his Learning Potential Assessment Device were built in dialogue with the Vygotskian tradition, and the subsequent literature, in the work of Carol Lidz and others, codified the approach for educational and clinical practice, where it is used above all with children whom static testing has misread: children from linguistic minorities, children with interrupted schooling, children whose unaided scores record the history of their opportunities rather than the reach of their learning.

The branch is the survey's cleanest exhibit of a category earning its keep against a rival on evidence. Static and dynamic measures of the same children diverge systematically, and the divergence is not noise: dynamic measures predict response to instruction, the thing schools actually need to know, in cases where static measures predict only the continuation of past advantage. The dialectical content of the practice is precise rather than atmospheric. The examinee is treated as a process in formation, the examiner is inside the unit of analysis rather than outside it, since the mediation is part of what is measured, and the score is a statement about a relation, between this learner and this mediation, rather than an attribute the learner carries away. Every one of those commitments contradicts the assumptions of classical test theory, and every one descends from the toolkit's first and third tools through a documented channel. Practitioners of dynamic assessment are not obliged to know this, and many do not. But the practice's internal controversies, about standardisation, about examiner training, about what counts as a fair comparison when the test deliberately differs between examinees, are the controversies of a field working out what it means to measure a becoming, and they are unintelligible except against the founding argument that a becoming is what there is to measure.

## The paradox repertoire: contradiction as a management discipline

Paradox theory was graded the weakest lineage in the survey, and it is only fair to record that it may be the strongest practice, since the field has built the most explicit practical repertoire for working with constitutive contradiction that any of the inheriting programmes possesses. The repertoire begins from the dynamic equilibrium model set out by Smith and Lewis: persistent tensions are to be met not by resolution but by a cycle of acceptance, differentiation, and integration. Acceptance is the founding discipline, the managerial act of treating the tension as a feature of the situation to be worked with rather than a problem signalling somebody's failure. Differentiation honours each pole separately, giving exploration its own structures, budgets, and protectors, and exploitation likewise, so that neither is silently sacrificed to the other's metrics. Integration then builds the linkages, the forums, the shared metrics, the senior judgement, through which the differentiated poles inform rather than merely coexist with each other. Around this cycle the literature has assembled its supporting findings: that either or framings of a persistent tension produce vicious cycles, in which the over chosen pole generates the very failures that provoke a lurch to its opposite; that both and framings, sustained by structures rather than by exhortation, produce the virtuous cycles the theory promises; and that the leader's cognitive and emotional capacity to hold a tension open, rather than to discharge it by decision, is a measurable variable with consequences for which cycle a unit enters.

None of this requires the dialectical vocabulary, and the field mostly does without it, which is the survey's point rather than a refutation of the field. But a reader who arrives at the repertoire from the classical literature experiences a recognition that the audit should record honestly. The vicious cycle, in which a one sided solution generates its opposite as a problem, is the pattern the dialectical tradition described as the self undermining of a one sided abstraction. The refusal to treat the tension as eliminable, joined to the insistence that working with it is a discipline rather than a compromise, is the contradiction tool with its metaphysics removed and its practical content not merely retained but extended beyond anything the classical texts attempted. If the question is where a manager, a head teacher, or a ward sister should go to learn how to work inside a constitutive contradiction, the honest answer this survey gives is the paradox literature, not the tradition that first named the phenomenon. The inheritance audit and the practical recommendation point to different shelves, and an article that cannot say so has confused genealogy with loyalty. The tradition's consolation, if it needs one, is of the kind this article has offered before: the category proved strong enough to be rebuilt, better, by people who owed it nothing.

## The philosopher who took the name back

One figure in the survey's landscape breaks the pattern of the missing name so completely that he serves as the exception by which the pattern is measured, and the boundary discussion of critical realism promised him fuller treatment than a paragraph allows. Roy Bhaskar's first work built a philosophy for the sciences on the distinction between the intransitive objects of knowledge and the transitive processes that produce knowledge of them, and on a stratified ontology in which mechanisms operate in open systems where their effects may or may not be actualised. That system already carried the inheritance in its bones: social structures as both condition and outcome of agency, the irreducibility of levels, explanation by underlying mechanism rather than by constant conjunction. In 1993 Bhaskar did what none of the quieter programmes did and published a large work with the inheritance in its title, Dialectic: The Pulse of Freedom, which attempted nothing less than a general dialectical philosophy centred on the category of absence, the real negation of what is present, as the motor of change and the ground of critique. The book's reception is the datum the audit needs. It was admired by the school that had formed around its author, argued with respectfully by its internal critics, and largely passed over by the mainstream philosophy of science in whose journals the earlier work had been a presence. The concepts travelled a shorter distance in the years when they carried the name than in the years when they had not.

Set beside the paradox literature, the comparison completes the survey's evidence about prices. In the same broad decades, one programme rebuilt the contradiction category under a trade name and entered the flagship journals of its field, while a philosopher of established reputation restored the old name to his system's title and watched the system's mainstream reach contract. The comparison is not a controlled experiment, and this article does not present it as one; the two bodies of work differ in difficulty, in genre, and in a dozen other ways. But the direction of the contrast is the direction the professional cost argument predicts, and it is corroborated by the intermediate cases, geography's low price and open name, the enclave's internal market and open name, psychology's high price and vanished name. A pattern that orders this many cases, in this many habitats, with its exceptions explicable by local prices, has earned the status this survey claims for it: not a law, not a conspiracy, but a finding about how the academic division of labour handles a marked inheritance, robust enough to be worth teaching and defeasible enough to be worth testing further.

## A word with too many pasts

Part of the name's unusability is semantic rather than political, and a short history of the word explains why even scholars sympathetic to the machinery reach for a synonym. Dialectic begins its career as the name of an art of conversation, the question and answer method Plato ascribed to Socrates, in which a thesis is tested by drawing out its consequences until it revises itself. Aristotle treated it as the logic of probable argument, Kant demoted it to the name of reason's characteristic illusion when it oversteps experience, and Hegel restored it to the centre of philosophy as the logic by which concepts and forms of life develop through their own internal oppositions. Marx inherited the Hegelian instrument and announced that he had inverted it, a metaphor two centuries of commentary have failed to settle. Engels generalised it into nature. The Soviet textbooks froze the generalisation into laws. Popular usage then dissolved the whole accumulation into a vague honorific for anything holistic, cyclical, or pleasantly paradoxical, until the word could be found in management journalism meaning little more than a debate with two sides.

A term carrying that many pasts is a liability in a research article, because every reader imports a different one. The psychologist hears the catechism, the philosopher hears Hegel, the journalist hears the seminar room cliché of thesis, antithesis, synthesis, a formula none of the classical figures used in the mechanical form the cliché requires. A founder choosing vocabulary for a new programme therefore faces a rational calculation quite apart from politics: the precise local term, zone, contradiction grade, paradoxical tension, relational configuration, costs nothing and communicates exactly, while the ancestral term costs a paragraph of disambiguation and still misleads half the audience. Semantic inflation is a sufficient explanation for a good deal of the silence, and the survey's argument does not require denying it. What the political and institutional causes explain, beyond the semantic ones, is the asymmetry of the silence: other inflated terms, revolution, paradigm, structure, survived their inflation and are still used with local definitions attached, while this one was dropped even by programmes whose founders had used it deliberately. Inflation explains why a word becomes expensive to use. It does not explain why the bill was paid so consistently, in so many currencies, by precisely the scholars with the strongest documented reason to keep the word. For that residue, the price argument remains necessary, and the two explanations together, a word made costly by its history and a lineage made costly by its politics, account for the pattern between them.

## Inside a Change Laboratory

The activity theoretical intervention deserves a closer description than its name usually receives, because it is the point at which the contradiction tool stops being an interpretation of other people's work and becomes a procedure a practitioner can be trained to run. A Change Laboratory is convened with the practitioners of a single activity, a hospital ward, a school, a municipal service, whose work exhibits the surface signs of structural trouble: recurring breakdowns, chronic workarounds, a sense among staff that the difficulties are nobody's fault and everybody's daily experience. The researchers bring mirror material, recorded fragments of the actual work, interviews, documents, disturbance data, and set it before the collective alongside the model of their own activity system, the triangle of elements through which the participants learn to see their practice as a system rather than as a set of personalities and pressures. Across a sequence of sessions the collective works the material through the contradiction grades, locating the disturbances in tensions within and between the elements, and then designs, first in model form and then in pilot practice, a reconceived object for the activity and the instruments the new object requires. The method's name for the whole movement is expansive learning, and its claim to be a learning theory rather than a consultancy technique rests on the cycle's structure: the object of learning is not a skill the participants lack but the activity they collectively own, transformed by their own analysis of its contradictions.

Two features of the procedure repay the survey's attention. The first is the role of the mirror. Contradictions, being structural, cannot be pointed at in a corridor; they are made visible by confronting practitioners with their own recorded disturbances and with a model that gives the disturbances a systemic address. The observation discipline described in the earlier section on discursive manifestations belongs to this moment of the work, the coding of talk for dilemmas, conflicts, and double binds supplying the evidence that the tensions are in the work and not in the analyst's prior theory. The second feature is the procedure's built in falsifier. A Change Laboratory that has misidentified its contradictions produces a reconceived object the practitioners cannot use, and the pilot phase exposes the failure in the unforgiving currency of work that still breaks down in the same places. Fields that inherit a category and submit it to that kind of practical audit are doing something the category's originators, for all their methodological sophistication, rarely had the institutional setting to do. The ward and the school have become, in this branch of the inheritance, the laboratories in which a nineteenth century category is tested with a rigour its own century could not have supplied, which is perhaps the single most persuasive exhibit in this entire survey for the proposition that the inheritance is alive. Living tools are the ones still being broken against hard material and repaired in public.

## Levins and the strategy of models

The control case of dialectical biology contains one further exhibit, small enough to be stated exactly and strong enough to carry more weight than its size suggests. In 1966 Richard Levins published a methodological paper, The Strategy of Model Building in Population Biology, which has been cited by working ecologists ever since as practical guidance, usually with no reference to its author's philosophy. The paper argues that a model of a complex system can optimise at most two of three desirable properties, generality, realism, and precision, and that the working scientist must therefore choose a strategy: sacrifice generality for realistic precision, sacrifice realism for general precision, or sacrifice precision for a model that is general and realistic but qualitative, yielding robust patterns rather than numbers. Levins's own allegiance was to the third strategy, and his reason was the toolkit speaking in a modeller's accent. A qualitative model of an internally related system, tested for the robustness of its conclusions across a family of neighbouring models, respects the constitution of the object in a way that a spuriously precise model of isolated variables cannot. Robustness, the survival of a result under the deliberate variation of the model's assumptions, becomes the criterion of truth available for systems whose parts constitute one another.

The exhibit matters because it is the inheritance at its most operational and least rhetorical. Here is a dialectical commitment, the priority of internal relations and the suspicion of false precision, converted into a decision procedure that population biologists with no interest in the commitment's ancestry have used for half a century to design their models. It also exhibits the survey's central pattern in miniature and with unusual clarity, since the paper's strategy is famous while its place in its author's philosophy is a fact known mainly to historians. The traffic between the two is not hidden. Levins himself connected them in print, in the essays where he reflected on his own practice. But the connection is optional for the user of the strategy in exactly the way the theoretical chapters of activity theory are optional for the user of the triangle, and for the same reason: the host field rewards the procedure and is indifferent, at best, to the philosophy that generated it. A survey that wanted a single compact proof that machinery can outlive its name inside serious science could rest its case on this paper alone, and the fact that it does not need to, that the same pattern recurs at the scale of whole fields, is the cumulative weight the unnamed inheritance claim actually rests on.

## For students and exam candidates

For students, this material converts most directly into evaluation credit, because examiners reward the candidate who can test a tradition against its afterlife rather than reciting the tradition in isolation. The portable asset is the graded map. Learn to distinguish a declared lineage, a convergence, and a resemblance, and to say which evidence separates them, since that distinction, applied to any claimed influence in any subject, is a transferable analytical move of exactly the kind higher mark bands describe. Use the five fields as illustrations, not as a list to be memorised: Vygotsky for the documented case, paradox theory for the resemblance case, relational sociology for the mixed case. In an essay, one field examined with its defeater stated will earn more than five fields asserted. Avoid the two symmetrical errors: claiming every relational method as Marxist, which markers read as annexation, and dismissing the lineage where founders declared it, which they read as unfamiliarity with the sources. Where a question asks whether a tradition is still relevant, the strongest available answer is neither yes nor no but an audit: relevance, graded field by field, with the standard of relevance stated in the opening paragraph.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The teaching opportunity in this material is unusually direct, because the survey's strongest case is already sitting in your syllabus under another description. Every trainee you teach will meet the zone of proximal development, and almost none will meet it as what its author said it was: an instrument built by applying Marx's method of concept formation to the problem of assessing a mind in formation. Restoring that context takes one seminar and changes the instrument's meaning for good students, who begin to see why the zone is defined against static testing rather than merely alongside it, and why its logic is genetic, a measure of becoming rather than of being. The same seminar can carry the survey's method. Set the lineage table as a document for source criticism: ask trainees to grade each row themselves, to find the founder's own statement in the Vygotsky case and its absence in the paradox case, and to draft the defeater for one row of their choosing. That exercise teaches evidence grading on material where the answers are neither trivial nor tribal, and it transfers to every other claimed influence they will meet in a professional lifetime, from learning styles to growth mindset. Activity theory supplies the practical follow through. A Change Laboratory logic, simplified for a school setting, gives staff a way to work on the contradictions of their own institution, attainment against inclusion, coverage against depth, as structural features to be modelled rather than as failings to be lamented, and trainees who have run even a miniature version stop hearing the word contradiction as a synonym for argument. Keep the vocabulary question open in the room. Whether naming the lineage helps a profession that mostly wants the instruments is a genuine question, and a cohort that has argued it from the evidence will handle the next unnamed inheritance they inherit, in whatever field, with better instruments than either piety or amnesia supplies.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

For researchers, treat the lineage table as a literature map before treating it as a thesis. Its practical use is prospecting: each row marks a field whose internal debates about a shared category are more advanced than the corresponding debate in your own field, and the fastest way to import the advance is to read across the row, contradiction taxonomies from activity theory into paradox research, positional explanation from field theory into developmental contexts, mutual constitution from biology into the study of institutions and their environments. Historians of science will find the transmission objects the tractable evidence: editions, translations, abridgements, and the citation curves that show attribution thinning as a method crosses a disciplinary border. The professional cost argument is testable at subfield resolution with appointment and journal records, and the survey would count a careful refutation at that resolution as a contribution, not a defeat. Journalists face the opposite temptation from scholars, the urge to write the hidden influence story, in which a familiar classroom technique is revealed as Marxist all along. The honest version is more interesting and has the advantage of being true: a set of tools built in a declared tradition proved so well fitted to certain problems that fields with every incentive to rename them kept the tools and dropped the name, and the dropping, not a conspiracy, is the story. Report the grades, named lineage, partial descent, resemblance, with the same prominence as the claim itself, and the piece will survive the specialists.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Is dialectics still used in social science?

Yes, largely under other names, and the honest answer has to be graded rather than flat. In developmental psychology descending from Vygotsky, in activity theory, and in dialectical biology, categories of internal relation and constitutive contradiction are in daily working use, and in the first and third of those fields the Marxist descent was declared by the founders themselves. In relational sociology the descent is partial and mixed with convergence from other sources, and in organisational paradox research and complexity studies the connection is mostly resemblance. What has nearly disappeared is the word. The machinery survives in field after field, renamed in each field's own vocabulary, and the survival is strongest exactly where the categories do load bearing work in research design, in the unit of analysis chosen, in the disturbances treated as data, and in the interventions built. A flat yes would overclaim the resemblance cases. A flat no would require explaining away founders who stated their sources in print.

### Q: Was Vygotsky's method dialectical?

By his own repeated declaration, yes, and the declaration is methodological rather than decorative. Vygotsky set out to build a psychology using the method Marx had used in Capital: find the simplest relation that generates a domain, treat the higher functions as social relations internalised, and explain a phenomenon by reconstructing the history of its formation. The zone of proximal development is that method turned into an instrument, measuring functions in the course of formation rather than only their completed products. Two cautions belong with the answer. His Marxism was restless and revisionary, quarrelling with the orthodoxies of his own milieu and drawing on Hegel, Spinoza, and the Gestalt school as well. And revisionist scholarship has shown that the received texts were heavily edited and the legend polished by his school, so the case should rest on the documented concepts and their stated rationale, where it is strong, rather than on the founder's heroic image, where the record is contested.

### Q: Is complexity theory a rediscovery of dialectics?

No, though the resemblance that prompts the question is genuine. Complexity science and the dialectical tradition share a family likeness: wholes with properties their parts lack, change that accumulates gradually and then reorganises a system, outcomes that cannot be read off from initial conditions. Emergence, the complexity field's central notion, covers ground the older tradition also claimed. The differences are decisive, however. Complexity research specifies mechanisms, nonlinear interaction, feedback, adaptation, and tests its claims in simulation and mathematics, while the dialectical tradition typically asserted the pattern of qualitative change with far more confidence than mechanism. The two also part on the status of categories: systems thinking searches for formal patterns indifferent to material, where the dialectical habit insists categories are welded to their historical material. There were episodes of contact between the traditions, and individual researchers crossed between them. But a resemblance with episodes of contact is not a rediscovery, and claiming complexity science as an inheritance would discredit the cases where descent is genuinely documented.

### Q: What is relational sociology?

Relational sociology is the programme, crystallised by Mustafa Emirbayer's 1997 manifesto in the American Journal of Sociology, that takes relations rather than self subsisting entities as the units of social analysis. Against substantialism, which first constitutes individuals, groups, or variables and then studies their interactions, the relational view holds that the terms of an analysis are themselves precipitates of transactions: a class, an identity, or an institution is a configuration of ties with a history, not an attribute carried from situation to situation. Its working branches include network analysis, sequence analysis, and the comparative historical study of processes, and its characteristic critical target is variable centred explanation that treats measurements as meaning the same thing in every configuration. Its ancestry is plural, pragmatism, Simmel, Elias, network structuralism, with a documented Marxist tributary entering through historical sociology, in Thompson's account of class as a historical relationship and the scholarship that argument formed. The programme is therefore best described as a convergence that a partial lineage fed.

### Q: Is dialectical biology a real field?

It is a real and identifiable research stance with named founders, canonical texts, and traceable influence, though it never became a large institutional field with its own departments. Its founding document is The Dialectical Biologist, published in 1985 by Richard Levins and Richard Lewontin, two senior Harvard scientists who announced the commitment in their title and argued that it did work inside the science: organisms and environments mutually constitute one another, wholes constrain parts that are nothing over and above the whole's organisation, and static categories are abstractions from processes. The stance fed the later mainstream programme of niche construction, in which organisms modify the selective environments they and their descendants inhabit, and Levins's earlier strategy of model building, trading precision for generality and realism and testing conclusions for robustness, is still cited as practical guidance by ecologists. The field's boundaries are porous and its label is used by a minority. Its findings and arguments, however, are part of the permanent record of evolutionary debate.

### Q: What is paradox theory in organisation studies?

Paradox theory is the research programme, formalised in the management literature around the start of the 2010s and given its most cited statement by Wendy Smith and Marianne Lewis in 2011, which holds that organisations face persistent, interdependent, contradictory demands that cannot be eliminated by choosing one side. Exploration against exploitation, global integration against local responsiveness, control against autonomy: earlier management thought treated such tensions as trade offs to be optimised or dilemmas to be decided, and the paradox programme treats them as constitutive features of organising, to be worked with through acceptance, differentiation of the poles, and integration between them. Its evidence links either or framings of persistent tensions to vicious cycles, in which over choosing one pole generates failures that provoke a lurch to the other, and both and framings to more durable performance. Its ancestry is plural and mostly internal to management studies, so its resemblance to the dialectical category of constitutive contradiction is best graded as an independent rebuilding with a few documented threads, not a descent.

### Q: Does activity theory come from Marx?

Yes, by a documented line of descent, though the distance matters. Activity theory descends from Vygotsky's cultural historical psychology through his collaborator Alexei Leontiev, who recast the inheritance around object oriented activity as the unit of analysis, and it was rebuilt for the study of work and learning by Yrjo Engestrom, whose 1987 Learning by Expanding made contradiction within and between activity systems the field's central diagnostic category. Engestrom's theoretical writings discuss the Marxian sources openly and at length. What happened downstream is the instructive part. As the field's instruments, the activity system model and the Change Laboratory method of intervention, travelled into education research, health services research, human computer interaction, and information systems, the genealogy became optional reading: a researcher can be trained in the method and publish with it while treating the theoretical chapters as background. The descent is a fact of the field's history. Whether any given study acknowledges it is a fact about that study's citation choices and its host discipline's incentives.

### Q: Do these fields need the dialectical vocabulary at all?

For producing their next findings, no, and this article has conceded that point to the deflationary objection rather than evading it. Each field states its claims in its own vocabulary, and the vocabulary of dialectics adds no prediction or measurement to a zone of proximal development study or a paradox intervention. The case for the vocabulary is of a different kind. It is a case about calibration, connection, and criticism. The founding arguments record what each category was built to do and where its makers learned it fails, and fields that inherit a category without that record rediscover its failure modes at their own expense. A map of the descent also lets researchers move findings across borders between fields that share machinery without knowing it. And a commitment held knowingly, with its history visible, can be argued with and revised, where a commitment absorbed as common sense can only be applied. The vocabulary functions less as a banner than as the index to an archive, and archives are judged by whether the workshop notes they keep are worth consulting.

### Q: Which disciplines dropped dialectics entirely?

Entirely is a strong word, and the honest answer names near total departures rather than absolute ones, since heterodox enclaves persist almost everywhere. Economics is the largest case: the discipline in which Marx's categories were forged organised its modern development around equilibrium modelling and the maximising individual, terms into which internally related categories like class position do not translate, so that Marxist economics survives as a self conscious heterodoxy at the margin of a mainstream that conducts its business without the toolkit. Mainstream experimental psychology is a second case, where the information processing model of mind leaves no working role for the social constitution of functions, even while developmental and educational research keep the Vygotskian inheritance in daily use. In both cases the mechanism of departure was not refutation but a change in what the field counts as a solution, which made the categories first untranslatable and then invisible. The pattern is the survey's standing lesson: categories die when host problems stop regenerating the phenomena they describe.

### Q: Is dialectics compatible with quantitative methods?

Yes, provided the commitment is understood as a discipline on measurement rather than as a veto on it, and the record of the inheriting fields shows the compatibility in practice. Network analysis, the most quantified branch of relational research, measures relations directly and builds its mathematics on their priority over attributes. Sequence analysis reconstructs biographies and careers as ordered processes. Activity theoretical and developmental researchers code discourse, model growth, and run intervention studies with comparison logic, and Levins built his whole strategy of model building for population biology on quantitative models chosen for robustness rather than for spurious precision. What the dialectical commitment rules out is a specific metaphysics of number: the assumption that a variable carries a fixed meaning across every configuration, so that a score, a class code, or an attainment measure can be pooled across contexts without asking what the contexts do to the construct. That is the historicity of categories operating as a warning about construct validity, and a researcher can honour it entirely inside a quantitative design.

### Q: Where does dialectical thinking survive in social science without the name?

In more places than the five fields at the centre of this article, and a reader equipped with the survival standard can prospect for it. It survives in dynamic assessment, where the examiner intervenes inside the test and measures responsiveness to help, a practice descended from the Vygotskian line through Feuerstein and codified by later scholars. It survives in human geography under its own flag, where Lefebvre's production of space and Harvey's openly dialectical writings made the relational account of space a mainstream position. It survives in the sociology of work's labour process tradition, where the contradiction between control and resistance organises the field from Braverman onward. It survives, as resemblance rather than descent, in dynamic systems theories of development, where behaviour is modelled as emerging from interacting components in real time. The prospecting rule is constant: look for the category doing load bearing work in a design or an explanation, grade the descent by its documents, and distrust any survival claim, including the ones in this paragraph, that cannot state what would count against it.

### Q: What should a journalist check before calling a method dialectical?

Four checks separate reporting from atmosphere. First, identify the category alleged to be dialectical, precisely enough that its absence is imaginable: internal relations, constitutive contradiction, process reconstruction, or the historicity of a construct, and not a general aura of holism. Second, ask which evidence type supports the claim: the founder's own declaration, an institutional line of descent, a documented citation channel, or a resemblance between machineries, and report the grade the evidence supports, since a resemblance announced as a lineage is the characteristic error of the hidden influence story. Third, ask what the category does in the research, what design, measurement, or intervention would collapse without it, because a category that decorates an introduction is not a survival. Fourth, ask a practitioner and a historian of the field the same question, and print the disagreement when they differ. The accurate story is usually the more interesting one: tools built in a declared tradition proved so well fitted to certain problems that fields renamed them, kept them, and dropped the name, for reasons mixing politics, pricing, and textbook amnesia.

### Q: How do systems theory and dialectics overlap without being identical?

They overlap in their enemies and in several of their instincts, and they differ in their founding gestures, which is why the overlap never becomes identity. Both traditions oppose atomism: both insist that wholes have properties their parts lack, that feedback and mutual determination are the ordinary case, and that a science of isolated variables misdescribes its objects. Both prize emergence, qualitatively new behaviour at higher levels of organisation, and both treat change as endogenous, generated inside systems rather than imposed on them. The founding gestures pull in opposite directions. Systems theory, from Bertalanffy onward, searches for formal isomorphisms, laws of organisation that hold whatever the material organised, and its descendants in complexity science cash that search out in mathematics and simulation. The dialectical tradition suspects exactly that abstraction, holding that categories are welded to the historical material in which they formed and that an isomorphism bought by discarding the material may be an artefact. One tradition generalises by emptying its concepts; the other, when it dares, by carrying the concrete along. The programmes meet often and merge never.

### Q: Why do field theorists and relational sociologists resist the dialectical label?

The resistance is usually about the label's associations rather than about the machinery, and it takes several distinguishable forms. In Pierre Bourdieu's case the quarrel was open and specific: he used the tradition's resources all his life, built his concept of field on the priority of relations, and resisted the label because he associated it with school loyalties, political tests, and a scholasticism he spent his career opposing. Relational sociologists inherit a different calculation. Their programme's sources are genuinely plural, pragmatist, Simmelian, Eliasian, network structural, and accepting a single ancestral label would misdescribe that plurality as well as expose the programme to the professional price that, in the decades of its formation, attached to a Marxist genealogy in flagship venues. There is also an intellectual ground: some resisters judge that the label now communicates less than the local vocabulary does, since dialectics carries Hegelian, Soviet, and journalistic meanings that a precise term like relational configuration avoids. Resistance is a compound of biography, price, plural ancestry, and semantics, and only the compound explains its consistency.

### Q: What would count as evidence that dialectics adds something to social explanation?

The evidence would have to be comparative and practical rather than testimonial: cases in which an analysis built on the categories identifies something a rival analysis misses, and the identification survives checking. The strongest exhibits have that shape. Dynamic assessment measures responsiveness to instruction that static testing demonstrably cannot see, and predicts response to teaching in populations static scores misclassify. Contradiction analyses in care pathways locate failure at the mismatch between two systems' objects, where interface repairs repeatedly relocated rather than removed it, and object renegotiation succeeded after protocol repair failed. Relational measures explain why identical resources yield unlike outcomes across positions. In each case the counterfactual is statable: remove the category and a finding, a prediction, or an intervention disappears. Researchers auditing such claims should keep founding texts and field notes in a single source linked system, as the study companion at [VaultBook's Marxism study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) supports, because an inheritance claim is only as good as the trail from category to document to result that the researcher can produce on demand.
