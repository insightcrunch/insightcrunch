Real utopias are institutional designs that already exist, or could plausibly be built, and that embody socialist principles inside societies still organised by capitalism. The phrase joins two words that ordinary usage keeps apart, and the joining is deliberate. A utopia, in the dismissive sense, names a place that exists nowhere and therefore instructs nobody. The real utopias project, developed by the American sociologist Erik Olin Wright across three decades, refuses that dismissal by changing the object of study. Instead of drawing an ideal society on a blank page, the project examines institutions: arrangements for deciding, owning, distributing, and governing that can be inspected, compared, and argued about one by one. Some of the institutions it examines operate already, in partial and compromised forms. Others exist as worked proposals, specified in enough detail that their workings can be criticised rather than merely applauded or deplored.

![A compass diagram beside institutional models of cooperatives, assemblies and funds, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-102.webp)

The project is deliberately concrete, and its concreteness is a methodological commitment before it is a political one. Wright held that emancipatory social science fails in a characteristic way when it moves directly from a critique of capitalism to an affirmation of socialism without passing through the middle level where institutions are designed. Critique can establish that an order produces domination and waste. Affirmation can state the values an alternative order should honour. Neither, by itself, answers the question a sceptical reader is entitled to ask: what would the alternative actually look like on a Tuesday morning, who would decide what, and by what procedure could a disputed decision be challenged? The real utopias project locates itself at that middle level. Its organising distinction, stated here at the outset because everything else in this article depends on it, separates three forms of power. State power operates through the making and enforcement of rules backed by coercion. Economic power operates through the ownership and control of productive resources. Social power operates through the capacity of people to mobilise themselves in voluntary association. Socialism, on the account the project defends, means the subordination of the first two forms to the third.

That definition will surprise readers who learned to equate socialism with public ownership, and the surprise is intended. The compass reframing, the namable claim this article tracks from beginning to end, holds that defining socialism by who decides rather than by who owns relocates the entire discussion. A nationalised industry directed by an unaccountable ministry scores badly on the reframed test even though its ownership is public. A worker cooperative whose members govern its decisions scores well even though its property form is unusual rather than state held. Participatory budgeting of a structural type, in which residents deliberate over a defined share of public investment, scores well on one dimension while leaving ownership untouched. An unconditional income floor scores well on a different dimension again, because it loosens the dependence of livelihood on an employer's permission. The reframing is what allows such unlike institutions to be treated as members of a single programme, graded by a shared standard instead of gathered by resemblance.

Wright's own route into these questions ran through class analysis, and readers who want the earlier work should begin with [Erik Olin Wright's class analysis](/2020/02/15/erik-olin-wright-class-analysis/), which traces how a sociologist of class structure arrived at institutional design. The school within which he worked, with its insistence that Marxist claims be stated precisely enough to be wrong, is surveyed in the [complete guide to analytical Marxism](/2020/01/01/analytical-marxism-complete-guide/). The present article has a narrower assignment. It explains what the real utopias project is, where its key terms came from, how its compass grades institutions, what its seven pathways and successive strategy lists actually say, which criticisms the project has attracted, and where its own author conceded ground. One correction of attribution runs through the whole discussion and deserves emphasis at the start. The phrase non-reformist reform, often credited to Wright because his project adopted and developed it, is not his. It was formulated by the French theorist André Gorz in a book of the mid-1960s, and restoring the credit is a small service the secondary literature frequently fails to perform.

A reader arriving from public policy, cooperative research, or participatory governance may never have searched for Marxism at all, and the project was designed with such readers in mind. Its questions are the questions those fields ask. Which decision procedures give affected people genuine control rather than ceremonial consultation? Which property arrangements align the interests of those who work in an enterprise with the interests of those who govern it? Which income arrangements reduce dependence without destroying the signals a complex economy needs? The project's Marxist inheritance shows in its answers, and in its conviction that the distribution of power, not the volume of output, is the variable that decides whether an institution deserves support. Whether that conviction survives the criticisms assembled later in this article is a separate matter, and the criticisms will be given at full strength before any verdict is offered.

## A phrase built to provoke a second look

The provocation in the title is worth pausing over, because misunderstanding it produces the most common misreading of the entire project. Real utopias does not mean idealised models, polished until their difficulties disappear. It means the opposite discipline. An institution earns the label by surviving three tests, which Wright stated as desirability, viability, and achievability. Desirability asks whether the design, if it worked as intended, would advance the values of equality, democracy, and community that the project takes from the socialist tradition. Viability asks whether the arrangement would keep working once installed, given the motivations of the people inside it and the pressures of the environment around it. Achievability asks whether a path exists from arrangements that exist already to the design in question, through steps that identifiable actors could actually take. A proposal failing the first test is not worth wanting. A proposal failing the second is a wish wearing institutional clothing. A proposal failing the third belongs to prophecy rather than to social science.

The three tests explain why the project's literature reads so differently from the utopian writing it superficially resembles. Classical utopias, from Thomas More onward, typically describe a whole society at rest, its conflicts resolved by design. The real utopias volumes describe fragments: a single decision procedure, a single property form, a single transfer mechanism, each examined for the ways it might fail. Failure analysis is not decoration in this literature. Contributors were invited to be severe with the proposals they examined, and several of the most cited contributions to the volumes are criticisms of the volume's own subject. The project thereby built its opposition into its method, a habit inherited from the analytical school and visible throughout Wright's writing. A design that cannot state the conditions of its own breakdown has not finished being a design.

### Does real utopias mean an ideal society described in full?

It does not. The phrase names a research programme examining single institutions and proposals, each tested for desirability, viability, and achievability, rather than a portrait of a complete social order. Whole-society blueprints are precisely what the programme refuses, because a blueprint cannot be falsified piece by piece or built step by step from existing arrangements.

There is a further discipline embedded in the three tests that readers sometimes miss. Achievability is not a prediction. Wright did not claim that the institutions he favoured would in fact be built, or that history was travelling in their direction. The claim is weaker and more useful: that a route exists, composed of steps each of which is intelligible to the actors who would have to take it. A route can exist and never be walked. The distinction matters because it separates the project from the deterministic strand of the Marxist tradition, in which the alternative order arrives through laws of development whether anyone designs it or not. Design, in Wright's hands, is what a tradition suspicious of blueprints does when it decides that refusing to specify alternatives has become the greater evasion. The refusal had respectable motives: specified alternatives can be attacked in detail, and movements prefer the safety of negation. The project's wager is that the safety costs more than the exposure.

The wager also explains the project's unusual tone. Its texts argue with their readers. A chapter proposing a property arrangement will pause to state the strongest reason the arrangement might entrench the very hierarchies it aims to dissolve, and the pause is not rhetorical throat clearing. Wright's conviction, stated across his methodological writings, was that a social science unable to specify mechanisms has not explained anything, however satisfying its vocabulary. Mechanisms are what institutions are made of. To say that a cooperative empowers its members is to owe an account of the meeting, the vote, the recall procedure, the information rights, and the exit options through which the empowerment supposedly flows. The real utopias project is, at bottom, an insistence that the socialist tradition pay that debt institution by institution.

## Conferences before synthesis: how the project began

The real utopias project began in 1991, not as a book but as a series of conferences and volumes, each devoted to a specific institutional proposal examined from several disciplinary directions. The timing deserves a sentence of attention. The project was launched in the years when state socialist regimes in Europe were collapsing, and when a certain kind of commentary treated the collapse as a verdict on every institutional alternative to market organisation. Wright's response was neither denial nor nostalgia. It was to relocate the question. If the command economies had failed, the failure belonged to a specific institutional configuration, one in which state power had absorbed social power almost entirely. Other configurations, in which association rather than command did the organising, remained untested by that verdict and could be examined on their own merits. The volumes were the instrument of that examination.

Each volume followed a common architecture. A target essay set out a proposal in institutional detail. Commentators, chosen for disagreement as much as for sympathy, subjected the proposal to criticism from economics, political theory, sociology, law, and the relevant policy fields. The proposer replied. The subjects accumulated across the decade and beyond: associational democracy, in which organised groups in civil society take formal roles in governance; a share-based scheme of social ownership, in which citizens hold coupons entitling them to dividends from a collectively held capital stock; participatory budgeting of the structural type in which residents allocate a portion of public investment through deliberative assemblies; an unconditional basic income; and measures directed at equality between women and men in the division of paid and unpaid work. The list matters because it defines what the project means by an institution. None of these is a slogan. Each is a mechanism, with rules that can be written down and consequences that can be traced.

The synthesis arrived in 2010, in the book that gathered the project's findings under the title Envisioning Real Utopias. The synthesis did three things the volumes, by their nature, could not. It stated the normative foundations explicitly, grounding the programme in commitments to equality, democracy, and community rather than in a philosophy of history. It supplied the compass, the threefold distinction of powers from which the pathways and strategies are derived. And it confronted the problem of transformation directly, asking not only what the destinations might be but how movement toward them could occur inside societies whose existing institutions resist it. A final, shorter statement followed in 2019, the year of Wright's death, recasting the strategy discussion into five named postures toward capitalism and arguing for one combination above the rest. The arc from conference series to synthesis to final statement spans twenty-eight years, and the later texts revise the earlier ones in ways this article will track carefully, because a persistent misreading treats the final formulation as an abandonment of the synthesis when it is in fact a restatement with shifted emphasis.

### When did the real utopias project begin, and in what form?

It began in 1991 as a sequence of conferences and edited volumes, each volume organised around one institutional proposal stated in detail and then criticised by commentators from several disciplines, with the proposer replying. The form came before the synthesis: the general theory of the project was assembled from these case examinations rather than imposed on them in advance.

Understanding the conference form explains a feature of the project that puzzles readers who meet it first in summary. The summaries tend to present the project as a doctrine, a set of positions to be learned. The volumes present it as a procedure, a way of arguing. A proposal enters, is damaged by criticism, and leaves either repaired or abandoned. Several proposals that entered the series left it in pieces, and Wright's own editorial introductions record the damage without flinching. This procedural character is the project's strongest defence against the charge of wishful thinking, and also the source of its most serious weakness, since a procedure for examining institutions is not yet a theory of how societies move. The transformation problem, deferred by the volumes, is where the synthesis had to do its hardest work, and where the criticisms collected later in this article concentrate their fire.

There is also a biographical thread worth one paragraph, because it explains the project's empirical temper. Wright spent the first half of his career building class schemas and testing them against survey data gathered across many countries, work described in the companion article on his class analysis. A scholar formed by that discipline does not easily accept an institutional claim that cannot be connected to observable arrangements. The real utopias project carries the same fingerprint. Its proposals are specified at the level where observation could, in principle, check them: who votes, who appoints, who can be recalled, what information must be published, what happens when the assembly and the ministry disagree. Readers comparing the project with the broader school should note that this fingerprint is exactly what the analytical Marxism guide identifies as the school's signature demand, the demand that large claims decompose into mechanisms small enough to inspect.

## The phrase real utopia before Wright: a short prehistory

The coupling of the real with the utopian has a prehistory the project's readers should know, because Wright's use of it is a deliberate intervention in an older quarrel. Utopian writing began as a literary form, the description of an imagined commonwealth, and acquired its dismissive sense gradually, as political movements learned to distinguish their scientific claims from their rivals' dreams. Within the socialist tradition the adjective utopian became a verdict: it marked the predecessors who had designed communities from first principles and appealed to reason and example, against the successors who claimed to derive their politics from an analysis of capitalism's own motion. The verdict was never entirely fair to its targets, several of whom built functioning communities and influenced legislation, but it fixed the word's meaning for a century: a utopia was what serious analysis was not. Wright's title reclaims the noun by qualifying it with the adjective his tradition least expected. The designs he gathers are utopian in their allegiance, they aim at a society answering to values the present order frustrates, and real in their method, each one inspectable, criticisable, and in many cases already operating in fragments. The reclaiming is a small act of terminological jujitsu with a large implication: if designs can be real, then the refusal to design loses its scientific alibi, and the tradition's century of abstinence must be defended on other grounds or abandoned. Much of the project's polemical energy, directed at friends more than at enemies, flows from that implication.

The prehistory also contains a warning the project heeded. The nineteenth century communities built from designs mostly failed, and failed instructively: their constitutions assumed the motivations their founders wished for, their economies ignored the surrounding market's discipline, their governance provided no procedure for revising the founding vision when experience damaged it. Read the project's three tests as answers to that record and their selection ceases to look arbitrary. Viability is the test the communities failed in their economies. Achievability is the test they failed in their isolation, designing arrivals without journeys. Desirability, the only test they passed, is the one the tradition's polemic remembered. A programme that begins by studying why its ancestors' experiments died, and builds its method from the autopsy, has a claim to be taken seriously even by readers who expect its own experiments to die of subtler causes.

### Why did Wright keep the word utopia despite its dismissive history in his tradition?

Because the dismissal had become an alibi. So long as designing alternatives counted as unscientific dreaming, the tradition could postpone specifying what it wanted indefinitely. Reclaiming the word, qualified by an adjective that binds it to inspectable institutions and operating fragments, forced the postponed question back onto the agenda: which arrangements, exactly, would embody the values the critique invokes?

## Institutions as arguments: the rhetorical function of the working example

One function of the project's cases is easy to overlook because it is rhetorical rather than analytical, yet it may be the function history remembers. A working institution argues. The cooperative that has paid its members and renewed its roof for decades refutes, merely by continuing, a family of claims about what production requires. The assembly whose budget decisions are executed refutes the claim that ordinary residents cannot govern expenditure. Refutation by existence is a distinctive logical act: it does not show that a form can be generalised, only that its impossibility, so often asserted, is false, and impossibility claims are the load bearing walls of every status quo's defence. Much political argument consists of protecting those walls with adjectives, unrealistic, unworkable, human nature, and a single functioning counter instance cracks them more efficiently than a treatise. The project understood this and curated its cases partly as ammunition: institutions whose survival could be pointed to, in argument, by people who would never read the volumes. The strategy has a corollary the critics exploit, since existence proves possibility at a scale and under conditions that may not travel, and the distance between possible somewhere and available everywhere is the whole territory of the absorption debate. But the rhetorical function should be recorded in any full account, because it explains the project's influence beyond its readership: its examples circulate in other people's arguments, detached from the grading device that gives them their precise meaning, doing the crude work of possibility wherever they land.

## The compass: three powers and a redefinition

The socialist compass is the project's organising device, and it is a classification before it is anything else, a point the critics press and the defence must handle honestly. Imagine the organisation of a society as a space defined by the relative weight of three kinds of power. State power is the capacity to make and enforce binding rules, exercised through governments, courts, administrations, and coercive apparatuses. Economic power is the capacity that flows from owning and controlling productive resources: the power to invest or withhold investment, to hire and dismiss, to set the terms on which others gain access to the means of working. Social power is the capacity rooted in voluntary association, the ability of people to mobilise themselves collectively through organisations they form, join, leave, and govern: unions, cooperatives, associations, movements, congregations, clubs, and the dense fabric of civil society generally.

Capitalism, in this vocabulary, names a configuration in which economic power dominates: the decisions that shape working lives and collective futures are governed principally by the owners of capital, with the state setting the perimeter and social power confined to the margins. Statism names the opposed configuration in which state power dominates, absorbing economic decisions into administration and reducing association to transmission. Socialism, by the compass definition, names neither public ownership nor planning as such. It names the configuration in which social power subordinates the other two: where the exercise of state power and the deployment of economic resources are made answerable to the organised capacities of associated people. The definition is deliberately a matter of degree. Actual societies sit at points inside the space, not at its corners, and movement within the space, rather than arrival at a corner, is what the project's strategies describe.

The compass reframing is the namable claim of this article, and it deserves to be stated with precision. Defining socialism as the subordination of state and economic power to social power, rather than as public ownership, relocates the definitional question from who owns to who decides. The relocation is not a verbal trick, because it changes the extension of the concept: it changes which institutions count as socialist, and by how much. A state-owned railway run by an insulated directorate counts for little. A municipally owned service whose budget and priorities are set through binding participatory procedures counts for more. A private firm converted into a cooperative counts along the dimension of internal decision rights while remaining exposed to market discipline along another. A basic income counts not because it socialises anything but because it weakens the dependence of each person's livelihood on the permission of an employer, which is a reduction in the reach of economic power. Four unlike arrangements become comparable because the compass supplies a common axis along which each moves the configuration, modestly or substantially, in the same direction.

### What does the socialist compass measure?

It measures the relative weight of social power against state power and economic power in the organisation of a society. Institutions, sectors, and whole configurations are located by asking how far their decisions are governed by voluntary association, how far by administrative command, and how far by the owners of productive resources, with socialism defined as the direction in which the first of these subordinates the other two.

Wright was explicit that the compass is a device for orientation rather than a map of terrain. A compass tells a traveller which direction a step moves in; it does not describe the ground, predict the weather, or guarantee arrival. The metaphor carries an admission inside it, and the critics noticed. Classification, however illuminating, is not explanation. To say that a cooperative moves a workplace in the socialist direction is to grade the cooperative, not to explain why cooperatives arise where they do, survive or fail as they do, or spread or stall as they do. The project answers that grading is the prior task: a tradition that cannot say which direction its favoured institutions move in cannot sensibly argue about routes. Whether the answer suffices is taken up in the criticisms section, where the taxonomy objection is stated in its strongest form and the project's reply, together with its limits, is examined without varnish.

One more feature of the compass needs emphasis before the pathways are introduced, because it governs everything downstream. The three powers are not moralised in advance. State power is not wicked, economic power is not wicked, and social power is not innocent. Associations can exclude, dominate, and ossify; a voluntary organisation can be a cartel of insiders as readily as a school of democracy. The compass grades configurations by which power subordinates which, and the normative argument for preferring social power's ascendancy rests on a further claim: that people subject to decisions should, so far as feasible, be the people who make them, and that association is the form in which that authorship can be exercised at scale. That further claim is contestable, and the project states it as a value commitment rather than disguising it as a finding. The honesty is characteristic, and it sets the terms on which the rest of the argument should be judged.

## Social power, examined closely

Social power is the load-bearing concept of the entire construction, and it repays slow examination. Wright defined it as power rooted in the capacity to mobilise people for collective action through voluntary association. Each element of the definition does work. Capacity distinguishes power from its exercise: an association that could mobilise its members possesses social power even in seasons when it stays quiet, just as a state possesses coercive power in peacetime. Mobilisation distinguishes social power from mere numbers: a million isolated individuals sharing an opinion hold no social power until organisation converts the opinion into coordinated action. Voluntariness distinguishes the associations of civil society from the compulsory memberships of the state and the contractual dependencies of employment: one joins, and one can leave, and the leaving disciplines the leadership in a way no ballot inside a compulsory body quite reproduces. Association, finally, names the durable form: not a crowd, not a mood, not a trending sentiment, but an organised body with membership, procedures, memory, and resources.

The concept's attraction for a Marxist is not hard to see. The tradition's central actor, the organised working class, is a social-power formation in exactly this sense: unions and parties are voluntary associations that convert dispersed grievances into collective capacity. Generalising the concept beyond class organisations is the project's quiet extension of its inheritance. Tenants' associations, consumer cooperatives, professional bodies, parent groups, religious congregations, and neighbourhood assemblies all mobilise people through association, and all therefore register on the compass, whatever their politics. The extension has a cost, examined among the criticisms: a concept that covers a revolutionary union and a bowling league with the same vocabulary must work hard to explain why one constrains economic power and the other does not. Wright's answer runs through the pathways. Social power becomes socialist in its effects when institutional arrangements connect association to the governance of state and economic decisions: when the tenants' association holds binding seats on the housing authority, when the cooperative's members elect the board that sets investment policy, when the assembly's budget decisions bind the administration. Association alone is civil society. Association wired into decision rights over resources is the socialist direction on the compass.

### How is social power different from popularity or public opinion?

Public opinion is a distribution of attitudes, measurable by sampling and capable of changing without any organisation existing to carry it. Social power requires organisation: durable associations with members, procedures, and the demonstrated capacity to mobilise those members for collective action. An attitude becomes social power only when an association exists that can convert agreement into coordinated pressure, participation, or withdrawal of cooperation.

Consider the concept at work on a single decision, traced concretely. A large employer in a town announces a closure. Economic power is exercised in the announcement itself: the owners may dispose of their property, and the legal order will uphold the disposal. State power appears in the perimeter: notice periods, redundancy obligations, planning permissions for the site's next use. Social power appears only if organisation exists to bring it into being: a union capable of striking, a community campaign capable of raising the reputational and political costs of closure, a cooperative capable of proposing a buyout with credible finance, a municipal association capable of tying the site's future permissions to employment conditions. Where none of that organisation exists, the compass records the configuration honestly: the decision was governed by economic power, tempered by state rules, with social power absent from the outcome. Where some of it exists, the decision is contested along a third axis. The example is structural, deliberately stripped of place and period, because the concept claims generality: wherever decisions are made, the three powers are present in some proportion, and the proportions can be compared.

The proportions can also change, and the mechanisms of change are the project's real subject. Social power grows when associations gain members, resources, legal standing, and decision rights. It shrinks when associations are dissolved, defunded, captured, or reduced to consultation. None of these changes requires a change in ownership, which is precisely why the compass reframing matters for strategy. A reform that grants an association binding decision rights over a budget line has moved the configuration, on this account, even though every property title in the jurisdiction remains exactly as it was. A reform that transfers ownership to a ministry while dissolving the associations that might have governed it has moved the configuration the other way, whatever its property form proclaims. Readers who grasp this inversion have grasped the project; readers who resist it have located the exact point where the criticisms will strike.

## Economic power reconsidered: the compass's first power

Economic power, the configuration's dominant term under capitalism, is likewise worth a slower examination than the anatomy allowed. The project follows the tradition in grounding it in ownership, then departs from the tradition in analysing its exercise. Ownership of productive assets confers a bundle of distinct capacities that analysis should separate. There is the capacity to direct the enterprise internally: hiring, organising work, setting its pace and its products. There is the capacity to appropriate the surplus the enterprise generates and to decide its reinvestment. There is the capacity of exit: to withdraw capital from a line, a firm, a region, a jurisdiction, and thereby to discipline every other actor who depends on its presence. And there is the quietest capacity, agenda power: the ability to decide which questions reach decision at all, since proposals threatening the bundle's core are filtered out of respectable consideration before any vote. The compass's grading of reforms becomes more exact as the bundle is separated. Codetermination touches the first capacity. Collective funds touch the second. An unconditional income floor touches the third, by reducing the dependence that makes exit threats terrifying to workforces and governments alike. The criterion's celebrated difficulty, judging in advance, is partly the difficulty of estimating which capacity a given reform actually reaches, beneath the language in which it is announced.

Exit deserves a paragraph of its own, because it is the capacity through which economic power governs without issuing a single order. A government contemplating a symbiotic reform that genuinely shifts jurisdiction must price the exit response: investment postponed, relocations announced, currency and bond markets registering their opinion in prices that every news bulletin will translate into a verdict on the government's competence. The anticipation of the response disciplines governments that were never threatened aloud, a mechanism the tradition calls structural dependence and the project's strategy chapters treat as the standing condition of symbiotic bargaining. Erosion's answer to structural dependence is characteristically indirect. Reforms are sequenced so that each step's exit cost to capital is small relative to the step's benefit to the order's functioning, while the associational capacities installed accumulate into a counterweight that later steps can spend. Whether such sequencing can outrun a determined exit campaign is the empirical question at the doctrine's heart, and the honest summary is that the project's literature poses it with unmatched clarity and answers it with illustrations rather than with proof.

### Which capacity of ownership does an unconditional income floor engage?

The capacity of exit, from the receiving end. A floor under every person's subsistence reduces the dependence of each worker on any particular employer and of each government on the goodwill of investors who trade on labour's desperation. The reform touches neither internal direction nor surplus appropriation directly; it weakens the threat that disciplines both workforces and administrations when either contemplates resistance.

Agenda power, the fourth capacity, connects the project's analysis to a wider literature on power's faces that the synthesis cites sparingly and uses constantly. Decisions are the visible tip of governance; beneath them lie the mobilisations of bias that decide what counts as a decision, whose grievance becomes an issue, which alternatives are realistic. Association is the project's answer at this depth as at the surface: only organised people can force an item onto an agenda organised against them, and the history of every reform in the repertoire begins with an association making decidable what the agenda had excluded. The budgeting assemblies, the cooperative foundings, the fund campaigns all started as agenda violations before they became institutions. A reader who grasps agenda power understands why the project, for all its constitutional detail, remains a theory of organisation first: constitutions register victories that organisation wins, and the register is not the victory.

## Democracy as authorship: the value beneath the device

The compass's preference for association rests on a conception of democracy that the synthesis states and this article has used without pausing to unfold. Democracy, in the thin usage of much political commentary, names a method of selecting governments: competitive elections, universal franchise, peaceful alternation. The project's usage is thicker and older. A person is governed democratically, on this account, in the measure that she is an author of the decisions governing her, where authorship includes setting the agenda, deliberating on the options, deciding, and holding the deciders to account. Elections deliver a thin slice of authorship over a small subset of decisions, those of the state, at long intervals, in bundled packages. The rest of life, the workplace, the service, the neighbourhood, the fund, is governed by decisions in which the governed figure as employees, clients, and audiences. The project's provocation is to apply the democratic standard to that rest of life without embarrassment, and to grade institutions by the authorship they deliver. A cooperative is, on this standard, a democratic reform of the firm even though no ballot for public office is involved. A participatory budget is a democratic reform of the municipality even though the council remains elected as before. The standard explains the project's otherwise puzzling serenity about forms: it will accept any institutional clothing, market, state, or association, provided the authorship inside is real, and it will condemn any form, however socialist its label, in which authorship is absent.

Authorship has internal conditions that the design principles section touched and that deserve naming as democratic theory. Authors need information they can trust, which is why publicity of criteria and independent technical support recur in the designs. Authors need time, which is why the redistribution of hours and care belongs inside the theory. Authors need the realistic possibility of prevailing, which is why bindingness is the literature's decisive variable: authorship over decisions that do not bind is theatre. And authors need protection in defeat, rights that survive losing a vote, without which minorities will not risk authorship at all. The liberal objection recorded earlier, that association's ascendancy might suffocate the unassociated, is answered at this level if it is answered anywhere: the authorship standard itself limits association's writ to decisions genuinely collective in their effects, and the project's values include the access and standing of each person, dissenter included, among the things institutions must secure.

### How does the project's idea of democracy differ from the electoral minimum?

The electoral minimum asks whether governments are chosen by competitive vote. The project's standard asks, of every institution that governs a part of life, whether the people subject to its decisions author them: setting agendas, deliberating, deciding with binding effect, and calling deciders to account. Elections satisfy the standard thinly and intermittently for state decisions alone, leaving workplaces, services, and funds to be graded by how much authorship their own constitutions deliver.

## The seven pathways to social empowerment

From the compass, the synthesis derives seven pathways along which social power can be strengthened relative to the other two forms. A pathway, in this usage, is not a strategy and not a prediction. It is a family of institutional configurations sharing a common mechanism: a characteristic way in which association comes to constrain either state power or economic power. The seven are worth taking one at a time, because summaries that list them without their mechanisms leave readers with a taxonomy when the text offers an anatomy.

Statist socialism is the pathway on which social power, organised politically, captures state power and uses the state's command over the economy to subordinate economic power. Its mechanism is the party and the plan: association, in the form of a mass political movement, takes the apparatus and directs investment from the centre. The pathway's historical record is the project's standing warning, because the mechanism contains its own reversal: the apparatus built to subordinate economic power to social power subordinates social power to itself, and association withers into administration. The compass registers the outcome without euphemism. A configuration in which the plan governs everything sits near the statist corner, however socialist its proclamations.

Social democratic regulation leaves ownership in private hands and deploys state power, under pressure from organised labour, to constrain economic power through law: labour rights, welfare provision, public services, and macroeconomic management. Its mechanism is the electoral coalition plus the union movement, and its characteristic achievement is decommodification, the partial removal of livelihood from market dependence. On the compass it represents state power, animated by social power organised in unions and parties, holding economic power within negotiated bounds. The pathway's limitation, in the project's telling, is that the subordination runs through the state rather than through association directly, leaving the achievement hostage to electoral cycles and administrative goodwill.

Associational democracy routes governance itself through organised groups. Its mechanism is the formal incorporation of associations into decision making: bodies representing affected interests hold seats, votes, and vetoes inside the institutions that govern a sector, a service, or a locality. Social power here does not merely pressure the state from outside; it occupies defined positions within the machinery, converting mobilisation into jurisdiction. The pathway raises immediate questions about which associations qualify, who certifies them, and how insider capture is prevented, and the project's volume devoted to it is largely an argument about those questions.

Social capitalism names the route by which social power constrains economic power from inside the capital structure itself, through collective funds and ownership stakes held on behalf of wage earners. Pension funds, wage earner funds, and similar devices place blocks of capital under governance answerable to organised labour or to citizens generally, so that investment decisions, the core of economic power, acquire a social principal. The mechanism is fiduciary rather than expropriatory: the funds accumulate through contributions and returns, and their governance rights grow with their holdings. The pathway's difficulties are equally fiduciary, since fund managers professionalise, beneficiaries disperse, and the social principal can fade into a legal formality.

The social economy pathway assembles a sector alongside state and market in which production is organised by associations, cooperatives, mutuals, and nonprofit bodies under democratic governance. Its mechanism is organisational form itself: enterprises whose constitutions assign decision rights to members, workers, users, or communities rather than to capital. The sector's outputs range across housing, care, food, finance, and culture. On the compass, each such enterprise is a small territory in which social power governs economic activity directly, and the pathway's open question, pressed hard by the critics, is whether the territories expand or remain an archipelago.

Cooperative market economies generalise that organisational form across the market itself. Where the social economy is a sector, this pathway imagines an economy in which the predominant firm is the worker cooperative, competing in markets while governed by its members. The mechanism is conversion and formation: firms are founded as cooperatives, or converted to cooperative ownership at succession, sale, or rescue, until the governance norm of the economy changes. Markets remain, with their discipline and their information functions, but the power inside the firm answers to the people who work in it.

Participatory socialism, the seventh pathway, carries the logic furthest toward direct popular governance of economic life, combining decentralised planning mechanisms with democratic participation at each level. Its mechanism is the nested council and the iterative plan: workplaces and communities formulate proposals, higher levels reconcile them, and the reconciliation returns for ratification. The pathway is the most demanding of the seven in its requirements for time, information, and civic energy, and the project treats it as a limiting case that clarifies the direction of travel rather than as a destination with a timetable.

### Are the seven pathways rival programmes or compatible routes?

They are presented as compatible routes that can be combined within one society, since each strengthens social power through a different mechanism and against a different constraint. A society might regulate through the social democratic pathway, govern services through associational democracy, and expand a cooperative sector simultaneously, with the compass registering the combined movement rather than forcing a choice among the three.

Two observations complete the anatomy. First, the pathways are ordered by the project's presentation from the most statist to the most associational, and the ordering is an argument: the further a pathway sits from direct association, the more its achievements depend on intermediaries that can defect. Second, every pathway except the first two routes around the classical question of ownership. That is the compass reframing at work in the taxonomy itself. Ownership matters to the project exactly insofar as it carries decision rights, and the pathways are the catalogue of ways decision rights can be redistributed without waiting for a transfer of titles that history keeps postponing.

## Pathways in depth: the statist and regulatory routes

The anatomy section named the mechanisms; this section and the two following slow down over each pathway's internal argument, since the pathways are where the project's anatomy becomes advice. The statist pathway's argument, in its strongest historical form, was a syllogism: economic power rests on private ownership; abolish the ownership and the power falls; the only instrument capable of abolition at scale is the state; therefore socialism requires the state's seizure and use by the associated, organised as a movement and a party. The project's treatment of the syllogism is diagnostic rather than contemptuous. Each premise is examined, and the failure is located in the last step's suppressed assumption: that the instrument remains an instrument. A state that has absorbed the economy's decisions acquires interests of its own, staffed by a stratum whose power is the administration itself, and the associated, whose organisations were the seizure's vehicle, find their organisations converted into the administration's transmission lines. The compass's contribution is to make the outcome describable without paradox: the configuration moved, dramatically, from economic dominance to state dominance, passing near the socialist corner only in the rhetoric of the journey. Movements formed by the syllogism's logic are thereby given a question their tradition lacked: not whether to use state power, but what institutional guarantees keep the using from becoming the used, and the guarantees the project favours are precisely the associational jurisdictions the statist pathway bypassed.

The social democratic pathway's internal argument is subtler and its self understanding richer, which is why the project treats it as the serious rival rather than the cautionary tale. Its syllogism ran: economic power is real but divisible; its incidents, hiring, firing, pricing, investing, can be regulated one by one; a labour movement organised industrially and electorally can win the regulations in sequence; and the sequence, sustained, civilises capitalism into something whose name matters less than its content. The project's quarrel is with the sequence's stability. Regulations rest on the coalition that enacted them; the coalition rests on conditions, full employment above all, that the regulations themselves do not control; and the incidents left unregulated, investment chief among them, retain the power to discipline the whole arrangement by withdrawal. The pathway's defenders answer that the same instability afflicts every route, and that the regulatory road at least delivers its goods along the way. The project's counter is the criterion again: within the regulatory repertoire, choose the regulations that build the coalition's own durable capacity, the beachhead regulations, over those that merely spend its temporary strength. The argument between the pathway and the project is thus an argument inside a shared practice about selection, which is why the renaming objection, examined earlier, has the plausibility it does: the two are close enough to quarrel over a criterion.

### What distinguishes the project's advice from the social democratic pathway it criticises?

Selection. The pathway as historically practised chose reforms chiefly by the welfare they delivered and the coalition they pleased. The project accepts the practice and imposes the Gorzian second evaluation on its choices: prefer the reform that also installs durable associational jurisdiction, since welfare delivered without capacity installed is renegotiable in a way that governed entitlements are not.

## Pathways in depth: association inside governance

The associational pathway's internal argument begins from a diagnosis of representation. Electoral representation, it observes, aggregates citizens as individuals at intervals, on packages of issues, through parties that must simplify to compete. Between elections, governance proceeds through administration, and the citizen's relation to it is that of client, complainant, or audience. Association offers a second channel of a different kind: continuous, issue specific, staffed by people with knowledge of the matter at hand, and accountable to a membership that experiences the decisions' effects. The pathway proposes to constitutionalise the second channel: to give associations defined jurisdiction inside governance, seats and votes on the bodies that regulate sectors, deliver services, and plan localities. Its theorists argue that the channel corrects characteristic failures of the first: the ignorance of distant administrators, the capture of regulators by the regulated industry's expertise, the voicelessness of affected minorities in majoritarian aggregation.

The pathway's difficulties are constitutional in the literal sense, and the volume the project devoted to the subject is an extended negotiation with them. Certification: which associations receive seats, by what test of membership, representativeness, and internal democracy, administered by whom? A test administered by the state hands the state the power to license its own opposition. A test of pure self declaration invites the manufacture of associations by the interests they are meant to check, the regulated industry founding its own tame counterparts. Equality: associations differ in members, money, and voice, and seating them as they are entrenches the inequality of civil society inside the state. Internal drift: the seated delegate professionalises, acquires the administration's perspective, and returns to the membership as its explainer rather than its voice, the iron law the labour movement's own history taught. The volume's contributors divide over whether the difficulties are design problems with institutional answers, rotation, sortition, public funding of counter expertise, recall, or symptoms of a category error, the attempt to make civil society's spontaneity do constitutional work. The project's mature position, reflected in the synthesis, treats associational democracy as a pathway to be travelled with the difficulties as permanent companions, graded by whether a given arrangement's answers to them are stated and revisable rather than assumed away.

### What is the certification problem in associational democracy?

It is the problem of deciding which associations qualify for formal seats in governance, and who decides. A state administered test lets the state license its own critics; pure self declaration lets regulated interests manufacture tame associations to occupy the seats meant to check them. Every workable scheme must answer the problem explicitly, and the project's literature treats the answer's quality as a grading criterion for the pathway's instances.

The social capitalism pathway, the fourth in the anatomy's order, rewards a slower look than it usually receives, because its mechanism is the most quietly radical in the catalogue. Collective funds accumulating ownership stakes on behalf of wage earners do not expropriate anyone; they buy, with contributions and returns, inside the market's own rules. Their radicalism is temporal: compounding works for whoever holds the fund, and a fund whose beneficiaries are the associated, governed by boards the associated elect, converts time from capital's ally into association's. The pathway's historical near instances were defeated politically at the threshold of significance, a fact the project records and the adversary criticism cites: the funds' opponents understood the mechanism perfectly, and mobilised at exactly the point where accumulation approached control. The episode is the project's own best evidence for the fourth criticism, that the powerful reply, and it is to the synthesis's credit that the episode is analysed rather than omitted. The pathway's design literature since has concentrated on dispersal and entrenchment: structures whose beneficiaries are too numerous to expropriate cheaply, whose charters sit beyond single statute reach, whose governance mixes election with sortition to resist both capture and apathy. Whether such engineering can outrun a determined adversary remains, on the evidence, an open question, and the article's grading of the strategies as unproven covers this pathway with particular exactness.

## Pathways in depth: the social economy and its neighbours

The social economy pathway's internal argument is the most modest in the catalogue and, for that reason, the most frequently underestimated. It claims no mechanism for capturing the centre and no timetable for generalising its form. It claims that a sector of production governed by association can exist inside a capitalist economy, deliver real goods and services, employ real people under real democratic governance, and persist. The persistence is the argument: each persisting institution is a refutation in actu of the claim that production requires the subordination of labour to capital's command, and refutations in actu have a force that pamphlets lack. The sector's theorists add a second claim about formation: people learn governance by governing, and a sector that schools its members in meetings, budgets, and accountability manufactures the citizenry the other pathways presuppose. The project's treatment endorses both claims and attaches its standing question: the sector's scale relative to the whole, and whether its internal virtues survive its growth, are empirical matters on which the record is mixed and the federation variable, examined earlier, appears decisive.

The cooperative market pathway generalises the sector into an economy and thereby inherits a different order of question. A market of cooperatives must solve, at scale, the problems the single cooperative solves by constitution: capital formation without sale of control, through member loans, indivisible reserves, and cooperative finance; the governance of large units, through representative structures whose drift must be constitutionally policed; the birth and death of firms, since a market economy requires exit, and a cooperative's members voting on their own firm's dissolution face a decision no capitalist workforce is asked to make about itself. The pathway's literature, including work the project commissioned, is candid that these are the points where the form is least tested, because the historical cooperative sectors grew inside economies whose commanding heights lay elsewhere. The candour is the project's method again: the pathway is stated with its untested joints exposed, and a reader is invited to grade it as a design with known unknowns rather than as a faith with none.

### How do the social economy and cooperative market pathways differ?

In scope and in claim. The social economy pathway builds a democratically governed sector alongside state and market, and claims persistence and demonstration. The cooperative market pathway imagines the cooperative form becoming the predominant organisation of production across the market itself, and must therefore answer economy wide questions about capital formation, large scale governance, and firm exit that the sectoral version can leave to its surroundings.

Participatory socialism, the seventh pathway, functions in the catalogue as the limiting case, and limiting cases earn their place by clarifying the variable. Push the subordination of the other powers to association toward its maximum and the planning problem appears in its purest form: if neither prices set by owners nor commands issued by a ministry are to coordinate production, then coordination must be achieved by the associated themselves, through procedures that gather local knowledge, reconcile conflicting claims, and return decisions for ratification, iteratively and at every scale. The pathway's designs, nested councils and iterative planning rounds, are attempts to specify that procedure. The project's grading of the pathway is the most tentative in the catalogue, not from embarrassment but from measurement: the procedure's costs in time and attention are enormous and fall on the same citizens whose associational energy every other pathway also needs, and the information problem that the market socialism debate made canonical waits at the centre of the design. Readers who want that problem argued at full length should go to the companion article on whether planning can beat the market, which owns the calculation and knowledge arguments; the present article's business is only to locate the pathway on the compass, at the far associational end, and to note that its distance from the other pathways is the measure of how much of the coordination burden association is being asked to carry.

The seven pathways, read in sequence a second time, reveal an architecture the anatomy section did not pause over. Each pathway assigns the leading role to a different bearer: the party state, the electoral coalition, the certified association, the collective fund, the sector, the firm, the council system. The catalogue is therefore also a catalogue of the tradition's successive hopes, each bearer having been tried somewhere and found partly wanting, and the project's pluralism about bearers is its historiographical honesty converted into method. No bearer is trusted alone. The combinations the strategy chapters recommend can be restated in the pathways' vocabulary as hedges among bearers, and the compass is the instrument that makes the hedging intelligible: since every bearer moves the configuration along the same axis, their partial contributions add, and their characteristic corruptions, being different, do not compound at the same point.

## Time, care, and the material basis of association

A short section with a long shadow. Social power is built from a material the project's founding volumes insisted on naming: time. Association happens in hours, at meetings, on committees, across the slow calendar of organisational life, and hours are distributed by the division of labour in employment and in the household. A population working long and unpredictable hours, then performing a second shift of unpaid care, possesses its citizenship in the evenings it is too exhausted to use. The project's inclusion of gender equality measures among its founding subjects follows from this materialism about its own central concept. Reserved and non-transferable portions of parental leave, care recognised inside pension formulas, working time norms designed for caregivers: such measures redistribute the hours from which association is built, and they therefore belong as part of a theory of power, not as a welfare afterthought. The point generalises beyond gender. Every pathway in the catalogue silently presupposes a population with the time to staff it, and a strategy doctrine that ignores the presupposition will find its associations staffed by the leisured and its compass grading a arrangement in which social power means the power of those with hours to spare. The under-specification criticism, in its distributional form, fastens here, and the project's treatment, pioneering in its volumes and thinner in its synthesis, is one of the places where a successor literature has the clearest brief.

### Why does a theory of association need a policy about working time and care?

Because association is built from hours. Meetings, committees, and organisational maintenance consume time that employment and unpaid care distribute unequally, so a population without disposable hours cannot staff the institutions every pathway requires. Measures redistributing care and shortening the working week are therefore, on the project's logic, constitutional measures for social power and not merely welfare provisions.

## The institutions under examination, type by type

Decay discipline governs this section strictly. The institutions the project examines are described as types: structural arrangements with rules that can be stated without naming any programme, place, or administration. The types are what the theory is about. A reader who wants the fortunes of particular implementations should consult the project's volumes, where the cases are named and dated; the argument here survives their omission, because the mechanisms under discussion belong to the types.

Participatory budgeting, as a type, assigns a defined portion of public investment spending to decisions made by residents through a structured annual cycle. Proposals originate in neighbourhood assemblies, are costed by technical staff answerable to published criteria, are prioritised by elected delegates, and bind the administration within the portion assigned. The type's critical features are the bindingness of the outcome and the publicity of the costing: a process whose decisions can be quietly overridden by officials is consultation wearing the type's clothing, and the project's analysis insists on the distinction. On the compass, the type moves a slice of state expenditure under social power while leaving the state's taxing and coercive apparatus untouched.

An unconditional basic income, as a type, pays a regular income to every member of a political community, individually, without means test and without work requirement, at a level set by political decision. The type's mechanism is exit power. A person whose subsistence does not depend on accepting any particular job can refuse degrading terms, bargain from a stronger position, leave a violent household, study, care, or organise. The project's interest in the type is therefore not primarily distributive, though the distributive effects are argued over at length in its volume. The interest is the type's effect on the balance inside every employment relation: economic power rests partly on the worker's necessity, and an unconditional floor reduces the necessity without touching the firm's ownership.

The worker cooperative, as a type, is a firm whose governing body is elected by its worker members on a one member one vote basis, whose surplus is allocated by that body under constitutional rules, and whose membership is tied to employment rather than to capital contribution. The type's mechanism is internal jurisdiction: the decisions that economic power ordinarily reserves to owners, concerning investment, closures, work organisation, and the distribution of surplus, are made by the associated producers themselves. The type's known strains are equally structural: raising capital without selling control, governing large scale through representative layers that can drift from the membership, and surviving in markets whose competitors face no such constitutional constraints.

Associational democracy, as a type, writes organised groups into the constitution of governance. Regulatory bodies, service boards, and planning authorities include seats reserved for associations representing those affected, chosen by the associations under public rules, with defined voting weight. The mechanism is the conversion of lobbying into jurisdiction: influence that ordinarily flows through access and pressure is replaced by formal rights that can be exercised openly and challenged legally. The type's strains are the certification problem, which associations count and who decides, and the insider problem, in which seated associations become partners in administration and lose their character as representatives of an mobilisable base.

Share-based social ownership, as a type, separates the income rights of capital from its control rights and socialises the first while dispersing the second. Citizens receive non-transferable coupons entitling them to a share of the dividends of a collectively held capital stock; firms are managed by boards operating in capital markets; the coupon income functions as a social dividend. The mechanism aims at the distributive core of economic power, the flow of profit to owners, while deliberately preserving market allocation of investment. The type attracted some of the severest commentary in the project's volumes, directed at its coupon market simulations and its silence about control, and the exchange remains a model of the project's method: a proposal stated precisely enough to be damaged, and damaged in print.

Measures for equality in the division of labour, as a type, redesign the interface between paid employment and unpaid care. Reserved parental leave portions that cannot be transferred between partners, care credits inside pension formulas, and working time norms that assume a caregiver in every household all belong to the type. The mechanism is the redistribution of time, which the project treats as a power resource as real as income: a gendered division of unpaid work constrains which associations women can join, which meetings they can attend, and which offices they can hold, so social power itself is unequally stocked until the division changes. The inclusion of this type among the project's founding subjects is one of its distinguishing marks, an insistence that the organisation of households belongs inside a theory of power rather than in a footnote about private life.

### What separates a real utopia from an ordinary policy proposal?

The tests and the compass together. A policy proposal is ordinarily defended by its effects on output, revenue, or welfare inside the power configuration as it stands. A real utopia must additionally be graded by its effect on the configuration itself: whether it strengthens social power relative to state and economic power, durably enough to count as movement rather than as decoration.

Walk the types once more with the grading question in hand and the project's unity comes into focus. The budgeting type grades on jurisdiction over expenditure. The income type grades on exit power inside employment. The cooperative grades on jurisdiction inside the firm. The associational type grades on jurisdiction inside governance. The coupon type grades on the destination of profit. The care type grades on the distribution of time. Six mechanisms, six different levers, one axis: in each case the question is whether associated people gain durable capacity to govern decisions that state or economic power governed before. A proposal can be excellent policy and score zero on this axis, and the project is untroubled by the divergence, because its claim is taxonomic before it is commendatory. It sorts the world of reforms into those that move the configuration and those that administer it, and it asks movements and governments to know which they are doing.

## Civic formation: where the associated come from

Every pathway in the catalogue presupposes people capable of running demanding institutions, and a critic is entitled to ask where such people are manufactured. The project's answer, distributed across its volumes, is that they are manufactured by the institutions themselves, in a bootstrapping the strategy depends on and rarely advertises. Participation is a skill with a learning curve: reading a budget, chairing a meeting that must finish, losing a vote without leaving the association, auditing a friend. Small jurisdictions are the training ground for larger ones, which is why the programme values modest participatory bodies out of proportion to the sums they govern. A residents' assembly allocating a minor budget line is, on this account, a school whose graduates staff the region's cooperatives, sit on its service boards, and supply its movements with officers who have actually governed something. The account reverses the usual direction of political education, in which citizens are first enlightened and then entrusted. Here they are entrusted, at small scale and with real stakes, and enlightenment follows responsibility the way competence follows practice in every other craft.

The bootstrapping has a dark twin the critics name and the volumes acknowledge. Institutions also manufacture their opposite: the experienced insider whose skills become a private asset, the delegate whose fluency in procedure distances her from the members who sent her, the graduate of a hundred meetings who now speaks a language the uninitiated cannot follow. Civic formation and oligarchy are the same process viewed at different temperatures, and the design principles, rotation, recall, term limits, sortition for some offices, are the programme's thermostat. Whether the thermostat holds is an empirical question varying by institution, and the honest literature says so. What the programme adds to the old observation is a refusal to treat the twin as a refutation. Every known method of governing anything manufactures insiders; the question is whether the constitution prices their advantage and schedules their replacement. A movement that expects formation without oligarchy will be disappointed into cynicism. A movement that designs for both products of the process has at least understood its own material.

### Why does the programme prize small participatory bodies beyond the sums they control?

Because they are schools of government. Allocating even a modest budget teaches budgeting, chairing, losing votes gracefully, and auditing, the exact competences larger jurisdictions require. Graduates of small bodies staff cooperatives, boards, and movements with people who have exercised real responsibility, so the modest institution manufactures the citizenry its larger relatives presuppose.

## Markets inside the programme: an uneasy settlement

Outsiders often assume the programme is anti market in a simple sense, and the texts support a more interesting position. Markets appear in the analysis as mechanisms with specific virtues, price signals that aggregate dispersed knowledge, exit that disciplines producers without committees, experiment that discovers wants no planner would have listed, and specific vices, the conversion of economic advantage into political voice, the treatment of care and nature as free inputs, the subordination of every internal jurisdiction to ownership. The pathways divide over how much of the mechanism to retain. The regulatory route retains nearly all of it and governs its perimeter. The cooperative route retains the mechanism and changes the firm that faces it. The associational route retains it for allocation while removing governance from its jurisdiction. Only the participatory route proposes to retire the mechanism from coordination duty, and it is the route the founder graded with most hesitation. The programme's settlement with markets is therefore instrumental and differentiated: keep the signals, contest the sovereignty. Critics from the market's side reply that the sovereignty is what generates the signals, that ownership blunted is information lost, and the argument between them is genuine and continuing. Critics from the tradition's side reply that a market retained is a master retained, whatever the perimeter's drafting, and that argument is genuine too. The programme sits between the two replies, which is either the position of reason or the position of maximum exposure, and readers will locate it according to their own analysis of whether information or power is the scarcer resource in the societies they know.

## Non-reformist reform: the term and its true origin

Here the article performs the correction its brief assigns it, and performs it carefully, because the details are the service. The concept of a non-reformist reform was formulated by André Gorz, the French writer and theorist, in a book published in French in the mid-1960s and subsequently translated; the standard English edition is the translation published in Boston in 1967 under the title Strategy for Labor. The originating text is therefore a translated text, and readers citing the concept should cite the edition they use, since the terminology of the translation, non-reformist reform among it, belongs to the English rendering of Gorz's French argument. Wright's project adopted the concept, developed it, and gave it a second life inside the theory of transformation. Adoption is not authorship, and the frequency with which the phrase is credited to the adopter rather than to Gorz is a small index of how intellectual credit migrates toward the famous.

### What is a non-reformist reform?

a reform conceived not by what is possible within existing constraints but by what should be made possible, and which shifts the balance of power so as to enable further advance. The concept comes from a French theorist writing in the mid-1960s rather than from the real utopias project.

Gorz's distinction, stated in his own terms before Wright's use of it, separates two kinds of reform by the logic of their selection. A reformist reform is chosen by the criterion of what is possible within the existing system: its feasibility is judged by the system's own constraints, and its effect, whatever its intentions, is to subordinate the goal to the system's rationality. A non-reformist reform is selected by the opposite criterion: it is conceived in terms of what ought to be made possible, judged by human needs and democratic possibility rather than by the tolerance of the existing order, and it is designed so that its achievement shifts the balance of power, creating new centres of democratic decision from which further advance becomes possible. The reform is thus evaluated twice: once for what it delivers, and once for what it makes deliverable next. A measure that delivers generously while leaving the balance untouched fails the second evaluation. A measure that delivers modestly while installing a durable capacity for further claims passes it.

The strategic setting of Gorz's argument matters for understanding its edge. He wrote against two opponents at once: against a revolutionary rhetoric that dismissed all reforms as betrayal while awaiting a rupture it did nothing to prepare, and against a reformist practice that accepted the system's criteria as the horizon of the thinkable. The non-reformist reform was his bridge concept: reforms could be a strategy rather than a substitute for one, provided they were selected by their power effects and accumulated into a sequence whose later terms the earlier terms made possible. The concept therefore belongs, in its origin, to an argument about the working class movement's posture toward an advanced capitalism its author judged stable. That it migrated, decades later, into an American sociologist's theory of institutional design is a history of the concept's portability: the criterion, choose reforms by the power they redistribute, travels across contexts because it is a criterion and not a programme.

The definition as a strategic category, its place in the older argument between reform and revolution, belongs to the companion article on the [reform or revolution debate](/2013/02/01/reform-or-revolution-debate/), which owns that territory; the present article owns only the origin, the attribution, and the use Wright made of the concept, and links rather than redefines beyond what the use requires. Within the real utopias project the concept does specific work. It supplies the selection rule for the symbiotic strategy examined below: among the reforms achievable through existing institutions, prefer those that expand social power's jurisdiction and capacity, and treat reforms that merely stabilise the configuration, however humane, as a different species of act. It also supplies the project's answer, in advance, to the charge that its favoured institutions are merely worthy: worthiness is not the criterion, and never was. The criterion is the direction of the power shift and the durability of the capacity installed.

Gorz himself revised his positions across a long career, moving through ecological and post-industrial arguments far from the industrial strategy of the mid-1960s text, and the concept travelled without him. Wright's acknowledgment of the source is explicit in the synthesis, which is part of why the secondary misattribution is so persistent a puzzle: the adopter credited the originator in print, and the credit was still lost in transmission. The episode generalises into a warning this series has issued before about other concepts. Terms detach from their makers when a later system gives them work to do, and the later system's fame does the rest. Restoring the attachment is not pedantry. A reader who knows the term is Gorz's knows to ask what argument it was coined for, and that argument, about selection criteria for reforms under a stable capitalism, is the one the real utopias project is continuing whether its readers realise it or not.

## Gorz in his own context: the argument the term came from

The terminological correction has been made; this section gives the originating argument the depth the correction promises, because a reader who knows only that Gorz coined the phrase knows a fact, while a reader who knows the argument the phrase served possesses an instrument. Gorz wrote as a theorist of the labour movement confronting a capitalism that had survived its predicted crises and learned to grow. The working class of his analysis was not immiserated in the classical portrait; it was integrated, its organisations recognised, its material condition improved by the very order it existed to oppose. In that setting, the inherited strategies both failed visibly. Revolutionary maximalism awaited a collapse that prosperity postponed indefinitely. Reformist gradualism, practising the possible, had become the administration of the given, its organisations partners in the management of the workforce they represented. The question his mid-1960s book posed was whether a third relation to reform existed: reforms as a strategy of transformation, chosen and sequenced so that each enlarged the movement's power and its sense of the possible.

His answer turned on the criterion of selection. The reformist reform, in his analysis, subordinates its objectives to the system's rationality: it asks what the economy can bear, what the administration can manage, what the electorate will tolerate, and it trims the demand until the answers converge. The trimming is invisible to its practitioners because the criteria feel like reality itself. The non-reformist reform begins from the opposite end: from needs and democratic possibilities that the system's rationality declares unrealistic, and it treats the declaration as the thing to be changed. Its demands are therefore formulated to be achievable only by shifting power: a reduction of working time that requires workers' control over its distribution, a welfare expansion governed by its recipients, a planning mechanism that seats the associated inside the investment decision. Each such reform, won, leaves behind not only its benefit but a new institutional fact, an enlarged jurisdiction, from which the next demand starts further forward. The sequence is the strategy: reforms as the construction of counter power by instalments.

### What did Gorz mean by saying a reform should be judged by what deserves to be made possible?

He meant that the feasibility test should be inverted. A reformist reform asks what the existing system permits and trims the demand to fit. A non-reformist reform starts from needs and democratic possibilities the system declares unrealistic, and is designed so that achieving it expands what can be demanded next, by shifting the balance of power rather than by accommodating to its existing terms.

Two features of the original argument were muted in its later adoption, and restoring them sharpens the reader's grip on the project's use of the term. The first is the role of refusal. Gorz's non-reformist reform is not merely a cleverly designed benefit; it characteristically imposes on capital a reform capital cannot absorb without changing its own operation, and its selection therefore involves an analysis of the adversary's absorptive capacity, the very analysis the fourth criticism finds missing in the later project. The second is the connection to working time and the critique of consumer integration, themes Gorz developed across his subsequent work: the demand for time, for hours returned from employment to life, recurs in his writing as the paradigmatic non-reformist demand, because time is the resource from which association, citizenship, and self development are all built, and because a capitalism that returns income while confiscating time has answered the demand in its own currency. The real utopias project's gendered measures, redistributing the time of care, descend from this line of Gorz's thought as surely as its selection criterion does, a lineage the project's citations acknowledge and its summaries rarely trace.

The untranslated source note required by the brief belongs here. The originating text appeared in French in the mid-1960s; the concept reaches English readers through translation, the standard English edition being the Boston translation of 1967 issued as Strategy for Labor, and citations of the concept should name the edition used, since the key terms of the English discussion, non-reformist reform prominent among them, are the translator's renderings of Gorz's French vocabulary. The caution generalises across this series: concepts that travel by translation arrive with their edges planed by another language's choices, and a reader arguing from the English phrase alone is arguing from a rendering, at one remove from the argument that coined it.

## Viability examined: the second test under pressure

Of the project's three tests, viability is the one its critics within the volumes pressed hardest, and the one a reader should learn to apply with the project's own severity. A design is viable if it would continue to function, producing its intended effects, once installed in a world of self interested actors, scarce attention, and hostile neighbours. The test decomposes into questions that can be asked of any proposal in the repertoire. What motivates the participants to sustain the institution's demanding procedures when the founding enthusiasm fades? What information must flow, to whom, at what cost, for its decisions to be competent? What prevents its offices from becoming property, its delegates from becoming a caste, its rules from becoming a liturgy recited over decisions taken elsewhere? What does the institution do when the surrounding economy offers its members a better private bargain for defecting? A proposal that answers these questions in its design has passed viability in the only sense available in advance. A proposal that answers them with the character of its future citizens has failed it, whatever its desirability.

The cooperative form illustrates the test's bite. Its viability literature, much of it produced inside the project's orbit, examines the degeneration pressure with empirical care: the tendency of successful cooperatives to hire wage labour rather than extend membership, to widen internal differentials, to professionalise governance beyond the membership's monitoring capacity. The responses are design responses: constitutional caps, membership rules tied to employment duration, federation level auditing, indivisible reserves that remove the demutualisation prize. Whether the responses suffice is an empirical question varying by sector and legal regime, and the project's method is vindicated precisely in the asking: a tradition that designs against named failure modes is doing something different from a tradition that denounces failure as betrayal after it occurs. The same discipline applied to participatory budgeting yields its known design frontier: the cycle's vulnerability to capture by the already organised, to fatigue as novelty fades, to the administration's quiet recapture of the agenda through its costing monopoly. Each vulnerability has a designed counter, rotation, sortition, independent technical support, and the counters have their own costs, which the volumes argue about in the open.

### Why does the project treat viability as a separate test from desirability?

Because an institution can be admirable in intention and unworkable in operation, and the failure is discovered by its participants at the price of their hopes. Viability asks whether the design would keep functioning among self interested actors under hostile pressure: whether its incentives, information flows, and safeguards against capture are specified in the design rather than entrusted to the virtue of future members.

Achievability, the third test, has its own internal structure worth one paragraph. Wright analysed it through the elements any trajectory requires: actors who could carry the steps, interests those actors actually have rather than interests imputed to them, and institutional way stations at which partial gains consolidate before the next step. The analysis is the project's bridge between the volumes and the strategies: a design can be viable as a destination and unachievable as a journey, and the strategy chapters exist to grade journeys. The grading is where the project's sociology does its quiet work, since the interests of actual actors, unions defending jurisdictions, parties defending electorates, officials defending budgets, are the medium through which every route must pass, and a route drawn without them is a line on the map rather than a path on the ground.

## Three strategies of transformation

Institutions describe destinations; strategies describe movement. The synthesis distinguishes three strategies of transformation, each defined by its characteristic relation to the existing order's institutions. The distinction is analytical: actual movements blend the three, and the project's argument concerns which elements of the blend deserve emphasis under stated conditions.

The ruptural strategy aims at a decisive break: the existing institutional order is smashed and replaced, through a confrontation in which the old state's coercive and administrative apparatus is dismantled and new institutions are constructed on cleared ground. Its classical form is the insurrectionary seizure of power followed by revolutionary reconstruction. The strategy's logic is discontinuity. Because the existing order's institutions embody and reproduce the power relations to be overcome, gradual use of them is judged a trap: the institutions reshape their users faster than the users reshape the institutions. Only a break, on this view, escapes the trap, and the break's costs are accepted as the price of a transformation that is genuine rather than cosmetic.

The interstitial strategy builds the alternative inside the interstices of the existing order: the spaces, margins, and unguarded niches where new institutions can be constructed without first capturing the centre. Cooperatives founded in the market's gaps, community land arrangements in the property system's slack, alternative currencies, free schools, mutual aid networks, and the whole social economy belong to this strategy's repertoire. Its logic is accumulation rather than confrontation. Each alternative institution is small, but the strategy wagers that smallness is a phase: the institutions federate, learn, demonstrate, and gradually constitute an alternative economic and social fabric growing within and against the old. The metaphor Wright reaches for is ecological: a new species establishing itself in the niches of an ecosystem it may eventually transform.

The symbiotic strategy uses the existing order's institutions in ways that simultaneously solve problems for the powerful and expand the power of the associated. Its logic is the negotiated deepening: reforms adopted because they help the existing order manage its own difficulties, designed so that their operation installs durable gains in social power. Labour market institutions that stabilise demand while entrenching union rights, welfare arrangements that support consumption while decommodifying livelihood, governance reforms that improve administration while seating associations inside it: the symbiotic repertoire works through the state rather than around it or against it. The strategy's name carries its risk inside itself, since symbiosis benefits both parties, and the question of which party benefits more, over time, is exactly the question the Gorzian criterion was built to ask.

### Which strategy does the project judge most plausible, and why?

The project judges rupture implausible as a route to a democratic socialism in developed capitalist democracies, on grounds of the state's resilience, the population's risk aversion, and the historical record of ruptures producing statist outcomes. It argues for combining the interstitial and symbiotic strategies, building alternatives in the margins while using state institutions to expand social power from within.

Wright's assessment of rupture is an assessment, not a principled rejection, and the distinction is one of the misreadings this article is charged with correcting. Nothing in the project's values forbids a break; the judgement is that, under the conditions of affluent capitalist democracies with entrenched states and populations whose material security is entangled with institutional continuity, a ruptural strategy cannot assemble the coalition it would need, and that where ruptures have occurred under other conditions, their institutional outcomes have contradicted the compass: state power swollen, social power dissolved. The judgement is empirical and revisable, addressed to specified conditions, and a reader who disputes it should dispute the conditions or the record rather than attribute to the project a dogma it does not hold. The companion discussion of the classical argument, in which rupture's defenders state their case at full length, is the reform or revolution debate, and the present article takes no side in that older quarrel beyond reporting where the project stands within it.

The positive argument joins the two surviving strategies into a single posture named in the synthesis and carried into the final formulation: eroding capitalism. Erosion is what happens when interstitial construction and symbiotic reform proceed together over long periods. The alternative institutions grow in the margins; the reforms enlarge the jurisdiction within which they grow; each gain in social power improves the conditions for the next; and the configuration of the whole shifts, not by a founding moment but by the accumulation of shifts, until the subordination the compass describes has been reversed in fact. The image is geological and deliberately unheroic. Erosion has no Bastille day. Its temporality is the temporality of institutional change as historians actually observe it: slow, reversible, uneven, and real.

## Rupture assessed: the strategy the project sets aside

Because the project's setting aside of rupture is its most consequential strategic judgement, the reasoning deserves a section of its own, presented as the project presents it and then weighed. Three considerations carry the argument. The first concerns the state. A developed capitalist democracy commands administrative depth, fiscal capacity, and coercive reserves of an order no insurrectionary movement has overcome from below in such a setting; the historical ruptures that succeeded did so amid war, collapse, or colonial withdrawal, conditions whose presence transforms the problem and whose absence defines the cases the project addresses. The second concerns the population. Where livelihoods, pensions, healthcare, and savings are woven through existing institutions, the constituency for discontinuity is structurally small, and a strategy requiring mass appetite for institutional demolition begins by requiring what the configuration prevents. The third concerns outcomes. The ruptures of the twentieth century that did succeed installed configurations the compass grades as statist: the break destroyed the old economic power and enthroned state power, with association dissolved into the apparatus. A strategy whose historical successes fail the project's own test is, for the project, refuted as a route to its goal even where it succeeded as a route to power.

The counterargument, which the project reports and which readers should hold alongside, is that the assessment mistakes a period for a law. Conditions change: states weaken, wars and crises dissolve the entanglements that bind populations to continuity, and moments arrive in which the interstitial and symbiotic repertoires are simply not the choices on offer. A strategy doctrine built for stability may find itself speechless in instability, and the classical tradition's ruptural repertoire, whatever its record, at least contains a grammar for such moments. Wright's reply is concessive in form: the assessment is indexed to conditions, and different conditions would require the argument to be rerun. The concession is genuine, and it marks the boundary of the project's claim. The real utopias literature is a theory of transformation for societies in which the question can be posed gradually, and it does not pretend otherwise.

### Does the project reject revolution on principle?

It does not. The rejection is a plausibility judgement addressed to developed capitalist democracies under conditions of institutional stability: rupture is judged unable to gather its necessary coalition there, and its historical outcomes are judged statist by the compass. The judgement is stated as revisable if the conditions change, which distinguishes an assessment from a dogma.

There is a quieter point inside the rupture discussion that connects it to the rest of the project. The ruptural strategy, in its classical form, treats institutions as instruments to be seized or smashed, and therefore has little need for institutional design in advance: the new order's forms can be improvised after the break, by the victorious association acting through its party and state. The real utopias project's entire existence is a dissent from that sequence. Design after victory arrives too late, because the institutions improvised under emergency conditions are built by the apparatus that emergency empowers, and the compass records the result. Design before, in detail, with failure modes examined, is the project's alternative discipline, and its suspicion of rupture is at bottom a suspicion of improvisation. Readers who find the suspicion persuasive will find the whole project persuasive in tendency; readers who judge emergency improvisation unavoidable will judge the project a fair weather doctrine. The article's verdict section returns to this fork with the defeater attached.

## The interstitial wager: building in the margins

The interstitial strategy carries the project's hopes and its heaviest burden of doubt, and the doubt will be given its own section among the criticisms. Here the strategy is stated as its defenders state it. Its premise is that capitalist societies are not seamless. Markets have gaps where needs are unprofitable; property law has slack where assets are unwanted; the state's attention is finite and its peripheries are unpatrolled. In those spaces, institutions embodying different power relations can be built legally, at small scale, with resources their builders control. The cooperative workshop, the community land trust holding land outside speculation, the mutual credit circle, the open source commons producing outside the wage relation: each is a pocket in which the compass's preferred configuration already operates, surrounded by a sea in which it does not.

The wager has three stages. In the first, the pockets demonstrate: they show, in working form, that production and provision can be governed by association without collapse into chaos or charity. Demonstration matters because the achievability test fails most alternatives at the level of imagination; people cannot choose what they cannot picture working. In the second stage, the pockets federate: they build shared institutions, finance, training, supply chains, and legal forms, converting isolated experiments into a sector with weight. In the third, the sector's weight alters the terms of the whole: labour markets tighten where exit into the sector is credible, policy bends toward the sector's needs, and the configuration shifts by accumulation. Wright was careful to present the stages as a logic rather than a forecast. Nothing guarantees the passage from the first stage to the third, and the historical record of alternative institutions is largely a record of arrest at the first stage, a fact the critics wield and the defence must absorb.

### Why do interstitial institutions risk absorption or marginality?

Because the environment selects. An alternative institution that trades in markets faces competitors without its constitutional constraints, and survives either by adopting the practices it was founded to escape or by retreating into a niche where its difference is affordable. The project's critics argue that the record shows one fate or the other, and that no mechanism has been specified that reliably produces a third outcome.

Absorption and marginality deserve separate statements, since they are different deaths. Absorption is success at the price of identity: the institution grows by converging on the forms around it, its democratic governance thinning into ceremony as scale, finance, and professional management impose the standard template. Marginality is identity at the price of weight: the institution keeps its constitution pure by staying small, serving a self selected membership, and exerting no pressure on the economic power beyond its own doorstep. The critics' dilemma, stated in its strongest form later, is that the interstitial record exhibits both deaths abundantly and the third outcome, scaled constraint without convergence, only as anecdote. The defence answers that the dilemma describes the interstitial strategy pursued alone, and that the project's actual proposal pairs it with the symbiotic strategy precisely so that law and policy can change the selection environment: procurement rules, finance access, and legal forms can be altered to make the third outcome less miraculous. Whether the pairing answers the dilemma or merely relocates it is the question on which the article's verdict will turn.

## The symbiotic wager: using the order against its grain

The symbiotic strategy is the project's most distinctive and most exposed proposal, because it works deliberately through the institutions of the order it aims to transform. Its premise is that the existing order has problems it cannot solve with its own resources alone: labour markets that fail to reproduce skills, care needs that markets undersupply, legitimacy deficits that pure coercion cannot repair, demand that stagnant wages cannot sustain. Where the order needs the cooperation of the associated, cooperation has a price, and the price can be charged in the currency of power. Reforms are then adopted for reasons the powerful endorse, while their design installs capacities the associated retain. A training system employers support for its skills can be governed by boards on which unions hold binding seats. A welfare reform treasuries support for its activation effects can be designed so that its entitlements decommodify rather than discipline. The same statute, drafted differently, is a different act on the compass, and the symbiotic strategist's craft is drafting.

This is where the Gorzian criterion earns its keep inside the project. Symbiosis without the criterion is ordinary reformism: cooperation purchased at whatever terms the powerful offer, with the gains measured in welfare rather than in power. The criterion converts symbiosis into a strategy by imposing the second evaluation on every bargain. Does the reform, beyond its benefits, enlarge the jurisdiction, resources, or organisational capacity of association? Does it install a beachhead from which the next claim is easier, or does it purchase tranquillity by demobilising the claimant? Wright's term for the desirable subset is the one Gorz supplied: the symbiotic strategy, properly conducted, is the pursuit of non-reformist reforms through the state's own machinery. The two halves of the project's strategy thus interlock. Interstitial construction supplies the demonstrations and the exit options; symbiotic reform supplies the legal and fiscal environment in which construction can scale; the criterion patrols the boundary between strategy and surrender.

### What makes a symbiotic reform different from ordinary reformism?

Its selection rule. Ordinary reformism evaluates a reform by the welfare it delivers within the existing configuration of power. The symbiotic strategy as the project defines it applies a second test drawn from Gorz: the reform must also shift the balance of power, enlarging the durable capacity of voluntary association to govern decisions, so that the achievement makes further achievement more attainable rather than less.

The exposure of the strategy is equally clear, and honesty requires stating it before the critics do. A bargain in which both sides gain invites the question of who can revoke it. Statutes can be amended by the majorities that passed them; boards can be restacked; entitlements can be reframed as burdens by a later administration with a different story about the order's problems. The symbiotic strategist answers that revocation has costs when the reform has built constituencies and capacities in the meantime, which is precisely the power the criterion told her to install. The answer is coherent. It is also, as the criticism section will show, an answer whose evidence is retrospective: the beachheads that held are celebrated, the beachheads that were washed away are reclassified as reforms that were never truly non-reformist, and a criterion that can only be applied after the outcome is known has limited value as a guide before it. Wright conceded the difficulty in substance, and the concession, quoted in spirit throughout this article, is that the project supplies no reliable advance test distinguishing the reform that will shift power from the reform that will be absorbed. What it supplies is the question, asked with unusual rigour, and a taxonomy for discussing the answers.

A comparison the project itself invites clarifies the symbiotic wager's scale of ambition. The historical settlement of the postwar decades in the social democracies can be read, through the compass, as a symbiotic achievement that stopped at the criterion's boundary: welfare and labour rights expanded enormously, while the jurisdiction of association inside the firm and over investment remained narrow, and when the settlement's economic weather changed, the parts of it that rested on goodwill were renegotiated downward. The fuller comparison, with its record and its reversals, belongs to the companion treatment of Marxism and social democracy, which owns that history; the point needed here is narrower. The project reads that settlement not as a refutation of symbiosis but as symbiosis conducted without the second evaluation, generous in benefits and negligent in beachheads, and therefore reversible in the specific way the criterion predicts. Readers will judge whether the reading is insight or hindsight when the verdict section assembles the evidence.

## Eroding capitalism: the combined posture

Eroding capitalism is the name the synthesis gives to the combined strategy, and the final formulation retains it as the recommended posture. The phrase should be read with geological literalness. Erosion is the wearing away of a formation by countless small actions of water and wind, each negligible, the sum transformative, and crucially, erosion works on the formation's exposed surfaces and weak strata rather than assaulting its mass. The interstitial institutions are the water in the metaphor: they work at the margins, in the gaps, along the cracks where the existing order's provision fails or its attention lapses. The symbiotic reforms are the weather: they change the climate in which the formation stands, enlarging the cracks and directing the flow. Neither, alone, is expected to level the mountain. Together, sustained, they are proposed as the realistic mechanism by which a configuration dominated by economic power becomes one subordinated to social power.

Three features of the erosion image carry argumentative weight. First, it has no terminus that can be dated. A configuration shifts by degrees, and the compass, being a device of degrees, registers progress without requiring a threshold ceremony at which capitalism ends and socialism begins. Second, it is reversible, and the project does not hide this: formations also accrete, margins are also colonised from the centre, and erosion's gains require defence by the same capacities that won them. Third, it redistributes agency. No single actor erodes; the strategy is a property of an ecology of actors, associations, reformers, and builders whose actions compound without central direction. The third feature is either the strategy's realism or its evasion, depending on the reader's theory of change: realism, because central direction is what the compass distrusts; evasion, because an ecology without a strategist cannot be held to the standards by which a strategy is judged to have succeeded or failed. The critics press the evasion reading, and the verdict section will have to answer for the project's coherence on exactly this point.

### Is eroding capitalism a prediction that capitalism will disappear?

It is not a prediction. Erosion names a strategy and a direction of change, not a law of development. The project explicitly denies that history guarantees the outcome; the combined strategies are proposed as the plausible route by which actors could shift the configuration, and the same analysis acknowledges that the movement can stall, reverse, or be absorbed at any stage.

The erosion posture also reframes what counts as a strategic success, and the reframing has practical consequences for the movements the project addresses. Under a ruptural doctrine, a cooperative is a distraction or a rehearsal; under erosion, it is the thing itself, a unit of the alternative configuration in operation. Under a purely electoral doctrine, a governance reform seating associations inside a public body is a technical adjustment; under erosion, it is a transfer of jurisdiction, to be weighed as seriously as a transfer of property. The reframing dignifies small institutional work with strategic significance, which is energising, and it risks inflating small institutional work beyond its weight, which is the marginality criticism returning in a friendlier costume. The project's own discipline against inflation is the achievability and scaling analysis built into the volumes: each institution must state its route from pocket to sector, and the statement can be doubted in public. Doubt, in this literature, is a form of respect.

## A brief history of the word erosion in political argument

Metaphors in strategy doctrines are never decoration, and the governing metaphor of this one repays a short history. Political argument has usually reached for architecture or war when it imagines change: foundations laid, bastions stormed, positions taken and held. The geological register entered through a different door, through reflection on how institutions actually alter when nobody is storming anything. Rivers do not besiege their banks. They arrive every day, at ordinary pressure, and the bank's surrender is recorded in the altered course rather than in any single flood. Reformers who watched their statutes accumulate across decades, each one minor, the sum unrecognisable, found in the river a truer picture of their experience than the siege had ever offered. Wright's adoption of the image was therefore a considered rejection of the tradition's dominant metaphorics, and the rejection carries an argument inside it. Siege metaphors flatter the besiegers: they promise a day of decision, a role for heroes, an enemy whose surrender can be witnessed. River metaphors flatter nobody. They assign the work to persistence, distribute it across anonymous contributors, and refuse to schedule the celebration. A movement choosing its metaphor is choosing its emotional economy, what it will celebrate, whom it will honour, how it will survive the long intervals between visible gains. The programme's preference for the river is of a piece with everything else in it: the suspicion of founding moments, the trust in accumulation, the insistence that the register of victories be kept in jurisdictions and capacities rather than in anniversaries. Readers who find the metaphor deflating have understood it correctly, and the deflation is the doctrine's honesty about the tempo of the change it proposes, offered in place of the exhilaration its rivals sell and cannot deliver.

## The final formulation: five strategies toward capitalism

In 2019, in the short book published in the last year of his life, Wright restated the strategy discussion in a form addressed to a general reader. The restatement names five strategies toward capitalism, and the mapping onto the earlier three is close enough that the final text should be read as a translation of the synthesis into a vernacular, with two additions that sharpen it. The five are smashing, dismantling, taming, resisting and escaping.

Smashing is the ruptural strategy under its vernacular name: the conviction that capitalism must be broken by force of a decisive confrontation before anything new can be built. Dismantling is the strategy of gradual institutional replacement conducted from above, through state action that progressively substitutes new rules and structures for capitalist ones: it is the reform path imagined as a directed sequence, a staircase of legislation descending from the existing order to a different one. Taming is the strategy of neutralising capitalism's harms while leaving its structure intact: regulation, welfare, and redistribution that repair the damage the system does, accepting the system as the permanent frame. Resisting is the strategy of protest and defence from outside the state: movements that block, expose, and refuse, without a programme for governing. Escaping is the interstitial strategy in vernacular dress: withdrawal into alternative institutions and lifeways built in the system's spaces, from cooperatives and communes to commons based production.

The analytical gain of the fivefold scheme is that it separates two postures the threefold scheme had folded into symbiosis. Taming and dismantling both work through the state, but they differ in intention and in criterion: taming asks whether the harm is repaired, dismantling asks whether the configuration is being replaced piece by piece. The Gorzian criterion, in the fivefold vocabulary, is what converts taming into dismantling: a harm repairing reform that also shifts power belongs to the staircase; one that does not remains a repair, however valuable. Similarly, resisting is separated from escaping, because protest and construction, though often practised by the same people on the same weekend, are different relations to the system, one confrontational and one secessionist, with different failure modes.

Wright's recommendation, stated plainly in the final text, is that the plausible posture is erosion, achieved by combining taming and escaping, with resisting as an auxiliary that defends the space the other two require. Smashing is set aside on the plausibility grounds already examined. Dismantling, as a self sufficient strategy, is doubted for a subtler reason: a staircase needs a builder with a plan and a majority patient across decades, and the state's own personnel and constraints make the directed sequence unlikely to survive its electoral interruptions; dismantling's reforms are therefore absorbed into the erosion posture, where they proceed as taming with the criterion applied, rather than as a master sequence. The final formulation thus narrows the synthesis's symbiotic strategy by splitting it and demoting its directed half, a genuine revision inside the restatement, and readers comparing the two texts should register it as such.

### Does the five strategy formulation abandon the earlier three strategy scheme?

It restates it with one real revision. Smashing corresponds to rupture and escaping to the interstitial strategy, while the symbiotic strategy is split into taming and dismantling, and dismantling is demoted from a self sufficient route to a component of erosion. Resisting is added as an auxiliary posture. The recommended combination, taming joined with escaping, is the synthesis's erosion under new names.

The final text's tone differs from the synthesis in a way scholars have noted and this article should record. The synthesis argues; the final book invites. Written for readers who might never open a sociological monograph, it presents the strategies as postures a person can adopt, in a workplace, a neighbourhood, a union, a life. The democratisation of address is itself an application of the project's theory: a strategy of erosion, which belongs to an ecology of actors, needs its actors to recognise themselves in the description. Whether the vernacular costs analytical precision is a fair question; the fivefold scheme's distinctions are cruder than the threefold scheme's mechanisms, and the article's own practice in what follows is to use the threefold scheme for analysis and the fivefold for orientation, as the two texts, read together, seem to intend.

One further element of the final formulation deserves emphasis because summaries omit it. Wright insists there that the strategies are not identities. The same movement, the same person, will smash rhetorically on Monday, tame legislatively on Tuesday, and escape into a cooperative on Saturday, and the analysis is not embarrassed by the mixture. What the scheme disciplines is not conduct but expectation: it asks each posture to state what it can and cannot deliver, so that taming is not mistaken for transformation, escaping is not mistaken for a general solution, and resisting is not mistaken for a government. The insistence returns the reader to the project's founding discipline, the refusal to let a word do the work of a mechanism.

## First criticism: absorption or marginality, with no account of which

The criticisms begin with the one the brief ranks first in the project's own reckoning. Interstitial institutions, the objection runs, meet one of two fates. They are absorbed: drawn into the market's logic, professionalised, scaled on capital's terms, until their democratic constitutions are vestigial. Or they remain marginal: pure, small, and weightless, a lifestyle enclave for the convinced, exercising no constraint on the economic power that surrounds them. The objection's sting is its second clause. The project, the critics argue, possesses no account of which fate a given institution will meet, and no mechanism that reliably produces escape from the dilemma. A strategy whose central process has two known outcomes, both failures, and no specified discriminator between failure and the hoped for third outcome, is less a strategy than a hope with a bibliography.

The objection can be sharpened with the selection logic the project itself teaches. Institutions do not grow in a vacuum; they grow in an environment that rewards some traits and starves others. A cooperative competing for contracts against firms that can cut labour costs without a membership vote faces a gradient pushing it toward the standard form: hire non-member labour, widen pay differentials to retain managers, demutualise to raise capital. Each step is locally rational. The sequence is the absorption the critics describe, and it requires no bad faith from anyone. Marginality is the same gradient read from the other side: the institution that refuses each step keeps its form and forfeits its growth. Between the gradient's two resolutions, the critics ask, where is the project's mechanism? Pointing to the symbiotic strategy, law that softens the gradient, is an answer of a kind, but it transfers the difficulty rather than dissolving it, since the law must be won and held by the very social power whose weakness made the gradient steep.

Wright's response, assembled from the synthesis and the final text, has three layers. The first concedes the dilemma's descriptive truth for the interstitial strategy pursued in isolation, and denies that the project ever proposed it in isolation. The second points to the gradient's variability: selection environments differ across sectors and legal regimes, and in some, member governed firms have persisted at scale across generations, which shows the gradient is a pressure and not a law. The third returns to the pairing: erosion is the name for the condition in which interstitial growth and symbiotic gradient softening proceed together, each making the other's next step cheaper. The response is the strongest the project can make, and its limit is visible: it converts the critics' demand for a mechanism into a claim about a conjunction, and conjunctions are harder to falsify and easier to invoke after the fact. The verdict section prices this exchange honestly.

## Second criticism: a taxonomy in place of a theory

The second criticism is methodological, and it strikes at the compass itself. The compass classifies. It locates institutions and configurations in a space of three powers and grades their direction of movement. Classification, however refined, is not theory: a theory explains why configurations arise, persist, and change, and it risks predictions that events can disappoint. The compass, the critics argue, risks nothing. Any institution can be located after inspection; any outcome can be redescribed in the vocabulary; no observation is specified that would embarrass the scheme. A device that accommodates everything explains nothing, and the project's seven pathways inherit the defect, being seven boxes elaborately labelled rather than seven hypotheses about the world.

The criticism gains force from the contrast with Wright's own earlier standards. The class analysis for which he first became known made claims that survey data could and did disappoint; its author revised frameworks when the evidence turned. The real utopias project, the critics continue, relaxes that discipline at exactly the point where the stakes rise: claims about whole societies replace claims about occupational categories, and the testing apparatus vanishes. What remains is a normative vocabulary of great clarity, presented in the grammar of social science. Clarity is a virtue, but it is the virtue of a good map legend, and the legend is not the territory's geology.

### Is the compass a theory or only a classification?

On the project's own presentation, it is a classification with a normative argument attached: it grades configurations by the subordination of powers and asserts that the grading tracks what socialism should mean. The critics' charge that it classifies without predicting is therefore partly conceded by the design; the dispute is whether grading is a legitimate prior task for a tradition that had lost the ability to say which direction its proposals moved in.

The defence, stated fairly, begins by accepting the description and disputing the verdict. Wright presents the compass as an orienting device, and orientation is a real intellectual function: the Marxist tradition, on his diagnosis, had suffered a specific failure of orientation, equating socialism with a property form and thereby grading statist configurations as advances and associational ones as deviations. A device that corrects the grading corrects the tradition's practical judgements even if it predicts nothing. The pathways, further, are not empty boxes: each specifies a mechanism, and mechanisms can be examined case by case, their presence or absence checked in the historical record. The defence concludes that the project is a research programme's scaffolding rather than a finished theory, and that demanding finished theory standards of scaffolding mistakes the building site for the building.

The reply has force, and its limit should be marked. Scaffolding is judged by the building that rises in it. Three decades after the project's founding, the critics are entitled to ask how much explanatory structure the scaffolding has carried: whether the pathways have generated findings that surprised their author, whether the compass has ever returned a grading its user resisted, whether the programme's case literature has accumulated into the mechanisms a theory of transformation would need. Those questions are empirical questions about a literature, and a fair verdict will treat the taxonomy criticism as partly answered in principle and partly standing in fact, its standing portion shrinking or growing with the programme's subsequent yield.

## Third criticism: social power under-specified

The third criticism targets the concept on which everything rests. Social power, the critics argue, is under-specified: the definition, power rooted in voluntary association's capacity to mobilise, does not distinguish association as such from any other form of collective action, and so cannot explain why some associations constrain economic power while others decorate it, some democratise their members while others drill them, some pursue emancipation while others pursue exclusion with equal organisational vigour. A reactionary movement is an association. A cartel is an association. A mob, briefly, is a mobilisation. If the compass grades configurations by the ascendancy of a power that takes all these forms, the grading inherits their ambiguity, and the normative work is smuggled back in through the choice of which associations the analyst admires.

Wright's writings contain the materials of a reply, and assembling them shows both its strength and its seams. The first material is the connection requirement: social power counts toward the socialist direction when it is wired into decision rights over state and economic resources, not when it merely exists. The bowling league fails the connection test; the tenants' association with binding seats passes it. The second material is an internal democracy condition that the project applies unevenly: associations whose own governance is oligarchic transmit domination rather than checking it, and the compass's spirit requires discounting their weight. The third material is a distinction between power over and power to: association that mobilises members to dominate outsiders exercises the first, while the project's normative claim attaches to the second, the collective capacity of people to govern the conditions of their own lives. Assembled, the reply gives the concept more specification than the critics allow. The seams show in the assembly itself: the conditions are gathered from across the corpus rather than stated in the definition, each condition invites its own measurement quarrels, and a concept needing this much repair work in the secondary literature has, at minimum, been left looser by its author than his own standards elsewhere would permit.

### Does any voluntary association count as social power on the compass?

Association as such registers as social power in the thin sense of organised mobilisation capacity, but the project's grading of direction depends on further conditions the critics find underdeveloped: connection to decision rights over state or economic resources, internal democratic governance, and orientation toward members governing their own conditions rather than dominating outsiders. The under-specification charge fastens on exactly those further conditions.

There is a deeper version of the criticism that should not be lost inside the definitional one. Even fully specified, social power may be the wrong primitive for a theory of transformation, because the capacities of association are themselves distributed by the configuration they are meant to change. Wealthy, educated, leisured populations associate densely; exhausted and precarious populations associate thinly. If the engine of transformation is stocked unequally by the order it opposes, the strategy built on it may systematically empower the already empowered, a distributional objection the project acknowledges in its treatment of time and care, the gendered measures of its founding volumes being one attempt to answer it, without ever quite promoting the objection to the centre of the theory. The critics who press this version are often the project's sympathisers, and their pressure marks the spot where a successor literature would begin.

## Fourth criticism: the missing adversary

The fourth criticism observes an absence at the centre of the project's strategic writing. Erosion proceeds, in the project's telling, as if the configuration's other powers were terrain rather than opponents. Economic power, however, is held by actors who read the same compass and can act on what they read. The critics ask: where, in the theory, is the response of the powerful to encroachment? An interstitial sector that begins to bite will meet legal challenge, capital withdrawal, competitive predation, and political mobilisation against its enabling laws. A symbiotic reform that installs real beachheads will meet the campaign to revoke it, funded at a scale association rarely matches. A strategy of gradual encroachment against an adversary who can observe the encroachment and reply needs an account of the reply, when it arrives, what tools it uses, and what answers it. The project's literature, the critics charge, describes the advance in detail and the reply in passing, and the asymmetry is not an oversight but a structural feature of a theory whose actors are institutions rather than antagonists.

The charge connects to an older reproach against reform strategies generally, that they model the state as an instrument and capital as a condition, when both are players. It also connects to the project's own historical materials. The social democratic pathway's reversals, which the project reads as symbiosis without beachheads, can equally be read as the adversary's reply arriving on schedule: when the settlement's costs to economic power grew salient and its benefits to that power faded, the political coalition sustaining it was dismantled by organised counter mobilisation. A theory that explains the reversal by the absence of its favoured criterion, and the advance by the presence of its favoured strategy, risks explaining everything by its own vocabulary, which is the taxonomy criticism returning at the level of history.

### How does the project answer the charge that it ignores counter mobilisation?

Imperfectly, on the critics' reading. The materials of an answer exist: beachheads are valuable precisely because they raise the cost of revocation, federated sectors can defend themselves politically, and erosion's dispersed agency makes the advance hard to target. The critics reply that these are gestures toward an adversary model rather than the model itself, and that the project's strategy sections rarely simulate the opponent's best response.

Wright's defenders can point to the honesty of the final text, which lists the obstacles to erosion without anaesthetic, and to the resisting strategy's role in the recommended combination, since resistance is precisely the posture of meeting the adversary's initiatives. The combination's logic can be restated in adversarial terms: escaping builds what must be defended, taming under the criterion installs defences in law, resisting mans them. Restated so, the project has the shape of an answer. What it lacks, the critics conclude, is the analysis: no typology of counter moves, no account of the conditions under which revocation succeeds, no treatment of capital's exit threat as the standing veto it functions as in every symbiotic bargain. The verdict section will count this criticism as substantially standing, mitigated by the combination's implicit adversarial structure, and it will attach the defeater accordingly.

## The strongest objection: social democracy renamed

Every project attracts one objection that condenses the others, and for the real utopias project the condensing objection is the classical one. It must be stated at full strength, attributed as the brief requires, and answered without evasion. The charge, in the form given it by critics in the tradition of Rosa Luxemburg's polemic against Eduard Bernstein, and renewed against Wright by contemporary critics writing in that lineage, runs as follows. A strategy that builds alternative institutions in the spaces capitalism leaves, and that uses existing institutions in ways which also serve the powerful, is a description of social democratic practice as it has existed for a century. Renaming its reforms non-reformist does not change what they are. The test of whether a reform shifts the balance of power can be applied only retrospectively, and applied retrospectively to the social democratic record it has generally come out negative: the great settlements were renegotiated downward when the weather changed, the beachheads proved to be sandbars, and the movement that administered the configuration ended by policing it. On this reading, the real utopias project is the newest and most sophisticated vocabulary in which an old practice redescribes itself to escape its own history.

Luxemburg's original argument deserves its own sentence, because the contemporary charge inherits its logic. Writing against Bernstein's revisionism, she argued that reforms pursued as an end in themselves, detached from a transformative goal that disciplines their selection, become the mechanism by which the movement adapts to the order it was founded to replace: the goal dissolves into the movement, and the movement into administration. The Gorzian criterion is, in effect, an attempt to rebuild inside reform practice the discipline Luxemburg demanded, a selection rule that keeps the goal operative in every bargain. The critics' counter is that a rule statable in a sentence and applicable only in hindsight is not a discipline but an incantation, and that Bernstein's heirs have never lacked for sentences.

### What is the project's best answer to the charge of renaming social democracy?

The answer identifies what the compass adds: a specification of which power each reform constrains and which it enlarges, so that reforms identical in welfare terms can be distinguished by their jurisdiction effects, and social democratic practice can be graded internally rather than praised or condemned as a bloc. The project concedes, as it must, that the specification supplies no advance criterion for telling the power shifting reform from the absorbable one.

The answer deserves weighing rather than mere statement. What the compass adds is real. Two reforms delivering identical incomes to identical households can differ entirely in their power effects: one paid as a discretionary benefit administered by officials who may interrogate and withdraw it, the other flowing from an entitlement governed by bodies on which recipients' associations hold seats. Social democratic historiography, grading by welfare delivered, scores them equal. The compass scores them differently, and the difference predicts, at least roughly, their different fates under hostile administrations: the governed entitlement builds its own defenders, the administered benefit builds a clientele. A vocabulary that sees this distinction sees something true that the older vocabulary missed, and a tradition that adopts the vocabulary will select its reforms differently at the margin. The critics' strongest point survives the answer, and it is the retrospective character of the decisive test. Between the reform that will entrench association and the reform that will be digested, the project offers the compass's direction and the criterion's question, but no instrument that reads the future. Wright's concession on this point, made across the late writings with characteristic plainness, is total: there is no such instrument, and anyone claiming one is selling something. The objection therefore ends in a draw with an edge: the project is not social democracy renamed, because its grading apparatus is genuinely additional; it is social democracy re-instrumented, whose instruments measure the past better than they guide the future, and whose author knew it.

## The comparison with social democracy, on the stated basis

The brief requires the comparison to be conducted on an explicit basis, and the basis is this: the comparison concerns whether the reforms proposed shift the balance of power or operate within it, which is the distinction the non-reformist reform concept exists to draw. Other bases are available, and choosing among them is itself an argumentative act. Compared by welfare delivered per decade, the historical social democracies outscore every rival tradition, and the project does not contest the record. Compared by property transferred, both score low. Compared on the stated basis, the comparison becomes internal and discriminating: within the social democratic record itself, reforms divide into those that installed durable associational jurisdiction and those that delivered benefits through administration, and the project's claim is that the first kind aged better than the second under subsequent pressure.

That claim can be examined without leaving the structural level this article maintains. Consider two families of reform in the abstract. In the first family, a reform creates a fund, a board, a council, or an entitlement whose governance includes representatives of the associated, chosen by their associations, with votes that bind. In the second family, a reform of identical cost creates a programme administered by a ministry under rules the ministry interprets, with beneficiaries as clients. The first family's reform manufactures its own constituency of defence: an organised body whose jurisdiction is threatened by revocation and which can mobilise against it. The second family's reform manufactures a budget line, defensible only by the governing party's general popularity. When the political weather turns, and the project's whole strategic theory assumes it turns, the two families face the storm with different equipment. The historical record of the settlements, read at this level of abstraction, is at least consistent with the claim: the elements of the settlements that survived their founders' defeats tend to be those with constituencies organised around them, and the elements that evaporated tend to be those that lived as administrative discretion.

The full history, with its countries, parties, and dates, belongs to the article on [Marxism and social democracy](/2019/11/15/marxism-vs-social-democracy/), and the present article deliberately borrows none of it, because the comparison's logic does not need the narrative and the decay discipline forbids dressing structural claims in borrowed particulars. What the logic yields, stated as a proposition a reader can carry away, is that the real utopias project and social democracy are related as a grading system is related to the record it grades. The project is parasitic on that record for its evidence, indebted to it for its repertoire, and distinct from it in exactly one respect: it possesses a criterion the record's own architects lacked, for distinguishing within their achievement the parts built to last from the parts built to be renegotiated. Whether possessing the criterion in advance would have changed the record is the counterfactual on which the whole dispute silently turns, and it is unanswerable, which is why the dispute persists.

### Where can a student pursue the social democratic record itself?

The companion article on Marxism and social democracy owns the history: the parties, the settlements, the reversals, and the theoretical argument between the traditions. The present article borrows only the comparison's logic, that reforms be graded by whether they move power itself or merely administer its existing distribution, and leaves the narrative and its evidence to the article charged with it.

## Misreadings the project attracts, corrected in sequence

Five misreadings recur in the secondary discussion, and the brief directs that each be corrected explicitly. The first, already corrected at the article's opening, credits Wright with the phrase non-reformist reform; the phrase is Gorz's, from the mid-1960s, adopted and developed by the project. The second takes real utopias to mean idealised models, polished societies in miniature; the project examines existing and proposed institutions, damaged in print by their critics, and the adjective real is a methodological vow rather than a compliment. The third supposes the project rejects rupture on principle; it rejects rupture on an assessment of plausibility under specified conditions, and the assessment is revisable with the conditions. The fourth mistakes the compass for a strategy; the compass is a classification and grading device, and the strategies are the separate apparatus of the threefold and fivefold schemes. A reader who argues with the compass as if it promised arrival has mistaken the instrument panel for the engine.

The fifth misreading concerns the relation between the two strategy schemes. The final formulation of 2019 is frequently reported as abandoning the synthesis of 2010, the five strategies replacing the three in a late change of mind. The texts do not support the report. The fivefold scheme restates the threefold with one revision, the splitting of the symbiotic into taming and dismantling and the demotion of dismantling, and one addition, resisting as a supporting posture. The recommended combination is identical in substance: build in the margins, use the state's machinery under the power criterion, sustain both across time. A scholar's last book restating his system's strategy for a general audience, in the year of his death, is a valediction rather than a retraction, and the secondary literature's appetite for late reversals should be resisted where the texts show continuity under translation.

### Why does the misattribution of non-reformist reform matter beyond courtesy?

Because the term carries its original argument with it. Gorz coined the concept inside a dispute about how a movement should select reforms under a stable capitalism, judging each by whether it shifts the balance of power. A reader who credits the term to the later project meets it as a label for worthy institutions and loses the selection rule, which is the concept's working part.

Two further confusions, less canonical but common in classroom use, deserve a paragraph each. The first confuses the project's achievability test with a prediction, and then faults the project when the institutions it examines fail to spread on schedule. Achievability asserts the existence of a route composed of steps identifiable actors could take; it asserts nothing about whether the actors will take them, and the project's own strategy chapters are an extended explanation of why they might not. The second confusion treats the seven pathways as a menu from which a society chooses one. The pathways are mechanisms, not menus: a single reform can travel several pathways at once, and the synthesis's own examples habitually combine associational governance with social economy organisation and social democratic regulation in one arrangement. The taxonomy sorts mechanisms the way anatomy sorts tissues, and a body is not required to pick one tissue.

## The verdict, with its defeater stated

The Cluster 24 discipline, continued through this run, requires every verdict to carry the statement of what would reverse it. The verdict on the real utopias project, assembled from the sections above, is as follows. The project's permanent contribution is the compass reframing: by defining socialism through the ascendancy of associational power over both administration and ownership, it repaired the tradition's grading apparatus, and the repair changes practical judgement wherever it is adopted, because institutions are selected differently when the question is who decides rather than who owns. Its strategy doctrine, erosion by the combination of interstitial construction and symbiotic reform under the Gorzian criterion, is the most carefully specified gradualism the tradition has produced, and its specification is also the exposure of its weakness: the doctrine's central discriminator, between the reform that shifts power and the reform destined for digestion, operates retrospectively, and its central process, interstitial growth, lacks a mechanism guaranteeing escape from absorption or marginality. The project is therefore best read as an instrument panel of exceptional quality mounted on an engine whose power remains unproven: the gradings can be trusted more than the itinerary.

The defeater is stated in the brief and is adopted here verbatim in substance. The judgement that interstitial strategies tend to absorption or marginality would be defeated by a documented case in which an interstitial institution scaled to constrain economic power at national level while neither dissolving into market logic nor shrinking into a niche. No such case stands in the record the project itself assembles, which is why the judgement stands. A second defeater attaches to the taxonomy criticism: the judgement that the compass classifies without explaining would be defeated by the programme's case literature yielding a mechanism, stated in advance, that correctly discriminated in a fresh instance between an institution that entrenched social power and one that was digested. A third attaches to the verdict's positive half: the claim that the compass reframing is a permanent contribution would be defeated by a showing that grading by decision rights and grading by ownership never diverge in practice, so that the relocation changes no judgement any analyst actually makes. The divergences exhibited in this article, the nationalised industry directed without accountability against the cooperative governed by its members, stand as the evidence that the defeat condition is not met.

### What single finding would most strengthen the project's strategy doctrine?

A documented instance of the third outcome: an institution born in the interstices that reached national scale as a constraint on economic power while retaining democratic governance by its associated members. One such case, examined with the project's own severity, would convert erosion from a conjunction of hopes into a mechanism with an existence proof, and would defeat the strongest standing criticism at its root.

Verdicts in this series are graded, and the grading here is deliberately uneven across the project's parts. The definitional contribution is graded as established. The pathways are graded as a durable anatomy, useful wherever institutions are compared. The strategies are graded as plausible in combination and unproven in outcome, with the burden of proof resting where the project itself, in its honest passages, places it: on the future conduct of the experiment it describes. The criticisms are graded as follows: the retrospective criterion objection stands; the missing adversary objection stands in substance, mitigated by the recommended combination's implicit structure; the under-specification objection stands in part, the specification being recoverable from the corpus but absent from the definition; the taxonomy objection is answered in principle and open in fact. A reader who finishes the article with those gradings, and with the defeaters that would change them, has what the project at its best provides: not a faith, and not a dismissal, but a set of instruments and a clear statement of what evidence would settle the argument about them.

## The definitional relocation as a series pattern

The brief notes that this article instantiates a pattern used elsewhere in the series for criterion split articles, and the pattern is worth making explicit because it teaches readers how to recognise the project's move when they meet it in other vocabularies. A definitional relocation takes a concept whose received definition sorts the world one way, and redefines it around a different variable, so that the sorting changes. Ownership and decision are the variables here. Under the ownership definition, the socialist question about any institution is exhausted by its title deeds; under the decision definition, the title deeds are the beginning of the inquiry and the governance is the rest of it. The relocation is substantive rather than verbal because the two definitions disagree about real cases, and the disagreement can be listed: they disagree about the unaccountable nationalised industry, about the cooperative, about the participatory budget, about the administered benefit and the governed entitlement. Where two definitions never disagree, a relocation is a synonym wearing a costume. Where they disagree across a range of central cases, the relocation is a theoretical act, and it must be judged as theories are judged, by the fruitfulness of the new sorting: whether it reveals connections the old sorting hid and predicts fragilities the old sorting missed.

Judged so, the relocation earns its keep on the evidence assembled in this article. It reveals the connection among six institution types that share no property form and no policy sector, uniting them as levers on a single variable. It predicts a fragility: that benefits delivered without jurisdiction will prove perishable in a way benefits delivered with jurisdiction will not, a prediction the social democratic record can be used to test, as the comparison section showed. And it explains a historical irony the ownership definition cannot state: that the configurations which carried the socialist name furthest from association were built by movements speaking the ownership definition fluently. A definition that explains its rivals' characteristic failure has done theoretical work. The critics' residual objection is not that the relocation is empty but that it is incomplete: a sorting, however fruitful, is the beginning of a theory rather than its completion, and the project's completion, the mechanisms of movement, is where the verdict found the engine unproven. Both halves of that sentence should be carried away together.

### Why does the relocation from ownership to decision change which institutions count?

Because the two criteria sort central cases differently. An enterprise owned publicly but directed by an insulated administration counts fully under the ownership criterion and barely under the decision criterion, while a member governed cooperative counts for nothing under the first and substantially under the second. A relocation that reverses the grading of central cases is a substantive theoretical act, not a change of vocabulary.

## Real utopias as a research programme: the whole argument assembled

Step back from the parts and regard the enterprise entire. A sociologist trained in the most demanding empirical wing of his tradition grows dissatisfied with a division of labour in which critics describe the present order's workings while its replacement remains a gesture. He organises a decade of conferences at which single institutional proposals are stated precisely and then attacked by the ablest opponents available. He gathers the results into a synthesis organised by a grading device borrowed from no one, a threefold scheme of powers in which the socialist direction is picked out by the ascendancy of association. He derives from the device a catalogue of routes, confronts the question of travel along them, borrows a selection criterion from a French predecessor whose name he restores to circulation, and recommends a patient combination of building at the edges and bargaining at the centre. In a final short book he translates the recommendation into five vernacular postures for readers who will never open the synthesis. That is the enterprise, and its unity is easy to miss in summary because each component circulates separately: the budgeting literature cites the case volume, the income literature cites another, the strategy vocabulary travels without the grading device, the grading device travels without the criterion. Assembled, the components interlock with unusual tightness. The device tells the routes apart. The routes tell the traveller where building is possible. The criterion tells the builder which bargains to accept. The tests tell everyone which proposals deserve the journey at all.

Regarded as a programme rather than as a book, the enterprise also displays a sociology worth remarking. Its conferences recruited across the boundaries the tradition usually patrols: economists who rejected its politics examined its property schemes, political theorists who shared its values disputed its constitutional designs, practitioners testified about the behaviour of institutions the theorists had only modelled. The recruitment was not ecumenism for its own sake. A design for a decision procedure needs the knowledge of people who have watched decision procedures fail, and that knowledge lives in disciplines and occupations the tradition does not train. The programme's volumes are therefore best shelved beside the literatures they recruited, and their shelf life has been correspondingly longer than the tradition's polemics of the same decades. Programmes die when their questions stop organising other people's work. By that test this one remains alive in the fields it entered, whatever its fate among the faithful.

### What holds the programme's parts together as one enterprise?

A single grading question asked at every level. The device asks it of configurations, the pathways ask it of mechanisms, the strategies ask it of movements, and the criterion asks it of individual reforms: does this arrangement, route, or bargain enlarge the capacity of associated people to direct the decisions that govern them, relative to administration and to ownership? Parts that circulate separately in other literatures belong to one enterprise because each is an application of that question at a different scale.

There is a temptation, in assembling the whole, to smooth its tensions into a system more coherent than the texts support. Resist it with the texts' own help. The founder revised his strategy scheme between the synthesis and the final book, splitting one category and demoting another. He conceded the retrospective character of his central criterion in terms his critics quote with gratitude. He left the definition of his central concept looser than his own earlier standards, and the looseness has been repaired by other hands in ways he did not supervise. A programme whose founder behaves so is not a system but an inquiry, and its assembly in this article has tried to keep the inquiry's open edges visible, since a reader who inherits only the polished version will be ambushed by the first competent critic met outside these pages.

## The state reconsidered: the compass's second power

State power received less direct theorising in the project than its other two powers, and the gap is worth exploring because the symbiotic strategy depends on a theory of the state it never quite states. What can be assembled from the corpus runs as follows. The state is not an instrument lying ready for whichever hand seizes it, nor a committee of the owning class, nor an autonomous leviathan with a single will. It is a terrain of apparatuses, each with its own personnel, routines, legal mandates, and constituencies, unevenly permeable to the other two powers. Some apparatuses are built to hear association: those with elected boards, statutory consultation, participatory mandates. Others are built to hear ownership: treasuries attentive to bond markets, regulators staffed from the industries they oversee. Others hear mainly themselves. The symbiotic strategist's practical knowledge is a map of this permeability, apparatus by apparatus, and her craft consists in routing reforms through the apparatuses that can be made to carry them while building associational jurisdiction into the routing. The ruptural tradition's error, on this assembled account, was to treat the terrain as a single fortress with a single gate. The social democratic tradition's error was to treat the terrain as neutral ground that any majority could occupy on equal terms. The project's implicit theory treats it as a landscape: traversable, defensible in places, and never finally owned by anyone, which is why erosion, a landscape metaphor, fits its strategy as conquest never could.

The assembled theory has consequences for how reforms should be drafted that the volumes illustrate repeatedly. A reform lodged in an apparatus with an associational constituency acquires defenders inside the state itself, officials whose careers and convictions attach to the participatory mandate. A reform entrusted to a hostile apparatus will be administered into harmlessness without any minister needing to repeal it, through guidance, delay, and the costing monopoly. Drafting is therefore strategy at the level of the clause: the choice of administering body, the appointment mechanism, the reporting duty, the review date. Readers accustomed to treating such details as beneath political theory will find the project beneath them constantly, and deliberately. Its wager is that the distribution of power in a developed society is decided substantially at this level, in the accumulation of clauses, and that a tradition which disdains the level abandons the decision to its opponents.

### Why does the project treat the choice of administering body as a strategic decision?

Because apparatuses differ in whom they are built to hear. A reform administered by a body with an associational constituency gains defenders inside the state, while the same reform lodged in a hostile apparatus can be neutralised through guidance, delay, and control of information without ever being repealed. The clause level choices about appointments, reporting, and review therefore decide much of what a statute accomplishes.

## Grading institutions in practice: the compass at work

An instrument is understood by using it, and this section applies the compass to a sequence of structural cases, each described as a type, in order to show the grading in motion and to expose the judgements a user must supply. The cases are arranged from the configuration's centre outward, and each grading states its axis, because the compass grades along dimensions rather than delivering a single score.

Take first a large firm of the standard form, owned by dispersed shareholders, directed by a professional board, employing thousands. Economic power governs its investment, its closures, its internal regime. State power sets the perimeter: company law, labour law, taxation. Social power appears at the edges, in whatever union organisation the workforce sustains and in the reputational campaigns outsiders can mount. The compass locates the firm near the capitalist pole, and the interesting grading begins when the form is varied. Introduce codetermination of a structural type, worker directors with binding votes on the board, and social power acquires jurisdiction inside the firm without any change in share ownership. Introduce an employee buyout that converts the firm to a cooperative, and jurisdiction passes wholesale while market exposure remains. Introduce a collective fund accumulating the firm's shares on behalf of its workforce, and the social capitalism pathway opens a third route to a similar destination. Three reforms, three mechanisms, one direction: the compass's user sees them as a family, where an ownership analysis sees a board reform, a property transfer, and a pension device, related by nothing.

Take next a public service, a hospital system organised conventionally, state owned and directed by a ministry through appointed managers. The ownership definition grades it as socialised and closes the file. The compass opens the file. State power governs; the question is what social power exists inside the governance: whether patients' and workers' associations hold seats with votes, whether budgets are set in procedures the associated can contest, whether professional hierarchies answer to any body the served population elects. A reform that seats such associations with binding weight moves the service on the compass while leaving its public ownership, its funding, and its clinical practice untouched, and the project's literature is full of such reforms precisely because the compass makes them visible as socialist measures in the reframed sense. A reform that does the opposite, converting the service to a contracted market of private providers under state purchase, moves it toward economic power by the same reading, whatever flag the reform flies.

Take finally an income system. A benefit of the administered type, means tested, discretionary at the margins, revocable by rule changes interpreted by officials, grades low on social power even when generous, because its recipients stand before the state as clients and before employers as supplicants whose alternative is the benefit's conditions. An unconditional floor of the type examined earlier grades higher on the exit dimension, because it converts the recipient's relation to every employer: refusal becomes affordable. The compass's grading here is the one most often disputed, since the floor installs no association and governs no institution directly. The project's reply is that power is relational before it is organisational: a change in what each party can walk away from is a change in the balance inside every negotiation the parties conduct, and a compass that could not register it would be blind to the mechanism by which labour markets actually distribute deference. The reply is characteristic of the whole construction: follow the decision, follow the walk away option, and grade by what they show.

### Can an institution score well on the compass while remaining privately owned?

Yes, along specified dimensions. A privately owned firm converted to governance by its worker members scores on the jurisdiction dimension while its market exposure is unchanged, and a private provider bound by governance in which users' associations hold binding seats scores on the dimension of answerability. The compass grades dimensions of power, and ownership is one fact among the facts that determine them.

The cases teach the instrument's demand on its user. The compass does not grade itself. Each application requires the analyst to specify the dimension, the mechanism, and the evidence, and two analysts can grade the same institution differently by attending to different dimensions, one to its internal jurisdiction, the other to its market dependence. The project regards this not as a defect but as the honest form of institutional judgement: the dimensions are real, they can diverge, and a single score would conceal the divergence. Critics who want the instrument to be an algorithm will be disappointed; the project offers a discipline of attention, and defends it as the most that institutional analysis can honestly promise.

## The pathways revisited: combinations in the wild

The seven pathways were presented anatomically; this section considers them as they appear in combination, because the synthesis's own examples rarely travel a single pathway and the reader should be equipped to parse mixtures. The characteristic mixture of the historical settlements joined the social democratic pathway to fragments of the associational: regulation and welfare from above, with unions recognised as governing partners in labour market institutions. The mixture's strength was its double root, electoral and organisational. Its weakness, on the project's reading, was the narrowness of the associational fragment: association governed the labour market's perimeter while the firm's interior and the investment decision remained economic power's unshared jurisdiction, and the erosion the project recommends can be described as the attempt to widen exactly that fragment.

A second characteristic mixture joins the social economy pathway to the cooperative market pathway at different scales: a sector of member governed enterprises, federated through their own finance and training institutions, gradually normalising the cooperative form until conversion becomes a standard option at firm succession and formation. The mixture's analytic interest is its treatment of scale. The single cooperative faces the absorption gradient alone; the federated sector manufactures its own environment, its own bank, its own wholesalers, its own legal expertise, and the gradient flattens because the institution's transactions occur increasingly inside the sector. Federation is thus the interstitial strategy's answer to the selection objection, and its historical partial successes and failures are the evidence over which the absorption debate is really fought. Where federation was built, alternative institutions have persisted at scale with constitutions intact across long periods; where it was not, the dilemma's two fates divide the record between them. The observation does not defeat the critics, since federated persistence has constrained economic power regionally and sectorally rather than at the national level the defeater specifies, but it refines the judgement the verdict recorded: the tendency to absorption or marginality is a tendency of unfederated interstitial growth, and the project's mechanism for escaping it, federation powered by symbiotic law, is specified enough to be argued with case by case.

### What does federation add that a single alternative institution lacks?

An environment. A lone cooperative buys, borrows, and sells inside institutions built for the standard firm, and the selection gradient presses it toward that form. A federated sector supplies its own its own credit, instruction, supply lines, and legal templates, so that growth occurs increasingly among institutions sharing its constitution, and the pressure to converge weakens as the sector's internal market deepens.

A third mixture is the one the project most hopes to encourage and least often names: the associational pathway joined to participatory budgeting of the structural type inside public governance, so that the state's own expenditure acquires islands of direct social jurisdiction which then serve as the training ground and the proof of concept for wider associational claims. The mixture matters to the strategy doctrine because it manufactures the strategist's scarcest resource, experienced association: citizens who have governed a budget line have acquired the skills and the confidence to govern larger ones, and the capacity installed is human before it is legal. The Gorzian criterion, applied to such a reform, returns its clearest positive verdict in the whole repertoire, which is why the type occupies so prominent a place in the project's affections despite the modesty of the sums it typically governs.

## Equality as access: the value that prices the designs

The project's equality principle, equal access to the material and social conditions a flourishing life requires, sounds gentler than the tradition's distributive demands and is in some respects more radical, because it prices institutions by a variable treasuries do not collect. Access to the conditions of flourishing includes the familiar material goods, income, housing, health, education, and then continues into goods the distributive tradition handled awkwardly: time free from necessary labour, standing in the institutions one inhabits, the realistic capability to refuse a degrading bargain, membership in associations that can act. Two citizens with identical incomes can stand at opposite ends of the access scale, one secure in a governed entitlement and a unionised workplace with hours that leave evenings intact, the other precarious, unrepresented, and too exhausted to use the citizenship both formally possess. A distributive metric scores them equal. The project's metric scores them as the inequality its programme exists to close, and the scoring explains the programme's characteristic preferences among reforms that a treasury would rank identically.

Access also disciplines the programme's attitude toward its own favourite institutions. A cooperative sector accessible only to workers with savings to stake, or skills the market already rewards, fails the equality value while passing the compass's directional test, and the volumes' design discussions repeatedly patch such failures with entry mechanisms: conversion funds that buy firms for their workforces, training endowments, membership rules that open as employment lengthens. The patching is the values negotiating among themselves in public, equality correcting democracy's tendency to empower the already organised. Seminars sometimes treat the project's three values as a harmonious triad recited for ceremony. The texts treat them as three creditors with competing claims on every design, and the quality of a design is partly the quality of the settlement it strikes among them.

### Why does the project measure equality by access rather than by outcomes alone?

Because identical outcomes can conceal opposite standings. Two households with the same income may differ utterly in security, in time left over from labour and care, in representation where they work, and in their capacity to refuse bad bargains. Access names the whole set of conditions from which a flourishing life can be built, and grading institutions by access catches inequalities of power and time that an income comparison certifies as equality.

## The commentators inside the gates: criticism as architecture

The founding volumes' architecture, proposal and commentary and reply, merits separate treatment because it shaped what the project could learn. A conventional edited collection gathers the like minded. These volumes were edited adversarially: commentators were recruited for the likelihood that they would damage the proposal, and several did damage that the published reply could not repair. The share based ownership scheme emerged from its volume with its coupon mechanics admired and its silence about control exposed, an exposure later treatments of the scheme have never quite answered. The associational democracy volume's certification quarrel, rehearsed earlier in this article, was not resolved by its proposer; it was mapped, which the proposer counted as the volume's achievement. The income volume's critics forced the distinction, now standard in that literature, between an income floor as an exit device and an income floor as a distributive transfer, and the project's own grading of the type follows the forced distinction. A reader who wants to understand why the synthesis concedes so much should read the volumes first: the concessions were not temperament, they were scars, each marking a place where a recruited opponent had landed a blow in print.

The architecture also explains a feature of the project's reception that puzzles newcomers, its authority among readers who reject its conclusions. Economists who find the strategy doctrine fanciful cite the volumes' design chapters as the serious treatment of their subjects. Political theorists who grade the compass as taxonomy cite the commentaries as models of how institutional argument should be conducted. Authority of this kind, earned by publishing one's own refutations, is rare in any tradition and nearly unknown in polemical ones. It is also fragile in a specific way: it depends on the founder's editorial will, and a successor literature that recruits only friends will spend the authority quickly. The programme's continuation, discussed earlier, will be measurable by this standard before any other: whether its conferences still seat the opponents most likely to wound it.

### What did the adversarial architecture of the volumes contribute that ordinary collections do not?

It converted criticism from an external event into a stage of production. Proposals were damaged before they hardened into doctrine, the damage was published beside the design, and the synthesis inherited a map of its own weak points drawn by the ablest available opponents. The project's famous concessions are the visible residue: each corresponds to a blow landed in its own volumes, which is why hostile readers trust the literature more than its conclusions.

## Why design was taboo: the tradition's reasons, examined

The project's founding gesture, specifying alternatives, broke a taboo with real reasons behind it, and the reasons deserve a fair hearing before the article's business closes. The tradition's suspicion of blueprints rested on three arguments. The epistemic argument held that a future society's institutions would be created by people formed in circumstances no theorist could foresee, so that detailed designs were fictions about strangers. The strategic argument held that specifying the destination divided movements in advance, since every clause of a blueprint manufactures a faction, while negation united everyone the present order injured. The moral argument, the deepest, held that designing institutions for others was itself a form of the domination the tradition opposed: the theorist as legislator, prescribing to future citizens the arrangements of their lives. Marx's own refusal to write recipes for the cook shops of the future gave all three arguments his authority.

Wright's project answers the three arguments in ascending order of difficulty. To the strategic argument it answers with the record: movements united by negation discovered their divisions at the moment of victory, when the unspecified destination had to be improvised by whoever held the apparatus, and the improvisation's results fill the statist century. Division postponed is not division avoided; it is division surrendered to the least accountable forum. To the epistemic argument it answers with the tests: designs are not prophecies about strangers but examinations of mechanisms, offered to contemporaries who can inspect the institutions proposed, many of which already operate among us in partial forms. The strangers objection applies to whole societies drawn on blank paper, not to a decision procedure whose workings can be watched in a functioning assembly. To the moral argument it answers with the authorship standard: a design proposed in public, criticised in public, and adopted only through the decisions of those who will live under it does not legislate for anyone; it equips citizens to legislate for themselves, and the refusal to design leaves the equipping to the tradition's opponents, who have never shared the scruple. The answers do not dissolve the taboo's wisdom entirely. A designed institution can still harden into a fetish, defended after its conditions have changed because its founders' prestige guards it. The project's own fallibilism, sunset clauses and scheduled reconsideration built into designs, is the taboo's surviving insight, absorbed into the practice that overcame it.

## The housing question as a lens: one domain traced through the whole apparatus

Abstract doctrines reveal their working parts when a single domain is traced through them from end to end, and housing serves well for the exercise because every power in the compass contests it and every institution type in the catalogue has a housing form. Begin with the configuration most readers inhabit. Land and dwellings are allocated predominantly by market exchange; owners of rental property exercise the landlord's everyday jurisdiction over repairs, renewals, and the terms of occupancy; the state zones, codes, and subsidises at the perimeter; tenants associate weakly, their organisations episodic and usually formed in emergency. Grade the domain and the compass's reading is immediate: a household's most consequential environment is governed by economic power in its most intimate form, the power to withdraw shelter, tempered by perimeter rules whose enforcement tenants must individually invoke.

Now walk the repertoire through the domain. The regulatory pathway appears as tenure law and habitability codes, state power constraining the landlord's jurisdiction at its edges while leaving the allocation mechanism untouched. The associational pathway appears where residents of publicly owned housing hold elected boards with authority over budgets and management contracts, a transfer of jurisdiction inside continued public ownership, and therefore a case the ownership test cannot see and the compass grades at once. The social economy pathway appears in the housing cooperative and the community land arrangement, the latter separating land from dwelling so that the plot is held by a member governed trust while the home upon it is occupied under long, inheritable, resale limited tenure: a property form engineered, clause by clause, to withdraw land from speculation while keeping households secure. The collective fund pathway appears where pension capital is directed into housing under beneficiary governance, with the standing question of whether the beneficiaries' interest as future pensioners and their interest as present tenants can be made to coincide inside one board. The income pathway appears as the floor that makes a rent strike survivable and a bad landlord refusable. Each form has its characteristic pathology from the catalogue: the board captured by its most available members, the trust's resale formula contested by heirs who see forgone windfalls, the cooperative's waiting list becoming a new exclusion. And each has its designed counter, stated in the volumes' spirit if not always in their pages.

The exercise's point is not housing policy, on which this article takes no position. The point is diagnostic: a reader who can trace one domain through device, pathways, strategies, criterion, and failure catalogue without losing the thread possesses the programme as a usable instrument rather than as a set of opinions about Erik Olin Wright. Teachers report that students who perform the exercise on a domain they know from life, a university's governance, a hospital's, a platform's, stop asking what the theory says and start asking what it sees. That transition, from recitation to perception, is the programme's pedagogical signature, and it is available on any domain where decisions govern people who did not make them, which is to say on nearly every domain there is.

### Why trace a single domain through the entire apparatus?

Because the apparatus is a set of connected instruments, and connection is what summaries lose. Following housing, or any familiar domain, through grading, pathways, strategy, criterion, and failure modes shows each instrument doing its distinct work on the same material. A reader who completes the trace can operate the programme independently, which is the difference between knowing a doctrine and possessing a method.

## What a sceptical reader should demand next: a short catechism

Articles in this series try to leave their readers armed, and the arming for this topic takes the form of questions to carry into any future encounter with the programme's claims, whether the encounter is a book, a lecture, a campaign document, or a ministerial announcement dressed in borrowed vocabulary. When a reform is praised as transformative, ask which jurisdiction it transfers and to whom, and accept no answer that names only beneficiaries. When an institution is celebrated as participatory, ask what its decisions bind, what overriding them would cost, and who pays for the technical advice it receives. When a cooperative or fund is exhibited as proof of concept, ask about its federation, its reserves, its succession rules, and its fifth year attendance, since the founding portrait and the fifth year rarely match. When the strategy vocabulary is invoked, ask which scheme is being quoted, threefold or fivefold, and whether the speaker knows the difference. When the criterion is applied to a bargain, ask what the speaker would count as the bargain's failure, and notice whether the answer could ever be given before the outcome is known. When the compass is dismissed as mere taxonomy, ask the dismisser to grade the administered configuration and the market led one with whatever instrument they prefer, and to show the working. None of these questions requires agreement with the programme. All of them were learned from it, which is perhaps the most durable definition of a research programme's success: that even its opponents conduct their arguments with its instruments in their hands, asking its questions in rooms it will never enter, long after its slogans have been forgotten by everyone except historians.

## Three configurations compared as types

Abstractions about a space of powers become clearer when points inside the space are described, and this section describes three configurations as types, without names or dates, so the compass's coordinates can be felt. The first type is the familiar market led configuration. Investment decisions sit with asset owners and their managers; the state maintains property, contract, and a welfare floor whose generosity varies with electoral weather; association is vigorous in civil society and weak inside the firm, where the employment relation is governed by contract between formally equal parties of vastly unequal staying power. Most readers live in some variant of this type, and the compass's description of it is deliberately unhysterical: it is a configuration, stable under many conditions, delivering real goods, whose democratic deficit is located precisely, in the unauthored character of the decisions that structure working life.

The second type is the administered configuration. The state owns the commanding assets and directs investment through a plan; association exists abundantly on paper, as unions, youth bodies, and professional societies, but each body is a transmission line for decisions taken in the apparatus, so that the density of organisation conceals the absence of authorship. The compass's grading of this type is the reframing's most important historical service. By the ownership test the type scored as socialism achieved, and its defenders said so for generations. By the authorship test it scores as the arrangement in which associational power is most thoroughly suppressed, suppressed in socialism's own vocabulary, which is why the suppression was so hard for its subjects to name. A grading device that can say this, inside the tradition that built the type, has earned its place in the tradition's toolkit whatever its other limits.

The third type is not a country but a direction made visible: the mixed configuration under erosion. Its firms include a substantial member governed sector with its own finance; its public services seat users and workers in their governance with votes that bind; its income system includes a floor that makes refusal affordable; its state's perimeter rules are drafted with associational jurisdiction as a stated aim. Nothing in the type is utopian in the dismissive sense: every element exists somewhere in partial form, which is the project's founding observation. The type's interest is combinatorial. The elements support each other: the floor strengthens the unions, the unions defend the floor, the governed services train the citizens who staff the cooperatives, the cooperatives demonstrate the governance the services borrow. Whether the combination compounds as the doctrine hopes, or merely coexists as a list, is the empirical question the verdict left open, and describing the type cannot answer it. Description has its own use, though: it shows a sceptical reader exactly what would have to be true, institution by institution, for the doctrine's destination to exist, and a destination that can be described can be argued about clause by clause, which is all the project ever claimed to make possible.

### What does describing configurations as types add to the compass?

It converts coordinates into pictures. A reader who can picture the market led, administered, and eroding configurations, and can say which powers direct investment, services, and livelihoods in each, possesses the compass as an instrument rather than as a slogan. The descriptions also expose the reframing's historical payoff: the administered type, triumphant by the ownership test, fails the authorship test completely.

## Open questions the programme bequeaths

An honest article ends its analytical business by inventorying what its subject leaves unsolved, in a form successors can use. The discriminator problem heads the inventory: no test usable in advance separates reforms destined to entrench association from reforms destined for digestion, and until one exists the strategy doctrine's guidance remains retrospective. The adversary problem follows: counter mobilisation needs the typology and timing analysis the project's own historical materials invite and its strategy chapters omit. The specification problem is third: the conditions under which association counts as social power in the grading sense, connection, internal democracy, orientation, need codification tight enough for two analysts to grade the same case alike. Fourth stands the scale problem in its sharpest form, the defeater's unmet condition: no documented passage from interstitial founding to national constraint with constitution intact. Fifth is the time problem in its theoretical form: the doctrine's patience requires a motivational account of why actors sustain effort across horizons longer than their own participation, and the materials, identity, institution building as its own reward, intergenerational organisation, are scattered rather than assembled. Sixth is the knowledge problem at the participatory end, where iterative planning must still meet the strong form of the dispersed knowledge objection on ground the companion literature has prepared.

The inventory is not an indictment, and its length should be read as the programme's fertility rather than its failure. Research programmes are valued, in the philosophy of science the founder respected, by the quality of the problems they hand on: whether the problems are precise, whether they matter, whether work on them teaches something even when they resist solution. By that standard the bequest is rich. Each problem in the inventory can be assigned, worked, and reported on with the project's own instruments, and several are already the subjects of literatures that cite the programme as their origin. A tradition that began by refusing to specify its destination has ended, in this branch of it, by specifying its unsolved problems with a precision its critics can use. The specification is the legacy, and it is not a small one.

## Movements and the project: the organising question

The project's strategies presuppose actors, and the actors it presupposes are movements: organised collectivities that build institutions, bargain for reforms, and defend both. The relation between the real utopias literature and the study of social movements is therefore internal rather than decorative, and readers pursuing it should continue to the companion article on [Marxism and social movements](/2022/04/01/marxism-and-social-movements/), which owns the general argument. The specific contribution to be registered here is the project's reframing of what movements are for. In the ruptural inheritance, a movement exists to gather force for the decisive moment, and its institutions are encampments. In the erosion doctrine, a movement exists to build and govern the alternative configuration piecemeal, and its institutions are the achievement itself, not the rehearsal for it. The reframing changes movement practice at the level of the weekly meeting: the cooperative's accounts, the budget assembly's costing dispute, the fund's board election become strategic events, and the skills they require, bookkeeping, procedure, negotiation, become movement skills, displacing the purely mobilisational repertoire of march and slogan without abolishing it.

The reframing also changes the movement's relation to expertise. Institutional design is technical: constitutions, finance, electoral systems for internal bodies, audit. A movement practising erosion needs its own technicians and must solve the problem of their accountability, since expertise unaccountable is the seed of the oligarchy every pathway fears. The project's volumes model one solution, the adversarial collaboration of proposer and critic inside a shared text, and the model's transferability to movement life is imperfect but suggestive: movements that institutionalise their internal critics, funding the red team rather than expelling it, reproduce in miniature the method that made the project's literature trustworthy. The point is rarely made in the secondary discussion, and it belongs in any full account of the project's practical teaching.

### What changes in a movement that adopts the erosion doctrine?

Its calendar and its curriculum. Institutions it builds and governs become strategic achievements rather than encampments awaiting a decisive moment, so bookkeeping, procedure, negotiation, and audit become movement skills alongside mobilisation. Its internal critics acquire a function, testing designs against failure modes, and its victories are graded by jurisdiction gained and capacity installed rather than by crowds assembled.

## Method: how the project argues, and why it persuades or fails to

A section on method is owed because the project's persuasiveness, where it persuades, is methodological before it is political. Three habits do the work. The first is the decomposition of large claims into mechanisms, borrowed from the analytical tradition and visible on every page: socialism becomes powers, powers become jurisdictions, jurisdictions become seats, votes, vetoes, and recall procedures. A reader can disagree with a mechanism, and the disagreement has somewhere to stand. The second habit is the built in adversary: the volumes' architecture of proposal, commentary, and reply institutionalises the strongest available criticism at the moment of a design's birth, so that the literature's readers meet objections in their strong form rather than in the straw versions polemic prefers. The third habit is the concession, practised by the founder as a discipline: the plausibility judgement on rupture is indexed to conditions, the criterion's retrospectivity is admitted, the compass's taxonomic character is owned. Concessions of this kind are sometimes read as weakness. In a literature competing for the allegiance of readers trained to detect evasion, they function as the opposite: they are the project's credentials, the evidence that its affirmations, where they come, have survived its own cross examination.

The method has a corresponding failure mode, and fairness requires naming it in the same section. Decomposition can become a substitute for judgement about wholes: a literature that grades every institution along its dimensions may lose the ability to say that a configuration, taken entire, is intolerable or admirable, and the project's prose occasionally exhibits the loss, hedging a total assessment into a schedule of partial ones. The taxonomy criticism, at its most thoughtful, is this observation generalised. The verdict's grading, instruments trusted above itinerary, is the article's own attempt to hold both truths: the method is the contribution, and the method's limit is the contribution's boundary.

### What is the project's characteristic argumentative habit?

Decomposition followed by concession. Large claims are broken into mechanisms compact enough to examine, the strongest criticism is built into the presentation rather than answered afterward, and the limits of the resulting claims are stated by the author before the critics can state them. The habits give the literature its unusual credibility with hostile readers and its equally unusual exposure to the charge of hedging.

## Erosion's temporality: patience as a theoretical problem

Every strategy doctrine contains a theory of time, and erosion's is the least examined part of the project. Rupture's time is punctual: before and after, divided by the event. Electoral reformism's time is cyclical: terms, campaigns, the alternation of governments. Erosion's time is cumulative and open ended: gains accrete, defences hold or fail, and no date can be assigned at which the account is closed. The doctrine's patience is therefore not a temperament but a structural requirement, and it generates three problems the literature touches without exhausting. The first is motivational: actors are asked to sustain effort whose payoff is a configuration their grandchildren will grade, and movements are not naturally patient institutions, since their members joined for grievances with dates on them. The second is epistemic: a cumulative process without milestones cannot easily tell its participants whether they are winning, and the compass, grading direction rather than distance travelled, offers orientation without a speedometer. The third is strategic: an adversary with a shorter time horizon can concentrate force at chosen moments, while the eroding side's strength is dispersed across decades and institutions, so that the doctrine must explain how dispersed patience defeats concentrated urgency at the points where they collide. The recommended combination is the project's implicit answer: resisting supplies the urgency at the points of collision, taming and escaping supply the patience between them. Whether the answer suffices is, once more, the kind of question the project teaches its readers to ask and cannot finally answer for them.

There is a consolation built into the doctrine's temporality that its critics rarely credit. A strategy of accumulation can lose repeatedly without being refuted, because its claims are about direction and capacity rather than about a scheduled event whose non arrival would falsify them. The same property, of course, is what the taxonomy criticism calls unfalsifiability, and the two descriptions are of one fact viewed from two sides. The article's position, following the verdict's grading, is that the fact is genuinely double edged: erosion's immunity to refutation by events is the condition of its plausibility as a long strategy and the reason its truth can only ever be established retrospectively, by a record its participants will not live to read entire. Doctrines should be chosen with their epistemic prices displayed, and the project's distinction is that it displays its own.

## Design principles distilled from the volumes

Readers who use the project as a design library, the policy audience described earlier, extract from the volumes a set of working principles that recur across institutions differing in everything else. The principles are stated here as the literature supports them, in prose as the series requires, each with the failure it guards against. Bindingness comes first: a participatory procedure whose outputs can be overridden without cost to the overrider is consultation, and the volumes' cases divide cleanly by whether the override existed. Publicity of criteria follows: costings, eligibility rules, and allocation formulas must be published in advance, since an unpublished criterion is a discretion, and discretion is where recapture lives. Rotation and recall guard the delegate layer: fixed terms, limits on consecutive service, and recall procedures answer the iron law's pressure before it consolidates. Independent technical support answers the administration's costing monopoly: an assembly that cannot cost its own proposals will adopt the proposals of whoever can. Indivisible reserves and asset locks answer the demutualisation prize in member governed firms: what cannot be divided cannot be cashed out, and the temptation that dissolves cooperatives from inside is removed by constitution rather than resisted by virtue. Federation level audit answers scale: the small unit's members can monitor a committee they know, and the federation's auditors, answerable to the units, extend the monitoring across the distances where familiarity fails. Sunset and review clauses answer the project's own fallibilism: an institution designed under uncertainty should carry the date of its reconsideration inside itself, so that revision is a procedure and not a crisis.

The principles share a common shape worth noticing. None of them trusts the participants' future selves. Each assumes enthusiasm will fade, offices will tempt, administrations will recapture, and majorities will tire, and each builds the counter into the constitution while the founders' energy is available to build it. The shape is the viability test converted into a checklist, and it explains the project's appeal to practitioners: a literature that assumes your institution will be attacked by entropy and designs accordingly is more useful than a literature that assumes your members will remain heroes. It also explains the project's distance from the utopian genre it echoes in its title. The classical utopia designs for the citizens it wishes existed. The real utopias literature designs for the citizens who do, tired, tempted, and busy, and grades its designs by whether tired and tempted people can still run them.

### Which design principle does the project's case literature treat as most decisive?

Bindingness. Across the volumes, the cases divide most cleanly by whether the participatory or associational body's decisions actually bind: whether budgets follow the assembly's priorities, whether the board's vote governs the managers, whether the override exists and what it costs. Procedures without binding force are reclassified by the literature as consultation, whatever their founding documents proclaim.

## The knowledge problem at the project's edge

One boundary of the project deserves a section because readers arriving from the economics of planning will look for it. The compass's far associational end, participatory socialism, must answer the knowledge objection in its strong form: that the information required to coordinate a complex economy is dispersed, tacit, and generated by the price system it would replace, so that no procedure of councils, however democratic, can gather what it needs. The project's treatment of the objection is respectful and partial. It notes that the objection's force varies by decision type: the knowledge needed to govern a service's priorities differs from the knowledge needed to set economy wide quantities, and the compass's graded space allows association to govern the first without claiming the second. It notes further that markets themselves aggregate knowledge through institutions, firms, platforms, forecasting industries, whose internal governance is not exempt from the compass's questions. And it leaves the strong form of the objection standing at the pathway's far end, where the iterative planning designs must meet it, referring the reader to the literature where the meeting is staged. That literature's home in this series is the article on [planning and the market](/2019/10/15/can-planning-beat-the-market/), and the present article's restraint is deliberate: the real utopias project's contribution to the knowledge debate is its insistence that the debate be held decision by decision rather than civilisation by civilisation, a reframing whose merits the companion article's evidence can be used to judge.

### How does the compass change the knowledge debate about planning?

It disaggregates it. Instead of asking whether planning in general can replace markets in general, the compass asks, for each class of decision, which power should govern it and what knowledge that governance requires. Some decisions move toward association under the reframing while others remain with market or state mechanisms, and the debate becomes a schedule of particular arguments rather than a single verdict on a system.

## The normative foundations: equality, democracy, community

The compass grades direction, but direction toward what is fixed by the project's stated values, and the synthesis states them with unusual explicitness for a Marxist text: equality, democracy, and community, each given a specific sense. Equality, in the project's usage, is not sameness of outcome but a principle about access: every person should have broadly equal access to the material and social conditions required to live a flourishing life, and inequalities that track inherited advantage in those conditions are the ones the principle condemns. Democracy is given the authorship sense developed earlier in this article: people subject to decisions should be their authors, individually where decisions are personal and collectively, through association, where they are social. Community, the least theorised of the three, names the value of social relations conducted as cooperation among equals rather than as command or exchange alone, and it does quiet work in the project, explaining why two arrangements equal in distribution and authorship can still differ in grade: the one that organises its members as mutual providers outranks the one that organises them as competing claimants on a common fund.

Stating the values explicitly was itself a move within the tradition. Classical Marxism often treated its normative commitments as corollaries of its science, to be derived from the analysis of capitalism rather than confessed as premises. The analytical school, and Wright within it, insisted on the confession, partly from philosophical conviction that ought cannot be derived from is without remainder, and partly from strategic clarity: a project that states its values can argue about them, trade among them when they conflict, and recognise allies who share them from outside the tradition. The real utopias volumes exploit exactly that openness. Contributors who would not sign the tradition's economics sign its values readily, and the design arguments proceed on the shared ground. Critics from within the tradition have occasionally read the explicitness as dilution, values replacing analysis. The synthesis's reply is that the analysis is present in the mechanisms and the values are present in the grading, and that a text which knows which of its sentences are which is more rigorous, not less, than a text that disguises the second kind as the first.

### Why does the project state its values instead of deriving them from its analysis?

Because the derivation cannot be completed honestly. The claim that people should author the decisions that govern them is a commitment, not a finding, and presenting it as a finding would corrupt both the commitment and the analysis. Stated openly, the values can be argued with, traded against each other where they conflict, and shared with allies outside the tradition who accept them on other grounds.

The three values can conflict, and the project's handling of the conflicts is a further test of its honesty. Equality of access may argue for centralised provision under state power, where democracy as authorship argues for associational governance that will vary by locality and therefore by outcome. Community may argue for the dense obligations of small scale, where viability argues for the anonymity of large. The synthesis does not dissolve these tensions with a formula. It treats them as the permanent material of institutional judgement, to be negotiated case by case with the compass in hand, and it grades designs partly by whether the negotiation is visible in them. A design that maximises one value by silently abolishing another fails the project's own standard of explicitness, a standard stricter than the tradition usually imposes and one of the reasons the literature rewards slow reading.

## Objections from other directions: efficiency and pluralism

The criticisms assembled so far come largely from the project's own tradition and its near neighbours. Two objections from further away deserve statement, since a full account owes the reader the whole perimeter. The efficiency objection, pressed by economists sympathetic to markets, holds that the compass systematically undervalues what economic power's decentralisation achieves: the processing of dispersed information, the discipline of exit, the discovery function of entrepreneurial experiment. Institutions graded well for their internal democracy may perform badly at these tasks, and a society that subordinated economic power wholesale would pay for its authorship in stagnation, a price the project's values do not entitle it to impose on people who did not choose the experiment. The project's reply distinguishes subordination from abolition: the compass's space contains configurations in which markets operate vigorously under associational and state governance of their perimeter and their commanding firms, and the pathways mostly propose to govern economic power rather than to replace its mechanisms. The reply's strength varies by pathway, strongest for the regulatory and associational routes, weakest at the participatory end, and the grading in the verdict already reflects the variation.

The pluralism objection, pressed by liberal theorists, holds that social power's ascendancy threatens the individual against the collective in a form the project's values should fear. Associations are not only schools of democracy; they are also orthodoxies with memberships, and a configuration in which life is mediated everywhere by association may suffocate the unassociated: the dissenter from the community's consensus, the person whose flourishing is solitary, the minority whose association is too small to seat. Liberal institutions, rights against majorities, markets that let a person exit a community's judgement into anonymous exchange, states bound by law against their own citizens' organised demands, exist partly as shelters from social power, and a compass that grades by social power's subordination of the others must say what protects the individual at the configuration's far end. The project's materials for a reply are real: its democracy value is authorship by the subject of the decision, which limits association's writ to genuinely collective matters; its equality value is access to conditions of flourishing, which the solitary life also claims; and its founder's liberalism about personal life was genuine. The reply, like several others in this article, exists in the corpus as materials rather than as a stated doctrine, and the objection is recorded here as live, its full staging belonging to the liberal critics who own it.

### Does subordinating economic power mean abolishing markets, on the project's account?

It does not. The compass sorts configurations according to the hierarchy among the three powers, and most pathways propose to govern markets through associational jurisdiction and state perimeter rules while retaining their information and allocation functions. Only the participatory pathway approaches replacing market coordination, and it is the pathway the project itself grades most tentatively.

## The criterion applied: three reforms graded

Abstractions accumulate; the reader is owed a worked exercise in which the Gorzian criterion and the compass grade concrete reform types side by side, so that the difference the apparatus makes can be seen rather than asserted. The three types are chosen to be close in welfare terms and far apart in power terms, the configuration in which the apparatus earns its keep.

The first type is a wage subsidy of the administered kind: the state supplements low wages from general revenue, paid through the tax system, calculated by officials, revocable by budget. Its welfare effects can be substantial and its defenders are serious. Graded by the compass, it moves nothing: economic power's jurisdiction inside the firm is untouched, the recipient's dependence is rerouted from employer to treasury without being reduced, and no association gains a seat, a fund, or a right. It may even grade negative on the exit dimension, since the subsidy can reconcile a workforce to wage levels it would otherwise contest, purchasing the employer's peace with public money. The criterion's verdict is swift: a reformist reform in Gorz's exact sense, chosen by what the system finds possible, stabilising the configuration it relieves.

The second type is a working time reduction of the standard statutory kind: the legal week shortened by law, overtime premia raised, enforcement by inspectorate. Welfare rises in the currency of hours. The compass registers a genuine but bounded movement: the employer's jurisdiction over the day's length is curtailed by state power, and the hours returned become available for the association from which social power is built, an indirect gain the time and care section theorised. The criterion asks its follow up question: does the reform install capacity for the next claim? Partly. The hours are capacity in raw form, but no jurisdiction is created and no organisation is endowed; the gain's conversion into power depends on what the associated do with evenings the statute cannot direct. The grading is positive, modest, and honest about its own indirectness.

The third type is a governance reform of the associational kind: a public service or a large firm brought under a board on which associations of workers and users hold binding seats, with published criteria, recall, and an independent technical office answerable to the board. Its immediate welfare effects may be nil; nothing is transferred and no hour is returned. Graded by welfare alone it is a nullity, and graded by the compass it is the strongest of the three: jurisdiction has moved, durably and visibly, from economic and state power to social power, and the body installed is itself a school and a fortress for further claims. The criterion's verdict completes the exercise's point. The three reforms, indistinguishable to a welfare calculus that ranks the first highest, are ranked by the project's apparatus in exactly the reverse order, and a movement selecting by the apparatus will build a different history from a movement selecting by welfare, which is the entire claim of the non-reformist reform concept stated in one exercise.

### Why can the compass rank a reform with no welfare effect above one with large welfare effects?

Because its variable is power rather than welfare. A governance reform that transfers binding jurisdiction to associations changes the configuration itself, installing capacity from which future gains can be won and defended. A transfer that leaves every jurisdiction untouched delivers its benefit at the pleasure of the configuration, and the project's criterion prices that dependence into the grade.

Exercises of this kind are also the project's best teaching device, and teachers using the article will find the persona section below building on them. Their limitation should be stated with the usual candour: the gradings depend on judgements about durability and conversion that the future will audit, and two competent graders can reverse the second and third types by disputing whether hours or seats convert more reliably into power. The apparatus disciplines the dispute, specifying what it is about, without abolishing it. A reader who expected an algorithm receives a structured argument instead, and the article's verdict has already explained why the project regards the substitution as honesty rather than failure.

## Wright's arc: from class maps to institutional designs

One biographical section is justified by an explanatory payoff: the real utopias project reads differently when its author's earlier career is kept in view. Wright spent the decades before 1991 constructing class schemas and putting them to comparative survey evidence, work whose governing virtue was falsifiability and whose governing frustration was the gap between mapping a structure and changing it. The maps grew more refined; the structure they mapped persisted; and the question the project answers can be heard forming inside the frustration: granted that the analysis is right about where power sits, what follows for the design of institutions that would sit it elsewhere? The turn from analysis to design was not a repudiation of the earlier work but its completion under a changed conception of what social science owes its readers. Explanation tells the reader how the world works. Design tells the reader what could be built inside it. Emancipatory social science, the phrase Wright used for the union of the two with an explicit normative compass, requires both, and the real utopias enterprise is the design half of a career whose analysis half is treated in the companion article.

The arc also explains the project's distinctive impatience with two audiences. Toward the tradition's rhetoricians, the impatience is the mapmaker's: a claim about institutions that cannot be decomposed into mechanisms has not yet reached the standard his survey work met as a matter of routine. Toward the policy world's technicians, the impatience is the theorist's: a design that cannot state its effect on the distribution of power has not yet asked the question his tradition exists to ask. The project stands at the crossing of the two impatiences, which is why it irritates both audiences in characteristic ways and why each audience, irritated, keeps returning to it. Longevity in social theory is rarely the reward of comfort.

### What did Wright's earlier class analysis contribute to the design project?

Habits and a question. The habits were decomposition into mechanisms, insistence on evidence that could disappoint, and comfort with revising frameworks in public. The question was the one the maps could not answer: granted the analysis of where power sits, what institutions would sit it elsewhere, and by what routes could they be built from the structure the maps described.

## A walk through one erosion sequence

Strategy doctrines are tested in the imagination by walking a sequence, and this section walks one, in structural terms throughout, so the reader can watch the combination's moving parts engage. The walk is an analytical exercise, not a case history: no place is named, no figures are given, and each step is stated at the level of mechanism where the doctrine makes its claims.

Begin with a service sector of a familiar shape: provision by a mixture of public bodies and contracted firms, a workforce with thin organisation, users with grievances and no channel. An interstitial step occurs at the margin: a group of workers and users founds a member governed provider of the social economy type, small, serving a neighbourhood the contractors underserve. Its constitution seats users on its board; its books are open to members; it survives its first years, which most such foundings do not, because a federation of similar providers supplies its back office and its credit line. Demonstration has occurred: a working institution in which the people who use a service and the people who staff it jointly govern it, available for inspection by anyone who doubts the form's viability.

A symbiotic step follows, of the kind the order's own problems invite. The public purchaser, struggling with quality failures in the contracted sector, opens a procurement framework in which governance standards count in tender evaluation: providers whose boards seat workers and users score for the accountability the purchaser needs. The reform solves the purchaser's problem and installs the criterion's beachhead, since the standard, once written, is a jurisdiction: associations now hold a legal lever over every provider's constitution, not only their own. Interstitial growth accelerates under the softened gradient, as the design principles section predicted: the sector's firms win work, hire, and convert two failing contractors by workforce buyout at succession. Resisting appears at the sequence's first collision, when a later administration proposes to strike the governance standard from the framework as a burden on competition; the federated sector, its user associations, and its workforce mobilise in the standard's defence, and the proposal is withdrawn, the beachhead's constituency having done exactly the work the criterion said beachheads do.

Grade the sequence on the compass and its claim is modest and legible. One sector's governance has moved measurably toward social power; the economy around it has not; the movement is reversible at several points and was nearly reversed at one. Grade it against the defeater and it fails, honourably: nothing here constrains economic power at national level, and the walk's honesty about scale is the doctrine's honesty too. What the walk shows is the combination's internal coherence: each strategy supplied what the others lacked, demonstration, environment, defence, and the criterion selected the steps. What it cannot show is the one thing the critics demand: that the sequence generalises, compounds, and survives its collisions at the scale where configurations are decided. The walk therefore ends where the verdict ended, with the engine's power unproven and its instruments in working order, and with the reader equipped to recognise the sequence's steps, and their absence, in the institutional news of any season.

### What does the walk through an erosion sequence establish, and what does it not establish?

It establishes internal coherence: the interstitial, symbiotic, and resisting elements supply each other's lacks, and the criterion selects among reforms intelligibly at each step. It does not establish scale. The defeater's condition, constraint on economic power at national level without absorption or marginality, remains unmet by construction in a sectoral walk, and the doctrine's central empirical claim stays unproven.

## Successors and the unfinished programme

Wright's death in 2019 closed the project's founding authorship and opened the question every research programme faces: whether its questions survive its founder as live inquiries or settle into a legacy to be curated. The early evidence, assessed structurally, is mixed in an instructive pattern. The catalogue function thrives: the institutions the project gathered continue to be studied, and the compass continues to be borrowed by fields that find its variable useful, often without the strategy doctrine attached. The doctrine's fate is less certain. Erosion as a named posture persists in the movements literature, and the five strategies circulate as a vocabulary, but the hard core of the doctrine, the criterion's application to live reforms by movements grading their own bargains, is practised unevenly and rarely with the founder's rigour. Programmes in this condition are not dead; they are awaiting the successor who finds the unsolved problem worth a career. The unsolved problems are identifiable from this article's verdict: a discriminator, usable in advance, between power shifting and absorbable reforms; an adversary model of counter mobilisation; a specification of social power's conditions tight enough to grade associations without smuggling. A successor who solved any one would restart the programme. A successor who solved none but taught the questions well would still be continuing it in the mode its founder, a teacher above all, might have ranked highest.

The project's place in the longer tradition can be fixed in a sentence before the article closes its analytical business. Marxism began as a critique of political economy that promised a science of transformation and delivered, across two centuries, powerful analyses, states that contradicted its values, and movements that administered what they had promised to abolish. The real utopias programme is the tradition's most disciplined attempt to convert the promise into engineering: to specify, institution by institution and mechanism by mechanism, what the values require, what the designs risk, and what the routes demand. Its failures, where the verdict found them, are the failures of engineering honestly attempted, unproven engines and retrospective instruments, and they differ in kind from the failures of prophecy and of apology that fill the tradition's other shelves. That difference is the project's claim on the reader's time, and it survives the criticisms this article has stated at full strength.

## Doubts from the workshop floor: practitioners cross examine the theory

Theory in this programme is answerable to practice in a way most social theory is not, because its claims concern institutions that identifiable people are trying to run. Practitioners who govern cooperatives, administer participatory processes, or manage collectively held funds bring a distinctive cross examination to the texts, and their questions deserve recording alongside the academic criticisms, since they arise from the friction the theory purports to describe. Their first question concerns attention. Democratic governance consumes the scarcest resource in any working institution, the hours of its most capable members, and practitioners ask whether the programme has honestly priced the meeting load its designs impose, or whether the volumes' enthusiasm for participation quietly assumes a citizenry with evenings to burn. The time and care analysis gives the programme its answer in principle: redistribute the hours. Practitioners reply that the redistribution is itself one of the reforms awaiting a coalition, so the designs presuppose at the start a good whose delivery is scheduled for later in the sequence. The exchange is unresolved in the literature, and it identifies a genuine circularity risk in the bootstrapping account: formation requires participation, participation requires time, time requires reforms, reforms require formation. Circles of this kind are not automatically vicious; engines also cycle. But a doctrine resting on a cycle owes its readers an account of where the cycle can be entered at its weakest point, and the programme's best answer, entry through the smallest jurisdictions where the time price is lowest, deserves more prominence than the texts give it.

Their second question concerns boredom, a variable political theory almost never models. Participatory institutions live or die by attendance across years of routine business, and routine is where the founding generation's successors must be recruited. Practitioners report that the glamour of self government fades at exactly the rate the institution succeeds, since success turns drama into maintenance. The programme's design literature answers with rotation and with the deliberate preservation of consequential decisions for the participatory body, on the theory that people attend when the agenda matters. The answer is plausible and incomplete, and the incompleteness points at a researchable question the successor literature has barely touched: the comparative survival of participatory bodies as a function of how much real decision weight they retain after their fifth year. A programme serious about viability should want that number, and wanting it is a fair test of whether the successor literature is continuing the founder's empiricism or merely curating his vocabulary.

### What is the practitioners' strongest question for the programme?

It concerns the price of attention. Democratic governance runs on the hours of capable members, and the redistribution of time that would supply those hours is itself a reform scheduled later in the sequence. Practitioners ask where the resulting circle is entered: the programme's best answer, through the smallest jurisdictions where the time price is lowest, needs the prominence the texts deny it.

## The programme and the climate emergency: a test the founder did not set

Successor debates increasingly press the programme into service on a problem its founding texts treat only in passing: the ecological transformation of production. The pressing is instructive whether or not it succeeds, because it tests the grading device on decisions of a kind the device was not built for. Decarbonising an economy involves choices with long horizons, irreversible thresholds, and victims who cannot associate, future persons and other species prominent among them. Association grades such choices awkwardly: the associated are present generations with present interests, and a compass that trusts their authorship must explain why authorship should bind where the affected parties cannot sit. Defenders answer that the device grades the governance of the transition, not the atmosphere's chemistry: whether the costs of transformation are imposed by owners protecting asset values, by administrations protecting incumbency, or distributed under procedures the burdened can contest. On that reading the ecological case becomes a vast new field for the old questions, who decides the pace, who owns the stranded assets, whose associations hold seats when the closures are scheduled. Critics answer that pace is the whole question where thresholds are real, and that a doctrine of patient accumulation is a dangerous counsellor in a house already alight. The exchange is young, the texts on both sides are thinner than the problem, and this article records it as the liveliest current front in the programme's afterlife, the place where its instruments are being tested against a case their maker did not foresee and where the verdict, characteristically, will be delivered by the record rather than by polemic.

## Measuring movement: what would count as evidence of erosion

The verdict complained that the doctrine's claims are established retrospectively, and the fairest response a successor could make is to specify, in advance, the observations that would count for and against erosion in a given setting. This closing analytical section sketches such a specification, as an exercise in the programme's own method. Begin with jurisdiction accounts: for a chosen territory and period, enumerate the significant decision domains, investment, land use, service priorities, workplace rules, credit allocation, and record for each domain which kind of power effectively governs it, using documentary evidence about seats, vetoes, budgets, and overrides rather than impressions. Repeat the accounting at intervals. Erosion, if the word means anything, should appear as a directional drift in the accounts, associational jurisdiction expanding across domains and surviving changes of administration. Add capacity accounts beside the jurisdiction accounts: membership density of associations with governing roles, their independent revenues, the stock of experienced officers they can field, since jurisdiction without capacity is a paper transfer awaiting recapture. Add a reversals register: every beachhead lost, with the mechanism of its loss recorded, hostile legislation, administrative neutralisation, market absorption, internal oligarchy, so that the doctrine's failure modes accumulate as data rather than as anecdotes traded in debate. A literature that kept such accounts for a generation could finally grade the strategy doctrine empirically, confirming the drift where it occurs and naming its absence where the rhetoric claims it. Nothing in the specification requires new theory. It requires the founder's oldest habit, counting what the argument is about, applied to the founder's own claims by hands willing to be disappointed by the totals. Whether the successor literature performs the exercise will decide, more than any polemic, what the programme's second life amounts to.

### What evidence, gathered in advance, would let the erosion doctrine be tested rather than asserted?

Jurisdiction accounts kept over time: a periodic census of who effectively governs each significant decision domain, supported by records of seats, vetoes, budgets, and overrides, together with capacity accounts for the associations involved and a register of reversed gains with their mechanisms. A directional drift surviving changes of administration would support the doctrine; its absence, honestly recorded, would grade it down.

## The project's readers: uptake beyond the tradition

The brief directs attention to the project's reception outside the Marxist tradition, and the reception is part of its significance. Participatory budgeting, basic income, and cooperative ownership are working topics in public policy, development studies, and organisational research, pursued by scholars and practitioners most of whom have no allegiance to the tradition the project inherits. For those readers, the real utopias literature functions as an entry point and a common table: its volumes gather the design arguments for institutions they study separately, and its compass gives their separate studies a shared question. A development scholar evaluating a budgeting process, an organisational researcher studying cooperative governance, a policy analyst modelling an income floor: each can locate a study inside the compass's space and discover the neighbouring studies the location implies. The project's founder understood this function and cultivated it; the volumes were edited to be usable by readers who skipped the theory, and the theory was written to reward the readers who arrived from the cases.

Civil society and third sector research has taken up the compass directly, since its central variable, the organised capacity of association, is that field's home territory. The uptake has been critical as well as appreciative: third sector scholars are professionally familiar with association's pathologies, its exclusions and oligarchies, and their engagement has sharpened the under-specification debate from inside the concept's home discipline. The exchange illustrates the project's general fate in the academy: it is used most productively by the fields positioned to damage it, and its concepts have been refined in the using. A theory of social power that survives contact with the people who study associations for a living will be a better theory than the one in the synthesis, and the programme's openness to that contact is among its methodological virtues.

### Why do policy readers who never search for Marxism arrive at this project?

Because its subjects are theirs. Participatory budgeting, basic income, and cooperative ownership are live questions in public policy, development, and organisational studies, and the project's volumes treat those institutions with design level detail and built in criticism. Such readers arrive for the cases and encounter the compass as the frame that connects their case to its neighbours.

The uptake has a limit the article should record symmetrically. Readers arriving from policy fields tend to strip the project of its strategy doctrine and keep its catalogue, treating the volumes as a design library for reforms whose purpose is better governance rather than transformed power. The project cannot prevent the stripping, and its founder's attitude toward it was permissive: institutions graded well by the compass do their work whatever banner they travel under. The critics from the tradition read the same fact less kindly, as evidence for the renaming objection: a programme whose parts are so readily absorbed into ordinary policy discourse may have been ordinary policy discourse in theoretical dress all along. The verdict has already priced this exchange; the section's purpose is to show the reader the two readings side by side, since the project's reception is one of the few bodies of evidence both sides cite.

## The books in sequence: a reader's path

A reader persuaded to go further needs an order, and the corpus supplies a natural one. Begin with the final text of 2019, the short general book, not because it is the most rigorous but because it is the map the author himself drew last: the five strategies, the erosion recommendation, the tone of invitation. Read it as an overture whose themes the larger works develop. Proceed then to the 2010 synthesis, where the compass is constructed, the pathways anatomised, the three strategies argued, and the criticisms of the programme anticipated in the author's own voice. The synthesis is the project's centre of gravity, and every other text in the corpus is either its preparation or its application. The volumes of the founding series come third, to be read selectively by institution: the budgeting volume for the governance type, the income volume for the exit mechanism, the associational volume for the jurisdiction questions, each with its commentaries, since the commentaries are where the project's method lives. Gorz's originating text, in the translation named earlier in this article, belongs fourth, read for the criterion in its native argument, after which the synthesis's strategy chapters can be reread with the lineage visible. The class analysis that preceded the project, treated in the companion article on Wright's class analysis, closes the path for readers who want the whole arc: the sociologist who mapped class structure for two decades before he permitted himself to design alternatives, and whose designs carry the mapmaker's habits.

### Which single text should a newcomer read first, and which is the indispensable one?

The final short book of 2019 is the right first text, since it states the strategies and the recommendation in the author's own vernacular. The indispensable text is the synthesis of 2010, where the compass, the pathways, and the transformation argument are built in full; everything else in the corpus either prepares for it or applies it, and no summary, including this article, substitutes for its argument.

Two warnings accompany the path. The secondary literature's summaries, this article included, compress the strategy doctrine into its slogans, erosion above all, and the compression loses the conditions the texts attach: the specified conditions for the rupture judgement, the conjunction on which the erosion claim rests, the concessions on the criterion's retrospective character. A reader who argues about the project from summaries will reproduce the misreadings catalogued earlier. The second warning concerns the volumes' age: institutional literatures move, legal regimes change, and the design arguments of the founding volumes should be checked against the subsequent research of the fields they entered before being cited as settled. The project's method, severity toward its own proposals, is the correct attitude toward the project's own texts.

## A note on the two vocabularies: translation effects inside one corpus

Scholars citing the strategy doctrine should be alert to a trap the corpus sets for the unwary, the coexistence of two vocabularies for substantially one doctrine. The synthesis speaks of ruptural, interstitial, and symbiotic strategies; the final book speaks of the five vernacular verbs of the final book. Journalists and students routinely quote the later terms while citing the earlier text, or attribute the fivefold scheme's distinctions to the synthesis where they do not appear. The trap matters because the schemes are not perfectly congruent: the later one splits a category and demotes the directed half, so an argument built from the synthesis's symbiotic concept cannot be refuted by pointing at the final book's scepticism about dismantling, and an argument built from the final book's five postures cannot claim the synthesis's authority for the resisting posture, which the earlier text does not name as a strategy at all. Careful work in this area therefore cites scheme and text together, and treats any summary that merges them, however convenient, as a small falsification. This article has observed the discipline throughout, employing the earlier vocabulary where mechanisms are at stake and the later one where general readers are addressed, and readers preparing their own accounts should imitate the separation until it becomes habit.

## How to argue about this topic: a synthesis for candidates and critics

Some readers will meet this material in examinations, interviews, and seminar disputes, where the premium is on structured judgement rather than on coverage. The structure that serves best mirrors the article's own: definition, mechanism, strategy, criticism, verdict with defeater. Open any answer by relocating the definition, since most confusion in the topic is definitional: state that the programme grades arrangements by the ascendancy of associational power over administration and ownership, and that every subsequent claim follows from that grading. Proceed to mechanisms, because the programme's distinctiveness lives there: a budgeting cycle with binding outputs, an income floor as a device of exit, a fund whose beneficiaries govern it. Examiners and critics alike reward the candidate who can name the moving parts over the candidate who recites the vocabulary. Then take the strategies in their correspondence, three names mapped to five, with the recommended pairing identified and the rejected option's rejection correctly characterised as a judgement about conditions rather than a principle. Then give the criticisms in their strongest available versions, including the concession about retrospective testing, because an answer that volunteers the concession demonstrates the understanding the concession belongs to. Close with a verdict that carries its defeater in the same breath: the judgement on interstitial growth stands until a case of scaling without absorption or marginality is documented, and saying so converts an opinion into an assessable claim.

The same structure disciplines advocacy outside examination rooms. A defender of the programme who begins from slogans, erosion as a cheer, invites the renaming charge and deserves it. A defender who begins from the grading device, shows it discriminating among real reform types, concedes the retrospective weakness, and specifies the evidence that would settle the remaining dispute has done the only thing that ever persuades a sceptical audience: demonstrated that the position can lose, and shown where. Critics are served symmetrically. The renaming charge lands when it is aimed at the criterion's retrospectivity and the adversary's absence from the strategy chapters. It misses when it is aimed at the grading device, which does discriminate, or at the attribution, which the texts state correctly. Precision about targets is the whole art here, and the programme, whatever its faults, is a school for that precision.

### What is the fastest route to a strong answer on this topic?

Work through five stations in order: the relocated definition, one mechanism described in working detail, the two strategy schemes with their correspondence, the criticisms at full strength including the concession on retrospective testing, and a verdict carrying its defeater. Coverage of slogans earns little; the stations earn marks because each demonstrates a distinction the topic exists to teach.

## The programme among its neighbours: liberalism, republicanism, anarchism

No doctrine develops in an empty field, and the project's character sharpens against the neighbouring traditions it resembles at one point or another. Liberalism shares its suspicion of concentrated power and its insistence on rights that survive majorities, then parts from it over the workplace and the market, where liberal analysis traditionally stops at the factory gate and treats the employment contract as freedom's exercise rather than as a jurisdiction to be graded. The project borrows liberalism's protections, since its authorship standard needs them, while refusing liberalism's perimeter, since its grading follows power into every institution that governs a life. Republicanism, in its revived contemporary form, shares the project's central intuition almost exactly: freedom as non-domination, the condition of not living at another's arbitrary mercy. The resemblance is close enough that the two literatures cite each other, and the difference is one of inheritance and emphasis: republicanism arrives with a theory of the state and a suspicion of faction, the project with a theory of class and a confidence in association, and each suspects the other's favourite bearer of becoming the domination it was meant to check. Anarchism shares the destination's silhouette, a society coordinated by voluntary association with coercive apparatus shrunk to remnants, and parts over the route and the state: the project's symbiotic strategy, working through existing machinery under a criterion, is precisely the collaboration anarchist analysis predicts will digest the collaborators. The prediction is the absorption criticism in older dress, and the project's answer, federation plus beachheads plus the criterion's vigilance, is either a refutation in progress or the newest costume of a very old hope. The article's verdict has priced the uncertainty; the neighbourhood map is offered so readers can locate the argument they personally find decisive, since different readers will be stopped by different neighbours.

### Which neighbouring tradition comes closest to the project's core intuition?

Revived republicanism, with its definition of freedom as non-domination: not living subject to another party's arbitrary will. Both traditions grade institutions by the standing they give the governed. They diverge in inheritance and in trusted bearer, republicanism leaning on law and the state it disciplines, the project on association and the organisations it builds, each wary of the other's instrument.

The neighbourhood also clarifies the project's odd double reputation, radical among policy readers and tame among revolutionaries. Both reputations are accurate reports from different borders. At the policy border, a literature that grades a budgeting process by its redistribution of jurisdiction and finds most celebrated reforms wanting is a radicalising presence. At the revolutionary border, a literature that prices rupture and declines to buy, that counsels patience and drafting, reads as the opposition's fifth column inside the tradition. A doctrine cannot choose its borders. It can only be exact about its claims, and let the neighbours' reactions locate it more precisely than its own self description could.

## Failure modes of the favoured institutions, catalogued honestly

A design literature earns trust by cataloguing the failures of its own favourites in the same detail it lavishes on their virtues, and the project's volumes set a standard this section summarises as a checklist of pathologies, each attached to the institution type that suffers it and the constitutional counter the literature proposes. Participatory budgeting suffers capture by the organised, since assemblies reward those with time, confidence, and prior organisation, so that the procedure can redistribute voice toward the already vocal; the counters are outreach duties, reserved representation for the unorganised, and rotation of delegate roles. It suffers fatigue, as the cycle's novelty fades and attendance thins to the committed; the counters are visible bindingness, since people return to procedures whose decisions they can see executed, and the periodic renewal of the agenda's scope. It suffers recapture through costing, when the administration's monopoly of technical estimation quietly vetoes what the assembly votes; the counter is an independent technical office with its own budget line.

The cooperative suffers the degeneration sequence rehearsed earlier, hiring outside the membership and widening differentials as it grows; the counters are constitutional, membership tied to employment by rule, differential caps, indivisible reserves. It suffers undercapitalisation, since lenders price its governance as risk and members cannot stake what they do not have; the counters are federation level finance and conversion funds. It suffers the succession crisis, when a founding generation's energy retires with it; the counter is the boring one, training pipelines and documented procedure, charisma converted into routine before the charisma leaves. Associational governance suffers the insider drift of its seated delegates and the licensing power of whoever certifies the associations; the counters are recall, term limits, public funding of counter expertise, and certification tests administered at arm's length from the government of the day. The income floor suffers a pathology of a different kind, political rather than organisational: its very universality makes it a large visible budget line, a target painted for every austerity campaign, and its defence depends on the constituency argument the criterion teaches, that programmes people govern and universally receive are harder to strip than programmes administered to a stigmatised few. The catalogue could continue, and in the volumes it does, for hundreds of pages. Its lesson for the reader is uniform: every institution in the repertoire fails in characteristic ways that can be named in advance, and the difference between the project's literature and a pamphlet is that the literature names them first.

### Which pathology does the literature treat as most instructive across all the types?

Recapture through expertise. Again and again the decisive failure arrives not as a vote lost or a law repealed but as a quiet monopoly of technical capacity, costing, drafting, auditing, settling into the hands of administrators or professionals, after which the democratic forms continue and the decisions migrate. The counter, independent technical support answerable to the associated, is therefore the design principle the cases teach most consistently.

## Reading the opponent's library: what the critics teach the defenders

The final analytical section reverses the article's usual direction and asks what the programme's defenders should steal from its critics, since a literature's maturity shows in the use it makes of opposition. From the Luxemburg lineage the defenders should steal urgency about time: the warning that a movement's goals dissolve into its routines is not refuted by the criterion, only answered by it, and the answer works only if the criterion is applied as a discipline in real bargains rather than recited as a heritage. From the economists the defenders should steal respect for exit and information: designs that ignore what participants can walk away from, and what decision makers can possibly know, will be educated by events at their participants' expense. From the liberals the defenders should steal the standing of the dissenter: any associational order that cannot describe the protected position of the person who refuses to associate has not finished its constitutional homework. From the historians the defenders should steal the adversary's diary: a habit of writing, for every advance, the counter move it invites and the date by which the counter move should be expected, so that defence is planned rather than improvised. A programme whose defenders read so would not thereby become correct. It would become harder to defeat for the reasons its critics have already published, which is the most any honest programme can ask of its reading habits.

## Professions and expertise: the programme's quiet difficulty

Between the associated citizen and the complex institution stands a figure the programme treats with respect and unease in equal measure: the professional. Modern services run on credentialed knowledge, medicine, engineering, accountancy, law, and any scheme of popular governance must decide what authority credentials carry against votes. Seat the users and workers, by all means, say the volumes' more careful contributors, and then watch the assembly confront a structural engineer's report or a clinician's protocol. Deference returns through the door of competence, and the jurisdiction so carefully transferred leaks back to the credentialed. The programme's responses are characteristic. Divide the decision: values, priorities, and acceptable risk belong to the governed body; technique, once the values are set, belongs to the professionals, whose duty is to present options honestly rather than to smuggle values into estimates. Fund counter expertise, so the body can commission a second engineering opinion rather than merely receive the first. Rotate the interface, so that no single professional becomes the body's permanent interpreter of reality. None of this abolishes the asymmetry, and the literature does not pretend otherwise. Knowledge really is unevenly held, and a democracy of authors must govern through experts without being governed by them, a balance every modern state also fails to strike and mostly fails to notice. The programme at least notices, and its noticing is one more instance of the method this article has tracked throughout: find the place where the favoured form quietly reproduces the unfavoured power, and design against that place specifically.

The professions, for their part, are not merely obstacles in this analysis. Professional ethics, at their best, encode a form of social responsibility that markets do not reward and states cannot command: the clinician's duty to the patient before the budget, the engineer's duty to safety before the client. Several contributors to the volumes argue that such ethics are associational resources, professionalism as a guild form of the very power the compass celebrates, and that well designed governance recruits the ethics rather than fighting them, giving professional bodies jurisdiction over standards while reserving priorities and resources to the wider authorship. The suggestion complicates any simple story about experts versus people, and the complication is welcome in a literature whose besetting risk is romanticism about whichever side it favours.

### How should a participatory body govern experts without surrendering to them?

By dividing the decision along its real seam. Priorities, values, and acceptable risk are set by the governing body; technique is delegated to professionals obliged to present options and uncertainties honestly; and the body holds its own budget for independent advice, so that a second opinion is a right rather than a favour. The arrangement governs expertise instead of either obeying or expelling it.

## Generations: the programme read by its inheritors

A word about how the programme reads to those who encountered it after its founder's death, since a text's meaning is partly made by the circumstances of its reading. Younger scholars and activists meet the real utopias literature in a landscape its author did not live to see: platform monopolies organising labour through algorithms, climate breakdown pricing itself into every institutional calculation, democratic governments themselves among the configurations under strain. The grading device travels into that landscape surprisingly well, because its variable, who authors the decisions, applies to a platform's algorithm as directly as to a factory's board: the couriers subject to the dispatch system hold no seats in its governance, and the compass registers the arrangement without needing new categories. The strategy doctrine travels less easily, since erosion's patience presupposes a stability the newer landscape does not promise, and inheritors divide between those who treat the doctrine as a fair weather instrument to be supplemented for storms and those who argue that storm conditions are precisely when interstitial building matters most, mutual aid in a flood being the oldest form of the strategy there is. The division is live, productive, and unresolved, and the programme's founder, who built his conferences out of unresolved divisions, would presumably have recognised the condition as health rather than decline.

## The seminar room test: teaching the topic against resistance

Anyone who has taught this material knows the moment it either catches or dies. A student, usually a good one, raises a hand and asks why the class is studying a programme whose own author conceded its central test could only be applied after the fact, whose flagship strategy has no completed example, and whose key term turns out to belong to somebody else. The question deserves the respect of a direct answer, and the answer is that the class is studying how a serious tradition behaves when it decides to be honest. Most doctrines protect themselves: their failures are blamed on circumstances, their terms shift quietly, their concessions are extracted by enemies rather than volunteered by authors. This programme published its opponents' best arguments inside its own founding volumes, credited its borrowed criterion to a predecessor the textbooks had forgotten, restated its strategy in plainer language when the first statement proved too easy to misread, and attached to its central judgement the evidence that would overturn it. Students who learn to demand those behaviours from every doctrine they meet, including the ones they love, have received the transferable part of the education, the part that survives forgetting whether the pathways numbered seven or nine. The content will fade, as content does. The standard remains, and it is a harsher standard than any the programme's critics apply to it, because the programme taught the standard to its own examiners first.

## What the programme asks of its readers: a closing inventory of demands

Programmes make demands, and this one's demands on its readers are unusually explicit, which is a final reason it rewards the attention this article has given it. It asks its sympathetic readers to surrender the comforts of negation: to stop treating the refusal to specify as rigour, and to accept that every design they favour will be damaged in public by people who know where designs break. It asks them to learn unfashionable skills, the reading of constitutions and budgets, the drafting of clauses, the patience of committee work, and to honour those skills as political capacities rather than as bureaucratic fall from grace. It asks them to grade their own victories by the jurisdiction they gain and the capacity they leave behind, a sterner accounting than crowds or coverage, and to report the grades honestly when they disappoint. It asks its hostile readers for something equally specific: to aim their criticism at the mechanisms, where the programme can be wounded, rather than at the vocabulary, where it cannot, and to state the evidence that would change their verdicts, as the programme's verdicts state theirs. And it asks all its readers to hold two thoughts that polemic prefers to separate: that the existing order's distribution of decision rights is a proper object of democratic complaint, and that complaining is the beginning of the work rather than the work itself.

The demands explain the article's own procedure, which has tried to follow the programme's method while examining it: steelman before verdict, mechanisms before slogans, concessions recorded where the texts record them, defeaters attached to every judgement that matters. A reader who finishes with the instruments in hand, the grading device, the criterion, the correspondence of the strategies, the catalogue of failure modes, and the inventory of unsolved problems, possesses what the programme exists to distribute. What such a reader builds with the instruments is not the programme's to say. That restraint, designed into the enterprise from its first conference, is either its deepest wisdom or its final evasion, and the article leaves the choice, as the programme does, to the associated judgement of its readers.

## For students and exam candidates

The examinable core of this article is a set of distinctions, and marks follow the distinctions rather than the narrative. Secure first the attribution: non-reformist reform is Gorz's concept, from the French text of the mid-1960s, in English in the 1967 translation Strategy for Labor, adopted and developed by Wright rather than coined by him. Secure second the compass: three powers, state, economic, and social, with socialism defined by making the first two answerable to the third, and the shift from ownership to decision stated as the project's namable claim. Secure third the two strategy schemes and their correspondence: ruptural, interstitial, and symbiotic in the synthesis, with erosion as the recommended combination, and the final formulation's five postures, running from smashing to escaping, with taming joined to escaping as the same recommendation restated. Secure fourth the criticisms in their strongest forms, absorption or marginality, taxonomy without prediction, under-specified social power, the missing adversary, and the renaming charge with the project's concession on the retrospective criterion. An answer that can state the concession accurately outranks an answer that can only applaud or denounce. The comparison with social democracy, pursued in depth in the companion article, should be cited on its stated basis: whether reforms move power itself or merely work inside its existing distribution. Keep the strategies table's grid in mind as the revision scaffold for the whole topic, and use the study tools collected at the [Marxism study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) page to rehearse the distinctions until the correspondences between the two schemes are automatic.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The article's richest teaching asset is the worked grading exercise: three reforms close in welfare and far apart in power, ranked in reverse order by the two calculi. Set it before the compass is explained and let the seminar discover the need for a second variable, then introduce the criterion as Gorz's answer to the need they have felt. The attribution correction makes a second exercise: distribute summaries that credit the term to Wright, as much of the secondary literature does, and have students trace the credit to the 1967 translation, attending to what the original argument contained that the adoption muted, the analysis of how much reform the adversary can digest, above all. The defeater discipline supplies the seminar's closing exercise: require every verdict offered in discussion to carry its defeater, the evidence that would reverse it, and grade the defeaters for specificity. The compass itself teaches best against a live institutional description the students know at first hand, a university, a hospital, a platform, graded dimension by dimension until the class experiences both the instrument's illumination and its refusal to aggregate into a single score. End with the renaming charge at full strength and the project's concession beside it; students who can hold both have learned the topic and a transferable intellectual habit at once.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

The research frontier this article leaves marked is empirical, and it can be stated as three commissions. First, the discriminator problem: the programme needs case work designed to find, in advance of outcomes, the features that distinguish reforms which entrenched associational jurisdiction from reforms which were digested, with the features stated as hypotheses before the cases are coded. Second, the adversary model: the counter mobilisation against encroachment needs its typology, its timing, its instruments, and its success conditions, built from the record of defeated beachheads as carefully as from the celebrated survivals. Third, the federation variable: the claim that federated interstitial sectors escape the absorption gradient where lone institutions succumb is the programme's most testable strategic assertion and deserves comparative treatment across sectors and legal regimes. Historians will find the project's own archive, the volumes with their commentaries, an unusually honest record of a tradition auditing its proposals in public. Journalists covering participatory governance, cooperative development, or income floor experiments will find the compass a serviceable interrogation device: ask of any celebrated reform which jurisdiction moved, whose seats were created, what the override costs, and whether the beneficiaries govern anything. The answers grade the story faster than the press release does.

## Conclusion: instruments in working order

The real utopias project asked the socialist tradition to grow up in a specific way: to stop treating the alternative as a destination to be proclaimed and start treating it as a set of institutions to be designed, tested, criticised, and built in the order the possible allows. Its compass reframed the tradition's central definition, relocating socialism from ownership to decision, and the reframing survives its critics because it changes judgements about real cases in directions the evidence supports. Its pathways anatomised the routes by which association can come to govern, and its strategies, threefold then fivefold, recommended the patient combination it called erosion, building in the margins while reforming from within under a criterion borrowed, with acknowledgment, from Gorz. The criticisms stand where this article has left them: the criterion reads the past better than the future, the adversary is undermodelled, the key concept is looser than its author's own standards, and no interstitial institution has yet met the defeater's condition at national scale. None of that dissolves the achievement. A tradition is fortunate when its most careful member leaves it better instruments than he found, together with an honest statement of what the instruments cannot do. The project's readers inherit both, and the argument about what to build with them is, by the project's own design, theirs to continue.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What are real utopias?

Real utopias are workable institutional designs, some operating already and others specified as proposals, which embody socialist principles and are examined one at a time rather than as a portrait of a whole society. Erik Olin Wright's project subjects each design to three examinations. Would it serve equality, democracy, and community if it functioned as intended? Would it go on functioning among people pursuing their own interests inside a hostile environment? And can a path be traced from arrangements that exist to the design proposed, step by step, through actors who might plausibly take those steps? Designs failing any examination are discarded or repaired in the project's own volumes.

### Q: Where does the term non-reformist reform come from?

The term originates with André Gorz, a French theorist and journalist, in a French language book of the mid-1960s, available to English readers in the 1967 Boston translation issued as Strategy for Labor. Gorz separated two logics of reform. One trims each demand to whatever the established order will tolerate, and he called its products reformist. The other begins from needs the order declares unrealistic, and shapes each demand so that winning it enlarges the winners' capacity to demand more; its products he called non-reformist. Wright borrowed the distinction decades later, credited its source in print, and made it the selection rule of his symbiotic strategy, yet secondary summaries persist in crediting the borrower.

### Q: What is the socialist compass?

The socialist compass is a device for grading how far any institution, sector, or society subordinates two kinds of power to a third. Rule making backed by coercion is the state's kind. Command over investment, hiring, and livelihoods, flowing from possession of productive assets, is the economic kind. The third kind belongs to association: organised people acting together through bodies they join and leave freely. A setting counts as socialist, on the device, in the measure that the associational kind directs the other two. Because the grading concerns direction and degree, an arrangement can register movement without any change in legal ownership, which is the reframing the whole project rests on.

### Q: What are the seven pathways to social empowerment?

Each pathway names a mechanism by which association gains leverage over the state or over capital. One route captures the state itself and governs the economy through it, accepting the danger that the apparatus becomes master. A second leaves ownership untouched and binds capital through labour law and welfare won electorally. A third writes organised groups directly into governing bodies with votes that bind. A fourth accumulates funds, held for wage earners collectively, whose shareholdings carry boardroom weight. A fifth builds a whole sector of enterprises governed by their members. A sixth imagines that member governed form becoming the market's norm. A seventh entrusts coordination to nested councils of producers and communities, planning iteratively from below.

### Q: What are the three strategies of transformation?

The synthesis separates strategies according to how each treats the institutions already in place. One treats them as a fortress to be stormed: break the apparatus, then build afresh, the ruptural path. Another treats the order as a landscape full of unguarded ground, where alternative institutions can be planted, linked, and thickened until they amount to a sector, the interstitial path. The third treats the order's machinery as usable: accept reforms the powerful want for their own reasons, but draft each so that it leaves organised people with more jurisdiction than before, the symbiotic path. Wright's counsel joins the second and third, a posture he calls erosion, and sets the first aside as implausible under the conditions his argument addresses.

### Q: What does eroding capitalism mean?

Erosion is the project's metaphor for transformation without a founding moment. Geological formations are not stormed; they are worn at their exposed edges by innumerable small actions, each trivial, jointly decisive. Alternative institutions working in the margins supply the wearing action, reforms drafted under Gorz's criterion alter the weather around the formation, and organised defence meets the counterattacks when they come. Nothing in the metaphor promises success, sets a date, or appoints a director. It asserts only that configurations dominated by ownership can be shifted, degree by degree, toward configurations answerable to association, and that the shifting is work identifiable people can do.

### Q: What is social power?

Social power is the leverage people possess when they are organised: when a durable body with members, procedures, and resources can turn shared grievances or purposes into coordinated action, and can credibly threaten or promise such action in a negotiation. It differs from opinion, which needs no organisation, and from wealth and office, which are held individually or by appointment. A tenants' body that can fill a hearing, a union that can stop a workplace, a cooperative federation that can withhold or supply cooperation: each holds this kind of power in measurable degree. The project adds conditions before grading it as socialist in effect, chiefly connection to real decision rights and democratic government inside the association itself.

### Q: Which institutions does the project examine?

The founding volumes each took one institutional proposal and argued it to pieces in public. The subjects were a scheme giving organised groups formal seats in governing sectoral bodies; a coupon arrangement separating the income from capital ownership and paying it to every citizen as a dividend; a yearly cycle in which residents of a locality deliberate and vote over a slice of public investment, with the result binding the administration; an income paid to each person without conditions or means tests; and a family of measures redistributing paid work and unpaid care between women and men, from leave portions reserved for each parent to pension credit for years spent caring. Cooperatives and collectively held investment funds joined the catalogue in the synthesis.

### Q: What are the criticisms of real utopias?

The objections cluster at the strategy doctrine. Alternative institutions planted in the margins either grow by surrendering their constitutions to market discipline or stay pure by staying tiny, and no mechanism is supplied that reliably yields growth with the constitution intact. The compass sorts the world elegantly but foretells nothing, a taxonomy wearing a theory's clothes. Its central notion, the power of association, is drawn too loosely to separate emancipatory organisation from organised selfishness or worse. The advance is described in detail while the counterattack of those losing power is sketched, though they can read the same map. And behind the cluster stands the oldest charge of all, that the whole vocabulary renames social democratic practice.

### Q: Is this just social democracy renamed?

Critics formed by Rosa Luxemburg's attack on Eduard Bernstein argue that it is: that bargaining inside the state and building alternatives in its shadow describes a century of social democratic conduct, and that a fresh label alters no conduct. The project's rejoinder grants the resemblance and disputes the identity. Its grading device separates, inside that shared conduct, reforms which merely deliver benefits from reforms which hand organised people jurisdiction they retain, and the historical record's own pattern, governed entitlements outlasting administered benefits when governments changed, is offered as evidence the separation tracks something real. The rejoinder ends in a concession the critics are entitled to quote: the separation can be drawn with confidence only after outcomes are known.

### Q: How does the compass reframing grade the strategies for change?

It grades them by a single question: which kind of power ends up directing which, once the strategy has done its work? Judged by that question, the storming strategy fails on its own historical evidence, since its victories delivered societies directed by state apparatuses with association dissolved. Planting alternatives passes on direction while failing, so far, on scale. Bargaining inside the machinery passes or fails case by case, according to the drafting: jurisdiction handed over counts, benefits alone do not. The combined posture of erosion is recommended precisely because it is the only one whose every element points the same way on the dial, whatever its unproven power to move whole societies.

### Q: What should a student separate between a reform that extends a system and one that shifts its balance of power?

Separate the gift from the lever. A reform extending a system hands people something valuable, money, services, protection, while every decision right stays exactly where it was; the gift can be trimmed or withdrawn by whoever administers it, and its recipients meet the administrator as clients. A reform shifting the balance hands people a lever as well: a voting seat on the body that governs the service, a fund their organisations direct, an entitlement administered under rules they help set. The lever outlasts the gift because it manufactures defenders with standing. Gorz's distinction, which the student should cite to its author, is precisely the discipline of asking after the lever whenever a gift is announced.

### Q: Why do interstitial institutions risk absorption?

The risk is structural rather than moral, which is why good intentions do not insure against it. A democratically governed enterprise must buy, borrow, hire, and sell in markets populated by rivals carrying none of its constitutional overheads. Matching their prices tempts it toward their methods: wage labour outside the membership, managerial salaries at market rates, outside capital with control attached. Refusing every temptation protects the constitution by forfeiting growth, the marginal fate. Federation is the literature's proposed escape, since a network supplying its own credit, training, and customers softens the pressure any single member faces, but the escape redistributes the risk rather than abolishing it.

### Q: How does the strategies table order the routes to transformation?

The table sorts the routes by their stance toward existing institutions. At one end sits confrontation, which would demolish them and begin again. Next comes construction in the gaps they leave, indifferent to their permission. Then comes employment of the machinery itself, accepting its agenda in order to amend its outputs and, under the criterion, its distribution of jurisdiction. The recommended combination closes the grid, yoking construction in the gaps to employment of the machinery, with organised protest assigned to guard both. A final column translates between the synthesis's three names and the five names of the author's last book, since the later vocabulary divides one earlier category and renames the rest.

### Q: What separates a real utopia from a policy proposal?

A policy proposal argues from consequences inside the order as it stands: so much welfare gained, so much revenue spent, so much harm prevented. A candidate real utopia must argue on that ground too, and then on a second ground the first never visits. It must show what it does to the distribution of decision rights, whether organised people emerge from it with more governing capacity than they had, durably enough to matter when administrations change. It must also state how it could fail, since the project's volumes treat failure analysis as part of a design rather than as disloyalty to it. Plenty of admirable policies fail the second examination, and the taxonomy exists so that nobody mistakes the two achievements.
