Few propositions in political economy carry as much weight, or attract as much argument, as Marx's claim that the rate of profit has a built in tendency to fall. Supporters treat the claim as the deepest insight in his economics, the point where the system's own success turns against its measure of success. Detractors treat the same claim as the weakest plank in the structure, an assertion dressed as a derivation. Between those poles sits a text that is stranger than either camp usually admits: a law stated together with the forces that work against it, in chapters assembled after its author's death by an editor working from an unfinished manuscript. A reader who wants to judge the argument has to hold all three elements at once, the mechanism, the counterweights, and the editorial history, because each one changes what the others can honestly be said to mean.

That triple task is the work of the pages ahead. The mechanism comes first, stated as the ratio it is, with the arithmetic made explicit enough that a reader can test each step rather than take the conclusion on trust. The counteracting influences come second, all six of them, named and examined one by one, because Marx placed them beside the law itself and any account that separates the two has already distorted its source. The editorial question comes third: the chapter divisions and headings that make the material read like a finished system were supplied by Friedrich Engels, not by Marx, and scholarship has argued hard over what that fact does to the law's standing. Only after those three passes does this guide turn to the objections, including the charge that the law is built so that no evidence could ever count against it.

## The claim in one ratio: how the rate of profit is built

Strip the argument to its skeleton and a single fraction remains. A capitalist advances money in two directions. One part buys means of production: buildings, machinery, raw materials, fuel, components. Marx calls this constant capital, because in his account its value passes into the product unchanged in size. The other part buys labour power, the capacity to work, and he calls this variable capital, because labour in action adds more value than the wage that purchased it. The excess is surplus value. The rate of profit then measures that surplus against the whole advance, constant and variable together, since the capitalist reckons gain against everything tied up in the enterprise, not against the wage bill alone.

Write the surplus as s, the constant portion as c, and the variable portion as v, and the rate of profit is s divided by c plus v. Every movement in the argument that follows operates on one of those three letters. A change in technology moves c relative to v. A change in working time or intensity moves s relative to v. A change in the prices of machines and materials moves c without any physical change at all. Much of the confusion surrounding the law dissolves once a reader insists on asking, of every claim made about it, which letter is alleged to have moved, in which direction, and by what cause. Polemics that skip that question trade in atmosphere. The ratio permits no atmosphere.

A second form of the fraction does the real analytical work. Divide top and bottom by v, and the rate of profit becomes the rate of surplus value, s over v, divided by one plus the ratio of constant to variable capital, c over v. That second ratio Marx calls the organic composition of capital, when it reflects the underlying technical arrangement of production. In this form the law becomes a race between two quantities. The rate of surplus value expresses how hard labour is being squeezed relative to its own cost. The composition expresses how much dead, embodied labour in plant and materials each living worker sets in motion. Should the denominator's composition grow persistently faster than the numerator's rate of exploitation, the overall rate sinks, even while workers are being exploited more intensely than before.

Nothing in the formula alone guarantees the outcome of that race, and this guide will not pretend otherwise. A formula can only show what must be true if one side outruns the other. Marx's substantive wager is about the direction capitalist development pushes the two quantities over long stretches: mechanisation, he argues, swells the mass of means of production per worker as a condition of raising productivity, while the rate of surplus value, though it can climb impressively, presses against limits set by the length of the working day, the physical needs of the worker, and social resistance. Whether that wager is sound is exactly what the debate article in this series examines; the task here is narrower and prior, namely to state the mechanism so precisely that assessment becomes possible. Readers who want the distinction between profit and surplus value itself drawn carefully can find it in the earlier guide to [surplus value vs profit](/2011/02/15/surplus-value-vs-profit/), which treats the ratios at the level this one builds on.

### What exactly is being divided by what?

The rate of profit divides surplus value, the new value workers add beyond the value of their own labour power, by the total capital advanced: constant capital spent on means of production plus variable capital spent on wages. Measuring gain against the whole advance, rather than wages alone, separates this ratio from the rate of surplus value.

A worked illustration fixes the point better than definitions can. Imagine a firm, in round invented numbers chosen purely for clarity, advancing 80 units in plant and materials and 20 in wages over a production period, with the workers adding 40 units of new value, of which 20 replaces their wages and 20 remains as surplus. The rate of surplus value stands at 100 percent, twenty over twenty. The rate of profit stands at 20 percent, twenty over the full hundred advanced. Years of mechanisation later, suppose the same firm advances 180 in plant and materials and still 20 in wages, while harder and more productive labour adds 50 of new value, 20 replacing wages and 30 remaining as surplus. Exploitation has risen by half, the mass of profit has risen by half, and the rate of profit has fallen to 15 percent, because thirty divided by two hundred is smaller than twenty divided by one hundred. Every party to the long dispute over this law is, at bottom, arguing about whether the pattern inside that illustration describes a real compulsion of the system or an artefact of chosen numbers.

## Where the argument lives in the text

Placement matters because readers often meet the law as a free floating slogan, detached from the book that houses it. The material appears in the third volume of Capital, the volume Engels assembled and published in 1894, eleven years after Marx's death, from manuscripts left in varying states of finish. Within that volume it occupies the third part, which Engels's edition presents as three chapters: one on the law as such, one on the influences that counteract it, and one on the unfolding of the law's internal contradictions, numbered thirteen, fourteen, and fifteen in the standard editions. The main underlying manuscript dated from the middle 1860s, roughly two decades before publication, and Marx never prepared this portion for the press himself.

That provenance carries two consequences a careful reader keeps separate. First, the argument belongs to Marx's mature economic work, drafted after the first volume's analysis of production and alongside the studies that fed the second volume on circulation; it is not a youthful speculation. Second, its literary form, the very look of a doctrine proceeding from law through counterweights to contradictions, is an editorial construction, because the draft carried no chapter divisions or headings of its own. Engels said plainly enough in his preface that editing the third volume had required substantial work of arrangement, though neither he nor his first readers could easily gauge how much interpretive weight the arrangement itself would come to bear. The full story of how the two men's hands divide across the joint corpus is told in the companion piece on the [marx engels authorship question](/2013/06/01/marx-engels-authorship-question/), and it repays reading alongside this one.

Earlier sightings of the idea exist, and they matter for judging how central Marx took it to be. The notebooks of the late 1850s published as the Grundrisse already contain the argument in embryo, complete with a judgement on its rank that the FAQ section below takes up. Between those notebooks and the third volume's draft, the reasoning was refined rather than replaced: the composition of capital acquired its name and its careful definition, the counteracting influences were gathered and ordered, and the connection to crises, handled in the third chapter of the part, grew more explicit. What never changed across the versions is the discipline this guide calls the same-part rule. In every mature statement, the pressures that retard or reverse the fall are presented as internal to the analysis, not as concessions extracted by critics after the fact.

A word on vocabulary will save confusion later. Marx's translators render his key term as the organic composition of capital, and his German carries a deliberate double sense: the composition is technical, a physical ratio between equipment and workers on a shop floor, and it is also a value ratio between two sums of money, and the argument moves between those senses in ways Section Four below untangles. Likewise the word law in this neighbourhood is treacherous, since Marx uses it for a pressure that operates through opposing pressures, a usage closer to a law of motion in the older political economy than to a statute or a physical necessity. Readers who arrive expecting the word to promise an exceptionless regularity will misread the text before finishing a page.

## The classical inheritance: falling profit before Marx

Marx did not invent anxiety about falling profits; he inherited it from the classical economists and relocated its cause, and knowing the older versions sharpens what is distinctive in his. Adam Smith had attributed a secular decline in profit to the accumulation of stock itself: as capitals multiply, competition among their owners bids down the return, a story that needs no machinery and no exploitation, only crowding. David Ricardo gave the tendency a sterner mechanism. In his system profit is squeezed from the other side, by wages: population growth forces cultivation onto worse land, food grows dearer, money wages must rise to keep workers at subsistence, and the surplus left for profit shrinks toward the stationary state that John Stuart Mill later contemplated with surprising equanimity, even approval. In the Ricardian theatre the villain is nature's diminishing returns in agriculture, and the falling rate is a tragedy of soil.

Marx's relocation is total. The pressure he describes originates inside industry itself, in the very productivity growth that classical optimism celebrated, and it operates even if food cheapens, even if real wages rise, even if new lands and new continents abolish diminishing returns at the margin. Where Ricardo's rate falls because labour becomes more expensive to feed, Marx's falls because labour becomes a shrinking fraction of the advance, displaced by the dead labour embodied in equipment. The two theories can even be distinguished in their attitude toward machinery: for the classicals, machinery that cheapens wage goods rescues profit by relieving the wage squeeze; for Marx, that same machinery is the prime suspect in the denominator's growth, whatever relief it brings the numerator. Later economists mostly abandoned the question, marginalist theory dissolving the general rate into firm level returns and interest into a price of time or abstinence, which is one reason the falling rate survived the twentieth century largely as a Marxist heirloom, tended by a tradition that kept the classical habit of asking about the system's aggregate proportions. When the question resurfaced in mainstream dress, in arguments about secular stagnation, declining equilibrium interest rates, and the falling labour share, the family resemblance was rarely acknowledged, though economists of stagnation would find the older literature, classical and Marx alike, more familiar than they might expect. Exposition gains from the genealogy because it shows the law answering a standing problem of political economy, the long run fate of the return on accumulation, with a mechanism no predecessor had proposed.

## A guided pass through the three chapters

Readers who take up the part itself, rather than summaries of it, deserve a map of what each chapter actually does, since the three perform different operations and are often cited as if they performed one.

The first chapter builds the law by controlled variation. Marx begins from the ratio in its rewritten form and varies one magnitude at a time: hold the rate of surplus value steady and let the composition climb, and the rate sinks in exact proportion; then let the rate of surplus value climb too, and trace the conditions under which the fall survives, slows, or reverses. The style is closer to a laboratory notebook than to a sermon. Cases are enumerated, magnitudes are pushed to extremes to expose the structure, and the chapter keeps returning to the point that a given percentage change in composition outweighs the same percentage change in exploitation once composition stands high, because the denominator carries the accumulated weight of all past mechanisation while the numerator starts each period afresh. Buried in this austere exercise is the text's most quoted conceptual move: the identification of the falling rate with the rising productivity of labour itself, two descriptions, Marx argues, of a single process viewed from different ends. Development of the productive forces is the glory of the system on one page and the solvent of its profit rate on the next, and the doubleness is deliberate.

The second chapter changes register entirely. Where the first abstracts, the second inventories, gathering the counteracting influences as a naturalist gathers specimens, with historical asides and practical observations that read almost like field notes from decades of watching British industry. Its brevity relative to its neighbours misleads many readers into treating it as an appendix, yet its placement is the argument: the inventory stands inside the exposition, before any crisis is mentioned, so that no subsequent use of the law can plead ignorance of the offsets. Teachers who assign only the first and third chapters have, whatever their intentions, edited the doctrine back into the caricature the same-part rule forbids.

The third chapter is the longest and the wildest, and its subject is what happens when the pressure the first chapter derived meets the resistance the second catalogued. Marx follows the falling rate into its consequences: capital accumulates beyond the point where the available workforce can valorise it at the accustomed rate, producing the paradox of excess capital alongside a surplus population, too much of both factors and no profitable way to combine them. Existing capital is devalued as newer, cheaper methods destroy the worth of older plant before its time, a process he calls moral depreciation when it strikes through obsolescence rather than wear. The profit squeezed out of production is fought over by fractions of the possessing class, industrial, commercial, financial, landed, and the state, each claimant's gain tightening the others' margins. Overproduction of capital, carefully distinguished from overproduction of goods, names the condition in which additional investment would lower rather than raise the mass of profit, so accumulation stalls not for want of opportunities in the physical sense but for want of profitable ones. The chapter breaks off in the manuscript amid these explorations, which is one reason its status as a finished crisis theory should be handled with the caution the editorial section below develops. What it unquestionably supplies is the bridge between an abstract ratio and the lived rhythm of boom, overreach, and breakdown that nineteenth century readers knew from experience and that gave the law its imaginative grip.

A practical reading order follows from the three chapters' different characters. Beginners should read the second chapter first, since the inventory of counterweights is the most concrete writing in the part and anchors everything else in observable mechanisms; then the first chapter, slowly, with pencil work on the cases, until the formula's behaviour feels inevitable rather than asserted; then the third, read as a set of explorations rather than as a finished theory, with permission to find it fragmentary, because it is. That order reverses the printed one for a reason: readers who begin with the law's derivation tend to memorise a slogan and then skim the offsets as fine print, reproducing in miniature the deformation the same-part rule exists to prevent, whereas readers who begin with the offsets meet the law already knowing the company it keeps. Teachers who adopt the sequence report, in the experience this series has gathered, that the seminar's quality changes at once, because the first question from the floor stops being whether the rate must fall and becomes which mechanism did what in a given decade, a question a room can actually work on. The text rewards being read against its own grain, and an editor's architecture, however useful as a map, should never be mistaken for the territory's own roads.

## The part within the volume: neighbours in the third book

The third part sits inside a larger architecture, and its meaning shifts slightly when the neighbours are kept in view. The volume's first two parts build the world the law inhabits: surplus value is transformed into profit, profit into average profit, and commodities acquire prices of production that systematically deviate from their values so that the equalisation described earlier can proceed. Only once that machinery exists does the falling tendency become statable, because the general rate whose fall is at issue is itself a product of the transformation; in a world without equalisation there would be sectoral rates in plenty and no single magnitude for a tendency to act upon. The later parts then follow the surplus into its subdivisions: merchant's profit and interest in the great central parts on commercial and interest bearing capital, ground rent in the part on landed property, and the revenues and their sources in the closing part, where the trinity formula of capital interest, land rent, and labour wages is dissected as the everyday religion of the system's appearance. Read against that itinerary, the third part is the hinge: the last place where the analysis treats the total surplus against the total capital before the split into competing revenue streams begins to govern perception. The law's invisibility to participants, treated in the credit section below, is thus no accident of inattention; it is scheduled by the book's own structure, in which the magnitude the law describes exists purely at the level of the whole and is dismantled, for every practical purpose, by the time income reaches anyone's ledger.

One neighbour deserves a sentence of its own, because confusion between the two is endemic. The volume also discusses the rate of interest and remarks on its behaviour across development, yet the two rates obey different logics in Marx's treatment: interest is a division of profit settled by the competition between lenders and borrowers, with no natural level, while the profit rate is set in production by composition and exploitation. A falling yield on bonds therefore neither confirms nor illustrates the falling tendency of profit, and arguments that slide between the two have left the reservation the scope rules mark out.

## The mechanism, step by step

Begin where Marx begins, with a single capital and a single change. Productivity in some branch of industry rises because a new machine, or a reorganised line, lets the same crew turn out more product per hour. The innovating firm enjoys a temporary advantage: its costs per unit sit below the social average while its prices still answer to that average, so its individual rate of profit swells. Rivals face a choice between adopting the same equipment and losing their market. Adoption spreads. As it spreads, two things happen together. The branch's total outlay tilts toward plant and materials, since each worker now tends more machinery, handles more throughput, and consumes more raw material per shift. And the temporary advantage evaporates, because the social average itself has moved to the new method, dragging prices down toward the new costs.

Generalise that sequence across the whole economy and the law's engine is visible. Competition does not merely permit mechanisation; it punishes refusal. Each firm experiences the decision as a defence of its own margin, yet the aggregate outcome is a rising mass of constant capital relative to the labour set in motion. Marx insists on this indirect route for a reason. The law is not a claim that capitalists aim at a lower rate of profit, which would be absurd, nor a claim that any single innovation must depress the general rate, which would be false. It is a claim about the unintended sum of individually rational moves: survival strategies that each protect one balance sheet while jointly transforming the proportions of the total social capital.

Set against that engine, the counterweight operates on the numerator. Rising productivity cheapens wage goods, the food, clothing, and housing workers buy, so the share of the working day needed to reproduce the wage shrinks and the surplus share grows, even at an unchanged working day and unchanged real living standard. Marx calls this relative surplus value, and its production is the subject of the earlier study of [absolute and relative surplus value](/2010/11/15/absolute-and-relative-surplus-value/), which traces how lengthening the day and cheapening the wage basket differ as strategies. The same technical change that bloats the denominator also fattens the numerator, and the race described in Section One is precisely between these twin offspring of mechanisation. Nobody honest can settle that race by pointing at the formula. What the formula supplies is the bookkeeping: it tells disputants exactly which magnitudes their empirical and theoretical claims must be about.

Two features of the mechanism deserve emphasis because summaries habitually drop them. The first is the time horizon. Marx writes about a movement across the long development of the capitalist mode of production, through cycles of boom and slump, not about year to year wiggles in anyone's accounts. Short runs can move in any direction, and the text expects them to. The second is the level of analysis. The rate under discussion belongs to the total social capital, an aggregate no firm observes directly. Individual sectors routinely defy the aggregate trend for decades, because composition differs enormously between, say, software services and steelmaking, and capital migrates toward the higher returns, partially levelling rates through the equalisation process the third volume describes elsewhere. A sector's healthy margins therefore neither confirm nor refute a proposition about the whole.

### Why does machinery enlarge the denominator faster than the numerator?

Machinery raises the mass of plant, energy, and materials each worker handles, so constant capital per worker climbs as a condition of higher output per head. The numerator grows only through a longer or denser working day, or cheaper wage goods shrinking necessary labour, and those routes meet physical and social limits equipment spending avoids.

Notice what the mechanism does not require. It does not require wages to fall, in money or in goods; workers can grow better off while the composition rises around them. It does not require profits to shrink in total; the illustration in Section One had the mass climbing even as the rate descended. It does not require any capitalist to behave foolishly, nor any conspiracy, nor any external shock. And it does not rest on prices being forced down by competition among sellers of the same commodity, the popular muddle the misreadings section below addresses. The whole weight rests on proportions: on how the advance divides between dead labour and living labour as accumulation proceeds, and on the asymmetry Marx alleges between the ease of multiplying equipment and the difficulty of stretching the surplus share of a finite working day without limit.

## The chapter's own arithmetic: how the law chapter argues

It is worth watching the law chapter argue in its native style, because the style explains both its persuasiveness and the objections it provoked. The chapter proceeds by arraying illustrative capitals of equal total size, each divided differently between constant and variable portions, and each worked at the same rate of surplus value, so that the reader watches the rate of profit sink as the variable share shrinks: a capital split evenly yields a rate of fifty percent when exploitation stands at a hundred, since the surplus equals the wage half of the advance; shift the split so that wages form forty of the hundred and the rate falls to forty; at thirty of wages, thirty; at twenty, twenty; at a mere five of wages against ninety five of plant and materials, the same hundred percent exploitation returns a rate of five percent on the whole. The sequence is arithmetic, not evidence, and Marx knows it: the table demonstrates what the formula means, displaying the denominator's leverage in its purest form, before the text complicates the picture by letting the rate of surplus value vary and by asking what magnitudes the real development of industry actually supplies.

Two features of that argumentative style deserve the reader's permanent attention. The first is its honesty of construction: every number in the chapter is an assumption labelled by its place in a case, and no illustrative figure is ever dressed as a measurement, a discipline this guide has imitated in its own worked examples and one that popularisers of the law abandon at their readers' peril. The second is the precise location where demonstration ends and assertion begins. The tables prove that if composition rises faster than exploitation, the rate falls, and they cannot prove the antecedent; for that, the chapter appeals to the general account of accumulation developed across the whole work, the competitive compulsion to mechanise, the limits of the working day, the rising scale of plant. A reader who sees exactly where the proof stops will understand why the mechanical objection, which attacks the antecedent rather than the arithmetic, is the serious objection, and why generations of skirmishing over the tables themselves were skirmishing over the wrong ground. The chapter, read slowly, teaches its own best criticism, which may be the surest sign that its author understood the difference between a tendency and a theorem even when his editor's headings, and his followers' slogans, conspired to blur it.

## Reading the formula like an economist

The rewritten fraction rewards a closer, almost mathematical reading, because several disputes dissolve once its sensitivities are understood. Treat the rate of profit as depending on two variables, the rate of surplus value and the composition. A small rise in the composition lowers the rate by an amount that grows with the composition's existing level: when composition is low, early mechanisation barely dents profitability, since the denominator is still dominated by the wage bill, but when composition stands at five or ten, each further increment strikes a rate already thinned by all the increments before it. Sensitivity to exploitation runs the other way. A given percentage rise in the rate of surplus value lifts the rate of profit proportionally, whatever the composition, which sounds like parity until the bounds are remembered: exploitation is a share and cannot exceed the whole working day, while composition is a ratio of two stocks and answers to no such ceiling. The asymmetry the mechanical objection debates lives precisely here, in the difference between a variable bounded by a day and a variable bounded by nothing in the formula at all.

A second numerical experiment shows the third counterweight earning its keep. Begin from the illustration of Section One's later stage, with plant and materials at 180, wages at 20, and surplus at 30, giving a rate of 15 percent. Now let a wave of cheapening in the machine making and materials industries cut the value of that same physical equipment by a quarter, so the advance in constant capital falls to 135 while every machine still stands on the floor and every tonne still arrives. The rate recovers to just above 19 percent, with no change in technology in this firm, no change in its workers' effort, purely through revaluation of its inputs. Run the experiment in reverse, an era of dearer energy and materials, and the rate sinks without any new mechanisation at all. The lesson generalises uncomfortably for all parties: the statistic everyone fights over is hostage to valuation currents that have their own history, and a movement in the measured rate, up or down, is the beginning of an inquiry, never its conclusion. That is why the serious literature decomposes before it celebrates or mourns, separating what composition did from what prices did, and why single series brandished without decomposition should be read as rhetoric whatever their source.

## One economy in three acts: a longer worked example

Abstract races become intuitive when a single imaginary economy is followed across a long life, so consider a stylised economy in three acts, with invented round numbers throughout, chosen for transparency rather than realism. In the first act, its capitals advance 400 in plant and materials and 200 in wages, and their workers produce 200 of surplus: the rate of profit stands at 200 over 600, one third, and the rate of surplus value at 100 percent. Accumulation proceeds, driven by competition exactly as the mechanism describes. By the second act the advance has grown to 1,600 in constant capital and 400 in wages, employment has doubled, and harder, more productive labour yields 500 of surplus. Every absolute magnitude that participants watch has improved beyond recognition: output, employment, and the mass of profit are all far larger, exploitation has risen to 125 percent, and yet the rate of profit has slipped to 500 over 2,000, one quarter. No villainy, no shock, no policy error has intervened; the composition quadrupled its lead over the wage bill while exploitation added only a quarter to its share, and the arithmetic did the rest.

In the third act the offsets take the stage, as history insists they do. A generation of cheapening in the industries supplying equipment cuts the value of the vastly larger physical stock by a fifth, so constant capital enters the books at 1,280 rather than 1,600; trade integration cheapens the wage basket, lifting the rate of surplus value to 150 percent on the same wage advance of 400, which now supports a surplus of 600. The rate recovers to 600 over 1,680, a little under 36 percent, above even the first act's figure. A chronicler who sampled only the third act's endpoint would pronounce the tendency dead, and would be answering, once again, a claim the text never made. A chronicler equipped with the decomposition sees a composition effect that subtracted massively, a cheapening effect that added much of it back, and an exploitation effect that added more, with the net figure a resultant rather than a refutation. The three acts are the article in parable form: the tendency as pressure, the offsets as real forces with histories of their own, and the visible rate as a treaty signed by enemies who never stop fighting.

## From one capital to the general rate

A gap in most summaries deserves filling before the composition gets its own section, because readers reasonably ask how a ratio defined for an individual firm becomes a property of an economy. Marx's third volume answers with the equalisation process. Capitals of equal size in different branches produce wildly different surpluses, since branches employ different proportions of living labour, yet competition, acting through the migration of investment toward higher returns and away from lower ones, tends to redistribute the total surplus so that each capital draws profit roughly in proportion to its size. Prices adjust to make that redistribution real: commodities exchange at prices of production that guarantee the average rate to capitals of average composition, transferring surplus from labour intensive branches to capital intensive ones along the way. The general rate of profit is the pivot of that whole machinery, the average around which the transfers organise themselves.

Two consequences follow for the falling tendency. First, the relevant composition is the aggregate's, built from every branch weighted by scale, so arguments citing the profitability of any single glamorous sector, however genuine its margins, address a magnitude the law does not describe. Second, equalisation means the fall, if it comes, arrives as a shared condition rather than as a punishment visited on the most mechanised firms; indeed the most mechanised may be best placed, since they receive transfers through the price system and enjoy the lowest unit costs, while capitals stuck with obsolete compositions suffer doubly. This inversion, the pioneers of mechanisation prospering individually while the average they helped to move sinks beneath everyone, is the distributional irony at the centre of the doctrine, and it explains why no boardroom ever votes against the process that produces it. Each participant experiences adoption as rescue. Only the statistician of the whole sees the tide going out.

The equalisation picture also clarifies what kind of evidence could even bear on the law. A society's general rate is not printed anywhere; it must be constructed from national accounts by decisions about what counts as capital, which incomes count as profit, and how to treat sectors like finance, government, and housing whose outputs resist the categories. Different construction crews, equally competent, have produced falling, flat, and rising series for the same economies across the same decades, which is why this guide keeps the empirical question quarantined in its own article rather than letting it leak into exposition. The concept, though, is not elusive: it names the average return the price system enforces on capital as such, stripped of the accidents of branch and firm, and every participant in the dispute, Okishio included, accepts that the concept is coherent enough to argue about. Coherence of the target is the minimum exposition must secure, and on that minimum the tradition and its critics quietly agree.

## The organic composition, examined closely

Because the denominator carries the argument, its central concept deserves a section of its own. Marx distinguishes two ways of describing how production is arranged. The technical composition is a physical relation: spindles per spinner, tonnes of throughput per refinery hand, servers per administrator. The value composition is a monetary relation: the money laid out on means of production divided by the money laid out on wages. Where the value ratio mirrors the technical one, where it rises because the physical arrangement genuinely changed rather than because prices wobbled, Marx speaks of the organic composition. The adjective signals that the value figure is anchored in the organism of production itself, not in market noise.

That anchoring clause does heavy lifting and attracts heavy criticism, so it should be stated plainly. If steel doubles in price while a mill's layout stays fixed, the value composition jumps without any organic change, and the law's mechanism has not advanced one step; dearer inputs raise c on paper while the process that supposedly drives the fall, mechanisation, stands still. Conversely, if computers grow cheaper every year, a workplace can transform technically while its value composition barely moves, because each new generation of equipment costs less than the capacity it adds. Marx knew this second possibility intimately: cheapening of the elements of constant capital is his own third counteracting influence, and it works exactly through this gap between technical and value terms. A rigorous statement of the law therefore concerns the value ratio insofar as it registers technical change, and the measurement headaches that follow are real. The FAQ section returns to how the ratio can be operationalised at all.

Aggregation adds a further twist. The composition of the total social capital is an average across branches whose individual ratios differ wildly, weighted by their size. That average can rise for two distinct reasons: because techniques inside branches mechanise, or because the economy's centre of gravity shifts toward branches that were always equipment heavy. Both movements lift the aggregate denominator, but they are not the same process, and Marx's mechanism, competition driven adoption within a trade, speaks most directly to the first. Careful empirical workers in the debate literature have tried to separate the two effects, with divergent results that belong to the evidence discussion rather than here. For exposition the point to retain is simpler: composition names a relation, not a thing, and every use of it should prompt the question, relation between what, measured how, over which population of capitals.

There is also a floor and ceiling logic worth making explicit. The value composition cannot fall below zero, and in practice branches differ in how far mechanisation can run before living labour nearly vanishes from the immediate process, as in a lights out warehouse that still needs design, maintenance, logistics, and supervision somewhere in its chain. The rate of surplus value, meanwhile, has its own arithmetic boundaries. Necessary labour cannot shrink below the time needed to produce a subsistence basket, however defined by custom and struggle, and the working day cannot stretch past twenty four hours, of which the worker must surrender a portion to sleep and recovery on pain of destroying the labour power the system needs tomorrow. Marx leans on exactly this asymmetry: equipment per worker faces no analogous natural cap within the system's logic, while the surplus share of the day is squeezed between two hard edges. Critics reply that the cap on composition is set by economics rather than nature, since equipment must still pay its way, and that reply, too, is taken up in the objections below.

## Economy in the use of constant capital: the denominator fights back

Before the law is even stated, the third volume devotes a chapter to a phenomenon that functions as an unofficial counterweight, and completeness requires its inclusion: the economy in the use of constant capital, the systematic effort to make a given advance of plant and materials serve more production. Marx catalogues its forms with an engineer's relish. Concentration itself cheapens the fixed plant per unit of output, since one large establishment's buildings, power source, and transport links serve a throughput that would have required the same overheads multiplied across small workshops. Inventions that economise on motive power, on transmission, on the wear of machinery, all shrink the value consumed per unit of product. The excretions of production, waste in one branch, become inputs to another, so that the chemical industries of his century were already building fortunes on refuse their suppliers had paid to remove. And, in the chapter's darkest pages, capital economises on the conditions of labour itself, crowding workers into costly saving, under ventilated, under lit space, skimping the safety expenditures whose absence returns as injury and disease, an economy whose price is paid in bodies and which Marx records with controlled fury.

The bearing on the tendency is direct. These economies attack the denominator's growth from inside production, not through market prices but through the physical efficiency with which capital is used, and unlike the cheapening that arrives from supplier industries, they are available to each capital through its own exertions, which is why managers experience them as the daily substance of their craft. Why Marx did not promote the whole complex into the counteracting influences chapter is a fair textual question; the likeliest answer is architectural, since the economy chapter belongs to the volume's first part, on profit in general, while the six influences were gathered as the law's specific companions, but the boundary is the editor's and author's joint convenience rather than a law of nature. Exposition should therefore register the economy of constant capital as a seventh force in substance, unnamed in the canonical six, and a standing reminder that the list's authority is textual rather than metaphysical: it binds because Marx wrote it there, not because reality promised to stop at six.

## The working day: the numerator's political limit

The numerator's ceiling, invoked abstractly in the mechanism sections, has a concrete political history that shows what a limit looks like when a society reaches it. The struggle over the length of the working day, chronicled in the first volume's most famous chapter, began with capital's drive to extend hours toward the physical maximum and met, first, the resistance of exhaustion itself, since labour power consumed too fast must be replaced too fast, and then the resistance of organisation and law. Factory legislation, won piecemeal against the doctrine that any restriction would ruin industry, fixed a normal working day and thereby converted what had been the most elastic route to surplus value into a bounded one. Marx greeted the English Ten Hours Act as a victory of the political economy of labour over the political economy of capital, and its analytical significance for this guide is exact: once the day's length is socially fixed, the exploitation offset must travel by intensity and by the cheapening of wage goods, the slower and more contested routes, while the denominator's growth faces no equivalent statute. No parliament can legislate the composition of capital downward. The asymmetry between a numerator whose main avenue can be closed by law and a denominator whose avenue cannot is the historical flesh on the formula's abstract claim that the two sides of the race are differently bounded, and defenders of the law who want a concrete illustration of asymmetric limits could do worse than point at the statute book.

## Raw materials and the price of the advance

Constant capital is not only machinery, and the third volume's early chapters, in the part on the transformation of surplus value into profit, contain an analysis of raw material prices that every exposition should rescue from obscurity. Marx examines there how fluctuations in the price of a principal input, cotton is his standing example, move the rate of profit directly: a dear harvest or an interrupted supply raises the value of the material advance for an unchanged physical process, swelling the denominator and squeezing the rate, while a cheap and abundant supply does the reverse, and he notes the frantic economy in waste material that dear inputs inspire, an early sketch of recycling as a profitability strategy. The American Civil War's cotton famine stood behind the discussion as lived experience: mills on short time, material costs convulsing, profits whipsawed by a blockade an ocean away from the looms. The analytical yield for this guide's purposes is the confirmation, from Marx's own hand and before the law is even stated, that the denominator is a price sensitive magnitude through and through. Machinery dominates the long run story because its mass grows secularly, but materials dominate many a decade's actual experience, and an exposition that treats constant capital as a synonym for plant has missed the component through which harvests, wars, cartels, and geology reach into the rate. Energy in the industrial eras since has inherited cotton's role as the input whose price writes itself across every balance sheet at once, and the dear energy episodes of the twentieth century replayed the cotton famine's arithmetic at continental scale, compressing rates by inflating the advance while the physical apparatus stood unchanged. The lesson generalises into a reading rule: whenever a period's profitability moves sharply, interrogate the input price series before reaching for deep causes, since the shallowest term in the formula is quite capable of producing the movement unaided, and the law's tendency, pitched at the secular level, neither needs nor claims credit for the weather.

## The two departments: where the composition rise is manufactured

Marx's reproduction schemes, built in the second volume, divide the economy into a department producing means of production and a department producing consumption goods, and the falling tendency looks different, and in some ways clearer, from that vantage. Mechanisation is not an abstraction hovering over the whole; it is an expansion, above all, of the first department's weight. As production grows more roundabout, more of society's labour is devoted to making the machines, structures, and intermediate goods that make the final goods, a lengthening of the chain between effort and consumption that national accountants rediscovered a century later in input output tables. The composition of the social capital rises, in large part, because the department whose output is constant capital grows faster than the department whose output is consumed, which is the same fact viewed from the side of industries rather than of balance sheets.

The departmental view also supplies one of the law's most interesting internal tensions, and the third chapter of the part exploits it. Expansion of the first department is itself accumulation's favourite activity in a boom, since orders for plant and equipment are the boom's substance; yet every completed expansion raises the stock that future surplus must valorise, so the boom manufactures the conditions of its own profitability problem even while it lasts. Add the disproportion hazard, the two departments must grow in step if output is to sell smoothly, and a process that systematically tempts the first department ahead of the second builds imbalance into prosperity. Later Marxist crisis theories drew selectively on these materials, some elevating disproportion into the master explanation, others treating it as a transmission belt for the falling rate, and the crisis guide in this series separates those lineages. For exposition the point is that the tendency is not a free floating curse laid on profitability from outside the system's structure. It is woven into the proportions accumulation itself creates, department by department, machine hall by machine hall, which is why Marx regarded it as a law of the mode of production rather than as a misfortune that befalls it.

## Ricardo's machinery chapter and the automation question

The mechanism's most durable theatre is the recurring panic and promise around automation, and the panic has a pedigree older than Marx. Ricardo startled his readers in the third edition of his Principles by adding a chapter on machinery that reversed his earlier optimism, conceding that the substitution of machines for labour could diminish the demand for workers and injure the labouring class even while it enriched the owners of the machines and cheapened goods for everyone. The concession matters historically because it shows the displacement problem arising inside classical economics itself, from its most rigorous mind, a generation before the first volume of Capital. Marx read that chapter with respect and built his own machinery analysis partly upon it, while relocating the central question: where Ricardo asked what machinery does to employment and to the wage fund, Marx asked what it does to the proportions of capital, and thereby to the rate that governs accumulation's willingness to employ anyone at all.

Successive automation waves have replayed the same double inquiry in fresh costumes: power looms and the handloom weavers, tractors and the agricultural exodus, containerisation and the dockers, industrial robots and the assembly line, and in each case the short run distributional pain and the long run composition question arrive together and are argued as one. The falling rate apparatus contributes a specific discipline to such debates. It insists that the interesting variable is not the number of jobs a device destroys at its point of installation, a figure that tells nothing about the system, but the changing ratio between the value of the apparatus a society maintains and the living labour that operates it, set against whatever happens simultaneously to exploitation and to input prices. Debates that stay at the installation point generate heat about particular trades; debates that climb to the ratio generate the genuinely hard questions about whether an economy whose apparatus grows ever more elaborate per worker can sustain the returns that justify building the apparatus. Marx's law may be wrong, but it is wrong or right at that altitude, and most popular automation controversy never climbs to meet it.

## Population, machinery, and the human denominator

Behind variable capital stand people, and the tendency's operation has a human face that purely algebraic treatments miss. Every rise in composition achieved through labour saving equipment poses the question of what becomes of the workers the equipment displaces. The first volume's long chapter on machinery confronts the comforting doctrine that displaced hands are automatically reabsorbed by the expansion their cheapened product makes possible, the compensation theory of the classical economists, and dismantles it case by case: reabsorption happens, sometimes, somewhere, eventually, but it is contingent, partial, and paid for in transitions that ruin particular communities and trades. The reserve army discussed among the counterweights is the aggregate residue of that process, accumulation's own product returning to discipline the wages of those still employed.

The connection to the rate runs in both directions, and tracing it keeps the theory honest about its own anthropology. Displacement swells the reserve, the reserve restrains wages and feeds low composition trades, and those effects counteract the fall that the displacing machinery promotes; the system generates its brake from the same motion as its accelerator, using the insecurity of its workforce as the lubricant. Any exposition that presents the counterweights as benign equilibrating forces has failed to read its own list, in which the second and fourth influences operate through human deprivation, pressed wages and standing unemployment. Marx's tone in these passages is clinical rather than sentimental, but the clinical content is grim: the tendency's postponement, across long stretches of history, was purchased substantially out of the hides of the unemployed and the underpaid. Later traditions drew different morals from that observation, some building welfare and full employment commitments precisely to disable those two counterweights and accept whatever profitability consequences followed, others treating the offsets as proof that the system requires a margin of misery to function. Those morals belong to political argument, not to exposition, yet an exposition that concealed the mechanism's human cost would be propaganda of a subtler kind, and this guide declines to conceal it.

## The tendency across a single firm's life

Aggregate laws are easier to doubt than to feel, so it helps to watch the pressure work through one firm's biography. A young firm typically enters at the frontier of its trade's composition or below it, leasing flexible equipment, subcontracting capital heavy stages, living on the surplus its nimbleness extracts. Success forces the issue the law describes: growth demands dedicated plant, the plant must be financed and then valorised across its whole life, and the firm's balance sheet acquires the heavy denominator of its mature rivals. Maturity brings the second act's familiar symptoms, a management preoccupied with utilisation rates, with sweating the assets, with the quarterly arithmetic of return on capital employed, and a vulnerability to any entrant whose newer vintage of equipment resets the cost curve and inflicts moral depreciation on the incumbent's sunk stock. Decline, when it comes, rarely announces itself as a falling rate of profit in the abstract; it arrives as the discovery that replacement investment cannot earn its keep at the prices the newest rivals have made normal, so the plant is milked, maintenance deferred, and the capital quietly migrates elsewhere through the financial system. Nothing in that biography requires the firm's owners to have heard of the organic composition of capital, which is rather the point: the theory claims to describe pressures that operate through balance sheets and boardrooms whether or not anyone names them, and the biography is a legitimate expository device precisely because each of its stages can be checked against the ordinary literature of business, where sweating assets and stranded plant are household phrases.

## Concentration: the rising minimum size of a viable capital

Mechanisation carries a social corollary that the first volume names and the third presupposes: as composition rises, the minimum capital required to enter a trade on competitive terms rises with it. A handloom weaver's workshop could be assembled for the price of its looms and a shed; a modern weaving shed, a refinery, a fabrication plant, a logistics network each demand advances that exclude all but large accumulations or pooled share capital. Accumulation therefore concentrates, fewer and larger capitals dividing the field, and centralises, existing capitals absorbing one another through competition and credit, a process Marx distinguishes carefully from mere growth. The corollary feeds back into the rate in two directions. Large capitals can exploit economies that defend their individual returns, purchasing the offsets wholesale: research establishments that manufacture cheapening, global sourcing that manufactures the trade influence, legal and financial departments that manufacture the share capital manoeuvres. Small capitals, denied those defences, meet the tendency nearly naked, which is why the pressure so often appears in the record as a shakeout of the small rather than as a uniform thinning of returns. Concentration is thus both the tendency's child and its unevenly distributed shield, and any account of an era that ignores the changing size distribution of firms will misread whose profitability the statistics describe. The political consequences Marx expected from concentration, a socialised production process administered by ever fewer hands, belong to other articles in this series; the economic point for this one is that the denominator's rise and the ownership structure's consolidation are a single movement surveyed from two sides, and the law cannot be pictured accurately with either side cropped out.

## Turnover, fixed capital, and the annual rate

One refinement separates serious discussion of the law from slogans, and Marx's second volume supplies it: time. The formula as stated so far treats the capital advanced during a single production period, but owners reckon returns by the year, and capital that completes its circuit quickly can be advanced again, multiplying the annual yield of a given sum. A grocer whose stock turns over monthly and a railway whose plant lasts half a century stand at opposite poles. The annual rate of profit therefore depends on turnover speed as well as on composition and exploitation, and accumulation changes all three at once, since mechanisation typically lengthens turnover by locking value into durable plant even as it raises composition. Fixed capital is the name for the portion that surrenders its value piecemeal across many cycles, buildings and machinery above all, and its growth is the balance sheet signature of the tendency's engine.

Durability introduces a vulnerability the third chapter exploits. A machine's value is recovered gradually through the price of its output, on the assumption that its services remain worth what they cost. A rival's better machine breaks that assumption overnight: the older plant must now compete against lower costs, its remaining value evaporates in the market's judgement, and its owner suffers moral depreciation, a loss inflicted by progress rather than by rust. Marx saw in this a permanent insecurity at the heart of mechanised production, where every capital lives under sentence of obsolescence and the race to adopt is partly a race to impose that sentence on others first. For the law's arithmetic the point matters because the denominator is not a placid stock; it is a stock continually menaced by write downs, and crises, when they come, execute the write downs wholesale, resetting the composition's value by destruction. Defenders of the law treat that reset as the tendency's own corrective, built into the cycle. Critics treat the need for periodic destruction as evidence that the tendency describes pathology rather than law. Both readings are argued in the debate article; the exposition simply notes that turnover and durability are where the abstract ratio acquires a clock and a guillotine.

Turnover also hides a counterweight that Marx's list, focused on production, leaves implicit. Speeding circulation, through logistics, credit instruments, and faster communications, raises the annual rate without touching composition at all, and whole industries of the modern economy exist to sell exactly that acceleration. Whether such gains should count among the counteracting influences or as a separate dimension altogether is a genuine taxonomic question in the literature, and it illustrates something worth remembering about the famous six: they are Marx's list, drawn from his manuscript, not a closed canon handed down for all possible economies. Later writers have proposed additions and reorganisations, and the same-part rule, which binds readers to present the six as Marx's, does not forbid scholarship from asking whether the set is complete. Fidelity to a text and curiosity beyond it are compatible virtues, and this guide tries to practise the first while pointing at the second.

## The same-part rule and the artifact

Here the guide states its organising discipline, the rule from which its title borrows. Call it the same-part rule. The law and the forces opposing it were set out by Marx inside one part of one volume, the counteracting influences occupying the chapter immediately after the law's own statement, written as part of the same exposition and plainly integral to it. They are not a retreat drafted decades later, not an appendix squeezed in by nervous followers, and not a list compiled by hostile critics to soften an embarrassing doctrine. Any presentation that quotes the fall while omitting the chapter that follows is quoting selectively against the plain architecture of its source. Conversely, any presentation that wields the counterweights to declare the law meaningless has made the mirror image error, since a tendency with internal offsets is the doctrine, whole and entire, and arguing against a version stripped of them is arguing against a text that does not exist.

The rule sounds elementary, yet applying it transforms how the material is taught, cited, and fought over. Textbook summaries that give the law a paragraph and the countertendencies a clause fail the rule on proportions alone. Polemics that demand to know why, if the rate must fall, it so often visibly rises, fail the rule by treating an objection Marx built into his own exposition as a discovery of theirs. Defences that blame every contrary decade on the counterweights, without specifying which influence did the work and through which letter of the ratio, fail the rule from the other side, because the rule demands the offsets be used as Marx used them: as named mechanisms, each with its own channel, not as a fog machine for rescuing a slogan. Precision about the six is therefore not pedantry. It is the price of admission to an argument that has run for well over a century largely because its participants keep answering different propositions.

The table below, the law and its counter-tendencies, gathers the whole apparatus in one view. One row states the mechanism of the fall itself; six rows follow, one for each influence Marx names, recording what each does to the ratio, the broad period in which each has been especially prominent, and whether its power to offset the fall can plausibly operate without limit or only within bounds. Readers pressed for time can extract the article's skeleton from this table alone, though the sections around it supply the qualifications that keep the skeleton from misleading.

| Element | What it does to the ratio | Period of special prominence | Can it offset the fall without limit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| The law itself: rising organic composition | Enlarges constant capital relative to variable, pressing the rate down whenever exploitation rises more slowly | Operates across the whole era of mechanised industry | Not applicable; it is the pressure being offset |
| More intense exploitation | Raises surplus value relative to wages, lifting the numerator | Early factory industry and every later speed up drive | Only within bounds; the working day and the worker set edges |
| Wages pressed below their value | Cuts variable capital while output continues, lifting the surplus share | Depressions, weak labour organisation, gluts of jobseekers | Only temporarily; it undermines the workforce it depends on |
| Cheaper elements of constant capital | Shrinks the money value of plant and materials even as their mass grows | Eras of rapid productivity growth in machine making and extraction | Powerful and durable, yet it springs from the same productivity growth that mechanises |
| Relative surplus population | Holds wages down and supplies labour for low composition branches | Industrialisation's long expansion and its reserve of unemployed | Only while the reserve lasts and where such branches can expand |
| Foreign trade | Cheapens inputs and wage goods while opening outlets for surplus product | The great expansions of world commerce in the nineteenth century and after | Ambiguous; it also exports the composition rise to trading partners |
| Growth of share capital | Spreads returns across interest bearing holdings that accept a lower yield | The age of joint stock companies, railways, and large scale finance | Only partly; it reclassifies income rather than abolishing the pressure |

Read the final column as the article in miniature. Marx's own treatment implies that most of the offsets are bounded, conditional, or double edged, which is why he could hold both that the tendency is real and that its visible path would be anything but smooth. But bounded is not the same as negligible, and several of these forces have dominated whole eras, a fact the next section honours by taking each influence seriously as a mechanism in its own right rather than as a footnote to its master.

The rule also governs scholarly citation in a way worth making habitual. When a secondary source attributes a prediction to the law, the disciplined reader checks which of the part's three chapters the attribution draws on, and whether the counterweights chapter would qualify the sentence if it were allowed into the room. When a source celebrates the counterweights as Marx's own anticipation of every objection, the same reader checks whether the law chapter's derivation has been granted its full force first, since a defence that begins by dissolving the pressure has defended nothing. Examination boards, editors, and seminar leaders could do worse than adopt the rule as a marking criterion, because it converts a shouting match about loyalty into a checkable question about textual completeness: was the whole architecture represented, or only the storey the writer prefers? A remarkable share of the literature fails that elementary test on one side or the other, and it fails it in the first three pages, which is why this guide has placed the rule early and returned to it often.

## A note on the part's internal economy of emphasis

Something should be said about proportion inside the text itself, because the manuscript's allocation of space carries information that chapter titles flatten. The law's own exposition, for all its fame, is compact; the counterweights chapter is shorter still; the contradictions chapter sprawls, circling its themes, returning to overaccumulation and devaluation from fresh angles, breaking and resuming. One legitimate reading of that economy is that Marx's interest lay less in the ratio's statics than in the dynamics of a system whose profitability problem keeps forcing invention, expansion, and destruction, which would make the third chapter the part's true centre of gravity and the first its foyer. Another legitimate reading treats the sprawl as the mark of the least finished material, the place where the editor's arrangement had the most construction to do and therefore where confidence should be most heavily discounted. Both readings are live in the scholarship, and they pull interpretation in opposite directions, toward the law as dynamic theory or away from it as editorial mirage. The exposition takes no side between them; it notes that a reader's answer silently governs how the whole doctrine is weighted, and that surfacing the choice is already an improvement on inheriting it from a textbook's page counts.

## The six counteracting influences, one by one

Engels's chapter on the counteracting influences lists six, and commentary that rounds them down to two or three has usually kept the famous ones and dropped the awkward ones. All six are given here in Marx's order, each with its channel through the ratio, its historical theatre, and its internal limit. The order itself instructs: Marx begins inside the production process, with exploitation, moves outward through wages and input prices, and ends with the widening circles of population, trade, and finance.

### More intense exploitation

The first counterweight is the most direct. If the working day lengthens, the surplus portion grows at the expense of the necessary portion only when the extension adds hours beyond those needed to cover the wage; if the day stays fixed but labour intensifies, more value is produced per hour as pores in the day are filled and effort per minute rises; if productivity in wage goods industries climbs, necessary labour shrinks and the surplus share expands at an unchanged day. All three routes raise s relative to v, fattening the numerator of the rate of profit and pushing against the denominator's growth. Marx groups them as an increase in the degree of exploitation, and historically the route carried enormous weight: the factory system's first century combined lengthening, intensifying, and cheapening in a single onslaught, which is one reason the falling tendency, if it operated, did so invisibly for long stretches.

Its limits are the ones already flagged. Hours cannot exceed the day. Intensity cannot exceed what a human body can repeat across a working life without breaking down faster than replacements can be trained. Cheapening of wage goods depends on productivity in particular sectors and on what counts as the customary basket, which itself expands as societies grow richer: goods that begin as luxuries migrate into necessity, dragging necessary labour back up. None of this makes the counterweight weak. It makes it a variable with edges, and the whole dispute over the law can be reframed as a dispute over whether those edges bind before or after the composition's rise does its work.

### Wages pressed below the value of labour power

Marx lists separately a darker mechanism: wages driven below the value of labour power, so that workers receive less than the cost of reproducing their capacity to work. In ratio terms the effect is immediate, since v falls while the labour performed, and hence the new value created, initially continues; the surplus share swells by direct deduction rather than by productivity. He notes that this influence is constantly invoked in practice and belongs in the empirical catalogue, while remarking that, strictly, it sits awkwardly inside a theory that elsewhere assumes labour power sells at its value. Its inclusion signals candour about the gap between the model's assumptions and the world's behaviour.

Historically the mechanism belongs to slumps, to regions and trades where organisation is weak, and to periods when a swollen reserve of unemployed workers competes for scarce posts. Its self defeating character is plain enough: labour power persistently underpaid is labour power underfed, underhoused, and undertrained, which degrades the very productivity accumulation depends on, and provokes resistance ranging from unionisation to emigration to political upheaval. As an offset it therefore purchases time at the cost of the conditions for future growth, a pattern the wider crisis literature in this series meets again. It can arrest a falling rate for years. Treating it as a permanent foundation requires believing a system can thrive by consuming its own workforce, which even its beneficiaries have rarely managed for long.

### Cheapening of the elements of constant capital

The third influence is the subtlest and, in many periods, the strongest. The same rise in productivity that multiplies machinery per worker also cheapens each machine, each tonne of steel, each unit of computing power. Constant capital is a value magnitude: what counts in the denominator is not the physical mountain of equipment but the money sum that mountain represents. If the value of a unit of equipment halves while the number of units per worker doubles, the technical composition has soared and the value composition has not moved at all. Whole epochs of technical change have this character, with quality adjusted prices for equipment, above all information technology hardware, falling steeply across decades. Marx saw the principle clearly in the machinery and raw materials of his own century, cotton and iron among them, and gave it a full place in his list.

Yet the influence carries a built in entanglement. Cheapening presupposes productivity growth in the industries supplying constant capital, and that growth is itself achieved largely by mechanising those industries, which raises their internal composition and expands the total mass of equipment in use. Cheapening therefore slows the value rise of the denominator without stopping the physical transformation that drives it, and its strength varies by era depending on whether price falls in capital goods outrun the growth of their volume. Any honest empirics must track both series at once, which is one reason the measurement literature remains so unsettled: the physical story and the value story can point in opposite directions for a generation at a time.

### Relative surplus population

Fourth comes the reserve army of labour, treated at length in the first volume's chapter on the general law of accumulation and recalled here for its effect on the rate. A standing pool of unemployed and underemployed workers disciplines wages, obviously, but Marx gives the influence a second, less quoted channel. Cheap and abundant labour makes it profitable to open or expand lines of production with low compositions of capital, handicraft style workshops, labour intensive services, sweated trades, where hands substitute for machines precisely because hands are inexpensive. Such branches drag the social average composition downward, or brake its rise, and their profits, computed on small constant outlays, can run high. The reserve thus counteracts the tendency twice over, through the wage level and through the sectoral mix.

The limits mirror the mechanism. A reserve that is absorbed, as in the tight labour markets of a long boom, loses both channels at once, and the decades after mid century in the advanced economies showed how quickly wage pressure returns when unemployment stays low. A reserve that grows too large, meanwhile, signals demand failure and social distress that feed back into accumulation itself. Marx's own framing treats the surplus population as a product of the same accumulation that mechanises, endlessly regenerated in one place while being absorbed in another, so this counterweight is best pictured as a tide rather than a stock: always present somewhere, never guaranteed in the size or the location a given capital requires.

### Foreign trade

Trade operates on the ratio from several directions at once, which is why Marx's treatment of it is the most ambivalent of the six. Imports of cheaper raw materials and food shrink constant capital's bill and necessary labour's basket simultaneously, striking both sides of the fraction favourably for the home rate. Exports open markets where surplus product can be realised on a larger scale, letting production expand without the gluts that choke it at home, and capitals engaged in trade with regions of lower development can earn rates above the domestic norm, some of which feeds back into the general average. Colonial and semi colonial trade in Marx's century supplied instance after instance, from cheapened cotton to cheapened grain remaking the wage basket of industrial Europe.

The ambivalence enters because trade generalises the process it relieves. The partner economy drawn into the world market begins its own mechanisation, its own composition begins to climb, and the cheap supplies themselves rest on production that accumulation keeps transforming. Marx also notes the boomerang in demand terms: prosperity built on foreign outlets is hostage to foreign crises, a vulnerability his century demonstrated repeatedly. So trade can lift the rate of the trading nation for extended periods while diffusing, rather than defeating, the tendency across the system as a whole. Whether that distinction comforts or damns the law depends on whether the law is read as a proposition about national accounts or about the capitalist world economy, a question of scope that disputants do not always make explicit and should.

### The increase of share capital

The sixth influence is the most institutional. As joint stock forms spread, a growing slice of the social capital takes the shape of shares and bonds whose owners accept a return closer to an interest rate than to the full profit of enterprise. Railways in Marx's account are the type case: vast constant outlays, indispensable to the system, undertaken through share capital at yields that would look anaemic beside the profit rates of smaller, nimbler trades. Because this mass of capital does not press for the average rate of profit in the same way, entering the general equalisation only partially or on softened terms, its growth softens the fall of the measured average rate. The capital functions, society gets its infrastructure, and the statistic bends.

Commentators sometimes treat this entry as a bookkeeping trick, and there is bookkeeping in it, but the underlying reality is a genuine change in how returns are claimed and shared. Where ownership divides into a rentier layer content with interest and an active layer retaining profit of enterprise, the boundary of the rate of profit statistic becomes a matter of definition, and definitions are choices. Marx flags the phenomenon as a real feature of maturing capitalism, the socialisation of investment within private forms, and readers of the third volume will meet it again in the analysis of interest bearing capital. For the law's fortunes the lesson is measurement humility: part of what looks like the tendency stalling may be the yardstick quietly changing its markings as financial forms evolve.

## The six in combination: configurations across eras

Influences examined one at a time can mislead in the opposite direction from influences ignored, because history never runs them singly. What an era actually presents is a configuration, a characteristic bundle in which two or three of the offsets dominate while the underlying pressure from composition proceeds at its own pace underneath. Sketching three such configurations, at the level of mechanism rather than measurement, shows how the same law can coexist with wildly different visible records.

Consider first the classic factory era of the early and middle nineteenth century in Britain, the world Marx observed at closest range. Mechanisation advanced furiously in textiles and then in iron, transport, and engineering, yet the counterweights ran at matching strength. The working day lengthened and intensified under weak legal restraint; a growing population and the displacement of hand workers kept the reserve army stocked; colonial trade poured in cheapened cotton and carried off finished cloth; wage goods cheapened as agriculture and transport improved. A falling tendency in such a setting would be nearly invisible in any series a contemporary could construct, smothered by offsets operating at full throttle, which is roughly the picture the historical record gives: profitability fluctuated violently with the cycle while no secular collapse of returns announced itself to participants.

Swing forward to the decades after the Second World War in the advanced economies, a configuration with a different signature. Reconstruction and catch up growth spread equipment through economies whose capital stocks war had depleted, so composition rose from a lowered base; simultaneously, tight labour markets shrank the reserve army toward vanishing point in several countries, unions converted productivity gains into rising real wages, and the exploitation offset weakened accordingly. Cheap energy and materials, drawn from an expanding world trading system, did heavy counteracting work through the third and fifth influences instead. When that configuration broke down in the 1970s, with energy prices reversing and profitability squeezed across the industrial world, interpreters of every school reached for their preferred variable; Marxist writers of the period divided among profit squeeze accounts centred on wages, falling rate accounts centred on composition, and overproduction accounts centred on markets, a division the wider crisis guide maps.

A third configuration belongs to the era of globalised supply chains and information technology. Equipment prices fell with extraordinary speed in computing and communications, letting the third influence dominate whole decades: workplaces transformed technically while value composition advanced far more slowly. Production migrated toward lower wage regions, activating foreign trade and the global reserve army as offsets of the first rank, while financialisation swelled the share capital channel until the boundary between profit and interest grew genuinely difficult to police in the statistics. A reader equipped with the six can describe that era in the theory's own vocabulary without yet deciding whether the description vindicates or embarrasses the tendency, and that descriptive power, separable from any verdict, is part of what has kept the apparatus alive in scholarship long after obituaries for it were first drafted.

The configurations teach a methodological moral that the objections sections will need. Because the offsets are historically variable in strength and identity, the law's empirical signature is not a shape, a line sloping down, but a structure: pressure from composition, punctuated relief from whichever offsets an era supplies, and crises when relief fails. Assessing such a claim demands decomposition work era by era, exactly the labour the evidence article surveys. Anyone offering a simpler test, a single chart brandished in triumph or in mockery, has quietly exchanged the doctrine for its caricature again.

## Candidate additions: offsets later writers have proposed

Any honest exposition should mark where the canonical list ends and later construction begins, because the six have attracted proposed amendments for a century. Four candidates recur. Faster turnover, treated in an earlier section, heads the list: circulation time is dead time for valorisation, so logistics, payments technology, and inventory discipline raise the annual rate without altering production's composition, and several commentators treat the omission as an accident of the manuscript's ordering, since the second volume's turnover analysis was written in the same project. State activity forms a second candidate, though it cuts ambiguously: taxation drains surplus, while infrastructure, education, and research supplied publicly cheapen constant capital and raise labour's quality at collective expense, a socialisation of costs that functions like the third influence wearing institutional dress. Monopoly and market power form a third, since a firm shielded from equalisation can hold its price above the price of production and appropriate surplus transferred from competitive sectors, defending its own rate while the general rate does whatever it does; whether such defence should count as a counteracting influence or as a change in the unit of analysis is disputed. Resource and energy regimes form a fourth, with cheap energy eras operating as a giant cheapening of constant capital's most universal input, and dear energy eras as its reverse, a symmetry the configurations section exploited.

The amendments matter less for their individual merits than for what they reveal about the list's status. If the six are read as a closed canon, amendments are heresies to be resisted; if they are read as the mechanisms one manuscript happened to enumerate, amendments are the normal growth of a research programme, to be judged by the same standards of channel and footprint as the originals. Marx's own text supports the second reading more comfortably than the first, since his treatment of trade and share capital already reaches beyond the production process into whatever institutional material the argument needs. The same-part rule binds exposition to the six as Marx's; it does not canonise them against his successors.

## The six in interaction: offsets that compound and cancel

One more analytical step remains before the offsets can be left, because in practice they interact, sometimes reinforcing, sometimes sabotaging one another, and the interactions explain patterns that single influence accounts cannot. Begin with a reinforcing pair. Cheapening of constant capital and foreign trade compound each other in an open economy riding a productivity wave: imported equipment embodies foreign productivity gains, domestic machine making answers with its own, and the denominator's value is squeezed from both directions while trade simultaneously cheapens the wage basket and lifts the numerator. Eras of rapid globalisation have this compounded character, which is why profitability in such eras can look serenely healthy while physical composition races upward underneath, a combination that puzzles observers who track only one side of the fraction.

Now a cancelling pair. Wage suppression and the exploitation route through cheap wage goods pull opposite levers on the wage basket itself: suppressing wages below value degrades the worker's consumption, which over time degrades labour power's quality and the productivity on which cheapening depends, while expanding the reserve army can so weaken demand for consumption goods that the second department's realisation falters and the surplus the offsets were protecting cannot be sold. Marx's system has a name for the knot: the same working class whose exploitation fills the numerator is also the market whose restricted consumption haunts the third chapter's crisis account, so an offset pursued too successfully manufactures the conditions of a different contradiction. Share capital interacts differently again, less with production than with perception: by reclassifying returns it can mask a squeeze that the other offsets failed to prevent, allowing the measured rate to glide while the underlying gross return deteriorates, a masking that matters for the epistemic problems treated in the credit section below.

The interactions carry a lesson for how the six should be cited. A serious period analysis names not only which influences were strong but how they combined, and accepts that the net effect is an empirical resultant, not a sum that can be computed from the armchair. A lazy analysis picks whichever single influence fits the outcome and stops. The distance between those two practices is the distance between the theory as a research instrument and the theory as a liturgy, and it is the same distance the unfalsifiability section measures from the methodological side.

## Why the operative word is tendency

Vocabulary in this debate is not decoration; the noun a writer chooses announces the claim he thinks he is defending. A prediction names a value at a date and can be falsified by a calendar. A law, in the loose newspaper sense, names an irresistible sequence and can be refuted by a single durable exception. Marx's construction fits neither template. His formulation describes a pressure generated continuously by accumulation itself, opposed continuously by other pressures the same process generates, with the visible rate emerging as the net result of the contest. That is what tendency means in his usage, and the third chapter of the part, the one on internal contradictions, exists precisely to trace how the pressure expresses itself through crises and convulsions rather than through a smooth glide path. Judging the construct as if it promised a smooth glide path is the most common elementary error in the literature about it.

### Why is it called a tendency rather than a law?

Because Marx sets out six counteracting influences in the same part of the same volume, including cheaper constant capital, higher exploitation, and foreign trade. The tendency operates alongside forces that offset it, so the observable rate of profit is the net outcome, not a steady decline.

Working scientists use cognate constructions without embarrassment. A demographic tendency toward smaller households can dominate a century while reversing for a decade under housing pressure; a tendency toward concentration in an industry can define an era while particular years see break ups and new entrants. In each case the concept earns its keep by identifying which force is systematically regenerated by the process under study, as distinct from forces that are episodic, policy dependent, or imported from outside. Marx's claim, charitably and accurately read, is that mechanisation driven by competition is regenerated by accumulation in exactly that systematic way, whereas the counterweights, several of them at least, wax and wane with circumstance. Whether the charitable reading survives scrutiny is the debate article's business. What exposition owes the reader is the distinction itself, plainly drawn: a tendency asserts a persistent directional pressure plus named offsets, and it is refuted, if at all, by showing either that the pressure does not exist or that the offsets are equally systematic and stronger, not by exhibiting a cheerful decade.

The distinction also disciplines the law's friends. A defender who treats every falling interval as the tendency surfacing and every rising interval as counterweights at work, without independent evidence for either attribution, has converted a structured claim into a vocabulary for redescribing any outcome whatsoever. That conversion is the seed of the unfalsifiability charge examined later in this guide, and defenders who take the charge seriously answer it by specifying in advance which mechanism they believe dominated a given period and what observations would prove them wrong. Some have done exactly that, tying their readings to measured series for composition, exploitation, and input prices. Others have not, and the theory's reputation suffers for the difference. A tendency, handled well, is a research programme with moving parts that can be separately audited. Handled lazily, it is an alibi.

## Rate and mass, the distinction readers lose

Ask a room of intelligent non specialists what a falling rate of profit predicts and most will say: falling profits. The identification is natural and wrong, and Marx pauses in the text to block it explicitly. Rate and mass are different magnitudes with different behaviour. The mass of profit is the total surplus extracted across the economy, and it grows with the scale of employment, the length and intensity of work, and the number of workers set in motion. The rate divides that mass by the capital advanced, and the divisor grows with accumulation too, in Marx's account faster than the dividend over the long run. Two magnitudes can therefore march in opposite directions indefinitely, an economy generating record profits in absolute terms while each unit of invested capital earns a thinner percentage than its grandfather did.

His illustration turns on the growth of total capital. With a handful of large capitals, a lower percentage of a much larger sum yields a greater mass than a higher percentage of the earlier, smaller sum, so accumulation simultaneously satisfies the capitalist's hunger for absolute gain and frustrates his expectation of proportional return. Marx draws a pointed consequence: the same development that swells the mass concentrates it, since the growing minimum scale of viable investment squeezes out small capitals, and the frustration of the proportional expectation feeds the speculative restlessness and crisis proneness the third chapter of the part explores. The mass can also be divided among more claimants than before, shareholders, lenders, landlords, the state, so that a rising total is perfectly compatible with squeezed returns at any particular window where a given observer happens to stand.

### Can the total of profit expand while its rate contracts?

Yes, and Marx insists on the point, since the mass of profit depends on how much surplus value the whole workforce produces, which grows with employment, intensity, and productivity, while the rate divides that mass by a total capital growing through accumulation. A larger surplus spread over a still larger advance yields record profits alongside a thinner percentage return.

Business journalism makes the confusion hard to avoid, because headlines report masses: aggregate corporate profits, quarter after quarter, frequently at records. Nothing in the law is embarrassed by such headlines, and commentators who brandish them as refutations have mistaken the variable under discussion. The rate appears instead in quieter statistics: returns on capital employed, margins relative to assets, the yield investors accept on new commitments. Even there the correspondence with Marx's category is loose, since his rate is reckoned on the value of capital advanced for the whole social capital over a turnover period, while published returns use book values, market values, tax shaped definitions, and sector specific conventions. Translating between the two vocabularies is delicate work, and the empirical article in this series exists because the translation, not the theory's internal logic, is where most practical difficulty concentrates. Readers will find its address in the closing links of this guide, and the placement is deliberate: measurement quarrels deserve their own room.

One more distinction belongs in this section, because it governs how the rate's movements feel from inside the system. Marx separates the profit rate from the interest rate and from the profit of enterprise into which gross profit splits, and the split matters for perception. In a credit saturated economy the return that confronts the active capitalist is the residue after interest, and that residue can be squeezed by rising interest even when the gross rate stands still, or flattered by cheap credit while the gross rate sinks. Observers inside the machine therefore receive distorted signals about the magnitude the law describes, which helps explain why the tendency, if real, could operate for generations without becoming boardroom common sense. The mechanism hides behind its own subdivisions.

## Misreadings this guide exists to correct

Certain errors recur so predictably that collecting them serves readers better than scattering corrections through the text. The first is the steady decline caricature: the law does not forecast a monotone downward line for any published series, and a chart showing the rate rising across some decade answers a claim nobody's text makes. The second is the mass confusion just treated. The third credits the counteracting influences to embarrassed later editors or hostile critics, when the chapter is Marx's own, sitting in the middle of his own part of his own volume; Engels arranged the material, but the list, its membership, and its reasoning descend from the manuscript. The fourth recasts the law as a story about competition forcing prices down until margins vanish. Price competition redistributes profit and can ruin individual firms, yet the mechanism here works through the proportions of the advance, and would operate in a monopoly economy too, a point worth pausing on: a single giant capital mechanising against its own workforce faces the same arithmetic of composition, with no rival required.

A fifth error reverses the causality of innovation, claiming Marx held that capitalists adopt machinery in order to lower the profit rate or out of a taste for technology. The text's logic is the opposite: each adopts to cheapen his own product and defend his own margin, and the fall is the unintended aggregate signature of that defence, closer to a tragedy of the commons than to a plan. A sixth error, subtler, treats the law as a claim about the physical quantity of machinery, full stop. Physical growth is the usual companion, but the ratio that enters the formula is a value ratio, and eras of radical cheapening can pry the two apart for long periods, as the third counterweight showed. A seventh error inflates the law into a complete crisis theory. Crises in the third chapter of the part are one expression of the contradictions the law names, interacting with credit, disproportion between departments, and the restricted consumption of the mass of the population; the guide to the wider field, the [marxist crisis theory guide](/2013/10/01/marxist-crisis-theory-guide/), maps those families side by side and shows how much territory this single mechanism does not cover.

Correcting caricatures is not the same as vindicating the original, and the distinction should stay bright. A claim can be universally misstated and still be false; it can survive every popular objection and fall to a technical one. What the corrections purchase is a fair hearing, the chance for the argument to be assessed in the form its author gave it, with its internal tensions intact. The two hardest challenges, the mechanical indeterminacy objection and the unfalsifiability charge, are interesting precisely because they engage the real text rather than the caricature, and they get full sections below. First, though, comes the question that hangs over the text's very shape, because a reader who does not know who built the chapters cannot weigh what the chapters prove.

## The editorial question: who built the three chapters?

Open the third volume at its third part and the architecture looks inevitable: law, counteracting influences, internal contradictions, a triad with a Hegelian neatness that suits an author raised on that philosophy. The archive tells a less tidy story. Marx's principal manuscript for the volume, drafted in the mid 1860s, ran as a continuous investigation without chapter divisions or headings in this part, its arguments flowing in long exploratory waves, revisiting points, breaking off, resuming. When Engels took up the task of preparing the volume after his friend's death, he confronted hundreds of pages of such material across the whole book, and he imposed the sectional skeleton that readers now navigate by. He did not conceal the operation; his preface describes the third volume as the most laborious of his editorial tasks and flags where his interventions ran deepest. But a preface's candour about effort is not the same as a reader's awareness of consequence, and for a century the triad's neatness did quiet argumentative work, lending the law a systematic finish, a look of settled doctrine, that the underlying draft does not straightforwardly supply.

### How did a continuous manuscript become three titled chapters?

Engels divided the unheaded draft of the third part into the law, the counteracting influences, and the internal contradictions, supplying divisions and headings readers now cite. He described the volume as his most laborious editorial task, and the arrangement gave exploratory material the finished architecture of a doctrine, a shape later scholarship discounts when weighing how settled the argument was.

Modern editorial scholarship, armed with the complete historical critical edition of the Marx Engels corpus and its superior access to the manuscripts, has pressed the point into a stronger thesis. Michael Heinrich, the argument's best known proponent, contends that the textual evidence shows a thinker still wrestling with the problem rather than a doctrine in repose: the draft's treatment is comparatively brief and tentative beside the volume's more worked parts, and Marx's economic studies of the following decade, the notebooks and excerpts of the 1870s, return to crisis and profitability with fresh questions and without the air of a man consulting his own settled law. On this reading the law's canonical status is substantially an artefact of editing and reception, and the honest Marx is the one caught mid inquiry. The argument reframes a century of dispute at a stroke, since generations of defenders and attackers alike aimed at a target whose contours an editor had sharpened.

The responses have been vigorous, and neutrality requires giving them their full weight. Defenders of the law's standing reply, first, that nothing in the manuscript contradicts the triad's content: Engels divided and titled, but the propositions, the six influences, and the contradiction theme are present in the draft, so the edition sharpens emphasis without inventing doctrine. Second, they note that Marx continued to work within the framework the law names, returning to composition and profitability in later studies, and that exploration is compatible with conviction when a thinker tests his tools rather than discarding them. Third, they caution against inferring doubt from silence or tentativeness in private notebooks, since Marx's drafts are tentative everywhere, including on points he unquestionably held, the style of a mind that thought by writing. Scholars such as Andrew Kliman and David Harvey, in different registers, have treated the law as genuinely Marx's and central to his critique, and the controversy in the journals and conference volumes remains open, with the manuscript evidence cited industriously by both camps.

What should a reader do with a live dispute of this kind? Three disciplines help. Distinguish the layers of the question: what the draft says, what the edition made of it, and what Marx believed in his final decade are separable issues, and evidence decisive for one may barely touch the others. Notice that the dispute changes the law's authority more than its content: the mechanism stands or falls by argument and evidence regardless of how firmly its author held it, and a proposition does not become true because Marx was sure of it, nor false because he wavered. And keep the series pattern in view. This is not the only place where an editor's hand shaped a canonical Marxist text; the German Ideology, the Paris manuscripts, and the posthumous volumes all reached readers through other people's decisions about order and completeness, and habits formed on one case transfer with care to the others. The authorship companion linked earlier pursues the general problem at the length it deserves.

For the exposition that occupies this guide, the editorial question carries one practical consequence worth stating plainly. Claims of the form Marx's system requires the fall, or Marx predicted collapse by this route, lean on the finished architecture and should be discounted accordingly. Claims of the form Marx developed a mechanism, worried at it, and left its status productively unsettled, fit the documentary record better and lose none of the mechanism's interest. Precision about provenance is not a debunking exercise. It is the same service the same-part rule performs for the counterweights: it returns the argument to the shape the evidence supports, no larger and no smaller.

## The later notebooks and the question of doubt, in detail

Because the editorial dispute turns on what Marx did with the problem after the mid 1860s draft, the evidence deserves a closer look than summaries usually give it. The known materials of the following decade are extensive: excerpt notebooks on the money market and on banking, studies of agricultural and ground rent questions fed by Russian and American sources, mathematical manuscripts in which the relations among profit, surplus value, and their rates are worried at algebraically, and preparations toward revising the first volume for its French edition, in which he reworked passages with a care that shows a mind still revising fundamentals. Heinrich's reading fastens on the pattern: the falling rate, so prominent in the notebooks of the 1850s and present in the 1860s draft, does not reappear in the later work with the prominence or the systematic framing a settled doctrine would lead one to expect, while crisis and profitability are approached through fresher, more empirical enquiries. From that pattern he infers a thinker drifting from his earlier construction under the pressure of his own research, and he adds the textual point that the draft chapter on the law is comparatively brief and provisional beside the volume's most laboured parts.

The counter reading disputes the inference rather than the inventory. Later silence, its proponents argue, is what a research programme looks like when its author has moved to other volumes' problems and to the empirical spadework those problems required; the mathematical manuscripts show continued engagement with the rate's determinants, not abandonment of them; and the standard for settled doctrine that the argument applies would convict almost every major thinker of doubt about almost every major claim, since drafts and notebooks are tentative by genre. There is also a chronological caution: the 1860s draft was written before the first volume was finished, so the absence of a later restatement may say more about the order in which Marx worked than about any change of mind. A neutral exposition reports the standoff as a standoff, noting only that the dispute has permanently changed citation practice among careful scholars, who now specify whether a claim rests on the published volume, the draft behind it, or the notebooks behind the draft. That habit, three layers of provenance distinguished on sight, is the editorial question's most durable gift to readers, whatever its eventual verdict.

## The road from the notebooks to the volume

The argument's biography clarifies several features that puzzle first time readers of the finished text, beginning with its confidence. In the Grundrisse, the sprawling notebooks of the winter of 1857 and 1858 in which Marx first attempted a total draft of his economics, the falling rate appears with prophetic emphasis, bound up with the thought that capital itself erects barriers to its own development and drives beyond them, and it is there that he awards it the rank of the most important law of modern political economy. The notebooks were written in a fever of anticipated crisis, with a commercial crash unfolding around their author, and the prose carries that weather: brilliant, compressed, speculative, willing to leap. Nobody should be surprised that a doctrine born in such conditions arrived with more certainty than its derivation yet supported.

The two decades that followed were spent, on this topic as on others, in the unglamorous labour of categories. The first volume, published in 1867, built the foundations the law requires and pointedly withholds the law itself: surplus value's production is analysed there, its division and the equalisation of rates reserved for later, so that readers of the lifetime published text never encountered the doctrine in its author's own finished presentation. The main manuscript for the third volume dates from the middle 1860s, drafted before the first volume appeared, and its treatment of the tendency is notably more patient than the notebooks', organised around cases and magnitudes, though still exploratory in the ways the editorial section described. Later, in the 1870s, Marx's economic studies ranged widely over new material, Russian statistics, American developments, fresh mathematical attempts at the rate's behaviour among them, and the interpretation of that ranging, restlessness or refinement, is exactly what the Heinrich controversy contests.

Engels's construction of the volume in the 1880s and early 1890s closed the biography by freezing it. Whatever Marx might have done with another decade, the text the world received was the mid 1860s draft in early 1890s dress, and the reception split accordingly: political movements took the triad of chapters as doctrine within a system, while the archive minded scholarship of a later age kept asking what a draft can bear. Both receptions are facts about the text now, inseparable from its meaning in the culture, and a guide serves readers best by holding the biography steadily in view. A proposition with a birth in prophetic notebooks, a youth of categorical labour, and a posthumous christening by its author's closest collaborator should be read with corresponding alertness, neither worshipped as system nor dismissed as juvenilia, but interrogated as the mature, unfinished work it is.

## The first reception: the 1890s and the revisionist quarrel

The volume appeared in 1894 into a movement that had organised itself around expectations the book was promptly conscripted to serve, and the conscription shaped the law's public meaning for a century. Mass socialist parties, above all the German party whose theorists had awaited the volume as the completion of their doctrine, read the third part as the scientific warrant for capitalism's approaching exhaustion: the tendency, vulgarised into a schedule, promised that history itself was compounding against the system. Within a few years Eduard Bernstein, Engels's literary executor and no hostile outsider, published the articles and then the book that opened the revisionist controversy, questioning whether the anticipated collapse was in fact arriving and whether the theory required it, pointing to rising real wages, the persistence of small enterprise, and the system's evident adaptability. The falling rate stood near the centre of that quarrel, less as a text to be read than as a token in a struggle over strategy: defend the token and one defended revolutionary patience; question it and one licensed reformist practice. Rosa Luxemburg's rejoinders and the orthodox literature of the following decades kept the association alive, tying the law to the breakdown question with a tightness the manuscript, as the editorial scholarship has since shown, does not obviously authorise.

The episode left two deposits in the text's afterlife. The first is the persistent popular equation of the falling rate with a collapse prophecy, which the misreadings section has already had to correct; that equation is a product of the 1890s reception, not of the three chapters. The second is a habit, on all sides, of treating the law as a loyalty test, so that scholarship about manuscripts or measurements gets read as coded politics; the Heinrich dispute of our own period has repeatedly been fought in that inherited key, with textual arguments answered as if they were factional ones. An exposition cannot repeal a century of reception, but it can refuse to write inside its categories, which is why this guide has kept the breakdown question outside its scope conditions and treated the editorial evidence as evidence. Readers who understand how the token was minted will be slower to spend it.

## The mechanical objection, at full strength

Now the objections, and they begin with the most technically serious one, pressed from inside Marxist economics itself since the middle of the twentieth century. Stated at full strength, it runs as follows. The formula shows the rate falling only if the composition rises faster than the rate of surplus value. But the very technical changes that raise the composition, mechanisation above all, simultaneously raise productivity, and higher productivity cheapens wage goods, which raises the rate of surplus value. The two variables are not independent dials; they are twin effects of one cause. Nothing in the mathematics guarantees that the denominator's growth outruns the numerator's, and Marx's derivation, the objection continues, simply assumes the race's result in the course of describing the runners. A law that asserts a direction without deriving it from its own premises is not a law but a conjecture wearing a law's clothes, and the honest verdict is indeterminacy: the ratio can move either way depending on the relative speeds, which theory alone cannot fix.

The objection gains force from noticing how the composition itself is measured. In value terms, mechanisation's effect on c over v is blunted by the cheapening of equipment, Marx's own third counterweight, so the denominator's allegedly relentless rise is itself hostage to price movements the theory cannot schedule in advance. Add the numerator's elasticity, intensity gains, global sourcing of cheaper wage goods, the migration of new needs into the subsistence basket cutting both ways, and the critic concludes that the exposition has identified a possibility, even an important one, while mistaking it for a necessity. Versions of this charge were developed by critics within the tradition who valued the rest of the framework and regretted what they saw as an overclaim at its centre, which is partly why it has stung more than external dismissals: it accepts the categories and denies the conclusion.

The reply, in its strongest available form, concedes the logical point and contests the framing. Yes, the formula alone fixes nothing; the law was never a theorem of algebra but a claim about asymmetric constraints in the world the algebra describes. The numerator's growth is bounded by the physiology and politics of the working day and by the composition of the wage basket, while mechanisation's pressure on the physical ratio of equipment to workers answers to no corresponding ceiling, because each round of productivity growth redefines the baseline from which the next round starts. Defenders add an empirical corollary: across long sweeps of industrial history, capital per worker has in fact risen persistently and massively, by orders of magnitude no plausible rise in the surplus share of the day could match arithmetically, since that share is trapped between zero and one while capital per head is not trapped at all. The rate of surplus value can double or treble; the equipment mediating each worker's labour has multiplied far beyond any such factor. On those magnitudes, they argue, the race is not indeterminate in practice even if the formula leaves it open in principle.

A fair exposition leaves the exchange there, scored honestly. The objection establishes that the law is not derivable from its definitions, and any defender who claims otherwise has overreached. The reply establishes that the law can still function as a substantive, falsifiable in principle claim about which constraints bind in practice, and that its defenders can point to the orders of magnitude involved rather than to logic alone. What neither side can do is settle the matter from the armchair, which is why the series routes the assessment to its own article, the [falling rate of profit debate](/2013/11/15/falling-rate-of-profit-debate/), where the contending arguments are weighed in full, and the data to the evidence article linked in the FAQ section below. Exposition that smuggles in a verdict, either way, has failed its office.

## A further objection: perhaps the composition does not rise in value terms

Between the mechanical objection and the methodological one sits a third challenge, less famous than either, which some economists regard as the most damaging of all. Grant the formula, grant the asymmetric limits, and then ask the awkward question: has the value composition actually risen across the long history of capitalism, once cheapening is properly netted out? The physical side is undisputed; capital per worker in any material sense has multiplied beyond recognition. But the denominator is a value, and the same productivity revolution that multiplied the machines also slashed the labour embodied in each of them. If the two movements roughly cancel in value terms, the tendency's engine is a physical intuition smuggled into a value argument, and the law fails not in its logic but in its premise. Defenders answer on two levels. They dispute the netting, pointing to the sheer scale of fixed investment in structures, infrastructure, and systems whose cheapening has been modest beside electronics, and to periods and countries where measured composition on replacement cost plainly climbs. And they reformulate, arguing that even a composition held flat by cheapening leaves the system more fragile, since the capital at risk in a crisis, the employment hostage to valorisation, and the minimum scale of entry all grow with the physical stock regardless of its unit values. The exchange shows the debate at its best: a premise everyone can state, evidence everyone can inspect, and conclusions that turn on construction choices in the statistics, which is why the measurement discussion in the evidence article matters more to this law's fate than any further refinement of its algebra.

## Dividing a squeezed surplus: interest, rent, and the fight over shares

A falling general rate does not distribute its pain evenly, and the third volume's later parts explain why the squeeze so often detonates in the financial and property spheres rather than in the factory. Interest and rent are claims with rigidities of their own: loans contract at fixed terms, leases run for years, and both are senior in practice to the active capitalist's residue. When the gross rate sinks toward the level of those standing claims, the profit of enterprise, the slice that rewards the functioning capitalist and justifies his risks in his own eyes, is compressed from both sides at once, by the fall in the whole and by the stickiness of the parts already promised away. Marx draws from this a portrait of mounting antagonism within the possessing class itself, lenders against borrowers, landlords against tenants of capital, each fraction experiencing the others as parasites on a shrinking fund, and of a corresponding ideological fog in which every fraction's revenue appears as the natural price of its own contribution. The closing part's anatomy of the trinity formula grows directly from that soil. For the law's exposition the moral is modest but useful: the social temperature of a profitability squeeze depends on the division machinery it passes through, which is why eras of gently falling rates can be eras of savage distributional politics, and why observers who watch only the production statistics miss the explosion when it comes in a rent strike, a debt crisis, or a banking panic wearing political clothes.

## Signals in a credit economy: why the rate hides

Between the tendency and any observer stands the credit system, and its distortions explain a puzzle that troubles newcomers: if the pressure is so fundamental, why does it not announce itself plainly to the people whose money is at stake? Part of the answer lies in the split of gross profit into interest and profit of enterprise, sketched earlier. The active industrialist experiences the residue after the lender is paid, and that residue is a leveraged magnitude, flattering in booms when credit is cheap and the gross rate stands still, savage in contractions when interest claims stay fixed while surplus shrinks. The magnitude the law describes, the gross return on total capital, is nobody's direct experience; each participant meets a derivative of it, geared and filtered.

A second filter is fictitious capital, Marx's term for claims on future revenue, shares and bonds and their modern descendants, whose prices capitalise expected income at the going interest rate rather than tracking any capital actually functioning in production. Asset markets can therefore boom on expectations while the underlying rate stagnates, and the wealth effect of the boom disguises the stagnation from exactly the class best placed to notice it. When expectations break, the same machinery runs in reverse with interest. None of this is decoration on the theory: the third volume's later parts treat credit and interest bearing capital at great length, and the law's chapter on internal contradictions already leans on the credit system as the mechanism by which overaccumulation expresses itself as speculative fever before it expresses itself as collapse. For the exposition's purposes the lesson is epistemic. The absence of a felt emergency in financial pages is weak evidence about the tendency, because the instruments through which the system perceives itself were analysed by the theory's author as systematically distorting lenses. Critics may reply that a theory which discounts contrary appearances in advance has taken another step toward the immunity examined next, and the reply has enough force to deserve its section rather than a footnote. It gets one.

## What counts as capital: the measurement problem in depth

Since a premise of the doctrine turns out to be hostage to statistics, the statistics deserve a section of their own, at the level of the choices involved rather than of results, which belong to the evidence article. Four decisions dominate every attempt to build the series the law needs, and each can flip a conclusion.

The first is valuation. A capital stock carried at historic cost, what was actually paid for plant across the decades, embodies the prices of vanished eras and understates the weight of old equipment relative to new; the same stock at replacement cost, what it would cost to reproduce at present prices, lets cheapening shrink the denominator in step with productivity. Whole schools in the empirical literature divide on this choice, which is not a technicality but a theoretical commitment in disguise, since historic cost honours the sums actually advanced and hazarded, while replacement cost honours the current sacrifice reproduction would demand. Marx's own texts can be, and have been, cited for both. The second decision concerns depreciation: whether profit is taken gross, before the wearing out of plant is charged, or net, and over what assumed lifetimes the wearing is spread. Gross series flatter profitability in mechanised eras, when depreciation is a fat slice of output; net series punish it; the choice interacts with composition itself, since a rising capital stock drags a rising depreciation charge behind it like a shadow.

The third decision draws the boundary of the aggregate. Finance poses the sharpest puzzle: its profits are real income to its owners, yet within Marx's categories financial returns are largely a redistribution of surplus produced elsewhere, so including the sector at face value double counts the numerator while its balance sheet inflates the denominator with claims rather than with functioning capital. Housing, government production, and the intellectual property that modern accounts capitalise each pose their own version of the puzzle, and national statistical revisions that suddenly capitalise research spending can rewrite a series overnight without a single factory changing hands. The fourth decision concerns utilisation: a plant idle in a slump advances just as much capital as a plant running flat out, yet produces no surplus while it stands, so measured rates plunge in crises for reasons that belong to the cycle rather than to any secular tendency, and separating cycle from trend across a century of data is a statistical art with its own controversies.

None of this makes measurement futile; it makes it argumentative, in the honourable sense that the assumptions must be defended rather than hidden. The practical upshot for readers of the wider literature is a checklist discipline: before crediting any chart of the profit rate, falling or rising, ask how the stock was valued, whether profit is gross or net, where the boundary was drawn, and how the cycle was handled. A study that survives those four questions has earned its hearing. A study that cannot answer them has produced a number in search of an argument, and the law deserves better from its friends and its enemies alike.

## The unfalsifiability charge, at full strength

The second great objection is methodological, and it deserves statement without softening. A theory that can accommodate every possible observation, the charge runs, explains nothing, because a claim compatible with all outcomes forbids none. The falling rate apparatus, critics say, is engineered for exactly that immunity. When the measured rate falls, the tendency is confirmed at work. When the measured rate rises, the counteracting influences are confirmed at work. When it stagnates, the two are pronounced in balance. Heads, the law wins; tails, the evidence loses. Worse, the counterweights are elastic enough to absorb any surprise after the fact: some influence can always be found, in a list of six plus the reserve of unspecified interactions, to carry the explanatory load. A construct so insulated, the charge concludes, belongs to metaphysics or to rhetoric, and its survival owes more to the loyalty of its adherents than to any discipline the world has imposed on it. Versions of this complaint have come from philosophers of science, from mainstream economists, and from Marxists themselves who fear the doctrine discredits the parts of the tradition built to stricter standards.

Feel the force of the point before reaching for the reply, because the reply is only worth making against the strong version. If all a theory ever does is rename outcomes in its own vocabulary, it has no empirical content, and the falling rate literature undeniably contains stretches of writing that do exactly that, waving at countertendencies as a generic solvent whenever a series misbehaves. A defence that ignores those stretches defends a fantasy version of the tradition. The question is whether the construct itself, handled with the discipline its own text prescribes, is condemned to that vacuity, or whether the vacuity is a failure mode of careless use.

### What observation would defenders have to accept as counting against the tendency?

A durable rise in the measured rate across a long period, in an economy whose value composition was also rising and where the six named influences could be independently shown weak or absent, would contradict the claim directly. The test demands separate measurement of the offsets rather than inference from the rate itself, which keeps the claim honest.

The reply builds from that discipline. The same-part rule, turned methodological, forbids invoking a counterweight without naming it and identifying its channel and, where possible, its measurable footprint: wage share movements for the exploitation and wage suppression influences, capital goods price indices for cheapening, unemployment and sectoral data for the reserve army, trade composition for foreign commerce, financial structure for share capital. A period attribution that cannot produce such footprints is not an application of the theory but an incantation, and critics are entitled to scorn it as one. With the footprints produced, the claim becomes a structured account whose parts can be separately checked and separately wrong. Several research programmes in the debate literature operate in precisely this spirit, decomposing movements in profitability into composition effects, exploitation effects, and price effects, and publishing results that sometimes discomfort the law's friends. That such work exists, and is cited by both sides, shows the construct can support falsifiable practice even if its slogan form invites abuse.

Two honest qualifications complete the section. First, tendencies in social science generally sit nearer the edge of falsifiability than laboratory laws, because their offsets are real features of open systems rather than excuses; the same methodological debate surrounds tendencies in economics of every school, from gravitation metaphors in price theory onward, and singling out Marx's construct for a standard its rivals would also fail is its own kind of special pleading. Second, the charge retains permanent value as a discipline on the theory's users even where it fails as a refutation of the theory: every invocation of a countertendency should be forced to show its papers. This guide has tried to model that standard by treating each influence as a mechanism with limits, rather than as a member of a rescue squad, and readers should demand the same of anything else they read on the subject, from whatever camp.

## The choice of technique problem: Okishio

A distinct technical challenge, often confused with the mechanical objection but different in structure, comes from the Japanese economist Nobuo Okishio, whose result of the early 1960s has organised discussion ever since. Where the mechanical objection says the race's outcome is indeterminate, Okishio's theorem claims to fix the outcome against Marx under stated conditions. Suppose capitalists adopt only techniques that cut their own costs at ruling prices, as competition compels, and suppose the real wage, the basket workers actually receive, stays constant. Then, the theorem shows within an equilibrium model, the general rate of profit after the dust settles cannot be lower than before; viable innovation, individually chosen, raises or preserves the social rate. If sound and applicable, the result cuts the law off at the root, because it denies that individually rational mechanisation can produce the aggregate fall.

### What is the Okishio challenge in plain terms?

Okishio showed that if capitalists adopt only techniques that reduce their own costs at existing prices, and the real wage basket stays unchanged, then the new general rate of profit cannot fall once prices adjust. Competition driven mechanisation, on those assumptions, lifts or maintains the social rate, which contradicts the idea that rational adoption by itself generates a downward tendency.

The ensuing literature is a case study in how much depends on assumptions a theorem wears lightly. Critics in the temporal single system tradition, Andrew Kliman prominent among them, argue that the result rests on valuing inputs and outputs simultaneously at post innovation prices, whereas real accumulation unfolds in time, with capital advanced at yesterday's prices and products sold at tomorrow's, and that under temporal valuation the fall reappears as a possibility the theorem cannot exclude. Others dispute the constant real wage premise, noting that productivity growth historically arrives with rising real wages, which changes the arithmetic; still others question the equilibrium framing itself, since the law's home terrain is the disequilibrium scramble of accumulation, not a comparison of settled states. Defenders of Okishio reply that relaxing any single assumption does not rescue a general tendency, that each rescue must be argued case by case, and that the theorem at minimum destroyed the casual version of the law in which mechanisation falling profit follows as a matter of course. That minimum achievement is widely conceded across the dispute, and it marks the point where casual exposition, of the kind this guide refuses, genuinely ends. The full weighing belongs to the debate article; the exposition's duty is to ensure no reader leaves believing the theorem does not exist, or believing it settles everything, since serious people continue to deny both shortcuts.

One further rejoinder deserves recording because it changes the question rather than the assumptions. Anwar Shaikh has argued that the theorem's picture of technique choice is itself too gentle: real competition, on his account, is turbulent rather than equilibrating, and firms judge a new method not by its effect on some social average but by whether it undercuts rivals at the point of production, with the regulating capitals of each industry setting the terms the rest must meet. On that terrain, he contends, a falling rate emerges from the battle itself rather than being excluded by it, and his empirical work decomposes profitability movements in ways he reads as favourable to the tendency. Critics reply that turbulent gravitation is a research programme with its own burden of proof, not a refutation of a theorem proved inside its stated frame. The exchange, still argued in the journals, confirms the pattern this guide keeps meeting: the law's fate is decided in the workshops of valuation, equilibrium method, and measurement, far from the slogans under which it marches in public.

## Neighbours, not twins: the labour share and the profit squeeze

Public debate often conscripts the falling rate into service for adjacent claims it does not make, and two such neighbours deserve formal separation. The first is the falling labour share, the much discussed decline, across many economies in recent decades, in the proportion of national income paid as wages. A falling labour share means a rising profit share, and the careless reader files it as the opposite of Marx's tendency, or, with equal carelessness, as its confirmation. It is neither, because shares and rates are different animals: the share divides income between classes within a period, while the rate divides a period's surplus by a stock accumulated across many periods. A rising profit share can perfectly well coexist with a falling profit rate whenever the capital stock grows faster than the surplus, which is precisely the configuration the doctrine describes; indeed the three act illustration above contains exactly that combination in its second act. Conflating share with rate is the mass confusion of Section Ten wearing statistical dress.

The second neighbour is the profit squeeze theory of crisis, developed by writers such as Andrew Glyn and Bob Sutcliffe to explain the profitability collapse of the late 1960s and 1970s in Britain and beyond. On that account the rate fell because workers, organised and confident at full employment, captured a rising share and resisted the intensification that might have restored margins: the numerator was squeezed by the wage, not outrun by the denominator. The two theories are rivals inside the same tradition about the same episodes, and the rivalry is instructive, because it shows the falling rate doctrine facing competition not only from outside critics but from sibling explanations with different mechanisms and different politics. Episodes can in principle be decomposed to arbitrate between them, asking how much of a given fall came through composition and how much through distribution, and the debate literature contains exactly such arbitrations with mixed outcomes. The exposition's obligation is taxonomic hygiene: when a writer says profitability fell, the disciplined reader asks which ratio, moved by which variable, on whose account, before allowing the falling rate of profit to be either credited or blamed.

## Restatements within the tradition

A doctrine's afterlife tells a reader which parts of it later generations found load bearing, and the falling rate has been restated so often, in such different keys, that the variations themselves instruct. Henryk Grossmann, writing in the late 1920s, gave the law its most uncompromising form, embedding it in a reproduction model in which accumulation, pursued far enough, reaches a point where the surplus can no longer simultaneously feed the capitalists, expand constant capital at the required pace, and expand employment at the accustomed wage; his breakdown emphasis drew immediate criticism from within the movement, including from writers who accepted the tendency while denying that any such arithmetical terminus had been derived, and the quarrel fixed a template: within the tradition, the fight is rarely over whether composition rises, but over what the rise licenses one to conclude.

Ernest Mandel's later work bent the argument into the theory of long waves, treating extended periods of rising and sinking profitability as the skeleton of capitalist periodisation, with counteracting influences promoted, demoted, and rearranged into turning points explained by class struggle outcomes, wars, and technological revolutions. Critics found the wave machinery too accommodating, able to explain any periodisation after the fact, which is the unfalsifiability anxiety returning in historical dress. David Harvey's restatements emphasised the geographical and financial escape valves, surplus capital absorbed by spatial expansion, by credit creation, by asset inflation, each fix displacing the pressure rather than dissolving it, a reading that leans heavily on the trade and share capital influences while extending them beyond anything the manuscript names. Andrew Kliman and the temporal single system school, by contrast, restated the law by repairing its foundations, arguing that consistent temporal valuation restores the fall as a genuine possibility against Okishio and that much twentieth century dismissal rested on valuation conventions Marx would not have recognised. A reader need not arbitrate among these heirs to learn from their disagreement: the core that survives every restatement is the race between composition and exploitation, while everything downstream, breakdown, waves, fixes, remains contested territory where the tradition argues with itself. That pattern, a stable mechanism wrapped in unstable conclusions, is exactly what the same-part rule would predict for a doctrine whose author stated pressure and offsets together and declined to publish the synthesis.

## From tendency to crisis, without smuggling a verdict

The third chapter of the part in Engels's arrangement, the one on the law's internal contradictions, connects the falling tendency to the periodic convulsions of the system, and a short orientation belongs here even though the assessment of crisis theory belongs elsewhere. The connecting thought is that a rate under persistent pressure does not decline like a temperature on a chart; it is defended, violently and inventively, by the agents whose incomes depend on it. Credit expands to sustain investment whose returns no longer justify it at sober valuations. Speculation detaches asset prices from the surplus available to service them. Production runs ahead of the consumption the wage structure permits, inventories pile up, and the eventual correction arrives not as a gentle repricing but as a crisis in which capital values are destroyed wholesale, plant written down, debts defaulted, workers dismissed. In Marx's presentation the destruction is itself functional within the system's brutal logic: devalued constant capital cheapens the denominator, unemployment rebuilds the reserve that disciplines wages, and the rate recovers on the ruins, setting the stage for a new round of accumulation at restored profitability.

Presented that way, the falling tendency is less a prediction about a statistic than the deep grammar of a cycle, and much of the law's hold on later imaginations comes from this chapter rather than from the formula. It also explains the law's persistent entanglement with collapse theories it does not actually contain. Marx's text describes recovery mechanisms built into the crisis itself, so a reading that extracts inevitable final breakdown from this part is adding a conclusion the argument withholds; breakdown extrapolations belong to later interpreters and are argued over in the wider crisis literature. The families of Marxist crisis explanation, underconsumption, disproportionality, profit squeeze, and the falling rate among them, are mapped comparatively in the crisis theory guide already linked, and readers who want the empirical record assembled and interrogated should go next to the dedicated study of [rate of profit empirical evidence](/2013/12/01/rate-of-profit-empirical-evidence/), where series, definitions, and country cases receive the scrutiny this exposition deliberately postpones.

### Which of the six offsets has carried the greatest weight across the longest stretches?

Cheapening of the elements of constant capital has the strongest claim, since productivity growth in machine making, extraction, transport, and electronics has cut the value of equipment and materials per unit of capacity across long periods. Exploitation gains run it close in particular eras, while wage suppression and the reserve army have dominated shorter, harsher intervals rather than whole epochs.

That ranking, offered as orientation rather than verdict, illustrates the article's method one final time. The six influences are not a blur of unspecified resistance; they have different signatures, different theatres, and different strengths in different periods, and an exposition that keeps them distinct equips a reader to interrogate any empirical claim made in the law's name. What remains is to equip the three audiences this series always addresses, and then to answer the questions readers actually bring.

## What the tendency would look like if it were gaining

Speculation about signatures disciplines both camps, so it is worth asking what the world should look like if the tendency were, on balance, gaining ground across a long period despite its offsets. Several fingerprints recur in the sympathetic literature. The capital output ratio should drift upward, more accumulated value standing behind each unit of annual product. Returns on new investment at the margin should disappoint the returns embedded in older vintages, so that expansion requires either cheaper credit or lower expectations to proceed. Firms should lean harder on the offsets: lengthening effective working time through flexibility, sourcing from ever cheaper labour pools, financial engineering to reclassify and leverage returns, and political pressure to socialise costs. Crises should exhibit the devaluation signature, with recoveries built on written down capital rather than on any solution to the underlying proportions. Sceptics can accept the list as a fair statement of what would count, while noting that each fingerprint admits rival explanations, from demographics to monopoly to measurement drift, which is why fingerprint matching persuades fewer neutrals than its practitioners hope.

The most prominent recent encounter between the doctrine and a neighbouring framework came through Thomas Piketty's work on capital and inequality, which placed a return on capital, his r, at the centre of a famous inequality: when r exceeds the growth rate, inherited wealth compounds faster than the economy grows. Piketty's long run portrait has the return comparatively stable across centuries, punctured by wars and inflations rather than ground down by accumulation, and his mechanism for its resilience, substitution toward capital as it accumulates, is in effect a claim that the offsets, above all cheapening and reallocation, systematically win the race Marx described. Marxist critics answered variously: disputing his capital concept, which folds housing and financial assets into a single stock; disputing his valuation at market prices, which lets asset booms masquerade as productive capacity; and disputing the stability finding itself on their own series. The encounter matters for readers of this guide less for its score than for its demonstration that the question Marx posed, what happens to the return on capital as capital accumulates, remains a live organising question of political economy, asked now with better data and the same conceptual traps. Recognising the traps, shares against rates, stocks against flows, values against prices, is most of what this exposition has been for.

## Services and the shifting sectoral boundary

One structural transformation of the advanced economies bears directly on the aggregate composition and is too often left out of expositions: the long migration of employment from agriculture and manufacturing into services. Many services, in their classic forms, work with thin constant capital per worker, a practitioner's skill, a room, a modest kit, beside the refinery's towers of embodied investment, so a sectoral shift toward services mechanically lowers the social average composition even while composition inside each sector climbs. The aggregate race therefore runs on two tracks at once, within sector mechanisation pushing the average up, between sector migration pushing it down, and the net movement is an empirical resultant that neither track's partisans may claim as their own. Modern complications multiply quickly. Some services industrialise, logistics and retail building vast automated infrastructures whose composition rivals any factory's, while digital services rest on enormous fixed investments in networks and data centres paired with comparatively small direct workforces, a profile that inverts the classic picture entirely. Health and education, the great employers of the service era, display the cost behaviour economists call the Baumol effect, their productivity growing slowly precisely because the labour is the product, which holds their compositions low and their relative costs rising. A serious decomposition of any modern economy's rate must therefore report the sectoral arithmetic alongside the within sector story, and partisans of the tendency who quote only factory evidence, or sceptics who quote only the service share, are each exhibiting half a mechanism. The law was framed for an economy whose commanding heights were mills and railways; its translation to an economy of hospitals, platforms, and warehouses is possible, the categories travel, but the translation is work, and the work is exactly where the contemporary debate, surveyed in the evidence article, is being done.

## One law, many economies: the tendency on a world scale

The scope rules described the claim's home as the total social capital, and a final complication asks which society's totality is meant once capital crosses borders. Marx wrote for a world market already knitting national capitals together, and his trade influence presupposes the traffic, yet the general rate in his exposition is usually framed within a single economy's equalisation. Reality supplies a gradient instead: capital migrates toward higher returns across frontiers, but imperfectly, checked by risk, distance, law, and the immobility of plant once sunk, so national rates persist at differing levels for generations, with the advanced economies' capitals harvesting, through trade and investment, rates in less developed regions that their home compositions would never permit. Two opposite morals are drawn from the gradient. One tradition, running through the theories of imperialism that followed Marx, treats the harvest from abroad as the master counterweight of the mature economies, the explanation of why the tendency failed to bite where it was born, purchased at the price of exporting its pressures. Another points out that integration progressively globalises the composition itself, as supplier regions mechanise in their turn, so that the relevant totality enlarges until the law's proper unit becomes the world economy and national refuges close one by one. The empirical article in this series takes up country cases in the detail they require; exposition need only fix the point that the tendency's scope was always going to be contested terrain, because the system's own expansion keeps redrawing the boundary inside which its average is computed, and a claim defined over a totality whose borders move is a claim whose testing must specify its map as carefully as its dates.

## Scope conditions: where the claim claims to apply

Precision about scope prevents a family of pointless arguments, so the boundaries deserve explicit statement. The law is a claim about the capitalist mode of production as a whole, at the level of the total social capital, across its long development. It is not a claim about any individual firm, whose fortunes depend on its position in the equalisation process; not about any single branch, whose composition may sit far from the average and stay there; not about pre capitalist economies, where production is not organised by competing capitals advancing value for profit; and not about any short interval, since the offsets can dominate intervals of considerable length. Arguments that violate a scope condition do not test the doctrine; they test a mannequin dressed in its clothes.

Two further boundaries cut deeper. The first separates the value analysis from its monetary expression. The ratio at the doctrine's heart is defined over values, quantities of socially necessary labour time, while every observable statistic is denominated in prices that deviate from values in systematic ways the third volume itself analyses. Bridging the two is possible, the transformation literature attempts nothing else, but the bridge is a piece of theory in its own right, and any empirical claim that ignores it has skipped a step its author would have caught. The second boundary separates the tendency from the wider claim that capitalism must end. Nothing in the three chapters dates, schedules, or necessitates a terminal crisis; the contradictions chapter describes a self correcting violence, corrections included, and the leap from recurrent crisis to final collapse is made by later hands for their own reasons. Readers who keep those two boundaries in view will find that many celebrated refutations and many celebrated confirmations alike dissolve into arguments about other propositions, while the actual doctrine, narrower and stranger than either, remains standing in the middle, awaiting the assessment this series reserves for its debate article.

What, then, can a reader now do with the argument that could not be done before the exposition? State the mechanism as a race between two ratios and name the letter any new claim must move. Recite the six offsets as mechanisms with channels and limits rather than as a slogan's bodyguard. Distinguish rate from mass on sight, in journalism and in textbooks alike. Discount claims that lean on the finished architecture of chapters without acknowledging the editor who built them, and claims that lean on the offsets without producing their footprints. And carry the unanswered questions to the right addresses: assessment to the debate article, evidence to the evidence article, placement among the crisis families to the crisis guide. Exposition ends where judgement begins, and it ends, if it has done its work, with the reader harder to fool in either direction.

## Feedback: when a falling rate slows its own cause

A last refinement keeps the exposition honest about dynamics, because the tendency does not act on a passive system. Accumulation is funded and motivated by profit, so a rate under pressure eventually discourages the investment that was driving composition upward: projects at the margin are shelved, expansion plans shrink to replacement, and the growth of the denominator decelerates precisely because the numerator's yield disappointed. The process therefore carries a brake of its own, quite apart from crises and counterweights, and several formal treatments in the literature build the brake in explicitly, letting the pace of mechanisation respond to the profitability it erodes and finding oscillatory behaviour, long swings of advance and hesitation, rather than a one way grind. The brake also works through expectations and credit: lenders who see thinning returns shorten maturities and raise spreads, raising the interest hurdle the squeezed profit of enterprise must clear, which prunes investment further and can turn a gentle deceleration into the abrupt stop the contradictions chapter dramatises. Feedback of this kind cuts both ways in the dispute. Defenders cite it as the reason the tendency expresses itself in waves and crises rather than in a smooth statistical descent, a signature the configurations sections found in the record. Sceptics answer that a tendency equipped with brakes, offsets, devaluations, and waves has accumulated enough auxiliary machinery to accommodate any conceivable history, which is the unfalsifiability charge returning by the service door. The exposition's position has been consistent: each auxiliary must show its mechanism and its footprint, and the feedback loop can, in investment rates, credit spreads, and the vintage structure of the capital stock. Where those footprints are produced, the loop is an explanation; where they are waved at, it is an alibi, and readers now hold the checklist that tells the two apart.

## Why the argument refuses to die

Obituaries for this doctrine have been published at regular intervals for a hundred years, by economists certain that marginalism had dissolved its categories, by historians certain that rising living standards had refuted its spirit, and by former believers certain that its failures disgraced the tradition that housed it. The doctrine's survival is itself a datum worth explaining, and the explanation is not merely sociological loyalty, though loyalty has played its part. The argument survives because it answers a question that every economics must answer somehow and most answer by assumption: what happens to the return on accumulated capital as accumulation proceeds without limit? Theories that treat the rate as set by time preference, by technology's marginal product, or by monetary policy each smuggle their answer into a premise, where it sits invisible and therefore unargued. Marx's construction at least drags the question into the open and proposes a mechanism whose parts can be separately inspected, which is why each generation's crisis sends readers back to the three chapters with fresh suspicions that the mechanism might be the one their own framework lacks. It survives, second, because its failure modes are instructive: the indeterminacy objection taught economists to demand derivations, the Okishio theorem taught them to specify equilibrium and valuation, the measurement wars taught them that aggregates are arguments. A doctrine can earn its keep as the whetstone of its rivals even while its own edge stays contested. And it survives, third, because the phenomenon it names, the uneasy sense that each successive mountain of capital must be coaxed to yield what a molehill once yielded freely, keeps presenting itself to participants in forms their official theories describe awkwardly, from the secular decline of interest rates to the spectacle of corporations drowning in cash they cannot profitably place. Whether the mechanism in the manuscript is the right account of that unease is the debate this series stages elsewhere. That the unease is real, recurrent, and badly served by slogans in either direction, is the conviction with which this exposition is content to close.

## A checklist for reading any profitability claim

Everything in this guide converges on a practical instrument, and stating it plainly, in prose, gives readers something to carry into the next article, broadcast, or textbook they meet. When a claim about profitability and Marx's law appears, ask first which magnitude is actually being discussed, rate or mass, share or return, and refuse the conversation until the answer is fixed, since half of all disagreements in this field are two magnitudes wearing one name. Ask second which letter of the ratio the claim moves, and by what mechanism, and treat any answer that cannot name a letter as atmosphere. Ask third which counteracting influences the period supplied and which the claimant has measured rather than assumed, remembering that invoking an offset without its footprint is the theory's characteristic vice. Ask fourth how the capital stock was valued and where the aggregate's boundary was drawn, the two statistical choices that silently decide most empirical verdicts. Ask fifth what scope the claim respects, whole economy or sector, long run or interval, and whether its evidence matches that scope or merely gestures at it. Ask sixth, and last, what textual layer the authority rests on when Marx is quoted: the finished first volume, the posthumous third in its editor's architecture, or the notebooks behind both, since the same sentence carries different weight at different layers. Six questions, none requiring mathematics beyond division, and a reader armed with them can grade nearly everything written on the subject, including, as a final exercise, this guide.

The checklist has one further use that deserves mention before the personas take over, because it converts private reading into a teachable habit. A student who runs the six questions against a single assigned chapter, in writing, produces exactly the kind of paragraph examiners reward: magnitude fixed, mechanism named, offsets interrogated, statistics cross examined, scope respected, provenance noted. A journalist who runs them against a draft paragraph catches, before an editor or a hostile reader can, the sentence where a share slipped into a rate's clothing or where a decade was asked to refute a century. And a researcher who runs them against a new dataset's documentation learns in an afternoon whether the series can speak to the doctrine at all, a cheaper education than discovering the mismatch after the regressions are run. Instruments earn their keep by travelling, and this one was built to travel from a nineteenth century manuscript to whatever screen the reader is holding, unchanged in form because the confusions it polices have proved unchanged in substance.

## A glossary of the moving parts, in prose

Exposition of this density accumulates terms, and a compact restatement of the load bearing ones, in connected prose, saves readers from carrying ten definitions in suspension. Constant capital is the value advanced for means of production, so named because that value reappears in the product without growing in the production process itself. Variable capital is the value advanced for labour power, so named because labour in action creates more value than its own price, and the difference, surplus value, is the source from which every revenue in the system is ultimately drawn. The rate of surplus value compares that surplus with the wage advance alone and measures exploitation in the technical sense. The rate of profit compares the same surplus with the whole advance and measures what the owner of the total capital receives on his commitment. The organic composition, defined in its own section above, relates the two advances to each other under the discipline of the underlying technique. Prices of production are the equalisation adjusted prices that let capitals of differing composition earn the average rate, and moral depreciation is the loss of value that strikes equipment when progress makes it obsolete before it is worn out. Fictitious capital names the tradable claims on future revenue whose prices obey the interest rate and expectation rather than any stock of functioning plant, and the relative surplus population names the layer of workers rendered superfluous to accumulation's immediate needs by the very process that employs the rest. A reader who can move among those nine terms without hesitation owns the vocabulary of the whole debate; the arguments are then free to be about the world rather than about the words.

## For students and exam candidates

Examiners reward three things on this topic and punish a fourth. First, the formula, stated cleanly with every symbol defined: rate of profit equals surplus value over constant plus variable capital, rewritten as the rate of surplus value divided by one plus the organic composition. Second, the six counteracting influences, listed completely and in an order that shows they were learned as a set rather than sampled; dropping share capital and relative surplus population is the commonest way strong candidates bleed marks. Third, the tendency distinction, deployed explicitly: the rate need not fall in any given period, and an answer that concedes this while explaining why scores higher than one bluffing a monotone decline. The punished habit is verdict smuggling, asserting the law true or false when the question asked for exposition. Where a question does invite evaluation, the mechanical objection and the Okishio result are the two criticisms markers most respect, precisely because both engage the mechanism instead of the slogan. Keep a worked numerical example ready, with invented round numbers labelled as illustration, and practise converting it: raise the composition, hold exploitation, watch the rate; then raise exploitation and watch the race. Ninety seconds of that arithmetic, reproduced in the hall, anchors an essay that generalities cannot.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The productive seminar on this material begins with arithmetic, not ideology: put the two period illustration on the board, let students compute both rates, and watch the room discover that exploitation and profitability can climb in opposite directions. From there, the same-part rule makes an excellent discussion charter. Divide the counteracting influences among groups, require each group to name its influence's channel through the ratio and its historical theatre, and then ask the assembled class which influences a given decade's data would even in principle reveal. The editorial question sustains a second session almost by itself, especially if students compare a headed edition's third part with a description of the unheaded manuscript and debate what headings do to arguments. Guard against the two seminar failure modes: the session that becomes a referendum on capitalism, decided by prior allegiance, and the session that becomes antiquarian, all provenance and no mechanism. The text rewards groups that keep asking the boring, decisive question of every claim in the room: which letter moved, and how do you know.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Researchers should start from the manuscripts rather than from the received architecture: the third part's draft, its place in the volume's genesis, and the later economic notebooks are the terrain on which the Heinrich dispute is fought, and citations of chapter and verse from the standard edition should carry a note that divisions and headings are editorial. The measurement literature repays triage by definition: separate studies by whether their composition series use current or historic cost valuation, whether their profit concept is net or gross of depreciation and taxes, and which sectors sit inside the aggregate, since most headline disagreements trace back to those choices rather than to the data. Keeping a personal research store where the ratio decompositions, the rival series, and the manuscript findings sit side by side, tagged by definition used, is the only way the field stays navigable; the study notes companion at [VaultBook Marxism study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) was built for exactly that kind of structured hoarding. Journalists, meanwhile, should retire two reflexes: treating record aggregate profits as evidence about the rate, and treating any single decade's profitability as a verdict on a claim about long run pressure and offset. The honest story is usually in the decomposition, and it is a better story besides.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is the tendency of the rate of profit to fall?

It is Marx's argument that capitalist accumulation systematically presses the general rate of profit downward over the long run. The rate divides surplus value by the total capital advanced. Competition drives mechanisation, mechanisation raises the mass of means of production handled by each worker, and that rising organic composition enlarges the denominator faster, on Marx's account, than exploitation can enlarge the numerator. The claim concerns a directional pressure working against named counteracting influences, not a forecast that any measured series must decline steadily from year to year.

### Q: How does the falling rate of profit work mechanically?

Take the rate as surplus value divided by constant plus variable capital, then divide top and bottom by variable capital to get the rate of surplus value over one plus the organic composition. Mechanisation raises the composition term, since each worker sets more plant and material in motion. Unless the rate of surplus value rises proportionally, which would require the surplus share of the working day to keep expanding, the overall rate sinks. The mechanism is that race between two ratios, both moved by the same productivity growth that competition enforces.

### Q: What are the counteracting influences?

Marx names six in the chapter following the law's own statement. Exploitation can intensify, through a longer or denser working day or cheaper wage goods. Wages can be pressed below the value of labour power. The elements of constant capital can cheapen even as their physical mass grows. A relative surplus population can restrain wages and feed low composition branches. Foreign trade can cheapen inputs and food while widening markets. And the growth of share capital spreads returns across holdings content with something nearer an interest yield. Each works on an identifiable part of the ratio.

### Q: Why is it called a tendency rather than a law?

Because the downward pressure operates permanently alongside forces that offset it, several of them generated by the same accumulation process. Marx sets out those counteracting influences in the same part of the same volume as the law itself, so the doctrine was never a prediction of uninterrupted decline. The observable rate at any moment is the net outcome of the contest between the pressure and its offsets, which is why the rate can rise for long periods without the underlying claim being either confirmed or refuted by that fact alone.

### Q: Did Marx think this was his most important discovery?

In the Grundrisse notebooks he called the falling rate, in a much quoted phrase, the most important law of modern political economy, a judgement he attached to it before the mature statement in the third volume existed. The ranking is striking because it places an economic mechanism above better known doctrines in his own estimation. Readers should still handle the phrase carefully: it comes from private notebooks of the late 1850s, it praises the law's explanatory reach for barriers and crises, and later editorial scholarship asks how settled his commitment remained.

### Q: Where is the falling rate of profit set out in Capital?

The material occupies the third part of the third volume, the volume Engels prepared for publication in 1894 from manuscripts Marx left unfinished. Engels's edition presents the part as three chapters, on the law itself, on the counteracting influences, and on the development of the law's internal contradictions, numbered thirteen to fifteen in standard editions. The divisions and headings are the editor's, since the underlying manuscript of the mid 1860s ran continuously without them. The argument's embryo appears earlier, in the Grundrisse notebooks of the late 1850s.

### Q: Did Engels shape the presentation of this law?

Yes, in form rather than in content, on the standard scholarly account. The manuscript underlying the third part carried no chapter divisions or headings, so the crisp sequence of law, counteracting influences, and internal contradictions that readers cite is Engels's arrangement of material Marx wrote. His preface acknowledges the scale of his editorial labour on the volume. What remains disputed is the consequence: whether the arrangement merely organised a doctrine Marx held, or lent systematic finality to an argument its author was still questioning in his later studies.

### Q: Does a falling rate of profit mean falling profits?

No, and Marx is explicit that the two magnitudes can move in opposite directions. The mass of profit grows with the scale of production, the number of workers employed, and the intensity of their exploitation, even while the rate, which divides that mass by a growing total capital, declines. A bigger surplus spread across a still bigger advance means record absolute profits alongside a thinner percentage return. Confusing headline figures for aggregate corporate profit with the rate is the commonest elementary error in popular discussion of the law.

### Q: Can the rate of profit fall while exploitation rises?

Yes, and the possibility is the analytical heart of the argument. Exploitation is measured by the rate of surplus value, surplus labour relative to necessary labour, and it rises whenever the surplus share of the working day grows. The rate of profit falls whenever the organic composition rises faster than that rate of surplus value. A worked example makes it concrete: deepen exploitation by half while the capital advanced per worker more than doubles, and workers are squeezed harder inside a system whose return on each unit of capital has nevertheless shrunk.

### Q: Is the falling rate of profit still taught?

It remains a standard element of courses on Marxist economics, classical political economy, and the history of economic thought, usually taught as a pair with its counteracting influences and increasingly alongside the editorial scholarship about the third volume's construction. Examination syllabuses in several traditions still set it directly, and it anchors survey accounts of Marxist crisis theory. Outside the classroom it persists as a reference point in debates over automation, profitability, and long run growth, where it is invoked both by defenders of its insight and by critics who regard it as the tradition's weakest theorem.

### Q: How does the same-part rule stop readers mixing the law with its counter-tendencies?

The rule is a reading discipline: because Marx placed the law and its six counteracting influences in the same part of the same volume, no honest summary may present the law alone and no honest criticism may treat the influences as later excuses bolted on by defenders. Mixing happens when a writer quietly moves a claim from one side of that architecture to the other, stating the tendency as a bare prediction or dismissing the offsets as apologetics. Holding the two together, as the source holds them, prevents both distortions at once.

### Q: What should a student separate between a tendency and a prediction?

A prediction commits to an outcome at a time: a value, a date, a direction over a stated interval, and it fails when the world disagrees. A tendency commits to a pressure and to the mechanisms that oppose it, and it is assessed by examining whether the pressure exists, whether the offsets are real, and which dominated a period for identifiable reasons. A student who separates the two stops asking the sterile question of why the rate rose in some decade, and starts asking the examinable one: which forces were at work, through which channels, with what evidence.

### Q: Why does cheapening of constant capital slow the fall?

Because the denominator of the rate is a value sum, not a physical inventory. Productivity growth multiplies the machines and materials each worker handles, which pushes the composition up, but the same productivity growth cheapens each machine and each unit of material, which pulls the money value of that larger physical mass back down. When cheapening runs fast enough, the value composition can stagnate or fall while the technical composition soars, and the downward pressure on the rate is suspended for as long as input prices fall faster than volumes grow.

### Q: How is the organic composition of capital measured in the argument?

Conceptually it is the value composition, constant capital divided by variable capital, insofar as that ratio registers the technical composition, the physical relation between means of production and workers. Measuring it in practice forces choices: which assets count as constant capital, whether to value them at historic cost or replacement cost, how to treat depreciation, and which sectors enter the aggregate. Those choices explain much of the disagreement in the empirical literature. The argument itself needs only the directional claim, that mechanisation raises the ratio across long development, however the bookkeeping conventions are set.

### Q: What would rising unemployment do to the tendency in Marx's logic?

Rising unemployment enlarges the relative surplus population, and Marx counts that reserve among the counteracting influences. A swollen reserve disciplines wages, sometimes pressing them below the value of labour power, which lifts the surplus share. It also makes labour intensive, low composition branches profitable to expand, dragging the social average composition down or braking its rise. Both channels push the rate upward against the tendency. The relief is double edged, however, since mass unemployment also signals failing demand and can trigger the crisis conditions through which, in the third chapter of the part, values are destroyed and the rate later restored.
