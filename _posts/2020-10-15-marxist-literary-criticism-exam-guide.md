Literary theory becomes a different subject the moment it enters an examination hall, and nowhere is the transformation sharper than in the Marxist perspective. In the seminar room a candidate can range widely, follow an association, and let an essay discover its argument as it proceeds. Under timed conditions none of that freedom survives. The marker sees only what the page performs, and what the page must perform, on this topic, is a method applied to a text rather than a set of opinions about a story. A Marxist reading, for assessment purposes, analyses the conditions under which a work was produced, what its form presupposes, the class positions and sources of income arranged inside it, what the work cannot bring itself to say, and what its closing pages settle. It does not report the political opinions of an author, and it does not grade characters for their virtue. Candidates who grasp that definition early write a different species of answer from candidates who treat the perspective as a licence to hunt for class conflict in a plot.

Two errors account for most of the marks surrendered on this topic, and both are errors of aim rather than of knowledge. The first is writing about content when the question rewards analysis of form. An answer that recounts a strike in a novel, a miser in a play, or a factory in a poem, and then attaches political commentary to the recounting, reads to a marker as a summary wearing borrowed vocabulary. The second error is inferring the politics of a text from the politics of its author, treating a writer's known allegiances as a key that unlocks the work. A knowledgeable examiner regards that inference as a conceptual mistake, because the founding critical principle of the tradition itself denies it, and the sections ahead give the denial its proper name and its classic instance. Repair those two errors and an answer changes band. Leave them in place and no quantity of additional reading repairs the script, because the reading is being spent through a faulty instrument.

One further assurance belongs at the front of this guide, since anxiety about it quietly deforms scripts. Markers grade the reasoning visible on the page, and they do not grade the direction of a candidate's conclusion. A script arguing that Marxist criticism illuminates a set text can earn full credit, and a script arguing that the approach distorts that same text can earn full credit on identical conditions: the concepts handled accurately, the critics named precisely, the evidence drawn from the work, and the judgement stated together with the material that would count against it. Neither criticising nor defending Marxism risks marks, because the reasoning is assessed rather than the conclusion. Candidates who internalise that symmetry write braver and better answers, since they stop performing allegiance and start performing analysis, which is the only performance the scheme rewards.

## Literary theory under timed conditions: what the marker can reward

A marking scheme is a blunt instrument, and understanding its bluntness is a form of preparation. The scheme cannot reward sympathy with a perspective, breadth of private reading, or sincerity of political feeling. It rewards operations that leave traces on paper: a concept defined through its use, a critic attached to a claim, a feature of a text connected to an argument about the society that produced the text, a limitation conceded at the point where the evidence runs thin. Preparation organised around those traces looks different from preparation organised around coverage. Coverage asks whether a candidate has read about hegemony. Trace preparation asks whether the candidate can write three sentences in which hegemony does analytical work on a passage, sentences a stranger could follow and check against the passage itself.

The distinction explains a familiar injustice candidates complain about, in which a peer who knows less scores more. The peer usually knows less in the warehouse sense and more in the performance sense. Their stock of concepts is smaller, yet each concept arrives attached to a named critic, a type of textual evidence, and a known weakness. Under time pressure the smaller, better organised stock outperforms the larger, looser one, because the examination is a performance with a clock attached. This guide therefore treats knowledge as necessary and insufficient. Every section converts a portion of stock into a move: something a candidate does, in a paragraph, that a marker can see and credit.

Assessment contexts differ in costume while sharing that skeleton. School specifications place Marxist criticism among named critical perspectives and ask candidates to apply a perspective to a set text. Undergraduate programmes in English, film, media, and cultural studies set comparative and evaluative questions. Comparative literature papers test whether a method travels across languages and periods. Civil service optional papers in literature and national eligibility tests reward the same operations at greater length and with denser reference. Drama and theatre studies add the estrangement tradition of epic theatre to the expected repertoire. A candidate who learns the moves rather than the costume can walk into any of those rooms and recognise the task beneath the wording.

### How do you avoid writing about content in a Marxist reading?

ask what the form presupposes rather than what the plot depicts. A realist novel presupposes a knowable society and a narratable inner life; a first-person retrospective presupposes a survivor with leisure to write. Naming what the form assumes demonstrates method rather than summary.

That short answer carries the entire doctrine of the guide in miniature, and the remainder of the article unpacks it at the length the doctrine deserves. Notice what the answer refuses. It refuses the plot, which every candidate in the hall can recount and which therefore distinguishes nobody. It refuses the theme stated as a topic, since naming injustice as a theme says nothing about how a text organises a reader's knowledge of injustice. Instead it directs attention to presupposition: the silent assumptions a form must make before a single sentence can be written. A form that presupposes a knowable society has already taken a position on the world, and a candidate who names that position has begun to practise criticism rather than description.

Notice also the economy of the move. Presuppositions can be identified quickly, in the planning minutes, before prose begins. A candidate can interrogate any set text with a short sequence of enquiries. Who is permitted to narrate, and from what distance? What kind of knowledge does the narration claim about other minds? Where does the work's information about money come from, and whose labour remains offstage? What ending does the form make available, and what endings does it render unthinkable? Each enquiry takes seconds to pose and yields material that no plot summary can supply, because the material concerns the machinery of the telling rather than the events being told.

## The first two hundred words of any answer on this topic

Examiners form expectations early, and a script's opening movement teaches the marker how to read everything that follows. An opening that defines the perspective through its object earns trust. It might state that the answer will examine how the work's form organises class experience, which conditions of production shaped its making, and where its silences fall. An opening that begins with biography, with a plot rehearsal, or with a declaration of personal response teaches the marker to expect summary, and markers, being human, find what openings teach them to expect. The candidate cannot control the marker's mood, the hour of marking, or the scripts marked beforehand. The opening paragraph is entirely within the candidate's control, and it should be spent buying the presumption of competence.

Buying that presumption requires restraint as much as display. A first paragraph crammed with six critics and eight concepts signals stock being dumped rather than judgement being exercised. The strong opening names the question's actual demand, selects one leading concept, attaches it to one critic, and previews the route the answer will travel. Selection is itself evidence of command, because only a candidate who understands the field can choose within it. The candidate who deploys everything demonstrates that they cannot rank anything, and ranking is precisely the skill evaluative questions exist to test.

There is a further discipline for openings on evaluative questions, and it concerns the verdict. A verdict announced in the first paragraph and then defended for three pages reads as advocacy, whatever its direction. A verdict that arrives after the weighing, stated with its defeater attached, reads as judgement. The opening should therefore promise an enquiry rather than declare a result. It can signal the tensions the answer will explore, the respects in which the perspective succeeds and strains, without settling them in advance. Markers reward the architecture of a mind changing in an orderly fashion under the pressure of evidence. They are trained to distrust the architecture of a conclusion looking for premises.

Candidates sometimes ask whether an unconventional opening, a striking quotation or a bold provocation, earns credit. The honest reply is that ornament earns nothing unless the operations follow. A plain opening that sets up a form analysis will outscore a dazzling opening that sets up a summary, because the scheme pays for the operations and not for the drapery around them. Spend the opening on definition, selection, and route. The prose can afford elegance later, once the analytical engine is visibly running.

## The form move: the claim this guide is built upon

The form move is the namable claim at the centre of this guide, and it deserves a precise statement. Analysing what a work's form presupposes, rather than what its content depicts, is the single change that most reliably raises a mark on this topic, because the change converts description into method. Content is what happens in a work: the eviction, the inheritance, the strike, the marriage, the ruin. Form is the arrangement through which happenings reach a reader: the choice of narrator, the handling of time, the distribution of interiority, the chapter breaks, the verse shape, the camera position, the decision about where a scene begins and whose face it ends upon. Content can be summarised by anyone who has read the work once. Form must be analysed, and analysis is the activity the examination exists to certify.

The move has a theoretical pedigree inside the tradition, which candidates should be able to gesture toward without drowning an answer in it. Georg Lukács argued that realism's distinction lay not in its subject matter but in its capacity to render social totality, the web of relations within which individual fates become intelligible. A novel about workers could fail that test entirely if its people remained isolated emblems, while a novel about landowners could pass it if their world appeared as a system in motion. The lesson for candidates is liberating: the Marxist quality of a reading does not depend on finding proletarian content. It depends on tracing relations, and relations are carried by form at least as much as by event.

Presupposition is the sharpest tool the move supplies. Every form smuggles in a metaphysics. Omniscient narration presupposes that society is legible to a sufficiently elevated observer. Free indirect discourse presupposes that consciousness is porous, that a character's inner speech can be coloured by the idiom of their class and moment. The well-made plot presupposes that actions ripen into consequences in an orderly moral economy. The fragmentary lyric presupposes that experience arrives broken and that wholeness would be a lie. A candidate who states a presupposition and then tests it against the text has written criticism. A candidate who retells the story in the vocabulary of class has written a book report with footnotes of theory.

Testing matters as much as stating. Forms are rarely pure, and the interesting analytical moments arrive when a work strains against its own presuppositions: when an omniscient narrator confesses ignorance, when a retrospective voice admits that memory has edited the record, when a closed ending leaves a residue the resolution cannot digest. Those strains are where ideology becomes visible, because a form under strain reveals what it was built to hold. Candidates should hunt for the strain rather than the smooth surface. The smooth surface confirms the presupposition; the strain exposes it, and exposure is what a marker can reward with the higher bands.

### What does a form presuppose when a narrator knows every mind?

It presupposes that the social world is knowable from a single elevated vantage, that private motives explain public outcomes, and that no region of experience lies beyond narration. Naming that assumption lets a candidate ask whose knowledge the novel trusts, whose it never attempts, and what the asymmetry reveals about the world the form imagines.

The question generalises across media, which is why the move travels so well between papers. In film, the presuppositions sit in editing and framing: the continuity system presupposes a stable space a viewer can master, while the long take presupposes duration that cannot be hurried into meaning. In drama, the proscenium presupposes a world complete behind its frame, while direct address presupposes that the frame is a convention the audience has agreed to forget. In each case the candidate's task is identical. Identify the assumption the form cannot operate without, then ask what that assumption excludes, then find the moment where the exclusion shows.

Exclusion is the bridge between form and the rest of the Marxist repertoire. A form that presupposes a narratable inner life for some characters will often deny interiority to servants, labourers, colonised figures, or women, granting them speech without granting them minds. That denial is not a content point about representation in the shallow sense. It is a formal arrangement carrying ideological weight, and it can be evidenced precisely, scene by scene, in a way that thematic generalisation cannot. Candidates who learn to count interiority, to notice who receives a past, a hesitation, a private metaphor, possess an instrument that works on any text an examiner can set.

The form move also rescues candidates from a trap built into many set texts: the work whose content seems to argue against the perspective. A novel celebrating individual enterprise, a poem praising a patron, a play resolving conflict through royal mercy, all appear resistant if content governs. Form reopens them. Who narrates the celebration, and what does the narration need the reader to forget? What does the praise poem's structure owe to the economy of patronage that commissioned it? What must the merciful ending suppress about the grievances aired in the middle acts? None of those enquiries requires the candidate to pretend the work secretly agrees with Marxism. Each requires attention to machinery, and machinery is always there to be examined.

A word of proportion is needed, because candidates who discover the move sometimes apply it to the exclusion of everything else. Form analysis untethered from history becomes a parlour game, a formalism wearing Marxist dress. The tradition's own critics insisted that forms have histories: the realist novel rose with a particular organisation of society, the interior monologue answered a changed experience of selfhood, the montage sequence belonged to an industrial century. The strongest answers therefore pair every formal observation with a historical placement, however brief. The pairing need not be elaborate. A single sentence locating a form in the conditions that made it plausible does the work, and the matrix later in this guide builds that sentence into the planning routine.

## Side by side: a content paragraph and a form paragraph

Abstractions persuade slowly, so this guide follows the brief of its own doctrine and shows the move performed twice on identical material. Imagine a passage, described generically, in which a landowner's daughter visits a tenant household during a hard winter, distributes food, and returns home troubled. No words from any actual work are quoted, because the teaching point survives translation to any similar scene, and candidates should practise on scenes described in their own words rather than memorising ornaments from this page.

A content answer on that material writes itself, which is precisely its defect. It observes that the scene depicts inequality, that the daughter represents the benevolent face of the owning class, that the tenants embody the suffering of the poor, and that the author criticises a society permitting such contrasts. Every sentence is true and every sentence is inert. The paragraph could be written by a candidate who had read a plot summary on the journey to the hall. It names no mechanism, examines no arrangement, and risks nothing that evidence could disturb. A marker places it where such paragraphs belong, in the broad middle where accurate summary accumulates without ever becoming criticism.

A form answer begins elsewhere. It asks how the scene is narrated. If the visit is focalised through the daughter, the tenants exist as objects of her perception, their hunger rendered as the occasion for her moral education, and the form thereby reproduces the hierarchy the content appears to lament. If the narration grants the tenant mother an interior passage of equal weight, weighing the shame of accepting charity against the arithmetic of feeding children, the form redistributes dignity in a way the plot never announces. If the scene closes on the daughter's troubled sleep rather than on the household's empty table, the ending settles the question the visit raised by relocating it inside the conscience of the class that caused it. Each observation is checkable against the passage. Each converts the scene from an illustration of inequality into an argument about how inequality is made thinkable.

The contrast teaches more than either paragraph alone. Set side by side, the two treatments show that the form answer is not the content answer plus sophistication. It is a different object of study. The content answer studies the world inside the fiction. The form answer studies the fiction as an artefact made in a world, by choices, under pressures, for readers positioned in particular ways. Marxist criticism, as an examinable method, lives in the second study. Candidates who feel the difference in their fingers, who can take any paragraph of their own drafting and ask which study it belongs to, have acquired the skill this cluster of the series exists to transmit.

There is a practical exercise buried in that contrast, and teachers use it to great effect. Take a paragraph from a practice essay, strike every sentence that could have been written from a plot summary, and see what remains. In weak scripts almost nothing remains, which is diagnostic rather than shameful: the candidate has located the exact proportion of the answer that was never criticism. Rebuilding the paragraph around narration, presupposition, and settlement converts the same reading time into a higher band. The exercise is uncomfortable because it deletes honest work. It is also the fastest known route from summary to method, and discomfort in practice is cheaper than disappointment in results.

### How does the form move change a paragraph about a charitable visit?

It relocates attention from the gift to the telling. Instead of praising or blaming the giver, the candidate examines focalisation, the distribution of interiority, and the scene's closing image, asking whose consciousness organises the encounter and whose experience the arrangement treats as scenery. The scene becomes evidence about narration rather than an occasion for verdicts on characters.

That relocation has an ethical dividend candidates rarely anticipate. Content answers drift toward judging characters, and judgement of characters drifts toward moralising, and moralising is the tone markers associate with the least analytical scripts. Form answers judge arrangements instead, and arrangements can be judged precisely without scolding anybody. The candidate sounds like a critic because the object of criticism is a made thing, open to inspection, rather than a person, fictional or otherwise, whose soul the candidate cannot know. Tone, in examinations, is not decoration. It is evidence of the candidate's conception of the task.

One more refinement completes the doctrine before the guide turns to its second great correction. The form move includes the ending of a work within its scope, and endings repay attention out of proportion to their length. A resolution settles the disturbances the middle raised: marriages redistribute property, inheritances restore a line, deaths remove the figure who could not be accommodated, journeys export the problem beyond the final page. Asking what a resolution settles, and what it must leave unsettled or unmentioned in order to settle it, gives candidates a closing analytical movement of great reliability. Many strong answers end there, with the settlement examined and its cost itemised, because the movement demonstrates form, ideology, and judgement in a single gesture.

## The author-politics correction: what a work shows against what its maker believed

The second correction is smaller to state and larger in consequence than candidates expect. The politics of a text cannot be inferred from the politics of its author. A writer's allegiance, party membership, pamphleteering, or private conviction does not determine what the work shows, because a work is not a speech. It is a construction in which observed reality, inherited form, commercial pressure, and imaginative sympathy all contend, and the contention can carry the finished artefact far from the maker's declared position, in either direction. Examiners treat the inference from biography to text as a conceptual error because the tradition's founding critical documents explicitly refuse it, and a candidate who commits the error announces unfamiliarity with the very perspective the answer claims to apply.

The classic instance involves Honoré de Balzac, the French novelist of the early nineteenth century whose political sympathies lay with legitimist and Catholic conservatism. Friedrich Engels, writing to the novelist Margaret Harkness in 1888, held Balzac up as a greater teacher of social reality than the professedly progressive writers of the day, precisely because Balzac's fidelity to observed society overcame his own allegiances. Engels called this the triumph of realism: the work shows the necessity of the old order's decline and the energy of the rising classes, although the author's heart belonged to the declining world. The instance is examinable gold. It is specific, it is attributable, and it arms a candidate against the commonest objection an examiner can raise, since the objection collapses the moment the tradition's own authority is quoted against it.

Candidates should understand why the triumph occurs, because reciting the label without the mechanism earns only partial credit. Realist method compels attention to the texture of a world: its money, its marriages, its ambitions, its small humiliations. A writer attending honestly to that texture will register forces their ideology would prefer to deny, because the forces are load-bearing in the society being depicted. Remove them and the fiction stops convincing. The novelist's craft loyalty thus works against the novelist's political loyalty, and the craft loyalty, being exercised on every page, usually wins in the artefact whatever wins in the author's journalism. That mechanism, stated in two or three sentences, converts the Balzac instance from a name dropped into an argument deployed.

The correction cuts in both directions, and strong answers say so. A writer of radical conviction can produce a work whose form betrays the conviction: a revolutionary message carried by a narrative that treats ordinary people as a backdrop for a heroic individual's will has reproduced, in its machinery, the hierarchy its speeches denounce. Conversely, a writer of conservative temperament can produce, through patient observation, the most damning available record of a social order's inner decay. Symmetry is the sign that a candidate grasps the principle rather than wielding it as a convenience. The principle is not that radical authors secretly fail or that conservative authors secretly succeed. The principle is that texts are evidence of a different kind from manifestos, and must be read with instruments appropriate to their kind.

### What should replace biography as the key to a work?

State them briefly if they are known, then set them aside as non-determinative, and test the work itself for what it shows. Name the triumph of realism where a writer's observation outruns allegiance, or its reverse where form betrays conviction, and let the textual evidence carry the argument from that point onward.

Handling the matter in prose requires a small repertoire of sentences, and candidates should assemble theirs before the examination rather than improvising under pressure. One serviceable pattern concedes the biography in a subordinate clause and pivots to the artefact in the main clause, so that grammar itself enacts the correction. Another pattern poses the question the biography cannot answer: whatever the author believed, why does the work grant its fullest interiority to figures the author's politics would dismiss? A third pattern uses the correction defensively, anticipating the examiner's objection by raising it first and answering it with the named instance. Each pattern takes a line or two. Each converts a vulnerability into a demonstration.

There is a neighbouring error worth separating from the main one, because candidates conflate them and lose the thread of both. The author's politics are not determinative, yet the author's situation is not irrelevant. Class position, education, access to publishers, dependence on patrons or on sales, censorship endured or courted: these belong to the conditions of production, and they shape what could be written, published, and read. The distinction is between politics as opinion, which the correction sets aside, and position as circumstance, which the method requires the candidate to examine. An answer that ignores biography entirely overshoots the correction. An answer that moves from opinion to circumstance, from what the writer thought to the situation the writer wrote from, has understood the tradition's actual teaching on the subject.

The correction also disciplines the treatment of characters. Candidates fresh to the perspective often grade fictional people: the landlord is condemned, the worker is praised, the heroine is faulted for false consciousness as though she might have chosen otherwise at the level of the plot. The tradition's serious critics did not read that way. A character is a construction, placed within relations the author inherited and arranged, and the analytical questions concern the construction: what the figure is permitted to know, to want, to become, and what the arrangement of permissions reveals about the world that made such arrangements plausible. Moral judgement of characters is the content error wearing ethical dress, and markers recognise the costume.

## Conditions of production and reception: the work as a made and sold thing

Every text an examiner can set was produced under material conditions and received under others, and a Marxist reading earns part of its distinction by attending to both. Production includes the obvious economics: who paid for the writing, through what advances, salaries, patronage, or piece rates; which publishers, theatres, studios, or broadcasters carried the work; what censorship, licensing, or market expectation pruned it. Production also includes the less obvious economics of form itself. Serial publication shaped the cliff edge chapter. The three volume novel answered a lending library's business model. The commercial stage's interval, the broadcast slot, the album's running order: each is a formal fact with a balance sheet behind it, and candidates who can connect one such fact to one feature of a set text write with a concreteness that thematic answers never achieve.

Reception completes the circuit. Works are read by publics positioned differently, and the same text can function as consolation for one audience and as indictment for another. The history of a work's reception, its canonisation, its school adoption, its adaptation into safer shapes, belongs to the material life of the artefact and can be examined without speculation about individual readers. A set text's presence on a syllabus is itself a reception fact worth a sentence: institutions select works that can be taught, and the selection quietly certifies certain kinds of disturbance as respectable. Candidates need not labour the point. A sentence acknowledging that the classroom is one of the places a work does its cultural work shows a marker that the candidate understands reception as a material process rather than a mystery of taste.

### What belongs in a point about the conditions of production?

A named mechanism rather than a gesture at context: the serial instalment that forced a cliff edge, the patron whose household the poem had to please, the studio system that assigned the director a property, the circulating library whose terms favoured a particular length. One mechanism, tied to one feature of the work, outweighs a paragraph of general background about the century.

The discipline here is specificity under rationing. Examination time forbids a history of publishing in every answer, and markers penalise the undigested background paragraph, the rehearsal of an era that touches the text at no point. The rule is one mechanism, one feature, one sentence of connection. A candidate who writes that a novel's monthly parts obliged its author to sustain several plot lines at equal tension, and that the resulting weave lets the narrative hold classes in a single frame, has integrated production into analysis. A candidate who writes that the century saw great changes in industry and society has integrated nothing, however accurate the changes were.

Silence is the companion concept, and it belongs in this section because silence is a production fact of a special kind. Pierre Macherey argued that a work is bound to what it cannot say, that its meaning includes the absences its ideology makes necessary. The candidate's version of the enquiry is humble and practical: what would this work have to confront if it followed its own premises one step further, and where exactly does it step aside? A novel of factory life that never enters the owner's counting house, a war play whose enemy has no language, a domestic drama in which servants appear without surnames or pasts: each silence is locatable, and locating one precisely, with the page or scene identified, is among the most creditworthy moves available on this topic.

Silences must be argued rather than merely alleged, and the standard of argument is worth stating. A convincing silence is one the work approaches and avoids, where the narrative machinery visibly swerves: a question raised and dropped, a character introduced and abandoned, a consequence implied and then replaced by a coincidence. An unconvincing silence is simply something the work is not about, since no artefact can be about everything. Candidates who observe the distinction write about swerves, and their claims survive a marker's scepticism. Candidates who list absences at random invite the scepticism and deserve it.

## Naming critics: the conversion of observation into evaluated points

A general observation about class in a text earns the credit due to observation. The same observation attached to a named critic and a specific concept earns the credit due to evaluation, because the attachment proves the candidate knows where the observation lives in a tradition of argument, what it was coined to explain, and where its limits are debated. Referring to Marxist critics in general earns nothing, for the same reason that referring to scientists in general would earn nothing in a physics answer. The tradition is not a mood. It is a sequence of identifiable thinkers who disagreed with one another in instructive ways, and examinable command means knowing a workable selection of them as individuals.

The selection need not be vast, and depth defeats breadth under time pressure. Six critics known properly, each with a concept, a characteristic application, and a known difficulty, will carry a candidate through any question the topic can generate. Eight or ten known as labels will collapse at the first request for specificity. The sections that follow walk through the standard repertoire in the order a candidate might deploy it, from the founding documents through the great systematiser of realism, the theorist of hegemony, the philosopher of ideology, the analyst of mechanical reproduction, the school of critical theory, the French theorist of silences, and the British and American critics who carried the method into cultural studies and the theory of narrative. Each portrait is painted for examination use: what the figure gives a candidate, and what caution the figure demands.

Begin with the founders, handled with care. Marx himself wrote literary judgements sparingly, in letters and asides, and his collaborator Engels supplied the tradition's most cited critical remarks, on Balzac and on the tendency novel. The candidate's use of the founders is therefore documentary rather than systematic: the 1859 preface's account of social being and consciousness for the base and superstructure vocabulary, the letters for the triumph of realism and for the warning against fiction as pamphlet. Engels insisted that a novel's tendency should arise from situation and action rather than being pasted onto them, a remark candidates can deploy against crude content hunting with the authority of the source itself. Showing the founders resisting the crude version of their own method buys a candidate protection against the commonest caricature in the field.

### Which name belongs in the paragraph being written?

Name the critic whose concept does the work in that paragraph: Lukács for realism and totality, Gramsci for hegemony, Althusser for ideology and interpellation, Macherey for silences, Williams for cultural materialism, Eagleton for the synthesis a candidate can apply quickly. One name per point, each attached to its concept, outperforms a parade of names attached to nothing.

Georg Lukács, the Hungarian philosopher whose studies of the European novel remain the tradition's most ambitious account of realism, gives candidates the concepts of totality, typicality, and reification. Totality names the ambition to render a society as an interconnected whole rather than a backdrop. Typicality names the character who concentrates the forces of an age without ceasing to be an individual. Reification, drawn from the analysis of the commodity form, names the process by which relations between people acquire the character of relations between things, frozen and apparently natural. Lukács suits questions on the nineteenth century novel, on realism against naturalism and modernism, and on any text where a candidate wants to argue that detail either connects to a whole or accumulates as inert inventory. His difficulty is his severity: his judgements demoted much modernist writing, and a candidate who names him should know that the demotion is contested, because examiners enjoy asking whether the candidate has noticed.

Antonio Gramsci, writing his notebooks in a fascist prison, contributes hegemony, the concept candidates reach for most and define least. Hegemony is leadership secured through consent as much as coercion, the process by which a class's worldview becomes the common sense of a society, absorbed through schools, churches, newspapers, and habits of speech. For literary answers its power lies in explaining how a text can serve an order without propaganda: by treating certain arrangements as natural, certain ambitions as universal, certain sorrows as private. Gramsci also supplies the national popular, a standard against which a literature's relation to its people's lived culture can be measured. His difficulty is elasticity. Because hegemony can be stretched to cover any cultural fact, candidates must discipline it with a mechanism, naming the institution or habit through which consent is organised in the case at hand.

Louis Althusser contributes the account of ideology as a material practice, lodged in institutions he called ideological state apparatuses, and the mechanism of interpellation, the hailing by which individuals recognise themselves as subjects in an order's terms. For candidates his most portable gift is symptomatic reading, the practice of attending to a text's problems and evasions as evidence of the questions it cannot pose. Althusser suits answers on education, family, and media within fiction, and on any work whose characters become themselves by answering a call they did not compose. His difficulty is abstraction and a functionalism that can make every artefact sound like a machine for reproduction, leaving no room for the resistance his own tradition values. A sentence acknowledging that pressure earns the name more credit than the name alone.

## The repertoire continued: reproduction, industry, silence, and synthesis

Walter Benjamin, the German critic whose essay on art in the age of mechanical reproduction remains the most teachable single document in the field, gives candidates the aura: the unique presence of a work anchored in tradition and ritual, which reproduction dissolves. His argument suits questions on photography, film, adaptation, and any set text whose life in copies, broadcasts, or school editions has altered what it is. Benjamin's value for examinations lies in his doubleness. He saw emancipatory possibility in reproducibility, the breaking of art's priestly distance, while mourning what reproducibility destroys. A candidate can therefore use him on either side of an evaluative question, which is exactly the flexibility evaluative questions reward. His difficulty is density of expression; candidates should paraphrase him plainly rather than decorate answers with his vocabulary untranslated.

Theodor Adorno and Max Horkheimer, architects of the Frankfurt School's critique, contribute the culture industry: their account of mass entertainment as a system that standardises pleasure, reconciles audiences to their conditions, and sells rebellion back as style. For candidates the concept arms answers on popular genres within a set text's world, on the entertainment a fiction depicts, and on the commercial forms, serials, bestsellers, studio products, through which texts reach publics. Their difficulty is contempt's nearness. Their analysis can slide into disdain for popular pleasure itself, and candidates should register the slide, since later cultural studies, including work inside the Marxist tradition, corrected it by studying audiences as active makers of meaning rather than as dupes. Naming the correction alongside the concept signals a candidate who reads the tradition as an argument rather than a catechism.

Macherey has already appeared in this guide under the heading of silence, and he returns here because his name belongs in the repertoire as firmly as any. His production model of the text, the work as something made from ideological materials it transforms unevenly, gives candidates a way to discuss contradiction without accusing authors of confusion. A text at odds with itself is, on his account, a text revealing the seams of the materials it worked upon. That is an examinable idea of great practical value, because set texts are chosen partly for their seams. Candidates should attach Macherey to a specific swerve or contradiction, never to a general claim that texts are complex, which is the kind of sentence markers have learned to price at zero.

Raymond Williams, the Welsh critic whose work anchors the companion article on [Raymond Williams and cultural materialism](/2020/06/15/raymond-williams-cultural-materialism/), contributes the most humane instruments in the kit: structures of feeling, the dominant, residual, and emergent scheme for sorting a culture's elements by their relation to its ruling order, and the insistence that culture is ordinary, a whole way of life rather than a cabinet of masterpieces. For candidates, Williams suits questions where a text registers change before institutions name it, where an older order's values persist as residue, or where a new sensibility strains toward a form it has not yet found. His difficulty is gentleness of terminology; his phrases sound mild, and candidates must show the analytical edge inside them, the sorting and dating work the scheme performs on evidence.

Terry Eagleton, whose synthesis the companion article on [Terry Eagleton and Marxist criticism](/2020/09/01/terry-eagleton-marxist-criticism/) examines at length, is the repertoire's great explainer and the candidate's most efficient ally. His introductory and synthetic writings organise the tradition into usable categories, base and superstructure, ideology, form, authorship, consumption, and his later work models how a Marxist reading behaves when it is witty, learned, and willing to concede. Candidates cite Eagleton for frameworks and for the demonstration that the method can be practised with stylistic pleasure. His difficulty, if it is one, is that citing the explainer is no substitute for engaging the explained; an answer built wholly on Eagleton reads as a summary of a summary. Use him to frame, then do the framing's work on the text.

Fredric Jameson completes the standard repertoire with the political unconscious, his proposal that narratives be read as socially symbolic acts, attempts in form to resolve contradictions that history leaves open. His slogan of always historicising is less useful in an examination than his three horizons of reading, which move from the individual text through its class discourse to the long conflict of modes of production. Candidates rarely need the whole apparatus. They need the governing intuition: a story's shape is an imaginary settlement of a real tension, and naming the tension the shape settles is a form move of the highest order. His difficulty is scale; his sentences and systems sprawl, and candidates should borrow the intuition while writing in their own plainer periods.

### How do you keep six critics distinct under time pressure?

Attach each name to one verb. Lukács connects detail to totality, Gramsci traces consent, Althusser listens for the hail, Benjamin watches copies dissolve aura, Macherey follows the swerve, Williams dates the feeling. A verb per critic prevents the blur in which every thinker is made to say that literature reflects society, a sentence true of none of them precisely.

The verb device deserves a paragraph of its own because it solves the commonest repertoire failure. Candidates revise critics as bundles of nouns, and nouns blur: ideology, hegemony, reification, and interpellation melt into a single grey assertion about power. Verbs stay distinct because actions differ. Connecting is not tracing; listening is not watching. Whoever can state what each critic does can reconstruct the noun from the verb under pressure, while the reverse reconstruction fails in the hall with miserable regularity. Revision organised around verbs is therefore not a trick of memory but a correct model of what the concepts are: instruments for performing distinguishable operations on texts.

A second failure deserves naming alongside the first. Candidates sometimes deploy a critic against the grain of the critic's own argument, citing Lukács in praise of fragmentation or Gramsci as a theorist of conspiracy. Markers notice, because the repertoire is their home ground. The protection is modesty of claim: use each critic for the operation the verb describes, and where a text resists the operation, say so and treat the resistance as a finding. Resistance honestly reported earns more than obedience falsely claimed, since it demonstrates the candidate is reading the text rather than reciting the toolkit over it.

## Concepts that work in the hall: a rationed toolkit

Concepts obey the economics of the repertoire. A candidate needs a small set understood to the point of application, and the set should be chosen for portability across texts and question types. Ideology comes first, defined with care: not false ideas in other people's heads, but the lived relation between people and their world, the representations through which conditions are experienced as natural, bearable, or just. So defined, ideology can be found in a work's assumptions rather than its speeches, which is where examiners want it found. The candidate's sentence pattern is equally portable: the work treats as natural what history made, and the treating can be shown in a habit of narration, a distribution of sympathy, a silence at the expected place.

Hegemony follows, as the process concept that explains ideology's maintenance without positing a puppet master. Base and superstructure follows with a health warning the founders themselves supply: the 1859 formulation is a starting orientation, and Engels spent letters of his old age protesting that the economic is decisive only in the last instance, with law, politics, and tradition exercising their own force in the meantime. Candidates who state the warning while using the model demonstrate exactly the sophistication the model is accused of lacking. Reification serves answers on work, money, and objects that seem to act while people watch. Alienation serves answers on labour depicted or performed, and on the estrangement of characters from their products, their activity, and one another. Commodity form serves answers where exchange organises relationships, where gifts carry prices and marriages carry settlements.

Interpellation and symptomatic reading, Althusser's pair, serve answers on becoming a subject: the schoolroom scene, the recruitment, the conversion, the moment a character answers to a name an institution has prepared. Cultural materialism, Williams's banner, serves as the framing commitment that cultural works are material practices within a whole way of life, neither reflections nor ornaments. Each concept in the rationed kit should be revisable as a single sentence of definition, a single sentence of application to a set text, and a single sentence of limitation. Candidates who can produce those three sentences for six concepts possess more usable theory than candidates who can lecture for an hour on any one of them.

### Which concepts travel best between texts and questions?

The portable ones with visible evidence: ideology as naturalised assumption, hegemony traced through an institution, reification where things act and people watch, the symptomatic silence where a narrative swerves, and structures of feeling where a text registers change early. Each can be defined in a sentence, evidenced in a scene, and limited in a clause.

Portability has a test candidates can apply during revision. A concept is portable if it generates a question the candidate can put to an unseen passage within a minute. Ideology asks what the passage treats as needing no explanation. Hegemony asks whose account of the world the passage's common sense repeats. Reification asks where an object or sum of money performs an action a person might have performed. The silence asks what the passage approaches and avoids. Structures of feeling ask what mood or tempo the passage shares with a moment of social change it never names. A concept that generates no question is luggage, and luggage should be left outside the hall however impressive it looks in a notebook.

The limitation sentence, third in each concept's triad, is where candidates most often default, and it is where the defeater discipline of this series enters the toolkit. Every concept has a characteristic blindness. Ideology, stretched, explains everything and therefore risks explaining nothing in particular. Hegemony, loosely used, becomes a synonym for influence. Reification can be alleged wherever objects matter, which is everywhere. Symptomatic reading can manufacture the silences it claims to find. A candidate who states the blindness before the marker finds it converts a weakness of the method into a strength of the answer, and the conversion is cheap: one clause, honestly placed, at the end of the paragraph the concept governed.

## From concepts to the page: paragraph architecture

Knowledge becomes marks only inside paragraphs, and paragraph architecture on this topic follows a learnable sequence. Open with the analytical claim, the presupposition, mechanism, or silence the paragraph will establish. Follow with the concept named and its critic attached, briefly defined through the use being made of it. Move to evidence, the scene, habit of narration, or formal feature, described precisely enough that a marker who knows the text recognises the reference and a marker who does not can still follow the reasoning. Then perform the connection, the sentence in which evidence and concept meet and something is concluded about the work's relation to its world. Close with the limit or the counterweight, the clause that states what the reading does not prove or what in the text resists it. Five movements, each a sentence or two, and the paragraph has done everything a scheme can ask.

The sequence sounds mechanical, and candidates worry that architecture will flatten their style. The worry inverts the truth. Architecture is what permits style to survive time pressure, because the candidate who knows the shape of the next paragraph spends attention on sentences rather than on panic. Jazz musicians practise scales so that improvisation has somewhere to stand. Examination prose works identically: the movements become reflex, and the reflex frees the mind for the only task that cannot be mechanised, which is noticing what is distinctive about the particular text in front of it.

### What separates a paragraph of analysis from a paragraph of summary?

The direction of its sentences. Summary sentences point backward into the story, reporting what happened next. Analytical sentences point outward from a feature to a claim, reporting what a choice of narration, structure, or image does. A paragraph whose sentences mostly begin with events belongs to summary whatever vocabulary it borrows; a paragraph organised around effects belongs to criticism.

Candidates can audit their own practice scripts with that test in under a minute per page, which makes it among the most efficient revision instruments this guide offers. Read a page and mark each sentence with its direction. Pages dense with backward pointing sentences are summaries in costume, and the costume fools nobody who marks for a living. The remedy is not to delete the events, since evidence requires them, but to subordinate them: let the event appear inside a clause governed by an analytical main clause, so that grammar performs the hierarchy the scheme rewards. The event becomes the occasion; the effect becomes the assertion.

One more architectural matter concerns quotation, since candidates either drown answers in it or starve them. Short quotation, a phrase or a clause, embedded in the candidate's own syntax, is the strongest evidence form on this topic, because the method's claims often turn on a word's connotation or a sentence's shape. Long quotation wastes the clock and transfers the writing to the author at the moment the candidate should be performing. The rule of this series for quotation in scholarship, brevity and verification, applies with doubled force in the hall: quote little, quote exactly, and make every quoted fragment work inside an argument the candidate owns.

## Applying the method to poetry: compression as an analytical gift

Poetry frightens candidates on this topic out of proportion to its difficulty, because the content hunt that fails in prose fails faster in verse, where there is less plot to recount and the recounting sounds thinner. The form move reverses the disadvantage. Poetry is form at maximum density: line, stanza, metre, rhyme, and image pattern are all choices a candidate can examine, and the shorter compass of a poem means the choices can be surveyed whole within an answer. The candidate who approaches a poem through its arrangements rather than its paraphrase discovers that the Marxist repertoire fits verse with surprising ease, since so many of its concepts concern how experience is organised, and verse is experience organised audibly.

Begin with the speaking voice and its presuppositions. A lyric speaker presupposes a self whose interior weather merits record, and that presupposition has a history: the expressive individual of lyric tradition is a social product, not a universal given. A dramatic monologue presupposes a listener positioned by rank, whose silence the poem exploits. A ballad presupposes a community that sings its knowledge collectively, carrying news and judgement in a form built for memory rather than for the page. Each presupposition can be stated in a sentence and tested against the poem's handling of address, and the test yields the paragraph's evidence without any paraphrase of content whatsoever.

Metre and rhyme repay the same attention. Regular metre can enact order, discipline, the tread of labour or of marching, and its disruptions can mark the points where experience refuses the order offered to it. Rhyme binds words into couples and families, and a candidate can ask which words a poem chooses to bind: whether wealth rhymes with health in a satire's knowing falseness, whether the binding is smooth where the matter is violent, whether a final unrhymed line leaves the settlement unsettled. None of this requires the candidate to claim that form mechanically encodes politics. It requires attention to what the arrangements make easy to feel and hard to feel, which is ideology's home territory.

### Where does a Marxist reading of a poem begin?

Work from the arrangements: the speaking voice and whom it presupposes, the metre and what its regularity or rupture enacts, the images and what labour or wealth they draw upon, the ending and what it settles. Treat the poem as made work, shaped by patronage, print, or performance, and let content enter as evidence inside formal claims.

The image stock of a poem is its economic unconscious made visible. Poets reach for the materials their world supplies: the harvest and the loom in agrarian centuries, the engine and the ledger in industrial ones, the garden enclosed against the common in ages of enclosure. A candidate who inventories a poem's images by the labour they presuppose has built an argument about the poem's world from its smallest units, and the argument has the great virtue of being checkable image by image. Where the stock shifts inside a single poem, where pastoral machinery gives way to the vocabulary of rent or wages, the shift marks a pressure point, and pressure points are where this method has always done its best work.

Production matters for poetry with special clarity because poems have patrons, occasions, and markets written into their occasions. The ode composed for a great house, the elegy commissioned by grief with means, the satire sold to a bookseller who knew its market, the song composed for a hall of working singers: each circumstance shapes address, length, and daring. Candidates need only one such circumstance, accurately tied to the poem set, to ground an answer materially. The caution is symmetrical with the prose case: circumstance conditions the poem without dictating its meaning, and the author-politics correction governs here as firmly as anywhere. A patron's ode can exceed its commission; a market satire can bite the hand that stocked the stall.

The ending of a poem deserves the settlement question in its sharpest form, because poems end with unusual decisiveness. A closing couplet that restores harmony, a final image that exports the trouble to weather or to fate, a refrain that returns unchanged although everything sung between its returns has altered: each is a settlement, and each can be priced. Candidates should ask what the poem's trouble would require for a genuine resolution and compare that requirement with the resolution the poem actually performs. The gap between the two is the poem's ideology rendered measurable, and measurable claims are the currency of high bands.

## Drama, film, and media: the method leaves the library

Drama adds bodies, buildings, and paying audiences to the candidate's materials, and the method expands to fit them without changing its logic. The stage is a workplace and a marketplace before it is a temple of art: companies are hired, theatres are leased, licences are granted or refused, and the repertory bends toward what can be afforded and permitted. A candidate writing on a set play can therefore ask production questions with literal force. What did this play's first stage allow it to show, and what did the licensing of its moment forbid? Which figures does the casting of the period make central, and which exist to carry messages between the scenes of their betters? The questions are Marxist in method and theatrical in substance, a combination markers in drama and theatre studies are trained to prize.

Bertolt Brecht stands at the centre of the dramatic repertoire because he built a theatre out of the method's premises. His estrangement effect, the family of devices by which a production prevents the audience from dissolving into empathy and instead invites judgement of the social arrangements displayed, is the form move institutionalised as stagecraft. Placards, visible lighting, songs that interrupt rather than express, actors who demonstrate their characters rather than disappearing into them: each device presupposes a spectator who is a citizen before a dreamer. Candidates should know the devices as devices, tied to the presupposition they serve, and should know the standing objection as well, that estrangement can become its own mannerism, a style consumed as comfortably as the empathy it replaced. The objection, fairly stated, is itself an application of the method to the method's theatre.

Film translates the enquiries into the grammar of the camera. The candidate asks who the camera accompanies, whose optical point of view organises sequences, which spaces receive establishing grandeur and which are entered only in fragments, how editing distributes knowledge between classes of character, and what the score instructs the audience to feel about labour, wealth, and law. Production questions carry industrial weight in film: the studio system, the star contract, the genre cycle answering a market, the censorship code pruning endings. A Marxist answer on film that never mentions the industry has missed the medium's most material fact, while an answer that mentions only the industry has missed the film. The balance is the same as in prose: a single mechanism, a single feature, a single sentence tying them, repeated at the points of greatest pressure.

Media studies extends the circuit to ownership and flow. The candidate examines who owns the channel, what the schedule or feed optimises, which publics are assembled as audiences and sold to advertisers, and how formats presuppose the lives of their consumers: the breakfast programme's assumption of a household's morning, the serial's assumption of an evening reclaimed from work. The culture industry concept does its heaviest lifting here, corrected by the audience studies that showed consumption to be active, selective, and often ironic. Candidates who can hold both halves, industrial determination and audience agency, in a single paragraph have reached the level the evaluative questions are built to detect.

### What does the camera presuppose in a classical narrative film?

It presupposes a viewer who can be everywhere without being seen, a space that coheres around the protagonist's movement, and a world whose causes are personal. Asking whose movement the coherence serves, and which labour the seamlessness hides, converts film description into the same presupposition analysis the novel receives, in the medium's own grammar.

Transfer is the skill being certified across all these media questions. Examiners set a poem, a play, a film, or a broadcast because the method claims to illuminate culture as such, and a candidate whose technique works only on the nineteenth century novel has learned a local habit rather than a method. The reassurance this guide offers is that the transfer requires no new theory. Presupposition, production, silence, settlement, and the named repertoire travel intact. Only the evidence changes its costume, from chapter and narrator to line and voice, from stage direction to shot. Candidates should practise the transfer deliberately, taking one concept through three media in revision, until the movement feels like translation rather than invention.

There is a discipline of restraint in media answers that mirrors the prose discipline. Candidates tempted by the sociology of an industry can fill pages with ownership history that never touches the set work. Candidates tempted by close reading can fill pages with shot description that never rises to a claim. The paragraph architecture already given governs both temptations: claim, concept with its critic, evidence, connection, limit. A film answer built in those paragraphs will contain industry and image in their proper proportions, because the architecture rations each paragraph to one connection and forbids the freestanding survey.

## Comparison questions: stating what each approach takes as its object

Comparison questions defeat candidates in a predictable way: the answer compares two approaches on a single criterion, usually reach or political virtue, and thereby misrepresents both. A critical approach is not a team. It is an instrument built to answer a family of questions, and instruments are compared by stating what each takes as its object, what evidence each trusts, and what each is structurally unable to see. The candidate who opens a comparison by defining objects has already outperformed the candidate who opens by declaring preferences, because the definition performs the comparison's real work before any verdict is attempted.

Set the Marxist object plainly. The approach takes as its object the work's relation to the material organisation of its society: production, class, ideology, the forms through which a social order becomes thinkable. Its trusted evidence is historical and formal together, the document and the device. Its structural blindness, honestly stated, concerns whatever in a work exceeds its social conditions: the private resonance, the verbal music valued for itself, the metaphysical reach that no sociology exhausts. Stating the blindness without flinching earns the right to praise the vision, since the marker can see the praise is weighed rather than inherited.

Formalism, the comparison the companion article on [Marxist and formalist criticism](/2020/10/01/marxist-vs-formalist-criticism/) treats in depth, takes the work's literariness as its object: the devices by which language is made strange and perception renewed. Its evidence is internal, its blindness the world outside the device. The comparison writes itself once objects are stated. Where the question concerns how a work refreshes perception of the familiar, formalism sees further. Where the question concerns why the familiar was arranged so, and in whose interest the arrangement held, the Marxist instrument answers. Candidates should resist the examination's invitation to crown a winner and instead allocate territories, since allocation is comparison and coronation is merely preference with a ceremony.

Feminist criticism takes as its object the work's organisation of gender: whose desire structures the plot, whose labour the household hides, which voices the form licenses. Its overlap with the Marxist approach is real, since both attend to hidden labour and naturalised hierarchy, and its divergence is equally real, since it holds that patriarchy is not reducible to class and survives changes in ownership. Postcolonial criticism takes empire as its object: the representation of colonised peoples, the forms empire used to narrate itself, the resistance written back. Its divergence from the Marxist approach concerns the primacy it grants to culture and discourse, and the suspicion, voiced within its own ranks, that class categories forged in Europe travel badly. Each divergence, stated cleanly, is worth more than a page of vague agreement that all approaches value context.

### What makes two critical approaches genuinely comparable?

Open by defining the object each approach studies, the evidence each one credits, and the territory each is built to miss, then divide the set passage between them according to those definitions. Comparison by object prevents the single criterion error, in which two instruments built for different questions are graded on one scale and both are misrepresented.

New historicism and cultural materialism require the finest discrimination, because candidates conflate them and examiners know it. Both refuse the separation of text from history. They differ in commitment and in temper. Cultural materialism, in Williams's line and its British continuation, retains an explicit politics: it reads culture to identify the emergent and to intervene in the present arrangement of meanings, and it keeps faith with the possibility of describing a whole social order. New historicism, in its American line, is more sceptical of wholes, more drawn to the anecdote and the circulation of social energy between text and context, and less willing to underwrite a programme. A candidate who can state that difference in two sentences, commitment and temper, has separated what revision guides habitually merge, and the separation is precisely the kind of discrimination comparison questions exist to reward.

Reader response approaches take the reading event as their object, locating meaning in the encounter between text and interpretive community. Against them the Marxist approach insists that readers are positioned by history before they are free in interpretation, that the community's shared strategies are themselves ideological products. Psychoanalytic criticism takes desire and the unconscious as its object, and the Marxist reply has historically been suspicion of the universal family romance, answered in turn by attempts, within both traditions, to think class and desire together. A candidate need not settle these old negotiations. The examinable skill is cartography: place each approach by its object, and the map itself, accurately drawn, constitutes most of the answer's value.

The verdict in a comparison answer should be allocative and conditional. It might conclude that for the question set, concerning a work's treatment of labour, the Marxist instrument yields more, while conceding the passage where the rival instrument sees what Marxism misses, and stating the evidence that would change the allocation. That shape, allocation plus concession plus condition, is the defeater discipline wearing comparative dress. It also happens to be true to the intellectual facts, since approaches genuinely do divide the world between them, and a candidate whose verdict reflects that division sounds like a scholar because, for the length of the answer, they are one.

## Evaluation and the defeater: judging a reading as the series judges itself

Evaluative questions ask whether Marxist criticism is useful, illuminating, limited, or distorting, and they are the questions on which the defeater standard does its finest work. The standard, taught throughout this series and applied to its own verdicts, is simple to state. After reaching a judgement, state what would count against it. A reading that acknowledges its own counter-evidence is stronger than one that does not, because the acknowledgement proves the judgement was reached by weighing rather than by loyalty. Candidates should be taught the standard as a sentence habit: the judgement, then the phrase that introduces its condition, then the specific textual or historical material that, if found, would require the judgement to be revised or withdrawn.

The specificity is the whole art. A defeater that gestures at evidence in general, claiming the judgement would change if the facts were different, is a courtesy rather than a condition, and markers price it accordingly. A working defeater names the findable thing: the scene in which the supposedly silenced figure speaks with full interiority, the letter in which the author engineers the effect the reading called ideological, the production record showing the formal feature was imposed by a censor rather than chosen under market pressure. Each named defeater tells the marker that the candidate knows what their own claim predicts about the world of the text, which is the deepest form of understanding an examination can certify.

Candidates sometimes fear that stating a defeater weakens an answer, as though confidence were the graded commodity. The fear misunderstands the commodity. What is graded is command, and command includes knowing the borders of one's own claim. The advocate who concedes nothing sounds, to a professional reader, like a person who has not looked for the opposing evidence, and the suspicion spreads backward over the whole script. The candidate who concedes precisely, at the point of genuine vulnerability, protects everything else they have written, because the marker extends trust to a writer who has demonstrated the habit of testing. Concession is not the tax on a strong answer. Properly placed, it is the strong answer's signature.

### When does criticising the method earn marks rather than lose them?

Yes, where the question invites evaluation, and the safety has a structure: criticise the method at its concepts, evidence, and blindnesses, with named critics on both sides, and state what would change your judgement. A well-evidenced negative verdict scores as highly as a positive one, since the reasoning is assessed and the conclusion is not.

The tell that separates invitation from trap deserves emphasis, because candidates misread it in both directions. Some questions require the perspective to be applied rather than assessed: they instruct the candidate to give a Marxist reading of a passage, and an answer that spends its length doubting the method has refused the task, however sophisticated the doubt. Other questions make usefulness itself the issue, and an answer that merely applies the method, however skilfully, has equally refused the task. Reading the verb of the question is therefore the first evaluative act. Apply, and the method is the instrument. Assess, discuss, evaluate, and the method is the object. Candidates who spend twenty seconds classifying the verb before planning have insured themselves against the most expensive misreading available on this topic.

Where criticism of the method is invited, it should be the strongest available, since weak objections insult the marker's intelligence and the candidate's. The serious charges are known. The approach can flatten works into documents, reading through the verbal surface to a social content and discarding the surface that made the work art. It can explain a work's origin and mistake the explanation for an account of its value. It can find in every text the ideology it brought to the text, a circularity its own symptomatic method should have caught. It can underrate the reader's freedom and the work's capacity to outlive its conditions, surviving into worlds its makers could not imagine and speaking there with undiminished force. Each charge, stated fairly, deserves its reply, and the replies exist: the form move was developed precisely against flattening, the distinction between origin and value is the tradition's own, the defeater habit answers circularity, and reception history is a Marxist topic before it is an objection to one.

The candidate's verdict, after the weighing, may land on either side or in the allocative middle, and the landing matters less than the visible route. A verdict that Marxist criticism is indispensable for some questions and blind for others, with the territories mapped and the defeater stated, represents the method's actual standing among serious practitioners, which is why it scores: markers reward answers that resemble the truth of a field. A verdict of total triumph or total dismissal resembles the truth of no field that has survived a century of argument, and markers, who live inside the argument, know the difference in their bones.

## The common mistakes, gathered in one place

Mistakes on this topic recur with such fidelity that they can be catalogued, and a catalogue rehearsed before the examination functions as a pre-flight check. The first entry is the content hunt already dissected: class conflict located in a plot and reported as though location were analysis. The second is the biographical inference, the author's politics read off the text or the text read off the author's politics, against the tradition's explicit correction. The third is moral judgement of characters, the grading of fictional people for virtue, which mistakes the object of criticism. The fourth is the unnamed critic, the gesture at Marxist critics in general, which forfeits the evaluation credit that names carry. The fifth is the concept salad, six terms deployed in a paragraph and none of them evidenced, which signals stock without command.

The sixth mistake is the background avalanche, the opening pages of era summary, industrial revolutions and expanding cities, that never touch the set text at a specific point. The seventh is the unexamined base and superstructure diagram, culture treated as a mirror held up to an economy, without the mediations the founders' own later letters insist upon. The eighth is the conflation of cultural materialism with new historicism, examined in the comparison sections, a discrimination examiners probe deliberately. The ninth is the verdict without a defeater, judgement as posture. The tenth is the refusal of the question's verb, assessing where application was demanded or applying where assessment was demanded, the most expensive mistake in the catalogue because it wastes an otherwise competent script entire.

### Which errors recur most faithfully in scripts on this topic?

Writing about content rather than form, inferring the text's politics from the author's, judging characters morally, invoking critics without naming them, deploying concepts without evidence, drowning the opening in background, treating culture as a mirror of the economy, conflating neighbouring approaches, and delivering a verdict with no statement of what would count against it.

Each mistake has a corresponding drill, and drills convert a catalogue from a warning into a training programme. For the content hunt, the strike-through exercise described earlier. For the biographical inference, the Balzac paragraph rehearsed until it can be written in four minutes. For unnamed critics, the verb attachment drill. For concept salad, the triad of definition, application, limitation, enforced per concept. For the avalanche, a rule that background may occupy no sentence that fails to name the text. For the mirror error, the Engels warning quoted in revision until its caution feels like common sense. For the conflation, two sentences of separation memorised as a pair. For the missing defeater, a closing sentence template practised until it feels incomplete to omit it. For the verb refusal, twenty seconds of classification before any planning begins. A candidate who runs the drills owns the catalogue as armour rather than as a list of fears.

There is also a mistake of temperament that underlies several entries and deserves separate mention: the belief that the examination rewards intensity of conviction. It rewards accuracy of operation, and the two are easily confused by candidates whose engagement with the material is genuinely political. The tradition itself supplies the correction. Its best critics were fierce in commitment and fastidious in evidence, and the fastidiousness is the part the examination can see. A candidate may hold any view of the perspective, including contempt, and score at the ceiling, provided the operations are performed with care. A candidate may hold the perspective as a creed and score at the floor, if the creed substitutes for the operations. The hall is not a rally, and treating it as an instrument panel rather than a platform is the temperament the marks reward.

## Structure of a timed answer: the hour as an argument

Time is the examination's hidden syllabus, and answers on this topic fail from its mismanagement as often as from ignorance. The planning minutes are the highest yielding minutes of the hour, and they should be spent on the matrix logic the next section tabulates: identify the question type, select the leading concept, attach its critic, choose the evidence, and pre-write the verdict's defeater in a phrase. Five decisions, each small, and the answer's skeleton stands. Candidates who skip planning to gain writing time lose more than they gain, because unplanned answers spend their length discovering a structure that planning would have supplied in advance, and the discovery is visible to the marker as drift.

The body of the answer should follow the order the method itself suggests. Begin with the definition of the approach through its object, briefly, as a working instrument rather than a history lesson. Move to the form move early, while the marker's expectations are forming, applying the leading concept to the strongest evidence. Admit the production or reception point at the place where it connects, not in a separate pen of background. Handle the author's politics, where relevant, with the correction and its named instance. Reserve a movement for the strongest counter-consideration, the resistance the text offers the reading or the blindness the approach brings to the text. Close with the verdict and its defeater in adjacent sentences, so the script's final impression is of judgement under discipline.

Length discipline follows from structure. A paragraph that has performed its five movements has finished, and the candidate should let it finish rather than restating its claim in new vocabulary, a habit that inflates scripts while starving later paragraphs of time. The closing movement is the one candidates sacrifice when time runs short, and it is the movement carrying the defeater, which is to say the movement that distinguishes the band. Protect it by rationing the middle. An answer with one fewer illustration and a complete close will outscore the illustrated answer that trails into summary, because the close is where evaluation lives and evaluation is what the question was for.

### What sequence should a timed essay on this topic follow?

Open with a definition of the approach through what it studies. Choose a single leading concept and fasten it to a single critic. Work the form move on evidence you can point to, insert a point about making or reception at its natural joint, handle biography through the correction, give the opposing case its paragraph, and end on a judgement that carries the condition of its own reversal.

That sequence, memorised as a route rather than as prose, is the single most portable asset a candidate can carry into the hall on this topic. It fits the application question, the comparison question with a doubled middle, and the evaluation question with an expanded counter movement. It fits the passage question, where the evidence is supplied, and the whole text question, where the evidence must be chosen. Routes relieve the candidate of architectural invention under pressure, and the relief shows in the prose as calm. Markers cannot see calm directly. They see its traces: proportion, sequence, and a close that arrives on time, and they reward the traces as command of the subject, which, by then, is what the traces are.

## The literature answer matrix: the findable artifact of this guide

The literature answer matrix gathers the guide's doctrine into a table a candidate can rehearse, copy onto revision cards, and consult in the planning minutes. Each row is a recurring question type. The columns name the concept to lead with, the critic to attach, the evaluation that lifts the band, and the trap the question type sets. The matrix assumes the cluster's content articles have supplied the substance; its own function is sequencing, telling the candidate which substance to fetch and in what order to spend it. The full procedure for reading a whole work stands in the companion article on [a Marxist reading of a novel](/2020/08/01/marxist-reading-of-a-novel/), and the theory behind the rows is set out in [Marxist literary criticism explained](/2020/07/01/marxist-literary-criticism-explained/); the matrix is the bridge between those resources and the clock.

| Question type | Concept to lead with | Critic to name | Evaluation that lifts the band | Trap |
| Apply a Marxist reading to a set text | The form move: what the work's form presupposes | Lukács, on realism and totality | State what in the text resists the reading, and what evidence would overturn the judgement | Retelling the plot with class vocabulary attached |
| Compare Marxist with another critical perspective | The object each approach takes as its own | Williams, for cultural materialism against its neighbours | Allocate the question's territory between the approaches on a named passage | Grading two instruments on a single criterion |
| Evaluate the usefulness of Marxist criticism | Ideology as naturalised assumption | Eagleton, for the synthesis and its conceded limits | Deliver a verdict with its defeater stated in the adjacent sentence | Performing allegiance or hostility instead of weighing |
| Analyse a passage from a materialist standpoint | The symptomatic silence and the narrative swerve | Macherey, on what the work cannot say | Show the swerve precisely, and admit what the reading cannot prove from the passage alone | Listing absences the passage never approaches |
| Discuss form and ideology | Hegemony traced through an institution or habit | Gramsci, on consent organised as common sense | Name the mechanism of consent, and the point where the work's form strains against it | Treating ideology as opinions expressed by characters |

Read column by column, the matrix teaches the guide in miniature. The concept column enforces the form move and its companion instruments, forbidding the content hunt by design, since no row permits plot as a leading idea. The critic column enforces the naming rule: every row attaches its concept to a person, because an unattributed concept drifts toward the general gesture that earns nothing. The evaluation column enforces the defeater discipline in row specific forms, resistance admitted, territory allocated, condition stated, proof bounded. The trap column is the examiner's view of each row, and a candidate who has memorised the traps has effectively read the marking scheme's mind, since the traps are the failures the scheme's lower bands describe.

### How does a rehearsed table change the planning minutes?

By settling the sequence before prose begins. The row for the question type supplies the leading concept, the critic, the evaluative lift, and the danger, so the planning minutes become selection rather than invention, and the writing minutes are spent on evidence and connection inside a shape that already evaluates.

The matrix also governs revision, and this may be its larger use. Completing a row from memory, in prose, without notes, is what owning that question type means. A candidate who stalls on the evaluation column has located a precise weakness, and the weakness has an address in this guide: the defeater sections for the stall on conditions, the comparison sections for the stall on allocation, the silence sections for the stall on proof. Revision organised by rows is diagnostic in a way that rereading chapters never achieves. Chapters tell a candidate what exists. Rows tell a candidate what they cannot yet do, which is the only knowledge that changes a result.

Candidates should build the matrix physically. A card per row, carried and rehearsed in odd minutes, converts the table from a page consulted into a reflex available. The physicality matters because the examination forbids the page; whatever cannot be summoned from memory under pressure does not exist for assessment purposes. The companion tools named at the end of this guide serve exactly that conversion, turning the matrix into cards and the cards into a sequence, and the sequence into the calm that markers read as command.

## Unseen passages and set texts: two theatres, one method

The passage question and the whole text question look like different examinations and are, in method, the same examination wearing different evidence rules. The passage arrives with its evidence supplied, which tempts candidates into paraphrase, since the material sits in front of them asking to be retold. The discipline is to treat the passage as a specimen of form: its narration, its presuppositions, its distribution of interiority, its swerves and settlements, all present in miniature. A passage answer that performs the form move on three features will defeat a passage answer that paraphrases thirty, because the marker is grading the operation and the operation is visible only in the treatment of features.

The whole text question reverses the temptation. Evidence must be chosen from a large stock under time pressure, and candidates drown in choice, writing a little about everything and establishing nothing. The discipline is rationing: select the scenes or passages where the leading concept bites hardest, where the form's presupposition is most exposed or most strained, and spend the answer there. Depth of treatment on chosen ground signals command. Coverage of the whole map signals anxiety, and markers, who were candidates once, recognise the signal and price it with sympathy and severity in equal measure.

### What distinction keeps a passage answer from becoming paraphrase?

Separate the event from the arrangement that delivers it. The plot is what happens; the form is who tells it, from where, with what knowledge, in what order, granting which minds interiority and which endings possibility. Writing that keeps the separation visible, subordinating events inside analytical claims, is criticism; writing that loses it becomes summary.

Between the two theatres sits the comparative text question, where two works must be held in one argument. The matrix logic extends naturally: one leading concept, applied to both works in turn, with the comparison drawn at the level of presupposition rather than of plot resemblance. Two novels may share a subject and presuppose opposite worlds; two works of different centuries may share a presupposition and illuminate each other across the gap. Candidates who compare presuppositions write comparative answers of real distinction, because the comparison itself becomes a finding rather than a filing system. The sequence for revision across the whole cluster, including the order in which to revisit the companion articles, is laid out in the [Marxism revision guide](/2022/01/15/marxism-revision-guide/), which this guide treats as its timetable rather than repeating it.

A last word on evidence handling belongs here, since both theatres punish the same vice: the floating reference. A reference floats when it names an event without locating the feature that carries the argument: a character suffers, a marriage occurs, a letter arrives. Anchored references name the feature: the suffering is narrated from the employer's parlour, the marriage is settled in a scene of account books, the letter is read aloud by the one character who can read. Anchoring takes seconds longer per reference and transforms the credit each reference earns, because the anchor is where the analysis lives. Candidates should revise by anchoring: take practice paragraphs and force every reference to carry its feature, until the habit survives the clock.

## Revision method: converting stock into reflex

Revision for this topic fails in a characteristic way: candidates reread. Rereading feels like work and functions like exposure, and exposure produces familiarity, which the brain mistakes for command until the hall exposes the mistake. Command is a production skill. It is built by producing: writing the Balzac paragraph against a timer, completing matrix rows from memory, anchoring references in old practice scripts, drafting defeaters for verdicts reached in earlier essays. Every effective technique for this topic shares that productive character, and every ineffective one shares the passive character of rereading, however many hours it consumes and however beautiful the resulting notes appear.

The efficient unit of revision is the triad already described: for each concept, a sentence of definition, a sentence of application to a set text, a sentence of limitation. Six concepts yield eighteen sentences, and a candidate who can write those eighteen sentences from memory, accurately, possesses a kit that will serve any question. The triads should be written, not recited, because the examination is a writing event and the reflex must be trained in the medium of its use. Writing also exposes the gaps that recitation glosses: the concept whose application sentence will not come, the critic whose limitation is unknown. Each exposed gap is a gift, since it arrives while repair is still possible.

### What does productive revision look like on this topic?

Revise by production in small units: concept triads written from memory, matrix rows completed without notes, the Balzac paragraph and the defeater sentence practised against a timer, practice scripts audited for summary sentences and floating references. Replace rereading with retrieval, since the examination grades what can be produced under pressure rather than what feels familiar at a desk.

Spacing and mixing complete the method. Concepts revised in rotation, one from each family per session, interfere productively and resemble the hall's demand to switch instruments between questions. Timed practice should begin earlier than comfort suggests, because the clock changes the skill being trained, and a candidate whose first timed attempt happens in the examination has trained for a different event. Past questions, gathered through the companion route described later in this guide, supply the practice material; the candidate's task is to answer them in matrix order, mark the answers against the trap column, and revise the specific column where the marks leaked. That loop, question, answer, trap audit, targeted repair, is the whole science of preparation for this topic, and it is learnable in the weeks a syllabus allows.

Candidates should also revise the corrections as set pieces. The triumph of realism paragraph, the two sentences separating cultural materialism from new historicism, the verb attachments for six critics, the closing template of verdict plus defeater: these are the fixed assets of the topic, the passages whose substance recurs across questions and whose phrasing can be polished in advance without impropriety, since every candidate's versions will differ in application. Polishing set pieces is not memorising essays. The essay must still be built for its question. Set pieces are the load bearing paragraphs inside the building, and buildings stand better when their beams are seasoned.

One caution closes the method, concerning the night before and the morning itself. New theory learned at that point cannot be converted into reflex and will displace calmer assets in the memory's front rank. The final hours belong to the matrix, the triads, and the set pieces, rehearsed lightly and then set down. Rest is a revision technique, since the operations this topic grades, selection, connection, judgement under condition, are exactly the operations fatigue degrades first. A candidate who arrives rested with a rationed kit will outperform a candidate who arrives exhausted with a warehouse, and the examination, indifferent to both stories, will simply record the difference as marks.

## Base and superstructure: handling the model without the mirror

No element of the repertoire is more frequently taught and more frequently mishandled than the account of base and superstructure drawn from Marx's 1859 preface to his critique of political economy. In its crude classroom form the model proposes that a society's economic base, its relations and forces of production, generates a superstructure of law, politics, and culture that reflects and legitimises it. Candidates reproduce the diagram, apply the word reflection to a novel, and lose marks to an examiner who knows that the tradition's own custodians spent decades complicating exactly that picture. The complication is not revisionist decoration. It is the examinable substance, and candidates who command it can use the model with a sophistication that disarms the standard objection before the objection is raised.

Engels himself supplied the first complication, in letters written in the eighteen nineties to younger correspondents who had turned the model into a mechanism. He insisted that the economic moment is decisive only in the last instance, that legal, political, and philosophical forms exercise their own influence on historical struggles, and that he and Marx were partly to blame for the misunderstanding, having emphasised the economic side against adversaries who denied it altogether. Citing that self-correction gives a candidate a remarkable asset: the founders' collaborator, in his own words, refusing the mirror. Deployed in an essay, the refusal converts the model from a slogan into a research programme, a direction of enquiry that asks how economic relations condition cultural forms without pretending to deduce a sonnet from a steam engine.

Mediation is the name the tradition gives to everything the mirror leaves out. Between a mode of production and a poem stand publishers and patrons, genres with their own histories, languages with their own resistances, audiences with their own expectations, and the accumulated conventions of forms that outlive the conditions that birthed them. Williams gave the point its most useful classroom shape by insisting that the base is not a fixed object but a process, a set of activities and relations in continuous motion, and that cultural practice belongs inside that process rather than floating above it as decoration. On his account a novel is not a reflection of a society's life but a part of that life, one of the places where the society understands and misunderstands itself. Candidates who absorb that reformulation write differently: they ask what work a text does within its world rather than what picture it copies from its world, and the shift in the question is visible to markers within a paragraph.

### Why does the mirror metaphor lose marks on this topic?

Because it asserts the very mechanism the question wants examined. To say a work reflects its economy is to skip the publishers, genres, languages, and audiences through which any conditioning must travel, and to ignore the founders' own insistence that the economic decides only in the last instance. Naming one mediation converts the slogan into an argument a marker can credit.

The practical consequence for answers is a rule of travel: never move from economy to text in a single step. Take at least one intermediate station, and name it. The station might be an institution, the circulating library, the patent theatre, the studio, the examination syllabus itself. It might be a genre, the sensation novel, the problem play, the confessional lyric, each carrying conventions that predate any individual author. It might be a material fact of language, the price of paper, the spread of literacy, the standardisation of spelling that made a national reading public imaginable. Each station slows the argument and strengthens it, because conditioning that travels through named stations can be evidenced, while conditioning asserted in a leap can only be believed.

Relative autonomy is the companion phrase candidates should own, borrowed from the Althusserian vocabulary and usable without its full machinery. Forms have their own histories and their own inertia. The sonnet persisted across feudal, bourgeois, and industrial orders, changing its work while keeping its shape. The realist novel outlived the moment of its rise and was put to uses its inventors never imagined, in colonies and in socialist states and in the hands of writers hostile to everything its first masters valued. A model that cannot accommodate that persistence will fail on real texts, and examiners set real texts partly to watch models fail or flex. The candidate who grants forms their relative autonomy, while still asking what each era makes a form do, holds the model at its defensible strength.

There is an evaluative dividend here that candidates rarely collect. When a question asks whether the base and superstructure model remains useful, the strongest answers neither defend the diagram nor discard it, but restate it as a claim about conditioning and mediation, support the restatement with the Engels letters and the Williams reformulation, and then state the defeater: evidence that a cultural form's career can be fully explained without reference to material conditions at any station would retire the model, and the candidate invites the examiner to notice that no such career has yet been produced. That is the defeater discipline operating on the tradition's most vulnerable asset, and it converts the asset's vulnerability into the answer's distinction.

## The realism debates: Lukács, Brecht, and the quarrel over modernism

The tradition's internal quarrels are examinable treasure, because a candidate who knows that Marxist critics disagreed violently with one another about form can never be accused of treating the approach as a monolith. The richest of these quarrels unfolded in the nineteen thirties around realism and modernism, in exile journals and under the shadow of fascism, and its positions map the choices candidates still face when a set text refuses the realist template. Lukács held the line for the great realist inheritance: Balzac and Tolstoy and Thomas Mann rendered totality, the interconnected social whole, while modernist experiment, in his judgement, froze experience into static fragments and mistook anxiety for knowledge. Ernst Bloch answered that fragmentation might be the truthful registration of a fractured world, that montage and rupture could know things the seamless narrative could not.

Bertolt Brecht, whose notes on the quarrel were published only later, supplied the position candidates find most usable. He argued that realism is not a style inherited from a canon but a stance toward reality, a laying bare of the causal network of society, and that the means of laying bare must change as reality changes. A technique that revealed the network in one decade could conceal it in another, once audiences had learned to consume the technique as comfort. The implication for examinations is direct: a candidate may defend a formally difficult, fragmented, or estranging set text in Marxist terms, with Brecht's authority, against the objection that the method demands Balzac. Equally, a candidate may criticise a smooth realist text for performing a totality it has not earned. The quarrel gives the candidate licensed positions on both sides, which is precisely what evaluative range means.

Adorno's presence in the quarrel adds the necessary thorn. He suspected both the administered realism of official culture and the easy affirmations of committed art, holding that the truth of a work often lodges in its refusal of reconciliation, in forms that decline to pretend the world coheres. A candidate need not adopt his severity to profit from it. The profit lies in having a Marxist warrant for treating difficulty, dissonance, and unresolved endings as knowledge rather than failure, balanced against Lukács's warrant for treating achieved coherence as knowledge of another kind. Between the two warrants a candidate can locate almost any set text and argue the location, which is a far stronger performance than praising or blaming the text for resembling one master.

### Which side should a candidate take in the realism quarrel?

Neither side as a loyalty, and both sides as instruments. State what each position takes realism to be, a rendering of totality for Lukács, a laying bare of causal networks by whatever means the moment requires for Brecht, then test the set text against both definitions and report where it satisfies one and strains the other. The quarrel is the answer's range, not its allegiance.

The quarrel also teaches a meta-lesson about the tradition that candidates can state outright for credit. Marxist criticism at its best has been an argument conducted in public about what literature is and does, with positions staked, answered, and revised across decades. The examination version of the approach, a checklist of class content, is a fossil of that argument with the life removed. Candidates who restore the argument, who let Lukács and Brecht contend inside their answer over the evidence of the set text, demonstrate the approach as its serious practitioners practised it. Markers reward the restoration because it is indistinguishable from understanding the subject at the level the subject deserves.

There is a caution to file beside the treasure. The quarrel's details, its journals and dates and exiles, are background, and background obeys the ration: no sentence that fails to touch the set text. Two sentences of placement suffice to license the positions; the rest of the answer belongs to the text under discussion. Candidates who narrate the nineteen thirties for a page have mistaken the history of criticism for its practice, a substitution markers are trained to detect and discount. Hold the quarrel as a pair of instruments, not as a story, and it will serve any question that mentions realism, modernism, or the politics of form.

## Ideology in depth: from false consciousness to lived relation

Ideology is the concept candidates use most and define worst, and the repair begins by retiring the definition most of them arrive with. In the weak version, ideology means false ideas, usually other people's, implanted by the powerful to keep the powerless quiet. That version fails analytically, because it cannot explain why the powerful believe their own legitimations, why the powerless often see through legitimations and comply anyway, or why a novel's deepest ideological work happens in passages where nothing is being asserted at all. The stronger tradition defines ideology as representation of a lived relation: the imaginary way individuals experience their real conditions, carried not primarily in doctrines but in practices, rituals, images, and stories. A candidate who works with the stronger definition can find ideology in a work's furniture rather than only in its speeches, and furniture is where examiners prefer to find it.

Eagleton's synthetic work helpfully distinguishes levels at which the term operates, and candidates can borrow the distinctions without the apparatus. There is the general ideology of a social formation, the climate of assumptions within which anything can be thought. There is the ideology of an author, the worldview a writer brings, formed by class, education, and milieu, which the author-politics correction teaches candidates to treat as material rather than verdict. And there is the aesthetic ideology, the assumptions built into forms and genres themselves, the sonnet's decorum, the novel's faith in interiority, the documentary's claim on fact. An answer that specifies which level it means at each use sounds precise because it is precise, and precision about levels prevents the commonest ideology error, which is sliding between levels mid-paragraph so that a claim proven about a genre is spent as though proven about a society.

False consciousness survives, in careful hands, as a limited tool rather than a worldview. There are moments when a text or a character plainly misrecognises a situation in ways the work itself exposes: the heir who believes the estate runs on benevolence while the narrative shows the rent roll, the clerk who blames his ruin on fate while the plot itemises the bank. The candidate's discipline is to let the work do the exposing. Where the text itself supplies the correcting evidence, naming the misrecognition is analysis. Where the candidate must import the correction from doctrine, the claim weakens into assertion, and markers feel the difference. The rule generalises: this method is strongest when its verdicts are assembled from materials the work provides, and weakest when materials are imposed, and a candidate who states that rule as their own practice has understood something many practitioners never articulate.

### Where does a text keep its ideology if not in its statements?

In its defaults: what it treats as needing no explanation, whose inner life it funds with attention, which labour it renders invisible by rendering its products visible, what its endings treat as settlement. Defaults are evidenced by distribution and silence rather than by quotation of opinions, which is why they survive a marker's scepticism better than extracted doctrines do.

Naturalisation is the mechanism candidates should name whenever they claim ideology is at work, because the name obliges them to show the work being done. To naturalise is to present a historical arrangement as though it were a fact of nature or of human nature in general: the market as weather, the hierarchy as anatomy, the domestic division of labour as the shape of love itself. Texts naturalise through metaphor, through the unmarked case, through the distribution of the word normal and its silent partners. A candidate who tracks one naturalising metaphor through a set text, showing how it recurs at moments of strain, has built an ideology argument of real sophistication from humble materials, and the argument's modesty is its strength, since every step can be pointed to on the page.

The counter-concept completes the toolkit. Ideologies are never seamless, because the lived relation they organise contains real contradictions, and texts register the seams even when they labour to hide them. Macherey's swerve, Williams's emergent, the strain against a form's own presupposition: the repertoire keeps returning to the seam because the seam is where a society's account of itself fails to cover its experience. Candidates should therefore distrust any reading, including their own first drafts, in which the ideology of a text appears total and untroubled. Such readings describe the critic's system rather than the work. The defeater habit applies here with special force: state where the text's dominant account cracks, and let the crack qualify the verdict, and the answer rises a band on the strength of its own complication.

## The historical novel and the shape of the past

Genre history gives candidates a second route into form, complementary to presupposition, and the historical novel is its classic exhibit. Lukács's study of the genre argued that the great historical fiction of the early nineteenth century, above all Walter Scott's, arose when history became a mass experience, through revolution and the wars that carried conscription and upheaval into ordinary lives. Scott's formal choices followed from that experience: the middling hero, neither great nor base, whose fortunes let opposing social forces meet within one narrative; the great historical figures kept at the margins, entering briefly; the past rendered as the prehistory of the present, a process of change with causes rather than a pageant of costumes. A candidate armed with that account can write about any historical fiction with immediate analytical grip, asking whether its past is process or pageant, whether its hero mediates forces or merely tours them.

The account also supplies a discriminating test for the costume drama in prose or on screen, where the past serves as décor for romances and intrigues that could be set anywhere. Lukács called the later degeneration of the genre a descent into antiquarianism, detail accumulated for its own sake, the past as furniture warehouse. Candidates can apply the distinction without the polemic: does the work's period detail do causal work, constraining what characters can do and become, or does it merely certify authenticity while the plot proceeds as though centuries were interchangeable? The question is a form question in historical dress, and it generates paragraphs on texts that otherwise tempt candidates into plot summary, since costume invites description and causation demands analysis.

Historical fiction on examination papers often concerns national pasts, revolutions, civil wars, and the founding of orders, and the Marxist repertoire brings one further instrument to such texts: the analysis of anachronism as ideology. Every historical work smuggles its own moment's questions into the past it depicts, and the smuggling is not a scandal but a datum. The candidate asks which of the work's burning issues belong to the depicted century and which belong to the writing one, and what the importation accomplishes: consolation, warning, legitimation, the rehearsal of a present quarrel at a safe distance. That enquiry, pursued with dates kept straight and claims kept modest, is among the most impressive performances available on this topic, because it applies the method to the work's temporality itself, the deepest presupposition a historical form carries.

### What makes a historical novel Marxist in its method rather than its subject?

Its treatment of the past as a process with causes, in which ordinary lives register the movement of forces, rather than as a gallery of great figures or a warehouse of period detail. A novel about a revolution can be pageant; a novel about a provincial marriage can be process, if its world is shown becoming what it is through forces the narrative makes intelligible.

Drama's historical variant deserves a sentence in this connection, because Shakespeare's histories sit on more syllabuses than any novelist, and candidates reach for them with this method often. The repertoire's questions travel well: whose labour sustains the courts the plays display, how the common scenes comment on the great ones, what the form of the chronicle play presupposes about history as the deeds of kings, and where the plays strain that presupposition by giving the tavern and the battlefield's underside their own eloquence. A candidate who asks those questions of a history play is practising the method at its most characteristic, finding the social whole pressing at the edges of a form built to celebrate its summit.

## Eagleton's categories and the architecture of a complete reading

Candidates who want a scaffold for whole text answers could do worse than the categories Eagleton proposed for organising a Marxist reading, because the categories enforce completeness without dictating conclusions. His scheme directs attention in turn to the general mode of production within which a work appears, the literary mode of production through which it is made and distributed, the general ideology of its moment, the ideology its author brings, the aesthetic ideology embedded in its forms, and the text itself as the product of those determinations meeting. Recited as a list, the scheme is dead weight. Used as a checklist of questions, one per category, it guarantees that an answer touches production, form, and belief in their distinctness, which is the completeness markers describe when they praise a script's range.

The literary mode of production is the category candidates most neglect and markers most reward, because it forces the material questions into the open. How was this kind of work produced in its period: by patronage, by subscription, by the circulating library, by the studio, by the broadcaster's commissioning round? Who owned the means of its making, the press, the theatre, the distribution network, and what did ownership select for and against? A candidate who can answer those questions for a set text in three sentences has grounded every later claim, since claims about a work's daring or caution become testable against the conditions under which daring was priced. The category also dignifies the study of minor apparatus, the preface, the dedication, the serial wrapper, as evidence rather than trivia.

Aesthetic ideology, the category of form's own assumptions, is where the scaffold joins the form move. Genres are contracts with readers about what the world is like and what may happen in it. The marriage plot contracts that desire and property will be reconciled in a settlement. The detective story contracts that the social order, disturbed by a crime, contains the means of its own repair and that knowledge, properly assembled, restores it. The confession contracts that a self, narrated honestly, becomes intelligible and perhaps absolved. Each contract can be honoured, strained, or broken by a particular work, and the candidate's paragraph lives in the verb chosen. A work that honours its contract while exposing its price is doing something different from a work that breaks the contract outright, and the difference is a finding, not a nuance.

### How do Eagleton's categories help without becoming a checklist recited?

Use each category as a question rather than a heading, and answer only the questions the set text makes live. Production asks who paid and who distributed; authorial ideology asks what the writer brought; aesthetic ideology asks what the genre contracts; the text asks where those pressures meet and strain. A scaffold that generates questions disappears into the answer, while a scaffold recited as headings sits on top of it.

The scheme's final virtue is diagnostic for revision. A candidate reviewing a practice essay can sort its paragraphs by category and discover the habitual omission: the script rich in general ideology and empty of production, or diligent on the author's beliefs and silent on the genre's contract. The omission, once seen, is a revision target with an address. Teachers use the sort as a marking shorthand for the same reason. It converts the vague judgement that an answer feels thin into the precise observation that two categories never appear, and precision about thinness is what allows a candidate to thicken the next attempt rather than merely lengthen it.

There is a standing warning to attach to any scaffold, and candidates should state it when they use one conspicuously. Determinations meet in a text; they do not queue to be applied. A reading that processes a work category by category, like an inspection line, will miss the way a single scene can be at once a product of the market, a strain in the genre, and a crack in the general ideology. The strongest answers therefore use the categories in preparation and dissolve them in performance, letting the argument's own logic order the paragraphs while the checklist, invisible, guarantees that nothing material was forgotten. Scaffolding is for building. The finished structure should not creak with it.

## Gender, class, and the intersection objection

Sooner or later every thoughtful candidate meets the objection that the Marxist repertoire subordinates other oppressions to class, treating gender, race, and colonial subjection as side effects of an economic main event. The objection is serious, it has been pressed from inside and outside the tradition for a century, and examination questions invite it whenever a set text's energies run along those lines. Candidates need a treatment that neither dismisses the objection nor surrenders the method to it, and the materials for that treatment exist within the tradition's own history of self-correction.

Begin with the acknowledgment that carries weight: classical formulations often did treat the woman question and the colonial question as destined for resolution by the resolution of the class question, and the record of movements and states acting on that assumption gives the objection its evidence. A candidate who concedes that record plainly has earned the standing to describe what followed. What followed was a long labour of revision, much of it by Marxist women and by critics writing from the colonised world, insisting that domestic labour is labour, that the reproduction of the workforce is production's hidden half, that households have economies with their own exploitations, and that empire was not capitalism's decoration but one of its engines. Social reproduction theory, the name under which much of that labour travels, asks who replenishes the worker, who cooks, cleans, nurses, and raises, and at whose cost, and it asks those questions of literary texts with devastating effect: the great house rests on the servants' hall, the hero's freedom on the heroine's confinement, the plot's leisure on labour the form declines to narrate.

For the candidate the practical yield is a set of enquiries that strengthen rather than replace the standard kit. Follow the unpaid work in the text. Ask whose time is treated as valuable and whose as infinitely available. Ask which characters the form grants a future and which it grants only a function. Ask how marriage operates as an economic institution inside the fiction, what settlements, portions, and expectations move beneath the language of feeling, and whether the work knows it is showing you that machinery or shows it unknowing. Those enquiries are continuous with the form move and the silence, since the invisibility of reproductive labour is the classic symptomatic absence, present in its products and absent in its performance.

### How should a candidate answer the charge that the method reduces everything to class?

Concede the historical record of the reduction where it occurred, then show the tradition's own corrections at work on the text: social reproduction's attention to unpaid labour, the analysis of marriage as an economic institution, the insistence that empire was an engine rather than an ornament. A method shown correcting itself on evidence is harder to dismiss than a method defended as flawless.

The comparison discipline governs the verdict. A feminist reading and a Marxist reading of the same passage are instruments with different objects that happen to share some evidence, and the candidate's task is allocation: the account of hidden labour may be common ground, the explanation of its persistence contested ground, the valuation of the ending disputed ground. Stating the grounds distinctly, rather than merging the approaches into a general radicalism, is what the question rewards. The same allocation serves the postcolonial comparison, where the Marxist emphasis on material extraction and the postcolonial emphasis on representation and discourse divide the territory of a colonial text between them, and where the most interesting finding is often the point at which the two accounts require each other to complete an explanation.

Candidates should also know the objection's mirror image, since evaluative balance is the topic's signature virtue. Approaches centred on gender or discourse have their own structural blindnesses, toward the wage relation, toward the ownership of the means of cultural production, toward the sheer cost of things. A set text's publishing history does not care which approach the candidate prefers; it sits in the record waiting for the instrument that can read it. The candidate who can say, of a given question, which instrument the evidence calls for, and can state what the chosen instrument will miss, has reached the allocative maturity that the highest bands describe. That maturity, not loyalty, is the destination of all the training this guide prescribes.

## Can the method judge quality? The question of aesthetic value

A persistent suspicion shadows the approach in examination rooms: that it can explain a work's origins and functions but has nothing to say about whether the work is any good, and that a criticism unable to judge value has amputated criticism's oldest duty. Candidates meet the suspicion in evaluative questions and in the sceptical asides of other approaches, and they need better than embarrassment in reply. The tradition's honest position is layered, and the layers can be stated in an answer with profit.

The first layer concedes the distinction. To explain how a work came to be, what it does in its culture, and whose interests its forms serve is not yet to say whether it achieves what its kind attempts. Genesis is not judgement, and critics inside the tradition have insisted on the point against their own vulgarisers. The second layer observes that the tradition nevertheless carries criteria, though they are criteria of a distinctive kind. Lukács valued the work that renders its world as a knowable totality over the work that inventories surfaces. Brecht valued the work that makes its audience think about arrangements over the work that bathes them in feeling. Benjamin valued the work whose form registers its moment's technical truth. Each criterion is contestable, and each is genuinely aesthetic as well as political, since each concerns what a work does with its means.

The third layer is the one candidates find most liberating, because it returns the question to the text. A work's own procedures can be judged by their adequacy to the work's own undertaking: whether its form sustains the knowledge it claims, whether its ending earns the settlement it performs, whether its silences are the price of its vision or the evasion of it. Those judgements require no external yardstick, and the Marxist repertoire sharpens them by supplying the vocabulary of presupposition and settlement in which they are conducted. A candidate who writes that a novel's final reconciliation is unearned because the narrative has shown the reader the very forces that make reconciliation implausible has judged value with the method's tools, and the judgement is literary before it is anything else.

### Does explaining a work's origins settle whether it is good?

No, and strong answers say so before building their judgement on other ground: the adequacy of form to undertaking, the earning of settlements, the honesty with which a work faces what its own evidence shows. Origin explains possibility; it does not distribute merit, and a candidate who separates the two questions can use the method for both without confusing either.

There is a further consideration that examiners, who are usually practising critics, quietly reward: the method's account of why value judgements themselves have histories. Canons are made by institutions, schools, publishers, prize committees, and the syllabus on which the candidate's set text sits is a canon in miniature. To notice that fact is not to declare all judgement arbitrary, a conclusion the tradition's serious figures rejected, but to add a question to the candidate's repertoire: not only is this work good, but good by whose criteria, preserved by which institutions, at the expense of which forgotten contemporaries whose work served other publics. That question, asked once and briefly in an answer, signals a candidate who understands that taste has a material history, which is, after all, one of the approach's founding insights applied to criticism itself.

Candidates should be warned against the cheap version of that move, in which a work's canonical status is treated as proof of its ideological service and its merits dismissed unexamined. The cheap version commits the biographical error at institutional scale, inferring the text from its circumstances of preservation. The correction is the same as before: circumstances condition, they do not determine, and a work may be preserved by institutions for reasons its own energies subvert. The candidate who holds both truths, that canons are made and that made canons can contain works that bite the hands that shelved them, writes about value with the doubleness the subject requires.

## Working an unseen passage: a demonstration in slow motion

Unseen work concentrates every anxiety this guide addresses, so it earns a demonstration at walking pace. Suppose the passage set describes, in a narrator's voice, a great house on the morning of a sale: the rooms inventoried, the servants already dismissed and watching from the yard, a daughter of the house moving among the lots with a catalogue, remembering christmases. No source is quoted and none is needed; passages of this family appear wherever literatures process the decline of a landed order, and the method's treatment generalises from the features rather than from any single text.

The first minute belongs to presupposition. The narration, if it moves inside the daughter's memory, presupposes that the house's meaning is the family's meaning, that the sale is legible as private grief. The servants in the yard are positioned as witnesses to a story that is not theirs, although the sale decides their livelihoods more brutally than it decides the daughter's sentiments. That asymmetry, interiority funded here and withheld there, is the passage's ideological signature, and naming it in the answer's second paragraph announces a candidate who reads form. The inventory itself is a second signature: a catalogue is the commodity form entering the prose, the house translated into lots and prices while the narration lingers on recollection, and the friction between the catalogue's grammar and the memory's grammar is the passage arguing with itself in a way a candidate can quote, phrase by phrase, without importing anything.

The second movement belongs to silence and swerve. Ask what the passage nears and then steps around. It approaches the question of where the family's wealth came from, since a sale invites an accounting, and it avoids the question if the narration lets the house's origins rest in a haze of ancestors and portraits. It approaches the servants' futures and avoids them if the yard empties between paragraphs and the narrative follows the daughter to a carriage. Each avoidance, precisely located, is evidence; and the candidate should also record the resistance, the detail that complicates the reading, perhaps a servant granted a single line of dialogue whose dryness punctures the elegy. That line is the passage's counter-evidence, and the answer that builds it into the verdict, rather than stepping around it, performs the defeater discipline on unseen ground where it is hardest and most rewarded.

### What is the first thing to look for in an unseen passage on this topic?

The distribution of interiority: whose mind the narration enters, whose it reports from outside, and what that distribution presupposes about whose experience counts as the story. Follow that with the passage's treatment of money and labour, its swerves around origins and futures, and the settlement its final sentences perform. Features first, themes afterward, always.

The third movement is the verdict, and on unseen material the verdict should be modest in scope and exact in condition. The candidate judges what this passage, as a specimen, shows the method capable of: the reading has illuminated the elegy's politics by attending to its arrangements, and it has not settled the value of the whole work from which the passage came, nor the author's position, nor the period's literature in general. Stating those bounds is not timidity. It is calibration, and markers reward calibration on unseen work above almost everything, because the unseen answer is where candidates most often claim empires on the evidence of a page. The defeater closes the performance: were the servants' interiority granted in the passage's continuation, the reading's central claim would require revision, and the candidate says so in a sentence. The answer ends as an act of judgement under discipline, which is the whole doctrine of this guide compressed into an ending.

A practical note on annotation belongs here, because unseen performance is built in the minutes before writing. Candidates should mark the passage for features, not for themes: a symbol in the margin for each grant or denial of interiority, a line under each appearance of money's vocabulary, a bracket at each swerve. Three passes with three questions, presupposition, labour, settlement, produce an annotated page from which paragraphs assemble themselves. The candidate who annotates for themes produces a page of underlined sentiments and a summary. The candidate who annotates for features produces a map of the machinery, and machinery, once mapped, can be described under any time pressure the hall imposes.

## Hegemony on the page: common sense inside the fiction

Hegemony earns its keep in literary answers when it is brought down from the level of societies to the level of sentences, and this section performs that descent, since candidates routinely invoke the concept at an altitude where nothing can be evidenced. A society's common sense lives inside fiction as the set of propositions a narrative never argues because it assumes them: that a younger son must make his way, that a woman's reputation is a form of property, that the colonies are a place fortunes come from, that a servant's ambition is comic and a master's is noble. The candidate's task is to catch the narrative in the act of assuming, and the catching is done at the level of the unmarked sentence, the aside, the comparison offered as obvious, the future a narrator projects for a character without pausing to justify the projection.

The mechanism matters as much as the catch. Gramsci's point was that consent is organised, not spontaneous, and fiction shows the organisers at work: the schoolroom where the lesson naturalises an order, the sermon that translates hierarchy into providence, the newspaper read aloud in the kitchen, the popular song that makes a settlement singable. A passage containing any of these is a gift to the prepared candidate, because the institution is on the page, doing the work the concept describes, and the paragraph can be built by quotation and comment rather than by assertion. Where no institution appears, the candidate can still trace the mechanism through repetition: the phrase that recurs across a novel in different mouths, the proverb every class pronounces, the story everyone in the fiction tells about how the world goes. Repetition across difference is hegemony's fingerprint, since it shows an account of the world travelling beyond the class that authored it.

Resistance, Gramsci's other bequest, must be given its due or the concept becomes a theory of total control that its author never held. Common sense, in his account, is contradictory, a folklore of fragments in which submission and scepticism coexist in the same head. Texts register that incoherence faithfully when they are honest: the servant who repeats the proverb and mocks it in the same scene, the heroine who accepts the marriage market's rules and games them with open eyes, the crowd that cheers the procession and loots the bakery by evening. Candidates should hunt the incoherence, because an answer that finds consent and its cracks in the same passage has reproduced the concept's actual shape, while an answer that finds consent alone has reproduced its caricature.

### How can a candidate evidence hegemony without inventing it?

Look for the unargued assumption, the institution shown organising belief, or the phrase that travels across classes unchanged, and quote the smallest unit that carries the proof. Then look for the crack in the same material, the mockery or gaming that coexists with consent, since hegemony in its serious form is always contested ground rather than a sealed system.

There is a refinement available to strong candidates in the treatment of popular forms inside set texts: the ballad sung, the melodrama attended, the serial read, the music hall turn applauded. The tradition has argued about such material for a century, between the culture industry's suspicion that it reconciles audiences to their lot and the cultural materialist insistence that popular forms carry emergent energies, the hopes and angers of their publics in coded and pleasurable shapes. A candidate can stage that argument over a single scene of entertainment within a fiction and earn evaluative credit from the staging alone, because the scene becomes the meeting point of two named positions, each with its evidence in the same lines. Few performances signal command as clearly as a small piece of text made to host a large, fairly conducted dispute.

The section closes with the standing caution, applied to this concept with special reason. Hegemony is the repertoire's most elastic term, and elasticity is a temptation to allege it wherever influence flows. The discipline is the mechanism rule from the production sections: no hegemony claim without its organising institution, travelling phrase, or unargued assumption produced as evidence. A claim so evidenced can still be wrong, and its defeater can still be stated, but it cannot be empty, and emptiness, not error, is what the lower bands are built to house.

## Estrangement and the epic theatre: Brecht as a complete lesson

Brecht deserves a section of his own in an examination guide because he is the rare figure in whom the whole method becomes visible at once: a theory of society, a theory of form, a practice of production, and a quarrel with his own tradition, all documented in plays, poems, and essays that candidates can cite with precision. His theatre was built as an argument against the Aristotelian inheritance as he understood it, against the machinery by which an audience is made to feel with a hero until feeling replaces judgement. The epic theatre proposed the opposite contract: a spectator relaxed, alert, and smoking, as his famous image has it, watching events as a person watches a street accident, interested, forming opinions, aware that things could have gone otherwise and therefore could go otherwise again.

The devices follow from the contract, and candidates should know them as a system rather than a list of quirks. Songs interrupt the action to comment on it, in a different register, so that feeling is quoted rather than indulged. Placards and projections announce outcomes in advance, destroying suspense in order to redirect attention from what happens to how and why it happens. Actors demonstrate their roles, showing the character's choices as choices, with the road not taken left visible. Settings are indicated rather than illusioned, the apparatus of the stage left honest. Each device presupposes the spectator the contract imagines, and each can be analysed by the form move's standard question: what does this arrangement assume about its audience, and what does it refuse to assume? The refusal is the signature. Where conventional staging assumes a spectator who must be made to forget the theatre, Brecht assumes one who must be helped to remember it.

The production side is equally instructive, because Brecht's theatre was also an institution, with a company, a building, a state patron whose support came with expectations, and a repertory negotiated under real constraints. Candidates writing on the plays can therefore practise the full circuit of this guide's method on a single career: form analysed through presupposition, production examined through the ensemble and its circumstances, ideology traced through the plays' treatment of war, property, and goodness, and the author's politics corrected by the works themselves, which are invariably more sceptical, funnier, and less obedient than the doctrine their author professed in prose. The famous difficulties of the plays, the good person whom goodness ruins, the mother whose business sense devours her children, are the triumph of realism operating in a revolutionary rather than a legitimist, which is a refinement of the correction that strong candidates can state for real distinction.

### What does the estrangement effect presuppose about its audience?

It presupposes spectators capable of judgement who are prevented from exercising it only by habits of empathy the staging can interrupt. The devices therefore treat the audience as citizens temporarily seated rather than as dreamers temporarily entranced, and the politics of the theatre sits in that presupposition before any line of dialogue is considered.

The standing objection must be given its paragraph, since this guide's doctrine requires objections at full strength. Estrangement can harden into mannerism. Devices invented to startle an audience into thought become, with familiarity, a style that audiences consume as connoisseurs, enjoying the interruption as they once enjoyed the illusion, and the theatre of judgement becomes another flavour of theatre. Brecht knew the risk and revised productions restlessly against it, which is itself the lesson: the method's forms have histories, their effects decay, and fidelity to the method means renewing the means rather than embalming them. Making that point shows an understanding of the tradition that goes beyond any single play, namely that its formal inventions are arguments whose force is perishable, and that the argument must be re-won in each generation's materials.

For candidates in drama and theatre studies, the practical yield is a portable paragraph pattern. Take any device in a set production or text, state the contract it implies with its audience, name the presupposition about spectatorship, connect the presupposition to the play's social argument, and close with the decay or resistance the device meets in performance. The pattern works on a placard, a direct address in a much older play, a chorus, a soliloquy, since all of these are arrangements of the audience's relation to the action. Soliloquy, indeed, is estrangement's ancestor in the repertoire: a character steps out of the fiction's flow and recruits the audience as confidant and judge, and candidates who notice the kinship can write about early drama with the method's tools without anachronism, by attending to the contract rather than to the terminology.

## Reading the question: verbs, traps, and the allocation of an hour

Examination questions are compressed instructions, and the compression hides decisions candidates must unpack before writing. The verb carries the largest decision. Apply instructs the candidate to use the approach as an instrument on a text, and the answer's centre of gravity sits in the text's features. Assess and evaluate promote the approach itself to object, and the centre of gravity shifts to the weighing of strengths and blindnesses. Discuss licenses an exploration that must still arrive somewhere, a verdict deferred rather than avoided. Compare demands the cartography of objects described earlier. How far and to what extent are invitations to calibration, and they punish both the total endorsement and the total dismissal, since extent questions are built for answers that measure. A candidate who underlines the verb and writes its demand in the margin, in plain words, has performed the cheapest high yield act in the entire examination.

The noun phrases carry the second decision. A question about Marxist criticism and the novel directs attention to a genre's contract. A question about a named author directs attention to the correction, to what the works show against or beyond the writer's positions. A question mentioning usefulness directs attention to the allocative verdict, which purposes the approach serves and which it starves. A question mentioning a single concept, ideology or hegemony or realism, demands the concept's depth rather than the repertoire's breadth, its definition refined, its mechanism traced, its limitation conceded. Candidates who answer the question's nouns rather than the topic's general shape write answers that feel, to a marker, addressed, and addressed answers are the ones that climb.

Traps are set in the phrasing as deliberately as in the matrix's final column. A question that quotes a critic's dismissal invites the candidate to agree or disagree with the dismissal, and the trap is to do either without first reconstructing the argument the dismissal compresses. A question that asks whether a work is a Marxist text invites the category error of treating method as membership, and the strong answer dissolves the premise politely, examining what the method finds in the work whatever the work's allegiances. A question that names two approaches and asks which is better invites the single criterion error in its purest form. Naming the trap in the planning margin, in three or four words, is usually enough to avoid it in the prose, because traps work on the unexamined and lose their mechanism once examined.

### What should a candidate do in the first five minutes with the paper?

Read every question on the topic, underline each verb, and write beside each question the matrix row it belongs to and the trap it sets. Choose on the basis of evidence owned rather than topic liked, since a question whose evidence the candidate commands will outscore a beloved question answered from stock, and mark the chosen question's leading concept and critic before any prose is drafted.

Allocation of the hour follows from the plan, and it should be written down, because intentions about time dissolve under adrenaline while written allocations can be checked against the clock. A defensible pattern gives the opening tenth to planning, the great middle to the movements of analysis in their matrix order, a protected final portion to the counter-consideration and the closing verdict with its defeater, and a small residue to rereading for the mechanical errors, the unnamed critic, the floating reference, that haste plants in scripts. The proportions matter less than the protection of the close. Candidates cut the close because it sits last; the scheme rewards it because it carries the evaluation; the written allocation is the device by which the candidate's calm self defends the close against the candidate's hurried self.

Choice of question deserves one more paragraph, because candidates choose badly in a consistent direction. They choose the question that resembles their revision, which is sound, or the question that resembles their opinions, which is not. Opinions are cheap in the hall; every candidate has them. Evidence is the scarce asset, the scenes known precisely, the critic's argument owned in its steps, the production fact tied to its feature. A question should be chosen by inventorying evidence against each option for two minutes, and the inventory, not the heart, should decide. The method of this guide is unusually well suited to that discipline, since its kit is rationed and its matrix enumerates in advance what each question type will demand, making the inventory a comparison of stocks against stated requirements rather than a guess.

## The sentence level: habits that read as command

At the level of the sentence, markers form impressions they would struggle to justify in a report and act on anyway, because sentence habits correlate with habits of thought. Certain habits recur in high band scripts on this topic and can be adopted deliberately. The first is the subordinated event, already prescribed: analytical main clauses governing evidential subordinate clauses, so that the grammar itself performs the priority of criticism over summary. The second is the named mechanism, the refusal of vague causation. Sentences in which things influence, reflect, or highlight other things say almost nothing; sentences in which a serial deadline forces a cliff edge, or a rhyme binds wealth to health, or a narrator's silence protects a settlement, say something checkable. Verbs are where causation hides, and candidates should revise their practice scripts at the verb, replacing the fog verbs with mechanisms wherever the sentence permits.

The third habit is the calibrated quantifier. Weak scripts deal in absolutes: always, never, entirely, completely. Strong scripts deal in the vocabulary of extent: largely, in this scene, at the level of narration rather than of incident, with one significant exception. Calibration is not timidity; it is accuracy about scope, and it prepares the ground for the defeater, which is calibration's final form. The fourth habit is the defined term at first use, briefly, inside the sentence that needs it, rather than in a prefatory glossary. A concept defined in action, hegemony, the organisation of consent through the schoolroom and the song, carries its meaning into the evidence that follows it, while a concept defined in a preamble has usually evaporated by the time the evidence arrives.

### Which sentence habits most reliably signal an analytical script?

Subordinating events inside analytical claims, naming mechanisms instead of fog verbs of influence, calibrating claims with honest quantifiers, and defining each concept inside the sentence that first uses it. None of these requires knowledge the candidate lacks; all of them convert existing knowledge into the visible shape of thought that marking schemes are built to reward.

The fifth habit concerns the handling of other voices. Strong scripts ventriloquise positions before judging them: the objection is stated in a form its holder would accept, the reply in a form its maker would recognise, and only then does the candidate's own sentence arrive. That courtesy is also a proof, since a position cannot be fairly summarised by a candidate who has not understood it, and markers know the proof when they see it. Weak scripts caricature the side they will reject, and the caricature advertises the verdict's cheapness more loudly than any assertion could. On this topic, where the candidate's own sympathies are often engaged, the discipline of fair summary is the single most legible sign that reasoning, rather than allegiance, is driving the script.

A final habit is the economy of the first person. Candidates either hide behind a false impersonality, writing that it can be seen and one might argue, or sprawl into confession, reporting their feelings about texts and theorists. The examined voice sits between: a candidate may own a judgement plainly, in the first person or out of it, provided the judgement arrives with its evidence and its condition attached. Ownership without evidence is confession; evidence without ownership is evasion. The scripts that climb are the ones in which a mind can be felt taking responsibility for claims it has shown its working for, and that feeling, manufactured sentence by sentence, is as close as prose comes to a signature.

## Structures of feeling: dating what a text registers early

Williams's most delicate instrument deserves its own treatment, because candidates either ignore it or use it as a perfume, a phrase sprinkled over paragraphs to suggest sensitivity. A structure of feeling, in its disciplined sense, names the lived experience of a moment before that experience has hardened into institutions, doctrines, or recognised ideologies: the meanings and values as they are actively lived and felt, at the edge of semantic availability, in the generation that is undergoing a change it cannot yet describe in the inherited vocabulary. For literary study the concept is precious because literature is often where the not yet describable first becomes describable, in rhythms, tones, and arrangements of sympathy that precede any programme.

The candidate's use of the concept follows a recognisable pattern. Identify a pressure in the text that its own official language cannot accommodate: a new kind of loneliness in a new kind of city, a new tempo of work entering the imagery, a new relation between generations that the fiction registers as awkwardness before anyone names it as a condition. Show the pressure at the level of form, in sentence rhythms that hurry or stall, in imagery drawn from a technology the plot never discusses, in endings that fail because the inherited settlements no longer fit the feelings the middle has generated. Then date the pressure, cautiously, against the social change it plausibly registers, and state the defeater: a demonstration that the same formal pressure appears identically in works written before the change would dissolve the dating, and the candidate must be willing to let it dissolve.

Williams's companion scheme, the sorting of a culture's elements into dominant, residual, and emergent, gives the concept its analytical teeth and should always travel with it. The dominant is the effective central system of meanings, the order's own account of itself at full strength. The residual is what was once dominant and persists, genuinely active in the present though often reclassified as tradition, rural wisdom, or the archaic charm of an older form. The emergent is the new meanings and practices continually being created, some of which the dominant will absorb in altered form and some of which it cannot absorb without changing itself. A candidate who sorts a set text's elements under those three heads has performed a complete cultural analysis in miniature, and the sorting generates paragraphs almost mechanically: one for the residual form carrying an older order's values, one for the dominant settlement the plot endorses, one for the emergent feeling the work can register but cannot resolve.

### How does a structure of feeling differ from a theme?

A theme can be stated as a topic and illustrated from content. A structure of feeling must be evidenced in the grain of the writing, its rhythms, imagery, tones, and distributions of sympathy, and dated against a change in lived experience that the text registers before it can name. The difference is the difference between saying a novel concerns loneliness and showing a new loneliness entering its sentences.

The concept's characteristic risk is vagueness, and the guide's standing disciplines answer it. Evidence or silence: no structure of feeling without formal features produced. Mechanism or nothing: the change being registered must be describable in material terms, a migration, a mechanisation, a new medium, a reorganisation of household or working day. Defeater stated: the dating must declare what would falsify it. Under those three disciplines the concept becomes one of the most rigorous in the kit rather than the least, and candidates who submit it to the disciplines can use it on the most delicate material, the tremor in a lyric, the unease in a comedy, without dissolving into impressionism.

There is a reason this instrument sits so well in an examination guide, beyond its analytical power. It rewards the kind of reading examinations can actually certify: close, patient, formal, and historically placed, with claims proportioned to evidence. It also democratises the repertoire. A candidate whose set text is slight, comic, or domestic, apparently poor in the grand materials of class conflict, can find in its textures a structure of feeling as rich as any epic's, because the concept dignifies the small registration, the changed inflection of ordinary life. Many candidates believe their texts are too minor for the method. Williams is the standing refutation of that belief, and examiners who set minor texts often set them precisely to see whether candidates know it.

## The culture industry and the popular: pleasure under examination

Popular material appears inside set texts constantly, as the novel a character reads, the song a crowd sings, the serial a household awaits, the film a heroine dreams through, and it also constitutes whole set texts wherever syllabuses admit genre fiction, drama of the commercial stage, or broadcast work. The Marxist repertoire's treatment of the popular is a debate, not a doctrine, and candidates should present it as one, with the Frankfurt suspicion on one side and the cultural materialist defence on the other, each position attached to its names and its evidence.

The suspicion, in Adorno and Horkheimer's classic statement, holds that industrially produced pleasure is standardised beneath its variety, that it trains audiences in the rhythms of their own subordination, reconciles them to the working week by selling its inversion as leisure, and absorbs even rebellion as a marketable style. The candidate's evidence for the suspicion, inside a fiction, is the depicted machinery of distraction: the romance read to endure the factory, the lottery hope that replaces the wage claim, the celebrity whose rise story teaches that the order rewards its deserving. The suspicion's strength is its account of scale and intention, of pleasures designed in offices. Its blindness, which the tradition's own later figures named, is its treatment of audiences as a single passive body and of pleasure itself as a symptom, a blindness that sits oddly in a method founded on respect for ordinary people's experience.

The defence, developed through Williams's insistence that culture is ordinary and through the audience studies that followed, holds that consumers are makers: they select, misread productively, sing the chorus and change the words, build communities of taste that industries court but do not compose. Inside a fiction, the evidence is the scene of use rather than the object used: the serial argued over, the song repurposed for a strike or a wedding, the melodrama whose villain the pit renames for a local landlord. A candidate who analyses use rather than denouncing objects has taken the defence's methodological point, which is that meaning is completed in consumption and consumption is a material practice with its own conditions, its own venues, wages of attention, and economies of sharing.

### How should a candidate write about popular pleasure in a set text?

Examine the occasion of consumption instead of pronouncing on the object consumed. Ask who consumes, where, at what cost in money and time, in whose company, and to what ends the pleasure is put, consolation, rehearsal of feeling, coded protest, communal glue. Stage the Frankfurt suspicion against the cultural materialist defence on that evidence and allocate rather than crown.

The evaluative verdict in such answers should follow the guide's allocative pattern. Industrial determination is real: formats, lengths, and endings are shaped by markets in ways a candidate can document from a work's own publishing or production history. Audience agency is also real: the record of appropriation, parody, and repurposing is as documentable as the record of manufacture. The interesting finding is usually the ratio in the case at hand, and ratios differ by period, medium, and public, which is why the question survives as an examination staple. A candidate who reports a ratio, with evidence on both sides and a defeater attached, has done the thing itself rather than described the debate about the thing.

One further instrument belongs in this section, since popular texts raise it acutely: the analysis of genre as a contract with a market. A genre's conventions are promises made to a purchasing public, and the promises encode that public's permitted satisfactions: the detective's restored order, the romance's secured union, the sensation novel's licensed shock returned safely to respectability. A candidate can read a set text's handling of its genre contract as evidence about its public's world, what that world needed to believe and what it paid to feel. The reading is Marxist in the strictest sense, since it treats form as a social relation between producers and consumers, mediated by money, and it works on texts whose content offers the content hunter almost nothing at all.

## The novel as the bourgeois form: a genre history candidates can use

Genre histories give answers a long lever, and no genre history in the repertoire is longer than the novel's. The standard account, which candidates should be able to sketch and qualify, ties the form's rise to the rise of the class whose experience it was built to carry: a reading public of merchants, professionals, and their households, possessed of leisure, privacy, and money for books; a world in which individual fortune is made and lost rather than inherited as fate; and a philosophy of experience that trusts the particular and the everyday as the places where truth resides. Ian Watt's classic study gave that account its scholarly form, linking formal realism, the detailed registration of time, place, and interior process, to the economic individualism of the rising order. Candidates need not swallow the account whole. They need it as a frame within which a set novel's choices become legible as choices.

The frame generates the questions automatically. How does the novel distribute its attention between the counting house and the drawing room, and what does the distribution presuppose about where life happens? How does it handle time, as the medium of accumulation and improvement, of careers and educations, or as a cycle of seasons and generations that mocks accumulation? What does it do with money's specifics: are sums named, rents calculated, portions and debts itemised, or does wealth float as an atmosphere of carriages and houses? The great realists itemise, because their form's claim on reality runs through the ledger, and a candidate who counts the money in a novel, who notices where precision stops and vagueness begins, possesses evidence of the most checkable kind. Precision usually stops at the threshold of the labour that produces the wealth, and the stopping point is the form's ideology drawn as a map.

The hero's shape belongs to the same enquiry. Lukács described the novel as the epic of a world in which meaning is no longer given, its hero a seeker whose biography must manufacture the significance that older forms received from gods and communities. That seeker is recognisably the individual of the new order, thrown on his own resources, educated by experience, judged by outcomes. Candidates can test any set novel against the description: whether its protagonist's journey integrates self and world or exposes their irreconcilability, whether the ending grants the integration honestly or purchases it with coincidence and inheritance. The test works on novels hostile to the order as readily as on its celebrations, since the seeker's failure, properly analysed, is as much evidence about the form's presuppositions as the seeker's success.

### Why does the novel itemise money in some places and blur it in others?

Because its realism is selective in a patterned way: the sums that govern marriages, careers, and inheritances are exact, since those are the transactions its readers must judge, while the labour and extraction that supply the sums are blurred, since precision there would raise questions the form's settlement needs to leave sleeping. The map of precision and blur is the ideology of the form made visible.

Against the standard account candidates should hold the qualifications, since examiners reward the held qualification. Novels arose in more places and for more publics than the single rising class story allows; women readers and writers were central to the form's making in ways the early accounts understated; the form's materials include older romances, letters, criminal biographies, and spiritual autobiographies whose publics were not bourgeois in any simple sense. Stating the qualification does not weaken the frame. It converts the frame from a myth of origin into a tool whose limits the candidate commands, and the conversion is another instance of this guide's recurring lesson: every instrument in the kit earns more credit with its limitation attached than without it.

The novel's later careers extend the lesson usefully for comparative questions. The form travelled, into colonial societies where it was turned to purposes its inventors never imagined, into socialist states where it was conscripted and where it also, in the hands of its best practitioners, registered the gap between the promised order and the lived one. A form's portability across orders is evidence for its relative autonomy, and its transformations under travel are evidence for the conditioning that autonomy never abolishes. Candidates who can narrate one such journey briefly, a form arriving in a new world and changing its presuppositions under new pressures, have a paragraph that serves history questions, comparison questions, and evaluation questions alike, which is the definition of an efficient asset in a rationed kit.

## Authorship: the writer as worker

The author is the repertoire's most familiar figure and its most mythologised, wrapped in the ideology of the solitary genius whose work springs from inwardness alone. The method's counter-account treats authorship as labour performed under conditions: a writer works with materials not of their choosing, a language, a set of genres, a market or a patron, a body of expectations about what a book is, and the work bears the marks of that labour process as surely as any manufactured thing. Candidates can use the counter-account without a trace of disrespect, since it restores to writing its dignity as work, and it generates enquiries that biography, in the gossip sense, never reaches.

The enquiries are concrete. What was the writer's relation to the means of literary production: did they own their copyright or sell it, write for a salary, a patron's table, a magazine's column, a studio's contract? How did the payment structure shape the work's length, its pacing, its cliff edges and deadlines, its very unit of composition? What division of labour surrounded the solitary name: editors, printers, illustrators, translators, actors, censors, each leaving traces in the artefact? What did the writer's class position make easy to see and hard to see, not as a verdict on their sincerity but as a fact about vantage, of the same kind a surveyor records about an instrument's placement? Each enquiry converts the author from a source of opinions into a worker in a situation, which is where the author-politics correction always intended the candidate to arrive.

### How does treating the author as a worker change an essay's evidence?

It replaces speculation about intention with documents of process: contracts and payment forms that explain length and pacing, editorial and censorship traces that explain shape, the division of labour that explains finish. Intention may still be discussed, but it is discussed as one worker's plan inside a production the plan did not wholly govern, which is the proportion the evidence supports.

The romantic ideology of authorship is itself an examinable object, since it has a history and a function. The figure of the genius, answerable only to inspiration, arose with the literary marketplace it appears to scorn, serving as the brand under which a new kind of property, the author's copyright in original expression, could be claimed and sold. Candidates who can state that paradox, that the myth of the unmarketable soul was the market's own invention, possess a paragraph of genuine intellectual glamour that is also, underneath the glamour, a precise application of the method to the method's own subject. Such paragraphs should be rationed to one per answer, since glamour, like quotation, devalues with circulation.

A caution about evidence standards closes the section. Production facts must be facts, drawn from the documented record of the set text or its kind, not invented colour. Where the record is unknown to the candidate, the honest sentence is the structural one: works of this kind, in this period, were typically produced under such conditions, and the features visible in this work are consistent with them. That sentence, modest and accurate, earns more than a fabricated anecdote earns, and it protects the candidate against the examiner who knows the record and is waiting, with professional patience, for the invention that forfeits trust in everything around it.

## Censorship, the market, and the shapes of the unsayable

Silence has been treated in this guide as a property of ideology inside texts, and it has a second source candidates should distinguish from the first: the enforced silence of censorship and the quieter enforcement of the market. The distinction matters because the evidence differs. Ideological silence is argued from swerves within the work. Enforced silence can often be documented from outside it, in licences refused, passages cut, editions altered, prosecutions threatened or staged, and the candidate who can bring one documented enforcement to bear on a set text writes with an authority that purely internal argument cannot match. The two silences also interact, since writers internalise the censor and the market until enforcement becomes anticipation, and anticipation becomes style: the indirection, allegory, and historical displacement by which whole literatures learned to say things at one remove.

Historical displacement deserves its sentence of honour in this connection, because it links the section to the historical novel's treatment earlier. Setting a work in another century can be a way of discussing the present under conditions where the present is unmentionable, and audiences trained by those conditions become expert readers of displacement, hearing the contemporary charge in the period costume. Candidates can ask of any historical set text whether displacement is among its functions, and the asking requires evidence: parallels too pointed to be accidental, anachronisms that lean toward the writing moment, a reception history in which the first audiences demonstrably took the point. With such evidence the claim is scholarship; without it, the claim is the candidate's ingenuity admiring itself, and markers are paid to know the difference.

The market's enforcement is quieter and, for most set texts in most periods, more pervasive than the censor's. It works through advances and refusals, through the length a publisher will risk, the subject a magazine will carry, the ending a circulating library's subscribers will tolerate, the rating a broadcaster will defend. Its traces in a work are therefore formal before they are thematic: the softened conclusion, the subplot abandoned where it threatened the saleable centre, the serial's pace set by the need to hold subscribers rather than by the story's inner clock. A candidate who can attach one such trace to one documented condition of the work's sale has performed the production analysis this guide keeps prescribing, at the point where it most often decides what a work is permitted to be.

### How can a candidate distinguish a censored silence from an ideological one?

By the evidence's address. A censored silence points outward to a documented act or standing threat, a licence, a cut, a prosecution, a publisher's condition. An ideological silence points inward to a swerve, a question the work raises and drops because its own assumptions cannot process it. Strong answers keep the two apart and let each kind of proof do its own work.

Self-censorship under anticipation is the hardest case and the most rewarding, because it is where the two silences merge in a single style. The writer who has absorbed the limits writes obliquely without any order being given, and the obliquity becomes an aesthetic: irony as armour, the child narrator whose innocence licenses what an adult voice could not say, the animal fable, the foreign setting, the dream. Candidates should treat such styles as evidence of conditions rather than as mere cleverness, while remembering the correction that governs the whole topic: conditions explain the style's necessity without exhausting its meaning, since the oblique work often outlives and outshines the direct work its moment forbade, and the surplus is the work's own.

There is an examination trap coiled in this material, and naming it protects candidates from a seductive error. The trap is the romance of suppression, in which every difficulty in a text is credited to persecution and every silence becomes proof of the writer's secret radicalism. The romance flatters the candidate's politics and the writer's ghost at the price of the evidence, and it collapses on contact with the record, which usually shows a messier mixture of commerce, convention, conviction, and chance. The discipline is the standing one: enforcement claims require documents, ideological claims require swerves, and the space between the two is where honest candidates write the words perhaps and consistent with, which markers read, correctly, as the accent of a person who knows what proof is.

## Language itself: style as a social fact

The deepest stratum of the method, and the one candidates reach last, treats language not as the transparent medium of a work's meanings but as a social material with its own history and its own class markings. The Russian thinker Valentin Voloshinov, whose work on the philosophy of language appeared under the shadow of attribution disputes that need not detain an examination answer, argued that the word is a social territory, accented differently by every group that speaks it, and that the sign becomes an arena of the class struggle in miniature. Whether or not a candidate cites the name, the insight is usable everywhere: dialogue in fiction is a record of accents contending, of registers claimed and refused, of who may speak in which code to whom, and the contention can be analysed with the same precision as any other distribution in the text.

Register is the candidate's practical handle on this stratum. Every set text orchestrates levels of language: the narrator's standard, the dialect rendered in spelling, the professional jargon, the liturgical echo, the slang that dates a passage to its decade. The orchestration is a social map. Ask whose speech the narration treats as the norm against which others are deviations, whose dialect is rendered phonetically, as spectacle, and whose is allowed the dignity of standard spelling, whose words are translated for the reader and whose are left to stand as the reader's presumed mother tongue. Those decisions, made sentence by sentence, distribute respect with an exactness that no thematic statement in the work can override, and a candidate who audits them has found evidence at the level where ideology is hardest to fake, because it is laid down before reflection begins.

Style at the level of the sentence carries the same charge. The periodic sentence, balanced and subordinated, performs a world in which relations are ordered and knowable. The paratactic rush of short clauses performs a world of impacts without hierarchy. Free indirect discourse, the repertoire's favourite device, performs the porosity of minds to their moment's idiom, letting a character's inner speech be colonised by the clichés of their class and time, so that the reader watches ideology thinking itself inside a skull. Candidates need not master a stylistics syllabus to profit here. They need the habit of asking, of any passage, what its sentences presuppose about the order of the world they move through, which is the form move operating at its smallest scale and therefore at its most undeniable.

### What does free indirect discourse show a Marxist reader?

It shows consciousness as a social material: a character's most private thoughts arriving in the borrowed phrases, valuations, and clichés of their world, so that ideology is caught operating inside the self rather than merely pressing on it from outside. The device is evidence that the boundary between inner and outer, on which the ideology of the autonomous individual rests, is a formal effect rather than a fact.

The politics of standard language completes the stratum. Every national literature carries a history of which variety became the printed norm, whose speech was corrected in the schoolroom, which tongues were demoted to dialect or to silence in the record. A set text written in a standard that was fought for, or against the standard of a colonising power, or in a mixture that refuses the hierarchy of codes, is making its largest political gesture at the level of its medium, before any of its statements are reached. Candidates who can say one accurate sentence about their text's language politics, grounded in the work's actual practice rather than in generalities about empowerment, add a dimension that markers rarely see and therefore remember, and remembered scripts are the ones revisited when bands are argued over.

## Tragedy, comedy, and the class life of genres

Genres are not neutral containers, and two of the oldest carry class histories so legible that candidates can use them as training grounds for the whole method. Tragedy, in its classical and Renaissance forms, presupposed persons of high station, on the theory that only the fall of the great could carry the requisite weight, a presupposition Aristotle recorded and centuries of practice enforced. The democratisation of tragedy, the slow admission of merchants, clerks, wives, and workers to the dignity of catastrophic seriousness, is one of literary history's clearest records of a social order's self-image changing under pressure. A candidate who asks of any serious set text whose suffering the form permits to count as tragic, and whose it processes as pathos, accident, or comedy, has located the genre's ideology at its foundation.

Raymond Williams wrote the indispensable modern account of that transformation, tracing how the tragic idea itself changed as its social basis changed, from the fall of princes through the liberal tragedy of the individual against society to the revolutionary and existential variants of the industrial centuries. The account's examination value is its demonstration that even the most apparently timeless of categories, what tragedy is, turns out on inspection to be a historical variable, defined differently by different orders for different needs. Deploying one step of that demonstration, the admission of the common hero or the redefinition of the tragic flaw as a social contradiction, writes about genre with the method's characteristic move: the eternal form dissolves into a dated practice, and the dating is the analysis.

Comedy carries the complementary history. Its classical business is the exposure and correction of folly within an order that survives the correction, ending in marriages that reweave the social fabric. The question the method teaches candidates to ask is whose folly the comedy corrects and whose it never touches: the upstart, the miser, the pedant, and the hypocrite are traditional butts, while the arrangements that produce upstarts and misers stand offstage, unmocked. Against that conservative grain stands comedy's other inheritance, the carnival tradition that Bakhtin recovered, in which laughter inverts hierarchy, crowns the fool, and speaks the unofficial truth of the marketplace. A set comedy can be placed, with evidence, on the spectrum between correction and carnival, and the placing is a genuine finding about the work's politics of form, earned from its jokes' targets and its ending's settlements rather than from its author's known opinions.

### How does a genre's history become evidence in an answer?

By treating the genre's conventions as dated decisions rather than eternal rules. State what the convention presupposed when it hardened, whose experience it centred, and then show the set text honouring, straining, or breaking the convention at a located moment. The history supplies the baseline; the text's deviation from it, or obedience to it, supplies the finding.

Melodrama and the sensation forms extend the training ground into the industrial centuries, where they belong to the new urban publics and their hungers: for legible virtue and villainy in a world grown illegible, for the recognition scene in which the lost are restored and the deserving named, for poetic justice administered with a speed the actual courts and markets never matched. The method reads those hungers as evidence about the world that produced them, a world in which the individual's fate had become opaque, dependent on distant owners, paper instruments, and accidents of birth concealed and revealed. Candidates should resist the condescension that clings to melodrama in survey courses. Its excesses are data, and the candidate who reads them as a public's emotional technology, rather than as failed realism, has grasped the cultural materialist point at its most practical.

The Gothic offers a final exhibit, compact enough to close the section. Its castles, doubles, persecuted heroines, and buried crimes have been read, by critics inside this tradition, as the return of what a rational, commercial order repressed: the feudal past inside the bourgeois present, the violence of acquisition dressed as inheritance, the household's secrets as the truth of its respectability. Whether a candidate endorses that reading of a set Gothic text or contests it, the contest is conducted on the method's home ground, where a form's furniture is treated as a society's dream work. The section's lesson generalises beyond its exhibits. Any genre, the western, the campus novel, the police procedural, the dystopia, can be interrogated the same way: date its conventions, name its presuppositions, locate its settlements, and the genre becomes an archive of the order that needed it.

## The reader's position: implied audiences and real ones

Every work builds a seat for its reader and invites a particular person to sit in it, and the method treats that seat as evidence. The implied reader of a text can be reconstructed from its explanations and its silences of information: what the narration bothers to explain marks the outsider, what it leaves unexplained marks the insider it assumes. A novel that explains the rituals of a trade union meeting assumes readers who have never attended one. A novel that leaves the rituals of a hunt or a boardroom unglossed assumes readers at home in those rooms, or willing to be flattered as such. Candidates can reconstruct the seat in a paragraph and then ask the material questions: who could afford the book, the ticket, the leisure, the education the seat presupposes, and how does the work's politics change when the seat's occupant is named?

Real readers complicate the picture in ways the tradition's reception studies have made examinable. Works escape their intended seats. The improving tale is read for its sensation scenes, the radical pamphlet novel is preserved as a period romance, the colonial school text is read against the grain by the colonised schoolchild who will grow up to answer it in kind. Candidates need not narrate rebellions of reading at length. They need the principle, stated once and evidenced once: the meaning a work does in the world is a function of its readers' positions as well as of its maker's design, and a criticism that attends only to design has told half the material story. That principle is also the answer's protection against the charge of reduction, since a method that builds the reader's freedom into its own account cannot be accused of treating audiences as clay.

The classroom is a reception site candidates know from inside, and a sentence of reflexivity about it earns its place in evaluative answers. The set text arrives already framed: by the edition's introduction, the teacher's emphases, the examination's own past questions, which teach each cohort what kind of object the work is to be treated as. A candidate who notices the frame demonstrates the method applied to the candidate's own situation, and examiners, who built the frame, recognise the notice as sophistication rather than impertinence, provided it is offered as analysis and not as complaint. One sentence is the ration. More becomes a grievance, and grievances, like glamour, devalue with circulation.

### Whose knowledge does a narration flatter, and why does it matter?

It matters because flattery is a market relation. A text that assumes its reader's ease with the rituals of wealth recruits that reader into the world it depicts, while a text that explains those rituals positions its reader outside, as pupil or as critic. Locating the flattered reader tells the candidate which class's experience the form treats as the universal seat, which is ideology at the level of address.

Book history supplies the hard evidence for this section's claims, and candidates in the more scholarly assessment contexts can cite its findings in general terms: the price of a volume relative to a wage, the format that signalled a public, duodecimo for the pocket, the triple decker for the library subscriber, the paperback for the railway stall. Format is destiny's paperwork. A work issued dear in three volumes and a work issued cheap in one did not merely reach different readers; they were, in pacing, in subplot management, in the very unit of the chapter, different kinds of object, shaped by the economics of their containers. The candidate who knows one such fact about a set text's first life possesses the production point in its most concrete form, and concrete points are the ones that survive cross-examination by a sceptical marker.

Adaptation extends the reception story forward and gives media candidates their version of the same enquiry. A work remade for film, for radio, for the stage, or for the serial screen is re-seated each time: new publics, new censors, new economics, new settlements where the old ending cannot be sold. Comparing a set text with one adaptation's handling of its most ideological scene, the scene of labour, of inheritance, of rebellion, produces paragraphs in which every claim is evidenced twice, once in each version's choices. Candidates should choose the scene where the versions diverge most sharply, since divergence is the data; where the adaptation merely illustrates the text, nothing has been learned that the text alone could not teach.

## Time in narrative: the politics of pacing and order

Narrative time is a resource distributed unequally, and its distribution is political in the strict sense that it allocates significance. A work spends its pages somewhere: in the drawing room rather than the kitchen, in the crisis rather than the decade of routine that made the crisis, in the hero's education rather than in the unrecorded years of those who fed and taught him. Candidates can audit the allocation. Which stretches of a life receive scenes and which receive summary, which labours are shown in their duration and which are elided between a bell and a finished table, whose waiting is rendered as suspense and whose as dead time the narrative hurries past? The audit converts an intuition about a work's sympathies into a ledger, and ledgers persuade markers because they can be checked.

Chronology's order carries a second politics. The retrospective narration, in which a survivor recounts a past from the safety of its outcome, presupposes that the past is settleable, that its meaning ripens in the telling. The narration that withholds outcomes, that traps the reader in the present tense of events whose significance nobody yet commands, presupposes a world less governable. Historians of the form have tied those choices to their moments: the confident retrospect of an order that believed in progress, the fractured timelines of a century that had watched progress burn. Candidates should make such ties modestly, as consistency rather than proof, and should always return to the text's own distribution of knowledge, who is allowed to know the ending while living the middle, since that allowance is power in its narrative form.

The ending's settlement has been this guide's recurring measure, and it can now be given its full theoretical dress. Fredric Jameson's account of narrative as a socially symbolic act proposes that stories are imaginary resolutions of real contradictions: the work cannot solve in life what its society cannot solve, so it solves it in form, by marriage, by death, by departure, by the discovery that the antagonists were kin. The candidate's use is diagnostic. Name the contradiction the work has been honest enough to raise, then examine the resolution's mechanism and price: what must be forgotten, forgiven without cause, exported beyond the border, or killed before its time, for the settlement to hold? The price list is the ideology, itemised at the moment of maximum pressure, and an answer that ends with the price list has ended with its strongest evidence in its hands.

### What does an ending have to forget in order to settle?

Whatever the middle was honest about: the debt that cannot be repaid, the labour that cannot be acknowledged, the grievance that has no redress inside the order the ending restores. Listing the forgetting, item by item against the scenes that established each item, turns the settlement from a plot fact into the answer's verdict, evidenced at the point where the work is most itself.

There is a discipline of fairness to observe in ending analysis, since endings are easy to prosecute. A settlement is not exposed as false merely because it settles; every form must end somewhere, and the demand that a work refuse all resolution is a demand for a different art, which the candidate may prefer but may not smuggle in as a standard. The fair question is comparative and internal: given the materials this work assembled, is its settlement earned by them or imposed on them, and does the work know the difference? Some works signal their own settlements as fragile, with an irony, a residue, a final image that reopens what the plot has closed, and that signal is the work performing the candidate's criticism in advance. Finding the signal is the highest form of the exercise, because it converts the verdict from the candidate's imposition into the work's own confession, quoted at the moment it is made.

## Space in narrative: houses, cities, and the geography of class

If time is one axis of the method's attention, space is the other, and it is often the more immediately legible. Works build worlds with addresses. The great house and the cottage, the office and the workshop, the capital and the province, the colony and the metropole: each pairing distributes value before a word of dialogue is spoken, and candidates can read the distribution as directly as they read a map's legend. Ask which spaces the narration enters freely and which it approaches only through a visitor's eyes, which interiors are rendered with the intimacy of habit and which with the inventory of a stranger, which thresholds characters may cross and which crossings require disguise, errand, or catastrophe. The answers compose a geography of permission, and permission is class in its spatial form.

The country and the city carry the repertoire's most storied spatial argument, developed by Williams in a study that traced the paired images across centuries of English writing: the country as the lost home of a knowable community, honest labour, and organic relation; the city as the seat of money, crowds, and estrangement, and also, in the counter-image, of energy, variety, and the future. Williams showed that the contrast is a structure of feeling with a history, each generation relocating the lost harmonious country a lifetime further back, so that the golden age retreats at walking pace ahead of the rememberer. Candidates can use that recession as a diagnostic: when a set text locates its harmony, and what the location's date reveals about the text's own moment of loss. The diagnostic works because it converts nostalgia, the vaguest of literary atmospheres, into a datable claim.

Interiors deserve a paragraph of their own, since the domestic interior is where the novel's class analysis is most often transacted. Rooms are capital made habitable: their size, light, furnishing, and service encode an income and a staff, and the narration's handling of them encodes an attitude. The inventory that lingers lovingly over objects performs one relation to property; the room rendered only through the labour that maintains it, the fire laid, the floor scrubbed by hands the plot forgets, performs another. Candidates should attend to thresholds and back stairs, to the architecture of service that great houses build into their plans, the separate circulation by which the machinery of comfort remains invisible to the comfortable. A floor plan is an ideology with corridors, and a candidate who reads one aloud in an answer has found evidence no thematic paraphrase can equal.

### How does a candidate read a house as evidence rather than as setting?

Treat the building as a distribution: of space, light, privacy, and labour. Ask who may enter which rooms, whose work maintains the comfort, which circulation keeps that work invisible, and how the narration's own movements among the rooms repeat or expose the distribution. Setting becomes evidence when the description is audited rather than admired.

Public space completes the geography. The street, the crowd, the railway carriage, the exhibition, the department store: the nineteenth century invented stages on which classes could observe one another, and its literatures are full of the observation's pleasures and panics. The flâneur, the detached stroller of the modern city whom Benjamin made famous, is a class figure in his very detachment, a man whose leisure purchases the right to look without being implicated. Candidates can ask of any urban text who is granted the strolling gaze and who exists to be gazed at, who reads the crowd as a spectacle and who experiences it as a press of dangers and wages. The crowd itself has a politics of representation, rendered as a many headed threat in frightened texts and as the people's festival in hopeful ones, and the rendering can be dated and placed with the same confidence as any other formal fact.

Colonial space extends the geography to its largest scale, and candidates handling texts of empire should map the metropole's drawing rooms against the unmapped interiors from which their incomes flowed. The classic finding of this tradition of reading concerns the great house whose elegance is funded from plantations the narration never visits, the fortune whose origins sit in a silence at the work's centre. That finding, made famous by postcolonial criticism's reading of the English country house novel, is a symptomatic silence at the scale of a world economy, and it shows candidates the method operating at full stretch: form, production, and empire in a single paragraph. The caution is the standing one. The silence must be argued from the work's approaches and avoidances, its references to the source of wealth that stop at the water's edge, not merely alleged from the fact that empires existed.

## Work on the page: labour depicted, labour hidden

Labour is the tradition's home subject, and yet its depiction in literature is strangely scarce, a scarcity that is itself the first datum. Works are full of the results of work, the cleaned room, the served meal, the built wall, the harvested field, and remarkably empty of work's duration, its repetitions, its skilled knowledge, its cost to the body. Candidates can build an entire analytical movement on that imbalance, provided they build it from the text's own allocations rather than from indignation. The audit is simple to perform and hard to argue with: choose a process the work depends upon and trace its appearances, noting where the narrative shows the doing and where it shows only the done. The pattern of showing and eliding, repeated across a work, is a formal fact with ideological weight, because a world in which products appear without producers is the commodity's own dream of itself, rendered as narrative habit.

Where labour is depicted, its treatment divides instructively. There is labour as spectacle, the forge or the harvest rendered for a reader positioned as a visitor, picturesque in its exertion. There is labour as ordeal, rendered from inside the worker's fatigue and fear, usually in works written from or toward the labouring classes. There is labour as skill, the rarest rendering, in which the knowledge in a worker's hands is given the same respectful attention that other novels give to a lover's thoughts or a detective's deductions. And there is labour as rhythm, in the work songs, the chanted counts, the machinery's beat that organises a scene's prose or a film's editing, where the depiction lives in the form rather than in any description. Candidates who can distinguish those modes in a set text, and ask which mode the work reserves for which class of work, possess a discriminating instrument that few of their competitors will bring into the hall.

Alienation, the young Marx's account of the worker's separation from the product, the process, fellow workers, and his own human capacities, gives candidates the vocabulary for the depictions they find. Its examination use should be disciplined by its status: a philosophical account from the eighteen forties, developed before the mature economic works, treasured by some branches of the tradition and treated cautiously by others. A candidate who knows that status can deploy the concept with the sophistication markers reward, using its four separations as a checklist against a depiction of work and noting where the text's rendering matches the account and where it resists, in the pride of craft, the solidarity of the shop floor, the sabotage that reasserts a human relation to the process by breaking it. Resistance, once again, is a finding rather than an embarrassment.

### What does it mean to say a work hides labour it depends upon?

It means the narrative consumes the products of work, meals, rooms, fortunes, victories, while declining to spend its own attention on the doing, so that effort appears in the story as nature appears in a landscape, as given. The claim is evidenced by allocation, scene against summary, presence against elision, and it survives scepticism because any reader can repeat the audit on the same pages.

The writer's own labour belongs in this section's frame, closing a circle the guide opened with authorship as work. A novel about a factory written in a garret, a poem of harvests composed at a patron's desk, a play of the mines rehearsed by actors who will eat whether the house fills or not: the ironies of production are not gossip but data, the conditions under which representations of work were themselves worked into being. Candidates should use those ironies sparingly and exactly, as they use all production material, one mechanism and one feature at a time. Used so, they protect the answer from the naivety of treating any depiction, however sympathetic, as a transparent window onto the labour it shows. Every window was made, by hands, for a market, in a room with its own economics, and the method's maturity consists in remembering the making while still, and genuinely, looking through the glass.

There is a final, quieter point about labour that separates strong candidates from the rest, and it concerns the labour of reading itself as examinations arrange it. The set text was not written to be studied against a clock, annotated in three colours, and reproduced in timed prose. The examination transforms a work of art into an instrument of sorting, and the transformation is a material fact about the work's present life that a reflective sentence can acknowledge. Candidates who acknowledge it, once, in an evaluative answer, show the method's reflexivity at its most attractive: the willingness to include its own situation among the conditions it analyses. The sentence costs nothing and signals everything, which is why this guide, itself a product of the sorting industry, ends its doctrinal sections by recommending it.

## Money as a character: exchange, debt, and the plot

Money in fiction is rarely mere arithmetic. It is a narrative technology, a device for connecting strangers, motivating journeys, forcing meetings, and timing revelations, and the method reads it as the social relation it is: a claim on other people's labour, portable, impersonal, and endlessly convertible. Candidates can track money through a set text as they would track a character, noting its entrances, its disguises, its moments of candour. Fortunes arrive by inheritance, by marriage, by speculation, by discovery, by crime, and the distribution of those arrivals is a moral economy in code: which sources of wealth a work treats as clean, which it marks as tainted, which it never investigates at all. The code can be cracked by any candidate willing to keep a ledger, and the ledger, like all ledgers, persuades by accumulation rather than by eloquence.

Debt is money's most dramatic form and the plot's oldest engine. The debtor lives in a future owned by someone else, and narratives of debt, from the bond in the Venetian play to the mortgage in the provincial novel, organise time itself around repayment's deadline. Candidates can ask who in a work is permitted to owe and who must always pay on the nail, whose credit is believed and whose word requires collateral, how the work distributes the shame of indebtedness between the feckless borrower and the system that profits from his fecklessness. Those questions convert financial machinery into character analysis of the deepest kind, since a person's relation to credit is a person's relation to the future, and a society reveals its real hierarchy in nothing more clearly than in whose promises it accepts as money.

Speculation and paper wealth mark the point where money's abstraction becomes a theme the works themselves notice. Bubbles, shares, forged bills, and fortunes that exist only as confidence recur in literatures of every commercial century, and they attract this method because they stage reification in its purest form: relations between people, promises, trusts, fears, masquerading as things with prices, until the masquerade collapses and the relations stand revealed in the panic. A candidate writing on such material should keep the analysis at the level of the work's own staging, how the narrative makes paper feel solid and then makes it evaporate, rather than delivering a lecture on finance. The text has done the theorising in images; the candidate's task is to show the working.

### Why do plots so often turn on inheritances and settlements?

Because inheritance is the point where a society's official story, that position follows merit and effort, meets its actual mechanism, that position follows birth and the law of property. A plot that must move a fortune between generations is forced to show the mechanism, and what a plot is forced to show, a candidate can analyse without importing anything the work has not already supplied.

Marriage, in the literatures of property, is inheritance's twin institution, and the method's treatment of it should be exact rather than merely knowing. The marriage plot's settlements, portions, jointures, and expectations are not background colour but the plot's actual stakes, the sums on which the heroine's future and the family's strategy turn. Candidates who read the love story and the settlement as one story, refusing the modern separation of feeling from finance that the works themselves do not observe, recover the texts' own understanding of their world. The recovery often reverses the apparent politics of a scene: the mercenary character may be the only one speaking the truth of the institution, while the romantic rebel's freedom is quietly funded by the very arrangements the rebel scorns. Such reversals, evidenced from the text's own arithmetic, are the paragraphs markers remember.

## Education inside the fiction, and the fiction inside education

Schools appear in literature with a frequency that this method is built to notice, because the school is the institution where a society reproduces its order inside its children's heads, and novelists have always known that the classroom is a theatre of power in miniature. The candidate's enquiries follow Althusser's insight about ideological apparatuses while staying with the evidence of the page. What does the fictional school teach besides its lessons: punctuality, deference, the ranking of children into orders of worth, the lesson that some voices explain and others answer? Which children does the narrative follow into the classroom and which it leaves at the gate, and what futures does the form distribute from that threshold? How are teachers themselves positioned, as authorities, as drudges, as the order's poorly paid missionaries, and does the work see the irony of their position or merely use it?

Bildung, the formation of the hero through experience, is the school plot generalised into the novel's master pattern, and its politics deserve the candidate's closest attention because they are the form's own politics. The education plot presupposes that the world is a curriculum and the self its student, that experience grades and promotes, and that the ending's settlement, a profession, a marriage, a chastened philosophy, is a graduation. Candidates can ask what the curriculum excludes: the educations that happen off the hero's path, in kitchens and workshops and other people's disappointments, which the narrative samples only as scenery for the hero's growth. The question converts a beloved form into an object of analysis without denying its pleasures, which is the method's characteristic courtesy, and it yields the finding that the formation novel's deepest ideology is its distribution of the right to be formed.

The examination system that sets these texts is, of course, an ideological apparatus in the same analysis, and the tradition has never exempted its own institutions from its instruments. Universities and examination boards select the canon, frame the questions, and certify the answers, and a candidate's awareness of that framing has already been recommended in this guide as a single reflective sentence. There is a larger use for teachers in the same insight: the syllabus is a reading of the culture before it is a course of study, and its inclusions and exclusions can be taught as an object lesson in the method itself. Students who have analysed why their set text is set have understood something about cultural authority that no amount of analysis of the text alone could teach them, and they carry the understanding into the hall as calm, since a candidate who sees the frame is less likely to be frightened by it.

### What does a school scene show that a lesson summary cannot?

It shows the hidden curriculum in action: the ranking, the deference, the distribution of speech and silence, the futures being allocated by tone of voice and quality of cloth. A summary reports what was taught. An analysis of the scene reports what was learned, which includes everything the institution teaches about a child's place, delivered in the medium of its arrangements.

Reading itself, inside fictions, is a class act with a history, and candidates can mine it wherever a character is shown with a book. Who reads, what, where, and at whose expense: the lady with the novel in the carriage, the clerk with the improving volume, the servant girl with the forbidden romance, the autodidact with his borrowed library card and his fierce, unguided canon. Those scenes are the literature of a society thinking about its own circulation, and they encode its anxieties precisely, about women reading, workers reading, the wrong people reading the wrong things and drawing conclusions. A candidate who gathers a set text's scenes of reading into one paragraph has built an argument about the work's theory of its own audience, which is among the most sophisticated objects this method can construct, and it is constructed entirely from evidence the work supplies about itself.

The autodidact deserves a closing word, because the figure concentrates the tradition's hopes and its realism in a single character type. The self taught reader is the proof that the apparatus does not fully determine its products, that the schoolroom's exclusions can be answered from the library's margins, and generations of working class autobiography testify that the answering happened, at scale, in fact. Candidates who meet the figure in a set text should resist both sentimentality and dismissal. The autodidact's achievement is real, and its price, the exhaustion, the isolation, the knowledge acquired without the credentials that make knowledge payable, is also real, and the work that honours both has understood its subject. The candidate who can hold the pair together in a paragraph has learned the guide's deepest habit, which is to let every finding carry its counterweight in the same hand.

## The unconscious of the text: dreams, doubles, and symptoms

The symptomatic method, practised through this guide on silences and swerves, has a natural extension into the territory where texts behave like dreamers: the doubles, repetitions, hauntings, and irrational recurrences by which fictions say what their official narratives cannot. Candidates sometimes fear that this territory belongs to psychoanalytic criticism alone, and that entering it concedes the comparison question in advance. The fear is unfounded. The two traditions have quarrelled and collaborated for a century, and the Marxist route into the text's unconscious runs through history rather than through the nursery: what returns, in a work's ghosts and doubles, is what its social world has repressed, the violence of its founding, the labour of its comforts, the dead whose claims the settlement declined to pay.

The Gothic has already supplied one exhibit, and the principle generalises. A double can be read as the return of a disowned relation: the master and the servant who is his shadow and his judge, the respectable merchant and the ruined speculator who share a name's other half, the coloniser and the colonised figure who knows the house's secrets because his hands built it. Repetition can be read as the rhythm of a compulsion the society cannot examine, the debt that must be reincurred, the scandal that must recur in each generation wearing new clothes. The candidate's discipline is the standing one: the reading must be argued from the work's patterns, its placements and recurrences, and must state what would count against it, since symptomatic reading, ungoverned, can find anything anywhere, and a method that finds everything has found nothing that a marker is obliged to credit.

Jameson's political unconscious names the destination of this line of work, and candidates can use his central proposal in its modest form: that a narrative's deepest energies are historical, that its fantasies are strategies for containing contradictions its world cannot resolve, and that interpretation's task is to restore to the text the history it has repressed into form. The proposal, modestly used, authorises the candidate's boldest paragraphs, the ones that move from a haunting to an expropriation, from a double to a disowned labour force, from a cursed inheritance to the violence that founded the estate. Boldness under governance is the exact quality the highest bands describe, and the governance is supplied by the same paragraph architecture and defeater habit that govern everything else in this guide.

### How can a candidate use a ghost in a set text without leaving the method?

Ask what the haunting dates from and what it wants. Trace the ghost to the work's buried transaction, the dispossession, the unpaid debt, the unburied labour, and show how the narrative's official story depends on keeping that transaction buried. The ghost becomes the form's own symptomatic reading of its world, and the candidate is extending the work's analysis rather than importing one.

There is a reciprocal caution with which to close, because the traffic between the traditions runs in both directions and honesty requires the acknowledgement. Some recurrences in texts resist historical restoration and belong to the permanent furniture of human fear and desire: death, the parent, the stranger at the door. A method that annexes every motif to a social origin overreaches, and the overreach is the mirror image of the formalism that annexes every motif to the psyche. The allocative verdict governs here as in the comparison sections: this haunting yields to history, that one does not, and the candidate's discrimination between them, evidenced in both directions, is worth more than a unified theory of ghosts. Examiners set haunted texts partly to watch candidates discriminate, and the discrimination, performed calmly, is the answer's distinction.

## Character as construction: typicality, flatness, and the distribution of souls

Character is where candidates' habits are most deeply formed by ordinary reading, and where the method's re-education is therefore most necessary. Ordinary reading treats characters as persons, with motives to be sympathised with and choices to be judged. The method treats them as constructions, arrangements of permissions: what a figure is allowed to know, to want, to say, to become, and what the arrangement reveals about the world that made such permissions plausible. The shift does not forbid sympathy; it postpones it until after the construction has been described, and the postponement is what markers are detecting when they praise an answer's maturity.

Lukács's concept of the typical gives the construction its positive account. The typical character, in his usage, is neither an average nor an allegory but a figure in whom the essential forces of an age achieve individual life, so that the person's private fate renders a public process intelligible. Candidates can use the concept as a test with two cutting edges. Applied generously, it identifies the figures through whom a work thinks its world, often not the nominal heroes but the strivers, failures, and go betweens in whom an order's promises and prices are both visible. Applied critically, it exposes the figures a work cannot make typical, the ones it must keep flat, comic, or monstrous because granting them full interiority would force the narrative to confront what its settlement excludes. Flatness, on this account, is not always a failure of art. It is frequently a decision of ideology, and the decision can be located, figure by figure, in the distribution of minds.

The distribution of souls, to give the audit its sharpest name, is countable, and countable evidence is the kind sceptical markers cannot wave away. A candidate can report, of a set text, which figures receive a past, a hesitation rendered from inside, a private metaphor, a scene alone; which receive only functions, entrances with messages, exits with trays; and which receive the ambiguous middle kingdom of reported thought without direct access. The census takes minutes to perform in revision and yields a paragraph whose every claim points at a page. It also tends to surprise the candidate who performs it, since works reveal distributions their readers absorbed without noticing, and surprise, in revision, is the sign that learning is still possible.

### Why does a minor character's flatness matter to this method?

Because flatness is allocated, and allocation is evidence. A figure denied interiority, a past, and a future is being used as furniture in other people's stories, and the using reproduces, inside the form, the social relation the work depicts outside it. The candidate who audits the allocation has found the ideology of the casting, which no summary of the plot will ever show.

Against the audit stands a legitimate defence of flatness that strong candidates should be able to make, since the defeater discipline requires the strongest counter case. Some flatness is economy: a narrative cannot fund every soul, and the concentration of interiority is a condition of depth anywhere. Some flatness is satire's instrument, the type deliberately held at two dimensions so that a social mechanism can be seen working without the fog of individuality. Some flatness is the character's own social truth, the record of what a world has done to a person's available dimensions. The audit's findings must therefore be argued, not merely reported: the candidate shows that this flatness falls along the lines of class, gender, or service, that it protects the settlement, that the work spends interiority lavishly where its sympathies are engaged and rations it exactly where its world requires servants. Pattern is the difference between an observation and an argument, and pattern is what the census, honestly kept, either finds or fails to find.

Names belong to the construction's smallest evidence, and candidates occasionally strike gold in them. The speaking name that announces a function, the surname that carries a trade or a place, the servant known by a Christian name alone, the woman renamed by marriage out of her family's line, the colonial figure renamed by a master's convenience: each is a small filing system in which a society stores its theory of persons. A paragraph on naming, where the text supports one, demonstrates attention at the level where ideology is laid down before reflection, and it has the additional virtue of being unmistakably the candidate's own work, since no revision guide can pre write the names of a particular text's people.

## Endings across genres: settlement as the universal question

The settlement question has recurred throughout this guide because it is the one enquiry that no genre escapes, and gathering its variants in one place equips candidates to close answers on any text the paper can set. The novel settles by marriage, inheritance, death, departure, and reconciliation, and its settlements redistribute property and persons together, which is why the audit of who gains, who pays, and who is forgotten works so reliably there. The drama settles by restoration or catastrophe, the throne refilled or the stage heaped, and its settlements are public, performed before the assembled world of the play, so that the audience itself is cast as the witness who ratifies the order restored. The lyric settles in a turn, a final image or address that reframes the feeling the poem has carried, and its settlements are the smallest and most testable of all, since a single closing line must bear the weight the novel spreads across a chapter.

Comedy's settlement is the most ideological of the set, because comedy must forgive. The blocking figure is expelled or converted, the couples are paired, the dance reweaves the fabric, and the candidate's question is the price of the forgiveness: what has been agreed, by the whole assembled company, never to mention again? Tragedy's settlement is the most deceptive, because catastrophe feels like the opposite of settlement while performing it: the deaths clear the stage of the contradiction's carriers, the survivor's speech reknits the state, and the audience is sent home with the order's continuity affirmed by the very scale of what it cost. A candidate who can show a tragedy's closing speech doing the work of a comic dance, at a higher price and in a graver register, has seen through genre to function, which is the method's oldest ambition.

Serial forms settle by deferral, and the deferral is their economics: the instalment must close enough to satisfy and open enough to sell the next, so its endings are settlements under erasure, marriages shadowed, fortunes provisional, villains only mostly dead. Candidates writing on serialised texts, in print or on screen, can make the deferral itself the finding, connecting the form's refusal of finality to the market that pays by the episode and to the audience's life, in which finality is equally rare. The connection is a production point and a form point in a single gesture, and gestures of that economy are what the planning minutes exist to prepare.

### What unites the settlement question across every genre?

Each genre must end by doing something with the disturbance it raised, and the something is always a distribution: of property, of pardon, of blame, of memory. Ask what the ending distributes, to whom, at whose expense, and with what agreed forgetting, and the question will open a novel, a tragedy, a lyric, or a serial with the same key.

The counter-case completes the doctrine, since some of the most examinable works refuse settlement altogether, ending in departure without destination, in questions restated, in images that decline to resolve into meaning. Refusal is also a distribution: it distributes the work's unresolved contradiction to the reader, who must carry it out of the book or the theatre into a world where the contradiction is still operating. Candidates should honour refusal as a formal decision with its own presuppositions, the belief that a false settlement would be a lie and that the audience can be trusted with an open wound. Honouring it, however, does not exempt it from the audit. The candidate still asks what the refusal costs and whom it serves, since an open ending can be its own kind of evasion, a prestige gesture that declines the responsibility of judgement. The audit has no favourites. That is what makes it an audit, and what makes its findings, favourable or not, worth a marker's trust.

## A worked plan, taken apart: from question to skeleton in five minutes

Doctrine becomes useful when it survives contact with an actual clock, so this section performs the planning of a plausible answer at the speed the hall allows, narrating each decision as it is made. The question supposed is of the commonest evaluative family: candidates are asked how far a Marxist reading illuminates a set text they know, with the text here left generic, since the performance's value lies in its moves rather than in any particular work's details. The clock starts, and the first act is classification: the verb phrase how far demands calibration and allocation, the object is the reading's usefulness, and the matrix row is the evaluation row, whose trap is the performance of allegiance or hostility in place of weighing. Thirty seconds spent, and the answer already knows its shape and its danger.

The second act selects the lead. The concept chosen is the form move itself, since an evaluation of a reading is best conducted by exhibiting the reading's strongest operation and testing it, and the critic attached is Lukács for totality, with Macherey held in reserve for the counter movement. The evidence is chosen by the bite test: the two scenes where the form's presuppositions are most exposed, one where interiority's distribution does the work, one where the ending settles at a visible price. The production point is selected at the same time, the one documented mechanism, serial, patron, or market, that connects to a feature already in the evidence, so that no paragraph will be spent on unconnected background. Two minutes spent, and the skeleton has its spine, its ribs, and its one piece of grounding.

The third act drafts the verdict before the body, reversing the order candidates instinctively follow and reaping the benefit: a verdict known in advance disciplines every paragraph that must earn it. The verdict supposed here is allocative: the reading illuminates the work's arrangements and its silences decisively, and illuminates its verbal music and its private resonances hardly at all, and the defeater is drafted in the same breath, the demonstration that the work's music is itself socially organised, in rhythms drawn from labour and liturgy, would breach the stated boundary and require the allocation to be redrawn. Three minutes spent. The answer's destination is fixed, its condition is fixed, and the writing that remains is the comparatively peaceful work of walking a surveyed road.

The fourth act writes the counter movement's commission into the plan, because counter movements omitted at planning are omitted forever. Its assignment is the strongest resistance: the passage where the work's sympathy overflows the reading's categories, where a figure the audit counted flat is granted, once, a sentence of devastating interiority, and the plan notes the paragraph's duty, which is to absorb that sentence into the verdict rather than to hide it. The fifth act allocates the hour's remainder across the movements in writing, with the close protected, and the planning ends at five minutes with the candidate in possession of something most of the hall never has: an answer whose argument exists before its prose does. The prose, when it comes, will still surprise its writer in places, and should; but the surprises will be local, matters of phrasing and sudden connection, not structural collapses discovered at the half hour with the close unprotected and the verdict unbuilt.

### Why draft the verdict during planning rather than after the body?

Because a verdict known in advance disciplines selection: every paragraph must earn its place in an argument whose destination is fixed, and drift becomes visible the moment it begins. The verdict remains revisable if the writing uncovers stronger evidence, and the revision, when it happens, is itself a finding the close can report with its defeater attached.

Candidates should rehearse this five minute performance as deliberately as they rehearse any set piece, using past questions and a visible timer, until the acts compress into reflex. The rehearsal feels artificial for the first several attempts, which is the sensation of a skill being built rather than a talent being awaited. After a dozen repetitions the sequence stops feeling like a procedure imposed on thinking and starts feeling like thinking itself, briskly conducted, and that transformation, from procedure to habit, is the whole object of examination training on this or any topic. The matrix on the card, the triads in the memory, the verdict drafted early, the close protected: these are not ornaments on the candidate's knowledge. They are the form the knowledge takes when it is finally usable, and usable knowledge is the only kind the hall has ever paid for.

## Final cautions: the ethics of the method in the hall

A guide of this length owes its readers a short list of cautions that are ethical before they are tactical, because the method handles real histories of suffering and hope, and the examination's compression can tempt candidates into handling them as counters. The first caution concerns scale. The tradition's subjects include exploitation, famine's politics, colonial violence, and the betrayals of emancipatory hopes, and a candidate who writes about such material in a set text should let the prose register the weight, briefly and without theatricality, rather than processing catastrophe into a demonstration of technique. Markers are readers before they are graders, and a sentence that knows what it is talking about earns a trust that no apparatus can simulate.

The second caution concerns persons, living traditions, and the candidate's classmates in the hall. The method is an instrument for analysing works, not a licence for judging the believers of any doctrine, and answers that sneer at faith, at conservatism, at radical hope, or at the ordinary reader's pleasures violate the tradition's own best practice, which was to take opponents seriously enough to answer them at full strength. Steel is the guide's word for the treatment owed to every position in an answer: the objection at its strongest, the reply at its fairest, the rival approach at its most capable. The habit is ethical and tactical in the same act, since nothing in a script signals weakness to a professional reader as clearly as a victory over a position nobody holds.

The third caution concerns the candidate's own honesty about evidence. This guide has insisted on documented production facts, argued silences, and verdicts with conditions, and the insistence converges on a single virtue: do not claim more than the page in front of you, or the record behind you, supports. The examination will end, the marks will be recorded, and both will matter less, across a life, than the habit the preparation built. A candidate trained to state what would count against their own judgement has been trained in something no syllabus can list and every serious occupation requires. If this guide and the cluster behind it have a final claim to make for the form move and its companion disciplines, it is that they are rehearsals for that habit, conducted on poems and plays because poems and plays are where the rehearsal is safest and the evidence is richest.

### What is the last check before handing in a script on this topic?

Read the close. Confirm that a verdict is present, that its defeater stands in the adjacent sentence, that at least one critic is named with an argument rather than a label, and that the final paragraph answers the question that was set rather than the question that was revised for. Four confirmations, one minute, and the script leaves carrying the signature the whole method exists to teach.

## The short story and the sketch: small forms, sharp evidence

Short fiction is underrated by candidates preparing this topic, who assume the method needs the novel's acreage to operate. The opposite is nearer the truth. The short story's economy forces every presupposition into a small compass where it can be surveyed entire, and its history is entangled with the magazine market that created it, so production and form arrive in the same small package. The candidate's enquiries begin with the form's famous compression: a story must establish a world in pages, and what it chooses to establish first, the room, the wage, the face, the weather, is a declaration of what it takes to be the grounds of a life. Stories that open with an object, a tool, a garment, a sum of money, are announcing a materialist instinct before any event occurs, and candidates can say so with the object as evidence.

The sketch and the tale, the story's older cousins, carry class histories of their own. The sketch was the periodical's way of sampling the city's types, the costermonger, the seamstress, the clerk, for readers who consumed the metropolis as a gallery, and its form presupposes the strolling observer and the observed specimen. The tale carries an older, communal voice, the fireside rather than the magazine, and its survival inside modern collections is a residual form in Williams's sense, an older organisation of feeling persisting within a newer market. Candidates who can sort a collection's pieces by those inheritances, and show a single story straining between the sketch's taxonomy and the tale's sympathy, have performed genre history, form analysis, and cultural sorting in one movement.

The short story's ending deserves the settlement audit in a concentrated form, because the form specialises in endings that withhold: the epiphany that changes nothing outward, the gesture repeated from the opening now legible as a life sentence, the door closed on a future the reader must supply. Withholding, in a market built on the twist, is also a commercial decision, and the candidate can hold both truths: the open ending as an aesthetic of modern lives that do not resolve, and the open ending as a product perfectly adapted to the magazine that must leave its reader thoughtful rather than satisfied, since thoughtfulness buys the next issue's more expensive advertisements. That doubleness, aesthetic and economic in one breath, is the method's signature, and the short form displays it at a scale a timed answer can actually carry.

### Why does compression help a candidate rather than hinder one?

Because every choice is visible. In a novel a presupposition can hide in the acreage; in a story the first paragraph's selection of detail, the single granted interiority, the closing image's settlement, all sit within a page or two of one another, so the candidate's claims can be evidenced completely instead of sampled, and complete evidence is what the form move has wanted all along.

The short story also disciplines the candidate's greatest vice, which is sprawl. An answer on a story cannot hide behind coverage, since there is little to cover; it must analyse or confess itself empty within a paragraph. Teachers therefore use stories as the training ground for the whole method, setting unseen stories before unseen passages of any other kind, and candidates should adopt the practice in revision: take a story never studied, spend five minutes on the matrix enquiries, presupposition, interiority, money, silence, settlement, and write one paragraph per enquiry. The exercise, repeated across a dozen stories, builds the reflex the hall demands more reliably than any amount of rereading the set novel, because it trains transfer, the skill of meeting new material with old instruments, which is the definition of a method as opposed to a repertoire of prepared remarks.

## Translation and the world republic of letters

Comparative literature candidates meet a version of the production question that other candidates can ignore: the text arrives through translation, and translation is labour, selection, and power before it is equivalence. Which works get translated, into which languages, by whom, at whose expense, and with what framing apparatus of introductions and covers, composes a map of the world's literary hierarchies as material as any chart of trade. A candidate writing on a translated set text can ask what the translation's existence proves about the work's journey, what its choices domesticate and what they leave foreign, and whose reading the translated text presupposes. Those questions are this method's questions, addressed to the medium of the text's arrival rather than to its origin, and they are unanswerable by content summary, which is one measure of their worth.

The domestication enquiry repays precision. A translation that smooths a work's class dialects into a uniform standard has performed, at the level of language, the same distribution of respect the guide examined in original composition, granting some voices the dignity of the norm and demoting others to colour. A translation that preserves the friction, that lets the reader feel the social distance between registers, has made the opposite decision, with its own costs in fluency and its own gains in truth. Candidates need not read the source language to analyse the decision's effects in the text they have, though those who can should say what the original does at the contested points, briefly and without display. The examinable object is the social life of the text in its received form, and the received form is the one in front of the whole hall.

World literature as a category carries its own ideology, which strong candidates can name in evaluative answers. The celebration of literature's global circulation can obscure the inequalities of the circulation itself: the languages that export and the languages that only import, the metropolitan publishers and prize committees that consecrate, the forms, above all the novel of a certain shape, that travel because they fit the consecrating institutions' expectations. To say this is not to refuse the genuine enlargement that translation brings, any more than the culture industry analysis refused pleasure. It is to insist that circulation has conditions, and that a criticism attentive to conditions will ask after them wherever texts move, including across the borders of language, where the movement is most often sentimentalised as pure gain.

### What should a candidate ask about a set text that arrives in translation?

Ask who carried it across and at whose cost, what its framing claims for it, which of its registers the translation dignifies and which it flattens, and what public the translated form presupposes. The text's journey is part of its material life, and the method that examines a work's production at home has no reason to stop examining at the border.

There is a humility proper to this section with which to close it. Most candidates, and most markers, read most of the world's literature in translation, and the method's enquiries are a way of honouring that condition rather than lamenting it. To ask after the labour and the power inside a translation is to treat the translated work as a made thing among made things, continuous with the serial novel shaped by its instalments and the play shaped by its company. The candidate who can extend the production analysis across a language border, in a paragraph, without pretending to a philology they do not possess, demonstrates the transferability that this entire guide has been training, and demonstrates it at the frontier where methods most often stop.

## Oral forms, song, and the literature that was never printed

The repertoire's archive is biased toward print, and candidates who notice the bias gain a paragraph that examiners remember, because it applies the production analysis to the archive itself. For most of human history and for much of the world's population within the print centuries, literature travelled by voice: the ballad, the work song, the tale told in the kitchen and the field, the sermon, the street rhyme, the music hall chorus, the radio play's ancestor in the recitation. Those forms had their own economics, in which the means of production was a memory and a voice, distribution was a gathering, and the audience could answer back, alter the chorus, and carry the altered version to the next parish. A candidate who sets a printed set text against that background can ask what print changed: what was gained in fixity and reach, what was lost in answerability, and whose literature the archive preserved because paper kept it and whose it let evaporate because only voices held it.

Song is the sharpest exhibit, because song fuses the guide's themes in a single artefact. A work song coordinates labour's rhythm and comments on labour's conditions in the same breath; its form is its function, and its analysis requires no separation of the aesthetic from the material that other forms force candidates to argue for. A broadside ballad is a commodity, printed cheap, sold in streets, pasted on walls, that also served as a newspaper, a satire, and a memorial for publics the official record ignored. A hymn organises consent and, in the mouths of congregations who bent it to their own struggles, organised resistance with the same harmonies. Candidates who can take one song inside a set text, a chorus in a novel, a ballad in a play, a hymn at a funeral, and read it with those doublenesses in view, have found a paragraph that performs production, hegemony, and audience agency simultaneously, which is economy of a high order.

The folk tale's economy deserves its sentence as well. Tales collected, edited, and printed by scholars became national treasures and children's classics, and the collecting was an industry with its own selections: the tellers, often women, often servants, anonymised into the folk; the tales cleaned of their coarseness and their politics for the nursery market; the peasant world preserved as charm at the moment of its destruction. A candidate writing on any work that draws on tale material can ask what the drawing cost the sources, and the asking is not ingratitude toward the collectors, without whom nothing would survive to be asked about. It is the method's standing double vision, preservation and appropriation in one act, applied to the archive's own foundations.

### Why does the archive's bias toward print matter to an examination answer?

Because it explains the shape of the evidence before the candidate uses it. What survived was what paper, patrons, and institutions chose to keep, so the record of a culture's imagination is itself a product of the conditions the method studies. A candidate who notes the filter, once, writes about every other piece of evidence in the answer with a more accurate sense of its weight.

Performance in the oral line also corrects a habit print teaches, the habit of treating a text as finished. A song or a tale exists in versions, each performance an edition, each audience a collaborator, and authorship dissolves into a tradition that individuals ornament. Candidates meeting such material, in the drama especially, where every production re edits the play, should let the version principle inform their claims: speak of this text, this production, this recorded performance, rather than of the work as a timeless object, and the precision will read as scholarship because it is scholarship. The method has always insisted that works are made; the oral tradition insists they are remade, and the remaking, nightly, seasonally, generationally, is production analysis extended across time, which is where this guide's long argument about form, labour, and judgement properly comes to rest.

## Children, servants, and strangers: the minor figures as major evidence

A method is known by what it does with the figures a work itself treats as minor, and this section gathers the guide's scattered audits into a single doctrine of the margins. Servants, children, messengers, nurses, clerks, porters, and passing strangers carry the ordinary world's weight in most fictions, and they carry it, typically, in a few lines each, without pasts, without surnames in some cases, without the interiority the form lavishes on their betters. The candidate's doctrine is that the margins are a text's most honest pages, because the centre is where a work performs its intentions while the margins are where its assumptions operate unobserved. An author watches the hero's politics. Nobody watches the porter's, and so the porter's treatment records what the work, and behind it the world that made the work plausible, actually believed about the distribution of souls.

Children occupy a special place in the audit, because childhood is where a society's theory of the person is most exposed. The child in fiction may be a miniature adult, a soul in training, an innocent whose perception indicts the grown world, a worker whose labour the plot requires and whose schooling it never mentions, an heir whose value is a future estate, or an encumbrance whose death the plot can spend for pathos. Each construction presupposes an economics: of the family that can afford innocence, of the parish that farms out its orphans, of the factory that prices small hands. Candidates can ask of any set text which childhoods it funds with attention and which it uses as instruments, and the answer will frequently reorganise their understanding of the whole work, since a fiction's treatment of its least powerful figures is the least guarded evidence of its moral economy.

Strangers perform a complementary office. The visitor from elsewhere, the traveller, the foreign governess, the returned emigrant, the detective from the capital, exists in a plot to see what natives cannot, and the device presupposes that a society is legible to the outsider's eye in proportion to the outsider's freedom from its assumptions. Candidates can interrogate the device rather than merely enjoy it: what does the stranger's licence cost, whose hospitality funds it, which inhabitants are permitted to be strange in return, and what happens to the figures who attempt the journey in the opposite direction, from the margins to the centre, with the centre's own eyes turned on them in suspicion? The interrogation converts a plot mechanism into an analysis of knowledge and power, which is the conversion this guide has practised on every device it has touched.

### Why do the margins of a text deserve the candidate's closest audit?

Because assumptions operate there unobserved. A work's centre performs its intentions and can be defended by its author; its margins, the servant's single line, the child's unconsidered labour, the stranger's licence, record what the work's world took for granted, and what a world takes for granted is the precise object this method exists to examine.

There is a craft consequence for candidates who adopt the doctrine of the margins, and it concerns the management of evidence in an answer. Marginal evidence is small: a line, a stage direction, an unexplained competence, a death disposed of in a subordinate clause. Small evidence cannot carry a paragraph by weight, so it must carry it by placement, set against the centre's contrasting abundance in the same sentence's architecture, the hero's three pages of hesitation against the nurse's single line of dismissal. Juxtaposition is the margin's rhetoric, and candidates who master it write paragraphs of unusual density, in which the argument is performed by the arrangement of the evidence itself. Such paragraphs are also, not coincidentally, the ones most likely to be quoted in examiners' reports as examples of what the topic can do at its best, because they show a candidate reading a work the way the method's founders read their world, from the bottom up and from the edges in.

The doctrine has its defeater, and stating it completes the section in the guide's customary way. Not every margin is a suppression: some figures are minor because the story is not theirs, and a work that funded every soul equally would be a census rather than a narrative. The audit's claim is therefore never that a particular figure deserved more pages, but that the pattern of allocation, repeated and aligned with the lines of class, gender, service, and strangeness, reveals the work's presuppositions about whose experience constitutes a story. Were the allocation random with respect to those lines, the claim would fall, and the candidate should say so, in the answer, in a sentence. The sentence is the doctrine's honesty, and like every defeater in this guide it costs a line and buys the argument's credibility entire.

## Irony: the method's most double edged instrument

Irony saturates the literatures candidates are set, and it deserves a doctrinal section because the method's handling of it is distinctive and because candidates mishandle it in a characteristic way, treating every irony as a secret agreement between author and reader at the expense of the characters. Irony is a distribution of knowledge: someone in the transaction knows more than someone else, and the surplus is power. The candidate's first question is therefore always the same. Who holds the surplus in this passage, the narrator over the character, the reader over the narrator, one character over another, the work over its own genre? And the second question follows the first like a shadow. At whose expense is the surplus spent, and does the work ever return the expenditure, granting the ironised figure a moment in which the knowledge is theirs and the joke's direction reverses?

Dramatic irony in the theatre makes the distribution architectural, since the audience's knowledge is built into the seating: we watch a figure walk toward what we know and they cannot, and the pleasure of the arrangement is inseparable from a small cruelty that candidates should name rather than merely enjoy. The cruelty has a politics when the figure walking blind is marked by class or station, when the audience is invited to feel its superiority as the superiority of the knowing classes over the labouring innocent, the provincial in the capital, the servant who misunderstands the letter she carries. Against those arrangements stand the works that reverse the current, granting the supposedly ignorant figure the scene's only accurate knowledge and letting the audience's surplus drain away into embarrassment. The reversal is a formal event with an ideological charge, and candidates who track a set work's ironies by their direction, noting where the current runs and where it turns, possess a map of the work's sympathies more reliable than any declaration the work contains.

Verbal irony at the sentence level carries the same charge in miniature. The narrator who calls a ruinous speculation a prudent venture, who describes an eviction in the language of improvement, who lets an official phrase stand in a context that rots it, is conducting an argument about the language of power itself, about the words in which a society misdescribes its arrangements to itself. Candidates should quote such sentences exactly and briefly, since the irony lives in the wording and dies in paraphrase, and should identify the misdescription's source, the report, the advertisement, the sermon, the leading article, whose idiom the narration is borrowing in order to expose. That identification connects the smallest unit of style to the largest machinery of ideology, the organised production of a world's account of itself, and a paragraph that makes the connection has travelled the method's whole road in a few sentences.

### How can a candidate tell whether an irony is critical or merely cruel?

Follow the knowledge and the cost. Ask who is granted the surplus of understanding, who pays for the joke with their dignity, and whether the work ever reverses the direction, letting the ironised figure know, speak, or judge in return. An irony whose current runs only downward, from the knowing to the marked, serves the order it pretends to mock; an irony that turns is criticism in action.

The self irony of works, the moment a text mocks its own procedures, belongs in this section as its most sophisticated exhibit. The novel that parodies its own marriage plot while completing it, the play that shows its scenery being struck while the fiction continues, the poem that apologises for its metaphors in the act of spending them, each is conducting the symptomatic reading on itself, exposing its presuppositions before a critic can. Candidates often treat such moments as jokes to be noted and passed over. They are better treated as the work's own criticism, admitted into evidence as the strongest kind, since a presupposition confessed by the form itself requires no inference from the candidate and can be quoted against every simpler reading in the field. Where a set text ironises its own settlement, its own narrator, its own genre contract, the candidate's task is almost ceremonial: present the confession, state what it concedes, and build the verdict on ground the work itself has cleared.

A caution closes the doctrine, matching the caution that closed the treatment of silence. Irony, alleged everywhere, becomes the critic's vanity, a way of making every text agree with the reader by reversing whatever it says. The discipline is evidence of design: recurrence, placement at moments of strain, the corroboration of pattern across a work, and the willingness to record the passages where no irony can honestly be found and the text means, flatly and terribly, exactly what it says. Some sentences in the literatures of exploitation are not ironic, and a candidate who can leave them unironic, who can let a plain statement stand as the work's plain indictment or plain complacency, demonstrates a restraint that markers trust more than ingenuity. Trust, accumulated across a script, is the currency in which the highest bands are ultimately paid, and every doctrine in this guide, from the form move to the defeater, is finally a technique for deserving it.

## Sentiment: the politics of feeling in the fiction of reform

Sentimental literature poses the method a delicate problem and therefore earns a final doctrinal section, because the works that made generations weep over orphans, seamstresses, prisoners, and slaves also moved some of those generations toward reform, while the tradition's sterner voices suspected tears of being a substitute for action, a feeling purchased precisely so that nothing else need be spent. Candidates meeting a set text in the sentimental line need both halves of that argument and the means to test them against the work's own machinery of feeling. The first question is mechanical: how does the work manufacture its tears, through what scenes of innocence imperilled, what delays of rescue, what deathbed speeches polished for quotation, what music of prose rhythm at the moment of maximum pathos? Manufacture is not a term of contempt here. It is the production analysis applied to emotion, and it respects the craft involved while refusing to let the craft pass as nature.

The second question concerns the tears' destination. A feeling must go somewhere: toward a subscription, a petition, a changed law, a private resolve, or toward the pillow, discharged and done. Works differ measurably in the exits they build for the emotion they raise. Some end with the machinery of redress displayed, the society founded, the bill described, the reader's role specified as a citizen's errand. Some end with the sufferer translated to heaven and the reader consoled that justice operates elsewhere, an exit that returns the world to its arrangements with the reader's conscience laundered. Candidates can audit the exits as they audit endings generally, asking what the settlement settles and what it sedates, and the audit converts the old quarrel about sentimentality from a matter of taste into a matter of evidence, which is where this guide has insisted every quarrel on this topic belongs.

### When do a work's tears become an argument rather than an evasion?

When the feeling is routed toward the arrangements that caused the suffering, and the work shows its reader the route: the institution named, the remedy specified, the cost acknowledged. Tears that terminate in the sufferer's virtue or in heaven's compensation console the reader at the price of the world; tears that terminate in an address, a fund, a law, or a changed practice are the beginning of politics in the form of emotion.

The defence of sentiment must be stated at full strength before the section closes, in keeping with the guide's rule for all positions. Feeling is knowledge of a kind that analysis cannot replace: the recognition of another's suffering as real, prior to any account of its causes, and whole movements have begun in that recognition, carried by readers whose tears were the first form their solidarity took. The tradition's own history honours the fact, since its founding documents are themselves works of heated feeling disciplined by enquiry, and its greatest novels of industry and empire taught publics to feel what statistics could not make them feel. The candidate's verdict, as ever, should be allocative: this work's sentiment instructs because it attaches feeling to mechanism, that work's sentiment sedates because it attaches feeling to resignation, and the difference, demonstrated scene by scene, is a finding about the works rather than a prejudice about the mode. With that allocation the doctrinal sections end, their instruments complete: presupposition and production, silence and settlement, the named repertoire and the rationed concepts, the corrections, the comparisons, and over them all the habit of the stated defeater, ready to be carried into the hall.

## The dedication, the preface, and the threshold of the book

A book begins before its first chapter, and the threshold materials, dedication, preface, epigraph, and contents page, form a small archive that candidates almost never use and examiners almost always respect, because the threshold is where a work declares its alliances and its intended public in its own words. A dedication names a patron, a friend, a class of readers, a dead comrade, and the naming is a social act performed in print, a public placing of the work within a network of obligation and hope. A preface argues with reviewers not yet read, instructs the reader in the desired approach, apologises for the subject's difficulty or the author's presumption, and in doing so reveals what the work fears and what it expects its world to think of it. Candidates can spend one paragraph on a threshold and gain the kind of evidence that feels discovered rather than assigned, because it is discovered, sitting in the edition's front matter where the course of study rarely lingers.

Epigraphs deserve the closest threshold attention, since they are chosen with a care disproportionate to their size. A quotation placed at a work's gate is a claim about kinship: this book belongs in the company of that scripture, that economist, that poet, that folk saying, and the claim can be honoured, ironised, or quietly contradicted by everything that follows. Candidates should ask what authority the epigraph borrows and what the borrowing costs, whether the work lives up to its gate or spends its length retreating from it, and whose voices are admitted to the threshold of a literature and whose are quoted only inside, as specimens. The contents page, humblest of thresholds, has its own testimony: chapter titles that editorialise, part divisions that impose a philosophy of history on the material, the decision to name a section for a place, a person, or a sum of money. Each is a small presupposition, filed where only the methodical reader looks.

### Why should a candidate spend precious minutes on a book's front matter?

Because the threshold is the work's own account of its alliances, fears, and intended readers, stated before the fiction's protections descend. A dedication can date a patronage, a preface can confess a market anxiety, an epigraph can declare a kinship the text then betrays, and each confession is evidence of a kind the body of the work, guarded and arranged, rarely offers so directly.

Editions extend the threshold across time, and a reflective sentence about the candidate's own edition belongs to the same family as the earlier reflexivity about the syllabus. Introductions frame, notes explain or decline to explain, covers promise a genre, and the school edition's apparatus instructs its young readers in what kind of object they hold, often with a confidence the work itself would not survive. The candidate need not indict the edition that carried the text to the hall. The candidate need only show, once, an awareness that texts arrive mediated, since that awareness is the production doctrine applied to the object in the candidate's own hands, and there is no more fitting place for the doctrine to end its tour of the work than at the door through which every reader, examiner included, originally walked in.

## A closing discipline: the reading that states its own counter-evidence

Everything taught in this guide converges on a habit small enough to fit in one sentence and large enough to organise a candidate's whole practice. Judge, and state what would count against the judgement. The habit began in this series as a standard for verdicts about history and doctrine, and it ends, in this cluster, as a standard for verdicts about poems and plays, because the intellectual virtue is the same in both theatres: a claim that knows its own conditions is a claim that has been thought rather than inherited. Candidates who carry the habit out of the hall carry the best thing the study of criticism can give a person, which is not a set of conclusions about literature but a way of holding conclusions about anything.

The habit also completes the answer to the anxious question with which many candidates begin. They ask whether the examination is a place where a method they may doubt must be performed as faith. The guide's answer has been consistent: the examination is a place where a method must be performed as method, accurately, evidently, and under judgement, and where the judgement is free. A candidate may conclude that the form move illuminates everything it touches, or that it illuminates a province and distorts the rest, or that its century of service has exhausted it. Each conclusion, reached by the route mapped here and carrying its defeater on its face, will be graded as what it is: the reasoning of a critic. That is the whole promise of the topic, and it is enough.

## For students and exam candidates

Your task is smaller than it feels, and naming its true size is the first service this section can do you. You are not required to master a century of theory. You are required to perform a handful of operations on a text you know, in an order a marker can follow, within an hour you can budget. The operations are these: define the approach by its object in your opening; lead with one concept attached to one named critic; apply the form move, asking what the work's arrangements presuppose rather than what its plot depicts; anchor every reference to the feature that carries your claim; place one point about production or reception where it connects; handle the author's politics with the correction and its Balzac instance where the question invites it; admit the strongest resistance your reading meets; and close with a verdict that states what would count against it. Practise those operations as operations, in timed paragraphs, until the sequence feels like a route you walk rather than a list you recall.

Build your kit to the ration this guide prescribes and refuse the warehouse. Six critics with a verb each. Six concepts with a triad each: definition, application to your set text, limitation. The literature answer matrix copied onto cards, one row per card, rehearsed until each row can be completed from memory in prose. The set pieces polished: the triumph of realism paragraph, the paired sentences that keep cultural materialism distinct from new historicism, the closing template of verdict and defeater. The companion resources exist to stock and organise exactly this kit. The [Marxism revision guide](/2022/01/15/marxism-revision-guide/) sequences the work across your revision weeks. For previous year questions to practise against, the UPSC previous year question explorer at [ReportMedic](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html) lets candidates in the civil service stream work from actual papers rather than invented ones, testing the matrix against questions examiners really set. For the kit itself, the Marxist study notes tool at [VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) turns the answer matrix and the concept triads into revision cards you can drill in short sessions, which is the format in which cards outperform chapters.

Two protections deserve repetition because anxiety erodes them first. No marks are hazarded by attacking the approach and none by upholding it, since what is graded is the quality of the reasoning displayed, and a verdict against Marxist criticism, properly evidenced, reaches the identical ceiling as a verdict in its favour. And the question's verb governs your duty: where a question demands application, apply, and where it demands evaluation, evaluate, spending twenty seconds on the distinction before you plan. Guard those two protections and the rest is technique, and technique, unlike inspiration, can be trained on a schedule. Walk in with a rationed kit, a rehearsed route, and a rested mind, and the paper becomes a place where you demonstrate a method you own. That demonstration, neither larger nor smaller, is what the marks were always for.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The most directly usable material in this guide is the side by side demonstration: one passage, described generically, treated first as content and then as form, with the difference named rather than merely displayed. Students grasp the form move in minutes from that contrast after failing to grasp it from a term of definitions, because the contrast lets them feel the two objects of study diverge. Build lessons around the divergence. Take any scene from the set text, draft the content paragraph collectively, and let the class discover its inertness by asking what in it could not have been written from a summary. Then rebuild the paragraph around focalisation, interiority, and settlement, and let the class watch the same material become evidence. The exercise teaches the topic's central distinction as an experience, and experiences survive into examination halls where definitions evaporate.

Assessment literacy deserves explicit teaching alongside the method. Students should hear, from a person they trust, that markers grade reasoning rather than conclusions, that criticising the approach is safe where evaluation is invited, and that the defeater sentence is a strength rather than a hedge. Each of those assurances contradicts a fear students carry silently, and silent fears deform scripts more reliably than ignorance does. Mark practice work against the trap column of the matrix, naming the specific trap each script fell into, since a named trap becomes a checklist item while a general exhortation to analyse more deeply becomes nothing at all. Finally, teach the corrections as set pieces worth memorising in substance: the triumph of realism, the separation of neighbouring approaches, the verb attached to each critic. Students who own the set pieces spend their examination attention on the text, which is where you have always wanted it spent.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

The research interest of this topic lies in the gap between a method's scholarly life and its examination afterlife. The repertoire examined here survives in syllabuses because it is teachable, and its teachability has edited it: concepts that resist the triad of definition, application, and limitation fade from the canon of the classroom whatever their standing in the journals, while portable instruments, the form move, the symptomatic silence, the triumph of realism, harden into the tradition's public shape. Scholars tracing the reception of Marxist criticism will find in examination culture a selective pressure as real as any in publishing, shaping which critics remain names and which become footnotes. The matrix and its kindred tables across this series are therefore primary documents of a sort, recording what a century of argument looks like after it has been rationed for the hall, and the rationing itself, what it keeps, what it drops, what it forbids, is a chapter in the intellectual history of the method that remains largely unwritten.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How do you write a Marxist criticism essay?

Begin by saying what the approach studies: a work's ties to the material ordering of the society that produced it. Pick one governing idea, tie it to the thinker who forged it, and put the form move to work on evidence a reader can locate. Weave in a single point about how the work was made or received, deal with the writer's allegiances through the correction where the question raises them, grant the opposing case a fair hearing, and finish with a judgement that names the discovery which would overturn it.
### Q: How do you avoid writing about content in a Marxist reading?

Refuse paraphrase as your paragraph's engine. Interrogate the telling instead of the tale: the vantage the narration occupies, the minds it enters, the knowledge it rations, the endings it makes imaginable. Probe for the assumption a chosen shape cannot do without, then hunt for the passage where the work chafes against that assumption. Demote events to supporting clauses inside claims about effects, and reread your practice pages marking whether each sentence reports a happening or establishes what a device accomplishes.
### Q: Which critics should you name in a literature essay?

Let the paragraph choose the name. Where totality and the realist inheritance are at stake, Lukács is the authority; where consent is being organised, Gramsci; where ideology hails its subjects, Althusser; where copies dissolve a work's aura, Benjamin; where a narrative swerves around the unsayable, Macherey; where a culture's feelings are changing ahead of its institutions, Williams; where a framework must be assembled quickly and fairly, Eagleton. A solitary name carrying an argument outweighs a procession of labels carrying none, because the marker can verify the first and only registers the second.
### Q: How do you handle the author's own politics in an essay?

Give the biography a sentence, not the verdict. A writer's allegiance is a fact about the person, while the work is a construction where observation, inherited shape, and the pressures of a market contend, and the contention can carry the artefact well beyond the maker's declared position. Engels made the point through Balzac, the legitimist whose fidelity to the society he watched overcame the politics he professed. Where a work shows more than its author believed, say so; where its machinery betrays its author's convictions, say that; and reserve your real curiosity for the writer's circumstances, the patrons, purchasers, and censors who formed the conditions of the writing.
### Q: What are the common mistakes in Marxist criticism essays?

Examiners meet the same failures every season. Scripts retell stories and call the retelling analysis. Scripts read a writer's opinions out of a text or a text out of a writer's opinions. Fictional people are graded for virtue while the constructions that produced them go unexamined. Thinkers are gestured at in crowds and named nowhere. Terms are scattered without a single located piece of evidence. Openings drown in period background that never touches the set work. Culture is treated as a photograph of the economy, neighbouring approaches are blurred into one another, the question's instruction is quietly swapped for another, and verdicts arrive with no indication of the evidence that would budge them.
### Q: How do you apply Marxist criticism to poetry?

Start where verse is densest: its arrangements. Consider the voice that speaks and the sort of self its speaking takes for granted, the hearer a monologue silently ranks, the community a ballad presumes will carry it. Follow the measure and ask what its steadiness performs or its breaking admits, follow the rhymes and note which words the poem chooses to wed, and inventory the pictures for the work and wealth they draw upon. Set the piece among its circumstances of patronage, printing, or performance, and weigh its close by comparing the resolution performed with the resolution its trouble genuinely required.
### Q: Which concepts work best in an exam?

Judge an idea by whether it hands you a question you can aim at unfamiliar material within moments. Naturalised assumption asks what a passage expects its reader to accept without argument. Hegemony asks whose version of common sense is being repeated and through which institution the repetition runs. Reification asks where objects and sums perform the actions of persons. The symptomatic gap asks what a narrative nears and then avoids. Structures of feeling ask which alteration in lived experience a work registers ahead of any vocabulary for it. Hold each idea in three sentences: what it means, where it bites on your set text, and where it stops working.
### Q: How do you compare Marxist with another critical approach?

Start from purposes rather than preferences. One approach studies how language estranges perception, another how a work sits inside the material ordering of its world, another how gender or empire organises its meanings. Declare each approach's chosen territory, the sort of proof it honours, and the region it is poorly built to inspect, then share the set passage between them accordingly, crediting each with the ground it genuinely illuminates. Let the verdict apportion rather than crown, concede the stretch where the rival instrument reaches further, and add the observation that would make you reapportion.
### Q: Is it safe to criticise Marxist criticism in an essay?

Nobody is penalised for a direction. Where a question makes usefulness its subject, the grade follows the quality of the weighing: objections pressed at the level of ideas, proof, and blind spots, authorities cited on more than one side, and a stated condition under which the verdict would move. A hostile conclusion built that way reaches the same ceiling as a friendly one. The genuine danger sits elsewhere, in questions that order you to use the approach on a text; there, pages of doubt about the instrument simply decline the assigned task, and markers treat the refusal accordingly.
### Q: How do you revise literary theory efficiently?

Work by making things, not by rereading them. For every governing idea, compose three sentences unaided: a definition, a use on your set text, and a boundary where the idea fails. Fill in each row of the answer matrix without glancing at notes, and treat any hesitation as a map reference pointing at the passage of guidance that repairs it. Rehearse the fixed assets against a clock, the Balzac paragraph, the separation of neighbouring approaches, the verdict that carries its own condition. Mark finished practice answers against the trap column, fix the column that leaked, and let the final days protect sleep as deliberately as knowledge.
### Q: How does the form move order a literature answer?

It gives the essay a skeleton before sentences begin. The introduction frames the approach and picks the governing idea, the opening movement puts the form move on the firmest evidence while expectations are still being formed, the middle admits making, circumstance, and biography at the joints where they belong, the movement before the close confronts the sternest objection the reading faces, and the close delivers judgement with its reversal condition attached. A route settled beforehand returns the writer's attention to the sole task no rehearsal can perform, which is seeing what sets this particular text apart.
### Q: What should a student separate between writing about a plot and analysing a form?

Keep two objects apart in every sentence you write: the happening, and the machinery that delivers the happening to a reader. Happenings belong in subordinate positions, cited as proof. Machinery belongs in the main clause, where claims live: the vantage adopted, the minds funded with interior life, the knowledge rationed, the settlement arranged. Test a page by asking of each sentence whether it moves the story forward or establishes an effect. Pages where happenings dominate are summaries however theoretical their vocabulary sounds, and the repair is grammatical before it is anything else: demote the event, promote the effect.
### Q: Why does a reading that states its own counter-evidence score better?

Markers are grading a process they can only infer from the page, and a named condition is the clearest evidence that weighing actually happened. Anyone can announce a conclusion; a conclusion accompanied by the specific scene, document, or production fact that would force its withdrawal shows a mind that knows what its own claim predicts about the work. Vague humility earns nothing, since it risks nothing. A precise defeater risks the whole verdict in one sentence, and the risk is exactly what persuades a professional reader to trust the sentences that surround it, extending credit backward across the entire script.
### Q: How does the literature answer matrix order a timed answer?

It settles, for each recurring species of question, the choices that otherwise consume the opening minutes. One column fixes the governing idea, another fastens that idea to its thinker, another prescribes the evaluative gesture the species rewards, whether resistance confessed, territory shared, or a condition declared, and the last column warns against the failure that species habitually provokes. Planning becomes selection from a rehearsed grid instead of invention under pressure. During revision the grid turns diagnostic: wherever a candidate stalls while completing a row unaided, a named weakness has been located, together with the guidance that addresses it.
### Q: What separates demonstrating a method from summarising a story?

Recall can be counterfeited by anyone who has absorbed a plot, so it separates nobody. Demonstration leaves fingerprints a marker can check: an assumption identified and then tested against the work, an idea fastened to a located feature, an evasion argued from a visible swerve, a settlement priced against the resolution the trouble actually demanded. Try the subtraction test on your own drafting. Delete each sentence that a plot summary could have supplied and inspect the remainder. Where a method was truly at work, an argument survives the deletion standing. Where nothing survives, the page was summary in costume, and you have found the work still waiting to be done.
