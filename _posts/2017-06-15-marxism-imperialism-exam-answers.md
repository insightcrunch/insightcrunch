Imperialism is examined in more rooms than almost any other topic in this series, and it is marked differently in each of them. A politics examiner, a history examiner, a development studies examiner, and an international relations examiner can all set a question containing the same word while rewarding four distinguishable kinds of answer. What every one of those markers rewards, across all four disciplines, is a candidate who separates the senses of the term before arguing, who can name more than one Marxist theory and state how the theories differ, and who handles inconvenient evidence without flinching. What every one of them penalises is the answer that recites a single famous pamphlet as though it were the whole tradition, the answer that decorates assertions with unsourced figures, and the answer that defends or denounces Marx's journalism on India instead of reporting what it said, what was wrong with it, and how its author later changed his mind.

![Marxism and imperialism exam guide, the imperialism answer matrix and the stage and empire opening - Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-93.webp)

This guide is built around those rewards and penalties. It assumes the content is available elsewhere and links to it rather than repeating it: the full treatment of the theories lives in the cluster's content guide, and the difficult primary material has an article of its own. The task here is narrower and, for a candidate, more valuable. It is to convert knowledge of a contested literature into marks, under time pressure, in whichever examination room the question happens to be asked. That conversion has a technique, and the technique can be taught. Its core is a single opening move, named in this guide as the stage and empire opening, supported by a planning instrument called the imperialism answer matrix, and completed by a small set of disciplines: name rivals rather than recite a doctrine, evaluate against a named critic, grade the classical predictions against the record, and refuse every figure whose provenance cannot be defended in the room.

## Imperialism across four examination rooms

The first thing a candidate should grasp is that the topic's portability is the source of its danger. Because imperialism appears in politics, history, international relations, and development papers, revision material tends to be written as though one answer could serve all four. It cannot. A history paper on empire and decolonisation expects the candidate to work with chronology and evidence, weighing what happened against what the theories predicted. A politics paper, where Marxism appears as a named theoretical perspective, expects the candidate to reconstruct an argument accurately and then evaluate it, often against a liberal or realist rival. An international relations paper treats the Marxist accounts as one family within a theory survey and rewards precise placement: what distinguishes this family's explanation of expansion from the explanations offered by realism or liberalism. A development paper examines imperialism alongside dependency, and expects the candidate to know where the classical theory ends and the dependency literature begins, since examiners in that field regard confusing the two as a diagnostic error.

Civil service examinations and national eligibility tests add a fifth room with its own conventions. There the topic appears in political science optionals and in tests of teaching eligibility, where imperialism and neo-colonialism are named syllabus territory and where a structured, even-handed, textbook-secure answer outperforms a brilliant but wayward one. The conventions differ in detail, yet the underlying reward is constant: the candidate who defines before arguing, compares rather than recites, and evaluates with names attached collects marks in every room. The candidate who writes the same memorised essay in all five collects marks in perhaps one.

This is why the guide refuses to offer a model essay. A model essay can be memorised, and memorised essays are exactly what markers are trained to discount, because a memorised essay answers the question the candidate hoped for rather than the question on the paper. What transfers between rooms is not an essay but a method: an opening distinction, a roster of theories with their mechanisms, a scorecard of predictions, a protocol for the hard primary material, and a matrix for assembling those parts under the command word actually set. Learn the method and the same preparation serves the history candidate weighing decolonisation, the politics candidate evaluating a perspective, and the development candidate separating dependency from its parent theory.

A word about what this guide will not do. It will not state any syllabus code, paper number, or mark allocation, because those change and because an answer's quality does not depend on them. It will not use any contemporary state or conflict as an example, because an examination answer on a classical theory gains nothing from journalism and risks a great deal. And it will not supply figures for colonial mortality, famine, or extraction, for reasons set out in the protocol below: contested numbers quoted without provenance are penalised by historians and suspected by everyone else. What it supplies instead is the architecture of a strong answer, which is the part of performance that revision guides usually leave to luck.

## The stage and empire opening

Every strong answer on this topic begins by performing a separation that weaker answers never perform. The word imperialism carries at least two senses that examination questions habitually blur. In one sense it names a stage: a phase in the development of capitalism, reached when competition gives way to monopoly, banks fuse with industrial firms, and the export of capital becomes as significant as the export of goods. In the other sense it names empire: the territorial possession and direct rule of foreign peoples, with governors, garrisons, and flags. The two senses overlap in history, since the stage coincided with the great age of annexation, but they are not the same claim, and they do not stand or fall together. An answer that announces, in its opening sentences, which sense it is addressing and how the senses relate has already done analytical work that a descriptive answer never catches up with. That announcement is the stage and empire opening, and this guide treats it as the highest-return move available on the topic.

The reason the move earns marks is that it pre-empts the commonest evaluative objection in the field. Critics of the Marxist accounts regularly observe that empires existed for millennia before capitalism: Rome ruled provinces, Spain drained bullion from the Americas, and chartered companies governed trading stations with private armies long before an industrial economy existed anywhere. Deployed against an answer that has not separated the senses, the objection looks fatal, because the answer seems to claim that capitalism invented conquest. Deployed against an answer that opened with the distinction, the objection dissolves into the answer's own framework. The Marxist theories, in the stage sense, never claimed that capitalism invented territorial expansion. They claimed that a particular kind of expansion, driven by monopoly, finance, and the search for outlets for accumulated capital, characterised a particular phase of one economic system. Rome is a counterexample to a claim nobody in the tradition needed to make, and a candidate who shows this in the first paragraph has converted the examiner's favourite objection into evidence of the candidate's precision.

### Why does the opening distinction matter more than the definition you memorise?

A memorised definition lists features and stops; the opening distinction does work, because it tells the marker which object the answer will evaluate, forestalls the objection that conquest predates capitalism, and gives every later paragraph a classificatory home. Markers reward the distinction because it converts description into analysis within the first five lines.

The point deserves underlining, because candidates habitually invest their revision in the wrong asset. A flawless recitation of Lenin's five features, memorised to the comma, earns exposition marks and nothing more, and it earns them at a cost: the recitation consumes the first page of the answer, leaving the evaluation, where the higher bands live, compressed into a hurried final paragraph. The opening distinction costs three sentences. Written well, it also organises everything that follows, because each theory can then be introduced by locating it against the distinction: this writer theorised the stage, that writer described the empire, a third denied that the stage required the empire at all. A candidate who has grasped the distinction possesses a filing system for the whole literature. A candidate who has merely memorised a definition possesses a paragraph.

There is a second, quieter benefit. Questions on this topic are often worded loosely, because examiners know the word is loose and are testing whether candidates notice. A question asking whether imperialism ended with decolonisation means one thing in the empire sense and quite another in the stage sense, and the strong answer says so, answers both readings briefly, and shows that the apparent disagreement in the literature is partly a disagreement about which question was being asked. That performance, the exposure of a verbal ambiguity doing real argumentative work, is precisely what top-band descriptors in every discipline describe, whether the vocabulary is analysis, evaluation, or critical engagement. The stage and empire opening is therefore not a trick of presentation. It is the topic's central intellectual problem, stated at the point where it can govern the answer.

Worked openings make the technique concrete. For an evaluate question in a politics paper, the candidate might write that the theory under examination treats imperialism as a stage of capitalism rather than a policy of territorial acquisition, that the distinction matters because the theory's predictions concern the behaviour of capital rather than the conduct of governors, and that the evaluation will therefore test those predictions rather than the morality of empire. For a history question on decolonisation, the candidate might write that whether the transfer of power refuted the theory depends on which claim is being tested, since the ending of formal empire falsifies one claim about the system's needs while leaving the structural claims untouched. For a development question, the candidate might open by separating the classical stage theory from the later literature on dependence, noting that the two are cousins rather than synonyms and that the answer will keep them apart. Each opening is short, each is different, and each announces a mind sorting material rather than discharging it.

Notice, too, what the opening permits later in the answer. Once the senses are separated, the candidate can concede gracefully. Concession is the most underused mark-earning behaviour in examination writing on contested topics: the candidate who concedes that the territorial prediction failed, or that capital flowed mainly between wealthy economies, loses nothing, because the concession is located inside a framework that specifies which claim has been damaged. The candidate who never separated the senses cannot concede anything safely, since every concession looks like a surrender of the whole position. Markers trust conceders. An answer that grades its own subject's claims, sparing some and sacrificing others, reads as judgement; an answer that defends everything reads as advocacy, and advocacy is marked as such in every discipline this topic touches.

## The theories a candidate must be able to name

The single most expensive error on this topic is presenting Lenin's account as the Marxist theory of imperialism. It is inaccurate, because the tradition contains several rival accounts that disagree with one another about mechanism, and it is expensive, because the marks for comparison, which are built into every evaluate and discuss command, cannot be earned by an answer with only one theory in it. The roster matters more than the depth of any single entry on it. Content summaries live in the cluster's guide to [marxism and imperialism](/2017/02/01/marxism-and-imperialism-guide/) and in the dedicated study of [Lenin's pamphlet and its argument](/2014/04/15/lenin-imperialism-readers-guide/); what follows is the examination version, each theory reduced to the mechanism a candidate must be able to state in two sentences and the difference that makes it a separate theory rather than a footnote.

### Which theories of imperialism must you name in an exam?

name at least two beyond Lenin. The underconsumptionist account of 1902, the finance capital analysis of 1910, and the accumulation theory of 1913 all precede him. Naming more than one demonstrates that Marxism contains rival theories rather than a single doctrine.

Begin with the writer who is not a Marxist at all. John Atkinson Hobson, an English liberal economist, published his study of imperialism in 1902 and located the driving force in underconsumption: income distributed so unequally that the wealthy could not spend what they received and workers could not buy what they produced, leaving surplus capital seeking investment abroad and a small set of financial and commercial interests capturing foreign policy to protect those investments. Hobson's remedy was redistribution at home, which would dry up the surplus and remove the pressure. The examination significance of Hobson is threefold. He is chronologically first among the modern theorists. He supplies the liberal comparison that compare questions demand. And his presence at the head of the roster disproves, in one name, the lazy assumption that theorising imperialism was a Marxist monopoly.

Rudolf Hilferding, an Austrian socialist, published his analysis of finance capital in 1910 and shifted the mechanism. For Hilferding, the decisive development was the fusion of bank capital and industrial capital into a single bloc, protected by tariffs, hungry for exclusive territory in which its investments would be safe from rivals, and served by a state whose foreign policy expressed its needs. Where Hobson's surplus was a distributional accident that reform could cure, Hilferding's finance capital was a structural stage that reform could only manage. The difference between those two mechanisms, accident versus stage, is exactly the kind of distinction an examiner is listening for, and it costs a candidate one sentence to display it.

Rosa Luxemburg's account of accumulation, published in 1913, supplied a third mechanism and the most uncompromising logic of the three. Capitalism, on her argument, cannot realise its surplus value within a closed capitalist system; it requires non-capitalist surroundings, peasants, artisans, and colonial territories, into which to sell, from which to draw materials and labour, and against which to expand by loan, by trade, and where necessary by force. Imperialism is therefore not a policy chosen by governments seduced by financiers but the political expression of an economic necessity, and it ends only when the non-capitalist reservoir is exhausted, at which point the system confronts its limit. Luxemburg's mechanism is distinct from both predecessors: not maldistribution, not bank fusion, but a realisation problem written into accumulation itself.

Nikolai Bukharin, writing during the First World War, systematised the stage argument by pairing two tendencies: capital organising itself nationally into state-backed blocs, and the world economy binding those blocs together, so that competition between capitals was displaced upward into armed competition between states. Vladimir Lenin, drawing on Hobson, Hilferding, and Bukharin, produced in 1916 the account that became canonical: imperialism as the monopoly stage of capitalism, summarised in five features covering monopoly, finance capital, capital export, the division of the world among combines, and the completed territorial partition of the globe among the great powers. Lenin's version is the best known because it was the most teachable and because its author's party won a revolution the following year, and candidates should know both reasons, since the canonisation of one theory among rivals is itself an examinable fact about how traditions form. Karl Kautsky completes the roster with the dissenting possibility the others rejected: ultra-imperialism, a phase in which the great capitalist powers might cartellise the world jointly and exploit it in concert rather than fight over its division. Kautsky's conjecture was denounced by Lenin as a reactionary fantasy, yet its partial resemblance to the cooperation of the advanced economies after the Second World War makes it the roster's most interesting evaluative asset, and a candidate who can deploy it shows an examiner that the tradition's internal quarrels track real possibilities.

Six names, six mechanisms, one shared conviction: that expansion is structural rather than incidental. A candidate who can state each mechanism in two sentences and name one disagreement between any two holders of them is equipped for every theory question this topic generates. A candidate who can recite only the five features is equipped for one question, and it is rarely the question set.

## The imperialism answer matrix

Revision produces notes; examinations reward retrieval under pressure, and the two are not the same. The imperialism answer matrix is this guide's instrument for converting notes into retrieval. It is a table whose rows are the question types that recur on this topic and whose columns force the decisions a candidate otherwise has to improvise in the room: which discipline is asking, which theories the answer must name, which evaluation move lifts the answer into the higher band, and which trap the question has laid for the unprepared. A candidate who has built the matrix during revision, and rehearsed reading a row across in ninety seconds, walks into the examination with a plan for each likely question rather than a hope that the revised essay happens to fit.

### What does the answer matrix do that a set of notes cannot?

Notes store content in the order it was learned; the matrix stores decisions in the order they must be taken, pairing each recurring question type with the theories it requires, the evaluation that earns credit, and the error it is designed to catch. Under time pressure, retrieval follows the structure in which material was rehearsed.

| Question type | Discipline that asks it | Theories to name | Evaluation that lifts the band | The trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explain the Marxist theory of imperialism | Politics, international relations | Hobson, Hilferding, Lenin | Distinguish the mechanisms rather than listing features, and note the tradition disagrees with itself | Presenting one pamphlet as the whole theory |
| Evaluate the Marxist theory of imperialism | Politics, international relations, eligibility tests | Lenin and Luxemburg against Schumpeter and the liberal account | Grade the predictions separately: some failed, some held | Defending every claim or dismissing every claim |
| Compare Marxist and liberal accounts | Politics, international relations | Hobson against Hilferding and Lenin | Locate the disagreement in mechanism: maldistribution versus structural stage | Describing both sides without identifying where they differ |
| Assess Marx's writings on colonialism | History, politics, civil service optionals | Marx against his later self, with the critics named | State the early position, its factual problems, and the later change, in that order | Answering with a defence or a denunciation |
| Discuss whether decolonisation refuted the theory | History, development studies | Lenin and Nkrumah against the liberal reading | Specify which claim the evidence tests before judging it | Treating the end of formal empire as a verdict on every claim |

The matrix should be read as a discipline, not as a script. Its first column teaches the candidate to classify the question before answering it, which is the examination equivalent of the stage and empire opening: both are acts of separation performed before argument begins. Its second column is a reminder that the same words conceal different expectations, so that a candidate sitting a history paper reaches for chronology and evidence while a candidate in a politics paper reaches for reconstruction and evaluation. Its third column enforces plurality, the roster discipline of the previous section. Its fourth column is the most valuable, because it states in advance the move that distinguishes the higher band, sparing the candidate the commonest examination experience on this topic: knowing the content and still writing a middling answer for want of one evaluative step. Its final column names the trap, and traps on this topic are few and recurrent, which is why they can be catalogued at all.

Building the matrix is also a revision method. Whoever can fill a row unaided knows that question; a candidate who stalls on a cell has located a precise gap, the evaluation for the comparison question, say, or the trap in the decolonisation question, and can repair that gap rather than rereading everything. Filled in by hand, argued over with a teacher, and redrawn from memory in the final week, the matrix compresses the topic into a single page that can be reviewed in the last hour before the paper. One effective destination for it is a set of revision cards: the matrix's cells, transferred to cards with the question type on one side and the theories, evaluation, and trap on the other, turn the table into a self-testing instrument. The persona section below describes how the two companion tools named in this guide support exactly that transfer.

A caution belongs here, because instruments can be misused. The matrix orders an answer; it does not write one. Candidates who treat the fourth column as a formula, inserting a token evaluation sentence at a fixed point regardless of the question's wording, produce the mechanical performance markers describe as scaffolded, and scaffolding shows. The matrix's purpose is to free attention during the examination, attention the candidate can then spend on the actual wording of the actual question, which always contains some specification (a period, a region, a named writer, a command word with teeth) that no table can anticipate. Plan with the matrix, then answer the question on the paper. That sequence, instrument first and obedience to the wording second, is the whole method in miniature.

## Writing the explain answer

The explain question is the topic's bread and butter, and it is lost in a characteristic way: the candidate explains one theory, usually Lenin's, at generous length, and discovers too late that the marks were distributed across the tradition. An explain answer on imperialism should be built as a guided tour of mechanisms. Open with the stage and empire distinction, so the explanation has a frame. Then present the theories in chronological order, which has the incidental virtue of showing development: a liberal diagnosis of maldistribution, a socialist analysis of finance, an accumulation argument about the system's need for an outside, a wartime systematisation, and the canonical summary that absorbed its predecessors. For each theory, the candidate needs the holder's name, the date of the work, the mechanism in two sentences, and one sentence locating it against its neighbours: what it kept from the previous account, what it replaced, what it could not explain.

### How should an explain answer handle the five features?

Treat the famous list as one theory's summary rather than the topic's contents page: state the features compactly, credit them to their author and moment, then spend the saved space on the rival mechanisms the list absorbed. Examiners reward the candidate who can place the catalogue inside the tradition that produced it.

Two disciplines govern the explain answer's quality. The first is the separation of naming from summarising, a distinction candidates chronically blur. To name a theory is to identify its holder, its work, and its mechanism with enough precision that the marker knows the candidate possesses it. To summarise a theory is to reproduce its argument at length. Naming is cheap and earns credit quickly; summarising is expensive and earns credit slowly, and examination writing on a survey topic should be weighted heavily toward naming with one or two theories summarised where the question's wording invites depth. The second discipline is located reference: at least one claim in the answer should be tied to a specific text, a specific controversy, or a specific episode, since an explanation floating free of every particular reads as textbook vapour. The controversy between Lenin and Kautsky over whether the powers could combine, conducted in print while the powers were at war, is the roster's most serviceable located reference, because it shows the tradition arguing about its own future in real time.

Explain answers also benefit from a closing movement that candidates often omit: a sentence or two on what the family of theories shares despite its quarrels. The shared conviction, that expansion arises from the structure of the economic system rather than from the wickedness or wisdom of individual statesmen, is the family's signature, and stating it demonstrates that the candidate has understood the survey as a survey. Without that sentence, an explain answer is a list of pamphlets. With it, the answer is an account of a research programme, which is what the question, in every discipline, is really requesting.

## Writing the evaluate answer: the scorecard of predictions

Evaluation is where this topic's marks concentrate, and evaluation has a correct object. The classical theories were not only interpretations; they were predictions, and a century and more of record now stands against which the predictions can be graded. The strong evaluate answer therefore takes the form of a scorecard: the candidate extracts the claims that history could test, tests them one at a time, and delivers a separate verdict on each before offering any general conclusion. This structure is the candidate's protection against the two symmetric failures of evaluation on contested topics, blanket defence and blanket dismissal, both of which markers read as the absence of judgement rather than its presence.

### Which classical predictions failed, and how should a candidate write about failure?

Name the failures precisely and without protective vagueness: the expectation that rivalry among the great capitalist powers must issue in repeated general war, and the picture of capital export flowing chiefly toward colonies. Then show what each failure damages and what it leaves standing, since a graded failure is analysis and an ungraded one is surrender.

The failures first, stated at full strength, because a scorecard that begins with its subject's successes reads as advocacy. The classical literature, Lenin and Bukharin above all, expected rivalry among the advanced capitalist states over the division and redivision of the world to generate recurring general war, as it had in 1914. Since 1945 the advanced capitalist powers have not fought one another at all, a peace among them longer than any the theory's framework led its holders to expect, and the institutional cooperation of those powers through alliances, trade regimes, and joint financial arrangements resembles Kautsky's derided conjecture more than Lenin's forecast. Second, the geography of capital export disappointed the classical picture: the great bulk of foreign investment, in the classical period and since, has flowed among already developed economies rather than from metropole to colony, a finding pressed by the historian D. K. Fieldhouse in his revision of the economic theories and fatal to any simple version of the claim that colonies existed because capital needed them as outlets. Third, the system survived the loss of its colonies. Decolonisation, treated in detail below, removed the formal empires within a generation, and metropolitan capitalism did not collapse, stagnate, or even visibly falter in consequence, which tells against the strongest version of the claim that empire was a necessity rather than an opportunity.

The survivals deserve the same precision. The concentration of capital into giant firms and financial blocs, which the stage theorists treated as the signature of the epoch, has proceeded further than any of them imagined, and the fusion of financial and industrial power that Hilferding described is a commonplace description of the modern corporation rather than a controversial one. The insistence that states actively construct and defend the conditions for accumulation abroad, through law, diplomacy, infrastructure, and at times force, remains a live analytical commitment across several schools, including schools with no Marxist allegiance. And the relational claim, that value moves along chains of production and exchange in patterns that systematically advantage the wealthy end, survived translation into the dependency literature and into the study of global supply chains, where the questions the classical writers posed about who captures value are asked with better data. A scorecard that records three failures and three survivals, each specified, supports a conclusion with genuine content: the theory was wrong about the political destiny of the system it described and substantially right about several of its economic mechanisms, and its claims must therefore be retired individually rather than as a set.

### How does naming a critic raise an evaluation from comment to argument?

A comment says the theory has weaknesses; an argument says which weakness, identified by whom, on what evidence. Attaching the capital export objection to Fieldhouse, or the atavism thesis to Schumpeter, converts an opinion the marker must take on trust into a position the marker can credit, because it demonstrates acquaintance with the critical literature rather than with a textbook's summary of it.

The critics should be known as individuals, not as a fog. Schumpeter, writing in the aftermath of the First World War, argued that imperialism was an atavism, the survival into a rationalising capitalist order of the war machine and expansionist habits of pre-capitalist aristocracies, so that capitalism properly understood tended toward peace and the phenomenon was a residue rather than a product. The argument is the strongest liberal-adjacent rival because it accepts the phenomenon and reverses its causation. Fieldhouse attacked from the archive, arguing that the economic theories explained neither the timing nor the geography of the scramble for colonies, and that the motives recoverable from the documents were strategic and political as often as financial. Tony Brewer's critical survey of the Marxist theories remains the standard map of the internal disagreements and of where each theory's logic breaks, and a candidate who has absorbed Brewer's distinctions can evaluate with a scalpel where others swing a club. Norman Angell's pre-war argument that conquest had ceased to pay, whatever its predictive embarrassments, states the liberal puzzle cleanly: if empire was economically irrational for the nations that held it, an economic theory of empire owes an account of the irrationality, and Hobson's distributional answer, irrational for the nation and profitable for a narrow set of interests within it, is the tradition's most elegant reply. Evaluation conducted at this level, named critics with named arguments and named replies, is what the higher bands describe.

One further discipline completes the evaluate answer: the candidate should state, at least once, what evidence would change the judgement being offered. The habit sounds modest and is in fact the strongest signal of intellectual command a timed answer can send, because it shows the candidate holding the theory as a set of testable claims rather than as a creed or a target. If the advanced economies returned to armed rivalry over territory, the Leninist forecast would revive; if value flows along production chains were shown to advantage the poor end systematically, the relational claim would fall. A judgement accompanied by its own revision conditions is a judgement a marker can trust, and trust, in the marking of contested topics, is the currency in which top bands are paid.

## Comparing Marxist and liberal accounts

Comparison questions fail in a predictable shape: two summaries placed side by side, first the Marxist account, then the liberal account, with a concluding sentence observing that they differ. That shape earns description marks and forfeits the comparison marks, because comparison is not juxtaposition. Comparison is the identification of the points at which two accounts give different answers to the same question, and the explanation of why they differ. The technique is to interrogate both accounts with a fixed set of questions and to write the answer question by question rather than theory by theory. What drives expansion? Who benefits? Is the phenomenon curable, and by what? What would count as evidence against the account? Run both traditions through those four interrogations and the comparison writes itself, because the differences emerge at the level of mechanism rather than at the level of slogan.

### What separates the liberal explanation from the Marxist one at the level of mechanism?

The liberal account treats expansion as a malfunction: surplus capital, generated by maldistribution at home, captures policy and drives it abroad, so reform of distribution removes the pressure. The Marxist accounts treat expansion as a function: the system's normal operation at a certain stage generates the outward drive, so no reform within the system removes it.

That single contrast, malfunction against function, generates most of the others. Because Hobson's mechanism is a distributional accident, his imperialism is contingent: a different tax policy, a broader franchise, stronger unions, and the surplus shrinks, the pressure eases, and the empire loses its economic sponsor. Because Hilferding's and Lenin's mechanisms are structural, their imperialism is necessary within the stage: tariffs, blocs, and partition follow from finance capital's organisation as reliably as competition followed from the earlier stage's, and only a change of system ends them. The two families also divide over the beneficiary. For the liberal account, empire is a racket benefiting a narrow fraction, financiers, armaments makers, colonial officials, at the expense of the nation that funds it, which is why the liberal can appeal to the nation's real interests against the racket. For the Marxist accounts, the benefits, while unevenly shared, are bound into the reproduction of the whole order, which is why appeals to the national interest miss the point: the interest being served is the system's, not the nation's, and the two are not the same. Finally, the families divide over the evidence of their own failure. A liberal can welcome decolonisation and inter-capitalist peace as the system maturing out of its malfunction. A Marxist must either revise the stage theory, as many did, or relocate the claim from territory to relation, as the dependency writers did, and a candidate who can narrate those revisions as responses to evidence is performing exactly the operation comparison questions exist to elicit.

The liberal family has its own internal range, and a strong comparison acknowledges it. Gallagher and Robinson's celebrated argument about the imperialism of free trade dissolved the boundary between formal and informal empire, showing a mid-Victorian power expanding continuously while preferring influence to annexation wherever influence sufficed, a finding that complicates every simple periodisation, Marxist and liberal alike, and delights examiners when deployed accurately. Cain and Hopkins later relocated the driving interest from industry to the gentlemanly alliance of finance and landed wealth centred on the City, a revision inside the liberal and economic reading that candidates can use to show the rival camp also argues with itself. Symmetry of treatment is the comparison answer's final discipline: each side presented as a living literature with internal disputes, each side's strongest version engaged, and the verdict, wherever it lands, earned by the interrogations rather than announced in advance.

## Marx on colonialism: the question candidates fear

Every cluster of topics has one question that candidates privately hope will not appear, and on this topic it is the question about Marx's own writings on colonialism. It is the question most likely to arise in history and civil service papers and the least likely to have been prepared for, because revision material either avoids it or arms the candidate with a slogan. The slogan comes in two varieties, Marx the apologist for empire and Marx the anti-colonial hero, and both are examination poison, because the question is invariably set by someone who knows the record contains material for neither slogan in its simple form. The creditable answer is a sequence, and the sequence never varies: state the early position accurately, state the factual problems with it, state the later change, in that order. Candidates who run the sequence in order can be trusted by a marker within a paragraph. Candidates who begin with a verdict, either way, announce that they have come to perform rather than to report.

### How should a candidate present Marx's articles on India from the eighteen fifties?

Report the argument before judging it: England, however sordid its motives, was cast as the unconscious instrument of a transformation that would dissolve a stagnant village order and lay foundations for a modern society. Then name the premises the argument rested on, because the evaluation the marker wants begins with the quality of the evidence available to the author.

The early position is the pair of articles Marx wrote for an American newspaper in the summer of 1853, on the British rule in India and its future results. Their argument, stated without softening, is that British conquest, driven by the basest motives and accompanied by misery on a vast scale, was nevertheless performing a double mission: destroying the old society of self-sufficient villages and Oriental despotism, and laying the material foundations, railways, telegraphs, a press, a modern army and administration, from which a regenerated society could eventually grow. Indian society, in this account, had no history of its own, only a history of successive conquerors, and its stagnation was the premise of the whole analysis: since the village order could not transform itself, transformation had to arrive from outside, and England, whatever its crimes, was its vehicle. The articles contain genuine horror at the destruction being wrought and genuine contempt for the motives of the conquerors, and a candidate should register both, since the position is frequently caricatured as approval. It is not approval. It is something a marker finds more interesting and more difficult: a judgement that a brutal process was historically progressive, delivered by a writer who described the brutality unsparingly.

The factual problems come second, and they are not marginal. The picture of the Indian village as changeless, self-sufficient, and outside history rested on a thin and interested evidential base, travellers' accounts, official reports, and the French physician Bernier's much earlier description of Mughal India, filtered through a colonial archive that had its own reasons for depicting the conquered society as inert. Later Indian historiography, and the work of scholars such as Irfan Habib and Kosambi, reconstructed a pre-colonial economy with markets, manufactures, merchant capital, and considerable dynamism, in which the textile production the British destroyed was among the world's most advanced rather than a stagnant handicraft. The doctrine of an Asiatic mode of production, a social form outside the sequence of modes Marx used for Europe, was left unresolved by its author and worried at by his successors for a century, partly because it sat awkwardly beside the claim that the stages of history were universal. A candidate who states the premises and their evidential weakness, with the historiography named, has done the thing the question is testing: the evaluation of a canonical writer's argument against the evidence available then and the scholarship available since.

### What changed in Marx's position between the India articles and the Irish letters?

The later writing withdraws the premise that liberation must be delivered from outside. Covering the revolt that swept northern India within four years of the articles, he reported the rebels as historical actors rather than as scenery, and on Ireland he eventually reversed his earlier expectation entirely, concluding that the English working class would achieve nothing until it had broken with the domination of the neighbouring island.

The change can be documented rather than merely asserted, which is what makes it such an examinable sequence. In 1857, when the Indian revolt broke out, Marx's journalism treated the insurgents' cause with a sympathy absent from the articles of four years earlier and directed its contempt at the reprisals of the colonial power, a shift in the distribution of agency: Indians now made events rather than suffering them. Through the following decade his analysis of the American Civil War placed enslaved labour and its emancipation at the centre of the world's politics. And on Ireland the reversal is explicit and self-acknowledged. He had earlier expected the English working class, once victorious, to settle the Irish question as a by-product. By the end of the sixties he had concluded the opposite: that the subjugation of Ireland was the material basis of the English ruling order's strength and the secret of the English working class's impotence, divided as it was against Irish labour, and that consequently, in the formulation of his confidential communication to the International's council, the lever must be applied in Ireland. The colonised country had moved, in his analysis, from object of history to lever of it. A candidate who can narrate that movement, with the Irish reversal as its clearest documented instance, possesses the strongest available answer to the question, because it lets the record itself supply the complexity that slogans flatten.

There remains the matter candidates most dread being asked about directly: the private correspondence. Marx's letters to Engels contain, alongside the working material of their collaboration, language about peoples and individuals that is racist in character, including an 1862 letter about his rival Ferdinand Lassalle that directs antisemitic and racial epithets at its subject; the letter survives in the forty-first volume of the standard collected works, where any reader may verify the characterisation. Engels's journalism of 1849 on the national struggles of the revolutionary years similarly dismisses several smaller Slavic peoples in terms that deny them a historical future and treat their absorption by larger nations as progress; the articles appeared in the newspaper the two men edited, and the characterisation is a matter of the published record. The cluster's policy, which this guide teaches candidates to follow, is that such material is characterised accurately and cited to its source, and never reproduced, because the epithets themselves add no analytical content that the characterisation does not carry, while reproducing them in an examination answer adds risk without adding marks. What the material establishes, and what an answer should say it establishes, is a fact about the limits of the men's own universalism in private and journalistic registers, which the assessment of their colonial writings must record alongside the change of position documented in the published work. Neither fact cancels the other, and the answer that reports both, in sequence, without theatre in either direction, is the answer the question was designed to find.

A last discipline governs this whole section of an answer: the refusal of the isolated quotation. Both the defence and the prosecution of Marx on colonialism proceed, in the polemical literature, by excerpting: one sentence from 1853 exhibited as the whole man, or one sentence from the Irish correspondence exhibited as his absolution. Examination answers should name that procedure when they meet it in a question's framing and decline to imitate it, insisting on the sequence, premises, and revision that the full record supports. The full treatment of this material, with its controversies among scholars laid out at length, stands in the companion article on [Marx on colonialism: the hard questions](/2017/03/01/marx-on-colonialism-debate/), and candidates who expect the question should read it entire rather than rely on any summary, including this one.

## Decolonisation as evidence

Decolonisation is the topic's favourite body of evidence and its most mishandled. Candidates reach for it as though it were a single fact with a single implication, usually that the theory was refuted, and markers watch the same mishandling recur every season. The transfer of power across Asia and Africa in the generation after the Second World War is not one fact but a test that different claims pass and fail differently, and the technique for using it is the technique this guide has taught throughout: specify, before judging, exactly which claim the evidence is being brought against. Used that way, decolonisation becomes the evaluate answer's richest asset. Used loosely, it is a confession that the candidate has one undifferentiated theory in mind and one undifferentiated event to throw at it.

### Which claims does decolonisation actually test?

It tests the territorial claim directly: if formal colonies were indispensable to the metropolitan economies, their surrender should have damaged those economies, and it did not. It tests the relational claim only weakly, since the transfers the relation describes were always said to survive changes of flag. Matching the evidence to the claim is the entire discipline.

Against the claim that the advanced economies required formal possession of colonies, the evidence is devastating and should be conceded in full. The decades of fastest metropolitan growth coincided with the shedding of empire, investment among the wealthy economies dwarfed investment in the former possessions, and no metropolitan economy collapsed, or measurably faltered, for want of its flag over foreign territory. Kautsky's ghost stirs here too: the advanced powers, having divided the world, proceeded to cooperate in exploiting it through trade and finance rather than fight for its redivision, which is closer to the ultra-imperialist conjecture than to the forecast of recurring partition wars. Against the claim that the end of formal rule ended the relationship, the evidence is far weaker, and the tradition's own writers said so at the time. Kwame Nkrumah, leading the first sub-Saharan African state to independence, argued within a decade that the new states remained bound by investment, trade, and monetary arrangements that transferred value outward as reliably as direct rule had, a condition he named neo-colonialism and called the last stage of the phenomenon. A candidate need not endorse Nkrumah's argument to use it: its existence demonstrates that the tradition contained, in advance, an interpretation of decolonisation under which the event confirms the relational claim while refuting the territorial one, and the candidate who stages that internal disagreement has understood the evidence better than the candidate who brandishes it.

There is also a properly historical use of decolonisation that candidates underuse. The manner of the transfers, negotiated here, fought for there, shaped by wars of liberation in Algeria, Vietnam, and the Portuguese colonies, supplies evidence about the agency of the colonised that bears directly on the assessment of Marx's early premises examined above: societies depicted in 1853 as without history made a great deal of it in the twentieth century. And the post-independence record, the one-party states, the economic difficulties, the persistence of commodity dependence, belongs in the evidence column for the anti-colonial movements' own theories, treated at length in the cluster's study of [anti-colonial Marxism in practice](/2017/05/01/anti-colonial-marxism-case-studies/). The governing rule is simple to state and hard to keep: decolonisation refutes specified claims, confirms others, and leaves the rest untouched, and an answer earns its marks by performing the sorting rather than by announcing a verdict on the whole.

## Imperialism and dependency: revising them together

Development papers set the trap this section exists to disarm. Candidates arrive with imperialism and dependency theory fused into a single warm conviction that the rich exploit the poor, and examiners, who regard the distinction between the two literatures as basic professional equipment, mark the fusion down. The literatures are related, and the relation should be stated precisely. The classical theories of imperialism were written between 1902 and the First World War, by Europeans, about the behaviour of the advanced capitalist states: their question was why those states expanded. The dependency literature was written after the Second World War, largely by Latin American and other peripheral scholars, about the condition of the underdeveloped economies: its question was why those economies did not develop. Same world, opposite ends of the telescope, different mechanisms, different remedies. A candidate who can state that paragraph has already separated what the trap joins.

### Where does dependency theory part company with the classical accounts?

The classical writers asked what drove the centres outward; the dependency writers asked what the resulting connection did to the periphery, and several of them answered in terms the classical canon did not contain: development and underdevelopment as two faces of one process, produced together rather than one lagging behind the other.

The names and mechanisms a development answer needs are distinct from the classical roster and should be revised as a separate list. Raúl Prebisch and the United Nations economists in Latin America argued that the terms of trade moved secularly against exporters of primary products, so that integration into world markets itself transferred the gains of technical progress to the industrial centres. Andre Gunder Frank radicalised the point into the development of underdevelopment: the satellite's poverty was not an original condition awaiting modernisation but a product of its incorporation, over centuries, into chains running to the metropoles. Fernando Henrique Cardoso, writing partly against Frank, restored agency and internal class alliances to the picture, showing development of a dependent kind occurring within the relationship rather than being simply blocked by it. Immanuel Wallerstein generalised the whole scheme into a world-systems framework of core, semi-periphery, and periphery, a descendant of the classical tradition by way of dependency rather than a member of the classical canon. The full exposition lives in the cluster's article on [dependency theory](/2016/07/01/dependency-theory-explained/), and the revision discipline is to keep two rosters, two chronologies, and two questions in mind at once, drawing the connection between them explicitly when a question invites it and refusing the invitation when it does not. Questions that invite it are identifiable: they mention underdevelopment, the periphery, or the post-war decades. Questions that do not, the classical theory questions, are damaged by imports from the later literature, however sophisticated the imports sound.

## Examples that work in the room

An example earns its place in a timed answer by doing a job: locating a mechanism, testing a claim, or marking a change. Examples that merely decorate, a colony named, a date dropped, a war gestured at, consume the candidate's scarcest resource, which is not knowledge but lines. The selection principle is therefore functional. For each theory in the roster, the candidate should hold one episode in which its mechanism is visible, chosen for clarity rather than drama. The scramble for Africa in the last two decades of the nineteenth century, in which European powers partitioned a continent at a conference table and then raced to make paper claims effective, serves the stage theories' discussion of partition and rivalry, and serves Fieldhouse's critique equally well, since the diplomatic record shows strategic and prestige motives jostling the economic ones, which is precisely why it is a good example: it argues in both directions. The chartered companies, administering territory as a commercial asset well into the modern period, serve Hilferding's fusion of economic and political power. The City of London's overseas lending, flowing predominantly to settler economies and other developed regions rather than to the tropical colonies, is the standing exhibit for the geography objection to the capital export thesis. The cooperation of the advanced economies after the Second World War, their joint institutions and absent wars, is the exhibit for Kautsky's conjecture and against the forecast of permanent rivalry.

### What makes an example earn its place in a timed answer?

An example earns its place when it can be attached to a claim in a single sentence that states what the episode shows: this mechanism visible here, this prediction embarrassed there. If the attachment sentence cannot be written, the example is decoration, and decoration is the first thing a candidate under time pressure should cut.

Two further rules protect the candidate. First, keep examples historical and specific rather than contemporary and vague: the classical record supplies everything the questions require, and an answer reaching for the present day inherits disputes the marker may grade from the other side. Second, dose the examples: one well-attached episode per movement of the argument outperforms a spray of names, because the marker is crediting the attachment, the reasoning that binds episode to claim, and attachment takes sentences that a spray leaves no room for. The candidates who lose marks on evidence are rarely those who know too few examples. They are those who deploy examples as proof by accumulation, as though the theory could be voted true, rather than as instruments for testing specified claims one at a time.

## Quotations that earn their keep

Candidates collect quotations the way magpies collect foil, and the resulting answers glitter without illuminating. The discipline this guide teaches is austere: a quotation earns credit only when it is located, short, and made to work, and a small stock of such quotations, each attached to its use, outperforms a notebook of ornaments. Located means the marker is told who wrote it, in what work or document, and in what argument. Short means short enough to be quoted exactly, since a misquotation of a famous line damages credibility out of proportion to its size. Made to work means the sentence following the quotation states what the quotation establishes, because examiners credit the gloss, not the decoration.

The serviceable stock on this topic is small. From Marx's 1853 article, the double mission formulation, England's role in India described as a double mission in India: one destructive, the other regenerating, condenses the early position into a line an answer can then evaluate against the historiography. From the Irish correspondence, the lever sentence, the lever must be applied in Ireland, marks the reversal with an economy no paraphrase matches, and pairing the two quotations across an answer enacts the sequence this guide teaches, early position and later change, in the primary words. From Gallagher and Robinson, the phrase the imperialism of free trade supplies the label for informal empire and signals acquaintance with the historiographical mainstream. From Nkrumah, the title concept neo-colonialism, glossed in the candidate's own words, carries the relational reply on decolonisation. Hobson's taproot metaphor, his location of the economic taproot of the phenomenon in the interests profiting from it, rewards the candidate who uses it to distinguish his mechanism from the stage theories'. That is five exhibits, and five are enough; the candidate who deploys three of them accurately, each doing a stated job, will outscore the candidate who deploys fifteen loosely.

Two prohibitions complete the guidance. Never quote the private correspondence discussed in the previous section: the characterisation carries the analytical content, the epithets carry none, and a reproduced slur in an examination script creates a risk entirely out of proportion to any conceivable gain. And never quote a figure. A number inside quotation marks looks doubly authoritative and is doubly dangerous, for the reasons the protocol below sets out. Quotations are for formulations, the sentences in which a writer's position crystallised. Numbers require provenance, method, and defence, none of which a timed answer can supply, and the historian's rule governs all four disciplines on this point: an unsourced figure is worse than no figure, because it advertises that the candidate cannot tell evidence from decoration.

## The contested figures protocol

This protocol is stated as an instruction because examiners enforce it as one. Candidates must not cite colonial mortality, famine, or extraction figures as facts in examination answers. The numbers circulating in the literature and in popular argument on this topic are among the most contested in modern historiography: they rest on demographic reconstructions, counterfactual baselines, and accounting conventions that specialists dispute, and a figure quoted without its method is not evidence but a slogan with decimals. History papers penalise unsourced figures explicitly. Papers in the other disciplines may not name the penalty, but a marker who knows the literature, and on this topic the markers do, discounts the judgement of a candidate who deploys the largest available number as though it settled something.

The protocol does not require silence about scale. It requires scale to be described qualitatively, or attributed. A candidate may write that the mortality associated with particular famines and conquests was enormous and remains disputed among demographers, and that formulation, honest about both magnitude and contest, earns more credit than any integer. A candidate may attribute a specific estimate to the scholar who produced it, with the method named in a phrase, since attribution converts a bare assertion into a located claim the marker can assess. What the candidate may not do is select a figure from the contested range and present it as the fact of the matter, and may not use a figure as the load-bearing member of an evaluation, because evaluations built on contested numbers collapse the moment a marker declines the number. Build evaluations on mechanisms, predictions, and the shape of the record, which are sturdy, and let figures appear, if at all, as attributed illustrations of arguments already made. The rule protects the candidate in both directions: it also forbids the minimising figure, deployed to shrink the record, since the offence is not the direction of the number but its unsourced certainty.

## The common mistakes, catalogued

Mistakes on this topic recur with a regularity that makes them cataloguable, and a catalogue is a gift: every item in it is a mark another candidate will lose. The first and costliest has governed this guide from its opening page: presenting Lenin's pamphlet as the Marxist theory of imperialism, which misdescribes the tradition and makes comparison structurally impossible. The second is its mirror: treating dependency theory and the classical theory as interchangeable, which development examiners read as a failure to learn either. The third is the biographical shortcut on Marx, in which the founder is presented as simply pro-colonial on the evidence of 1853 or simply anti-colonial on the evidence of Ireland, when the record, as the dedicated section showed, contains a position, its flawed premises, and a documented change, and the question is testing whether the candidate can hold all three. The fourth is decolonisation deployed as an unspecified refutation, a flag-lowering ceremony asked to falsify claims it never touched. The fifth is the unsourced figure, governed by the protocol above. The sixth is quotation spray, governed by the discipline of the previous section. The seventh, subtler than the rest, is answering the question the candidate revised for: the explain answer delivered to an evaluate command, the comparison delivered as two summaries, the assessment of Marx's writings delivered as a verdict on Marxism. Each mistake is a failure of the same underlying skill, which is obedience to the actual wording on the actual paper, and each is preventable by the matrix discipline of classification before composition.

There is one more mistake, and it belongs to strong candidates rather than weak ones: the performance of balance as a substitute for judgement. An answer that gives every side equal weight, finds merit everywhere, and concludes that the truth lies somewhere in between has not evaluated; it has abdicated, and markers distinguish the two performances reliably. Balance in this guide's sense is a property of the evidence-gathering, not of the conclusion. The candidate assembles the scorecard fairly, credits the critics at full strength, concedes the failures without varnish, and then judges, because a judgement is what the command word purchased. Where the judgement lands is the candidate's affair. That it lands somewhere, with reasons, is the examiner's requirement, and the next section exists to remove the anxiety that most often prevents it.

## Can a critical answer score full marks?

Behind the technical questions sits the question candidates actually ask, usually of each other rather than of their teachers: is it safe? Is it safe to criticise the Marxist theory of imperialism in a discipline where the perspective is taught sympathetically, by lecturers who may hold it? And the mirror question, asked in rooms where the theory is taught as a specimen of error: is it safe to defend it? Candidates who carry this anxiety into the examination write hedged, ventriloquised answers, guessing at the marker's allegiance instead of arguing the question, and the guessing is legible on the page. This guide therefore states the position plainly, in its own voice, as the fact around which its advice is organised. A model answer concluding against Marxist theories of imperialism can score full marks. A model answer concluding in their favour can score full marks. What is assessed, in every discipline this guide serves, is the quality of the performance: the accuracy with which positions are stated, the fairness with which critics are represented, the precision with which evidence is matched to claims, and the explicitness of the reasoning that connects them. A marker examining this topic professionally is examining candidates on a contested literature, and the marking instruments for contested literatures are built to reward evidenced argument wherever it lands, because the alternative, rewarding allegiance, would make the examination unmarkable among examiners who themselves disagree.

The claim can be tested against the behaviour of the assessment system rather than merely asserted. Questions on this topic are set with command words that require judgement, and marking guidance for such questions describes levels of evaluation, not conclusions: the top level is characterised by sustained, substantiated judgement, a description that specifies the reasoning's quality and is silent about its destination. Examiners are trained, moderated, and sampled, and a marker who rewarded only congenial conclusions would be visible in moderation precisely because the scripts of equally competent candidates conclude in opposite directions. The candidate's protection is therefore not caution but craft. Name what would change the judgement, as the evaluate section advised, because a conclusion accompanied by its own revision conditions is unimpeachable: it shows the marker a mind that would have concluded otherwise had the evidence pointed otherwise, which is the definition of the intellectual virtue the examination exists to certify.

The counter-view deserves its paragraph, because part of it is true and candidates should know which part. Some command words do not want judgement at all. Describe, outline, and state purchase exposition, and a candidate who delivers a verdict to an exposition command has misread the purchase as surely as one who delivers exposition to an evaluate command. The tell is in the verb and its objects: commands that name a proposition and ask for judgement upon it (evaluate, assess, to what extent, how far, discuss) license and require a conclusion; commands that name a body of material and ask for its organised presentation (describe, explain, outline, summarise) require the judgement to be withheld or confined to the selection and arrangement. Between them sits the treacherous discuss, which in most traditions of marking expects exploration terminating in a judgement and penalises the answer that explores without landing. Read the verb as a contract, deliver the operation it purchases, and the safety question largely dissolves: the candidate was never being asked to guess an allegiance, only to execute an operation well. On this topic, in any of its examination rooms, a well-executed operation concluding against the Marxist accounts, or for them, is a top-band performance, and candidates should write their conclusions with the confidence that the system, whatever the private sympathies of any individual reader, is constructed to pay for exactly that.

### Why do examiners reward the candidate who names rivals over the candidate who defends one line?

Because the topic's assessment objectives are built around the existence of a contested literature. A defence of a single line can demonstrate knowledge of that line; it cannot demonstrate the comparative and evaluative operations the higher bands describe. Naming rivals, accurately and fairly, is the visible evidence that those operations are available to the candidate.

There is a deeper version of the same point, and it closes this section. The anxiety about safety rests on a picture of examiners as partisans guarding a doctrine. The reality of assessment on contested topics is closer to the opposite: the examiner's professional pleasure, and it is a real pleasure, documented in every examiner's report that praises answers for challenging the question's assumptions, lies in meeting a candidate who treats the literature as a set of arguments to be weighed rather than as a catechism to be recited or a heresy to be exposed. The Marxist tradition on imperialism is unusually suited to that treatment, because it argued with itself from its first decade: Hobson's liberalism against Hilferding's structuralism, Luxemburg's necessity against Kautsky's possibility, Lenin's canonisation against the record's later verdicts. A candidate who joins that argument, on whichever side the evidence seems to them to favour, is doing the thing the subject itself does. The examination rewards it because the discipline rewards it, and the candidate may safely believe both.

## Command words: exposition and judgement

Since so much turns on the verb, the guide ends its technical instruction with the verb. Examination questions on imperialism arrive wearing a small wardrobe of command words, and each word purchases a different operation. Explain purchases mechanism: the candidate shows how the theory's parts connect, why monopoly is said to generate expansion, why underconsumption is said to seek outlets, and earns marks for the connective tissue rather than for verdicts. Evaluate purchases judgement on worth or validity, and demands the scorecard method, claims tested against the record with critics named. Assess and to what extent purchase a weighed judgement with the weighing shown, the extent being itself the answer's most important sentence, since a judgement of partly, with the parts specified, is the natural resting place of an honest scorecard. Compare purchases the interrogation method, both accounts run through the same questions, differences located in mechanism. Discuss purchases exploration with a landing. Describe and outline purchase organised presentation, and the candidate who notices the modesty of those verbs and answers them without inflation saves the effort others waste.

### When does a command word ask for exposition rather than judgement?

When the verb names an operation on material rather than a verdict on a proposition: describe, outline, state, and summarise ask the candidate to present a position faithfully, while evaluate, assess, and to what extent ask the candidate to grade it. The object of the verb settles borderline cases, and discuss, which sounds neutral, in most marking traditions expects a judgement at the end of the exploration.

The practical drill is simple and worth performing in revision. Take a term's worth of past questions on the topic, cover the content, and sort the questions by verb alone, predicting from the verb what a top answer would have to do. Candidates who can perform that sort reliably have internalised the contract system, and their answers stop suffering the commonest structural accident on this topic: the magnificent evaluation delivered to a question that asked for an explanation, marked down not for any error of content but for breach of contract. Examiners' reports return to that accident with weariness across subjects, and on a topic where the candidate's opinions are engaged, as they are here, the temptation to deliver the verdict one is burning to give, to the question that never asked for it, is the final trap the matrix exists to spring harmlessly.

## Building the revision: from notes to the matrix

The guide turns to the revision process itself, since a method unrehearsed is a method unavailable under pressure. The sequence has four passes. In the first pass, the candidate assembles content from the cluster's articles, the content guide for the theories in their full form, the companion study of Marx's colonial writings for the hard question, and the dependency article for the development separation, reducing each theory to its two-sentence mechanism and each controversy to its two strongest statements. General essay architecture, the planning of introductions and conclusions across subjects, belongs to a different article in this series, the guide to [how to write a Marxism essay](/2021/10/15/how-to-write-a-marxism-essay/), and the treatment of economic assessment to [the economics exam guide](/2016/08/15/marxist-economics-exam-answers/); this article's concern is only the imperialism material and its examination handling. In the second pass, the candidate builds the answer matrix from the assembled content, filling each cell without notes and treating every stall as a located gap. In the third pass, the candidate writes openings only: the stage and empire opening performed for each question type in the matrix, five openings in twenty minutes, until the move is fluent enough to survive nerves. In the fourth pass, the candidate sits timed answers, at least one per question type most feared, and marks them against the trap column before against any mark scheme, since on this topic the traps, once avoided, leave content the candidate demonstrably possesses to earn its marks.

Spaced rehearsal of the matrix itself deserves the final word of instruction. The table is small enough to be redrawn from memory, and redrawing it, blank cells and all, at intervals across the revision period is the single highest-yield activity this guide can prescribe, because the act of reconstruction exposes exactly which cells have faded: a candidate who discovers in April that the evaluation for the comparison row will not come to mind has found a cheap gap, while the same discovery made in the examination room is an expensive one. The matrix, the roster, the scorecard, and the sequence for the hard question are the four artefacts a candidate carries into the room. None of them is an essay. All of them together are the means by which essays get written in the room, fitted to the question actually set, which is the only kind of essay the higher bands have ever rewarded.

## The tradition arguing with itself: disagreements as examination assets

The roster of the previous sections is a list of agreements to disagree, and the disagreements are worth a section of their own, because examiners prize them and candidates neglect them. A candidate who knows only that the tradition contains several theories, without knowing what the theories dispute, owns a catalogue rather than a literature. Three quarrels repay detailed knowledge. The first is the dispute over ultra-imperialism. Kautsky proposed, as the First World War began, that the phase of competing imperialisms might give way to a phase of joint exploitation, the great capitals cartelising the world as firms cartelise an industry. Lenin's contempt for the idea was total: it diverted attention, he argued, from the sharpening antagonisms of the present to a pacified abstraction, and it ignored that agreements among predators merely register a temporary balance of forces before the next redivision. The quarrel matters to candidates because history subsequently supplied evidence for the despised side: the advanced powers after the Second World War built joint institutions, accepted a hegemonic ordering of their relations, and refrained from war among themselves for a span no Leninist forecast allowed. The strong answer does not award the quarrel to Kautsky outright, since rivalry persisted by other means and the ordering rested on conditions that have visibly shifted, but it stages the quarrel as the tradition's own anticipation of the question its critics later pressed, which converts an external objection into an internal resource.

The second quarrel concerns the nation. Lenin, against the Austro-Marxist theorists of national cultural autonomy and against Luxemburg, defended the right of nations to self-determination, up to secession, as a democratic demand the socialist movement was bound to support, while Luxemburg, from her Polish vantage, held that in the imperialist epoch the slogan was either utopian or a gift to the national bourgeoisie, and that the Polish question in particular admitted no solution outside the international revolution. The quarrel is not antiquarian. It determines how the tradition reads wars of national liberation, how it distinguishes the nationalism of the oppressor from that of the oppressed, and how its later adherents positioned themselves toward the anti-colonial movements that remade the century. In an answer, the quarrel does double duty: it demonstrates that the tradition's engagement with colonialism was argumentative rather than dogmatic, and it supplies the intellectual background without which the anti-colonial case studies elsewhere in this cluster read as mere illustration.

The third quarrel is the quietest and the most consequential for evaluation: the dispute over whether colonies were economically necessary to the system at all. Luxemburg's accumulation argument made the non-capitalist outside a strict necessity, without which surplus value could not be realised; critics within and beyond the tradition attacked the realisation argument's logic from the moment it appeared, noting that the reproduction schemes in Marx's own second volume model a closed capitalist system without obvious breakdown, and later writers developed the objection into the geography argument, that the actual flows of capital declined to follow the theory's map. A candidate evaluating the tradition should know that its most logically ambitious member was also its most contested, and that the contest was opened by sympathisers before it was pressed by opponents. The pattern generalises, and stating it earns credit in any room: on this subject, the tradition's severest and earliest critics were frequently its own adherents, and an answer that borrows their criticisms is not deserting the subject but joining its internal life.

### How can you tell a listing answer from a comparing one while you write?

A listing answer assigns each theory a paragraph and lets the paragraphs sit side by side; a comparing answer runs the theories through shared questions, so the same issue is answered differently in adjacent sentences and the difference becomes the content. The test is mechanical: if the paragraphs could be reordered without loss, the answer lists.

## History papers: chronology, evidence, and the shape of the record

The history candidate faces the topic in its most evidentiary form, and the expectations differ enough from the politics room to deserve separate treatment. History questions on empire and decolonisation reward the handling of sequence: what the record shows happening, in what order, and which theoretical claim each phase of the sequence touches. The candidate's first discipline is therefore periodisation. The classical theory was written about a specific conjuncture, the decades between the scramble for Africa and the First World War, when partition was completed, capital export boomed, and the powers armed. Claims framed for that conjuncture should be tested first against that conjuncture, where some perform respectably: the partition did happen, the rivalries were real, the war did arrive, and the entanglement of finance with flag is documented in episode after episode. Tested against the following half-century the same claims perform unevenly, as the scorecard section recorded. A history answer that runs the test in both periods, and says plainly that the theory describes its own moment better than the moment that followed, has performed the historian's characteristic operation, and it is worth more than any amount of theoretical fluency unanchored in dates.

Evidence handling is the second discipline. The history marker wants to see the candidate distinguish kinds of evidence and notice what each kind can and cannot show. Diplomatic documents show motives as governments recorded them, which is not the same as motives as they operated. Trade and investment statistics show the geography of flows, on which the capital export debate turns, but they must be handled with the protocol's respect for provenance, described in pattern rather than brandished as integers. The writings of the colonised, petitions, newspapers, the records of movements, show the agency that the 1853 premises denied, and a history answer that cites the existence and character of such sources, even without quotation, signals a candidate who knows where the archive's centre of gravity has moved. The third discipline is the refusal of hindsight's cheap victories. Judging the classical writers for failing to foresee the aeroplane age's geopolitics is as unhistorical as judging Marx for the colonial archive he was given; the historian's question is what the theory explained in its evidence-world and what it failed to explain even there, and the geography objection scores precisely because it fails the theory on evidence contemporaneous with it. Candidates who grasp that distinction write evaluations that historians trust, on this topic and on every other.

## Politics and international relations papers: placing the perspective

In the politics room, and still more in the international relations room, imperialism appears as one perspective among a surveyed set, and the marks attach to placement: the candidate must show where the Marxist family's explanation sits in the field of explanations and what it sees that its neighbours miss. Realism, the neighbouring giant, explains expansion by the security competition of states under anarchy: territory, buffers, and bases are sought because rivals seek them, and economics enters as an instrument of power rather than as its cause. Liberalism explains expansion, where it does not dismiss it as atavism, by the capture of policy by sectional interests or by the misperceptions of statesmen, and expects commerce and institutions to pacify what they bind. The Marxist family reverses the realist order of causation and refuses the liberal consolation: the international system is not a board on which states happen to compete but a structure generated by the accumulation process, so that the states themselves, their capacities and their appetites, are products of the economic order they administer. Placement stated at that level, causation reversed against realism and consolation refused against liberalism, is what a theory survey question is purchasing, and candidates who deliver it in the opening movement find the rest of the answer arranging itself.

The placement has a second level that strong candidates add: the family's account of what the international order is for. Where realism sees an anarchy to be managed and liberalism a society to be thickened with institutions, the classical Marxist writers saw a hierarchy to be explained, an ordering of states and peoples by their position in a single world economy, and the later world-systems literature made that hierarchy its explicit architecture. Questions asking what distinguishes the Marxist perspective in international relations are answered by that word, hierarchy, more efficiently than by any feature list: the perspective's claim is that the system's ordering principle is economic and stratified, that wealth and poverty among nations are produced together by the same processes, and that the politics of states dances on a structure it did not choose. Candidates should also know the perspective's characteristic vulnerability in this room, because placement includes it: the classical accounts are stronger on why the system expands than on the variation among states within it, stronger on structure than on the decisions of particular governments at particular moments, and critics in the realist tradition press exactly there, asking why, if structure determines, the same structure produced annexation in one decade and informal influence in another. The free trade imperialism argument of Gallagher and Robinson, though written by historians, is the candidate's bridge across that objection, since it shows the structure expressing itself through whichever instrument the moment made cheapest, annexation or influence, and an answer that deploys it has answered the strongest placement objection with the literature's own resources.

## Development papers: the extension into neo-colonialism and unequal exchange

The development room examines the tradition's second life, the forms in which the classical claims survived their own century, and candidates need the extension's map as well as its boundary. Three extensions recur. Neo-colonialism, named by Nkrumah and generalised by the independence generation, holds that formal sovereignty can coexist with continued external direction of an economy through investment, aid, trade structure, and monetary membership, so that the empire sense of imperialism ends while the relational sense persists; its examination use is precise, since it converts decolonisation from a refutation into a period boundary. Unequal exchange, developed by Arghiri Emmanuel, proposed a mechanism the classical writers lacked: where wages differ durably between countries while profit rates equalise through competition, exchange at market prices transfers value from the low-wage to the high-wage economy even in trade between juridical equals, so that the relation reproduces itself without flags, governors, or monopoly in the classical sense. World-systems analysis, Wallerstein's construction, absorbed the dependency inheritance into a five-century history of a single capitalist world economy stratified into core, semi-periphery, and periphery, with the semi-periphery doing the political work of stabilising the stratification. Each extension should be examinable in the same format as the classical roster: holder, mechanism, difference from the parent, and the objection it attracts, for unequal exchange the objection that wage differences reflect productivity and cannot be treated as an independent variable, for world-systems the objection that a framework explaining everything risks explaining nothing in particular.

The boundary discipline of the dependency section applies with equal force here, in reverse. Development questions license the extensions; classical questions do not, and the candidate who answers a question on Lenin with Emmanuel has committed a chronological category error that markers notice even when the material is sophisticated. The rooms have house rules, and respecting them is not pedantry but the substance of placement, the skill every one of these papers is ultimately testing.

## A worked assembly: one evaluate answer, movement by movement

Method becomes credible only when it is seen assembled, so this section walks through the construction of an evaluate answer at the level of movements rather than prose, leaving the candidate to supply sentences fitted to the wording set. The question imagined is the generic one the matrix's second row addresses: evaluate the Marxist theory of imperialism, in a politics paper. The first movement is the opening, and its three sentences perform the stage and empire separation, announce that the theory under evaluation is chiefly the stage claim, and forecast the scorecard method, so the marker knows within five lines that claims will be graded individually. The second movement reconstructs, briefly and fairly, the account to be evaluated: not the five features as a chant but the mechanism, monopoly and finance generating capital export, partition, and rivalry, with Hobson's and Luxemburg's variants flagged in a sentence each so the reconstruction itself demonstrates the plurality the guide insists on. The third movement runs the scorecard's debit column: war among the advanced powers absent for the span the theory least expected, capital flowing chiefly among the wealthy, the system flourishing after the flags came down, with Fieldhouse named on geography and the liberal atavism thesis admitted as the standing alternative explanation. The fourth movement runs the credit column with equal specificity: concentration and finance beyond the founders' imagining, the state's activism in accumulation's service, the relational pattern surviving translation into the dependency and supply chain literatures. The fifth movement handles the tradition's internal anticipation of the debits, Kautsky's conjecture and the neo-colonial relocation, which prevents the evaluation from posing as a raid from outside and shows the candidate commanding the literature's self-criticism. The sixth movement judges, and its judgement, to be worth anything, must be differentiated: the theory retired as a prophecy of the system's political destiny, retained in part as an anatomy of its economic structure, its claims henceforth to be cited individually rather than endorsed or rejected as a set. The seventh movement, one or two sentences, states the revision condition: the judgement on rivalry would change if the advanced economies returned to armed partition, and the judgement on the relational claim would change if value flows were shown to run systematically toward the periphery. Seven movements, none of them long, each doing a named job the matrix predicted. A candidate who has rehearsed the assembly can vary it for any evaluate or assess wording in any room, which is the difference between knowing a method and having memorised a performance.

## The heirs revise: the theory after its founders

A candidate's evaluation gains depth from knowing that the tradition did not freeze in 1917, and the post-war revisions repay the same roster treatment as the founders. Paul Baran, writing in the fifties, redirected the surplus concept toward the monopoly stage's waste and its blockage of development in the periphery, and Baran and Sweezy's joint work on monopoly capital restated the stage theory for an economy of giant corporations whose problem was absorbing an ever-rising surplus, a restatement the economics guide in this cluster treats in its own right. Harry Magdoff documented the American century's entanglement of foreign policy with corporate expansion, supplying the stage theory with an archive its founders lacked. The Monthly Review tradition generally kept the classical questions alive through decades when academic fashion had filed them under error, which is itself a datum about how research programmes survive their predictions. Meanwhile the European debate produced its own refinements, and the dependency and world-systems literatures, treated above, can be read as the tradition's centre of gravity migrating from the metropole's motives to the periphery's condition. For examination purposes the revisions matter in two ways. They show the candidate that evaluation is a continuing activity inside the tradition, not a verdict delivered on it from outside in a single generation. And they supply the strongest available reply to the dismissal that the theory died with its predictions: parts of it did die, and its heirs performed the autopsies and salvaged the organs, which is what living traditions do and dead ones do not.

There is also a lesson in scholarly manners here that candidates absorb by osmosis and markers reward without naming. The heirs who revised most fruitfully, Cardoso qualifying Frank, Brewer mapping the whole contested field, Fieldhouse attacking from the archive he respected enough to master, shared a discipline the polemical literature lacks: they stated the position they were revising in its strongest form before laying a glove on it. The series to which this guide belongs applies that discipline throughout, steelmanning before verdict, and the candidate who practises it in the examination room is not performing a courtesy but deploying the most reliable known technology for earning the trust of a reader who disagrees. An examiner who watches a candidate state Lenin's argument better than Lenin's defenders usually manage, and then grade it without mercy, has received the strongest possible evidence that the grading is earned.

## Ireland as the hinge case

Because the question on Marx's colonial writings is the cluster's most feared, it deserves a worked case in addition to its protocol, and Ireland is the case, since the reversal there is documented in the subject's own hand and examiners know it. The candidate should be able to run the Irish sequence as fluently as the Indian one. In the earlier phase, Ireland appears in the analysis as a subordinate clause of the English question: the English working class, once emancipated, would settle Ireland's status, and Irish agitation figures as a distraction or an adjunct. The Fenian movement and the Manchester rescue of 1867, with its executions, forced the reconsideration, and the reasoning Marx then worked through in his letters and in the International's confidential communications can be stated as a chain. Ireland's subjugation supplied the English landed and ruling order with a material base and a standing pretext for military strength. The competition of Irish labour in English labour markets divided the English working class against itself, into hostile camps whose antagonism the press and the pulpit cultivated, and that division was, in his phrase family, the secret of the class's impotence despite its organisation. Therefore the order of operations reversed: not England first and Ireland as consequence, but Ireland as the lever whose application would move England. The doctrinal deposit is the sentence from the confidential communication that the quotation section commended, and the methodological deposit, for the candidate, is the spectacle of a theorist publicly correcting his own sequence in the light of a movement he had undervalued, which is the single most effective exhibit against the charge, heard whenever the hard question is set, that the founder's colonial writings admit of no development. They admit of a documented development, and the Irish hinge is where it turns.

An answer on the colonial writings that deploys both sequences, India and Ireland, in the protocol's order, position and premises and change, and then notes the private correspondence as the register where no comparable development occurred, has done everything the question can ask. It has also modelled the intellectual operation the whole cluster teaches, in history and in politics alike: grade the parts of a record separately, let the verdicts differ, and refuse the consolations of the single word.

## The revision clinic: three weak paragraphs, diagnosed

Guides tell; clinics show. Three specimens follow, each a paragraph of the kind markers meet weekly on this topic, each diagnosed and recast. The specimens are inventions, composites of performances every examiner will recognise.

The first specimen reads: Lenin said imperialism is the highest stage of capitalism and has five features, monopoly, finance capital, export of capital, division of the world by trusts, and division of the world by powers. This theory explains the First World War and colonialism. The diagnosis writes itself. The paragraph summarises one theory as the theory, recites features without mechanism, and asserts explanation where the question will ask for demonstration. The recast keeps the same knowledge and spends it differently: it opens by noting that Lenin's summary of 1916 was the most teachable of several rival accounts, states the mechanism the features summarise, the displacement of competition by monopoly and of goods export by capital export, and ends by locating the account against Hobson's, whose mechanism it borrowed and whose remedy it rejected. Nothing has been added except structure, and the structure is the difference between bands.

The second specimen reads: Marx supported colonialism because he said British rule in India was progressive, which shows the racism of his theory. The diagnosis: a verdict first, a premise suppressed, a change of position suppressed, and a leap from a man's failing to a theory's truth that the article on the hard questions teaches candidates to refuse. The recast runs the protocol's sequence: the 1853 argument stated in its own terms, the village and despotism premises named and their evidential fragility noted, the later sympathy with revolt and the Irish reversal recorded, and the conclusion confined to what the sequence supports, that the founder's colonial writings contain an early position his own later work substantially revised, alongside private language neither position excuses. Same facts, opposite intellectual character.

The third specimen reads: Decolonisation after the war proved the Marxist theory wrong, because the colonies became independent and capitalism survived. The diagnosis: an unspecified refutation, one event asked to falsify every claim at once. The recast performs the sorting: the survival of metropolitan capitalism after the flags came down falsifies the claim that formal possession was indispensable, leaves open the relational claim its holders immediately restated as neo-colonialism, and must be set beside the peace among the advanced powers and the geography of investment to complete the scorecard. In each case the cure is identical, and it is the guide's whole doctrine in one line: separate before judging, name before grading, and let every verdict state the claim it is a verdict on.

## Terms that must be kept apart

Precision on this topic is largely a matter of refusing to let neighbouring terms trade places, and a short glossary discipline belongs in every candidate's revision. Imperialism and colonialism are the first pair. Colonialism names the practice and the system of acquiring and ruling colonies; imperialism, in the literature examined here, names either the stage of capitalism said to generate the practice or the wider relation said to survive it. Answers that use the words interchangeably forfeit the opening distinction before the opening ends. Formal and informal empire are the second pair: rule by annexation against dominion exercised through trade, finance, and treaty, the distinction Gallagher and Robinson forced on the historiography and the one that lets a candidate discuss expansion in periods and places where no flag was planted. Dependence and dependency form the third pair, and development examiners guard it jealously: dependence is a condition, a relationship of reliance any economy may exhibit; dependency is the theory that the condition is produced and reproduced by the relationship itself. Neo-colonialism must be kept from doing duty as a mere synonym for influence: it names a specific condition of formal sovereignty with continued external direction, and its force as a concept depends on the contrast with the direct rule it succeeded. Finance capital and monopoly capital, finally, are cousins rather than twins: Hilferding's term names the fusion of bank and industrial capital, while the later American usage names the stage's dominant firms and their surplus problem, and a candidate who knows which writer owns which term displays the located knowledge markers credit. Five pairs, ten terms, each distinction a small deposit of precision, and precision, compounded across an answer, is what a band descriptor means by command of the material.

## Civil service optionals and eligibility tests: the structured answer

The fifth examination room has conventions of its own, and candidates transferring from university papers underestimate them at their cost. In civil service examinations, where political science optionals examine imperialism and neo-colonialism as named territory, and in national eligibility tests for teaching, the valued performance is the structured answer: a visible architecture of introduction, ordered body, and conclusion, even-handed in tone, secure in its textbook facts, and complete in its coverage of the question's parts. Brilliance that wanders is marked below competence that organises, because the assessment is certifying, among other things, the candidate's fitness to present a subject to others, in a classroom or an administration, without distortion. The techniques of this guide transfer directly, with adjustments of emphasis. The stage and empire opening becomes the introduction's defining sentence. The roster becomes a coverage obligation: an answer naming only Lenin reads, in this room, not as an error of sophistication but as an omission of syllabus. The scorecard becomes the balanced conclusion the room prizes, provided the weighing is shown rather than the scales merely described. And the protocol on Marx's colonial writings becomes, if anything, more important, since questions on the founder's views of India are a fixture of this room's tradition and are set by examiners who have read the 1853 articles entire. The candidate should also respect the room's preference for the impersonal register: arguments carried by the material rather than by the candidate's personality, first person used sparingly or not at all, and no contemporary controversy imported into a paper whose questions were framed to test durable knowledge. What must not be imported from the university rooms is the habit of treating structure as scaffolding to be kicked away; in this room, structure is the performance.

Eligibility tests add one further convention worth naming: they are frequently taken by candidates who will teach the topic, and their questions on it carry a pedagogical shadow, asking not only what the theory holds but how the field's disputes stand. The candidate who can present the ultra-imperialism quarrel, the self-determination quarrel, and the necessity quarrel as the tradition's internal syllabus, the arguments through which a student best learns it, is answering the shadow question inside the literal one. That is the deepest version of placement this guide teaches, and it is fitting that the room certifying teachers should reward it most.

## The clock: managing time inside the answer

Every technique in this guide assumes a resource the examination strictly rations, and a section on the rationing belongs in any honest guide. The governing principle is that time follows the marks, and the marks, on this topic, sit disproportionately in the operations candidates perform last and rush: evaluation, comparison, the sorting of evidence. The commonest temporal accident is the front-loaded answer, in which exposition swells to fill the first two thirds of the allowance because exposition is the part the candidate can write without deciding anything, and the judgement the question purchased is compressed into a breathless coda. The matrix disciplines the accident at the planning stage: a candidate who has decided, before writing, the movements an answer requires can budget lines to movements, reserving the evaluation's share in advance rather than discovering its absence at the end. A second accident is the unplanned question, the one whose type the candidate did not rehearse, met by an improvised structure that wanders. The remedy is the classification habit itself: ninety seconds spent assigning the question to a matrix row, or recognising it as a hybrid of two rows, buys the structure the improvisation lacks. A third accident is quotation and example sprawl, the answer that keeps finding one more exhibit; the dose discipline of the evidence sections, one attached episode per movement, is also a time discipline. And the final accident is the unfinished conclusion, the judgement gestured at as the invigilator calls time. Candidates should know that an answer's conclusion can be drafted, in skeleton, during the planning minutes and held in reserve, so that even an answer overtaken by the clock can end with its judgement stated, briefly and completely, rather than trailing into the margin. None of this is glamorous. All of it is the difference, repeated across cohorts, between knowledge that scores and knowledge that merely attends.

## What examiners' reports complain about

Subjects accumulate a public memory in the reports examiners publish after each season, and the complaints on this topic are so consistent that a candidate can revise against them directly. The reports complain, first, of the single-theory answer, the pamphlet recited as a tradition. They complain of assertion without location, theories attributed to the tradition in general, criticisms credited to critics in general, in scripts where no writer is named doing anything in particular. They complain of evaluation by adjective, answers in which theories are called simplistic, outdated, or one-sided without the claim being specified that earns the adjective. They complain of the evidence gesture, the wave at history, decolonisation said to have shown something without the something being stated. They complain of the unexamined presupposition, questions asking whether a theory explains a war answered without asking which of the theory's claims about war is in play. And they complain, with a weariness that crosses decades, of the answer to the question not set, the prepared essay discharged at a neighbouring wording. Set against the complaints, the same reports record what earns their praise, and the praised features are exactly this guide's contents: the opening that distinguishes, the roster deployed with mechanisms, the critic named with the argument, the concession located inside a framework, the judgement accompanied by its conditions. A candidate need not take this guide's word for its method. The method is a transcription of the marking community's own published account of what it rewards, applied to the topic where the rewards are most reliably on offer.

## The located reference, practised

A phrase used earlier in this guide deserves its own drill, because it names the cheapest mark-bearing habit available. A located reference ties a claim to a specific text, a specific controversy, or a specific episode, with enough coordinates that a marker can verify the candidate's possession of it. Its opposite is the floating generalisation, the theory holds that, critics argue that, which may be true and is worth almost nothing, since it could have been written by a candidate who had read only a summary of a summary. Locating costs little: a name and a date, a work and its argument's occasion, an episode and the claim it tests. The Lenin and Kautsky quarrel, conducted in print during a world war over whether the belligerents could combine, is located. The confidential communication on Ireland, in which a theorist instructs his own organisation that the lever must be applied in the colony rather than the metropole, is located. The investment statistics' geography, pressed by a named historian against the classical map, is located. An answer salted with four or five such references reads as the work of a candidate who has been inside the literature, and markers, who are readers before they are graders, respond to that reading experience in the only currency they control.

The drill for revision is to build a small stock of located references deliberately, two per matrix row, chosen because they serve the row's evaluation move. For the explanation row, the pamphlet war over ultra-imperialism. For the evaluation row, the archive-based geography objection and the long peace among the advanced powers. For the comparison row, Hobson's remedy of redistribution, which exposes the mechanism difference in a single policy. For the colonial writings row, the Bernier inheritance behind the village picture and the confidential communication behind the reversal. For the decolonisation row, Nkrumah's naming of the successor condition within a decade of the first independence he led. Ten references, each a sentence in the room, and the candidate who commands them will find that the answers built around them acquire the texture markers describe as scholarly. Texture is not a decorative quality. It is the visible residue of knowledge organised at the level where claims can be checked, and on a topic whose popular treatment is all slogan, texture is the surest discriminator the examination possesses.

## Answering the question nobody revised for

However complete the preparation, papers contain the question from the unrevised corner, and the guide owes candidates a procedure for it, since panic writes worse answers than ignorance does. The procedure begins with the classification discipline: the unrevised question is almost never a new species, only a new specimen, and the matrix's rows are species. A question joining imperialism to the outbreak of the First World War is the evaluation row wearing a case; a question joining it to the mandate system or to a particular colony's economy is the explanation row with an evidence demand; a question asking whether the theory survives its century is the scorecard in a single sentence. Assign the specimen to its row and the row's theories, evaluation, and trap become available at once. The second step is to inventory honestly: thirty seconds listing what can actually be stated, mechanisms, quarrels, located references, against the row's requirements, accepting that the answer will be thinner than a rehearsed one and resolving that it will be thinner in coverage rather than in structure. A short answer with the opening distinction, two theories properly mechanised, one evaluation with a name attached, and a stated judgement outperforms a long answer that wanders the whole topic hoping to bump into relevance. The third step is to let the question's own wording supply the organisation: its nouns become the answer's movements, its verb the answer's operation. Candidates consistently underrate how much structure a well-set question donates to a candidate willing to accept the donation. Panic is the refusal of the donation, the insistence on writing the prepared essay instead, and the matrix's final service is to make the donation acceptable by giving the candidate a standing method independent of the topic's corner from which the question came.

## The voices the founders did not hear

One further body of material raises answers on this topic from competent to distinguished, and it concerns a silence. The classical literature theorised the colonised world voluminously and consulted it barely; the colonised appear as terrain, as markets, as labour, as the outside that accumulation required, and almost never as theorists of their own condition. The twentieth century corrected the silence from the other side, and candidates should know the correction's outline. The anti-colonial movements produced their own analysts, of whom the cluster's case studies treat the most consequential, and their writings reversed the telescope with a completeness the founders never attempted: where the classical writers asked what expansion did for the expanding system, the colonised writers asked what it did to the societies it entered, and several of them, Nkrumah among them, wrote as heads of the states the theory had treated as objects. Later scholarship, including the postcolonial literature the neighbouring cluster articles examine, made the silence itself an object of study, asking what it meant that a tradition devoted to emancipation had so long theorised half the world in the third person. An examination answer need not adopt any verdict on that question to profit from knowing it exists. The profit is positional: the candidate who can note, in an evaluation, that the tradition's own subjects eventually joined its authorship, and that the joining changed the questions the tradition asked, has identified the deepest revision the theory underwent, deeper than any correction of its economics. Markers notice the move because it demonstrates that the candidate sees the literature as a historical event and not merely as a list of positions, and historical sight, on this topic above all, is what the higher bands are built to detect.

## Reading the question's presuppositions

Set questions smuggle assumptions, and part of what the higher bands reward is the candidate's detection of the contraband. This topic's questions carry characteristic loads. A question asking why the Marxist theory failed presupposes a unity, one theory, and a verdict, failure, and the strong answer accepts the invitation to evaluate while declining both presuppositions, grading claim by claim and remarking, lightly, that the tradition's plurality complicates any single obituary. A question asking whether decolonisation ended imperialism presupposes that the term has one sense, and the answer that opens by separating the senses has not evaded the question but answered it at the level where it can be answered. A question asking candidates to assess Marx's defence of empire presupposes a defence that the record, read entire, does not straightforwardly contain, and the answer that says so, before running the protocol's sequence, has performed the most valuable service a marker can witness: it has corrected the examination's framing with evidence rather than with attitude. The technique is uniform and must be used with courtesy. Detect the presupposition, name it in a clause, show the record that complicates it, and then answer the question as corrected, at full length and in good faith. Candidates sometimes fear that correcting a question reads as impertinence. In the marking of contested topics it reads as the opposite, provided the correction is earned by material the marker recognises, and provided the corrected question is then answered rather than merely scolded. The line between the candidate who argues with the paper and the candidate who argues with the question is the line between refusing the task and refining it, and it is drawn exactly where the evidence is.

## The war that examined the theory first

Before any examiner tested the classical accounts, events did, and the First World War remains the single most instructive case in the topic because the theorists wrote in its shadow and argued about its meaning while it burned. The war arrived, for the stage theorists, as a ghastly confirmation: the powers had partitioned the world, rearmed on the proceeds of monopoly and finance, and fallen on one another over the redivision, exactly as the literature's logic required. Bukharin systematised the reading even as the guns fired, and Lenin's pamphlet is incomprehensible outside the war's context, since it was written as an intervention in the socialist movement's collapse, an explanation of why the international solidarity of the working class had shattered along national lines in August of the war's first year. The candidate should know that the pamphlet's occasion is part of its content: it is a theory of imperialism written to explain a betrayal, the failure of the parties of the Second International to oppose their own governments' war, and its doctrine of the labour aristocracy, the stratum of workers bribed by colonial super-profits into loyalty, exists to answer that political question. The companion article on the labour aristocracy thesis in this cluster examines that doctrine in full, and the examination point here is the general one: theories in this literature were weapons before they were textbooks, and knowing what each was written to do sharpens the statement of what it claims.

The war also administered the first refutation, in the same movement with which it supplied the confirmation. The peace that followed did not stabilise the partition; the victors' empires reached their greatest extent and began, within a generation, to dissolve; the second general war, when it came, aligned the powers in configurations the partition logic strained to explain, fascist states against a coalition spanning capitalist democracies and the Soviet Union; and the long peace among the advanced capitalist states that followed the second war has already been entered in the scorecard. A candidate narrating the war should therefore give it both entries, confirmation in its outbreak's anatomy and refutation in its sequel's, because the war is the topic in miniature: every large event in it supports one claim of the tradition and embarrasses another, and the sorting, never the event itself, is where the marks are. History candidates in particular should note the methodological moral, since their discipline's questions return to the war from every angle the syllabus affords: evidence on this topic is not ammunition for a side but a test rig for specified claims, and the candidate who treats it as a rig, bolting each claim down and running the current of the record through it, writes the answers the topic's examiners, in all its rooms, have been describing in their reports for as long as the topic has been set.

## A worked comparison: the liberal and the canonical account under interrogation

The comparison section above prescribed a method; this one submits to it, running the liberal founder and the canonical Marxist through the guide's four interrogations at the depth a top answer requires, so that candidates can see the grain of the writing the method produces. The first interrogation asks what drives expansion. For Hobson, the driver is a flow of unspendable income: the concentration of earnings at the top of a maldistributed economy leaves the rich with savings they cannot consume and industry with goods the poor cannot buy, and the surplus seeks employment abroad, dragging policy behind it through the influence of the interests that place it. For Lenin, the driver is a stage of organisation: competition has matured into monopoly, banks have merged with industry, and the combined blocs require outlets for capital, exclusive sources of material, and territories reserved against rivals, as a condition of their continued accumulation. The mechanisms share a surface, money moving outward, and differ in everything beneath it: one's surplus is an accident of distribution, curable by politics; the other's is a property of the system's maturity, curable only by the system's supersession.

The second interrogation asks who benefits. Hobson's answer is a sociology of capture: a confederacy of interests, investors, armaments contractors, exporters to protected markets, colonial administrators and the press that serves them, profits from a policy the nation funds and bleeds for, which is why his book is an appeal to the nation's real interest against its parasites. Lenin's answer declines the national frame: benefit accrues to the monopoly blocs and, through them, in crumbs and careers, to a privileged stratum of the metropolitan working class itself, which is why appeals to the national interest are, in his analysis, part of the mechanism rather than a remedy for it. The third interrogation asks after the cure. Hobson prescribes social reform, the redistribution that would raise working-class consumption, absorb the surplus at home, and retire the taproot; his imperialism is a policy that a better politics can end. Lenin prescribes nothing within the system's terms, because on his account there is nothing within those terms to prescribe: the stage can no more renounce expansion than the competitive stage could renounce competition. The fourth interrogation asks what evidence would embarrass each account, and here the comparison turns examinable gold. Hobson's is embarrassed by expansion that precedes the surplus or proceeds without it, by annexations whose economics his mechanism cannot reach; the free trade imperialism finding, expansion by a state ideologically committed against the interests he indicted, was pressed on exactly that seam. Lenin's is embarrassed by the geography of investment and by the peace of the advanced powers, the scorecard's standing debits. A comparison answer that walks the two accounts through the four interrogations, in this order, with this specificity, has done everything a compare command purchases, and its conclusion, wherever it lands, has been earned in public, sentence by sentence. That publicity of earning is what distinguishes the band. Markers do not in the end reward conclusions; they reward conclusions whose cost of production is visible, because visible cost is the only reliable proxy, in a timed script, for thought.

## How this topic entered the examination room

A short genealogy steadies candidates who wonder why a literature of wartime pamphlets occupies so much syllabus territory. The theory of imperialism entered higher education on the back of the century's events: the expansion of the universities after the Second World War, the arrival of students from the decolonising world in metropolitan classrooms, and the wars of liberation that made the classical questions urgent to a generation of teachers who had themselves been formed by them. Politics syllabuses installed the Marxist accounts as the standing alternative to liberal and realist common sense; history syllabuses installed the debate as the organising controversy of the age of empire; development studies, born in the decades of independence, installed dependency as part of its founding equipment. The topic's examination habits were set in those decades and retain their marks: the premium on comparison, the insistence on the primary texts, the recurring question about the founder's journalism. Knowing the genealogy is not itself examinable, but it explains the rooms' furniture to the candidate who must perform in them, and it explains a feature of the marking that candidates otherwise misread as eccentricity: the examiners' tenderness for the sequence on Marx's colonial writings. That sequence is treasured because it rehearses, in miniature, the intellectual history of the disciplines themselves, which began by inheriting the founder's premises about the non-European world and spent fifty years, in company with scholars from that world, examining the inheritance. The candidate who runs the sequence well is demonstrating membership in that ongoing examination, and the marks follow the membership.

## The distinctions examiners probe: six mechanisms under pressure

The roster section stated each mechanism in two sentences. Examiners, however, do not test the two sentences; they test the joints between them, the points where one mechanism can be confused with its neighbour. This section works the joints. It is not a second exposition of the theories, which the content guide supplies in full, but a set of discrimination drills: for each writer, the confusion the writer attracts, and the sentence that dissolves it.

Hobson attracts the confusion of being read as a Marxist manqué, a socialist before the fact. The dissolution is his remedy. A writer who believes the disease is maldistribution, and prescribes redistribution as the cure, is conducting an argument inside the liberal theory of the state: the state is an instrument that interests capture and citizens can recapture. Everything in Hobson follows from that location, his faith in publicity, his appeal to the national interest, his insistence that the confederacy of interests is a fraction and not the system. The candidate who can state why Hobson's politics follow from his mechanism, rather than merely accompanying it, owns the liberal pole of every comparison question on the paper.

Hilferding attracts the confusion of being read as Lenin with footnotes, since the canonical account absorbed so much of his construction. The dissolution is his setting. Finance capital fuses banks and industry within national economies protected by tariffs, and the protected national bloc, not the world market, is his unit; his imperialism is therefore an account of blocs organising territory as exclusive hinterlands, with the state as the bloc's political form. Where Lenin's pamphlet is a wartime anatomy of a world already partitioned, Hilferding's book is a pre-war anatomy of a capitalism reorganising itself in national fortresses, and the candidate who keeps the settings distinct can explain, what the confused answer cannot, why the tradition's theory of the state differs between them.

Luxemburg attracts the gravest confusion, because her mechanism is the most demanding and the most often caricatured as a mere prophecy of doom. The dissolution is her problem. The accumulation argument begins from a technical question inside Marx's reproduction schemes: in a society of only workers and capitalists, who buys the portion of the social product in which surplus value is embodied, given that workers cannot and capitalists, qua accumulators, will not consume it all? Her answer, that realisation requires buyers outside the capitalist relation, peasants, artisans, colonies, and that the system's expansion is the continuous, violent incorporation of that outside, makes imperialism the expression of an accounting necessity rather than of anyone's greed or strategy. The criticisms her solution attracted, from the reproduction schemes' defenders who showed a closed system balancing on paper, belong in the candidate's kit beside the mechanism, since the examiners who set Luxemburg know the objection is part of her literature. State the problem, state the answer, state the objection: three sentences, and the most caricatured theory in the roster has been handled at the level its examiners respect.

Bukharin attracts the confusion of invisibility, absorbed into Lenin's shadow though his book supplied the canonical account's architecture. The dissolution is his pair of tendencies. Capital, on Bukharin's analysis, moves simultaneously toward national organisation, state-supported blocs eliminating competition within their borders, and toward international entanglement, a world economy binding the blocs in mutual dependence, and imperialism is the friction of those two movements: competition suppressed inside the bloc reappears between blocs, armed. The pair gives candidates a diagnostic instrument the five features do not: any episode can be interrogated for which tendency it expresses, and the post-war order, blocs entangled and rivalry managed, can be described in his vocabulary with a precision that earns credit in the international relations room especially.

Lenin attracts the confusion of being treated as the tradition's origin rather than its codifier, and the dissolution is his sources, worn openly in the pamphlet's own pages: Hobson's data and diagnosis, Hilferding's finance, Bukharin's system, marshalled for a polemic about a movement's collapse. The candidate who states the debts does not diminish the pamphlet; the pamphlet's power was synthetic and political, and its five features were designed as a definition for combat, which is why they enumerate rather than argue. Examiners probing the joint ask why the features read as a list, and the answer, that the text was built to fix a definition against opponents who dissolved the phenomenon into policy errors, demonstrates the located knowledge this guide has prized throughout. Kautsky, finally, attracts the confusion of being read as the roster's fool, the man who predicted harmony and was refuted by trenches. The dissolution is the century's second half. His ultra-imperialism was a claim about a possible phase, cartelisation among capitals extended to the world, and the candidate who treats it as a phase claim rather than a prophecy can set it against the post-war decades with the seriousness its partial vindication deserves, while noting, against it, that the phase rested on a hegemonic foundation no cartel of equals supplied. Between them, the six drills cover the joints at which scripts on this topic are separated into bands, and a revision programme that works them, one confusion and its dissolution at a time, converts the roster from a memory feat into an instrument.

## The scorecard, itemised

The evaluate section taught the scorecard's method. This section files its entries, claim by claim, at the granularity an assess answer needs, because vague verdicts on large claims are the precise failure the method exists to prevent. Each entry states the claim, the record brought against it, and the grade a candidate can defend.

The claim of monopoly: that competition gives way, at the stage in question, to the dominance of giant firms and combines. The record is the strongest in the file. Concentration proceeded across the following century in industry, retail, finance, and eventually in sectors the founders never saw, and no serious rival account denies the tendency, quarrelling only over its significance. Grade: held, and held so completely that candidates must guard against treating it as a victory for the whole theory, since the claim was the roster's least controversial even among its contemporaries.

The claim of finance: that bank and industrial capital fuse, and that finance thereby becomes an organising power in the system. The record is mixed in an instructive way. The specific institutional form Hilferding described, the German universal bank directing industry from within, did not generalise; the Anglo-American pattern of market finance differed and differs. The underlying claim, that financial institutions and markets acquired directive weight over production and policy, has been confirmed on a scale its author would have found lurid. Grade: held in substance, revised in form, and an ideal scorecard entry precisely because the grade has two parts.

The claim of capital export: that the export of capital eclipses the export of goods and flows toward the colonial world as the stage's characteristic movement. The record is the file's most damaging. Export of capital grew enormously, but its destinations clustered among developed and settler economies; the tropical colonies absorbed a small share of the whole, and the investment that did reach them followed flags and guarantees as often as it summoned them. Fieldhouse built the historiographical objection here, and it stands. Grade: failed in its geography, sustained only in the weaker form that capital export became a major systemic feature, which no rival had doubted.

The claim of partition and rivalry: that the completed division of the world among the powers condemns them to wars of redivision as the system's normal politics. The record divides by period. For the decades around the theory's writing, the claim describes the scramble, the crises, and the catastrophe of the First World War with real power. For the long period since the Second World War, the advanced powers' abstention from war with one another, and their construction of joint institutions, contradict the claim in its strong form, while rivalry displaced into proxy conflicts and economic competition offers it partial shelter. Grade: held for its conjuncture, failed as a permanent law, and the entry teaches candidates why periodisation is an evaluative act and not a courtesy.

The claim of colonial necessity: that the system requires colonies, as outlets, sources, or fields, such that their loss would cripple it. The record is decisive against the strong form: the flags came down and the metropolitan economies entered their fastest sustained growth. The weaker relational form, that the end of rule did not end the transfers or the hierarchy, survives as a live research claim rather than as a demonstrated law. Grade: failed in the form its holders most often asserted, and candidates should say so without flinching, since the flinch is visible.

The claim of the labour aristocracy: that super-profits from the colonies bribe a stratum of the metropolitan working class into loyalty, explaining reformism and the collapse of internationalism. The record is the file's most contested. The mechanism's magnitudes were never established to the standard the contested figures protocol demands, the comparative evidence of working-class politics across empires and non-empires cuts in several directions, and the doctrine's origin as an explanation of a specific political catastrophe should make candidates handle it as a hypothesis with a history rather than as a finding. The companion article on the thesis treats the evidence entire; the scorecard entry is: unproven, and instructive chiefly as the point where the tradition's political needs are most visible inside its economics.

The claim of the outside: Luxemburg's necessity, that accumulation requires a non-capitalist milieu and must falter as the milieu is exhausted. The system's subsequent history, a century of accumulation within an ever more completely capitalist world, tells against the strict form, while the formal criticisms of the realisation argument tell against its logic on paper as well. What survives is the sensibility rather than the theorem: the attention to the violence of incorporation, to primitive accumulation as a continuing process rather than a finished prehistory, which later writers took up under other names. Grade: failed as a mechanism, influential as a sensibility, a distinction candidates should learn to make because several entries in the history of theory share it.

Seven entries, seven different grades. That dispersion is the scorecard's lesson and the evaluate answer's destination: the theory was not a monolith that history passed or failed, but a portfolio of claims of very different quality, and the candidate who grades the portfolio entry by entry has performed the exact operation that distinguishes evaluation from allegiance in either direction.

## Recurring question shapes and their handling

Across the rooms, the topic's questions recur in a handful of shapes whose handlings can be rehearsed as forms. The origin shape asks how the theory arose or what problem it was built to solve, and rewards the candidate who treats the literature as dated interventions: underconsumption diagnosed amid the Boer War's controversies, finance analysed in the tariff blocs' decade, the canonical pamphlet forged in the International's collapse. The essence shape asks what the theory holds, and is the explain row in formal dress. The truth shape asks whether the theory was right, and demands the scorecard, never a referendum. The founder shape asks about Marx's own writings and triggers the protocol sequence. The survival shape asks whether the theory remains useful or has been overtaken, and its handling is the scorecard compressed, concluded by the relocation story: claims that died with their conjuncture, claims that migrated into dependency and the study of production chains, claims awaiting a record that would revive them. The comparison shape sets the family against a named rival, liberalism usually, realism in the theory room, modernisation theory in the development room, and is handled by interrogation. The application shape, rarest and most dangerous, invites the candidate to bring the theory to a case, and its handling consists of choosing the case the theory fits best, an episode from the classical conjuncture rather than from the headlines, and stating the fit's limits before the marker can. Anyone who can assign any set wording to its shape within a minute owns the paper's structure before writing a word of its content, and ownership of structure, under time pressure, is indistinguishable from calm.

## Introductions and conclusions: the architecture of trust

Markers form their expectations early and confirm them late, which makes the opening and closing movements of an answer disproportionately valuable and worth separate instruction. The introduction's duties on this topic are three: perform the governing distinction, whether the stage and empire separation or the classification the question requires; forecast the answer's method in a sentence, the scorecard, the interrogation, the sequence; and withhold everything else. Introductions fail by bulk, the candidate unloading context, biography, and the century's whole history before the question is touched, and they fail by throat-clearing, the announced intention to discuss various aspects. A four-sentence introduction that distinguishes, forecasts, and stops outperforms a page of panorama in every room, because it demonstrates the two qualities the whole script will be scanned for: command of the question and economy of means.

The conclusion's duties are the mirror set. It must land the judgement the command word purchased, at the level of differentiation the body earned: not the theory was partly right, which is a shrug, but the specified distribution of grades, the prophecy retired, the anatomy retained, the relation still contested. It must avoid imports, since new evidence or a new critic in the final paragraph reads as material the candidate could not place, which is a confession about the planning. And it should close with the revision condition where the command was evaluative, the sentence that states what would change the judgement, because that sentence performs the intellectual virtue the entire assessment is built to certify and leaves the marker with it ringing. Candidates underrate conclusions because they write them tired; the remedy, given in the time section, is to draft the conclusion's skeleton in the planning minutes, when the mind is fresh and the judgement already formed by the matrix, and to spend the body's writing earning the right to deliver it.

## Paraphrase: the candidate's own voice as evidence

A final craft matter pervades all the others. Scripts on this topic divide audibly into those written in the candidate's own voice and those ventriloquising a textbook, and markers reward the former because paraphrase is comprehension made visible. The candidate who renders Hilferding's fusion of bank and industry in fresh sentences, choosing new verbs and a new order, demonstrates possession of the mechanism; the candidate who reproduces the summary's phrasing demonstrates possession of the summary. Revision should therefore practise translation: take each mechanism and say it three ways, in the vocabulary of the pamphlet, in plain modern prose, and in the vocabulary of the examination room it will be used in. The drill feels redundant and is anything but, since under pressure the mind reaches for the formulation practised most thoroughly, and a candidate with three formulations can fit the answer to the question's wording instead of bending the question to the answer's. The same drill disciplines the handling of the tradition's own terms of art. Stage, finance capital, realisation, partition, relation: each such term should be usable inside a defining clause the first time it appears in an answer, so that no marker is asked to take the candidate's vocabulary on credit. Answers written in a voice, with terms defined in passing and mechanisms owned rather than quoted, are the scripts examiners describe as authoritative, and authority, on a topic where every candidate has read the same summaries, is the scarcest and most rewarded commodity in the room.

## The economics underneath: surplus, realisation, and terms candidates misuse

Beneath the examination technique sits a small core of economic reasoning that candidates are assumed to command and frequently do not, and its repair is high-yield revision because markers probe it whenever an answer leans on it. Three notions do most of the work. The first is surplus in its several costumes: Hobson's unspendable income, the surplus value of the Marxist accounts, Baran's economic surplus as the gap between what an economy produces and what it needs to consume. The costumes differ in definition and in remedy, and a candidate who slides between them mid-answer produces the appearance of an argument where none exists. The discipline is to declare the costume in use: whose surplus, defined how, doing what work in the mechanism being stated. The second notion is realisation, the conversion of produced value into money through sale, which candidates invoke as a slogan wherever Luxemburg is mentioned. Realisation is a problem about buyers: the product must be purchased by someone whose money does not itself originate in the same circuit, or the circuit must be shown to close by other means, and the entire Luxemburg controversy, her outside, her critics' schemes, turns on whether the circuit can close. An answer that can state the problem in that plain form owns the theory; an answer that repeats that capitalism needs markets owns a postcard of it. The third notion is the rate and direction of flow: capital export is a flow, measured over time, toward destinations, and nearly every empirical dispute in the file, Fieldhouse's geography, the colonial necessity question, the dependency transfers, is a dispute about flows and their directions dressed in theoretical costume. Candidates who habitually ask of any claim in this literature, what is said to flow, from where, to where, drawn by what, will find the claims sorting themselves into the testable and the rhetorical with gratifying speed. The question is the economics underneath the whole topic compressed into a habit, and habits, under examination pressure, are worth more than knowledge.

A fourth notion, uneven development, deserves its sentence because the tradition's later life runs through it. The claim that capitalism develops its regions unevenly, and that the unevenness is produced by the development itself rather than inherited from before it, is the bridge between the classical file and the dependency literature, and between both and the study of production chains with which the cluster closes. Candidates meeting the phrase should be able to give it that bridging work to do rather than treating it as an incantation, and should know its corollary, that backwardness in this literature is a relationship and not a stage of lateness, the single idea for which the periphery's scholars most changed the tradition that theorised them.

## Modernisation theory: the third party in the development room

Development papers are rarely two-sided, and candidates who arrive expecting a duel between Marxist and liberal accounts meet a third party they have not revised. Modernisation theory, the post-war doctrine associated with Walt Rostow's stages of growth and with the sociologists of tradition and modernity, held that underdevelopment was an original condition, a society's earliness on a path the developed world had already travelled, and that the remedies were capital transfer, technology, and the diffusion of modern values and institutions. Its examination significance is the precision of its opposition to the dependency claim. Where dependency holds that development and underdevelopment are twins, produced together by one process, modernisation holds that they are ages, separated by time and traversable by effort and assistance. The two claims cannot both be true of the same relationship, and the development room's favourite questions are engineered on exactly that fault line. The candidate's handling follows this guide's standing method: state each claim in its strongest form, locate the evidence each side marshals, the modernisers pointing to societies that did traverse the path and to the transfers that financed the traversal, the dependency writers pointing to the deterioration of the primary exporters' terms and the enclave character of the growth the transfers bought, and grade by specification, asking of each society in question which pattern its record fits. Modernisation theory also supplies the candidate with a useful humility exhibit, since its confidence that the path was known and short is itself now a historical artefact, and saying so, with the same even hand applied to every school in this guide, demonstrates the disposition the whole assessment rewards: all the literatures in the room have a record, and all of them are graded against it.

## The ideological register: what the theories say about justification

One dimension of the topic is systematically under-revised because it belongs to no room's centre, and it earns marks in all of them precisely for that reason: the ideological register, the set of claims the literature makes about how expansion was justified to those who carried it out and those who paid for it. The classical writers were not silent here. Hobson's analysis includes an account of the press and the manufactured enthusiasms that reconciled a taxpaying public to policies against its interest. Lenin's pamphlet anatomises the ideology of the stage, the union of monopoly with a chauvinism that split the labour movement, and his labour aristocracy doctrine is in part a theory of how justification is purchased. The colonised writers added the register's sharpest instrument, the analysis of the civilising mission as a functioning fiction, a story the expansion told itself whose falsity was beside the point because its work was done whether or not it was believed. And the historiography of empire has since made the culture of justification a field of its own, examining the exhibitions, the schoolbooks, the adventure fiction, and the racial sciences through which the metropolitan publics were taught to feel their dominion as a duty or a thrill.

The examination uses of the register are three. It deepens the explanation answer, since a theory of expansion that cannot account for its own legitimation is incomplete on its face, and the founders knew it. It sharpens the assessment of the founder's writings, because the man who dissected the ideologies of his age in his journalism while harbouring the prejudices the correspondence records presents the sequence problem in its most concentrated form, and a candidate who can hold that concentration steady, neither dissolving the analysis into the prejudice nor the prejudice into the analysis, has done the hardest thing the topic sets. And it arms the comparison answer, for the liberal and Marxist accounts diverge here as cleanly as on mechanism: the liberal treats the ideology as a fraud practised by the interested on the public, corrigible by exposure, while the Marxist treats it as a necessary appearance of the structure, regenerated as fast as it is exposed, which is why exposure alone has never retired it. A paragraph on the register, placed where a question's wording opens the door, is among the most efficient band-lifters available to a prepared candidate, and its efficiency is explained by its rarity in scripts.

## Answering with the archive: three kinds of document

History candidates, and candidates in any room whose question turns on evidence, benefit from a small typology of the documents this topic's arguments actually rest on, since naming the kind of evidence in play is itself a creditable act. The first kind is the programmatic text: pamphlets, party resolutions, the confidential communications of organisations, in which actors state what they intend and believe. Such texts evidence intention and self-understanding superbly and outcomes not at all, and the candidate who cites Lenin's definition as evidence of what the theory claimed, rather than of what the world did, is using the kind correctly. The second kind is the administrative record: the correspondence of colonial offices, the proceedings of conferences, the reports of commissions, in which the machinery of rule described itself. This record evidences motive as the machinery wished it minuted, which the free trade imperialism debate taught historians to read against the grain, comparing the minute with the movement of forces the minute omits. The third kind is the statistical series: the trade returns, investment estimates, and price indices on which the geography and terms of trade disputes turn. Series evidence aggregates and direction superbly and mechanism not at all, and it carries the provenance burdens the figures protocol describes. An answer that moves among the three kinds knowingly, drawing claims about belief from the first, about conduct from the second read sceptically, and about pattern from the third read cautiously, performs the historian's craft in a form any marker in any room can recognise, because the craft is the same craft the scholars in the file practised on one another. Fieldhouse read the administrative record against the programmatic claims; the dependency statisticians read the series against the modernisers' programmes; the whole literature is an argument among archives, and the candidate who joins it at that level has stopped revising a topic and started practising a discipline.

## The interwar laboratory: mandates and the theory between the wars

Between the classical moment and the post-war revisions lies a period candidates skip, to their cost, because the interwar decades are where the theory's claims met their first sustained administrative test. The settlement of the First World War did not partition the losers' colonies among the victors as annexations, in the manner the classical literature described as the system's reflex. It placed them under mandates, a legal form in which the mandatory power administered territory in trust, at least in the text of the instrument, for the inhabitants' eventual self-rule. The form matters to the examination file in two directions. For the theory's critics, the mandate was evidence that the age of annexation had ended, that international society had begun, however hypocritically, to juridify empire and thereby to concede the principle that would dissolve it. For the theory's defenders, the mandates were partition in fancy dress, the same territories assigned to the same powers by a procedure whose novelty was the stationery. The candidate who can stage that disagreement, and note that hypocrisy in international forms is itself a datum, since powers do not trouble to disguise what they feel no pressure to justify, possesses an interwar paragraph that lifts any answer touching the period.

The decades also tested the theory's economic file in a laboratory no one designed. The depression of the thirties drove the imperial powers toward closure, imperial preference, blocked currencies, and the systematic organisation of colonial economies for metropolitan need, a movement that looked, to contemporaries across the spectrum, like the stage theory's bloc logic hardening into practice. Then the war that followed scrambled the pattern again: the Atlantic Charter's language of self-determination, issued by powers holding empires, the exhaustion of the European metropoles, the superpowers' rival courtships of the uncommitted world, and the emergence of an international organisation in which the colonised would shortly hold the majority of votes. Within twenty years of the mandates' invention, the system they were meant to stabilise had begun its terminal crisis. The candidate's handling of the interwar should therefore refuse both simple stories, the story in which the theory predicted the closure and the story in which events refuted the theory wholesale, and should instead enter the period in the scorecard under the partition claim, as the decades when the form of rule changed faster than the fact of hierarchy, which is the scorecard's most portable finding and the seed of the neo-colonial argument the independence generation would shortly make in its own name.

## When the question names a writer

A distinct shape of question names a single author and asks for the view, the contribution, or the assessment of that author, and it mishandles itself in a characteristic way: the candidate, relieved to be asked for the one thing memorised, delivers the summary and stops, forfeiting the marks that were always located outside the named figure. The single-author question is a survey question in disguise. Its marks divide among the accurate reconstruction of the named view, which is the minority share, the placement of the view among its rivals and predecessors, and the assessment of the view against its critics and the record. The handling therefore runs: reconstruct, from the mechanism outward, in the space the reconstruction earns and not a line more; place, naming what the author took from the predecessors and what the successors took from the author, since a writer's contribution is a delta and cannot be stated without the field it altered; assess, with the named critics and the scorecard entries that touch the author's distinctive claims. A question on Luxemburg is an invitation to the realisation controversy and the outside's fate; a question on Hobson, to the liberal comparison and the remedy's logic; a question on Kautsky, to the phase claim and its long trial. Candidates who hear the invitation go to where the marks are. Candidates who hear only the name go to where their notes are, and the difference, once again, is the difference between answering the question and answering the revision.

## Depth and breadth: calibrating the survey answer

Every survey topic poses the same allocation problem, and this topic poses it acutely because its roster is long and its examiner generous to breadth. The calibration this guide recommends can be stated as a ratio and defended as a principle. Across a full answer, the majority of the candidate's substance should be distributed as accurate naming: mechanisms stated compactly, quarrels located, critics attached to arguments. A minority, roughly a third, should be concentrated depth: one theory, usually the one the question's wording foregrounds, expounded at the level where its internal joints show, and one controversy worked at the level where its evidence shows. The ratio follows from the mark structure. Breadth establishes that the candidate possesses the literature, without which depth reads as a fragment; depth establishes that the candidate can operate inside a position rather than merely inventory it, without which breadth reads as a catalogue. The errors are the pure forms: the pure survey, six mechanisms at postcard length, which markers describe as superficial with a unanimity that should frighten; and the pure depth answer, one pamphlet in loving detail, which is the single-theory mistake wearing a scholar's clothes. Calibration is also the answer to the candidate's most practical anxiety, the fear that the paper will ask for the corner of the roster least revised. A breadth-first allocation, with depth concentrated where the question points, converts an unrevised corner from a catastrophe into a thin paragraph inside a structure that still earns its operations' marks. The matrix was built for exactly this conversion, and candidates who trust it discover that the roster's length, which frightened them in October, has become by the examination the topic's chief kindness: there is always another name to deploy, another quarrel to stage, another claim to grade, and an answer so supplied does not run dry.

## Liberation movements as a test the theory set itself

The classical theory implied a forecast about the colonised that candidates rarely extract, and extracting it completes the scorecard's coverage. If the stage's expansion incorporated the non-capitalist world by violence and administration, the incorporated world would eventually answer, and the tradition's writers disagreed, instructively, about the form the answer would take and the agency that would carry it. Lenin's wartime writings recast the national movements of the oppressed peoples as a reserve and an ally of the metropolitan revolution, a revaluation completed in the theses on the national and colonial questions debated at the International's second congress, where the Indian delegate M. N. Roy pressed the assembly, against its European instincts, to take the colonial revolution as a force in its own right rather than as a sideshow of Europe's drama. The exchange matters beyond its occasion. It is the moment the theory's object began, institutionally, to talk back inside the theory's own forum, and the line runs from that congress through the anti-colonial case studies in this cluster to the independence generation's authorship of neo-colonialism. The forecast embedded in the exchange, that the colonial world would become an independent variable in world politics rather than a prize within it, is among the tradition's best predictions, and candidates should enter it in the scorecard's credit column with the same exactness they apply to the debits: held, and held against the instincts of many of the theory's own holders.

The assessment of the movements' subsequent record belongs to the companion case studies, where the one-party states, the economic difficulties, and the violence of several post-independence orders are stated plainly, and the examination use here is confined to the methodological point the record enforces. A theory may predict a force's arrival and misjudge everything about the force's conduct once arrived, and the scorecard that grades arrival and conduct as separate entries will always outperform the verdict that merges them. Candidates are prone to the merger in both directions, crediting the tradition with the movements' victories as though victory validated the whole file, or charging it with the successor regimes' failures as though the founders had drafted their constitutions. Both moves are the unspecified refutation in new dress. The founders predicted an agency; the agency arrived and behaved as agencies do, variously, under pressures the theory described in part and missed in part; and the candidate who reports that distribution, movement by movement and claim by claim, has understood the deepest habit this guide teaches, which is that intellectual honesty on contested subjects is not a midpoint between partisans but a finer granularity than either partisan can afford.

## The final checklist, in prose

Candidates like a checklist, and this guide, having forbidden itself the list form on principle throughout the series, will supply the substance in sentences. Before the examination, confirm that the roster can be produced from memory with a mechanism apiece and one quarrel among any two holders. Confirm that the scorecard's seven entries can be graded aloud, each with its evidence and its grade, in under five minutes. Confirm that the protocol sequence for the founder's writings can be run without notes: position, premises, change, correspondence, each in its order and its register. Confirm that the matrix can be redrawn blank and filled, and that its trap column can be recited as a litany of refusals: not one theory, not two literatures fused, not a verdict on a man in place of a record, not an event asked to falsify everything, not a number without a name and a method behind it. In the room, classify the question before composing, spend the planning minutes on movements rather than on sentences, draft the conclusion's skeleton while the judgement is fresh, and reserve the evaluation's lines in advance against the exposition's appetite. While writing, define each term of art in a clause at its first appearance, attach every criticism to its critic, and let every example arrive with the sentence that states what it shows. At the close, land the judgement the verb purchased, state the condition that would revise it, and stop. A candidate who can perform that litany has converted a contested literature into a method, which is the whole of what this guide, and the cluster it closes, set out to teach.

## The state inside the theory

Politics papers probe one joint more reliably than any other, and it deserves extraction from the sections where it has appeared in passing: the place of the state in the classical accounts. The theories divide over it in ways candidates can state as a spectrum. At one end, the state in Hobson is an instrument lying ready to hand, captured by the confederacy of interests and recapturable by an aroused public, which is why his book is a work of democratic exhortation. In the middle, the state in Hilferding is the political form of the national bloc itself, no longer an instrument distinct from the capital it serves but the bloc's own organ, raising tariffs, building fleets, and reserving territory as internal functions of the fused structure. At the far end, the dependency and world-systems writers dissolve the metropolitan state into the world economy's stratification, interesting chiefly as the agent that polices a position in the hierarchy. The spectrum gives the politics candidate a comparison axis orthogonal to the mechanism axis, and the two axes together generate the discriminating questions examiners actually ask: whether a writer's state can act against the dominant economic interest, whether it can arbitrate among capitals, whether its foreign policy has any autonomy from the flows it guards. Later debates inside the tradition, over the state's relative autonomy and its role in managing the system's collective interests against individual capitals, refined the middle position without abandoning it, and a candidate who knows that the refinement exists, and that it was driven by the spectacle of states disciplining their own champions in the system's name, can handle the state question at whatever depth any room requires. The practical payoff is a sentence pattern worth rehearsing: in this writer, the state is an instrument, a form, or an agent of the structure, and the difference explains the writer's politics, since instruments can be seized, forms must be transformed, and structures must be superseded.

## Race in the classical file

No assessment of this literature is complete, and no examiner on the cluster's territory will treat it as complete, without an account of how race figures in the classical writings, and the honest summary is that it figures largely as a silence surrounded by noise. The noise is the correspondence and journalism already handled under the protocol, the private epithets and the public dismissals of peoples without history. The silence is structural: the classical theories explain expansion by mechanisms, surplus, finance, realisation, in which the racial ordering of the world appears, when it appears at all, as an instrument or an ideology of rule rather than as a constitutive feature of the system being analysed. The correction began early and from inside the wider tradition's reach. W. E. B. Du Bois, writing on the African roots of the Great War while the classical debate was still warm, argued that the scramble for Africa and the racial organisation of labour lay near the war's causes, and that the democratic nations' publics were reconciled to empire by a wage of racial standing paid alongside the wage of labour, an analysis that anticipated by decades the questions later scholarship would put to the whole file. The cluster's articles on Black Marxism and on Fanon treat the correction's development at the length it deserves, through the argument that capitalism did not merely use a pre-existing racial order but was racial in its formation, and through the colonial psychiatry that made the colonised subject's experience an theoretical datum. For the examination, the handling follows the guide's universal method. State the classical position accurately, including its silence. State the correction in its strongest form, attributed to its makers. Grade the exchange by specification: the correction does not disturb the mechanisms' economics, and it permanently alters what an adequate theory of the system must explain, since a theory of a world order that cannot see one of the order's organising principles is incomplete in a way its founders' categories could not register. Candidates who can perform that grading own the most sophisticated evaluation the topic affords, and they own it without a single slogan in either direction.

## Periodisation: when the stage is said to begin

A quiet dispute runs beneath the whole file and surfaces in questions more often than candidates expect: the dispute over when the phenomenon, in the stage sense, begins. The canonical account dates the turn to the opening of the twentieth century, when monopoly and finance achieved dominance. Hobson's materials reach back into the preceding decades, and the historians' scramble begins in the eighteen eighties, while Gallagher and Robinson pushed continuous expansion back to the mid-Victorian decades and dissolved the boundary the periodisation requires. Later writers proposed still other hinges, the long depression of the later nineteenth century, the merger movements, the closing of the world's unclaimed territory. The candidate's handling is to treat the dating as a claim rather than as a fact: each proposed hinge encodes a mechanism, since to date the stage from monopoly is to assert that monopoly is its essence, to date it from the scramble is to assert partition, and to deny the hinge altogether, with the free trade argument, is to assert that expansion is a constant of the system's foreign relations whose instruments merely rotate. A question that asks when imperialism began is therefore asking which theory is true, in disguise, and the answer that unmasks the disguise, surveying the hinges and the mechanisms they encode before adopting one, converts a chronology question into the topic's central theoretical act. This is the stage and empire opening's temporal form, and it obeys the same law: the separation performed first is the analysis, and everything after it is evidence.

## Small print: the facts scripts get wrong

Examiners forgive large disagreements and remember small errors, because small errors locate the boundary of a candidate's acquaintance. A hygiene list for this topic, again in sentences, will repay memorisation. Lenin's pamphlet is subtitled the highest stage of capitalism in its standard English rendering, a translation choice for a Russian word that can also bear the sense of newest or final, and candidates who note the rendering show they have handled the text rather than a poster of it. Hobson was a liberal who influenced socialists, not a socialist, and his book appeared in 1902, before every Marxist entry in the roster; scripts that enrol him in the tradition or date him after Lenin announce themselves. Hilferding's finance capital study appeared in 1910 and Luxemburg's accumulation study in 1913, and the order matters because the canonical account of 1916 answers both. Bukharin's world economy book was composed during the war and published after it, with an introduction by Lenin, a sequence that explains the two men's overlap without recourse to plagiarism in either direction. Kautsky's ultra-imperialism was an article-length intervention, not a book, and its author was the leading theorist of the orthodox Marxism from which Lenin was breaking, which is what gave the quarrel its venom. Marx's India articles were journalism for an American newspaper, written to deadlines for money, and both their brilliance and their evidential thinness are conditioned by that fact, which candidates should state before critics state it for them. The International whose confidential communication carries the Irish lever sentence was the First, and its council sat in London, a small fact that locates the document in the founder's organisational life rather than in a void. None of these facts is an argument. All of them together are the sound of a candidate who has read around the topic's edges, and examiners, who set questions at the edges deliberately, listen for exactly that sound.

## The settler case: colonies that cut across the categories

One class of case embarrasses every simple version of the file and therefore earns the prepared candidate disproportionate credit: the settler colonies. The territories that became Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa, together with the formally independent but financially bound republics of South America, received the great bulk of the era's exported capital, the flow the classical theory directed, in its rhetoric, toward the tropics. They were colonies of settlement, populated substantially by Europeans who displaced or destroyed the peoples found there, and they developed, within the imperial frame, into wealthy economies whose citizens enjoyed metropolitan living standards. The case cuts across the file's categories in every direction. Against the capital export objection, the settler flows show that the geography embarrassment is more subtle than a simple failure: capital did move outward in vast quantities, but toward kin economies integrated by language, law, and migration, which suggests the mechanism was real and its map misdrawn. Against the dependency pattern, the settler economies developed, spectacularly, inside the relationship that was supposed to underdevelop, which forced the later literature to distinguish kinds of incorporation, settlement against extraction, with different consequences. Against the national story, the settler states became imperialists in their own right within a generation, sub-imperial powers with frontiers and subject peoples of their own. A candidate deploying the settler case should deploy it for those discriminations and no other purpose: it is a scalpel for grading claims about flows and development, and its presence in an answer announces that the candidate's evidence was chosen for its argumentative yield rather than for its familiarity. The indigenous catastrophe that settlement entailed belongs in the answer's moral register without decoration and without figures, under the same protocol that governs the rest of the file: the displacement and destruction of the peoples of those continents is a matter of record, its scale described qualitatively, its place in the analysis stated as the foundation on which the settler economies' integration rested.

## The informal empire debate at full strength

The comparison section commended Gallagher and Robinson; the history room rewards the full debate, and it is worth the candidate's while to know its rounds. The opening claim, made in the early fifties, held that the mid-Victorian decades, conventionally read as an anti-imperial interlude between earlier expansion and the scramble, were in fact decades of continuous imperial growth conducted by informal means where possible and formal rule where necessary, so that the periodisation of empire into enthusiasm and reluctance collapsed. The evidence was the relentless expansion of trade, influence, and territory throughout the supposed interlude, and the implication was methodological as much as empirical: the imperialism of a period is measured by its effective dominion, not by the colour on its maps. The counter-attack, pressed by D. C. M. Platt and others, held that the informal empire was a mirage of the archives, that British trade and investment in the supposedly informal dependencies were too small to constitute dominion, and that the governments of the era were innocent of the systematic intentions the thesis required. The refinement, in the gentlemanly capitalism of Cain and Hopkins, relocated the engine from the industrial north to the financial and service wealth of the southeast, arguing that the alliance of landed and City interests drove expansion across both the formal and informal registers for two centuries. The candidate's profit from the debate is threefold. It supplies the strongest available solvent for naive periodisation, in either camp's hands. It models, at the level of professional historiography, the guide's own doctrine that claims must be specified before they can be tested, since the rounds of the debate consist largely of the parties discovering they meant different things by empire. And it arms the comparison answer with a liberal historiography that is nobody's caricature, a living research front rather than a textbook effigy, which is what the strongest comparative writing in every room is built from.

## Short formats: objective questions and the brief structured answer

Not every room asks for essays. Eligibility tests and many civil service preliminaries examine the topic through objective items and short structured answers, and the techniques of this guide compress rather than change. The objective item on this literature tests the same discriminations as the essay, at higher speed and lower mercy: it asks which writer held which mechanism, which work appeared in which order, which claim belongs to which controversy, and it punishes precisely the confusions the discrimination drills addressed, Hobson enrolled among the Marxists, dependency fused with the classical file, the pamphlet mistaken for the tradition. The candidate's preparation is identical in content and different in tempo: the roster and the quarrels must be retrievable as isolated facts, without the scaffolding of an argument to carry them, which is what the matrix, rehearsed by cells, provides. The brief structured answer, the paragraph or page demanded by a part-question, applies the essay method at miniature scale: one distinction or classification in the opening sentence, the mechanism or evidence in the middle, and a closing sentence that states the limit or the grade. Candidates err in short formats by attempting essays in them, trailing off mid-argument when the space closes, or by attempting telegrams, strings of unconnected facts that demonstrate possession without use. The miniature method avoids both: a complete small performance, opening, substance, and judgement, sized to its container. It is worth practising deliberately, since the short answer is where the method's portability is proved, and a candidate who can run the stage and empire distinction in two sentences owns it more completely than one who needs a page.

## The revision calendar: the last month, the last week, the last hour

This guide ends its instruction where candidates actually live, inside a shrinking calendar. In the last month, the work is construction: the roster learned to mechanism depth, the quarrels staged, the scorecard assembled with its evidence, the cluster's articles walked with the matrix in hand, and one timed answer written for each of the two most feared question types, marked against the trap column before any other standard. In the last week, the work is compression: the matrix redrawn from memory until no cell stalls, the openings rehearsed aloud until the stage and empire move can be performed in a state of mild panic, the located references reduced to a card apiece, and the protocol sequence for the founder's writings run daily, since it is the asset most likely to be needed and least likely to be improvised. In the last hour, the work is triage and calm: the matrix reviewed once entire, the five serviceable quotations checked for exactness, the figures protocol recited as a refusal, and then the materials put away, because the final hour's yield lies in arriving with a method settled rather than in acquiring one more fact. Candidates consistently misjudge the calendar's economics, spending the last month re-reading what they know and the last hour cramming what they do not, when the evidence of every examiner's report runs the other way: marks at the margin come from operations rehearsed to fluency, and fluency is a function of the calendar's discipline. The topic helps the disciplined. It is finite, its roster is closed, its quarrels are catalogued, its traps are few and named. Few subjects on any paper repay a month's methodical preparation as reliably, and none repays it more honestly, since the method and the subject, distinctions drawn before judgements, claims graded one by one, are in the end the same discipline wearing two names.

## The pamphlet as a form

The literature under examination was written in a form candidates should notice, because the form explains the texts' virtues and their vices alike. The classical works are interventions: a study written against a war and an empire's apologists, a party theoretician's system built in exile, a wartime pamphlet composed in a Zurich reading room to explain a movement's collapse, a dissent fired off in a journal as the armies mobilised. Intervention writing is compressed, polemical, and organised for combat, which is why its definitions enumerate rather than argue, why its opponents are sometimes effigies, and why its predictions are stated with a confidence the evidence available could not underwrite. Knowing the form disciplines the candidate's judgements in both directions. It forbids the naive dismissal, since a pamphlet that reorganised a century's thinking is not refuted by the observation that it was a pamphlet. And it forbids the naive reverence, since a text built to win an argument in 1916 is not dishonoured by the admission that its list of features was a weapon before it was a finding. The assessment the examiners reward sits exactly between the forbiddens: the text honoured as an act of mind, located in its occasion, and graded as a set of claims against a record its author could not see. Candidates can signal the whole stance in a clause, noting of any classical work that it was written as an intervention and reads as one, and the clause does more evaluative work than many paragraphs, because it tells the marker the candidate knows what kind of object is being graded. Genre is not a garnish on this topic. A literature of pamphlets produced a politics of pamphlets, congresses fought over definitions, movements split over the correct reading of a stage, and the candidate who grasps that the form and the fate are connected has hold of the thread that runs from the reading room in Zurich to the examination room in which the thread is now being pulled.

## Writing about scholars who disagree

A refinement of evaluation remains, and it separates the strongest scripts from the merely strong: the handling of living scholarly disagreement, the places where the file is still open and the authorities in the field stand on both sides. The geography of investment, the weight of the economic motives in the scramble, the standing of the realisation argument, the reach of the informal empire thesis, on each of these the candidate will meet, in any serious reading, credentialed disagreement that no textbook summary can settle. The examination technique for open questions has three moves. Name the parties and their positions with the works attached, since an open question is mapped by its holders and not by its slogans. Identify the evidence or the conceptual distinction on which the disagreement turns, since scholarly quarrels that survive are almost always about a definable point, a series read two ways, a term carrying two objects, and stating the point converts the quarrel from a spectacle into an instrument. And then, if the command word asks for judgement, judge the point rather than the persons, saying which reading of the evidence the candidate finds stronger and what would strengthen the other. The moves protect the candidate from the two failures open questions invite: the pretence that the matter is settled, which any informed marker knows to be false, and the shrug that presents disagreement as a reason to have no view, which the command word forbids. Handled this way, an open question becomes the script's best room, the one where the candidate's performance most closely resembles the discipline's own practice, and markers reward the resemblance because resemblance to practice is the whole of what advanced assessment, in any of this topic's rooms, has ever been able to mean.

## What the topic teaches beyond itself

The cluster closes with a reflection its exam guide is entitled to make, since candidates carry the method out of the room and into every other contested subject they will meet. The operations this guide drilled were never really about imperialism. Separating the senses of a contested term before arguing is the first move in law, in philosophy, and in every empirical science that inherits its vocabulary from ordinary speech. Grading a portfolio of claims one by one, rather than endorsing or damning the portfolio, is the form intellectual honesty takes wherever a tradition, a theory, or a government's record is under judgement. Attaching criticisms to named critics, and claims to located texts, is the difference between participating in a literature and repeating its weather. Refusing figures whose provenance cannot be defended is the beginning of all quantitative conscience. And stating what would change one's judgement is the signature of a mind that holds its conclusions as conclusions rather than as property. The Marxist literature on imperialism happens to be an unusually good gymnasium for these operations, because it is old enough to have a record, plural enough to forbid recitation, and contested enough that no side's slogans survive contact with the file entire. A candidate who has learned the operations here owns them everywhere. That, more than any roster or scorecard, is the preparation this guide was written to give, and it is the reason the cluster's last word belongs to an examination room: the room is where the operations are tested, and the operations are what the testing was always for.

## The metropole changed: empire's return flow

The classical file treats influence as flowing outward, from centre to periphery, and one of the record's correctives, useful in every room, is the return flow: the colonies changed the colonisers. The point is examinable in several registers. Demographically, the post-war decades brought the colonised to the metropoles as workers and citizens, reversing in person the movement the theory described in capital, and remaking the politics of the former imperial states from inside, a development the classical writers did not foresee and which no account of the century can omit. Culturally, the metropoles' languages, cuisines, literatures, and musics absorbed the empire they administered, and their national stories are still being renegotiated against the archive of what was done in their names, a renegotiation visible in every controversy over a statue, a syllabus, or a museum's holdings. Institutionally, the techniques of colonial administration, surveillance, census, camp, and counter-insurgency, were developed in the periphery and imported home, a circulation historians have documented in detail and which inverts the 1853 picture completely: the supposed school of modernity in the colony turns out to have been, among other things, a laboratory whose experiments returned. The candidate's use of the return flow is as a grading instrument. A theory of a one-way system, tested against a two-way record, requires amendment rather than burial, since the mechanisms of incorporation it described were real even where their direction was misdrawn. Stated in that form, as an amendment with a direction attached, the return flow earns its paragraph in evaluate and assess answers, and it earns a place in the history room's treatment of the post-war metropoles, where the candidates who see only flags coming down miss half the story the flags concealed.

## The commodity frontier: land, crops, and the ecological ledger

History and development papers share one further body of evidence that candidates underuse because it sits between their categories: the ecological transformation of the colonised world. Incorporation reorganised landscapes. Forests became plantations, subsistence acreage became export monoculture, mines and railways redrew watersheds, and species, crops, and diseases moved along the sea lanes in both directions, with consequences that outlasted every flag. The file's relevance to the examination is that it supplies the relational claim with its most durable evidence and its least contested illustrations. A plantation economy locked into a single export crop, its food imported, its soil mined, its prices set in distant exchanges, exhibits the structure the dependency writers described, in a form that can be narrated from the record of particular territories without recourse to a single disputed aggregate. Candidates should handle the material with the standing disciplines: narrate mechanisms and sequences rather than brandish totals, name the territories and the crops where the record is firm, and acknowledge the scholarship's own development, since environmental history has complicated the simple ledger in both directions, documenting indigenous adaptations and resistances alongside the impositions. An answer that can move from the theory's abstraction, the incorporation of an outside, to a hillside planted in rows for a market an ocean away, and back to the abstraction with the claim graded, performs the exact circuit between concept and evidence that the higher bands in the history room exist to certify, and it does so on terrain where the candidate's opponents, whichever they are, must meet the record rather than the rhetoric.

## Writing about violence in an answer

One craft problem remains, and it is a matter of professional conduct as much as of marks. This topic's record is saturated with violence: conquest, famine policy, forced labour, reprisal, wars of liberation and the terror that answered them. Candidates must write about it, and the manner matters. Two failures bound the acceptable register. The first is sensationalism, the lingering catalogue of atrocity deployed for emotional pressure, which markers read as a substitute for analysis and which dishonours its subject by converting suffering into rhetoric. The second is euphemism, the administrative vocabulary, pacification, unrest, incidents, through which the record's violence is laundered into procedure, which markers read as either naivety or complicity and which falsifies the evidence the answer claims to weigh. Between the failures lies the professional register the scholars in the file model at their best: violence stated plainly, in the active voice, with agents named where the record names them, at the scale the argument requires and no further, and with consequences followed into the structures the violence built or broke. The register connects to this guide's protocol on sources. The same discipline that characterises offensive material without reproducing it, and describes magnitudes without inventing them, governs the narration of atrocity: accuracy and specificity, without theatre in either direction. Candidates sometimes fear that plain statement will read as partisanship. On this topic the opposite holds. The euphemisms are the partisan inheritance, bequeathed by the administrations that coined them, and the plain sentence is the neutral instrument, which is why examiners, who have read more scripts on the subject than any candidate, trust it on sight.

## Structure and decision: the agency question inside every answer

Underneath the scorecard's entries runs a philosophical fault line that the strongest candidates learn to write about directly, because examiners in the politics and international relations rooms are listening for it. The classical theories are structural: they explain expansion by the organisation of capital, and individual statesmen appear in them as executors of pressures they did not create. The standing objection, pressed by historians from the documents, is that the record shows choices: cabinets that declined annexations the structure supposedly required, officials who forced expansion on reluctant governments, firms that lobbied against the adventures attributed to their class. An answer that ignores the objection defends a determinism its own authors practised loosely, since the founders, whatever their theory, wrote pamphlets aimed at decisions and expected decisions to matter. The mature handling, and it can be stated in a paragraph, distinguishes levels. Structure explains the menu: which options recur, which interests mobilise, which pressures bear on any government of the period regardless of its personnel. Decision explains the selection: why this territory, in that year, this instrument, annexation here and a treaty there. The free trade imperialism argument earns its keep at exactly this joint, since it shows a single structure selecting different instruments as costs shifted, and Fieldhouse's archive, properly read, disciplines the structural claim without dissolving it, by showing how often the selection ran ahead of, or against, the economic interest the theory assigned it. Scripts that can hold the two levels apart, assigning each episode its structural conditions and its decided form, write about causation the way the disciplines themselves aspire to, and the aspiration is legible to any marker within a page. The converse failure, an answer in which structure does everything and nobody ever decides anything, is the theoretical form of the unspecified refutation: impressive in bulk, empty at every point where a question actually bites.

## The geography of the argument: centre, periphery, and the space between

The file's spatial vocabulary deserves the same discipline as its terms of art, because candidates lose precision by letting the map's labels drift. The classical writers spoke of the advanced countries and the colonies, a binary adequate to a world of flags. The dependency literature complicated the map into centre and periphery, defined by function rather than by wealth alone: the centre as where the surplus is accumulated and the decisions concentrated, the periphery as where production is organised for demands set elsewhere. Wallerstein inserted the semi-periphery between them, neither fully commanding nor fully commanded, and gave it political work, absorbing pressures that would otherwise polarise the system into confrontation. Each vocabulary encodes its theory, and the candidate who mixes them mid-answer, colonies doing duty for peripheries, peripheries for the developing world of the aid reports, produces an argument whose geography no examiner can reconstruct. The discipline is declaration: adopt the map of the writer under discussion, note where a later writer redrew it, and resist the journalistic synonyms that flatten function into mere richness and poverty, since the file's whole point is that the poor region and the peripheral region are different concepts, one a condition and the other a position in a structure, and the difference is examinable. A sentence of declaration costs little. In the development room it can be the difference between the script the examiner quotes in the report as a model and the script quoted as a warning.

## Editions, translations, and the candidate who reads further

A closing piece of hygiene concerns the texts themselves, for the candidate who reads beyond the summaries, as the strongest always do. The classical works circulate in editions of very different character. The pamphlets were written for the moment and revised rarely; their standard English forms are serviceable, though the rendering of key terms, the stage vocabulary in Lenin's subtitle being the famous case, should be noticed rather than absorbed unconsciously. Hobson's study exists in the shadow of its own influence, often read in extract, and the extract habit is dangerous with him because his argument's liberalism lives in his remedy chapters, which anthologies cut. Luxemburg's accumulation study is long, technical in its middle, and frequently encountered through its critics' summaries, a mediation the candidate should correct at least for its opening and closing movements, where her problem and her peroration stand. The founder's journalism and correspondence are cited from the collected works, the fifty-volume English edition whose forty-first volume carries the letters this cluster's protocol concerns, and a reference to that apparatus, volume and context named, is the located reference in its most scholarly dress. None of this apparatus belongs in bulk in an examination script. A clause here, a volume there, deployed where the argument turns on a text's character, signals the acquaintance beneath the performance, and markers, who live among editions, register the signal at full value. The candidate who reads further should therefore read as the guide has taught: for mechanisms, for quarrels, for the occasions of the texts, and for the places where the record corrected the writers, since those are the places from which examination questions, in every room this topic inhabits, are invariably set.

## Self-marking: grading practice answers against the band

Revision improves fastest when the candidate stops being only the author of practice answers and becomes, in alternation, their examiner. The exercise this guide prescribes is blunt. Take a timed answer written a week earlier, cold enough to read as a stranger's, and mark it against the matrix row of its question, awarding or withholding credit movement by movement: the opening distinction present or absent, the roster deployed or recited, the critics named or gestured at, the evidence attached or sprayed, the judgement landed or evaded. The exercise hurts, because most candidates discover their scripts commit, in private, the precise sins the examiners' reports catalogue in public. That discovery, made in the study rather than inferred from a disappointing result, is the cheapest education the topic offers. Two refinements sharpen it. Mark in a different colour and a different sitting from the writing, since the author's memory of intentions is the enemy of the reader's evidence, and the marker has only the evidence. And mark the openings of several answers consecutively, ignoring their bodies, since the opening is where the band is most reliably predicted and where a term's worth of practice produces the largest measurable gain. Candidates who have graded themselves stop experiencing the examiner as a lottery. The examiner becomes a reader applying standards the candidate has already applied, to a script the candidate has already repaired, and the confidence that follows is not the brittle confidence of the well-drilled but the justified confidence of the self-corrected, which survives even the question nobody revised for.

## Questions that join clusters: synthesis under examination conditions

The hardest papers join topics, and candidates should expect the joins and rehearse the hinges. Imperialism joins the theory of the state wherever a question asks how far economic structure determines foreign policy, and the hinge is the spectrum of the state's place, instrument, form, and structural agent, laid out earlier in this guide. Imperialism joins crisis theory wherever a question asks whether expansion was the system's escape from its own contradictions, and the hinge is the surplus in its several costumes, Hobson's unspendable income against Luxemburg's unrealisable value against the monopoly school's unabsorbable surplus, three different escapes proposed for three different traps. Imperialism joins the national question wherever a question asks about liberation movements, self-determination, or the politics of the oppressed nations, and the hinge is the wartime quarrel between the right of secession defended as a democratic demand and the same slogan dismissed as a bourgeois snare in the epoch of monopoly. Imperialism joins the feminism and social reproduction cluster at exactly one well-marked point, the analysis of whose labour reproduces the system and at whose expense, a point the strongest candidates can develop through the colonial household and the migrant labour system, where the reproduction of the workforce was organised across the colour line by design. A joined question is not two questions; it is one question about a connection, and it is answered by stating the connection's mechanism at the outset, then letting each side of the join contribute the evidence its own cluster supplied. Candidates who answer joined questions by writing two half-essays in sequence commit the listing error at the scale of whole topics. Candidates who state the hinge in the opening paragraph, and thereafter move material across it in both directions, perform the synthesis the paper was built to find, and they find, as a reward, that joined questions are marked by examiners who set them precisely because so few scripts attempt the join at all.

## The drain argument: the file's oldest voice

Long before the classical roster assembled, the colonised world had produced its own economic indictment, and candidates, particularly those facing the civil service room, should command it as the file's senior document. Dadabhai Naoroji, an Indian liberal, a member of the British Parliament, and a founder of the Indian National Congress, argued across the later nineteenth century that India's poverty was not an inheritance but a product: wealth was being drained from India to Britain through the structure of the connection itself, the home charges, the remitted salaries and pensions, the export surplus for which India received no equivalent return. The argument matters to this guide on four grounds. It precedes Hobson, which breaks the metropolitan monopoly on the theory's authorship that lazy summaries assume. Its mechanism differs from every entry in the roster: not surplus seeking an outlet, not realisation failing, but a unilateral transfer administered through the ordinary operations of rule, taxation in one country financing expenditure in another. Its evidence was statistical and official, drawn from the empire's own published accounts, which made it the first demonstration of the archive turned against its makers. And its fate instructs: the drain argument was absorbed, transformed, and radicalised by the later tradition, while its author's liberal remedies, representation and reform within the connection, were overtaken by the independence his analysis helped to make imaginable. In an answer, Naoroji does the work this guide prizes most. He supplies the located reference that proves the candidate's reading reached beyond the canon, he complicates the founder sequence by showing an Indian contemporary reasoning about empire's economics while Marx was still writing, and he anchors the relational claim in a mechanism concrete enough to be examined without a single contested aggregate, since the transfer's channels can be enumerated even where its totals are disputed. Handle the totals under the protocol, as attributed estimates with methods attached, and the channels as the argument.

## A reading order for the whole file

Candidates with a term rather than a month need a sequence, and the estate repays being walked in an order that builds the examination skills in the order the examination tests them. Begin with the founders in the order of their quarrels rather than of their fame: the liberal diagnosis first, since its mechanism is the plainest and its remedy the most instructive, then the finance analysis, then the accumulation argument with its technical core faced rather than skipped, then the wartime codification read as what it is, a synthesis with sources, and the dissent on cartelisation read last among the classics, since its force depends on knowing what it dissented from. Turn then to the critics in the order of their weapons: the atavism thesis for the reversal of causation, the archive for the geography, the survey for the map of the whole contested field, since Brewer's distinctions will thereafter organise everything else the candidate reads. Turn then to the primary embarrassment, the founder's colonial writings, in the protocol's own sequence, journalism and its premises before correspondence and its register, and read the companion article's evidence entire rather than trusting any guide's compression, including this one's. Close with the heirs: the dependency roster, the world system's architecture, the naming of the neo-colonial condition, and the production-chain literature in which the oldest questions are asked with the newest instruments. Walked in that order, the file stops being a syllabus and becomes what it was for those who made it, an argument conducted across a century by people answering one another, and a candidate who has heard the argument, rather than memorised its stations, will find the examination's questions strangely familiar, since the examiners, who set them, heard the same argument and are asking, in their several rooms, whether the candidate has heard it too.

## Against inevitability: hindsight and the conditional mood

A stylistic discipline with substantive consequences remains to be named, because scripts on this topic fail it constantly and markers feel the failure before they can name it. The classical story invites hindsight's grammar: the stage arrived, the partition followed, the war resulted, the empires fell, as though the record unrolled from the theory like a proof. Candidates absorb the grammar from summaries and reproduce answers in which everything was always going to happen, which quietly destroys the evaluation the answer exists to perform, since a claim that explains whatever occurred explains nothing in particular. The corrective is the conditional mood, practised as a habit of writing. Claims should be stated with their conditions attached: if the blocs' rivalry over a divided world could not be cartelised away, then redivision wars followed, and the interest of the post-war decades is precisely that the condition failed. Events should be narrated with their forks visible: the mandate settlement could have hardened into annexation or dissolved into trusteeship in earnest, and the theory's parties disagreed about which because the outcome was not written anywhere. Even the founders benefit from the discipline. Marx in 1853 did not know that the village order he pronounced eternal had a politics, or that the railway he welcomed as regeneration's instrument would carry the armies of a national movement within the century, and an answer that restores his ignorance to him judges his premises more fairly than one that grades him against a future he could not consult. The conditional mood is not decoration. It is the grammatical form of the scorecard's deepest commitment, that claims are tested against a record that might have been otherwise, and candidates who write in it find their evaluations acquiring, almost without effort, the precision that the higher bands describe in their own more bureaucratic vocabulary.

## Defending the answer aloud: vivas, interviews, and the seminar defence

Some candidates meet this topic a second time with their voices, in vivas, interviews, and assessed seminars, and the translation from script to speech follows rules worth stating. The oral examiner probes the same joints as the written one, the plurality of the theories, the sequence on the founder, the grading of the record, but probes them conversationally, which changes the candidate's risks. The first risk is the recitation reflex: a spoken answer that unrolls a memorised essay sounds, in a room, exactly like what it is, and oral examiners interrupt it, kindly or otherwise, to find the mind behind it. The defence against interruption is to answer in movements rather than in essays, one distinction, one mechanism, one grade, each complete enough to stand if the conversation turns. The second risk is the ambush of the single hard case, the letter, the epithet, the failed prediction, produced by an examiner testing whether the candidate's composure survives contact with the file's worst pages. The protocol is the composure: characterise, cite, grade the part rather than the whole, and let the examiner discover that the worst page was already in the candidate's scorecard. The third risk is the invitation to slogan, the question framed so that a chant would satisfy a partisan and ruin a scholar, and the defence is the guide's whole method performed aloud, slowly: separate the senses, name the rival, specify the claim, state the condition under which the judgement would change. Candidates who can do that standing, in sentences they are composing as they speak, possess the topic in the only sense that finally matters, and they will find that oral examiners, like written ones, relax into the pleasure of examining someone who has plainly read the file and thought about it, which is the state every technique in this guide was designed, from its first paragraph, to produce.

## Who got to theorise: the roster's own composition

A final observation belongs in a guide that closes two clusters, one on feminism and one on empire, because the roster itself exhibits the condition both clusters study. Of the classical theorists of imperialism, one was a woman, and she was murdered by the counter-revolution her theory had opposed; of the colonised world that the theories described, not one voice stood in the canon as first assembled, though Naoroji had published before most of it and Du Bois wrote while it formed. The examination relevance is not decorative. Questions in the development and history rooms increasingly ask candidates to reflect on the literature's standpoint, on what its authors could and could not see from where they stood, and the candidate's handling should follow the file's own method rather than the polemics on either side. State the composition as a fact. Connect it, precisely, to the file's documented blind spots: the silence on race as a structure, the third-person treatment of peoples who were in fact writing, the surprise the tradition kept registering when the objects of its analysis acted as subjects. Credit the corrections to their makers, inside the tradition and at its edges, and grade the whole as the scorecard grades everything, by parts. A literature is not refuted by its roster, any more than it is vindicated by its intentions, but a roster explains a literature's silences as surely as a mechanism explains its claims, and the candidate who can say which silence belongs to which standpoint has reached the level of reflection at which this subject, in all its rooms, finally becomes indistinguishable from the study of how knowledge itself is made. That is the note on which a guide to examining empire should end its instruction, since the candidates who carry it away carry the cluster's two halves in a single thought: theories are written by situated people, about a world that talks back, and the marks, in the end, go to those who heard it talking.

## A note on the answer's conscience

Something remains that is not technique, and a guide that omitted it would misdescribe the subject it teaches. This topic examines the domination of most of the world by a handful of states within living memory's reach, and the candidates who write about it are frequently the grandchildren of both sides of that history, sometimes in the same family. A candidate's inheritance is not an argument, and this guide has insisted throughout that arguments are what is marked. But the subject's gravity explains the temperature of its examination rooms, the care of its examiners about language, and the protocol this cluster adopted for its worst pages, and a candidate who understands why the care exists will keep the protocols without experiencing them as censorship. State what the record contains. Characterise what cannot be quoted. Decline the figures that cannot be defended and the euphemisms that cannot be believed. The disciplines are the same ones the scholarship at its best observes, and observing them in a timed script, on the subject where they are hardest to observe, is a form of respect that markers recognise instantly, because it is the form their own profession takes when it is honest. Technique brought the candidate to the door of the higher bands. Conscience, of this plain and procedural kind, is what the bands were built to keep the door open for.

## The guide's last word to the candidate

Everything in the preceding pages reduces, in the room, to a handful of reflexes, and it is worth ending by naming them as plainly as the subject allows. When a question arrives, classify it before you answer it. When a term carries two objects, separate them before you argue. When a tradition offers several theories, name the rivals before you judge among them. When a claim meets a record, grade the claim and not the family. When a figure beckons, ask for its provenance or let it go. When a founder's record darkens, run the sequence and grade the parts. When a conclusion forms, state what would change it, and then conclude without apology in whichever direction the grading led, because the system you are being examined by was built, at its best, to pay for exactly that performance. The literature you have revised was written by people arguing in earnest about the largest questions their century offered, and the examination, whatever its machinery of bands and boards, is an invitation to join the argument under conditions of fairness they would have recognised. Accept the invitation on those terms, and the topic that frightens the unprepared will do what it has done for prepared candidates in every season since it entered the papers: it will give you the marks your method has already earned.

## For students and exam candidates

Your preparation divides by the room you will sit in, and the division should be made early enough to shape your revision rather than discovered from the paper. If your room is history, your centre of gravity is the record: the conjuncture the theory described, the sequence of partition, war, mandates, and decolonisation against which its claims are graded, and the founder's journalism handled as a primary source with flawed premises and a documented revision. Build your scorecard with dates attached and your examples chosen from the classical decades, and let the theories enter as the period's own attempts to understand itself. If your room is politics or international relations, your centre of gravity is the argument: the roster's mechanisms, the quarrels among their holders, the placement of the family against its realist and liberal neighbours, and the evaluation conducted with critics named. Build your matrix with the comparison row strongest, since that is the row your paper is likeliest to deal you. If your room is development studies, your centre of gravity is the boundary: the classical file kept separate from the dependency literature that inherited its questions, the modernisation doctrine revised as the third party it is, and the extensions, neo-colonialism, unequal exchange, world-systems, held ready as the later chapters they are and not as synonyms for the founders. If your room is a civil service optional or an eligibility test, your centre of gravity is structure and coverage: the organised answer, the complete roster, the balanced conclusion with its weighing shown, and the founder's writings answered by the sequence, which that room sets more faithfully than any other.

Whatever your room, two pieces of equipment serve you identically, and this guide's persona path routes you to them deliberately. ## For teachers and seminar leaders

Teaching this topic well means resisting the two pedagogical shortcuts that produce the examination failures this guide catalogues: teaching the canonical pamphlet as the theory, and teaching the founder's journalism as a morality tale in either direction. The classroom sequence that works mirrors the answer method. Open with the senses of the term, and let the class discover, by attempting to answer whether the phenomenon has ended, that they were arguing about different objects. Introduce the theories as rival mechanisms in chronological order, assigning each to a group required to state it in its strongest form and then to face the objection its neighbours press, since the steelmanning discipline is learned socially before it is performed individually. Teach the founder's writings across a full session with the sequence on the board, position, premises, change, correspondence, and the sources open, so that students watch a record being graded rather than a verdict being announced. The content base for all of this is the cluster's [marxism and imperialism guide](/2017/02/01/marxism-and-imperialism-guide/), which carries the full exposition this article compresses.

For marking practice, the matrix's fourth and fifth columns are your instruments: set students to grade one another's openings against the evaluation the row requires and the trap the row names, and the class will internalise the band structure faster than any rubric read aloud. Insist, in every piece of written feedback, on names: a criticism without a critic and a claim without a text should come back annotated until the habit forms. Insist equally on the figures protocol, and treat its breach in coursework as the serious fault it will be in the examination. And protect, explicitly and in advance, the student whose essays conclude against the theories you admire, or for the theories you do not: say in the classroom what this guide says in its central section, that the assessment rewards the performance and not the destination, and then mark so that the statement is believed. A class that trusts that promise writes honestly, and honest scripts are the only kind from which students actually learn.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

The research frontier on this file runs through three territories this guide has touched and the cluster treats at length. The economic file remains open where the series' geography and flow disputes began, as new archival work on investment, trade, and the fiscal history of the colonial states continues to revise the magnitudes on which the classical scorecard rests, and the contested character of the numbers is the reason the examination protocol exists. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How do you write an exam answer on imperialism?

Begin by separating the term's two governing senses, the phase of capitalism and the fact of territorial rule, and say which of them your answer addresses. Then work from the planning table: classify the question, select the theories its wording requires, and arrange your material in movements, reconstruction before evaluation, evidence attached to claims one at a time. Keep definitions inside clauses, name the holder of every position you state, and reserve the closing lines for a judgement that matches the command word. An answer built this way reads as an argument conducted in order, which is what markers in every discipline are trained to reward.

### Q: Which theories of imperialism must you name in an exam?

Your answer should command a spread rather than a single canonical statement. Carry the liberal diagnosis built on underconsumption and the interests that fed on it, the account of bank and industry fused into finance capital, the argument that accumulation needs a non-capitalist outside, the wartime systematisation of rival state blocs, the famous five-feature codification that absorbed them all, and the dissenting sketch of a cartelised world order. Six positions, each reducible to a mechanism in a couple of sentences, give you the comparative range that exam questions on this subject are designed to draw out.

### Q: How do you handle Marx's colonial writings in an answer?

Follow a fixed order and never improvise it. First state the youthful newspaper position faithfully: conquest as a brutal process cast, against its agents' intentions, as an instrument of historical transformation. Second, expose the foundations that position stood on, the picture of an unchanging village order drawn from travellers and official reports, and set against it the later scholarship on the pre-colonial economy. Third, record the alteration in the author's own stance, visible in his response to revolt and completed in the reversal over Ireland. Private letters belong in the answer only as characterised evidence, cited to their edition and never quoted for their abusive passages.

### Q: What are the common mistakes on imperialism?

The errors repeat because they are structural. Candidates recite one celebrated pamphlet as though a whole tradition stood behind it, merge the post-war dependency literature into the pre-war classical canon, reduce the founder to a single slogan about empire drawn from one phase of his writing, wave the end of formal rule at claims it never tested, quote enormous numbers with no source attached, spray quotations that do no work, and answer the question they prepared instead of the one on the paper. Every item on that list is preventable by classification before composition, which is why planning instruments outperform extra content on this topic.

### Q: How do you evaluate the Marxist theory of imperialism?

Refuse to grade it as one object. Break the family of accounts into the claims history could actually test: the spread of monopoly, the ascendancy of finance, the direction of capital flows, the expectation of wars over redivision, the supposed necessity of colonies, the purchasing of working-class loyalty. Then mark each claim against the record separately, conceding the failures without varnish, a generation of peace among the leading powers, investment concentrated among wealthy economies, growth that survived the flags coming down, while crediting what endured. Attach the objections to the scholars who made them. Close with the conditions under which your verdict would shift.

### Q: How do you use decolonisation as evidence?

Treat the transfer of power as a test rig rather than a verdict. Before citing it, specify the proposition it is supposed to test. Against the proposition that metropolitan economies could not function without possessing colonies, the aftermath is decisive, since prosperity accompanied the surrender of empire. Against the proposition that hierarchy and value transfer would end with the flags, it proves very little, and the independence generation's own writers renamed the surviving condition within years. Historians also use the manner of the transfers, negotiated in one territory and fought over in another, as evidence about the agency of the colonised peoples themselves.

### Q: Which quotations work for imperialism?

Choose few, choose short, and make each one labour. A formulation that condenses a position is worth quoting: the description of England's role in India as a mission both destructive and regenerative, because a whole early argument lives inside it, or the sentence insisting that the lever be applied in Ireland, because the later reversal lives inside that one. Phrases that became the literature's own labels, free trade imperialism among them, also earn their place. What never earns its place is a long passage reproduced for atmosphere, a number dressed as testimony, or anything drawn from private correspondence whose abusive language adds shock and subtracts credit.

### Q: How do you compare Marxist and liberal theories of imperialism?

Do not write two summaries and call the pair a comparison. Put both traditions through an identical set of questions and let the answers collide: what propels expansion outward, whose pockets the process fills, whether reform inside the system could end it, and what observations would count against each account. The deepest collision concerns status. In the liberal telling, expansion is a malfunction, a surplus generated by skewed incomes and steered abroad by interested parties, so better distribution drains it. In the Marxist tellings, expansion belongs to the system's normal functioning at a definite phase, so nothing short of systemic change retires it.

### Q: Which examples work for imperialism questions?

Select episodes for the work they perform, not for their fame. The partition of Africa at the conference table displays rivalry and division in a single scene, yet its diplomatic record also shows prestige and strategy crowding out finance, which makes it useful against the theory as well as for it. Overseas lending that flowed toward settler economies rather than tropical possessions tests the geography of the capital export claim. Chartered companies governing territory as a commercial asset exhibit the fusion of economic and political power. Post-war cooperation among the leading economies, their joint institutions and mutual abstention from war, tests the prophecy of permanent redivision.

### Q: How do you revise imperialism and dependency together?

Revise them as relatives with different birthdays, addresses, and questions. The older literature, written in Europe before the First World War, asks why the advanced states pushed outward. The younger, written largely from Latin America after the Second World War, asks why the economies at the receiving end failed to develop. Keep two rosters and two chronologies, and memorise the hinge figures who connect them, the theorists of unequal exchange and of the world system. In the exam room, let the question's vocabulary police the boundary: wording about underdevelopment or the periphery licenses the younger literature, while wording about the classical authors closes the door on it.

### Q: How does the stage and empire opening structure a first paragraph?

It performs three tasks in about three sentences. The first sentence separates the two objects the key term can denote, a phase in the life of an economic system and the practice of ruling foreign territory. The second announces which object your answer will examine and acknowledges the other in a subordinate clause, so the marker knows the ambiguity has been seen and managed. The third forecasts your procedure, whether that is grading predictions, staging a comparison, or running a sequence through a primary record. A paragraph built so does the work of an introduction, a definition, and a plan simultaneously.

### Q: What should a candidate separate between naming a theory and summarising it?

Separate the cost from the credit. Naming is economical: an author, a work, a date, and the mechanism stated tightly enough that no informed reader could confuse it with a neighbour's. Summarising is expensive: the argument unfolded step by step, its evidence paraded, its qualifications aired. On a survey topic the marks are spread across the field, so buy breadth with naming and purchase depth only where the question concentrates, typically on the figure it names or the controversy it stages. Candidates who summarise everything run out of room before evaluation begins; candidates who name intelligently arrive at evaluation with time, space, and six positions in play.

### Q: Why does the examiner reward naming rivals over defending one line?

Because the assessment is built to measure operations that a single-line defence cannot display. Anyone can be taught to hold a position; the higher bands describe a candidate who can reconstruct several positions fairly, set them against one another, and arbitrate between them with evidence. Rivals named accurately are the visible proof that those operations are available, and their absence tells the marker the ceiling of the script before the argument starts. There is also a matter of trust. A script that states the opposing case at full strength before answering it is a script whose judgements the reader can rely on, and reliance is what converts a good answer into a top-band one.

### Q: How does the imperialism answer matrix order a timed answer?

The table works because it moves decisions upstream, out of the pressurised minutes and into revision. Its rows catalogue the shapes questions take on this subject; its columns force the choices each shape demands, the discipline whose expectations govern, the theories that must appear, the evaluative step that separates the bands, and the error the wording is fishing for. Rehearsed until it can be redrawn from memory, it lets you spend the opening minutes of an answer classifying rather than panicking, and the middle minutes executing a known sequence of movements. What it cannot do is write sentences, and it should never be mistaken for a script.

### Q: What separates an answer that lists theories from one that compares them?

Interrogation. In a listing script each theory receives its own sealed compartment, definition, features, example, and the compartments could change places without the answer noticing. In a comparing script the theories are marched through shared questions in turn, so that on every issue, the engine of expansion, the beneficiaries, the possibility of reform, the reader watches two mechanisms produce two different answers side by side. The difference is visible in the connective tissue: comparisons are full of whereas, by contrast, and on the same point, while lists are full of dates and feature counts. Markers reward the collision, because the collision is the analysis.
