Latin America is examined wherever the twentieth century is examined, and a candidate who understands where the region sits in each paper has already solved half the problem of writing on it. History papers meet the material inside the Cold War and decolonisation: a revolution in Cuba that changed sides in the hemisphere's alignment, a constitutional road attempted in Chile and destroyed by a coup, insurgencies and counterinsurgencies across a continent. Politics and international relations papers meet it as theory and strategy: the dependency school's account of why development stalls, and the long argument between the armed road and the electoral road. Development studies meets it as the region that produced the most influential critique of development itself. Courses in Latin American studies meet it whole, as a tradition. Each setting rewards something different, and this guide is organised around those rewards rather than around the content, which lives in the nine articles that precede it in this cluster.

![A study desk with the Latin America answer matrix, revision cards and a timed practice script, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

One point needs stating plainly before any technique, because candidates consistently misjudge it and the misjudgement distorts everything they write. Assessment on this topic grades the quality of analysis, and it does not grade the direction of the conclusion. A model answer that concludes against the Cuban Revolution, against the Chilean road, against dependency theory, or against the redistributive governments of the later period can score full marks, provided its representation of each project is accurate, its evidence is handled with care, and its judgement engages the strongest version of what it rejects. A model answer that concludes in favour of any of those projects can fall into a low band if it reproduces a movement's account of itself as though reproduction were analysis. Examiners are testing whether the candidate knows the debates: where each project's claims are contested, which critics contest them, and how scholarly positions have moved. A script that knows the debates and then judges them, in either direction, is the script the marking scheme is built to reward. Keep that symmetry in view through every section that follows, because it is the licence under which all the technique in this guide operates.

The two errors that cost the most marks on this material are different in kind, and both are cheap to repair. The first is factual with an analytical price: treating the Cuban movement as communist from its victory onward. It was not, and the two years and more between the victory and the socialist declaration are not a detail but the single most examinable sequence in the cluster, because explaining that sequence is what turns a recital of events into analysis. The second error is one of register: writing about the redistributive governments of the later period, or about any government of the present day, in the language of political allegiance or political hostility rather than the language of structure and constraint. Examiners assess analysis of social formations; a position on a government is not analysis, and it risks marks in every discipline that touches this material. The sections below take each repair in turn, then work through the question types, the disciplines, and the sensitive material, in the order a marker would wish a candidate to have prepared them.

## Latin America in the exam room: what assessment rewards

Begin with what a marking scheme can actually see. A scheme cannot reward sympathy, and it cannot reward outrage; it can only reward operations performed on the page. On this topic the operations are consistent across examining bodies, even where the questions differ in wording and weight. The first operation is faithful representation: each project's positions stated in a form its own serious writers would recognise, with the region's plurality of Marxisms intact rather than flattened into a single imported doctrine. The second is anchoring: every major claim attached to a case and a concept, a dated episode and a named mechanism, placed precisely enough that the marker can verify the candidate is handling real material rather than a summary of summaries. The third is engagement with objection: a named critic, a specific argument, weighed rather than waved away. The fourth is a conclusion that the preceding analysis has earned, which on Latin American material almost always means a conclusion with a condition attached, because the record itself is conditional, persuasive on land and unpersuasive on industry, strong in one country and weak in its neighbour.

A candidate who performs those four operations in an answer of modest length will outscore a candidate who performs none of them in an answer twice as long. Length is not among the operations. This is why preparation for the topic should be organised by question type rather than by chapter: the chapter organisation stocks the warehouse, but the question type dictates which operations the marker will be watching for, and in what order. A sequence question watches for ordering and turning points. A feasibility question on Chile watches for constraints named and ranked. An evaluation question on dependency theory watches for the school's own revisions to be known before its critics are quoted. A strategy question watches for the separation of explanation from endorsement and for the discipline of the figures protocol. Stocking the warehouse matters, and the cluster of articles behind this guide exists for that purpose, but the warehouse is not the performance.

### What does a marker reward in the first paragraph of an answer on this topic?

A marker rewards a first paragraph that assigns the question to its type, states the answer's governing distinction, and names the case and concept the argument will run on. Two or three sentences performing those moves tell the reader which band's habits the script will display, and they cost the candidate almost nothing beyond the decision to write them deliberately.

The reward structure explains a pattern candidates find puzzling, which is that short answers on this material often beat long ones by a distance that feels unjust. The long answer usually fails not on knowledge but on allocation: it spends its length narrating Cuba when the question asked about the conditions of a road, or rehearsing the dependency vocabulary when the question asked whether the vocabulary still explains. The short answer wins because every paragraph performs an operation the scheme can see. Candidates preparing for this cluster should therefore rehearse allocation as a skill in its own right, deciding before the pen moves what each paragraph is for, and treating the narrative they know best as a resource to be drawn on rather than a story to be discharged in full.

## Two anchors: the claim this guide is built on

Every cluster in this series has a small stock of content that returns disproportionate marks, and for Latin America the stock has a name. The two anchors are the Cuban sequence and the Chilean constraint. The Cuban sequence is the fact that the socialist declaration followed the revolutionary victory by more than two years, with a legible chain of reform, reaction, expropriation and counter-pressure in between; the candidate who can order that chain and explain what drove each link owns the highest-return paragraph available on the region. The Chilean constraint is the fact that a government elected on a plurality attempted a legal transformation of property while unable to restrain the mobilisation on its own left flank; the candidate who can state that double bind precisely owns the second. Each anchor converts a narrative answer into an analytical one in a sentence, because each replaces a story with a structure: a sequence with causes, a failure with conditions.

The anchors earn their status for a further reason that candidates should understand, since it governs how examiners set questions. Both anchors are discriminating: they separate candidates who have studied the cases from candidates who have absorbed the folklore. The folklore says the Cuban revolutionaries were communists who seized power, and that the Chilean experiment was destroyed from outside. Both sentences contain a fragment of truth wrapped around an error, and both errors are exactly what a well-set question is designed to catch. A candidate who leads with an anchor signals in the opening lines that the folklore has been replaced by the record, and markers read the rest of such a script in a different posture. That is not a trick of presentation. It is the assessment system working as intended, rewarding the candidate who knows where the analytical weight sits.

## The Latin America answer matrix

The matrix below organises the cluster's preparation by question type. Each row names a question family, the discipline that most often asks it, the content a strong answer leads with, the evaluation that lifts the answer into the higher bands, and the trap that the row's weaker scripts fall into. Candidates should be able to reproduce the matrix from memory and to assign an unseen question to its row within the first minute of reading it, because the assignment determines the opening move, the stock to be drawn on, and the conclusion's shape.

### How does a candidate choose which row governs an unfamiliar question?

By its command and its object rather than by vocabulary. A question asking why a process unfolded belongs to the sequence row; a question asking whether an outcome was avoidable belongs to the feasibility row; a question naming a theory belongs to the evaluation row even dressed as history. The row supplies the opening move and the trap to avoid.

Rows can combine, and the stronger scripts are often those that notice a question straddling two. A question on why the Chilean road failed invites the feasibility row for its main architecture and the strategy row for its closing movement, since the road's destruction became the electoral strategy's defining exhibit across the continent. A question on the region's contribution to Marxist theory invites the dependency row and the liberation theology row in sequence, with the thinker shelf supplying the located names. The matrix is a planning instrument, not a straitjacket: its purpose is to ensure that no answer begins without a governing distinction and no answer ends without an evaluation, which are the two absences markers penalise most reliably.

## Which examinations ask what: the assessment map

The same cluster of content is examined by rooms that want different things from it, and a candidate who knows which room they are sitting in can shape one stock of knowledge into several performances. History papers at school and university level place the Cuban and Chilean cases inside the Cold War's global history and inside the longer story of the United States and its hemisphere; they reward chronology disciplined into causation, and they punish the essay that treats either case as a self-contained drama. Politics and international relations papers reach for the region when they examine revolutionary strategy, the state, and the critique of the world economy; they reward conceptual precision and the weighing of rival explanations. Development studies reaches for dependency theory as its founding quarrel and rewards candidates who can state the mechanism before judging it. Latin American studies programmes examine the whole tradition, from the Peruvian founder of an indigenous Marxism to the theologians, and reward range held together by argument. Civil service optional papers in world history and international relations borrow the region as a test of whether a candidate can handle a revolution analytically. Eligibility tests and university entrance examinations compress all of this into recognition: thinker to text, text to thesis, thesis to critic.

What changes as the material moves between those rooms is not the facts but the unit of credit. In a history essay the unit is the explained episode; in a politics essay it is the handled concept; in a development essay it is the mechanism and its evidence; in a compressed answer it is the distinction delivered in two sentences without loss. Candidates should therefore prepare the cluster in layers, a narrative layer that can be drawn on at any length, a conceptual layer that states each theory in a paragraph, and a judgement layer that holds the critics and the verdicts ready-made but revisable. The layers are rehearsed separately and combined under time, which is the only honest simulation of the room itself.

### What changes when identical content moves from a long essay to a compressed answer?

The content survives by shedding its connective tissue. The long essay affords the sequence entire, the objections in order, the verdict at leisure; the compressed answer keeps the governing distinction, one located example, and the verdict with its condition, and trusts the marker to recognise the architecture in miniature. Compressing well is a separate drill from learning the material.

School-level specifications deserve a sentence of their own, because their candidates are the youngest and their questions the most narrative in form. Even there, the anchors pay: a school candidate who orders the Cuban sequence correctly and states the Chilean plurality plainly is performing the same operations as the university candidate, at a scale the specification rewards. No room examining this material rewards the folklore version, and every room rewards the analytical one, which is why this guide spends so little time on the stories and so much on the moves.

## The Cuban sequence: the first anchor worked

The sequence begins with a coalition, not a party of the doctrine. The movement that took power in Cuba at the opening of 1959 was a broad insurrectionary alliance whose programme promised constitutional restoration, elections, and social reform within a nationalist frame; its organised communist contemporaries had initially dismissed its leader as a bourgeois adventurer and joined the insurrection late. That starting point is the anchor's first link, and the candidate who states it has already separated the script from the folklore. The second link is the agrarian reform of May 1959, which expropriated the great estates, foreign and Cuban alike, and thereby converted a political victory into a social revolution with enemies who held property, embassies, and markets. The third link is the reaction the reform summoned: the resignation and defection of moderates within the government, the organisation of counter-revolution, the tightening pressure from Washington as expropriations reached American property, and the search for an alternative market and patron that carried Havana toward Moscow. The fourth link is the spiral made visible: each defensive measure, the militias, the committees, the nationalisations of 1960, answered a pressure and created the conditions for the next, until the declaration of the revolution's socialist character in April 1961 named a destination the sequence had already reached.

Explaining the sequence means assigning each link its mechanism. Reform created the constituency and the enemy at once: the peasants who received land became the revolution's defenders, and the dispossessed became its organised opposition. Expropriation of foreign property internationalised the conflict, because the injured party was a state with the means to retaliate through quotas, embargoes, and covert action. Retaliation then supplied the internal argument for consolidation: a government under external siege treats pluralism as a vulnerability and unity as a weapon, and the fusion of the revolutionary organisations into a single party followed that logic. The declaration, when it came on the eve of the Playa Girón landing, was therefore less a conversion than a christening. A candidate who can narrate those mechanisms, rather than listing the events, has performed the operation the sequence question exists to test.

### How do you explain the Cuban radicalisation sequence?

state that the movement was not communist in 1959, that the socialist declaration came in April 1961, and then explain the intervening sequence of reform and reaction. That ordering converts a narrative answer into an analytical one and is the highest-return move on the topic.

Two features of the sequence reward emphasis because examiners probe for them. The first is the lateness and reluctance of the old communist party's embrace, which reverses the folklore's direction of influence: the established Marxist organisation did not make the revolution; the revolution, radicalising, absorbed and then subordinated the organisation. The second is the role of the middle-class defectors, whose departure across 1959 and 1960 is the sequence's internal barometer: each resignation marks a stage at which the revolution's direction became legible to those who had joined it for a different destination. An answer that uses the defections as evidence, rather than as colour, shows a marker that the candidate understands how contemporaries read the process while it was still open.

## Design or driven: the historiographical argument an answer must hold

Behind the sequence stands an argument among historians that a strong answer should stage rather than settle by assertion. The design explanation, associated with Theodore Draper's early and influential account, holds that the revolution's leader intended a communist outcome from the beginning and concealed the intention behind a democratic programme until power was secure enough to discard it. The driven explanation, developed by Hugh Thomas and refined by later archival scholarship, holds that the outcome was produced by the interaction of reform, reaction, and superpower pressure, with the destination chosen late and under constraint. Each explanation commands evidence. The design reading points to the leader's radical formation, his reading, and the speed with which institutions were fused once the declaration was made. The driven reading points to the coalition's genuine breadth, the communists' initial hostility, and the documentary record of improvisation under pressure.

The candidate's task is not to pick a team but to weigh the evidence types. Concealment claims are difficult to falsify, since every moderate act can be read as camouflage and every radical act as revelation; a strong answer says so, and notes that the design thesis therefore carries a burden it cannot fully discharge. The driven thesis, in turn, must explain why the sequence moved in one direction only, why no comparable pressure pushed the revolution toward the centre, and its best answers invoke the structure of the conflict: expropriation creates enemies faster than it creates friends, and a besieged government's options narrow along a single gradient. Julia Sweig's archival work on the insurrection's internal debates and Samuel Farber's critical history from the left complicate both camps, the first by documenting genuine pluralism inside the movement, the second by insisting that the outcome's authoritarian form was chosen rather than imposed. An answer that can deploy those complications has moved beyond the textbook quarrel, which is precisely the movement the higher bands describe.

### What separates the design explanation of the Cuban turn from the driven explanation?

The location of intention. The design explanation places the outcome inside the leader's concealed purpose from the start, so that events become the unfolding of a plan; the driven explanation places it inside the interaction of reform and reaction, so that events become the narrowing of choices. Each reads the same chronology; they differ over what the chronology proves.

Candidates should notice that this argument is the cluster's purest training in historical method, and that examiners know it. A question on the Cuban turn is rarely only about Cuba: it tests whether a candidate can hold two explanations of one sequence, identify what evidence would discriminate between them, and reach a verdict that specifies its own confidence. The verdict most defensible on the record is a weighted one: the destination was not inscribed in the movement's programme or its coalition, the direction of travel was set early by the agrarian reform's logic, and the speed and form of the arrival owed much to choices made under pressures that were themselves partly of the revolution's making. That sentence's structure, claim, mechanism, qualification, is transferable to every feasibility and causation question in the cluster.

## The founding error and its price

Return to the costliest error, because its price is worth understanding exactly. A candidate who writes that the communists took power in Cuba in 1959 has not merely misplaced a label; they have deleted the phenomenon the question exists to examine. If the movement was communist from the start, there is no radicalisation to explain, no sequence to order, no historiography to weigh, and the answer collapses into a description of a regime the candidate also knows less well than the process that produced it. Markers notice the error in the first paragraph, and the script's ceiling is set there. The repair is correspondingly valuable: the single sentence that dates the declaration to April 1961 and the victory to January 1959 reopens the whole analytical field, and it costs eleven words.

The error has cousins that markers watch for with the same attention. One is the assumption that the Soviet alliance preceded and caused the radicalisation, when the sequence runs the other way: the alliance was sought as the conflict with Washington deepened, and the sugar agreement of February 1960 followed the first expropriations rather than preceding them. Another is the treatment of the old communist party as the revolution's author, when its initial judgement of the insurrection was hostile and its cadres were absorbed on the new movement's terms. A third is the flattening of the coalition's early programme, the promises of elections and constitutional restoration, into a deception so complete that the thousands who acted on it become unintelligible. Each cousin shares the founding error's structure: a later state of affairs projected backward onto a moment when it did not yet exist. Candidates should learn to catch the projection in their own drafts, because it is the characteristic failure of answers written from outcomes backward.

### Why does the founding error about the Cuban movement cost so heavily?

Because it abolishes the question. The examinable phenomenon is the transformation of a broad insurrectionary coalition into a declared socialist revolution across two years; a script that begins from the end state has nothing left to explain, and its remaining pages can only describe a regime. Markers set the ceiling of such a script at its first paragraph.

There is a constructive habit that prevents the whole family of errors, and it is worth stating as a rule of the cluster. Date the states of affairs, not only the events. A candidate who writes, of any case, what was true at the opening date, what was true at the midpoint, and what was true at the close, is forced into sequence by the grammar of the exercise, and sequence is the antidote to projection. The habit serves Chile, where the government of 1970 and the government of 1973 were the same administration in law and different animals in fact, and it serves the later period, where the redistributive governments changed character as the commodity cycle turned. Examiners reward the habit because it is the visible form of thinking historically rather than retrospectively.

## The Chilean constraint: the second anchor worked

Chile supplies the cluster's second anchor because its case poses the opposite problem to Cuba's. The Cuban question is how a movement became something it had not declared; the Chilean question is how a government that declared everything in advance, won office constitutionally, and governed through the institutions could not complete its programme. The anchor's first element is the plurality. Salvador Allende won the presidency in 1970 with the largest share of a divided vote, not a majority, and the coalition he led, Popular Unity, held a minority in congress throughout. Every structural feature of the experiment follows from that arithmetic: a transformation programme legislated by a government that did not command the legislature, ratified by an electorate that had not given it a mandate of the size the programme implied, and dependent for its legality on institutions staffed by its opponents. The candidate who states the plurality in the opening paragraph, without any percentage attached, has located the case's governing condition.

The anchor's second element is the flank. To the government's left stood a revolutionary movement, strongest in the shantytowns and the newer unions, that regarded the constitutional road as a corridor to be accelerated rather than a settlement to be defended, and that organised land seizures, factory occupations, and neighbourhood supply committees beyond the government's control and often against its instructions. The flank placed the government in a double bind that no tactical skill could dissolve. Restraining the flank meant policing the government's own most committed supporters with the state's coercive apparatus, splitting the coalition and demoralising its base; indulging the flank meant confirming the opposition's charge that legality was a façade, and driving the middle classes and the officer corps toward the coup the government existed to forestall. Allende's oscillation between the two imperatives was not indecision but the visible motion of a trap closing. A strong answer names the trap, because naming it converts the Chilean narrative from a story of mistakes into an analysis of a structure.

### Which constraint did the Chilean government never solve?

The relation between its mandate and its programme. It held a plurality of votes and a minority of seats while attempting a transformation that required sustained majorities, and it faced a flank whose mobilisation it needed and could not govern. Every other difficulty, economic, diplomatic, military, expressed itself through that unsolved relation.

Around the anchor gather the case's other examinable elements, and a candidate should know how each is weighted. The copper nationalisation of 1971, carried unanimously through congress, shows the programme's popular core and its legal method at their strongest. The acceleration of agrarian reform and the wave of factory seizures show the flank outrunning the law. The shortages, the black market, and the truck owners' strikes of 1972 and 1973 show the economy becoming the opposition's chosen battlefield, with the middle classes mobilised as consumers before they were mobilised as citizens. The constitutional conflict with the judiciary and the comptroller shows the institutions withdrawing cooperation one by one. The final element is the military's movement from constitutionalism to intervention, prepared by the doctrine that the armed forces stood above the political order they served. An answer that arranges those elements as pressures on the anchor, rather than as a chronicle of doom, performs the operation the feasibility question rewards.

## External action: weighing the documented pressure

No element of the Chilean case is more mishandled in examination answers than the role of the United States, and the mishandling runs in both directions. One weak script dismisses the intervention as a conspiracy theory, which the documentary record refutes: the declassified materials, including the findings of the Church Committee, establish sustained covert action, funding of opposition media and parties, economic pressure designed to make the economy scream, and encouragement of military plotting across the government's life. The opposite weak script makes the intervention the sole sufficient cause of the outcome, which the same record does not establish and which the case's internal structure contradicts: the plurality, the flank, the congressional minority, and the economic crisis were Chilean facts, not imported ones. The strong script weighs. It treats external action as a real and documented pressure that raised the cost of every internal difficulty, shortened the government's time, and assured the coup's authors of a welcome, while insisting that pressure explains the timing and manner of the ending better than it explains the existence of the crisis.

The weighing habit generalises across the cluster, and candidates should practise it as a form. Wherever an external actor appears in these cases, the United States in Cuba and Chile, the Soviet Union as patron and market, the examiner is testing whether the candidate can hold agency in two places at once. The folklore of both camps denies one side's agency: one folklore makes the local actors puppets of Washington or Moscow, the other makes them innocents in a world without power. The record supports neither. Cuban radicalisation was driven by Cubans responding to pressures that included American retaliation; Chilean polarisation was driven by Chileans within a conflict Washington funded and encouraged. Sentences that distribute causation in that way, with the local mechanism named first and the external pressure weighed second, are the sentences the higher bands are made of.

### Why must an answer on Chile refuse the single-cause explanation?

Because the evidence is plural and the question knows it. The plurality, the flank, the economic breakdown, the institutional withdrawal, and the documented external campaign each did work the others cannot be made to do, and a script that crowns one cause has promised not to weigh the rest. Markers read single-cause answers as advocacy in costume.

Feasibility questions add a further demand that candidates often miss: they ask for a judgement about a road not taken, and the judgement must be disciplined by the constraints rather than by sympathy. The disciplined answer on Chile runs as follows in structure, whatever verdict it reaches. It states what the road required: a growing majority, a restrained flank, a neutral military, and an economy stable enough to keep the middle classes inside the constitutional order. It then tests each requirement against the record, finding the first unmet from the start, the second unmeetable in principle, the third eroding, and the fourth destroyed by the interaction of reform, scarcity, and organised opposition. The verdict, whether it concludes that the road was impossible from the plurality onward or that a slower programme might have held the centre, is then earned by the tests rather than announced before them. An answer concluding that the experiment was doomed and an answer concluding that it was murdered can both score full marks by that route; an answer that assumes either from its first line cannot.

## Chile as evidence: the portable case

Examiners value Chile beyond its own questions because the case functions as evidence in arguments about other things, and candidates should learn to deploy it portably. In a question on revolutionary strategy, Chile is the electoral road's defining exhibit, cited by its defenders as proof that the attempt was sabotaged and by its critics as proof that the attempt was misconceived. In a question on the state, Chile is the case where the institutions of a constitutional order, legislature, judiciary, and armed forces, are watched withdrawing consent from an elected government one by one, which makes it the cluster's best material on the difference between holding office and holding power. In a question on political economy, the Chilean inflation and shortages serve as the standard exhibit on the costs of redistribution attempted at speed within an open and dependent economy. In a question on the Cold War in the third world, Chile demonstrates the hemisphere doctrine in action, documented rather than alleged.

Portability has a discipline of its own. The portable use works only when the case is stated in its constrained form, plurality, flank, and institutional withdrawal, rather than in its folkloric form, a socialist government destroyed by a coup. The constrained form can be carried into an argument because it specifies mechanisms; the folkloric form can only be carried into a peroration. Candidates should practise the three-sentence version of Chile until it is automatic: a plurality government attempted a legal transformation of property; its own flank's mobilisation and its opponents' institutional and economic resistance narrowed its room month by month; documented external pressure hastened an ending whose conditions were domestically built. Those three sentences, adapted to the question, are worth more than a page of narrative in any paper that examines the region obliquely.

## Thinkers to name: the shelf and the claims they carry

Examiners reward named thinkers when the name carries a proposition, and penalise the parade of names that carries none. The rule for this cluster is few names, each loaded. A name earns its place when the candidate can attach to it a claim specific enough to be wrong, a text where the claim lives, and ideally the critic who answered it. The shelf below is organised by the work each thinker does in an answer, because that is how a candidate under time pressure retrieves them: not alphabetically, but by the question's need.

José Carlos Mariátegui is the shelf's foundation and the region's claim to theoretical originality. The Peruvian founder argued in his Seven Essays of 1928 that the problem of the Indian was at root the problem of the land, that the indigenous community, the ayllu, preserved a living collectivism on which a Peruvian socialism could be built rather than imported, and that socialism in America, in his famous sentence, "must not be an imitation and a copy; it must be a heroic creation". Each element is examinable. The land claim grounds the agrarian reading of the region's revolutions. The ayllu claim reverses the orthodox sequence, in which communal forms must dissolve before socialism becomes possible, and it anticipates by decades arguments later made from the founder's own late writings on the Russian commune. The heroic creation sentence supplies the cluster's epigraph and its method: doctrine adapted to a formation, or doctrine as catechism. The critic to attach is the Aprista rival Haya de la Torre, whose continental nationalism contested the same ground, and the later scholarly charge that Mariátegui romanticised the community he had studied mainly from Lima. A candidate who can state the claim, the reversal, and the charge owns the region's first row of the shelf.

### What does a located thinker do inside an answer that a general claim cannot?

A located thinker converts an assertion into evidence. The general claim that Latin American Marxism adapted doctrine to local conditions floats above the record; the sentence that Mariátegui grounded Peruvian socialism in the ayllu, against the orthodox sequence, in 1928, can be checked, weighed, and argued with. Markers reward the checkable sentence because it proves reading, not hearsay.

The dependency shelf carries three names that must not be blurred, because examiners treat their differences as the school's internal history. André Gunder Frank supplied the slogan and the mechanism in their sharpest form: the development of underdevelopment, the claim that the metropolis developed by underdeveloping its satellites through the extraction of surplus along a chain running from the world market to the remotest village. Fernando Henrique Cardoso, writing with Enzo Faletto, supplied the correction from inside: dependency was a situation to be analysed historically rather than a fate to be denounced, and dependent development, industrialisation under foreign capital's dominance, was possible, which the stagnationist reading had denied. Ruy Mauro Marini supplied the mechanism's hardest version, super-exploitation, the claim that dependent capital compensates for its weakness by paying labour below the value of its reproduction. Moving from Frank's chain to Cardoso's situations to Marini's wage narrates the school's maturation in three sentences, which is the dependency row's opening move.

The theological shelf carries Gustavo Gutiérrez, whose Theology of Liberation of 1971 gave the current its name and its method, and Enrique Dussel, whose philosophy of liberation pressed the argument toward the victim of the system as the starting point of thought. Beside them stand the names of practice that examiners accept as evidence when attached to claims: Camilo Torres, the Colombian priest who joined the guerrillas and was killed in 1966, as the limit case of the current's radical wing; Óscar Romero, the assassinated archbishop of San Salvador, as its witness rather than its theorist. The strategists' shelf carries Ernesto Guevara, whose foco doctrine is treated in the strategy sections below, and Régis Debray, the French philosopher whose Revolution in the Revolution? of 1967 codified the foco for a generation and whose later self-criticism is itself examinable. The historian's shelf, for candidates in history papers, carries the names already met: Draper and Thomas on Cuba, and for Chile the memoir and documentary record assembled by the Church Committee's investigators, whose findings converted allegation into archive.

Two disciplines govern the shelf's use. Attach each name to one claim only in any single answer, since a thinker made to carry three claims carries none convincingly. And prefer the thinker whose claim the question actually engages over the thinker the candidate knows best, since markers can tell the difference between a shelf consulted and a shelf emptied. The candidate who names Mariátegui in a dependency answer, or Frank in a liberation theology answer, reveals that the names are decoration; the candidate who names each where the argument needs them reveals a map of the field, which is what the shelf is for.

## Dependency theory: stating the mechanism before judging it

Evaluation questions on dependency theory fail in a characteristic way: the candidate recites the vocabulary, centre and periphery, metropolis and satellite, unequal exchange, without ever stating the mechanism the vocabulary names, and then judges a theory that has not yet appeared on the page. The repair is to build the mechanism first, in the order its authors built it. The school's prehistory lies in the structuralism of the regional commission that Raúl Prebisch built at the United Nations and led from its founding years, arguing from the terms of trade that the world economy's gains pooled at the industrial centre while the periphery's primary exports bought less with each decade, so that free trade between unequal structures reproduced the inequality it claimed to dissolve. Prebisch was no Marxist, and the distinction matters: the Marxist dependency writers radicalised a diagnosis they inherited, replacing policy failure with structure, and reform with the claim that the structure was the problem.

Frank's version made the structure a single chain of extraction. From the sixteenth century forward, on his account, Latin America was incorporated into a world capitalist system whose every link, the mine, the plantation, the merchant house, the national bourgeoisie, passed surplus upward, so that the regions most tightly bound to the metropolis in the past were precisely the most underdeveloped when the binding loosened. Underdevelopment was therefore not an original condition to be outgrown but a product continuously manufactured, the obverse of the centre's development and unintelligible without it. The policy moral followed: no national capitalism within the chain could develop the satellite, since the chain was the mechanism of its underdevelopment; only rupture could. Cardoso and Faletto, writing from exile and from the Brazilian experience of industrial growth under military rule, refused the moral and complicated the mechanism: dependency described concrete situations, alliances among foreign capital, the state, and local classes, and some of those situations delivered industrialisation of a dependent kind, which an answer must be able to describe without treating it as either refutation or vindication of the whole school.

### How did the dependency school change what a development answer must contain?

It relocated the explanation. Before the school, a development answer explained a poor country's poverty by its internal deficiencies, its savings and its culture; after the school, the answer must address the country's insertion into a world structure, the direction of surplus flow, and the alliances that insertion sustains. Even its victorious critics write answers shaped by the relocation.

Marini's super-exploitation deserves separate handling because it is the version examiners use to test whether a candidate knows the school beyond its slogans. Where Frank's mechanism operates between countries, Marini's operates inside the dependent firm: capital that cannot match the centre's productivity and loses value through unequal exchange restores its margin by driving wages below the cost of reproducing the worker, lengthening the day, and intensifying labour beyond the limits the centre's labour movements had imposed. The dependent economy's internal market is thereby deformed at its root, confined to luxury goods for the minority and deteriorating wage goods for the majority, which explains the coexistence of modern enclaves and mass misery that purely external accounts leave as scenery. Whoever can state that mechanism holds the school's strongest card ready, and the evaluation that follows can then be honest about the school's weaker ones.

## Evaluating dependency theory: the critics and the verdict

The evaluation that lifts a dependency answer into the higher bands has three movements, and candidates should rehearse them in order. The first movement states the school's strongest form, which is Cardoso's rather than Frank's: historically specific situations of dependency, analysed through the alliances they sustain, with dependent development admitted as a real and contradictory outcome. The second movement deploys the named critics. Ernesto Laclau's early critique charged Frank with defining capitalism by exchange rather than by relations of production, so that a hacienda selling to a market counted as capitalist whatever its labour regime, and the charge forced the school to specify its mode-of-production claims. Robert Brenner's broader attack on what he called neo-Smithian Marxism argued that the school explained underdevelopment by trade and circulation while neglecting class relations and property structures inside each society, which were, on his account, where development's fate is actually decided. Robert Packenham's study of the school's reception added a sociological turn: doctrines flourish, he argued, partly because of the intellectual and political needs they serve, and dependency's career owed something to its convenience as a warrant for blaming external structures. The third movement delivers a weighed verdict, and the verdict most defensible on the record runs as follows in structure. The school permanently changed the questions development answers must address; its strongest mechanisms survive in specified domains, super-exploitation in labour regimes, the terms of trade in commodity dependence; its universal claims, that dependent industrialisation was impossible and that rupture was the only exit, were falsified by the region's own subsequent industrial history and by the East Asian cases it could not explain.

Candidates should notice what that verdict's structure does. It separates the school's analytical legacy, which is large, from its predictive record, which is poor, and from its policy moral, which divided even its authors. An answer that performs the separation can conclude for or against the school's continuing value and be marked as analysis either way; an answer that treats the school as a single thing to be praised or buried cannot. The same three-part separation, legacy, record, moral, is the cluster's general template for evaluating any doctrine, and it will recur in the strategy and theology sections of this guide because examiners reward it wherever it appears.

### What is the strongest objection to the dependency argument, and who made it?

The objection that the school mistook circulation for production. Laclau showed that Frank's satellites were capitalist by definition wherever they traded, whatever their labour relations; Brenner generalised the charge, arguing that trade cannot explain why some societies develop and others stall, since the same insertion yields different outcomes under different class structures. The objection stands against the school's universal form.

One further refinement separates strong scripts from very strong ones, and it concerns the school's own self-correction. Cardoso's later reflections, and the work of the Chilean sociologist of the school's second generation, treated dependency less as a theory to be defended than as a method to be practised: begin from the concrete situation, identify the pact of domination that sustains it, and trace how external and internal forces interlock in that case rather than in general. An answer that knows the school revised itself disarms the commonest marker's objection, which is that the candidate has learned the school's first edition. It also models the intellectual honesty the examination is ultimately testing, since a tradition that cannot correct itself is a catechism, and the candidate's business is to show the tradition thinking.

## Liberation theology: the borrowing and the boundary

Questions joining liberation theology to Marxism are set because the relation is genuinely difficult, and they are failed, reliably, by the sentence that the liberation theologians were Marxists or were communists in clerical dress. The accurate relation has a name in this guide: the mediation distinction. The theologians adopted from the Marxist tradition a set of analytical instruments, the reading of society through its structures of domination, the suspicion of ideologies that bless an order, the preferential attention to the poor as the place where a society's truth is disclosed, and the dependency school's account of the continent's insertion into the world economy. They refused the doctrine that carries those instruments in their original home: historical materialism as a total account of reality, atheism as its premise, and class struggle as an ultimate horizon overriding the theological one. Gutiérrez's own formulation defined theology as critical reflection on praxis in the light of faith, a definition in which the Marxist inheritance supplies part of the reflection's social analysis while the reflection's ground and goal remain theological. An answer that states the borrowing and the boundary in its first paragraph has answered the question's real demand.

The institutional record supplies the answer's evidence and its drama. The Latin American bishops' conference at Medellín in 1968, before Gutiérrez's book appeared, had already named institutionalised violence and committed the church to the poor in language the older order found startling. The base communities that spread through Brazil and Central America in the following decades gave the current its social body: small groups reading scripture against their own conditions, in a practice the theologians theorised and the hierarchy alternately blessed and feared. The Vatican's instructions of 1984 and 1986 then drew the boundary from the other side, accepting the option for the poor while condemning the reduction of the gospel to a political programme and the uncritical importation of class analysis. That double movement, narrated with the assassinations of Romero in 1980 and of the Jesuit scholars of San Salvador in 1989 recorded as facts of the current's cost, gives a script the row's full content.

### Why is it an error to call the liberation theologians Marxists?

Because it mistakes a borrowing for an allegiance and erases the distinction the theologians themselves policed. They took analytical tools, the structural reading of poverty and the dependency account, while refusing the materialist doctrine those tools serve elsewhere, and their institutional superiors condemned precisely the confusion the careless sentence commits. The error destroys the question, which exists to test the distinction.

The evaluation available on this row is a double one, and strong answers stage both directions. From the Marxist side, critics charged that the theology borrowed the analysis while declining the conclusions, keeping the diagnosis of domination and replacing the revolutionary subject with a church whose own hierarchy embodied the order under critique. From the theological side, critics charged that the borrowing was less innocent than advertised, that class analysis imported a view of history and of the human person that no amount of careful distinction could fully quarantine. The candidate need not settle that quarrel; the candidate must show it exists, name its parties, and locate the current's historical significance where the record puts it: in the transformation of a continent's church, the formation of a generation of activists whose paths ran from the base communities into every subsequent movement, and the permanent alteration of what Christian social teaching must answer. The row's trap, calling the theologians Marxists, is avoided by the same habit that governs the whole cluster: state what a thing is before judging what it did.

## The Pink Tide: periodisation before judgement

The later period of the cluster, the wave of left and centre-left governments that governed much of the region across the opening decades of the twenty-first century, is where examination answers most often leave analysis for allegiance, and it is therefore where this guide's disciplines matter most. The first discipline is periodisation. The wave had a beginning in electoral victories won against the market-reforming order of the preceding decades, a height at which governments of the current held power across most of the region's population and product, and a recession as the commodity boom that financed it faded and its governments fell, were voted out, or exhausted their coalitions. An answer that fixes those phases, with their dates stated as period boundaries rather than as news, has already done the analytical work the question rewards, because it has converted a slogan into a period with a structure.

The second discipline is the refusal of the singular. The wave was not one project. It contained governments that pursued constitutional refoundation and the nationalisation of commanding resources, governments that administered a redistributive settlement inside an export economy they did not attempt to transform, and governments whose leftism was chiefly rhetorical over a conventional administration. Treating those as a single movement with a single record is the row's characteristic error, the mirror image of the founding error on Cuba: a later label projected across unlike cases. The strong answer distinguishes at least two families, the refoundational and the redistributive, and tests its claims against one of each before generalising, if it generalises at all.

## Redistribution and transformation: the distinction that grades the period

Beneath the periodisation sits the distinction the brief for this cluster treats as compulsory, and candidates should be able to state it in a sentence each way. Redistribution is the transfer of income and services toward the poor within an unchanged structure of ownership and insertion: conditional cash transfers, raised minimum wages, expanded health and pension coverage, financed in this period overwhelmingly from the rents of a commodity boom. Transformation is the alteration of the structure itself: ownership of the commanding resources, the economy's place in the world division of labour, the composition of what it sells and the terms on which it sells them. The distinction grades the period because the wave's governments were, by the record, far more successful at the first than at the second. Poverty fell and inequality narrowed across the region during the boom years, achievements an answer should record without embarrassment; the productive structures, the dependence on commodity exports, the exposure to the cycle, emerged from the period substantially intact, a limitation an answer should record with equal plainness. When the boom ended, the redistributive settlements were exposed in proportion as they had been financed from rent rather than built on transformed capacity, and the wave receded along the gradient the distinction predicts.

### What distinguishes redistribution from transformation in the later period?

Ownership and insertion. Redistribution moves income toward the poor through transfers and services while the structure that generates the income stands unchanged; transformation alters who owns the commanding resources and what the economy sells to the world. The later governments achieved the first broadly and the second scarcely, and the distinction is the measure of both facts.

The commodity condition is the analytical hinge, and examiners listen for it. The boom in prices for the region's exports, driven by Asian demand, delivered windfall revenues to governments of every colour in the period, and the left governments' distinctive choice was to direct a larger share of the windfall downward than their predecessors had. That choice was real, and its social effects were real; but it means the period's central experiment cannot be read as a test of socialist construction, since the financing was rent and the test the structure sets, what happens when the rent falls, was the test the period's end administered. A strong answer attaches the condition to every achievement it claims for the period and every failure it records, because the condition is what makes the achievements and the failures intelligible as one story rather than as ammunition for two.

### How should an answer periodise the Pink Tide without narrating it?

By phases and conditions rather than by elections and personalities. State the ascent against the market-reforming order, the height financed by the commodity boom, and the recession as the boom faded; attach the redistribution and transformation distinction to each phase; and let governments appear as instances of families within the period, never as the period's meaning in themselves.

Candidates should also know the period's best analytical literature by its arguments rather than its titles. The strongest defence of the wave reads it as a democratic deepening: movements of the excluded converted into governments, constitutions rewritten to recognise the region's plurality, social rights entrenched where only market rights had stood. The strongest criticism from the left reads it as a missed transformation: rents consumed as transfers, extractive frontiers extended into indigenous territories whose movements had carried the governments to power, and the old structures modernised rather than replaced. The strongest criticism from outside reads the refoundational family as an institutional corrosion, the concentration of power in executives that weakened the checks a democracy needs. Stating those three readings and assigning each its evidence completes the row; the verdict, again, is the candidate's own to reach and the marker's to respect if it is earned.

## The armed road: the foco stated fairly before its record

Strategy questions are the cluster's most sensitive, and they are governed by two rules that this section states before any content. Explain a strategy's claim in its proponents' terms before judging it, and assert no figure for the violence any strategy or its suppression involved. The foco theory, drawn from the Cuban experience and codified by Guevara's writings on guerrilla warfare and by Debray's codification, claimed that a small armed nucleus, the foco, inserted into a countryside of grievance, could create the subjective conditions for revolution by its own example and action, rather than waiting for those conditions to ripen or building a mass party first. The claim inverted the orthodox sequence as boldly as Mariátegui had inverted the agrarian one: the vanguard need not wait for the class; the guerrilla detachment, in Guevara's formulation, was the party in embryo, and political and military leadership were to be fused in the mountains rather than divided between a city directorate and an army.

Stated fairly, the claim had a logic rooted in its case of origin. Cuba's insurrection had indeed begun from a tiny landing party, the cities' political organisations had indeed lagged the Sierra, and the dictatorship had indeed rotted under pressure until its army dissolved. The theory's error, which the record then exposed across a continent, was to generalise the exception. In country after country through the nineteen sixties, focos were inserted and annihilated, isolated from peasantries whose grievances did not translate into support for armed strangers, hunted by armies that had studied the Cuban case as carefully as the guerrillas had, and abandoned by urban movements that had been instructed to wait. Guevara's own Bolivian campaign of 1966 and 1967 is the record's centre: a foco that won no peasant recruits of consequence, divided the local communist party whose support it needed, and ended with its leader's capture and execution, in a campaign whose diary, published after his death, records the isolation in his own hand. Debray, captured in the same campaign, later subjected the doctrine he had codified to a searching self-criticism, which examiners treat as part of the record rather than as a footnote to it.

### What did the foco claim, and what did its record show?

Its claim: a small armed nucleus, inserted among a grievance-laden peasantry, could create a revolution's subjective conditions by exemplary action, fusing party and army and dispensing with the long prior building of a mass movement. The record, Bolivia above all, showed the nucleus isolated, the peasantry unrecruited, and the state forewarned.

The urban variant and the negotiated exits complete the row's content. Uruguay's Tupamaros translated guerrilla war into the city and were destroyed by a military that used their campaign to dismantle the country's democracy itself, a sequence examiners use to test whether candidates can hold a strategy responsible for consequences its authors did not intend. The Central American insurgencies of the following decades, mass-based rather than foquista in form, fought states to negotiated settlements in El Salvador and Guatemala rather than to victories, and their peace accords, with the insurgencies converted into political parties, pose the strategy question in its mature form: what the armed road achieved there was not power but a seat at a table, bought at a cost this guide's figures protocol forbids the candidate to quantify and requires them to acknowledge in kind rather than in number. Colombia's long insurgency and its eventual accord extend the same pattern into the present century, and the candidate should handle it by the same rule, as a sequence of strategy and negotiation analysed structurally, without commentary on the politics of the governments involved.

## The electoral road and the comparison examiners want

Against the foco stands the electoral road, whose exhibits are Chile before 1973 and, in a different register, the later period's movement-governments that arrived by ballot and governed within constitutions they sometimes rewrote. The comparison question, armed against electoral, is set so often because it tests the cluster's central skill: judging strategies against their own stated aims under their actual conditions, rather than against the candidate's sympathies. The armed road's stated aim was power seized and held by a revolutionary force; measured by that aim, its record in the region is one victory, Cuba, whose conditions the foco theory then misread, followed by defeats, stalemates converted into negotiations, and in several cases the provocation of military regimes more durable than the orders the guerrillas had fought. The electoral road's stated aim was transformation achieved through constitutional means with majority consent; measured by that aim, its defining case was destroyed before completion, and its later instances achieved redistribution while leaving transformation largely unattempted, which is a different failure and must not be blurred into the first.

A strong comparison therefore refuses the scoreboard form, so many victories against so many defeats, and works instead through conditions. It asks what each road presupposes: the foco presupposes a rotting state, a recruitable peasantry, and a terrain of concealment; the ballot presupposes institutions that will honour an adverse result, a military that will obey it, and time. It then observes that the region's history is largely the record of those presuppositions failing in alternation, and that the movements which endured were those that learned to combine organisation, elections, and social mobilisation in proportions set by their own country's conditions rather than by a continental doctrine. The verdict available, and defensible in either direction once the conditions are laid out, is that the debate between the roads was miscast as a choice of instruments when it was actually a question about states: what kind of order confronted the movement, and what that order would permit, provoke, or survive. Candidates who reach that level of the argument are writing the answer the question was designed to find.

### How does an answer explain a grievance without endorsing a method?

By separating diagnosis from prescription inside the sentence itself. State the conditions the movement cited, land hunger, exclusion, repression, as facts with their evidence; state its chosen method as a choice, attributable to its doctrine; and reserve judgement for the weighing of consequences, where it belongs. The separation lets a script explain any movement without enlisting in it.

Violence in these answers is handled under the discipline the whole cluster observes: factual, without detail, and unweighted. A candidate records that campaigns were fought, that states answered with counterinsurgency, that massacres and disappearances occurred and are documented by the commissions and courts that later investigated them, and that the numbers remain contested among the sources; the candidate does not narrate atrocity, does not rank sufferings, and does not permit the horror of a record to substitute for its analysis. That restraint is not squeamishness. It is the marker's evidence that the candidate is performing history rather than testimony, and it protects the script in rooms where the events remain within living memory and family experience.

## The figures protocol: what must never be asserted

This cluster's material is saturated with numbers that function as combatants, and the protocol governing them is absolute enough to be stated as a rule without exceptions. Do not cite figures for deaths, disappearances, or prisoners in any of the cluster's episodes of repression; do not cite casualty figures for any insurgency or counterinsurgency; do not cite contested electoral percentages, including for the Chilean election that made Allende president; do not cite economic indicators, growth rates, poverty counts, or inflation figures for the later period. Every such number is contested in the literature, disputed along lines a marker can predict, and unsourced in the examination room by definition, since the candidate cannot carry the archive into the hall. An answer that leans on a contested figure tells the marker that its author learned the material from sources that do not distinguish assertion from evidence, and the tell is expensive precisely because it is so easy to avoid.

What the candidate does instead is describe scale by the method the brief for this cluster prescribes: state the direction, name the mechanism, and explain why the estimates vary. Estimates of repression vary because the records were kept by the institutions doing the repressing, because the commissions that later counted worked under mandates of different breadth, because the categories, killed, disappeared, detained, tortured, are drawn differently by different counters, and because exile and clandestinity scattered the witnesses. A paragraph that explains that variation demonstrates more command of the material than any figure could, because it shows the candidate understands what a number in this field is: the output of a counting process conducted under political conditions, by parties with interests in the count. The same applies to the later period's indicators, where the honest sentence names the direction of poverty and inequality during the boom, names the rent that financed the movement, and declines the decimal places.

### Why does an unsourced figure damage an otherwise sound answer?

Because it reverses the answer's evidential posture. Everywhere else the script has shown claims being weighed and sources being doubted; the asserted figure shows a claim swallowed whole, and a marker who catches one begins to re-read the sound passages as possibly swallowed too. The damage is to credibility, and credibility is the currency the whole performance trades in.

## Is criticism safe? The candidate's real question, answered

Beneath the technical questions candidates ask about this cluster sits one they ask in the corridor and rarely in the classroom: whether criticising these projects in an examination is dangerous. The candidate has read that examiners are academics, that academics hold views, and that the projects examined here, a revolution, a constitutional road, a school of theory, a family of governments, attract strong allegiance and strong hostility in the culture at large. The fear runs in both directions. One candidate fears that a script concluding against the Cuban Revolution will meet a marker who admires it; another fears that a script defending the Chilean road will meet a marker who regards the whole tradition as refuted. This guide's answer, stated at its opening and repeated here because the whole cluster depends on it, is that evidenced analysis is what is assessed, and that a model answer concluding against any of the projects examined in this cluster can score full marks, as can a model answer concluding in their favour, provided in each case that the representation is accurate, the strongest opposing case has been met, and the judgement follows from material the script itself has weighed.

What actually risks marks, in either direction, is not the verdict but the substitution of characterisation for analysis. The script that calls a government a dictatorship or a beacon, that describes a revolution as a liberation or a catastrophe in its opening moves, has told the marker that its conclusions preceded its evidence, and markers of every persuasion discount such scripts for the same reason: the marking scheme rewards operations, and characterisation is not among them. The candidate who fears the marker's politics should therefore take the precise opposite lesson from the one fear suggests. Safety lies not in trimming the verdict toward what a hypothetical marker might wish to hear, since no candidate can know that and the attempt reads as evasion, but in making the verdict's foundations so visibly earned that a marker of the opposite persuasion must credit the performance while dissenting from its close. That is what standardisation, second reading, and published criteria exist to secure, and on well-run examinations they do secure it.

The counter-view must be stated honestly, because candidates will meet it and should know its tell. Some assessment formats do not want judgement at all: objective tests, short factual papers, and certain school-level instruments reward accurate coverage, dates, definitions, attributions, and penalise nothing so much as the essayistic flourish that displaces a required fact. In such rooms a conclusion against a project earns nothing and a conclusion for it earns nothing; the currency is coverage. The tell is in the instrument's form rather than in its topic: where the questions are many, short, and factual, where the mark scheme allocates credit point by point to named content, the candidate should supply coverage and keep judgement sheathed. Where the questions are few, long, and command-worded, discuss, evaluate, assess, to what extent, the judgement is the assignment and withholding it is the error. Reading the instrument before choosing the register is itself an examinable skill, and it is the complete answer to the corridor question: criticism is safe wherever analysis is the currency, and irrelevant wherever coverage is, and in neither room is allegiance legal tender.

### What separates naming a position from judging it, and why does the separation protect marks?

Naming a position reports what a project claimed and did, in terms its serious defenders would accept; judging it weighs the claim against the record and the critics. The separation protects marks because the first operation is checkable by any marker whatever their views, and it builds the credit on which the second operation, the candidate's verdict, may then draw.

There is a final refinement, concerning tone, that candidates consistently undervalue. The language of graded judgement, the vocabulary of more and less, of conditions and residues, is the dialect in which high-band scripts on this material are written, and its opposite, the language of total verdicts, is the dialect of the pamphlet. A conclusion that a project's record is mixed in specified ways, strong on one axis and weak on another, with the axes named, will always outscore a conclusion that the project succeeded or failed entire, because the record of every project in this cluster is in fact mixed in specifiable ways, and the marker is rewarding correspondence with the record. Candidates should build a small stock of such constructions and practise them until they are habits: the achievement was real and its conditions were rented; the mechanism worked where its presuppositions held and failed where they did not; the defence explains the intention and does not reach the outcome. Sentences of that shape are the sound of a mind weighing, which is the sound the examination exists to hear.

## Characterising a government and analysing a structure: the separation that guards the later period

One distinction in this cluster does more protective work than any other, and it deserves its own section because candidates blur it precisely where the material becomes politically warm. To characterise a government is to assign it a moral and political identity: to call it democratic or authoritarian, progressive or corrupt, a success or a disaster, in the vocabulary of the platform and the editorial. To analyse a structure is to describe the formation within which any government of the period had to act: the economy's insertion into world markets, the distribution of property, the organisation of the state, the coalitions those facts sustain and the constraints they impose. The first operation takes a side in a living argument; the second equips a reader to understand every side's behaviour, including the behaviour of governments the candidate admires and of governments the candidate deplores. Examination questions on this cluster ask for the second operation even when their wording flirts with the first, and the candidate who supplies analysis where characterisation was invited will be rewarded by markers across the spectrum, because analysis is the one offering every marker is trained to credit.

The separation has a practical technique, and it can be drilled. When a draft sentence names a government and an adjective, rewrite it to name a structure and a mechanism. The government that is called populist becomes, under the technique, a coalition financed by commodity rent and held together by transfers, whose room for manoeuvre narrowed as prices fell; the revolution called totalitarian becomes a state formed under siege conditions in which a single party fused with the administration and the security apparatus answered to it. Neither rewrite takes a side. Both say more than the sentences they replace, because the adjective closed the question the mechanism opens. Candidates should run the technique over their practice scripts until the structural sentence is the one that arrives first, since under time pressure the first sentence is the one that gets written.

The same separation governs the treatment of periods before the candidate's lifetime, where its protection is less obvious and equally real. It is as much an error of method to characterise the Cuban state of the nineteen sixties in the vocabulary of approval or condemnation as to characterise a government of the present day, because the operation fails identically in both cases: it substitutes the candidate's stance for the case's structure. The cluster's rule is uniform. States and periods are analysed; claims are weighed; verdicts are earned and attached to specified axes; and no government, dead or living, is characterised in lieu of an account of what it faced, what it commanded, and what it did. A candidate who holds that line can write about any case in the region, however contested, in any room, and the line itself, visible in the script, is among the strongest signals a marker receives that the candidate has been taught the discipline rather than the slogans.

## History papers: a revolution inside the Cold War, and a school above it

History candidates meet this cluster twice, and the two meetings reward different preparations. The first meeting is the cases: Cuba and Chile as episodes in the Cold War's global history, in the hemisphere's long negotiation with the United States, and in the century of decolonisation whose Latin American chapter began a century and a half before the African and Asian ones. For that meeting the preparation is the anchors and the sequences, the Cuban radicalisation ordered and explained, the Chilean constraints stated and weighed, with the documentary record, the declassified archives and the Church Committee's findings, cited as the kind of evidence that distinguishes the modern treatment from the polemics of the period itself. The second meeting is historiographical: the dependency school and the national historiographies it disciplined taught a generation to write the region's past from land, labour, and surplus rather than from presidents and constitutions, and a question on how the region's history came to be written in that way rewards the candidate who can assess the school as an approach, its questions and its blind spots, rather than merely borrowing its conclusions.

The history essay's characteristic failure on this material is the narrative that knows the ending. Because the outcomes are famous, the coup, the execution in the Bolivian ravine, the long embargo, candidates write the cases as tragedies unfolding toward a known close, and the actors become figures in a pageant rather than people choosing under uncertainty. The discipline that corrects it is the one this guide stated among the founding error's repairs: reconstruct what was true and what was knowable at each date, and let the outcome arrive as it arrived for the participants, as one possibility among several until the moment it closed. Markers in history papers are trained to detect retrospective inevitability and to reward its opposite, and the Cuban sequence is the cluster's best training ground for the skill, since no case in the region tempts the backward projection more strongly.

## Politics and international relations papers: strategy, the state, and the world economy

Politics papers take from this cluster three assets, and candidates should prepare each as a self-contained performance. The first is strategy: the foco and the ballot as rival theories of how power changes hands, each with presuppositions a political scientist can state and a record against which to test them, treated in the strategy sections above. The second is the state: Chile as the case of an elected government discovering the difference between office and power, Cuba as the case of a revolutionary state fusing party, administration, and coercion into a single apparatus, and the later period as a set of cases in which executives elected on anti-system programmes governed through the systems they had promised to replace. The third is the world economy: dependency theory as the region's contribution to the theory of international relations, the claim that the units of the system are not like units, that core and periphery are positions in a structure of surplus transfer, and that a state's foreign policy cannot be read without its insertion. A politics candidate who can run any of those three assets at length, with the critics attached, is prepared for the cluster's appearance in any politics room.

International relations questions add a demand of their own: they ask candidates to weigh the hemisphere's hegemon as an actor among others, neither omnipotent nor incidental. The weighed treatment developed for Chile above is the model. Covert action, economic pressure, and diplomatic isolation are documented facts of the record and belong in the answer; the internal structures that made those instruments effective, or that generated crisis without them, belong there too, and the candidate's skill is shown in the proportions. Proportions are not a compromise between two folklores. They are a judgement about mechanisms, about which pressures operated on which decisions at which dates, and an answer that assigns its causes by date and decision will always outscore one that assigns them by sympathy.

## Development studies: the founding quarrel as living method

Development studies is the room where this cluster's theory does its most direct work, because the dependency school is one of the discipline's founding quarrels and the later period is one of its standard cases. The candidate's preparation should run in three layers. The mechanism layer states the school's accounts of underdevelopment in their strong forms, Prebisch's terms of trade, Frank's chain of extraction, Marini's super-exploitation, Cardoso's situations, so that the answer can deploy the version the question engages rather than a blended average of them all. The evidence layer holds the region's subsequent record ready: industrialisation that occurred without escaping dependence, commodity cycles that raised and then stranded whole economies, a redistributive period financed by rent and ended by its fall. The judgement layer holds the critics, Laclau and Brenner on method, the East Asian comparison on prediction, and the school's own revisionists on its behalf, so that the evaluation is a weighing of named positions rather than a mood.

Development questions increasingly ask candidates to advise as well as to analyse, to say what a government or an agency should learn from the region's experience, and the cluster supplies a disciplined form for such answers. The lesson is never a slogan, neither that markets fail nor that states fail, but a specification: which mechanism, under which conditions, produced which outcome, and what would have to be true for the lesson to travel. The candidate who writes lessons in that form, with the commodity condition attached to the later period and the insertion question attached to every national case, is performing the discipline's own method back to its examiners, which is the surest route to its higher bands.

## Latin American studies: the whole tradition in one room

Programmes in Latin American studies examine the cluster whole, and their questions reward the connective tissue other rooms treat as background. A question in such a room may ask how the region's Marxism differs from the European tradition it inherited, which is the Mariátegui question generalised: the answer runs through the communal land claim, the dependency relocation of the world economy's centre of gravity, the theological borrowing, and the strategic debate, and argues that the region's contribution was to make Marxism answer questions its founders had not faced, about indigenous peoples, dependent formations, and a church that could not be treated as scenery. Another question may ask candidates to account for the tradition's survival through the dictatorship decades, which rewards the exile and journal history: the reviews founded in Córdoba and Havana, the scholars who carried the debates into Mexican and European universities, the return of the exiles as the generation that staffed the transitions. Candidates in these rooms should prepare the tradition as a tradition, a conversation across a century with recurring questions, rather than as a shelf of separate topics, because the room's questions are set by people for whom the conversation is the subject.

## Civil service and eligibility examinations: compression and recognition

The competitive and eligibility rooms compress the cluster into two currencies, and candidates should train for each separately. The first currency is the compressed analytical answer of the optional and general papers, where the whole apparatus of this guide must be delivered at small scale: the governing distinction, one located example, one named critic, a verdict with its condition, in a few hundred words written against a clock that punishes hesitation. The drill for that currency is the matrix rehearsed as openings, each row's first paragraph written so often that it costs no thought, since in the compressed room the opening is where classification happens and classification is where the marks are. The second currency is recognition: the objective item that asks which thinker wrote which text, which concept belongs to which school, which sequence orders which events. Recognition is trained by the placement grid, thinker to text to thesis to critic, drilled to instant recall, and it is unforgiving of the blended knowledge that essay rooms forgive. A candidate strong in one currency and weak in the other should know which room they are entering and weight the final weeks accordingly, since the two trainings, compression and recognition, interfere with each other if mixed carelessly and reinforce each other if sequenced.

## School-level papers: the foundation treatment

School specifications treat the cluster's cases as part of the twentieth-century world, and their questions, though narrative in form, reward the same analytical moves at foundation scale. The Cuban question at school level asks why the revolution changed character, and the candidate who answers with the ordered sequence, reform, reaction, expropriation, declaration, has outperformed the candidate who answers with the folklore, whatever the specification's level. The Chilean question asks why a democracy fell, and the plurality and the flank, stated plainly, are the foundation form of the anchor. Teachers preparing school candidates should therefore resist the temptation to simplify the cases into their legends for younger students. The analytical version is not harder than the legend; it is merely different, and it has the advantage of being true, which the legend, examined closely by a bright sixteen-year-old, is not. The figures protocol applies at this level with full force, since school materials are among the worst offenders for asserted numbers, and a candidate trained to describe scale by direction and mechanism stands out in a school room exactly as in a university one.

## Command words and the allocation of effort

Every question in this cluster arrives wearing a command, and the command is the marker's contract with the candidate: it specifies which operations the scheme will pay for. Candidates lose marks in bulk by answering a neighbouring question to the one set, usually the one they prepared, and the command words are the guard against that loss. Explain asks for a mechanism laid out in order: the sequence questions live here, and the paid operations are ordering and causation. Assess and evaluate ask for a weighing: the criteria stated, the evidence arranged on both sides, a verdict that specifies its confidence. Discuss is the loosest command and the most dangerous, since it licenses range; the strong discuss answer imposes its own architecture, usually the matrix row's, on material that would otherwise sprawl. Compare asks for the two cases held against shared criteria, not described in adjacent solitudes; the strategy questions live here. To what extent is the cluster's most honest command and should be answered with a proportion, large on one axis and small on another, never with a yes or a no wearing qualifications as decoration.

Effort follows the commands. In a paper of several questions, the candidate's scarcest resource is not knowledge but minutes, and the allocation should be decided in the reading time before a word is written: which questions play to the anchors, which to the shelf, which must be answered from the matrix's general discipline alone. A question on the cluster's core should receive its full share and no more, since the marginal mark for the fifth page of a strong answer is small beside the mark for the first page of a competent one. Candidates habitually over-invest in the question they love, which on this material is usually Cuba, and starve the question they fear, which is usually the theory one; the allocation discipline exists to reverse exactly that instinct, because markers compare scripts across the whole paper and the starved answer's floor costs more than the loved answer's ceiling gains.

## Introductions and conclusions for this topic

The introduction on this material has three jobs and should be built to do them in four sentences at most. It assigns the question to its row, it states the governing distinction the answer will run on, and it names the anchors or the shelf the argument will draw from. Nothing else belongs there: not the candidate's summary of the whole topic, not a definition copied from a textbook's first page, not a promise of what the answer will show that the answer must then be twisted to keep. A model opening for the Chilean feasibility question runs in structure as follows: the question concerns a road, not an outcome; the road's conditions were a growing majority, a governed flank, and a neutral coercive apparatus; the answer tests each condition against the record and finds the failure distributed rather than singular. The marker has been told, in three sentences, that the script will weigh rather than narrate, and the rest of the script's task is to keep that promise.

Conclusions are where weak scripts on this cluster go to become pamphlets, because the candidate, released from the discipline of evidence, reaches for the large verdict the material seems to invite. The strong conclusion does the opposite: it gathers the answer's own findings, states the proportion they support, and attaches the condition under which the proportion would change. It refuses three temptations by name. It refuses the total verdict, the project vindicated or condemned entire, because the answer's own axes have shown a mixed record. It refuses the present-day application, the lesson addressed to living politics, because the guide's separation forbids it and the marker's scheme cannot pay for it. And it refuses the new evidence, the fact or the thinker produced at the close like a witness kept in reserve, because a conclusion that needs new material confesses that the body was misallocated. A conclusion of four sentences that performs the gathering, the proportion, and the condition has closed the answer in the same register that opened it, and symmetry of register is itself a band signal.

### What should the conclusion of a comparison answer refuse to do?

It should refuse the scoreboard and the coronation. A comparison earns its close by stating what the shared criteria have shown about each case's conditions, and by identifying the variable that best explains the divergence; it forfeits the close when it totals victories, crowns the road the candidate prefers, or dissolves the comparison into two separate verdicts stapled together.

## Anatomy of a strong answer: the Cuban sequence walked through

Take the cluster's most common substantial question, some form of why the Cuban Revolution became a socialist revolution, and watch a strong answer being built. The opening assigns the row and states the distinction: the question is about a transformation, and the answer will explain it as a sequence of reform and reaction rather than as the unfolding of an intention. The first movement establishes the starting point with the founding error explicitly refused: a broad coalition, a nationalist programme, a communist party that arrived late and sceptically. The second movement works the sequence link by link, agrarian reform creating constituency and enemy together, expropriation internationalising the conflict, retaliation licensing consolidation, each link given its mechanism and its date. The third movement stages the historiographical argument, design against driven, with the evidence types weighed and the archival complications admitted. The close delivers the weighted verdict, destination not inscribed, direction set early by the reform's logic, speed and form owed to choices under pressure, and stops. Nothing in that architecture is decorative; every movement performs an operation the scheme pays for, and the answer's length is whatever the operations require.

Notice what the walked answer does not contain. It contains no casualty figures for the insurrection or the repression that followed it, no characterisation of the state that resulted, no detour into the leader's biography beyond what the design thesis requires, and no paragraph narrating the insurrection's battles, which belong to the warehouse and not to this question. The discipline of exclusion is half the craft, and candidates should practise it deliberately: for each prepared topic, write the list of what a strong answer leaves out, and keep the list with the matrix.

## Anatomy of a strong answer: the Chilean feasibility walked through

The feasibility question, could the Chilean road have succeeded, is the cluster's purest test of disciplined counterfactual thinking, and its strong answer has a recognisable skeleton. The opening states the anchor: a plurality government, a transformation programme, a flank it could not govern. The first movement specifies the road's requirements as its own theorists understood them, majority growth through demonstrated competence, legality maintained as the experiment's defining claim, the armed forces held inside their constitutional doctrine. The second movement tests the requirements in turn against the record: the majority that did not grow, the flank that grew instead, the doctrine that dissolved as the officer corps polarised, the economy whose crisis converted the middle classes from spectators into combatants. The third movement weighs the external campaign as an accelerant acting on materials already combustible, documented and real, neither sovereign nor negligible. The close reaches its verdict by proportion: the road's failure was overdetermined in its conditions and contingent in its timing and manner, a sentence that candidates may then tilt, toward the structural or toward the contingent, according to the weight their own weighing has given, with full marks available at either tilt provided the tests were honestly run.

The skeleton's virtue is that it disciplines sympathy in both directions. The candidate who believes the road was murdered must still run the tests, and the tests show the flank and the plurality doing their work before any external hand is needed. The candidate who believes the road was impossible must still weigh the external campaign, and the archive shows it operating with resources and intent that no honest answer can wave away. Examiners set the question because it forces that double honesty, and they reward the script in which both movements are visibly performed.

## Anatomy of a strong answer: dependency theory evaluated step by step

The theory question completes the set of three anatomies, because its failure mode is the opposite of the narrative ones: not story without analysis but vocabulary without mechanism. The strong answer opens by assigning the row and promising the mechanism, then delivers the school's accounts in their strong and distinct forms, Prebisch's structural inheritance, Frank's chain, Marini's wage, Cardoso's situations, each in a compact paragraph that a marker can tick against the literature. The second movement runs the evaluation template: legacy separated from record, record separated from moral. The critics enter by name with their arguments specified, Laclau on the definition of capitalism, Brenner on circulation and class, the East Asian cases on prediction, and the school's internal revision is credited as the tradition correcting itself. The close states the weighed verdict, the school's questions permanent, its universal answers falsified, its strongest mechanisms surviving in specified domains, and the candidate's own position on the school's continuing use is then free to fall where the weighing has put it. Three anatomies, three rows, one discipline: assign, anchor, weigh, conclude in proportion. A candidate who has internalised the discipline owns the cluster; a candidate who has only stocked the warehouse owns a library they cannot spend under time.

## Mistakes and misreadings: the catalogue markers keep

Markers on this cluster keep an informal catalogue of the errors they meet every season, and candidates should study it as directly as they study the content, since each entry names a repair this guide has already supplied. The first entry is the founding error, the communist movement installed in Cuba from the victory onward, repaired by the dated sequence. The second is the single-cause Chile, the coup explained by intervention alone or by economics alone, repaired by the weighing discipline. The third is the singular Pink Tide, several unlike governments fused into one project with one record, repaired by the families and the periodisation. The fourth is the theologians mislabelled, liberation theology's practitioners described as Marxists, repaired by the mediation distinction. The fifth is the asserted figure, the casualty count, the percentage, the poverty decimal, repaired by the figures protocol. The sixth is the characterised government, the living or dead administration praised or damned in place of a structure analysed, repaired by the separation drilled in its own section.

Four further entries complete the catalogue and are worth naming because candidates commit them in good faith. The seventh is the borrowed verdict, the conclusion of a famous historian or a famous enemy reproduced as the candidate's own without the weighing that would earn it; markers recognise the famous sentences and mark the borrowing, not the brilliance. The eighth is the anachronistic shelf, dependency vocabulary applied to the colonial centuries without the school's own periodisation, or the later period's categories carried back into the nineteen sixties, which tells the marker the concepts are decorations rather than instruments. The ninth is the missing flank, the Chilean answer that weighs government, opposition, and Washington and never mentions the mobilisation to the government's left, an omission as diagnostic as the founding error because the flank is the case's analytical centre. The tenth is the conclusion that changes the subject, the closing paragraph that leaves the question to address the region's destiny or the candidate's hopes, which no scheme can pay for and every marker has learned to skim. A candidate who can recite the catalogue and match each entry to its repair has converted the cluster's folklore about itself into preparation.

## Party positions are not analysis: the note-sellers' trap

A commercial literature surrounds examinations on this material, digests and model answers sold by the topic, and its characteristic product deserves a warning because candidates mistake its fluency for the discipline this guide teaches. The note-sellers' version of the cluster is written from the movements' own self-descriptions, supplemented by their enemies' where a market exists for those, and its answers arrive with their conclusions pre-installed: the revolution heroic or criminal, the road noble or doomed, the school vindicated or buried, according to the list the publisher serves. Such material is not useless for facts, dates, and the skeleton of events, and a candidate short of time may mine it for the warehouse. Its danger is structural: it trains the candidate to reproduce a position in the belief that reproduction is what the room rewards, and the room, as this guide has insisted from its opening paragraph, rewards the operations and not the position. The tell that separates the two literatures is the treatment of the strongest opposing case. The note-sellers' answer caricatures it or omits it; the analytical answer builds it fairly and then answers it, and a candidate can apply that test to any preparation material, including this guide, before trusting it with a season's work.

## Examples that work: a stock that earns its place

An example earns its place in a timed answer when it is located, brief, and load-bearing: located in a place and a date, brief enough to cost three lines rather than thirty, and carrying a claim the argument needs rather than decorating one it has already made. The cluster's reliable stock, rehearsed until each item can be deployed in a sentence, runs as follows in prose. The agrarian reform of May 1959 as the sequence's hinge, the act that made the Cuban revolution social. The defection of the moderate ministers across the following year as the revolution's internal barometer. The February 1960 sugar pact as the alliance's true birthday, sealed after the expropriations rather than before them. The unanimous congressional vote for copper nationalisation in 1971 as the Chilean programme's popular core and legal method at their strongest. The truck owners' strikes as the moment the Chilean middle classes entered the conflict as an organised force. The Medellín conference of 1968 as the theological current's institutional beginning, before its founding text appeared. The Bolivian diary as the foco's refutation in its author's own hand. The peace accords of El Salvador and Guatemala as the armed road's mature outcome, negotiation rather than victory. Each item is a fact with a function, and the function, stated in the same sentence, is what the marker pays for.

### What makes an example earn its place in a timed answer?

Location, brevity, and load. The example is fixed in place and moment the marker can verify, it costs a sentence rather than a paragraph, and the argument would be weaker without it because it carries a mechanism or a turning point. An example that merely illustrates what prose already claimed is decoration, and decoration is what timed answers cannot afford.

Candidates should build the stock themselves from the cluster's articles, eight to twelve items per row of the matrix, and rehearse the deployment rather than the item: the sentence in which the reform's date does its work, the sentence in which the strikes convert an economic claim into a political one. A stock known as facts but not rehearsed as sentences will desert the candidate under time, because the room tests retrieval in the form the answer needs, and the answer needs sentences. This is the sense in which the revision method below insists that candidates rehearse the answer and not the topic: the topic is known in the study, the answer is performed in the hall, and the stock is the bridge between them.

## Precision work: the terms that must not blur

Every cluster has pairs of terms whose blurring costs marks out of proportion to the words involved, because the blur tells the marker that the concepts were learned as atmosphere. In this cluster the pairs are these. Radicalisation and revolution: the revolution is the seizure and its victory, the radicalisation is the transformation of the revolution's character across the following years, and an answer that uses the second word for the first event has lost the sequence before beginning. Dependency and dependence: dependence is a condition any economy may exhibit, dependency is the school's claim about a structure that produces underdevelopment, and the candidate who writes of a country's dependence when the question asks about the theory has answered a different and weaker question. Redistribution and transformation, whose separation grades the later period and whose blur converts its record into either triumph or fraud. Borrowing and allegiance, the theological pair, whose blur manufactures Marxist priests. Plurality and majority, the Chilean pair, whose blur dissolves the anchor. Explanation and endorsement, the strategy pair, whose blur makes every account of a guerrilla movement read as its recruiting pamphlet and every account of a coup read as its justification.

### Which pair of terms causes the most damage when a candidate blurs it?

Redistribution and transformation, because the blur governs the largest quantity of marks. The candidate who cannot separate moving income from changing ownership cannot grade the later period at all, and will write its record as either a triumph the evidence contradicts or a fraud the evidence contradicts, when the evidence supports a precise and interesting mixture of achievement and limit.

Precision extends to names and dates, the small coin of credibility. The candidate who dates the socialist declaration to 1961 and the victory to 1959, who gives Mariátegui his Seven Essays and Gutiérrez his Theology of Liberation, who knows that Debray codified what Guevara practised and that Cardoso corrected what Frank proclaimed, accumulates a credibility that carries the script's larger judgements. The candidate who scatters near-misses, the declaration misplaced by a year, the theologian's book assigned to the strategist, spends credibility the judgements then cannot draw on. Markers rarely annotate the small errors; they simply adjust the trust with which the rest is read, which is why the precision work, unglamorous as it is, belongs in the final fortnight's drills beside the matrix itself.

## The evaluation stock: critics a candidate can deploy

Evaluation is the operation candidates most often promise and least often perform, because performing it requires a stock of critics held ready with their arguments attached. The cluster's stock, organised by target, is compact enough to memorise and rich enough to serve every row. Against the Cuban design thesis, the archival historians and the revolution's own internal record; for it, the early Draper and the memoirs of defectors, each to be weighed as evidence of a type. Against the driven thesis, Farber's insistence that the authoritarian form was chosen, and the observation that pressure explains direction less well than it explains speed. On Chile, against the external sole cause, the internal record of plurality and flank; against the internal sole cause, the Church Committee's archive. On dependency, Laclau and Brenner on method, Cardoso on his own school's behalf, the East Asian record on prediction. On the foco, the Bolivian campaign itself, Debray's later self-criticism, and the Central American movements' own abandonment of the form. On the later period, the three readings already staged: democratic deepening, missed transformation, institutional corrosion. A candidate who holds that stock can evaluate in any room, because every question in the cluster is, at the point where marks multiply, an invitation to deploy two entries from it against each other and judge the issue.

## The examiner's view: schemes, standardisation and the second reader

Candidates imagine marking as a solitary reader with opinions, and the image feeds the corridor fear this guide answered earlier; the reality, on any examination worth the name, is a system built to make opinions expensive. Scripts are marked against a scheme agreed before the reading begins, in which the credit attaches to operations and content points rather than to conclusions. Markers are standardised on seed scripts, their early marking checked against the agreed standard, and their drift corrected or their work reallocated. Borderline and sampled scripts are second-read, and a marker whose severity or leniency departs from the standard is brought back to it by process rather than by persuasion. None of that machinery makes marking mechanical, and judgement remains judgement; but the machinery's direction is uniform, toward rewarding the performed operations wherever they appear and toward discounting the performance that substitutes stance for operation. The candidate who understands the system stops writing for an imagined sympathiser and starts writing for the scheme, which is the only reader whose preferences can actually be known in advance.

The scheme's preferences, for this cluster, can be inferred from the questions themselves, and candidates should practise the inference. A question that asks candidates to explain a sequence is a scheme that pays for ordering and mechanism. A question that asks whether a road could have succeeded is a scheme that pays for constraints tested and causes weighed. A question that names a theory pays for the mechanism stated and the critics deployed. Reading the set questions of past papers with that inference in mind, asking of each what its scheme must be paying for, is among the highest-yield revision exercises available, because it converts the archive of old papers from a source of guessed topics into a map of rewarded operations.

## The untranslated shelf: a note on sources and standing

Candidates preparing this cluster from English-language materials should be told plainly, because it affects how they cite and how they judge their own readiness, that much of the tradition's primary literature was written in Spanish and Portuguese and that a significant portion remains untranslated or available only in partial and ageing renderings. Mariátegui's essays circulate in a standard translation, and the dependency classics have their canonical English editions; but the internal debates of the Cuban revolution, many of the Chilean movement's documents, a large share of the theological corpus beyond its famous titles, and the memoir literature of the insurgencies and the exiles exist for the English reader mainly in excerpt, in anthologies, and in the citations of the secondary literature. That is not a gap in the candidate's preparation, and no candidate should apologise for it or inflate around it. The rule is simple: cite the translated work actually read, accurately, with its claim stated as its author made it; do not gesture at untranslated works as though their authority had been consulted, since markers who know the field can tell the gesture from the consultation at a glance, and the gesture costs the credibility the citation was meant to buy.

The standing of sources matters in the other direction as well. Where the record is documentary and public, the declassified archives, the findings of official commissions, the published diaries and correspondence, the candidate can write with a confidence the memoir literature does not support, and strong answers signal the difference in their verbs: the archive establishes, the commission found, the memoir recalls, the polemic claims. Examiners read those verbs as the candidate's source criticism made visible, and on material as contested as this cluster's, source criticism made visible is among the most valuable signals a script can send.

## When the question takes a side: hostile and loaded stems

Some questions arrive with a verdict built into the stem, a quotation asserting that the Chilean road was a fraud on the electorate, or that dependency theory was an excuse manufactured by failed governments, followed by the instruction to discuss. Candidates mishandle such stems in two directions: they adopt the stem's verdict as the answer's conclusion and merely illustrate it, or they spend the answer refuting the stem's author and never address the question the stem was set to open. The disciplined handling treats the stem as the first exhibit rather than as the judge. Restate the claim the quotation makes, in its strongest form and with its author's likely grounds; then test it against the record the cluster supplies, conceding what the record concedes and resisting what it resists; and close with the proportion the testing supports, which will rarely be the stem's proportion entire. The marker is watching for exactly that composure, since the loaded stem is set precisely to discover whether the candidate can hold a proposition at arm's length and examine it, on material where the candidate's own feelings are most likely to be conscripted by one side or the other.

## The short answer and the long essay: one stock, two scales

The cluster's stock must serve instruments of very different lengths, and candidates should practise the scaling as a drill rather than discovering it in the hall. The long essay spends the stock at full stretch: the sequence in its links, the debate in its parties, the verdict in its proportions. The short answer spends the same stock compressed to its load-bearing members: a governing distinction stated, an example placed, a verdict delivered with its condition attached, in the space of a paragraph or two. The compression is not a summary of the essay, and candidates who write summaries score as summarists; it is the same architecture at miniature scale, the moves performed in the same order with the connective tissue removed. The drill is to take each row of the matrix and write its short form until the short form can be produced on demand, because the short instruments are where strong candidates most often underperform, lulled by a preparation organised entirely around the essay they find congenial.

## Handling the unfamiliar question: when the paper surprises

Every candidate meets, sooner or later, a question that pairs topics never prepared together or approaches a prepared topic from an unprepared angle, and the cluster's discipline supplies the recovery procedure. Assign the question to its nearest row by command and object, as the matrix section taught. Open with the row's governing distinction, which buys the script its classification and the candidate their composure. Then work the question from the anchors outward: almost any Latin American question can be honestly approached through the sequence, the constraint, the mechanism of dependency, or the redistribution distinction, because those are the cluster's load-bearing structures and the unfamiliar question was set, whether its setter knew it or not, on ground they support. Close with the proportion the material reached supports, and resist the temptation to pad the unfamiliar answer with the prepared one; markers detect the substitution instantly, and the honest partial answer, visibly reasoning from real stock, outscores the disguised full answer that answers something else.

### How does a candidate recover when a question pairs two topics never prepared together?

By finding the relation the pairing asserts and making it the subject. A question joining dependency to the later period asks how a structure outlives the governments that denounced it; one joining the foco to the theologians asks where each believed history is made. Test the setter's implied argument; never write two summaries side by side.

## Timed practice: rehearsing the answer, not the topic

Preparation for this cluster divides into stocking and rehearsing, and the second is where most candidates' hours are misspent, because rereading feels like work and rehearsing feels like exposure. The rehearsal that pays is specific, and its raw material should be genuine: past papers for the drilling are most efficiently worked through [the ReportMedic previous-year question explorer](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html), which lets a candidate rehearse against real stems rather than invented ones. Write the opening paragraphs of every matrix row until each costs no thought, since openings classify scripts. Write each row's full answer once under time, and dissect the script against the mistakes catalogue with a pen in a second colour, logging every error under its catalogue heading. Rewrite the dissected answer's weakest movement only, since wholesale rewriting teaches less than surgical repair. Then maintain the cluster in short rotations, a timed script and a pass over the cards at lengthening intervals, because the marks in this territory are held by freshness of the moves rather than by depth of storage, and depth without freshness is the commonest way strong candidates underperform on material they once commanded.

The error log deserves emphasis because it converts practice from repetition into correction. A candidate's errors on this material are rarely random; they cluster, the asserted figure habit, the blurred pair, the conclusion that crowns a road, and the log makes the cluster visible, which is the precondition of repairing it. Before each timed session the log is reread rather than the content, a five-minute discipline that candidates resist and markers reward, since the repaired habits are exactly what separates the script that performs the operations from the script that knows about them.

## The agrarian reform as hinge: the sequence's load-bearing link

Candidates who can explain why one measure, among all the new government's acts, carries the sequence's explanatory weight possess the first anchor in its working form. The agrarian reform law of May 1959 did four things at once, and a strong answer can enumerate them as mechanisms rather than as effects. It created the revolution's mass constituency, binding the rural poor to the new order by the strongest of ties, the land they now held by its grant. It destroyed the old order's economic base in the countryside, expropriating the great estates and the cattle companies whose owners had anchored the previous regime's power. It struck foreign property at scale, since the largest holdings were American, and thereby internationalised the revolution's defence. And it split the governing coalition along its true fault line, between those who had joined to restore a constitution and those who intended a social transformation, a split the resignations of the following months made public. A measure that creates a constituency, destroys a base, summons an external enemy, and divides a coalition is not one event among others; it is the point at which the revolution acquired its direction, and examiners reward the candidate who can show the four mechanisms working rather than the candidate who dates the law correctly and moves on.

The reform also trains a transferable skill, the identification of the hinge in any sequence. Most processes examinable in this cluster have one act after which the range of possible outcomes narrows sharply: for Chile the hinge candidates are the copper nationalisation, which locked the programme's logic in place with unanimous legitimacy, and the first truck owners' strike, which announced that the conflict had acquired a social army outside the institutions. For the later period the hinge is the commodity turn itself, the moment prices fell and every settlement financed from rent had to be renegotiated under scarcity. Candidates should practise naming hinges and defending the nomination, because the nomination is an argument, and arguments are the currency: the marker who disagrees with the chosen hinge still credits the candidate who chose one and showed its mechanisms over the candidate who narrated all events at equal weight.

## The fusion of the organisations: consolidation as evidence

The institutional sequel of the Cuban sequence supplies evidence that candidates underuse, because it discriminates between the design and driven explanations more finely than the famous speeches do. Across 1961 and 1962 the revolutionary organisations, the July 26 Movement, the old communist party, and the student directorate, were fused into a single integrated body, the ancestor of the refounded Communist Party of 1965. The fusion's manner is the evidence. It was accomplished with purges and with a public scandal over sectarianism, in which the old party's cadres were accused of monopolising posts in the new apparatus and their most prominent organiser was removed and exiled, an episode that sits awkwardly with the design thesis: a movement executing a concealed plan does not usually need to purge the very party the plan supposedly served. It sits equally awkwardly with a purely driven account, since the choice of the single-party form, among the organisational futures available, was a choice, made by identifiable people for stated reasons of unity under siege. The candidate who can use the fusion in that double-edged way, as evidence that cuts both theses and refines each, is performing the weighing operation at its highest level, and examiners reserve their warmest marginalia for it.

The Committees for the Defense of the Revolution, founded in 1960 to organise vigilance at the level of the city block, belong in the same movement of an answer, as the consolidation's social depth made visible. Whatever judgement a candidate reaches on them, and the judgements in the literature range from popular participation to social control, their existence by the hundreds of thousands within two years of the victory is a fact about the revolution's character that the sequence must explain: a government confident of passive consent does not build such an apparatus, and a movement without genuine popular roots cannot. Holding those two readings together, apparatus of control and evidence of mobilisation, without collapsing into either, is the cluster's discipline in miniature.

## The embargo as evidence: a long act with two readings

The United States embargo, assembled in stages from 1960 and maintained across the decades that followed, enters examination answers most usefully as evidence in the argument about the revolution's character rather than as a grievance or an excuse. One reading treats the embargo as the siege that explains the siege state: a small economy cut off from its natural market and supplier, driven to the Soviet connection for survival, and hardened internally by the permanent emergency the blockade maintained. The other reading treats the embargo as the revolution's alibi, a standing external explanation for failures of production and distribution that the domestic system generated unaided, and points out that the siege form preceded the embargo's completion and survived every relaxation of pressure. A strong answer weighs the two by the sequence's own method, asking what the embargo can and cannot explain by date: it can explain the Soviet alliance's depth and the economy's deformities of supply; it cannot explain the organisational choices of 1961, which were made while other futures remained open, nor the persistence of those choices when pressure varied. The candidate who assigns an external fact its exact explanatory jurisdiction, no more and no less, has learned the cluster's central skill in its purest form, and the skill transfers to every external actor in every case the region offers.

## The other Chilean road: the centre's failure as context

Answers on Chile routinely omit the case's necessary prehistory, the failure of the centrist reform project that preceded Popular Unity, and the omission weakens them because the feasibility question is implicitly comparative: the road not taken must be specified before the road taken can be judged. The Christian Democratic government of Eduardo Frei, elected in 1964 on a programme called the revolution in liberty, attempted the moderate version of the transformation: agrarian reform within the law, the Chileanisation of copper by negotiated purchase, the mobilisation of the marginalised through state-sponsored organisation. Its record by 1970 was a study in the squeeze that defines the anchor. The reform's pace alienated the right without satisfying the left; the landowners resisted, the peasants occupied, the parties of the left denounced the government's gradualism while organising the constituency its reforms created. The centre's failure taught both flanks their lessons: the left concluded that gradualism was a corridor to exhaustion, the right concluded that reform itself was the corridor to revolution, and the electorate of 1970 distributed itself among three candidacies that between them left the winner with the famous plurality. A candidate who opens the Chilean answer with the centre's exhaustion has explained why the experiment began with so little room, and the explanation is worth more than a page spent on the experiment's final crisis.

## Mariátegui in depth: the communal claim and its examiners

The Peruvian founder rewards a depth of treatment beyond the shelf entry, because his case concentrates the cluster's theoretical question: what happens to a doctrine formed for industrial Europe when it is thought seriously from a formation it never described. Mariátegui's Peru was a country of coastal export enclaves, a sierra of great estates worked by an indigenous peasantry under labour regimes descended from the colony, and highland communities, the ayllus, that had preserved collective landholding through four centuries of pressure. Orthodox doctrine, in the form the international movement then taught it, prescribed a sequence: the communal forms would dissolve, a national bourgeoisie would develop the country, and socialism would arrive at the end of a development Peru had barely begun. Mariátegui's Seven Essays refused the sequence at its root. The Indian's problem was the land's problem; the latifundio was not a feudal survival awaiting capitalism's solvent but a functioning part of the export economy; and the ayllu was not a relic but a living institution of collective labour from which a Peruvian socialism might be constructed, an Indo-American socialism grown from the formation's own materials.

Examiners probe the claim's vulnerabilities, and candidates should hold them ready rather than leaving the founder in hagiography. The romanticisation charge, pressed by later scholars with the community's internal hierarchies in view, asks whether the ayllu of the essays is the institution the ethnography finds, stratified, patriarchal, and entangled with the estate economy, or an idealisation assembled for an argument. The stages charge, pressed from the orthodox side in his lifetime and since, asks whether skipping the bourgeois stage names a strategy or a wish, and points to the subsequent century's difficulty in building socialism on communal foundations anywhere. The organisational charge notes that the founder died in 1930 with his party newly founded and his movement already splitting over his legacy, so that the doctrine's institutional test never came. A strong answer stages those charges after the claim's full statement, and reaches the verdict the record supports: that the communal claim's value lies less in its ethnography than in its method, the refusal to treat a European sequence as a law of other people's history, which is the founding gesture of everything original in the region's tradition, from the dependency relocation to the theologians' borrowing.

### How does the communal claim reverse the orthodox sequence?

The orthodoxy required communal forms to dissolve into private property and wage labour before collectivism could be rebuilt higher; Mariátegui proposed to build from the communal form directly, developing the ayllu's collective practice under modern conditions rather than destroying it as progress's tuition. The reversal makes the survival the foundation, the region's first theoretical originality.

## Prebisch and the commission: the school's respectable parent

Dependency theory's prehistory is examinable in its own right, because the relation between the Marxist school and its structuralist parent tests whether a candidate can draw a boundary inside an intellectual family. Raúl Prebisch, the Argentine economist who led the United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America from its founding years, argued from the statistics of trade that the world economy was divided between an industrial centre that retained the fruits of its technical progress in higher incomes and a periphery whose own technical gains leaked abroad through falling prices for its primary exports. The policy conclusion was reformist and statist: industrialise behind protection, plan the substitution of imports, and correct by policy what the market's structure would otherwise perpetuate. Nothing in the programme required Marxism, and Prebisch's commission employed economists of many persuasions; its heresy was aimed at the doctrine of comparative advantage, not at capital.

The Marxist dependency writers took the diagnosis and replaced the remedy's premise. Where the structuralists saw a malfunction that policy could correct, Frank saw a system functioning exactly as constituted, in which the periphery's underdevelopment was the centre's development viewed from the other end, and correction required rupture rather than reform. The boundary matters for examinations because questions exploit it: the candidate who attributes the terms-of-trade argument to the Marxists, or rupture to Prebisch, has blurred the family at its joint. The strong answer narrates the inheritance and the quarrel together, noting that the school's founders were formed inside the commission's world, some as its employees and exiles, and that their radicalisation of its diagnosis is itself an instance of the cluster's pattern, a doctrine carried into a new formation and transformed by the carrying.

## Brazil as the dependency test case

Cardoso's correction of the school was not an abstraction; it was a reading of Brazil, and candidates who can run the Brazilian case possess the dependency row's strongest single performance. Brazil under military rule after 1964 industrialised rapidly while remaining dependent: foreign capital entered in alliance with the state and with domestic firms, the famous triple alliance that Peter Evans's study anatomised, and the economy grew by deepening the very insertion the stagnationist reading said must strangle it. Cardoso and Faletto's framework predicted the possibility where Frank's could not, because it treated dependency as a set of historically variable situations defined by their internal alliances, among which dependent development, growth with subordination, figured as one stable form. The case then discriminates among the school's versions as no thought experiment can: against the universal claim that dependency means stagnation, Brazil is the standing refutation; for the specified claim that dependency shapes the kind of development and distributes its fruits by the alliance's terms, Brazil is the standing confirmation, its growth accompanied by the wage compression Marini's mechanism describes.

The candidate's handling should mirror that discrimination. Deployed against Frank's strong form, Brazil closes the argument; deployed against the school entire, it overreaches, since the school's best version authored the categories the Brazilian analysis uses. Examiners set the case, or wait for it, precisely because it rewards the candidate who knows which claim a piece of evidence touches, the most transferable skill in the whole evaluation repertoire. The same discipline governs the East Asian comparison, which touches the school's predictive and universal claims while leaving its mechanisms, where their conditions hold, intact.

## The flank in depth: cordones, commands, and the government's dilemma

The Chilean flank repays detailed knowledge because it is the anchor's moving part, and candidates who can describe its organisation write the feasibility answer from inside the trap rather than from outside it. As the experiment radicalised, workers in the industrial belts of Santiago organised cordones industriales, territorial commands linking factories across a district, which coordinated production, distribution, and defence beyond the authority of the official union federation and largely beyond the governing coalition's discipline. In the shantytowns, communal commands organised supply and price vigilance; in the countryside, peasant councils pressed the agrarian reform faster than the law's timetable and beyond its legal limits. The revolutionary movement to the government's left, and the governing coalition's own radical wing, competed for leadership of those organs, which answered the question of where sovereignty was migrating with an clarity the formal constitution could not match. When the truck owners struck in October 1972, it was the cordones that kept factories supplied and production moving, a demonstration of capacity that proved the flank's indispensability and its autonomy in a single episode: the government survived by organs it did not control.

That is the dilemma in its concrete form, and an answer should state it without flinching in either direction. A government that dissolved the cordones would have disarmed its own defence and vindicated every charge that the constitutional road meant demobilisation; a government that blessed them conceded that a second power was forming inside its order, which the opposition, the courts, and finally the officer corps cited as the legal order's dissolution. Allende's actual course, alternately encouraging and restraining, read by sympathisers as vacillation and by enemies as duplicity, is better read as the trap's motion: each horn of the dilemma was genuinely ruinous, and the alternation was the sound of a statesman testing both. The verdict available to the candidate, once the flank is understood at this depth, is that the Chilean road's central problem was not a failure of will or of competence but a contradiction between the mobilisation a transformation requires and the legality a constitutional road promises, a contradiction no actor in the drama created and none could have argued away. That sentence, earned by the detail, is the feasibility answer's summit.

## The Soviet connection: patron, market, and limit

Cuba's turn toward the Soviet Union belongs in answers as a relationship with terms, not as an allegiance with a flag, and candidates should know its structure. The sugar agreement of February 1960 exchanged the island's staple for Soviet oil, machinery, and credit at the moment the American quota became an instrument of pressure; the connection deepened through the embargo's completion into a full integration, in which the island's economy was sustained by subsidised exchanges whose scale made the patron's fortunes the client's fate. The relationship's limits are as examinable as its benefits. The missile crisis of October 1962, settled between the superpowers over Havana's head and against its leader's furious objection, taught the client the exact measure of its standing: protected as an asset, consulted as an afterthought. The candidate who can hold those terms together, the lifeline and the leash in one account, writes about the Cold War's alliances as structures rather than as loyalties, which is the register international relations papers exist to reward.

The connection also disciplines the design argument from an unexpected side. A leadership executing a concealed communist plan from 1959 might have been expected to prepare the Soviet embrace in advance; the record shows instead a courtship conducted in public steps, each answering a pressure, from the first trade agreement through the arms purchases to the declaration, with the old party's Moscow connections serving as one channel among several rather than as the revolution's hidden wiring. Evidence of that kind, about timing and sequence, is the sort on which historiographical verdicts are actually built, and candidates should learn to prefer it to the memoirs' competing certainties.

## Base communities: the theological current's social body

Liberation theology is often answered as a shelf of books, and the stronger treatment restores its social body, because the current's historical weight lay in an organisational form as much as in a doctrine. The ecclesial base communities, small neighbourhood and village groups that gathered to read scripture against the conditions of their own lives, spread through Brazil's urban peripheries and the Central American countryside in the decades after Medellín, numbering by serious estimates in the tens of thousands in Brazil alone, though the candidate will note that this guide's protocol forbids leaning on any count and the communities' significance does not need one. In the communities, the poor of the theologians' texts appeared as interpreters rather than as objects: the reading method began from the community's reality, judged it in the text's light, and returned to action, a cycle the theologians formalised as the pastoral circle and the Marxist tradition would have recognised, in secular dress, as praxis. That recognition is the borrowing at ground level, deeper than any citation: a method of forming critical consciousness among the oppressed, kin to the literacy pedagogy of Paulo Freire, whose Pedagogy of the Oppressed, written from the same Brazilian northeast, supplied the current and the continent's movements with a shared educational grammar.

Examiners use the communities to test the mediation distinction in practice. The candidate who describes the communities as Marxist cells has committed the row's error at ground level; the candidate who describes them as devotional groups untouched by social analysis has missed the current entirely. The accurate account holds the two truths the distinction was built for: communities formed Christians who read their poverty structurally, organised cooperatives, unions, and land leagues from their readings, and supplied members to every movement of the period, while understanding themselves, and being understood by their theologians, as church. The assassinations that fell on the current's practitioners, catechists and priests killed in El Salvador and Guatemala in numbers this guide will not enumerate, belong in the answer as the record of what the borrowing cost when a state decided the distinction did not matter. The candidate records the fact, declines the figures, and lets the analysis carry the weight, which is the cluster's whole method in a single paragraph.

## Revision method: the cluster converted into cards and scripts

Efficient revision of this cluster follows from its architecture: since the marks attach to moves rather than to coverage, the revision units should be moves. The matrix becomes a set of cards in [the VaultBook study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html), one per row, carrying the governing distinction on one face and the trap on the other, with the row's anchor example and its named critic in the margin. The anchors become two rehearsed performances, the sequence ordered aloud in ninety seconds and the constraint stated in three sentences, practised until the performances survive distraction. The shelf becomes a grid for placing names, each thinker fixed to a book, a claim, and the critic who answered it, drilled in both directions, since recognition items ask it forward and essay openings need it backward. The critics become pairs, each thesis with its answerer, learned as couples rather than as singles, because evaluation is a relation and the memory should store it in the form the answer will use. Around those units the candidate wraps the drills already described: the openings written to automaticity, the timed scripts dissected against the catalogue, the error log reread before every session.

The sequence of the final weeks matters and can be specified. The warehouse closes first: no new content in the last fortnight, since late content arrives unrehearsed and displaces the moves that were ready. The middle week belongs to the timed scripts, one per row, dissected the same day while the performance's memory is warm. The final days belong to the compression drills, the short forms of every row, the placement grid at speed, and the precision pass over names and dates, ending with a single calm rehearsal of the two anchors the evening before. Candidates who invert that order, cramming content while their moves rust, arrive knowing more and scoring less, the commonest tragedy of well-read candidates on this material.

### How does a candidate revise this cluster in the final week without new material?

By converting stock into performance. The matrix rows become timed scripts, one each, dissected against the mistakes catalogue the day they are written; the anchors become ninety-second recitations; the shelf becomes a placement grid drilled at speed; and the last pass is precision only, names, dates, and the blurred pairs, with the error log reread before every session.

## The thirty-minute audit: checking a finished script against this guide

A candidate with half an hour of a timed session in reserve, or reviewing a practice script at leisure, can audit a finished answer against this guide in a fixed order, and the order is worth memorising because it runs from the expensive failures to the cheap ones. First, the founding facts: are the victory and the declaration dated and distinguished, is the Chilean plurality stated without a figure, are the theologians described by their borrowing rather than by an allegiance. Second, the figures sweep: search the script for every number that is not a date, and strike or convert each one that asserts a contested quantity. Third, the characterisation sweep: find every sentence that assigns a government an adjective of praise or blame, and rewrite it as a structure with a mechanism. Fourth, the anchors: does the answer's case material hang from the sequence or the constraint, or does it float as narrative. Fifth, the evaluation: is a named critic present with an argument, and does the verdict specify its axes and its condition. Sixth, the close: does the conclusion gather the answer's own findings, or does it crown, condemn, or wander into the present day. A script that survives the six passes has performed the cluster's discipline on itself, which is the closest a candidate can come to marking their own work as the scheme will.

## What mastery of this topic looks like

Mastery of this cluster has a recognisable profile, and candidates aiming at the highest bands should know its features so they can aim at the profile rather than at an abstraction. The master script is calm on contested ground: it states the strongest opposing case without caricature and without flinching, because its author has discovered that the opposing case, fairly built, makes the eventual verdict stronger rather than weaker. It is economical with the famous material, spending Cuba and Chile where the question needs them and declining to spend them where it does not. It dates its states of affairs, distinguishes its terms, declines its figures, and analyses its structures, by habits so practised that they survive the hall's pressure. Its conclusions sound like proportions rather than like banners. And its author can leave a question half-answered without panic when the stock runs out, because the discipline of the partial answer, reasoning visibly from real material, has been rehearsed as carefully as the full ones. None of that profile is a talent. Every feature is a drill in this guide, which is the guide's final claim: the distance between the folklore script and the master script is a set of named, practisable repairs.

## Sources and their standing: what the preparation should rest on

The preparation behind a strong performance rests on a hierarchy of sources, and candidates should know the hierarchy because questions about evidence reward those who do. At the base stand the primary texts in their standard translations: the Seven Essays, the Theology of Liberation, the dependency classics in their canonical editions, the published diaries and the documentary collections, read at least in the extracts the anthologies supply, since a claim cited from a text the candidate has opened carries a different weight from the same claim at third hand. Above them stand the scholarly histories, Thomas on Cuba, the archival literature that followed Sweig, the Chilean scholarship in its contending schools, Evans on the Brazilian alliance, each read as an argument with a position rather than as a quarry of facts. Above those stand the syntheses and the textbooks, useful for orientation and dangerous as a ceiling, since the textbook's settled version lags the scholarship by a generation and flattens precisely the quarrels the examinations reward. The candidate's reading should invert the usual proportions: less time in the synthesis, more in the quarrel, because the quarrel is the examinable substance and the synthesis is only its map.

## Nicaragua: the strategy debate's second laboratory

The Sandinista revolution of 1979 belongs in the strategy row as the case that complicated every doctrine the sixties had settled. The victorious movement was Marxist in formation and vocabulary, yet it won not by a foco's exemplary action but by a broad insurrection that fused guerrilla war, urban uprising, and a cross-class alliance against a dynasty whose kleptocracy had alienated even the business class that dictatorships usually protect. In power it attempted a mixed economy and political pluralism under conditions of mounting external war, and its decade became an argument about whether the siege explained the hardening, in the pattern candidates will recognise from Cuba, or whether the hardening was inscribed in the vanguard form itself. The electoral defeat of 1990, accepted by the movement and honoured with a transfer of power, then supplied the strategy debate with an exhibit neither road had predicted: a revolutionary government voted out and leaving office, which defenders cite as the pluralism's proof and critics as the revolution's epitaph. Running Nicaragua in that double form, as evidence weighed rather than as a banner carried, demonstrates the maturity the strategy questions are set to find, and the case's handling observes the usual disciplines: the war's costs described by their documented existence and their contested scale, never by figures, and the movement's later history treated as a period to be analysed rather than a banner to be saluted or spat upon.

## Mexico: the revolution that was institutionalised instead

A question on why some revolutions produce parties that rule for generations finds its Latin American exhibit in Mexico, and candidates in history and politics rooms should hold the case ready even though Mexico's revolution was not Marxist in doctrine. The revolution of 1910 and its violent decade destroyed the old order more thoroughly than most, and its victors then performed the operation the region's other movements rarely managed: they institutionalised the revolution itself, folding its armies, its labour movement, and its peasant leagues into a single party that governed for seventy years under successive names, absorbing opposition by incorporation and ruling through a corporatist architecture of sectors. The Mexican left's Marxist parties lived their whole history inside that architecture's shadow, alternately tolerated, co-opted, and repressed, most terribly in the massacre of students at Tlatelolco in 1968, an event candidates record as fact without figures in accordance with the protocol. The case's examinable value is the contrast it builds: where Cuba's revolution fused movement and state by ideology, Mexico's fused them by incorporation, and the comparison lets a candidate discuss what revolutionary institutionalisation does to a doctrine, a question examiners ask in forms ranging from the fate of the Mexican left to the general theory of parties that outlive their founding wars.

## The Vatican instructions: the boundary drawn from above

The theological row gains precision when the candidate can handle the Vatican's two instructions of the nineteen eighties as documents rather than as thunderbolts. The first instruction, of 1984, addressed the current's alleged reductions: faith dissolved into political praxis, the church's hierarchy replaced by a people's church defined against it, and class struggle installed as the motor of salvation history; its severity of tone made it the document the current's partisans cite as persecution. The second, of 1986, performed the distinction this guide has taught from the other side: it affirmed the preferential option for the poor as evangelical, endorsed a theology of liberation rightly understood, and reserved its condemnation for the borrowings that subordinated faith to a materialist account of history. Read together, the instructions map the boundary the theologians themselves claimed to observe, drawn by the institution with the authority to draw it, and the candidate who can summarise the pair accurately has the row's documentary core. The silencing of individual theologians and the disciplining of religious orders belong in the answer as institutional facts of the same record, stated plainly and without the vocabulary of either camp's pamphlets, since the row examines the candidate's ability to describe a church arguing with itself about a borrowing, which is a finer thing than either the romance or the denunciation allows.

## Exile and the journals: how the tradition survived its defeats

The tradition's continuity through the dictatorship decades is a set topic in the Latin American studies rooms and a useful reserve elsewhere, and its substance is a history of print and displacement. When the coups of the nineteen sixties and seventies closed the region's universities and parties, the debates migrated: to Mexico City, whose publishing houses and whose university became the continent's seminar in exile; to the journals founded or refounded abroad, among which the Córdoba review Pasado y Presente had already shown how a journal could carry a whole renovation of the doctrine; and to the European and North American universities that absorbed the exiled scholars and returned them, a generation later, with the transitions. Dependency theory itself was largely written in exile or under dictatorship, Cardoso and Faletto composing their classic as exiles in Chile and its sequel scattered by the Chilean coup, a biographical fact candidates may use because it explains the school's texture: a theory of structures built by men whom structures had personally displaced. The transitions of the nineteen eighties then repatriated the conversation, and the generation that staffed the restored democracies, as ministers, senators, and in Cardoso's case as a president who governed by a programme his younger self would have interrogated severely, carried the tradition's questions into office in forms its founders could not have foreseen. To narrate that survival as an institutional history, journals, universities, publishing houses, returns, rather than as a romance of ideas, is to perform the operation the room rewards: it treats thought as something with carriers, addresses, and rents to pay.

## Questions on decline: period discipline as the answer

Sooner or later every room asks some form of the decline question: why the tradition that produced the region's most original theory also produced so few durable victories, or why the movements weakened as their ideas conquered the universities. The disciplined answer refuses the question's implied single cause and works by periods, since the tradition's fortunes differ by decade and the causes differ with them. The armed generation was defeated militarily and politically by the early nineteen seventies, its doctrine refuted by its own record in the manner the strategy sections describe. The dictatorship decades destroyed organisations while dispersing ideas, a divergence the exile history explains. The transitions restored electoral politics on terms that rewarded moderation and punished the vocabulary of rupture, while the collapse of the European socialist states removed the tradition's external pole and its proof of possibility. The later period then revived the tradition's parties as governments of redistribution at the very moment its transformative programme reached its historical limit. A candidate who periodises the decline in that way, assigning each phase its mechanism, has answered the question at the level the setter intended; the candidate who answers with a single betrayal, a single error, or a single enemy has written the folklore of decline, which markers distinguish from its analysis at a glance.

## Discussion questions that build the answers

Seminar and tutorial preparation converts into examination performance when the discussion questions are set at the pitch the markers use, and the cluster's productive questions share a form: each forces a distinction this guide has named. Whether the Cuban outcome was inscribed in the movement or produced by its circumstances, argued with the fusion and the defections as evidence. Whether the Chilean road's contradiction was between mobilisation and legality or merely between a programme and its arithmetic. Whether dependency names a mechanism, a mood, or a method, with Brazil and the East Asian cases admitted as exhibits. Whether the theologians' borrowing can be quarantined from the doctrine it borrows from, argued from the Vatican documents and the communities' practice alike. Whether the later period's failure was a failure of nerve, of structure, or of the commodity calendar. A class or a study pair that argues those five questions to a standstill, with each side required to state the other's best case before answering it, has rehearsed every operation the cluster's papers reward, and the candidate who has survived such seminars finds the examination room quiet by comparison.

## Reading an exemplar closely: what the first page of a top script does

Published exemplars and the scripts teachers circulate reward a particular kind of reading, in which the candidate studies not the exemplar's content but its decisions. On the first page of a top script on this material, watch for four decisions in sequence. The question is assigned before it is answered, its row named in the opening sentences by the distinction the answer will run on. The folklore is refused early and explicitly, the founding error corrected or the single cause declined, so that the marker knows by the tenth line which species of script this is. The first anchor is laid down with its dates and its mechanism in a single paragraph that could stand alone as a compressed answer. And the opposition is introduced before the argument hardens, the critic named while the candidate's own position is still being built, which signals that the weighing will be genuine. A candidate who reads exemplars for those decisions, and then audits their own practice scripts for the same four, is using the exemplar literature as it deserves to be used: not as a text to imitate sentence by sentence, an imitation markers detect and discount, but as a repertoire of moves to internalise.

## The language of graded judgement: a phrasebook for this material

The phrasebook below is written as prose because its items must be absorbed as habits rather than consulted as lists, and each item is a construction a candidate can adapt rather than a formula to copy. For the mixed record: the achievement was genuine, and the conditions that financed it were rented rather than owned. For the weighed cause: the pressure hastened an ending whose materials were assembled at home. For the doctrine judged: the questions it taught the field to ask have outlived the answers it gave. For the strategy judged: the instrument was vindicated in the exception and refuted in the rule. For the case that resists both camps: neither the romance nor the denunciation survives the archive. For the conclusion that must specify: strong where its presuppositions held, silent where they did not, and instructive in both. Constructions of that shape share a grammar, a claim, a condition, and a proportion, and a candidate who writes six of them in an answer has given the marker six separate occasions to record that the script weighs what it touches. The opposite grammar, the unconditioned superlative, should be hunted through practice scripts and deleted wherever it appears, since on this material the superlative is never true enough to survive a knowledgeable reader.

## The workshop: converting notes into scripts

Between the stocked warehouse and the rehearsed performance stands a conversion process that deserves a section because candidates so often omit it, arriving with notes where scripts should be. The workshop runs in three passes over each row of the matrix. The first pass converts the row's content into an opening paragraph and a closing paragraph, the two pieces whose quality classifies the script, written in full sentences and learned verbatim if necessary, since verbatim openings are not a confession of weakness but a professional's economy under time. The second pass converts the row's middle into a sequence of paragraph briefs, each a single sentence naming the paragraph's operation, its evidence, and its critic, so that the full answer can be regenerated from the briefs rather than memorised as a block; block memorisation fails under the hall's pressure in a way that regenerative briefs do not, because the briefs survive the forgetting of any single paragraph. The third pass writes the answer once, timed, and dissects it. Candidates who run the workshop on all six rows possess something no amount of rereading supplies: six answers that exist as performances, with the cluster's remaining content held as reserve for the questions that surprise.

## Names, dates and attributions: the small precisions that classify a script

A final precision drill gathers the cluster's small facts into one place for the last fortnight's work, stated here as the sentences in which they will be used. The Cuban victory falls in January 1959 and the socialist declaration in April 1961, with the May 1959 agrarian reform standing between them as the hinge. The old party at first condemned the insurrection as an adventurist enterprise, then entered it late and on terms the victorious movement dictated. The Chilean plurality belongs to September 1970, the copper vote to 1971, the coup to September 1973, and the commissions that later counted the repression belong to the restored democracy, not to the dictatorship's own account of itself. Mariátegui's essays are of 1928 and Gutiérrez's book of 1971, with Medellín in 1968 standing before the book as its institutional precondition. Frank's manifesto essay is of 1966 and his book of 1967; Cardoso and Faletto's study was written in the later nineteen sixties and published at the decade's turn; Debray's codification is of 1967 and his self-criticism belongs to the following decade. Each of those sentences is a credibility deposit, and the candidate who can make them all without hesitation has finished the precision work the cluster requires.

A guide's exclusions are part of its teaching, and this one should state its own. It has not retold the cluster's stories, which live in the nine articles and in the histories those articles cite, because retelling is the warehouse and this is the armoury. It has not supplied model answers for memorisation, because a memorised answer answers the question its author imagined rather than the one on the paper, and markers are paid to notice the difference. And it has not offered a view on the politics of any living government, in the region or out of it, because the separation that protects candidates in the hall protects the guide on the page: analysis of structures is its whole business, and characterisation of governments is the one trade it refuses to ply.

## The great debate: Cuba argues about socialism itself

Few episodes in the cluster reward a candidate like the Cuban economic debate of the nineteen sixties, because it shows a revolutionary government arguing in public about the nature of the system it was building, with the arguments preserved. On one side stood the budgetary finance system associated with Guevara's ministry of industries: enterprises as branches of a single national firm, financed from the budget, without monetary relations among themselves, and with moral incentives, voluntary labour and consciousness, deliberately cultivated as the transition's engine, on the argument that building socialism with the weapons of capitalism, profit, individual material interest, the law of value left in command, would reproduce the consciousness the revolution existed to abolish. On the other side stood the economic calculus associated with Carlos Rafael Rodríguez and the Soviet manual: enterprise autonomy, self-financing, prices and profit retained as instruments, material incentives accepted as the lever a poor economy could not yet renounce. The Belgian Marxist Ernest Mandel and the French economist Charles Bettelheim intervened from abroad on opposing sides, which gives the candidate a debate with named parties at home and abroad, a rare luxury in this material.

The debate's examinable point is that it was never resolved on its merits; it was overtaken, first by the militarisation of the economy in the drive for the great sugar harvest of 1970, whose failure discredited voluntarism in practice, and then by the adoption of a Soviet-style planning system in the following decade, which the rectification campaign of the nineteen eighties in turn attacked in language borrowed from the defeated side. Narrating that arc, argument, overruling, failure, adoption, reaction, amounts to a complete answer to any question on socialist construction in the region, and possesses it in the cluster's preferred form: a debate with parties, evidence, and an outcome nobody intended. The debate also disciplines the Guevara portrait. The minister who emerges from its documents is neither the poster nor the demon of the two folklores but an administrator with a theory, whose strongest arguments were about consciousness and whose weakest were about accounting, and whose side lost. Examiners reward that portrait precisely because both folklores forbid it.

## Copper: the salary of a nation in an answer

Chilean answers gain a load-bearing paragraph from the copper industry, which the period's own language called the country's salary, and candidates should know how to spend it. Copper supplied the dominant share of Chile's export earnings and a decisive share of the state's revenue, and its ownership by two great American corporations made it the point where the dependency argument touched every Chilean household: the surplus question was not an abstraction but the national wage bill's foundation. Frei's Chileanisation bought the state a majority share by negotiation; Allende's nationalisation of 1971 took the whole industry by constitutional amendment passed unanimously, with compensation reduced by an excess profits deduction that the corporations and their government denounced and the Chilean congress, opposition included, had voted for in the amendment's own terms. The episode is the programme's legal method and popular mandate at their maximum, and its sequel is equally instructive: the nationalised industry thereafter had to be run, its engineers retained, its markets found under boycott pressure, its revenues made to carry a transformation whose other fronts were failing. Running copper from ownership through unanimity to administration compresses the Chilean case into a single commodity's biography, and the commodity's examinable moral is the cluster's recurring one: expropriation is an event, but ownership is a condition, and answers are graded on the condition.

## The economy as battlefield: scarcity, queues, and the middle

The Chilean crisis of 1972 and 1973 is often written as a morality tale by both camps, and the analytical treatment candidates should practise instead runs through mechanisms. A government committed to redistribution raised wages and froze prices while expanding the money supply and expropriating the commanding heights; production in the social area rose in the first year on idle capacity and then met the constraints of investment, imports, and skills. Scarcity appeared where prices were held below costs, distribution migrated to the black market and to the neighbourhood supply boards the government's supporters organised, and the boards themselves became a front in the political war, read by one side as popular organisation and by the other as partisan control of the dinner table. The truck owners, a stratum of small proprietors whose vehicles the economy could not function without, struck twice with financing whose external sources the later archive documented, and the strikes demonstrated that a transformation can be besieged through its logistics by classes the classical schema had classified as petty and therefore negligible. Inflation did the rest of the besieging, dissolving the real value of every wage gain the programme had delivered and converting the salaried middle, teachers, clerks, professionals, from the experiment's audience into its opposition. None of that requires the candidate to deny the documented external campaign, the credit squeeze, the invisible blockade on spare parts; it requires the candidate to place the campaign among mechanisms, amplifying some, exploiting others, creating none of the fundamental scarcities it fed upon. That placement, yet again, is the weighing operation, and the Chilean economy is its most demanding exercise because both camps' folklores are built from its true fragments.

## The old parties: loyalty, legality, and the cost of the line

The region's established communist parties form a background the answers too often skip, and their dilemmas supply context that examiners reward when it is deployed precisely. Founded in the Comintern's years and formed by its discipline, the parties carried into the postwar decades a strategy of stages and alliances prescribed from a centre whose priorities were not Latin American, and their histories record the cost: electoral fronts with bourgeois partners that dissolved on contact with power, insurrectionary lines adopted and abandoned on signals from abroad, and a reputation among the young for caution that the Cuban victory converted overnight into obsolescence. The Chilean party is the instructive exception that proves the pattern's interest: deeply rooted in the nitrate fields and the unions, committed across decades to the electoral road its continent's guerrillas despised, it entered Popular Unity as the coalition's most disciplined and most gradualist force, restraining where the flank pressed, and its reward was to share the destruction of a road it had done more than any other party to keep legal. Sketching that irony, the legalists destroyed by the illegality they had spent fifty years refusing, yields a paragraph that lifts any Chilean or strategy answer, because it shows the parties as actors with doctrines and dilemmas rather than as initials in a chronology.

The parties' second life, after the dictatorships, belongs to the periodisation sections: some dissolved into broader fronts, some survived as custodians of a name, and the tradition's centre of gravity moved from the party form toward the movement and the government of the later period. Examiners who ask about the left's organisational history are testing whether the candidate can follow a form's career, founding discipline, strategic inheritance, catastrophe, dispersal, and partial reassembly, and the career is best told through two or three parties handled in depth rather than through a gazetteer of initials.

## Indigenous movements and the tradition's unfinished argument

The later period returned the cluster's founding question, the place of the indigenous peoples in the region's Marxism, in a form the founder would have recognised and the parties had not expected. Across the Andes and into Mexico, indigenous movements rose in the decades around the century's turn as organised political subjects in their own right, with programmes joining land and territory to language, jurisdiction, and the demand that the state itself be refounded as plurinational. Their relation to the Marxist tradition was the Mariátegui argument resumed by other means: the movements claimed the communal inheritance the founder had theorised, while accusing the tradition's parties of a century of subordination, in which the Indian was to be dissolved into the peasant, the peasant into the proletariat, and every specific claim deferred to a revolution that would settle accounts later. The Zapatista rising of 1994 in Chiapas stated the accusation in its most quoted form, refusing the vanguard's seizure of power itself and demanding instead a world in which many worlds fit, a formulation examiners accept as the movement's own. The Bolivian movements carried the argument furthest, into government, where the encounter between an indigenous programme and an extractive economy reproduced, inside a single administration, the period's central tension between redistribution's financing and transformation's promise.

Candidates should handle this material as the tradition's living edge rather than as its epilogue, and with the usual disciplines doubled. The movements are analysed by their programmes and their structures, not celebrated or lamented; the governments they formed or opposed are periods and formations, not banners; and the founder's presence in the argument is noted as the examinable point, that a claim first made in Lima in 1928 about land and community remained, a century later, the ground on which the region's politics was still being contested. A question joining Mariátegui to the indigenous movements is among the finest the cluster offers, because it tests the whole arc: text, claim, reversal, charge, and return.

## The Special Period: an ally's collapse as an examination case

Cuba after 1989 supplies the cluster's case study in what happens to a dependent socialism when the dependence is withdrawn, and history and politics rooms both use it. The collapse of the European socialist states and then of the Soviet Union removed, within three years, the market that had taken the island's sugar at protected prices, the oil that had arrived below world cost, and the credit that had covered the difference, and the economy contracted by a magnitude candidates will describe through direction and mechanism rather than through any figure, as the protocol requires. The government's answer, named the Special Period in Time of Peace, combined emergency liberalisations it had spent decades refusing, foreign investment in tourism, the legalisation of the dollar, the reopening of farmers' markets, with an insistence that the social conquests and the political form were not negotiable, a combination the literature reads in the now familiar double way: as adaptation that saved the system by diluting it, or as the proof that the system's survival had become its only remaining programme. For the candidate, the case's value is that it replays the cluster's founding argument in reverse. In 1960 the question was whether alignment with a patron would deform the revolution; after 1989 the question was what the revolution consisted of once the patron's subsidy was gone, and an answer that can pose the second question with the first's evidence in hand has understood the case as examiners wish it understood, as a single long relationship with dependence, entered as a refuge and exited as an exposure.

## Uruguay's long road: the electoral left at its most durable

One further case belongs in the bank because it tests the electoral road under the region's most favourable conditions and therefore disciplines easy conclusions about Chile. Uruguay's Broad Front, founded in 1971 as a coalition of the left in a country of strong institutions, was proscribed by the dictatorship that followed, rebuilt through the transition, and eventually governed for fifteen consecutive years in the present century, administering a redistributive programme of notable social effect while leaving the export structure and the financial system substantially as it found them. The case's examinable uses are two. Against the claim that the electoral road is everywhere doomed by its Chilean exhibit, Uruguay shows a left governing, completing its terms, and surrendering office at the ballot box in an orderly transfer, in a small country whose institutions and whose flank were both unlike Chile's. Against the claim that the road transforms, Uruguay shows the same period's limit as its neighbours, redistribution achieved and structure conserved, in the country where the road faced the fewest excuses. Whoever holds the two uses together, the road vindicated as government and indicted as transformation by the same fifteen years, has the comparison the strategy row exists to produce, and the case's very quietness, no coup, no foco, no martyrology, is what makes it analytically loud.

## The Peruvian warning: a claimed inheritance and a contested one

Peru supplies the strategy row with its darkest exhibit and the thinker row with its most contested inheritance, and candidates should be able to handle both with the steadiness the whole guide has practised. In 1980 a party led by a former philosophy professor, Abimael Guzmán, launched an armed struggle from the Andean university city of Ayacucho, proclaiming itself the heir of Mariátegui and the bearer of a doctrine it named with its leader's own thought, and prosecuting a war against the Peruvian state and against the peasantry in whose name it claimed to fight, whose refusal to be liberated on the party's terms it answered with exemplary violence. The state's counterinsurgency replied in kind across the emergency zones, and the internal war consumed the country's politics for over a decade until the leadership's capture in 1992 by police intelligence work rather than by military victory. The truth and reconciliation commission that reported in 2003 assigned responsibility across the parties to the conflict, the insurgency bearing the largest share by its findings, and its figures, like every count in this cluster, are the commission's to state and not the candidate's to recite.

The examinable points are three, and none requires a single figure. The inheritance claim is contested at its root: the movement's Mariátegui was a founder conscripted, his communal and democratic instincts inverted into a doctrine of the leader's infallibility and the party's militarisation, and the scholars of the founder's work are among the claim's firmest refuters, which makes the case the cluster's standing lesson that a name on a banner is evidence of nothing but the banner. The strategy is the foco's negation from within the armed road itself: a protracted people's war built patiently over a decade of preparation, which nonetheless reproduced the foco's fatal isolation in a more terrible form, since a movement that makes war on its own claimed constituency has sealed itself more completely than any mountain encirclement could. And the ending instructs: the war was closed not by the army the insurgency had studied but by detectives following paper and rubbish in the capital, a reminder that states learn too, which candidates may set against every doctrine that treats the enemy as a fixed quantity. Handled so, the case is analysis; handled as atrocity narrative or as romance, it is the room's quickest route to the low bands, and markers know the difference in a paragraph.

## The movement's self-criticism as an answer resource

An underused stock in this cluster is the tradition's criticism of itself, and candidates who quarry it gain evaluations that no external critic can match for authority. Debray's later reckoning with the doctrine he had codified, in which the foco's theorist indicted the theory's contempt for politics and its cult of the military apparatus, supplies the strategy row with a verdict from the doctrine's own author. Cardoso's retrospective essays, in which the dependency school's co-founder assessed what the school had been for and what its categories could no longer do, supply the theory row with the same gift. The Cuban leadership's own rectification rhetoric, acknowledging the errors of the voluntarist decade in the act of repeating some of them, and the Chilean left's long post-coup self-examination, in which the coalition's parties dissected their maximalism and their illusions about the armed forces across a generation of exile writing, supply the case rows. Even the founder's tradition offers the pattern: the Peruvian movement's argument over Mariátegui's legacy began at his graveside and has never closed. The candidate's use of the stock follows a rule: quote the self-criticism for the fact it concedes, then weigh the concession as evidence, noting that a movement's own verdict on itself is an interested document like any other, valuable for what it admits against interest and suspect in what it excuses. That double handling, concession credited and excuse discounted, is source criticism again, and it is the last of this guide's techniques because it is the one that converts reading into judgement most directly.

## How much detail: calibrating depth to the room

Candidates ask, in the forums and the corridors, how much detail an answer on this material needs, and the honest answer is a calibration rather than a quantity. Detail is worth its cost when it performs one of three services: it dates a state of affairs, as the declaration's April does; it embodies a mechanism, as the cordones embody the flank; or it discriminates between explanations, as the fusion's purges discriminate between design and driven. Detail that performs none of those services, the battle narrative, the biography beyond the argument's need, the second example where the first is still working, is not neutral in a timed answer; it is a tax on the operations the scheme pays for, levied in the currency of minutes. The calibration differs by room in the manner the assessment map described: the long essay can afford a paragraph of setting for each movement of argument, the compressed answer can afford a clause, the objective item can afford nothing but the fact itself. Candidates should practise the calibration explicitly, taking a rehearsed essay paragraph and cutting it to its clause without losing its service, since the cut paragraph is the short answer's true embryo and the skill of cutting is the skill the compressed rooms examine under another name.

## Reading a paper's architecture: the questions as a set

A final examination skill, rarely taught, is to read the whole paper before answering any of it, treating the set of questions as the setter's map of the field. Papers on this material reveal their intentions in their combinations: a paper that pairs the Cuban sequence with a dependency evaluation is testing the movement from case to theory; a paper that pairs Chile with a strategy comparison is testing the movement from case to doctrine; a paper whose Latin American question stands alone among global questions is testing whether the candidate can import the cluster's discipline into a general performance. The reading takes minutes and repays them twice over, once in the choice of questions, since the candidate can select the combination their stock serves best where choice exists, and once in the writing, since an answer aware of its paper's architecture can borrow authority across questions, letting the sequence established in one answer stand as the premise of another without re-derivation. Setters design papers as arguments about what the field is; the candidate who reads the argument has been told, in advance, what the field's examiners think it is, which is the closest thing to a marking scheme a candidate will ever be handed before the day.

## The revolution institutionalised: Cuba's second decade as evidence

Answers on Cuba habitually stop at the declaration or at the missile crisis, and candidates who can carry the case into its second decade own material their competitors lack. The nineteen seventies brought the revolution's institutionalisation on the Soviet pattern it had once been expected to transcend: entry into the socialist trading system, a constitution in 1976 that formalised the party's leading role and created the assemblies of Popular Power, and a planning apparatus that replaced the voluntarist experiments the great harvest's failure had discredited. The decade matters to the design argument, because institutionalisation on an imported model is evidence about the destination's nature that neither the speeches of 1961 nor the memoirs can supply: whatever the leadership had intended in 1959, by 1976 it had built a state of a recognisable type, and the candidate must explain the type's adoption by the same weighing the sequence taught, part conviction, part dependence, part the logic of a siege that never lifted. The decade matters equally to the driven argument's refinement, since the institutionalisation coincided with the patron's subsidy at its height, and the candidate who notices that the system's most orthodox form was also its most subsidised has found the thread that runs to the Special Period and completes the case's arc.

The everyday texture of the system belongs in a strong answer as evidence rather than as colour. The ration book, the libreta, which guaranteed a subsidised basket to every citizen at the price of chronic shortage beyond it, is the revolution's social contract in a single artefact: equality of the floor, scarcity above it, and a population expert in the informal economies, the barter, the remittance, the tolerated illegality, by which the floor was supplemented. An answer that can read the ration book in that way, as an institution with a logic and a cost, has done with an artefact what the matrix does with a case, and examiners in the social history vein reward the move warmly. The same reading extends to the revolution's celebrated services, the health and education systems whose achievements the literature across the spectrum records and whose financing the subsidy underwrote, so that the honest sentence about them carries the by now familiar double structure: the achievement real, its conditions rented, and the renting visible only when the rent stopped.

## The farewell broadcast: a text and its afterlife

On the morning of the Chilean coup, with the palace under bombardment, Allende addressed the country by radio for the last time, and the address survives as the case's most studied text. Its famous sentence, "Workers of my country, I have faith in Chile and its destiny", repays the candidate's attention less as rhetoric than as evidence: a president who had staked everything on legality, refusing at the end both flight and the call to a civil war he judged his supporters would lose, and framing his death in advance as a seed rather than a defeat. The afterlife of the text is itself examinable. The dictatorship that followed spent seventeen years attempting to bury the experiment's memory, through censorship, through the rewriting of the record, through the conversion of the coup's date into a founding celebration; the resistance preserved the broadcast as its counter-founding document, circulated in cassettes and samizdat while its public utterance was forbidden. The commissions of the restored democracy then performed the state's own reckoning, establishing the repression's record on testimony and archive, and their existence, named and dated, is where an answer's account of the repression's scale should rest, as this guide's protocol directs: the commissions did the counting, the candidate cites the counters, and the figures stay where they were produced.

The candidate's handling of the broadcast models the cluster's treatment of all sacred texts, the founder's essays and the strategist's diary included. A text is evidence of what its author wished to be understood as meaning, at a moment whose pressures the answer must reconstruct; it is not a transparent window on the event it addresses, and it is not a relic to be venerated in the prose. The script that quotes the farewell sentence in order to analyse the choice it records, legality maintained past the point of its usefulness as the experiment's final claim on history, outperforms both the script that quotes it to weep and the script that omits it to seem hard-headed.

## Exile's harvest: the Chilean renovation

The Chilean left's response to its catastrophe, worked out across a generation of exile in Mexico, Europe, and the region's surviving democracies, supplies the cluster with its most instructive case of a tradition correcting itself, and candidates should hold it beside Debray and Cardoso in the self-criticism stock. The renovation, as its authors named it, began from the coup's autopsy: the coalition's maximalism had isolated it, its contempt for the political centre had been a strategic gift to its enemies, its theory of the armed forces had been a fantasy, and its understanding of democracy as a bourgeois form to be used rather than a value to be defended had left it unable to mourn, or to mobilise for, the order whose destruction it had treated as a formality. The renovated conclusions inverted the old certainties one by one: democracy as a good in itself and the only terrain on which socialism could be legitimate, alliances with the centre as a necessity rather than a betrayal, gradualism revalued, the market's existence accepted as a condition to be governed rather than an enemy to be abolished by decree. The European left's parallel evolution, the Eurocommunist moment and the Spanish transition's negotiated model, fed the renovation and was fed by it, the Chilean exiles serving as the argument's living evidence in every European seminar.

The renovation's examinable point is its sequel: the tradition that emerged from the autopsy governed Chile for a generation after the dictatorship, in coalition with the centre it had once disdained, administering an export economy whose structure it criticised and whose social fruits it redistributed with increasing ambition, which is the later period's pattern arriving early in the country that had paid most for the old certainties. Tracing that arc, catastrophe, autopsy, renovation, government, yields a complete narrative of how doctrines actually change: not by refutation in journals but by defeat in the world, metabolised by the defeated at the price of everything they had been. The candidate who reaches for the arc when a question asks about the tradition's prospects, rather than reaching for a prophecy, is writing the kind of history the cluster exists to teach.

## The workers' party: Brazil's union road to the later period

Brazil's path into the later period ran through an organisation the cluster's other cases do not supply, and candidates preparing the redistributive family should know its formation because it explains that family's character. The Workers' Party founded in 1980 grew from the great strikes of the industrial suburbs of São Paulo, where a new unionism had challenged both the employers and the military regime's labour code, and it gathered into one organisation the unionists, the base communities of the theological current, the returned exiles, and the intellectuals of the dependency generation. That triple parentage, factory, parish, and university, marked the party's programme from birth: socialist in allegiance, democratic in method by conviction rather than by tactic, and rooted in social movements it undertook to represent rather than to replace. Its long apprenticeship, three defeats at the presidential level across the nineteen nineties while it accumulated mayoralties and governorships, taught it the arts of coalition and administration before it won the presidency in 2002, and its governments then enacted the redistributive family's most complete programme: the conditional cash transfer consolidated and extended, the minimum wage raised in real terms year upon year, university places and credit widened, all inside an economic framework whose central bank, export model, and fiscal discipline it had once promised to break with and chose instead to operate.

The case's examinable tension is therefore the period's own, stated in its purest national form. A party born to transform governed to redistribute, and its defenders and its critics divide over the same record with the same facts: the poverty that fell and the structure that stood, the movements that gained a ministry and lost their independence, the financing that flowed while commodities boomed and the reckoning that arrived when they fell, ending in the impeachment crisis and the party's ejection from power amid corruption prosecutions whose political character is itself contested in the literature and which the candidate will therefore describe as a crisis and a contest, not as a verdict in either camp's vocabulary. Handled at that temperature, Brazil completes the later period's triptych beside the refoundational and the merely rhetorical families, and it gives the redistribution distinction its most documented body of evidence.

## The pact that broke: Venezuela as the wave's precondition

The later period's most dramatic national story began before the period itself, in the collapse of a party system once celebrated as the region's most stable, and the prehistory is examinable because it teaches how the wave's electorates were made. Venezuela's democracy after 1958 rested on a pact among its parties to share power and exclude the left, financed by oil rents distributed through the state, and for three decades the arrangement delivered stability at the price of a cartelised politics in which the two great parties alternated as managers of the same rent. When oil prices fell and the debt crisis bit, the pact's parties administered austerity to the population that had been promised the rent's permanence, and the explosion came in 1989, in the riots and repression of the Caracazo, whose dead the official and the independent counts have never agreed upon and whose number the candidate will accordingly not supply. The party system never recovered its legitimacy; the coup attempts of 1992 made a young officer its unlikely tribune, and his election in 1998 opened the refoundational family's most ambitious chapter, a new constitution, the oil industry brought under political command, and the rent redirected through missions to the poor on a scale the boom then financed.

The candidate's discipline for Venezuela is the guide's whole method under maximum pressure, because no case in the cluster attracts stronger allegiance or stronger hostility in the culture the candidate inhabits. The formation is analysed, rent, pact, cartel, collapse; the governments are periodised, ascent, boom, and the long emergency of the rent's fall; the record is stated by axes, the missions' social reach in the boom years and the institutional concentration that accompanied them, the economy's deepening dependence on the single export whose price the government did not control; and the catastrophe of the subsequent years is described by its documented mechanisms, the collapse of production and of the currency and the emigration of a population share the agencies count and the candidate cites as their counting, never as a figure of the script's own. A script that keeps that temperature on Venezuela can keep it anywhere, and examiners, who know exactly how hard the case is to write cold, mark the temperature as evidence of the discipline the whole cluster teaches.

## Neo-extractivism: the later period argues with itself

The later period generated its own internal critique, and candidates who can deploy it possess the period's most sophisticated evaluation, since it comes from the governments' own intellectual camp. The Uruguayan writer Eduardo Gudynas gave the critique its name, neo-extractivism: the pattern by which progressive governments, rather than transforming the region's insertion, deepened it, expanding mining, hydrocarbons, and industrial agriculture to finance redistribution, and thereby reproducing the dependency the tradition had diagnosed, with the state substituted for the foreign company as the extractor and the social programme as the legitimation. The critique's evidence ran through the period's defining conflicts, over mines and dams and roads in indigenous territories, where governments elected with indigenous support found themselves the defendants of extraction against the movements that had carried them, a collision the Bolivian and Ecuadorian cases made emblematic. The defence answered in the tradition's own terms: that rents captured by a democratic state and spent on the poor differ in kind from rents exported by a concessionaire, that the alternative to extraction for a poor country is not transformation but destitution, and that the critic's purity is a luxury of those who do not have to finance a clinic. Staging that argument, the period judging itself with its own concepts, and then weighing it against the commodity calendar that decided how long the defence's financing lasted, completes the later period's evaluation, and has it in the form markers trust most, because neither side of it comes from the period's enemies.

## Guatemala as template: the hemisphere's lesson of the fifties

History papers place the cluster's Cold War inside a longer arc, and the arc's first modern exhibit is Guatemala, whose treatment candidates should command as the template the later cases inherited. A democratically elected government under Jacobo Árbenz undertook in the early nineteen fifties an agrarian reform that expropriated the uncultivated lands of the great estates, above all those of the United Fruit Company, with compensation offered at the value the company had declared for tax purposes. The reform's collision with the company, whose connections reached into the highest levels of the American government, produced the hemisphere's first Cold War intervention of the new type: a covert operation, authorised in Washington and documented in its own archives, which manufactured an invasion by exiles, amplified it by radio propaganda, and induced the Guatemalan officer corps to withdraw its support, whereupon the government fell in 1954 and the reform was reversed. The candidate's use of the case follows the cluster's weighing discipline exactly. The operation is documented, not alleged; its success depended on the local instrument, an army that chose not to fight; and its consequences radiated for decades, through the civil war that followed the reversal and through the education of a generation of Latin American officers and revolutionaries alike, Guevara among the witnesses, who drew from Guatemala the lesson that a revolution which leaves the old army intact and the hegemon unappeased will not be permitted to finish. That lesson, learned at the template, explains the Cuban sequence's speed on the military question better than any doctrine does, and the candidate who can run the inference from 1954 to 1959 has connected the cluster's cases into the single history the examiners are examining.

## The women's movement: the tradition's internal frontier

No account of the cluster is complete for examination purposes without the current that pressed the tradition from inside on the question it had most confidently deferred. The region's Marxism, like its European parent, had treated women's subordination as a consequence of the property order that the revolution would dissolve in passing, and the women's movements that grew inside and against the left across the century's second half disputed the deferral with evidence from the movements' own households. In Cuba the revolution created its women's federation and mobilised women into literacy, militias, and the workforce on a scale the literature records across the spectrum, while the commanding heights of the party remained a male province and the double day survived the rhetoric that condemned it. In Chile the women's movement supplied the dictatorship's most inventive resistance and the experiment's most quoted internal slogan, democracy in the country and in the home, whose second clause indicted the left's own kitchens as precisely as its first indicted the junta. In Nicaragua the Sandinista women's organisation fought the same argument inside a revolution at war. The examinable pattern is the deferral's failure: each movement discovered that the revolution's timetable for women was always after, and the discovery produced, by the century's end, a feminist current that read the tradition's texts against its practice and demanded the account be settled in the present tense. Stating that pattern, with its national instances distinguished, puts the cluster's most reliable reserve paragraph at a candidate's disposal for any question on the tradition's limits, because the limit it names was named first and best by the tradition's own members.

## Students and detonators: the generation question

Examiners return periodically to the region's students, and candidates should understand why the topic recurs: the universities were the one institution where the tradition reproduced itself across every closure, and student movements served repeatedly as the detonator of crises their elders had failed to ignite. Mexico's 1968, in which a student movement in the capital was answered at Tlatelolco with a massacre whose toll remains contested and unrecited here, stands as the type case of a modernising authoritarianism revealing its limits before the world's cameras in an Olympic year. The Chilean universities incubated both the experiment's cadres and its flank's theorists, and the Peruvian university of Ayacucho incubated an insurgency, three outcomes from one institution that warn candidates against any single theory of what students are for. The thread that examiners reward is institutional rather than romantic: the university concentrated the region's scarcest resource, literate youth with time to read and grievances of their own, in cities where the state's legitimacy was thinnest, and every current in this cluster, party, foco, theology, and renovation alike, was staffed disproportionately from its lecture halls. A question on generations in the region's politics is therefore answerable from the cluster's whole stock, and the candidate who treats it as a question about institutions rather than about youth's eternal fire will write the answer the setter hoped to provoke.

## Argentina: the working class that chose another banner

Argentina poses the cluster's most instructive negative question, why the region's most industrialised and unionised working class gave its durable allegiance not to Marxism but to Peronism, and candidates in politics and history rooms should hold the case as the tradition's standing puzzle. Juan Perón, as labour secretary and then president in the nineteen forties, built the unions into the state's architecture, delivered wage gains and social rights on a scale no Argentine left government has matched, and gave the working class a political identity, Peronist, that proved heritable across proscription, exile, and massacre. The Marxist parties, divided between a socialism that accommodated itself to anti-Peronist coalitions of the respectable and a communism that followed its international line through every reversal, spent the postwar decades competing for a constituency that regarded them, not without evidence, as the parties of the people who despised Perón's people. The revolutionary generation of the nineteen sixties answered the puzzle by attempting to enter the banner rather than defeat it: the Montoneros fused guerrilla war with a claimed Peronist identity, grew into the largest urban insurgency of the continent's decade, and were destroyed in sequence by the returning Perón's own right wing and then by the military dictatorship of 1976, whose campaign of disappearance the subsequent trials and the mothers of the Plaza de Mayo made the hemisphere's emblem of state terror, recorded here as fact and, by the protocol, without its contested count.

The case's examinable yield is a law candidates can carry anywhere: a doctrine does not win a class by being correct about its interests, but by being present at the moments when its conditions were actually improved and its dignity actually recognised, and the Argentine left's tragedy was to have analysed the working class brilliantly while another movement fed it. Setters ask the Argentine question to discover whether a candidate can explain a tradition's failure by its rivals' successes rather than by its own errors alone, which is the weighing operation applied to the cluster's most painful exhibit.

## The Southern Cone's long night: dictatorship as a period

Between the Chilean coup of 1973 and the Argentine coup of 1976, and reaching back to Brazil's of 1964 and Uruguay's of 1973, the Southern Cone passed through a coordinated season of military rule whose unity as a period candidates should be able to state, since several rooms examine it as a single phenomenon. The regimes shared a doctrine, national security, which defined the enemy as internal and ideological, a war without fronts against subversion in the universities, the unions, the church, and the family; they shared techniques, the disappearance of persons as a method designed to terrorise without leaving the evidence a trial could use; and they shared, as the declassified record and the later trials established, a machinery of cross-border collaboration in the hunting of exiles, the arrangement known as Operation Condor. The candidate's handling observes the protocol at its strictest: the period's existence, its doctrine, its methods, and its documentary record are stated; its toll is left to the commissions and courts of the restored democracies, whose findings the answer cites as the counting's home. What the period did to the tradition is the examinable substance: it annihilated the armed generation, imprisoned and exiled the parties, and drove the survivors into the renovations and reassessments this guide has stocked, so that the left that returned to govern in the following century was, in personnel and in doctrine, the dictatorships' own unintended product. An answer that can hold the period whole, doctrine, method, collaboration, and consequence, without a single contested figure and without a single adjective doing an analysis's work, has passed the cluster's hardest test of temperature, and markers reserve for such answers the marks that distinguish a class.

## The Caribbean's other revolution: Grenada in miniature

A short case completes the strategy bank by miniaturising its themes. Grenada's New Jewel Movement, a Marxist party in the Caribbean's smallest state, took power in 1979 by a nearly bloodless coup against a corrupt and eccentric dictatorship, governed for four years as a revolutionary government of notable energy in health, education, and the mobilisation of its tiny population, and then destroyed itself in 1983 through the classic pathology of the vanguard form: a leadership dispute inside a central committee, resolved by the house arrest and then the killing of the prime minister, Maurice Bishop, by a rival faction's soldiers, whereupon the United States invaded within days and the experiment ended in occupation and trials. The case's examinable value is its scale, which strips the cluster's arguments to their skeletons. The siege reading and the chosen-form reading confront each other with almost no mediating mass: the external hostility was real and avowed, and the self-destruction that invited the invasion was the movement's own work, performed in a week, against a leader whose popularity the invaders' own polling acknowledged. To weigh Grenada in a paragraph, neither camp's miniature but both camps' evidence, is to demonstrate the proportion the whole guide has taught, and setters who include the small island are testing precisely that: whether the discipline survives when the case is too small to hide behind.

## The continental imagination: from Indo-America to the Tricontinental

The tradition's frame was continental from its birth, and candidates should know the frame's career because questions on the region's identity reward it. Mariátegui wrote of Indo-America as the unit of the coming socialism, a continent joined by a colonial past and an indigenous depth that no national frontier divided; Haya de la Torre built APRA as a continental alliance on the same intuition from the rival camp, so that the region's two founding movements of the left both thought in continents before they thought in countries. The Cuban revolution continentalised the frame in practice: its foreign policy of the nineteen sixties, the Tricontinental Conference at Havana in 1966 and the organisation of solidarity with Asia and Africa that followed, proposed the hemisphere's revolution as one front of a global war, and Guevara's final campaigns were the frame's logic carried into a life. The dependency school then gave the continental intuition its analytical form, since a theory of centre and periphery cannot be written from inside one small economy, and the later period inherited the frame as diplomacy, in the region's own institutions and summits from which the northern hegemon was absent. The candidate's use of the frame is as a claim to be weighed rather than a banner to be waved: the continent's unity is real in its structures, its insertion, its shared colonial grammar, and fictive in its politics, whose nations have pursued their revolutions and their rents in stubborn separation, and an answer that can state both halves has understood why the frame inspires the tradition's rhetoric and disappoints its strategists in equal measure.

## The state question restated: office, power, and the apparatus

Politics rooms examine the cluster, at bottom, as a set of experiments on the state, and a synthesis section earns its place by drawing the experiments into one argument candidates can deploy whole. Cuba answers the question of what a revolutionary movement does with a state it has captured entire: it fuses, party into administration, administration into coercion, until the distinction between the movement and the state it rules is a formality, and the answer's cost and benefit are the system's durability and its closure. Chile answers the question of what a state does to a movement that captures only its executive: the apparatus the government did not command, the judiciary, the congress, the officer corps, the central bank's habits, withdraws cooperation in sequence until the executive discovers it has held an office inside a power it never possessed. The later period answers the question in its third form: movements that captured executives inside states whose economic command they declined or failed to seize, and governed the interval between the rent's rise and its fall. The three answers compose a syllabus in themselves, and the candidate who can run them as one argument, with the verdict that the region's Marxism learned the state's weight by every method except anticipation, possesses the politics row at its highest level. The verdict's fairness should be noted, since it is the cluster's discipline applied to its own tradition: the European doctrine the region inherited had theorised the state as an instrument to be smashed or seized, and the region's century was spent discovering, case by case, that it is a terrain, an apparatus, and an adversary by turns, a discovery the tradition's best minds, the Chilean renovators above all, eventually wrote down at the price of everything they had attempted.

## Four productions: the originality claim weighed

The guide closes its content where the cluster's survey began, with the claim that Latin American Marxism was a production and not merely a reception of theory, because examination questions ask candidates to weigh that claim and the weighing is a fitting final exercise in the guide's method. The productions usually entered in evidence are four. The communal claim, that socialism could be built from the indigenous community rather than through its dissolution, made in Lima in the nineteen twenties against the international movement's own orthodoxy. The dependency relocation, that underdevelopment is manufactured by the same process that manufactures development, made across the continent's universities in the nineteen sixties and absorbed since into the common sense of half the social sciences. The theological borrowing, a church's analysts taking the tradition's instruments into a doctrine not its own and thereby altering both, made in the seminaries and the base communities. And the foco, the claim that the revolutionary subject could be created by the armed act itself, made in the Sierra's aftermath and refuted, alone among the four, by the test its authors themselves proposed. Weighed together, the four support a precise verdict: the tradition was original exactly where it answered questions its inheritance had not faced, the Indian, the dependent formation, the believing poor, the unripe continent, and derivative exactly where it repeated the inheritance's answers to questions the region had not asked. A candidate who can deliver that verdict, with its two halves evidenced, has answered the originality question at the level its setters intend, and has also, without noticing it, summarised the cluster: a tradition measured not by its loyalty to a doctrine but by the quality of its attention to its own ground.

## The tradition in the schoolroom: how the cluster is taught where it happened

One further topic serves the Latin American studies rooms and the teachers who supply them: the region teaches its own tradition, and the teaching is itself a historical force candidates may be asked to weigh. In the schools and universities of the continent, the figures of this cluster appear as founders, martyrs, and warnings, in proportions set by each country's politics: the Peruvian founder as a national classic whose essays are set texts, the Cuban sequence as a founding epic in one country and a cautionary tale in the classrooms of its exiles, the Chilean experiment as the open wound around which a restored democracy built its civic education, the theologians read in seminaries their younger selves would not have recognised. The candidate's handling follows the standing rule for sacred material: the schoolroom versions are evidence about the societies that teach them, data for the politics of memory, and not authorities on the events they commemorate. A question on how the region remembers its Marxism is answered by comparing the curricula as artefacts, and the comparison, conducted without reverence and without contempt, performs the same operation as the farewell broadcast's treatment above: the text as evidence of its moment, the moment reconstructed, the verdict proportioned.

## A bank of minor rows: four smaller topics and their handling

The matrix's six rows carry the cluster's weight, but papers reach into its corners, and four smaller topics deserve a paragraph of preparation each so that none arrives as a surprise. The Mexican revolution's institutionalisation, treated in its own section above, serves questions on parties and on revolutions compared. The Guatemalan template serves the Cold War's origins and the education of the Cuban generation. The Grenadian miniature serves the strategy debate at small scale. And the Haitian absence, the revolution of the enslaved that preceded every movement in this cluster by a century and haunted all of them as proof and as warning, serves the deep-history questions: the region's first social revolution, made by the enslaved of Saint-Domingue against the plantation order, stood behind every subsequent argument about race, property, and the possible, and the candidate who can place it as the tradition's unspoken premise, the event its thinkers cited rarely and feared accurately, holds a paragraph that lifts any answer on the region's revolutionary imagination. None of the four requires a section of its own in the warehouse; each requires a hinge, a mechanism, and a verdict, rehearsed in the manner of the examples stock.

## Bolivia's forgotten revolution: tin, suffrage, and the miners

The cluster's map has a blank that papers occasionally probe, and filling it strengthens every neighbouring answer. In April 1952, after an election annulled by a military junta, Bolivia's Nationalist Revolutionary Movement fought its way to power in three days of street warfare in which armed miners and factory workers defeated the army in the capital, an event without parallel in the region's century: a revolution made by an insurrection of the organised working class, in a country where the tin mines were the economy and the miners' federation its most disciplined force. The government that followed expropriated the great tin companies into a state mining corporation, decreed universal suffrage at a stroke, enfranchising the illiterate indigenous majority the republic had excluded for over a century, and enacted an agrarian reform that broke the haciendas of the altiplano. Within a dozen years the same governing movement, dependent on American aid and pressed by the fund missions of the stabilisation era, had turned its guns on the miners whose rifles had installed it, and the revolution's long decomposition into military coups began.

Why does the case matter to a candidate whose papers may never name it? Because it isolates variables the famous cases entangle. Here was a revolution neither communist in doctrine nor foquista in method, which nonetheless expropriated the commanding industry, armed a workers' militia, and enfranchised a nation, and which was undone not by invasion or by coup from its enemies but by the economics of a single export whose price it did not set, administered through aid relationships that converted a patron into a supervisor. Bolivia is dependency's argument enacted by nationalists who had never read the school, a generation before the school was written, and the candidate who can say so has found the case that proves the mechanism's reach beyond the doctrine's readership. Its afterlife completes the lesson: the miners, defeated and dispersed when the tin economy collapsed in the nineteen eighties, carried their organisation into the movements that later remade the country's politics, so that the century's Bolivian story reads as one long negotiation between a mining proletariat and the price of what it dug.

## The commodity syllabus: sugar, copper, oil, tin

Underneath the cluster's arguments runs a syllabus nobody sets and every examiner assumes, the region's commodities, each carrying a national history in concentrated form. Sugar made Cuba's plantation economy and its dependence on a single buyer's quota, so that the revolution's political economy can be read from the crop's calendar and its market's address. Copper made Chile's fiscal state, the salary whose ownership the whole society argued about because every school and hospital was built from it. Oil made Venezuela's rent and Mexico's revolutionary nationalism alike, the subsoil's wealth serving in one country as a pact's cement and in the other as a sovereignty symbol a governing party guarded for generations. Tin made Bolivia's miners and unmade them when the price fell. Silver, in the colonial centuries, made Potosí the treasury of an empire and the graveyard of the labour drafts that fed it, the original scene of the extraction the dependency school would later theorise. A candidate literate in that syllabus possesses a skeleton key: confronted with any country in the region, ask first what it sells, to whom, at whose price, and through whose hands the proceeds pass, and the country's politics will begin to arrange themselves into the patterns this guide has taught, insertion, alliance, rent, and the recurring discovery that the commodity's calendar governs the government's more faithfully than any programme does. The key does not replace the cases. It explains why the cases rhyme.

## The long view: what the cluster finally teaches

Step back from the rows and the anchors and ask what a season spent inside this material leaves in a candidate's equipment, because the answer is the guide's last argument for its own method. The cluster teaches a habit of explanation. Faced with any movement, government, or doctrine, the trained candidate now asks, in order: what formation did it arise from, what did it claim, what did the claim become under the pressures of its moment, who has judged it and by what evidence, and what proportion of the record each judgement commands. Those five questions, asked in that order, are portable to every other cluster in this series and to most of the history and politics a candidate will ever be set. They are also, not coincidentally, the questions the tradition at its best asked about itself, in the Lima essays that began from the land, in the exile seminars that began from the defeat, in the theologians' rooms that began from the poor man's actual street. The examination, at its highest level, is an invitation to think about the region the way the region's best minds thought about it: from the ground up, with the doctrine as an instrument and never as a shelter. A candidate who accepts the invitation has already been educated by the preparation, whatever the grade, and the grade, on the evidence of the scripts that perform these operations, tends to take care of itself.

## The hacienda: the institution behind the arguments

Every argument in this cluster stands, at one remove, on a single institution, and a candidate who understands the hacienda holds the region's master key in its oldest form. The great estate, assembled from the colonial land grants and the seizure of community lands, organised production through labour regimes that ranged from the wage to the debt-bound tenancy in which the peon's account at the estate store ensured he died owing more than he was born owing. Its political form was the gamonal, the landowner as local sovereign, dispenser of justice, employer, creditor, and electoral broker in one person, through whom the distant republic reached the countryside and beyond whom it did not reach at all. Its economic form was the paradox the dependency writers inherited: an estate producing for export markets with methods that resisted every productivity the market supposedly teaches, because its labour was too cheap to mechanise and its owner's income too secure to risk. Mariátegui's feudalism charge, the agrarian reforms of Mexico, Bolivia, Cuba, and Chile, the guerrillas' rural recruiting grounds and the theologians' base communities alike presuppose that institution and its tenures, and the candidate who can describe the hacienda's working, its store, its chapel, its jail, its fiesta obligations, in a paragraph, possesses the ground note to which the cluster's every melody returns. The institution's long survival, reformed in law and persistent in fact across much of the countryside, is likewise the answer's reserve explanation for why the region's transformations so often announced more than they altered: the estate could be expropriated in a season, and the relations it had bred, of deference, dependence, and brokerage, required generations.

## The cultural front: murals, novels, and the tradition's imagination

Latin American studies rooms, and the occasional enlightened history paper, examine the tradition through its cultural production, and the candidate should hold the front's outline ready. The Mexican muralists painted the revolution onto its public walls in the decades after 1920, Rivera, Orozco, and Siqueiros, the last a party militant who carried the doctrine into armed adventures of his own, and their vast didactic surfaces, the Indian past exalted, the hacienda condemned, the future industrial and collective, taught a largely illiterate citizenry the official story of its own revolution in images it could not avoid. The indigenista novel of the Andes performed the same office in prose for Mariátegui's generation, and the great Boom of the nineteen sixties, the novels that conquered world literature from within the tradition's orbit, carried its themes, the dictator, the estate, the buried violence of the official history, to a global readership that received them as magic and read past their politics. The Chilean Nueva Canción gave the experiment its soundtrack, and its most famous singer's murder in the coup's first days, in the stadium that now bears his name, made the cultural front's cost a matter of record. The candidate's handling observes the usual discipline in a warmer medium: the works are evidence of how the tradition imagined itself and wished to be imagined, to be analysed for their arguments and their silences, the mural's Indian exalted and voiceless, the novel's revolution betrayed by caudillos, rather than consumed as the tradition's advertising or its indictment. A question on culture and revolution rewards exactly that double vision, the artefact honoured as art and interrogated as claim, and it is among the most pleasant questions the cluster sets for the candidate who has prepared it.

## A glossary in prose: the terms that carry the region

Certain words do so much work in this cluster that a candidate's command of them is itself a band signal, and they are gathered here in the sentences where they earn their keep. Gamonalismo names the landowner's local sovereignty in the Andes, the estate as a state in miniature, and it is Mariátegui's word for the power his land argument had to break. Latifundio and its twin minifundio name the region's agrarian deformity at both ends, the estate too large to farm and the plot too small to feed, between which the countryside's politics was made. Inquilinaje and pongueaje name the labour tenures, the Chilean tenant bound to service and the Bolivian peon bound to the house, whose abolition the reforms promised and whose names in an answer prove the candidate has read past the summaries. Desarrollismo names the developmentalist faith of the postwar decades, the commission's gospel of industrialisation, against which the dependency school defined itself. Entreguismo, the sell-out, names the accusation each camp hurled at the other's foreign policy, and its career in the polemics is a small history of the hemisphere's argument with itself. Continuismo names the incumbent's art of remaining, the region's oldest constitutional disease, against which revolutions and term limits alike have been prescribed. Personalismo names the doctrine's opposite number, the politics of the caudillo, in which a man's following replaces a programme, and the tradition's parties defined their discipline against it. Sinarquía and gorilismo belong to the polemical shelf, the conspiracy of the right and the military brute of the Argentine vocabulary, useful to recognise in sources and unwise to adopt in answers, since the cluster's rule reserves characterisation for the sources and analysis for the candidate. Finally, compañero and its Portuguese cognate name the tradition's internal address, the word whose warmth and whose menace, it was also the word of the tribunal and the purge, summarise the century's doubleness in a single term of fellowship. A glossary learned at this depth is not vocabulary decoration. Each term compresses an institution, and the candidate who deploys three of them accurately in an answer has imported the region's own categories into the analysis, which is what thinking from inside a formation means.

## Worked openings: two first paragraphs taken apart

Abstract advice about openings hardens into skill when a candidate watches two of them being assembled. For the sequence question, consider what the first paragraph must accomplish and in what order. Its opening sentence assigns the row and refuses the folklore in a single motion: the puzzle is not why a communist movement behaved as communists do, but how a coalition that was not communist arrived at a socialist declaration. Its second sentence lays the anchor's dates side by side, victory and declaration, and names the interval as the answer's subject. Its third sentence promises the method, the links of reform and reaction weighed against the two explanations the historians offer. Nothing else is required, and everything else is waste: no survey of the island's history, no biography of its leader, no definition of socialism. For the feasibility question the assembly differs in its parts and not in its logic. The first sentence names the road and its wager, transformation by legality. The second states the anchor's two facts, the plurality and the ungoverned flank, as the conditions the wager had to survive. The third promises the tests, each condition examined against the record, with the external campaign weighed among the pressures rather than crowned above them. A candidate who has built those two paragraphs in practice, and understands that every other row's opening is the same machine with different parts, possesses the cluster's most transferable asset, because the machine works on questions the candidate has never seen, which is the only kind the hall guarantees.

## The night before and the morning itself

Conduct in the final hours is part of performance, and the cluster's version of it can be stated briefly because its principles are few. The night before belongs to the anchors and to nothing else: the sequence recited once, the constraint stated once, the placement grid walked once at conversational speed, and then the material closed, since the marginal value of a fourth hour among the notes is negative, purchased with the sleep on which the morning's judgement depends. The morning belongs to the paper's architecture: read every question before answering any, assign each to its row in the margin, choose the order that fronts the strongest performance while the mind is freshest, and fix the allocation of minutes in writing before the first paragraph is begun. Inside the writing, the disciplines of this guide reduce to three reflexes worth rehearsing until they fire unbidden. Date the states of affairs. Decline the figure. Analyse the structure and let the verdict arrive in proportion. A candidate whose reflexes are those three sentences has carried the guide into the hall in the only form the hall admits, as habit, and the rest, the warehouse, the shelf, the critics, the cases, will be summoned by the questions themselves, which is what they were stocked for.

## Choosing the scale: country, region, continent

A refinement that separates strong scripts in the final band is the deliberate choice of scale, because the cluster's questions can be pitched at three levels and each level rewards a different grain of answer. The country question, on Chile or on Cuba, rewards the case in its specificity, the named institutions, the dated turns, the actors in their dilemmas, and it punishes the candidate who flees upward into regional generalities the moment the detail runs out. The regional question, on the tradition or the school, rewards the pattern across cases, with each general claim anchored in at least two countries' evidence, and it punishes the single-case answer that mistakes its country for the continent. The continental question, on the hemisphere's place in a wider history, rewards the frame itself, insertion, hegemony, the commodity grammar, handled with the cases as illustrations rather than as subjects. Markers can tell within a paragraph whether the scale has been chosen or merely inherited from the candidate's preparation, because the chosen scale announces itself in the opening's grain: the institution named, the pattern stated, or the frame laid down. The drill is to take one rehearsed topic, the strategy debate serves well, and write its opening at all three scales until the hand learns the difference in texture, since the hall will demand whichever scale the candidate has practised least, and the difference between a chosen scale and an imposed one is visible in every line that follows.

## The witness and the archive: testimony as a source class

One source class pervades this cluster's modern literature and deserves a candidate's explicit protocol, because examinations increasingly reward those who can handle it: testimony. The region's catastrophes were documented first by their survivors, in the exile press, in the church's dossiers, in the collections assembled by the human rights organisations while the events were still deniable, and those testimonies later became the evidentiary base on which commissions and courts built their findings. The candidate's handling distinguishes three standings a testimony can hold. As evidence of experience, of what imprisonment, exile, or a queue at dawn felt like from inside, testimony is irreplaceable and needs no corroboration to be believed as experience. As evidence of fact, of who ordered what and when, it stands like any other source, strengthened by convergence and weakened by interest, and the strong answer cites it with its provenance attached. As evidence of memory's politics, of what a society has chosen to preserve and to forget, it is itself the object of study, and the commissions' reports can be read as the moment a state decided which testimonies it would underwrite. A script that moves among those standings knowingly, saying which use it is making of a witness at each appearance, performs source criticism of a sophistication markers associate with the highest bands, and it does so on the material where the cluster's other protocols, the declined figures, the refused adjectives, find their deepest justification: the witnesses are honoured by being read carefully, not by being counted carelessly.

The archive's other face should be named beside it. The perpetrators also kept records, and the declassification programmes of the intervening decades, above all the American releases on the Southern Cone, have given candidates a documentary base their teachers' generation lacked: cables in which officials discuss a coup's prospects in the idiom of weather, memoranda weighing a government's survival against a copper price, the paper trail of the condor years in the hunters' own filing systems. The weighed answer sets the two faces against each other, testimony for the experience and the cable for the intention, and notes where they corroborate and where each falls silent. That is the cluster's evidentiary ideal in its complete form, and a candidate who can describe it, even where a particular answer cannot fully practise it, has understood what the modern treatment of this material is for.

A word, in closing, about why this guide has insisted so stubbornly on temperature. The region's twentieth century is not a museum: its arguments continue in the mouths of living people, its wounds are carried by families the candidate may be sitting beside, and its banners still fly. An examination is one of the few institutions that asks for the century to be thought about rather than fought over, and the disciplines assembled here, the dated sequence, the weighed pressure, the declined figure, the structure preferred to the slogan, are simply the etiquette of that thinking made explicit. Observe the etiquette and the material becomes strangely peaceful to handle: the revolution, the road, the school, and the wave settle into their evidence, and the candidate is free to do the one thing the room rewards, which is to judge them all with a clear head and to show the working.

That freedom, finally, is the answer to the question with which every candidate begins and which this guide has taken as its spine. Nobody in the examination hall is asking to be told what to think about Latin America's Marxist century. They are asking whether the candidate can think about it at all, in public, under time, with the evidence marshalled and the passions governed. Show that, and the verdict, whichever way it falls, will be believed. That belief, earned in the open and on the record, is the whole of the craft this guide has tried to teach.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How do you write an exam answer on Latin American Marxism?

Start by deciding which family the question belongs to: a sequence to be explained, a road whose feasibility is in dispute, a theory to be weighed, a borrowing to be bounded, a pair of strategies to be compared, or a recent period to be periodised. Open with the distinction that family prizes, then build the body from anchored material, every large claim fastened to a particular episode and the mechanism driving it. Bring in one serious critic by name before you judge anything. Close with a verdict that carries its condition on its face, strong on one axis, weak on another, never a coronation and never a burial. Keep governments described through the structures they inhabited, keep every contested number out, and let the prose sound like weighing rather than like cheering.

### Q: How do you explain the Cuban radicalisation sequence?

Give the movement its true starting point first: a broad insurrectionary coalition with a nationalist programme, joined late and warily by the established communist party. Then work forward through the hinge of the agrarian reform, which won the revolution a rural constituency while expropriating the interests, foreign and domestic, that would organise against it. Show retaliation and defence chasing one another upward: expropriation answered by pressure from Washington, pressure answered by militias, nationalisations, and the search for a Soviet market, until the declaration of April 1961 named an outcome the preceding two years had assembled. Finish by weighing the two scholarly readings, the concealed design and the driven process, and say plainly where the evidence leaves the balance.

### Q: How do you use Chile as evidence in an answer?

Deploy the case in its constrained form rather than its legendary one. The serviceable version runs in three movements: a government carried into office by the largest bloc of a three-way split, holding a minority of seats, attempted to transform property relations by strictly legal means; the mobilisation running ahead of it on its own side, in the factories, the settlements, and the countryside, narrowed the space in which legality could be defended; and a documented external campaign pressed on an economy and an officer corps already polarising without it. Stated that way, the case becomes portable evidence about the gap between an office held and a power never possessed, about electoral roads and their preconditions, and about how constitutional orders withdraw consent. Stated as a government of the left felled by soldiers, it travels only into perorations.

### Q: Which Latin American thinkers should you name?

Few, and each one freighted with a proposition. Mariátegui for the founding reversal, the indigenous community treated as the foundation for a socialism grown at home rather than a survival awaiting dissolution. Frank for the chain that manufactures underdevelopment at one end of the world economy by developing the other. Cardoso, with Faletto, for the correction that turned a fate into a set of historical situations, some of which delivered dependent industrial growth. Marini for the wage driven below its reproduction cost. Gutiérrez for theology redefined as reflection upon practice, with Marxist instruments borrowed and materialist doctrine declined. Guevara for the armed nucleus that would create its own conditions, and Debray for codifying that claim and later recanting it. Attach to every name the critic who answered it, and the shelf becomes an argument instead of a roll call.

### Q: What are the common mistakes on Latin American Marxism?

Six return every season. Installing a communist government in Havana from the day of the victory, which abolishes the very transformation the questions exist to examine. Explaining the Chilean collapse by a single cause, foreign intervention crowned or dismissed, when the record shows several pressures doing distinct work. Fusing the redistributive governments of the recent period into one movement with one record, when their families differed in ambition and in kind. Calling the liberation theologians Marxists, which confuses borrowed instruments with held doctrine. Asserting numbers for the dead, the disappeared, the vote shares, or the poverty lines that no candidate can source in the hall and every marker knows are fought over. And grading governments with adjectives where the question asked for the anatomy of a structure. Each error has a cheap repair, and the repairs are the substance of this guide.

### Q: How do you write about the Pink Tide safely?

Fix the period before judging anything inside it. Mark its ascent against the market order that preceded it, its high years underwritten by expensive commodities, and its ebb as those prices fell away. Then split the wave into its families, the governments that rewrote constitutions and seized commanding resources, and the governments that taxed a boom and spent the proceeds downward while leaving ownership where it stood. Measure every government against the gap between moving income and remaking structure, and fasten the commodity condition to each achievement you record and each failure you charge, since the same windfall explains both the generosity and the fragility. Refer to administrations by country and years rather than by devotion or detestation, and the answer stays analytical however warm the surrounding argument becomes.

### Q: How do you evaluate dependency theory in an essay?

Build the machinery before appraising it. State the inherited diagnosis, an industrial centre keeping the gains of progress while primary exporters watch their terms of trade decay, then the radical versions in their distinct forms: surplus drained along a chain of satellites, situations of dependence whose internal alliances decide what growth is possible, labour paid beneath the cost of its own maintenance. Only then bring the prosecutors in. Laclau for defining a mode of production by exchange rather than by how people work; Brenner for locating development's fate in class relations inside each society rather than in trade between them; the Brazilian and East Asian records for falsifying the prediction that dependent industrialisation could not happen. Deliver the verdict in separated parts, questions inherited, predictions tested, mechanisms that still bite where their conditions hold.

### Q: Which examples work for Latin American Marxism questions?

The ones that are fixed in place and time and that carry weight in the argument. The land reform of May 1959, because it created the Cuban revolution's friends and its enemies in a single act. The ministers who walked out of that government across the following year, because their resignations chart its direction better than any speech. The unanimous vote that nationalised Chilean copper, because it shows a programme's legality and popularity at their peak together. The truck owners who stopped a country's wheels, because they reveal a middle stratum as a political army. The Bolivian miners who defeated an army in the streets in 1952 and were dispersed by an export price a generation later. The diary kept in a Bolivian ravine, because it refutes a doctrine in its author's handwriting. Choose for the mechanism each one embodies, and spend it in a sentence.

### Q: Is it safe to criticise the Cuban Revolution in an exam?

Yes, under the same condition that makes praise safe: the criticism must be analysis rather than posture. Marking schemes pay for operations, faithful representation, anchored evidence, a serious opposing case met fairly, and a judgement earned by evidence the script has itself put into the balance. A conclusion against the revolution, or against the Chilean road, or against the dependency school, reached by that road earns full credit in any properly standardised room, and so does a conclusion in their favour reached the same way. What forfeits credit, in either direction, is the adjective doing the argument's work, the regime blessed or damned in the opening paragraph before a single fact has been examined. Where an instrument is short and factual, supply coverage and keep verdicts sheathed; where the command asks you to assess, withholding judgement is the only unsafe move.

### Q: How do you revise Latin American Marxism efficiently?

Revise the moves, not the survey. Turn the answer matrix into cards, distinction on one side and pitfall on the other, and drill them until assigning an unseen question to its family takes under a minute. Practise the two anchors as recitations, the Cuban chain in ninety seconds, the Chilean double bind in three sentences. Convert the thinker shelf into a grid you can run in both directions, from a name to its claim and from a claim back to its name and its answerer. Write one timed script per family and mark it yourself against the catalogue of standard errors, logging your own recurring faults where you will reread them before the next attempt. In the final fortnight stop adding material altogether; spend the hours on openings, on compression, and on the small precisions of names and dates that quietly decide how far a marker trusts you.

### Q: How do the two anchors order a Latin America answer?

Each anchor supplies an answer with its spine. The Cuban anchor orders by time and transformation: begin from what the movement was at its victory, follow the chain of reform and reaction link by link, and let the declaration arrive where the record puts it, more than two years in. Any answer built on that spine is automatically an explanation rather than a chronicle, because the ordering itself asks why each stage produced the next. The Chilean anchor orders by conditions: state the narrow mandate and the ungoverned mobilisation first, then arrange every later pressure, economic, institutional, military, foreign, as a force acting on those two facts. An answer built on that spine is automatically a weighing rather than a lament. Choose whichever anchor the question touches, lay it down in the opening paragraph, and the middle and the close have their instructions already.

### Q: What should a student separate between characterising a government and analysing a structure?

Separate the verdict from the anatomy. Characterising hands a government its moral label, democratic or dictatorial, progressive or corrupt, and ends the inquiry at the moment it begins, since nothing follows from an adjective except agreement or offence. Analysing a structure describes the terrain any administration of the period had to cross: how the economy earns its foreign exchange, who owns the land and the commanding industries, which classes a coalition can be built from, what the armed forces will and will not obey. From an anatomy, conclusions can be drawn and defended; from a label, only sides can be taken. The practical test is grammatical. When a draft sentence hangs a complimentary or damning adjective on a government, rewrite it until the sentence names a mechanism instead, and the script crosses from commentary into the register that marking schemes are built to reward.

### Q: Why do examiners penalise contested figures in this cluster's answers?

Because an asserted figure, in this field, is a confession about sources. The numbers attached to repression, to insurgency, to close elections, and to the recent period's social indicators were produced by parties to the conflicts, counted under incompatible definitions, and remain under dispute among the commissions, courts, and scholars who own them. A candidate reciting one has learned the topic from material that cannot tell assertion from evidence, and a marker who spots the habit in one paragraph begins to discount the paragraphs around it. The penalty is therefore about credibility rather than arithmetic. The rewarded alternative costs no marks at all: give the direction of a change, name the mechanism that drove it, and explain briefly why honest counters disagree about its size, which shows a surer grip on the subject than any digit could.

### Q: How does the Latin America answer matrix order a timed answer?

It converts the first minute of panic into a decision. Read the question for its command and its object, match them to a row, and the row hands you your first move, your material, your critic, and your pitfall, before the pen has to improvise anything. Sequence questions get the chain and the hinge; feasibility questions get the conditions tested one by one; theory questions get the mechanism stated and then the prosecution heard; strategy questions get each road measured against its own promises. Because the allocation is settled in advance, the writing time goes where the marks are densest, and the answer's shape, distinction first, evidence anchored, evaluation named, verdict conditioned, repeats across the paper until it becomes the candidate's handwriting rather than an effort of will.

### Q: What separates a model answer on these projects from a position on their politics?

The order in which things happen. A position begins with its conclusion and ransacks the record for illustrations, discarding whatever resists and inflating whatever agrees, so that the verdict was never in doubt and the evidence was never in charge. A model answer begins with the projects as their own serious defenders state them, proceeds through the record with its pressures and its surprises intact, hears the strongest critics at their strongest, and only then delivers a judgement that the preceding pages have made the reader able to check. The two can end at the identical verdict and be separated by whole bands, because the scheme is paying for the road, not the destination. That is why a script condemning a revolution can outscore a script praising it, and why the candidate's private politics, whatever they are, can be left safely at the examination room door.
