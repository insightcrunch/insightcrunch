Ask a search engine about alienation in Marx and it will hand back a list. Four items, neatly parallel, usually decorated with four matching examples, as though a young philosopher in Paris had sat down one spring to compose a taxonomy and numbered the entries as he went. The list is not false, exactly. Each of its four items names something genuinely present in the text from which the list is drawn. But the list form itself is a quiet falsification, because the passage behind it is not a taxonomy at all. It is an argument with a direction. Two of the four relations are established directly from the conditions of wage labour, and the other two are then drawn out of those, step by step, with the inferential steps marked in the prose. Flatten that argument into four parallel bullet points and the reader keeps the vocabulary while losing the reasoning, which is rather like keeping the names of chess pieces while losing the rules that connect their moves.

![The four types of alienation in Marx, product, activity, species-being and other people as a derivation - Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

This article is a glossary entry with a thesis. The glossary work is real: each of the four relations will be defined precisely, bounded against its neighbours, supplied with examples that instantiate the relation rather than merely evoking a mood, and separated from the wrong definitions that circulate in revision notes and lecture slides. The thesis is the one the passage itself supports. Marx asserts two alienations and derives two, so the correct exposition is a chain rather than a list, and any account that presents four parallel items has lost the argument that connects them. That claim, the two-plus-two structure, will be stated, evidenced from the derivational language of the manuscript, defended against the strongest objection available, and used as the organising spine of everything that follows. A reader who finishes here should be able to do something a list cannot teach: start from the first relation, and show, rather than merely say, how the third and fourth arise.

Two precisions belong at the front, because they govern everything after. The first concerns authorship of the numbering. Marx never numbers the four as four types. He does not present a quartet, does not use the word type for them, and does not offer them as a finished classification of anything. The numbering is a later teaching device, assembled by editors, translators, textbook writers and sociology syllabuses in the middle decades of the twentieth century, and it is a good device as far as it goes. It becomes a bad one only when its tidy parallelism is mistaken for Marx's own presentation, and when students are marked for reproducing a structure their author never drew. The second precision concerns the word itself. The German term in the manuscript is Entfremdung, rendered variously as estrangement and as alienation, and the section of the first Paris manuscript in which the four relations appear carries the heading Estranged Labour. Estrangement and alienation in this article translate the same German word, and the choice between them in any given sentence is a matter of rhythm rather than doctrine.

## The four types of alienation stated precisely

Precision begins with the four relations stated one at a time, in the order the manuscript develops them, with the logical status of each marked as it arrives. The first is estrangement from the product of labour: the thing the worker makes confronts the worker as an alien object, a power independent of its producer, owned by another and turned against the one who made it. The second is estrangement from the activity of production: the labour itself is external to the worker, coerced rather than voluntary, an activity that belongs to another and in which the worker does not affirm but denies himself. The third is estrangement from species-being: because productive activity has been degraded into a mere means of staying alive, the worker is cut off from the free conscious activity that Marx takes to be the defining character of human life. The fourth is estrangement from other people: each person is set at odds with others who share the same human essence, and the worker in particular confronts the non-worker who owns the product and commands the labour. The first two are read off the wage relation directly. The second two are inferred. That asymmetry is the fact about the four that a list cannot show.

### What are the four types of alienation?

Alienation from the product of labour, from the activity of production, from species-being, and from other people. Marx asserts the first two from the conditions of wage labour and derives the second two from them, so they form an argument rather than a list.

Hold the four statements side by side for a moment and notice how unlike one another they are in kind. The first concerns a thing: a product, an object, something that can be pointed at, weighed, warehoused and sold. The second concerns a doing: the hours of activity themselves, the expended energy, the directed hands and attention. The third concerns a nature: not anything the worker makes or does on a particular shift, but what a human being is, the capacities whose exercise the first two estrangements have blocked. The fourth concerns other persons: the social field in which the worker moves, the colleague, the competitor, the owner. A list treats these four as though they occupied the same logical shelf, four species of one genus, coordinate and interchangeable. The manuscript treats them as stations on a path, where each later relation is intelligible only because an earlier one has been secured. Product and activity are where the argument touches ground. Species-being and other people are where it reaches its conclusions about what wage labour does to a human life and to human relations generally.

The order, moreover, is not interchangeable, and a quick experiment proves it. Try to state the third relation first, on its own, to a reader who has not yet been given the other two. Estrangement from species-being means that work, which ought to be the free expression of human powers, has shrunk into a mere means of survival. The sentence is grammatical, and a sympathetic reader can nod at it, but nothing in it yet explains why work has shrunk, or what has been lost beyond a feeling of flatness, or why the shrinkage is a structural feature rather than a run of bad jobs. Now supply the first two relations first. The product is taken, owned by another, accumulated as a hostile power. The activity is coerced, timed, directed from outside, not the worker's own. Then the third relation stops being a mood and becomes a conclusion: if what a person makes and what a person does all day are both alien, then the activity that defines the species has been turned into its opposite, an instrument for merely continuing to exist. The derivation does real work. It converts a lament into an argument, and arguments can be checked, challenged and taught in ways that laments cannot.

None of this requires the reader to accept the argument. It requires only that the argument be seen, since a surprising amount of criticism of the four types attacks the list rather than the chain, objecting that the categories overlap, or that one of them is redundant, or that the fourth is just the third said socially. Those objections land differently once the derivational structure is visible. Overlap between a premise and its conclusion is not a defect in a taxonomy; it is what derivation means. Whether the inferences hold is a separate and harder question, and this article will give the objection to them its full force in due course. But the first task of a glossary is descriptive accuracy about what the text does, and what the text does is derive. The fuller treatment of the parent concept, its history before Marx and its career after him, belongs to the series guide to [Marx on alienation](/2011/04/01/marx-on-alienation-complete-guide/), which owns that wider territory; the present article owns the structure of the four.

## The passage the list came from: estranged labour in the Paris manuscripts

The four relations occupy a single sustained stretch of writing in the first of the three manuscripts that make up the Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844, the notebook Marx filled in Paris when he was twenty six and reading political economy with a convert's ferocity. The section is headed Estranged Labour, and it begins not with a definition but with a fact borrowed from the economists themselves. Political economy, Marx says, proceeds from the fact of private property without explaining it, and he proposes to start instead from a fact the economists concede: the worker becomes poorer the more wealth he produces. One short sentence from the opening pages fixes the tone of the whole inquiry, since Marx writes that "the worker becomes all the poorer" as his production grows in power and scope, and that the worker sinks to the level of a commodity, indeed to the cheapest of commodities. From that conceded fact the analysis unfolds by asking what must be true of the labour relation for such an outcome to be its normal product rather than its occasional accident.

The material condition of the text matters for how its authority should be weighed, and a precision glossary is exactly where that weighing belongs. The manuscripts are notebooks: drafts, excerpts, arguments tried out at speed, with sentences that break off and a third manuscript that dwindles into fragments. Marx never published them, never prepared them for a printer, and gave no sign of regarding the estranged labour section as a finished statement of anything. They slept in his papers, passed after his death into the keeping of the German social democratic archive, and reached print only in 1932, in two rival editions prepared in Berlin and in Moscow, almost half a century after their author died. The anglophone world received them later still, in a translation by Martin Milligan published at the end of the 1950s, and then in the Penguin Early Writings of the 1970s. Every reader who has ever met the four types has therefore met them at several removes: a draft, edited posthumously, translated, excerpted, and finally reformatted by teachers. None of that makes the passage worthless. It makes the honest presentation of it a matter of showing the joins, including the join between what Marx wrote and what his teachers numbered.

The vocabulary of the passage is itself a small lesson in precision, because three German words do work that English routinely blurs. Entfremdung is estrangement, the becoming foreign of something that was one's own. Beside it stands Entäusserung, externalisation or divestment, the act of parting with something, which Marx also uses and which carries a legal echo of surrendering a right. Beneath both, and deliberately distinguished from them, sits Vergegenständlichung, objectification: the embodiment of labour in an object, which happens in every act of production whatsoever, in the freest workshop as much as in the bleakest mill. Objectification is not the disease. A potter objectifies labour in a pot and a joiner in a table, and no criticism follows from that alone. The disease begins when objectification takes the specific form in which the object is lost to its maker and returns as an alien power, and Marx's care in keeping the neutral term separate from the pathological ones is the first sign that the passage is an analysis rather than a complaint. Teachers who let objectification and estrangement collapse into one another have lost, at the level of vocabulary, the distinction the whole section is built to preserve.

What the economists supplied and what Marx added can now be stated cleanly. From Smith, Ricardo and their successors Marx took the description of the wage relation: labour power hired by the day or the week, a product owned by the employer, a wage fixed by the cost of keeping the worker alive and working. His addition was to interrogate that description for its human presuppositions, using a philosophical vocabulary inherited from Hegel and Feuerbach: from Hegel, the thought that a self is made and known through its activity and its products; from Feuerbach, the thought of a species essence, a Gattungswesen, that individual humans share and can be estranged from. The estranged labour section is the forge in which those inheritances are hammered against the economists' facts. Readers who want the manuscripts situated whole, their three notebooks mapped and their other great themes of money, need and communism set in place, should go to the series reading of the [Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts](/2011/04/15/economic-philosophic-manuscripts-guide/), which owns the text as a whole. Here the concern is narrower: one section, four relations, and the thread that runs from the first to the last.

One further feature of the passage deserves remark before the relations are taken in turn, because it explains the derivation's confidence. Marx proceeds, unusually for him, by an almost geometrical method. He announces that he will consider the fact of estranged labour under successive aspects, establishes the product relation, then turns to the act of production with the air of a man completing a proof, then draws the third relation explicitly as a consequence of the two already won, and the fourth as a consequence in its turn. The connective tissue is in the sentences themselves: phrases of inference, of having still a further aspect to deduce, of conclusions that follow. That is the textual basis on which the two-plus-two structure rests, and it is why the strongest objection to the derivation, examined later in this article, has to argue not that the connectives are absent but that they promise more than the argument delivers. A glossary that hides this texture behind four bullet points has not simplified the passage. It has replaced it.


## Hegel behind the manuscript: labour as self-making, grasped abstractly

The derivation did not spring from the notebooks unparented, and a glossary that traces its logic should name the parent it argues with. In Hegel's Phenomenology of Spirit, self-consciousness wins its independence through work: the bondsman, forming objects under another's direction, finds in the permanence of the thing he has shaped a mind of his own, and discovers himself in his product even inside servitude. The young Marx seized on the insight with gratitude, calling the Phenomenology's grasp of labour as the self-creating act of man its outstanding achievement. His estranged labour section is, in one of its aspects, that Hegelian scene rewritten under the conditions of political economy. Where Hegel's bondsman is educated by the object, Marx's worker is expropriated through it, because the object does not remain in a master's household as the durable witness of the maker's skill. It enters a market, accumulates as capital, and returns to command the maker as an alien power. The first relation is what becomes of Hegel's formative product when the product is a commodity owned by a third party and massed into an economic system.

The gratitude came with a reservation that shapes the whole manuscript, and the reservation is stated most fully in the third notebook's critique of Hegel. Hegel, Marx charged, grasps labour as the essence of man but knows only abstract, mental labour: the movements his dialectic describes are movements of thought, and the estrangement it overcomes is estrangement within consciousness, resolved when the philosopher recognises the world as spirit's own. Against that resolution the Paris manuscripts insist on the material scene: real objects, real hunger, real wages, an estrangement that no change of interpretation dissolves because it is lodged in property and activity rather than in mistaken ideas about them. This is why the manuscript's derivation begins from a fact of political economy rather than from a state of mind, and why its conclusions are framed as relations between persons and things rather than as errors of self-understanding. A reader who knows the Hegelian background can also see the derivational style itself as an inheritance under correction: the passage moves by taking up each relation and finding the next inside it, a dialectical gait learned from the Phenomenology and set to walk on economic ground. The two-plus-two structure is, among other things, the trace of that gait, which is one more reason to expound the four as a movement rather than to freeze them as a taxonomy their author, and his teacher, would both have refused.

## Alienation from the product of labour: the first relation, asserted

The first relation is the one the whole analysis can be built from, because it is the one a reader can verify against the plainest institutional facts. A wage worker makes something: a component, a garment, a loaf, a ledger entry, a line of code. The thing made is not the worker's. It belongs, by the contract that set the work going, to the employer who bought the labour power and supplied the materials. Marx's first move is to insist that this familiar arrangement be described in its full strangeness. The product, he writes, confronts its producer "as a power independent of the producer", an object that stands over against the worker, alien and increasingly mighty. Labour has been embodied in an object, made material, congealed; that much is objectification, and it is innocent. What is not innocent is the form objectification takes under this relation, where the embodiment appears as loss: the worker puts life into the object, and the life placed there no longer belongs to the worker but to the object and its owner. The more the worker expends, the less the worker retains. Production, which in the abstract is the human power to shape a world, here operates as a transfer in which the producer is drained by exactly the amount the product gains.

### What does it mean for the product to stand over against the worker as an alien power?

It means the finished object belongs to another person, grows stronger as the worker produces more of it, and returns to govern the worker's own life, since the accumulated stock of past products sets the terms on which living labour is hired, paced and paid.

Follow the growth of that power and the relation sharpens. A single day's product is a small thing: a pile of finished parts, a quota of packed boxes. A year's product, a decade's, a working lifetime's, is the factory itself, the warehouse network, the brand, the fleet, the platform, the entire material apparatus of an industry. That apparatus was made by labour and is renewed by labour daily, yet it confronts each morning's shift as a given world, owned elsewhere, its rhythms fixed, its demands non-negotiable. The worker meets objectified labour from earlier days as the given conditions of employment. Past products, crystallised as capital, hire, pace and discipline present producers. This is why Marx calls the product an alien power rather than merely someone else's property. Property language describes a legal title. Power language describes a relation of command, in which the dead labour stored in objects sets the terms under which living labour may work at all. The worker is not simply separated from the product at the factory gate, like a seller parting with a sold chair. The worker is governed by the product, in aggregate, for as long as the relation lasts.

The relation has a second edge, visible when the product is traced into the worker's own consumption. The classical illustration, older than Marx and sharpened by him, is the baker who must buy back bread. The worker produces the goods that constitute the material world of his own life, housing, clothing, food, transport, entertainment, yet encounters every one of them as a commodity priced against a wage, available only through purchase, and priced by a process in which his own contribution appears nowhere. His relation to the sensuous external world, to the entire stock of things among which he lives, is mediated by the wage and the market. What he made returns to him as a stranger twice over: first as the employer's property, then as the shop's merchandise. Nothing in this requires the goods to be shoddy or the wage to be unfair by the standards of the labour market. The estrangement lies in the form of the relation itself, in the fact that the producer can reach his own product only by passing through the two alien hands of owner and seller. A pay rise softens the experience without touching the structure, which is a point the first relation shares with all four: each names a form, not a grievance about amounts.

Examples clarify the relation when they track the product rather than the worker's feelings, and the discipline of choosing such examples is worth practising because so much popular writing on this topic chooses them badly. Consider the assembly worker in a vehicle plant who fits the same subassembly several hundred times a shift. The finished vehicles leave for markets she will never visit, priced beyond her wage, and she may go years without riding in the model she helps to build. Her relation to the product is exhaustively described by the first type: her labour is in those vehicles, her skill is in them, her working years are in them, and they are hers in no sense at all, neither to use, nor to direct, nor to withhold. Consider, by contrast, the joiner in a two-person workshop who builds a table to a customer's commission. The table also leaves; the joiner also sells. Yet the joiner's labour was governed from within the work itself, the object answers to decisions the maker took and can survey whole, and its departure is the completion of the maker's own plan rather than the plan's confiscation. Objectification has occurred in both cases. Estrangement from the product, as Marx isolates it, belongs to the first case, where the maker's exclusion from the object is total and structural: no say in what is made, no claim on it when made, no relation to it thereafter except as one more unit of the power that employs him.

Two clarifications protect the first relation from common softening. It is not the complaint that workers should own trinkets they happen to touch, a kind of finders-keepers doctrine for the production line. The relation is about the product as social power: the accumulated object world in its role as capital. Nor is it the complaint that production is divided, so that each worker makes only a part. Division of labour intensifies the estrangement, since the partial worker cannot even recognise a finished thing as his, but the manuscript's argument is deeper than the assembly line's monotony. Even the worker who makes a complete object alone, a whole shoe in a putting-out arrangement, is estranged from the product if the shoe is another's by contract and its accumulation confronts him as the merchant's growing power. Completeness of the object is not the variable. Ownership and command are. Hold that distinction and the first relation stays precise: alienation from the product is the producer's exclusion from the object he produces, such that the object, aggregated with all the others, stands over against him as an independent and governing power.

## Alienation from the act of production: the second relation, asserted

If the product is estranged, Marx reasons, the activity that produces it cannot be the worker's own either, for a product does not become alien in the hands of an activity that is free. The second relation therefore turns from the object to the doing, from what is made to the hours of making. Labour, in this relation, is "external to the worker": it does not belong to his essential being, is not the unfolding of his own purposes, and is affirmed nowhere in his experience of it. The manuscript's description is unsparing and worth following clause by clause. In his work the worker does not affirm himself but denies himself. He does not develop his physical and mental energies freely but mortifies his body and, in the translation's blunt phrase, ruins his mind. The work is not voluntary but coerced, and Marx names it with the term that strips away all euphemism: forced labour. It is not the satisfaction of a need. It is a means to satisfy needs external to it, the wage and what the wage buys. The worker consequently feels himself to be freely active only outside the work, in the animal functions of eating, drinking and procreating, while within the work, which ought to be the distinctively human function, he feels himself reduced towards the animal. The inversion is the signature of the second relation: the human is experienced as animal, the animal as human.

### How is alienation from the activity different from disliking a job?

Dislike is a feeling about work that may pass with a better post, whereas estrangement from the activity names a structure: the hours belong to another, the pace and purpose are set from outside, and the worker's energy is spent as a means to ends not his own.

The key analytical move in this second relation is the claim that the worker's activity is not his own activity at all. It belongs to another. The sentence sounds metaphorical and is meant literally, in the only sense ownership can bear for an activity: the direction of the activity, its purpose, its tempo, its beginning and its end, are disposed of by someone other than the person performing it. The buyer of labour power acquires, for the contracted hours, the disposition of a human being's powers, and the seller experiences those powers being exercised as a foreign force exercises them. Marx compresses this into the formula that the worker's activity is not spontaneous, not self-activity, but activity under the direction, control and compulsion of another. His life activity, the very medium in which a self is expressed and formed, passes under alien command for the length of the working day. Hence the peculiar doubleness that observers of wage labour have always noticed and that the second relation names exactly: the worker is present at his work as the instrument of purposes he did not choose, and absent from it as a person, so that the hours are simultaneously the most strenuous and the most empty of his day. They cost him the most and they are his the least.

Tiredness, by contrast, proves nothing either way, and the glossary reader should fix this boundary firmly because examination answers and pub arguments alike founder on it. A fell runner is exhausted and unestranged. A cabinetmaker finishing a difficult commission at midnight is tired to the bone and fully in possession of his activity, its difficulties chosen, its standards his own, its completion his. A data entry clerk may finish the day physically fresh and wholly estranged, every keystroke prescribed, every pause monitored, the activity's purpose invisible and its disposition entirely another's. Estrangement from the activity is not measured in fatigue, nor in the pleasantness of the surroundings, nor even in the worker's reported satisfaction, since a person can be content inside a relation whose structure the concept describes. It is measured by the locus of command: whose purposes the activity serves during its performance, whose decisions shape it, to whom its exercise belongs. Where the answers all point away from the performer, the second relation holds, whatever the performer's mood. Where the performer directs the activity, even under hard necessity and market pressure, the relation is absent or attenuated, whatever the performer's complaints. The concept classifies relations. It does not grade experiences.

Within the wage relation the second estrangement wears many costumes, and surveying them shows how much of working life the single relation organises. There is the estrangement of the prescribed gesture, the assembly operation reduced to a motion repeated past the point where attention is required, so that the worker's skill is not so much used as bypassed. There is the estrangement of the script, familiar from service work, in which even speech, the most personal of activities, is written elsewhere and performed under monitoring, the worker's voice hired out as a medium for sentences he must not alter. There is the estrangement of pace, where the tempo of activity is set by machinery, by algorithmic allocation, by targets cascading from a dashboard, so that the worker's own rhythm, the natural unit in which effort is experienced, is abolished and replaced. There is the estrangement of purpose, where the end of the activity is hidden by compartmentalisation, and the worker cannot say what the work is for, only what the next screen or bin or form demands. These are not four more types to be added to the list, and treating them as such would repeat the list error at a lower level. They are the one second relation, estrangement from the activity, appearing wherever the disposition of working hours has passed out of the worker's hands, which under the wage contract is the default and not the exception.

It remains to mark how the second relation locks into the first, because the manuscript's order conceals a subtlety. The two are presented in sequence, product then activity, and a careless reader files them as two separate misfortunes that happen to coincide. Marx's own treatment makes them two aspects of one fact, read from its result and from its process. The product is alien because the activity was alien; the activity's alien character is proved and made permanent by the alien product it leaves behind. Neither is derived from the other in the strong sense reserved for the third and fourth relations. Both are asserted, drawn directly out of the premise the economists themselves supply, that labour power is sold and its use disposed of by the buyer. This is the precise sense in which the structure is two plus two. The first pair stand on the ground of the wage relation, each exhibiting the same estrangement in the object and in the act. The second pair will now be built on top of them, and the building words in the text change accordingly, from description to inference, from what is the case to what follows.


## Alienation from species-being: the third relation, derived

The third relation is where the manuscript changes key. The first two relations described what happens to the worker's product and to the worker's hours. The third asks what those two facts, taken together, do to the worker as a human being, and it answers with a piece of philosophical anthropology that Marx took from Feuerbach and made his own. The German term is Gattungswesen, usually translated species-being: the character a creature has as a member of a kind, and in the human case the character of a being whose life activity is free and conscious. An animal, on this account, is immediately one with its life activity. It does not distinguish itself from the activity; it builds, hunts, feeds and flees as instinct directs, producing one-sidedly, only itself and its young, only under the domination of immediate physical need. The human being stands differently. A person makes life activity itself an object of will and consciousness, can produce even when free from physical need, can produce universally, forming objects in accordance with the standards of every species and not only his own, reproducing the whole of nature in thought and in craft. The builder raises the structure in imagination before raising it in fact. Production of this kind, free, conscious, universal, is not one human capacity among others. It is the species character, the life of the kind expressing itself in each individual.

### What is species-being in Marx's vocabulary?

Species-being, Gattungswesen in the German, names what Marx takes human beings to share as a kind: life activity itself, carried on freely and consciously, in which a person makes his own activity an object of his will and shapes the world deliberately rather than by instinct alone.

Nature enters the account at exactly this point, and its placement matters for readers sorting out rival lists of the types. Nature, for the manuscripts, is the human being's inorganic body: inorganic, because it is not the physical body itself, yet a body, because human life is materially continuous with it, drawing from nature both the means of life and the material, object and instruments of life activity. To be estranged from nature is therefore not an extra item to be stapled to the four. In the manuscript's architecture it is folded into the third relation, since estranging a person from free conscious activity estranges him in the same stroke from the natural world in which that activity was exercised, which now confronts him as alien property, as raw material owned by others, as scenery behind fences. Teaching lists that promote estrangement from nature into a fifth type are not inventing the theme, which is present and important. They are reorganising the text's own filing, and a precise reader should know that the filing has been changed, and where the theme actually sits in the original arrangement.

Now the derivation can be run, and it should be run slowly, because this is the step on which the two-plus-two structure stands or falls. Estranged labour has taken the product from the worker, so that his objectified life confronts him as a foreign power. Estranged labour has taken the activity from the worker, so that his working hours are not his own self-activity but a coerced performance belonging to another. What, then, has become of the species life that free conscious activity was supposed to be? It has been inverted. The activity that constitutes the human essence survives only as an instrument, a means pressed into the service of mere existence: the worker works in order to live, and the distinctively human capacity is exercised exclusively as a tool for keeping the animal organism going. Marx's sentence for the result is the most quoted in the whole section: estranged labour makes individual life into a means, such that "life itself appears only as a means to life". Eating, drinking, shelter and warmth, the animal functions, become the ends; the free conscious shaping of a world, the human function, becomes the means to them. A being whose essence is production experiences production as the price of eating. That inversion, stated with full generality, is the third relation: the worker is estranged from species-being because the first two estrangements have seized the activity in which species-being consists.

Notice what kind of step has just been taken, because classifying it correctly is the whole glossary point. Nothing new has been observed. No fresh fact about factories has been introduced. The third relation is extracted from the first two by combining them with a premise about what human beings are. Given the premise, the conclusion follows with real force: seize a creature's products and commandeer its characteristic activity, and you have estranged it from its kind essence, whatever that essence is. Deny the premise, hold that there is no species character of free conscious production, and the step has nothing to stand on, which is precisely the objection this article will state at full strength later. For the moment the descriptive point is enough. The manuscript presents the third relation as a consequence, marks it as one in its transitional sentences, and expects the reader to feel the ground shift from the economics of the wage bargain to the philosophy of human nature. The companion article in this series devoted to the concept itself, [Marx's species-being explained](/2011/05/15/marx-species-being-explained/), takes the premise apart in detail; here its role in the chain is what matters.

A distinction inside the third relation repays attention, because candidates and commentators routinely blur it. Estrangement from species-being is not the same as low morale, thwarted ambition or the sense that a job wastes one's talents, though all of those can accompany it. A brilliant engineer whose talents are lavishly used, on problems she finds absorbing, at the direction and for the ownership of an employer, with her products confronting her as alien property and her hours disposed of by contract, stands inside the first two relations completely, and the third follows in the manuscript's logic however fulfilled she feels. Conversely, a struggling smallholder, poor, anxious and often defeated by weather, works his own activity on his own plot towards his own product, and the third relation does not describe him, however hard his life is. The third relation tracks the form of activity, its conversion into a mere means, and not the quantity of satisfaction the activity yields. That is why it can be derived rather than surveyed. Feelings need questionnaires. Forms need arguments. The manuscript offers an argument, and the third relation is its first great conclusion.

## The marks of unestranged production

The derivation's premise can be examined from the positive side, through the marks by which the manuscripts recognise production that is not estranged, and the exercise is worthwhile because premises are most honestly tested by their contrasts. The first mark is universality. The animal produces under the dominion of immediate need and produces only itself, its nest, its young, its single narrow effect, repeating the pattern of its kind without variation. The human producer, by contrast, can work to the standard of any species, can carve to the measure of the material and build to the measure of a plan, can produce for needs not his own and for no need at all, in play, in inquiry, in craft pursued past the point of survival. The second mark follows from the first: production even when free from physical need, the surplus of making over necessity in which cathedrals, proofs, gardens and symphonies become possible, and which the manuscripts treat not as decoration on human life but as its signature. The third mark is prefiguration: the product exists in imagination before it exists in fact, so that the activity is conscious through and through, guided by a purpose the producer can state, revise and own. The fourth mark is the relation to nature as an inorganic body knowingly worked, a world recognised as the medium of human life rather than merely mined as a warehouse.

Set those four marks beside the wage relation as the first two relations describe it, and the third relation's derivation acquires its full visibility. Universality is replaced by the single operation endlessly repeated. Production free from need is replaced by production chained to the need that the wage must meet, so that the one sphere in which surplus making might flourish is spent entirely on necessity. Prefiguration passes to the designer, the engineer, the planning office, while the executing hands receive the plan as an external fact. Nature appears as owned raw material and as an environment fenced by other men's titles. Each mark of the species character is not merely absent but inverted, present in its opposite, and it is this systematic inversion, rather than any single deprivation, that the phrase estrangement from species-being compresses. The compression explains both the concept's power and its reception troubles. Readers who unpack the compression find an argument. Readers who meet only the compressed phrase find a slogan, and slogans are what rival lists of the types are mostly made of. A glossary exists to do the unpacking, and the unpacking has a price the reader should see stated: everything depends on whether the four marks describe a human essence or an attractive ideal. The manuscripts assert an essence. A modern defender may restate the marks as a standard of human flourishing, openly normative, and run the derivation in that key instead. The derivation survives the restatement with its joints intact, which suggests that its strength was never only Feuerbach's metaphysics. Its strength is the observation, hard to unsee once made, that the wage relation as described inverts, point by point, everything the manuscripts identify as distinctively human in production.

## Alienation from other people: the fourth relation, derived

From the third relation the manuscript takes its final step, and takes it quickly, with the words an immediate consequence doing the work of a paragraph. If a person is estranged from the product of labour, from the activity of production, and thereby from the species essence that other people share with him, then he is estranged from those other people themselves. The reasoning has an elegant severity. Species-being is not a private possession. It is precisely what is common, the human character present in every individual. To stand in an estranged relation to that common essence is therefore, without any further premise, to stand in an estranged relation to every other bearer of it. What holds of a man's relation to his work and to himself, Marx writes, holds also of his relation to the other man, to that man's labour and to the object of that labour. Each person looks at others according to the measure and the relation in which he finds himself as a worker: as a rival for the wage, as an instrument or an obstacle, as the occupant of a place in a system of places, rather than as a fellow instance of the free conscious activity that defines the kind. The social world arrives pre-estranged, because the relation to the self that mediates all social relations has been estranged first.

### Does alienation from other people simply mean loneliness?

No. Loneliness is a private feeling that can occur in any social arrangement, whereas this relation names a structure between persons: each meets the other through an estranged essence, workers confront one another as competitors, and both confront the owner who holds the product and commands the labour.

The fourth relation has a second, harder edge, which the manuscript develops by asking a disarmingly practical question. If the product of the worker's labour does not belong to the worker, and confronts him as an alien power, to whom does it belong? An alien power over human beings can only be another human being. The product belongs to a man other than the worker, to the non-worker, whose relation to the labour is enjoyment of its fruit without performance of its activity. In this way the fourth relation completes the circuit of the other three and gives them a social address. Estrangement is not a vapour hanging over the factory. It is a relation between determinate persons, in which one party's estranged activity is the other party's property and command. Marx draws the further consequence with characteristic boldness: through estranged labour the worker creates the relation of the capitalist to labour, so that private property, which political economy treats as the premise of the whole arrangement, appears in this analysis as its product and necessary consequence. The claim reverses the economists' order of explanation, and whatever its ultimate merits, it shows the fourth relation doing theoretical work no list could assign it: it is the joint where the philosophy of estrangement locks into the critique of property.

Competition among workers is the fourth relation's most recognisable daily form, and it deserves description rather than mere mention, since it shows estrangement operating horizontally, between people in the same position, and not only vertically between worker and owner. Where labour power is sold in a market, each seller's interest is set against the others by the form of the transaction itself. The worker who accepts a lower wage or a faster pace makes the labour of every other worker cheaper and more intense; the unemployed stand at the gate as a standing argument against the demands of the employed; skill, seniority and scarcity are converted into private defences hoarded against colleagues rather than held in common. None of this requires ill will. It requires only the structure, in which the other worker appears first as the measure of one's own replaceability. Marx's point in deriving the fourth relation is that this appearance is not a failure of solidarity to be corrected by exhortation alone. It is the social phenomenology of the first three estrangements, the way a world of estranged products and estranged activities necessarily looks from inside, person to person. Solidarity, where it is achieved, is achieved against the grain of the relation, which is why its achievements are real and why they are fragile.

It is worth pausing, finally, on how the four relations now stand as a set, because the fourth changes the colour of the whole. Read as a list, the types can sound like a psychology: a worker feels separated from the product, the process, himself, others, as though four flavours of discontent had been catalogued by a survey. Read as a derivation, they describe a single mechanism at four depths. At the surface, an object changes hands and accumulates as power. Beneath that, an activity changes owners and is lived as coercion. Beneath that, a human essence is inverted, its highest function demoted to a means of survival. At the bottom, a society of persons confronts itself through the estrangement, each meeting each as competitor, instrument or owner. The depth ordering explains a fact about the secondary literature that puzzles students: why writers who discuss only the fourth relation, the social one, sound so different from writers who discuss only the first, the economic one, though both cite the same ten pages. They are reading the same chain at different links. The chain is the concept. The links, taken singly, are its aspects, and the next sections assemble the chain in a form that can be carried away, tested and taught.


## The derivation chain

Everything argued so far can be compressed into a single findable artifact, the derivation chain, which sets the four relations in their manuscript order and records, for each one, what is alienated, what it is alienated from, the move in the text that establishes it, whether that move asserts or derives, and an example chosen to fit the relation rather than a general mood of dissatisfaction. Read down the status column and the article's thesis stands in miniature: asserted, asserted, derived, derived. Read across any row and the relation can be checked against the text. The chain is offered as the portable form of the passage, the thing a reader can reconstruct on a blank page, carry into a seminar, or use to audit any four-item list encountered elsewhere, since a list that cannot say which of its items are premises and which are conclusions has failed the first test the chain applies.

| Relation | What is alienated | Alienated from what | The establishing move in the manuscript | Asserted or derived | An example that fits the relation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product of labour | The object the worker produces, and with it the worker's own objectified life | The producer, who neither owns the object nor directs its use | The product confronts the producer as an alien power, independent of him, growing as he produces | Asserted from the wage relation | The assembly worker whose finished vehicles are priced beyond her wage and shipped to markets she never sees |
| Activity of production | The working hours themselves, their pace, purpose and direction | The worker, whose activity belongs to another and is lived as coercion | Labour is external to the worker, not voluntary but forced, a means to needs outside it | Asserted from the wage relation | The service worker whose speech follows a script she may not alter, timed and monitored throughout the shift |
| Species-being | Free conscious life activity, the character of the human kind | The individual, for whom that activity shrinks to a mere means of staying alive | Drawn from the first two relations: what seizes product and activity seizes the activity in which the species character consists | Derived from the first pair | The skilled maker whose craft survives only as an instrument for paying rent, its exercises dictated entirely by the wage it must earn |
| Other people | Fellow human beings, met as competitors, instruments or owners | Each person, who meets others through an estranged relation to the shared human essence | Stated as an immediate consequence, then grounded by assigning the alien product to its owner, the non-worker | Derived from the first three | Colleagues set against one another by a ranking system that converts each worker's diligence into the others' disadvantage |

Two features of the chain deserve emphasis because they are the features lists most reliably destroy. The first is the direction of dependence. Every arrow in the chain points the same way, from the wage relation through the object and the act, and only then to the essence and the society. Reverse the exposition, begin with estrangement from others as a kind of social malaise and end with the product as an illustration, and the explanatory claim evaporates: nothing any longer explains anything else, and the four become four symptoms of an unnamed unease, which is exactly the form in which the topic is usually taught and exactly the form in which it is most easily forgotten. The second feature is the asymmetry of the evidence. The first pair can be supported by pointing at institutions: contracts, property titles, the organisation of a shift. The second pair can be supported only by argument, by running the inferences and defending the premise they rest on. A treatment that gives all four the same tone of breezy certainty has therefore misdescribed its own epistemic situation twice over, claiming observation where it has inference and inference where it has observation.

## The two-plus-two structure: why the order is the argument

The claim this article has been building can now be given its sharpest form. Call it the two-plus-two structure: Marx asserts two alienations and derives two, so the correct exposition of the four types is a chain rather than a list, and any account that presents four parallel items has lost the argument that connects them. The claim is deliberately modest in one respect and deliberately aggressive in another. It is modest because it is a claim about the text, verifiable by reading ten pages with attention to their transitions, and it does not by itself assert that Marx's conclusions are true. It is aggressive because it convicts nearly the whole popular and much of the textbook literature on this topic of a structural misdescription, not a mere simplification. A simplification keeps the shape and drops the detail. The four-bullet list drops the shape and keeps only the detail, four definitions severed from the reasoning that gives each its place and two of them their entire warrant. Students examined on the list are being examined on the husk of an argument, and the best of them sense it, which is why the question whether the four are a list or a derivation recurs so persistently in seminars and in the queries readers type into search engines.

Test the claim the way the manuscript invites it to be tested, by watching the transitions rather than the headings. Between the product and the activity there is no inference, only a turn: having considered the object, the analysis considers the act that produced it, and finds the same estrangement viewed from the other side. The tone is descriptive, the evidence institutional, the modality that of report. Between the activity and species-being the prose itself changes register. The text announces a further step, speaks of what has still to be deduced, and proceeds to argue from what has been established to what must follow for the worker's relation to his own nature. Between species-being and other people the register changes again, to the briskness of corollary: an immediate consequence, the text says, and the brevity is itself the signal, since corollaries are stated shortly precisely because their work has been done upstream. A reader deaf to these transitions will see four discussions. A reader attentive to them sees a proof sketch with two axioms and two theorems, and the difference between those readers is the difference this glossary exists to make.

The structure also dissolves, at a stroke, the commonest criticism levelled at the four types in student essays and in the sceptical literature, the charge of redundancy. The third type, the critic says, merely restates the second in grander language, and the fourth merely restates the third in social language, so the famous four collapse into two, or one. Against a list the charge is formidable, since parallel items that overlap are indeed redundant. Against a chain it misses entirely. A conclusion is supposed to contain its premises in transformed form; that is what distinguishes a conclusion from a new topic. The third relation does restate the second, from the standpoint of the species rather than the shift, and it adds exactly what a restatement within a derivation adds: the generalisation that shows what the premises were really about. Criticising a derivation for the continuity between its steps is like criticising a sum for containing its addends. The live questions lie elsewhere, in whether the premises are true and whether the step to the third relation imports more than the premises contain. Those questions are serious, and the next section gives them the floor.

What the structure changes in practice can be summarised as three working rules for anyone who has to use the four types rather than merely admire them. A writer expounding them should present them in the manuscript's order and mark the inferential joints aloud, since the joints are where comprehension is tested. A teacher examining them should ask for the derivation and not the inventory, because the inventory can be memorised in four minutes while the derivation cannot be faked. A critic attacking them should aim at the joints: at the premise of species-being that carries the third relation, and at the immediacy claimed for the fourth, rather than at the first two relations, which rest on the comparatively solid ground of the wage contract's plain terms. These rules are developed for each audience in the persona sections near the end of this article. First, though, the objection must be faced in its strongest available form, because a derivation defended only against weak doubts has not been defended at all.

A deletion test gives students and critics alike a quick instrument for verifying the structure in any exposition they meet, including this one. Delete a relation in thought and observe what happens to its neighbours. Remove the product relation, and the activity relation loses its anchor in the world of things, becoming a complaint about how work feels; remove the activity relation, and the product's alien character becomes an unexplained legal fact, since nothing in the doing any longer accounts for the done. Remove species-being, and the first two relations stand intact as economics but fall silent about why any of it matters beyond the distribution of goods and hours; remove other people, and the analysis ends in a curious solitude, a theory of estrangement with no society in it, unable to say to whom the alien product belongs. Each deletion damages a different neighbour in a different way, and the pattern of damage is exactly what a chain predicts and a list cannot: in a genuine list, deleting an item leaves the others precisely as they were, definitionally complete and mutually indifferent. Run the test on any four-item presentation of this topic and its true structure announces itself. Where deletion leaves no wound, the reader is holding a list, whatever its author intended, and where deletion wounds asymmetrically, premises upstream of conclusions, the reader is holding the manuscript's argument in its working form.

## The strongest objection: a derivation read into an unfinished manuscript

The objection comes in two waves, and fairness requires that each be allowed its full height before any reply is attempted. The first wave is textual and biographical. The estranged labour section is a draft in a notebook its author never published, never revised for publication, and arguably never finished even to his own satisfaction, since the manuscripts break off, restart, and trail into fragments on other themes. To extract from such material a formal structure, two assertions and two derivations, neatly labelled and examinable, is to commit the scholar's characteristic sin of finding in a workshop the architecture of a cathedral. Drafts record thinking in motion. Their connectives may be scaffolding, reminders to the writer himself, transitions of convenience rather than claims of inference. The sequence product, activity, species-being, others might be the order in which topics occurred to a young man writing fast with Feuerbach open beside him, not the order of a proof. Later editors and teachers, hungry for doctrine and employed to examine it, had every incentive to freeze the sequence into a system, and the numbering of the four is the visible fossil of that freezing. On this wave of the objection, the two-plus-two structure says more about the needs of syllabuses than about the intentions of the manuscript.

The second wave is logical, and it is the stronger of the two. Grant that the text moves from the activity to species-being with inferential intent. What exactly is inferred, and from what? From the premises that the worker's product is taken and his activity coerced, nothing follows about a species essence unless the existence and content of that essence have already been supplied. The step to the third relation therefore does not derive species-being estrangement from the wage relation. It derives it from the wage relation plus Feuerbach's anthropology, a doctrine about free conscious activity as the human character that the young Marx had absorbed before he opened a page of Ricardo. That premise is doing all the work and earning none of the credit. A reader who doubts that human beings have a species essence at all, or who holds that whatever essence they have is not located in production, can accept every word of the first two relations and decline the third without any inconsistency. A derivation whose crucial step is an imported premise is an enthymeme dressed as a proof. The fourth relation fares no better on inspection. Its alleged immediacy is an assertion wearing a corollary's clothing: a person might plausibly be estranged from an abstract human essence while remaining warmly bound to concrete other people, in a family, a congregation, a crew of friends, and the text offers sentiment rather than argument at exactly the joint where argument was promised. On this second wave, the chain is not a chain. It is two observations, a borrowed philosophy, and a flourish.

The waves combine into a single verdict that the honest expositor must be able to state without flinching: the four types, presented as a derivation, rest on an unfinished manuscript, read through a Feuerbachian premise its author would himself later cease to avow in those terms, with the final step asserted rather than shown. If that verdict stands unqualified, the correct glossary entry for the four types would be a cautionary note about over-systematised juvenilia, and the famous quartet would shrink to a historically interesting sketch.

The reply begins with concessions, because several parts of the objection are simply true and the derivation is not served by denying them. The premise about species-being does enter from outside the economics. The manuscript is a draft. The numbering is not Marx's. The fourth step is compressed. All of that stands. What does not stand is the inference from these truths to the dismissal of the structure. Begin with the draft. Unfinished status impugns polish, not argument: notebooks and abandoned drafts can and do contain reasoning whose joints are as deliberate as anything in a published treatise, and the estranged labour section is not a heap of jottings but a sustained piece of continuous prose whose transitions announce themselves. The text does not merely juxtapose the relations. It flags the move to the third as a deduction still to be performed and the fourth as a consequence, in the author's own connective words. A reading that treats those flags as scaffolding must explain why a writer scaffolding for himself would bother to mark inference at all, and it must explain away, rather than merely discount, the text's own account of its procedure. The construction objection, pressed to its conclusion, does not show that the derivation was imposed on the passage. It shows that the derivation was found in an early passage, which no one disputes and which settles nothing about whether it is there.

The logical wave deserves an equally direct answer, and the answer is a distinction between a derivation that smuggles and a derivation that declares. A smuggled premise is a disgrace to an argument. A declared premise is its foundation, and foundations are legitimately open to challenge at the level of the premise rather than at the level of the inference. Marx's anthropology is not hidden in the passage. It is expounded, at length, in the open, in the discussion of what distinguishes human production from animal production, before the inference to the third relation is drawn. The reader is told exactly what species-being is claimed to be, given the marks of it, universal production, production free from immediate need, the imaginative prefiguration of the product, and only then shown what follows for such a being when its product and its activity are seized. That is not an enthymeme. It is a conditional proof with its condition displayed: if human life activity is free conscious production, then wage labour as described estranges the worker from his species-being. Conditionals of this kind are the normal currency of philosophy, and they are not refuted by observing that the condition can be denied. They are used, precisely, to locate disagreement at its true address. A reader who finishes the passage knowing that everything turns on the anthropology knows more, and knows it more exactly, than a reader handed four parallel definitions. The derivation's value lies partly in that location service, and it survives the denial of its premise in the way good arguments do: by making the denier say what, exactly, is being denied.

For the fourth relation, the reply concedes the compression and then points to the second, less noticed strand of its grounding. The move from species-being to other people is indeed swift when read as psychology, and the objector's warm crew of friends is a genuine possibility the text does not pause to exclude. But the manuscript does not rest the fourth relation on that move alone. It grounds it again, practically, in the assignment of the alien product: the product belongs to someone, that someone is the non-worker, and the relation between worker and owner is a relation between people, constituted by the estrangement itself. It grounds it a third way in the phenomenon of competition, in which the estranged structure of the labour market sets each worker's measure against every other. The fourth relation is thus less a corollary floated on the third than the point where the argument returns from essence to society by two roads, the road of shared nature and the road of property. That the text travels both roads in a few pages is a fact about its density, not a proof of its emptiness.

The verdict, then, is measured and double. As a matter of textual description, the two-plus-two structure stands: the derivation is in the manuscript, marked by its author, and the list that replaces it misdescribes the passage. As a matter of validity, the derivation is exactly as strong as its displayed premise, the account of species-being, and a reader who rejects that anthropology should reject the third relation while retaining the first two, which require no anthropology at all beyond the terms of the wage contract. What no reader is entitled to do is reject the third and fourth relations as redundancies invented by teachers, since they are present in the text as conclusions, nor to accept them as observations, since they are present as conclusions too. Conclusions must be argued with. That is the discipline the chain imposes, and imposing it is this article's whole purpose.


One further piece of internal evidence deserves recording against the construction objection, because it is the kind of detail an expository accident cannot easily explain. The manuscript does not merely pass through the four relations once and abandon them. It returns to them. When the analysis turns to the non-worker, the owner, it recapitulates the ground already covered, restating the worker's relations to product, activity and self as the established basis from which the relation between the two men is now to be drawn, and the recapitulation preserves the original order and the original division between what was shown from the wage relation and what was concluded from it. Writers summarising their own scaffolding rarely bother to reproduce its joints with such fidelity; writers summarising an argument they take themselves to have made do it instinctively. The recapitulation is not a proof of the two-plus-two structure on its own, and this article rests nothing on it alone. Set beside the flagged connectives, the shift of register at the third relation, and the corollary's brevity at the fourth, it completes a pattern of evidence that points one way: the sequence in the manuscript behaves like a derivation because its author was performing one, however unfinished the performance, and the burden of proof rests on the reading that would explain the pattern away.

## Who numbered the four: the textbook history of a teaching device

### Did Marx himself number the four types?

No. The passage presents the relations in sequence without numbering them, naming them as types, or treating them as a finished classification. The count of four and the parallel presentation were assembled by later editors, textbook writers and syllabus designers who needed a teachable form.

The device has a datable history, and tracing it is the surest way to keep its authority in proportion. When the manuscripts finally reached print in 1932, the first readers who grasped their importance treated the estranged labour section as a revelation of the young Marx's philosophical depth, not as a syllabus unit. Herbert Marcuse greeted the publication in an essay of that same year as an event that would put the interpretation of Marx on new foundations. Through the following decades the discussion ran in the channels of philosophy: the relation of the manuscripts to Hegel, the status of the human essence, the continuity or rupture with the author of Capital. Erich Fromm carried the concept to a mass anglophone readership in 1961 with Marx's Concept of Man, a book whose long excerpt from the manuscripts, in Milligan's translation, put estranged labour into the hands of readers who would never open a scholarly edition. Fromm's Marx is a humanist prophet of unalienated life, and the four relations appear in his pages as moments of a single lament for modern man. The numbered quartet, by contrast, is the child of the classroom rather than the study. As sociology and politics syllabuses expanded after mid-century, teachers needed the passage in a form that could be lectured in an hour and examined in three. A sequence of four, labelled and parallel, is supremely lecturable. Textbook writers supplied it, revision guides polished it, and the count hardened into common sense, until students could reasonably believe that Marx himself had drawn up the table of four in the spring of 1844.

Scholarly systematisation walked a middle path between the philosophers and the textbooks. Bertell Ollman's Alienation, published in 1971, reconstructed the concept with analytical care and distinguished the relations of the producer to his product, his activity, his species-being and his fellow men as the concept's internal structure, which gave the fourfold presentation a philosophical warrant the revision guides never sought. Yet even Ollman's scheme is a reconstruction, openly so, an analyst's map of a draft's terrain. The map is faithful. It remains a map. The glossary point for the reader is the division of labour between text and tradition: the sequence, the connectives, the inferential claims belong to Marx; the numbering, the parallel layout, the very word types belong to the tradition that taught him. Confusing those two provenances is the root error from which several standard misreadings grow. It leads students to ask which type is worst, as though ranking entries in a catalogue Marx compiled. It leads critics to charge Marx with a clumsy taxonomy when the taxonomy is theirs. And it conceals the derivation, which is the text's actual bequest, behind the tradition's packaging.

The variants in circulation confirm the diagnosis, because a classification fixed by its author does not wobble the way this one does. Some reputable textbooks give three types, folding estrangement from other people into estrangement from species-being, on the ground that the fourth adds no new content. Some give five, promoting estrangement from nature out of the third relation into an entry of its own. Some substitute alienation from self for the species-being entry, exchanging a technical term for a vaguer one that travels better in lectures. Some speak of aspects, some of forms, some of dimensions, each noun quietly encoding a different claim about the relations among the items. A reader equipped with the derivation chain can adjudicate all of these variants instead of choosing among them by taste. The three-type version has collapsed a conclusion into its premise. The five-type version has promoted a sub-theme. The self version has traded a premise with content, an anthropology that can be argued about, for a mood that cannot. The variants are not rival discoveries about a text. They are rival packagings, and the chain is the standard against which packaging can be judged.

The noun chosen for the set is itself a small archive of the tradition's intentions, and it rewards a moment's attention before the history is left. Aspects, the most cautious noun, claims only that the four are viewpoints on a single condition, and it is the noun the manuscript's own language of considering one fact under successive aspects most nearly licenses. Forms claims more, that estrangement takes four shapes which might in principle occur in varying mixtures. Dimensions borrows the authority of measurement, suggesting four scales along which a worker's condition could be scored, a suggestion the structural character of the relations does not honour. Types claims most of all, a classification with the quiet implication of a classifier, and it is no accident that the most misleading noun became the most successful, since it flatters the examination form that adopted it. A glossary cannot reform the search habits of the internet, and this article uses the received phrase in its title because that is the phrase readers bring. In its own exposition it prefers relations, the noun that keeps the derivation in view, since a relation implies its relata and its ground, while a type implies only a pigeonhole. Terminology is not decoration on this topic. At every level, from the count of four down to the noun that collects them, the packaging has argued against the passage, and the passage's defence begins with noticing the packaging.

## Near neighbours: the terms the four are confused with

A glossary earns its keep at the borders, and the four types have crowded borders. The oldest confusion is also the most instructive, because it was planted by Marx himself as a deliberate distinction: objectification. Every act of production objectifies labour, embodies it in a thing, and the potter, the poet and the programmer all do it. Estrangement is objectification under a specific social form, where the object is lost to the producer and returns as command. Writers who use the two words interchangeably make the theory say that making things is itself a kind of fall, a romantic primitivism Marx explicitly refuses, since his complaint is never that labour takes objective form but that the objective form is expropriated. The neighbour on the other side is externalisation, Entäusserung, the legal flavour of parting with what is one's own, which Marx uses for the act of surrender within the wage bargain. Three terms, three jobs: labour is externalised in the contract, objectified in the product, and the producer is estranged from both when the product and the activity return as alien powers. Collapse them and the first two relations collapse with them.

Behind Marx stands Feuerbach, and behind the four types stands a religious original that explains the concept's shape. For Feuerbach, religion is the original estrangement: humanity projects its own species essence, its wisdom, love and power, into a God who then confronts humanity as an alien and governing being, enriched exactly as his creators are impoverished. The parallel with the product relation is not an analogy imposed by later readers. It is the template Marx transposed, moving the projected essence from heaven to the economy: what God is in Feuerbach, the accumulated product, capital, is in the manuscripts, a human power that has escaped its producers and rules them. Knowing the template explains the derivation's confidence about its third step, since the logic of projection and impoverishment was worked out in the religious case before Marx borrowed it. It also arms the objection considered earlier, because a template borrowed from the criticism of religion carries its anthropology with it, and the critic who calls the third relation Feuerbachian is reporting a genealogy, not inventing a slur.

Within sociology the most consequential neighbour is anomie, and the cost of confusing the two is paid in examination halls every year. Anomie, in Durkheim's development of the term, names a state of normlessness, the failure of social regulation to bound desires and expectations, and it belongs to a theory of integration rather than a theory of production. A worker may suffer anomie in a job from which none of the four relations is absent, and may be fully estranged in all four relations while embedded in the most tightly normed workplace imaginable. The confusion is compounded by the American sociologist Melvin Seeman, whose 1959 attempt to give the concept of alienation empirical handles distinguished five variants of his own: powerlessness, meaninglessness, normlessness, isolation and self-estrangement. Seeman's five are a researcher's operational scheme, assembled from the literature for survey purposes, and they are not Marx's four in modern dress. His normlessness is borrowed from the anomie tradition, his isolation is a social-psychological condition closer to loneliness than to the fourth relation, and his self-estrangement, the sense of work as unfulfilling, corresponds roughly to the second relation with its structural teeth drawn. Syllabuses that teach Seeman and Marx in the same week without marking the border manufacture errors wholesale. The border is this: Marx's relations are forms of a property and activity structure, ascertained by analysis; Seeman's variants are states of expectation and feeling, ascertained by questionnaire. Both are legitimate objects of study. They are not the same object.

A later neighbour, reification, descends from the family but is not one of the four. Georg Lukács, writing in 1923 with the manuscripts still unpublished, built the concept of Verdinglichung, the becoming-thinglike of human relations, out of the commodity fetishism chapter of Capital, arguing that the commodity form stamps consciousness itself with a contemplative, fragmented character. Reification and estrangement overlap in territory and are routinely cited as synonyms, yet their homes differ: reification belongs to the mature critique of the commodity form and radiates outward to law, bureaucracy and science, while the four types belong to the early analysis of the labour relation and stop where that analysis stops. A precise writer uses reification for the fate of relations and consciousness under the generalised commodity form, and the four relations for the structure of estranged labour, and does not ask either term to do the other's work. The final neighbour is the bluntest: exploitation. Exploitation, in the vocabulary of the mature theory, names the extraction of surplus labour, a measurable relation between the value labour produces and the value labour power costs. It neither entails nor is entailed by any feeling of estrangement, and the two concepts classify different things, one a transfer, the other a form of activity and ownership. The contented specialist whose surplus is extracted at a high rate while she directs her own projects is exploited without much estrangement of the second kind. The harassed artisan squeezed by a merchant may be estranged in product and activity while the arithmetic of surplus runs modestly. Keeping the neighbours apart is not pedantry. Each confusion dissolves a distinction the theory needs, and the four types, properly fenced, are sharper instruments than the vague aura of dissatisfaction that popular usage calls by their name.

## Four scenes read through the chain

Abstract fences are best tested against scenes, and the chain can be carried through four workplaces to show what it classifies and what it refuses to classify. Take first the cabinetmaker in a small workshop under heavy market pressure, working long hours to commission, squeezed by timber prices and by the platforms through which customers arrive. His hardships are real and his autonomy is also real: he chooses his methods, surveys his product whole, and parts with it on terms he negotiated. The chain's verdict is discriminating. The second relation is largely absent, since the activity is his own; the first is attenuated, since the product is his until sold; the third does not bite, since his life activity remains the exercise of his craft and not merely a means, however pressured the exercise. Pressure, poverty and anxiety are present in quantity. Estrangement, in the manuscript's precise sense, is mostly absent, and a theory that cannot register that difference is not describing the world.

Take next the operative on a vehicle assembly line, the scene the tradition reaches for first. The chain lights up at every link. The product is wholly alien, aggregated into a corporate power that sets the plant's future and periodically closes plants like hers. The activity is prescribed to the second and the gesture, its tempo owned by the line. The third relation follows on the manuscript's premises, since the activity that might express free conscious production functions purely as the means to the wage that keeps her household alive. The fourth is engineered into the shift system, where absence cover, overtime allocation and productivity league tables set colleague against colleague in a daily arithmetic of comparison. Note what the scene contributes beyond illustration: it shows the four as one mechanism at four depths, not four misfortunes that coincidentally share a postcode. The line produces the product relation, the product relation presupposes the activity relation, and the last two articulate what the first two do to a life and a society.

The salaried professional tests the chain in the opposite direction, against the suspicion that the four types are a theory of manual work only. Consider a software architect in a large firm, well paid, absorbed in her problems, proud of her systems. The first relation holds in a purified form: the code she writes is the firm's, absolutely, including the right to bury it, sell it, or turn it to purposes she would refuse; her accumulated work, the platform's codebase, confronts new hires and old as an alien environment none of them owns. The second relation holds partially and interestingly: within the sprint, her activity is substantially self-directed, and to that extent the relation weakens, while the direction of the whole, what gets built and why, is disposed of elsewhere, and to that extent it stands. The third relation then comes out mixed in a way the chain predicts and a list cannot: precisely because her activity retains an element of free conscious production, the derivation to full species-being estrangement is interrupted at the point where its second premise fails. The framework's ability to return a graded verdict here, strong at the first link, partial at the second, conditional at the third, is evidence that it is an instrument rather than a slogan. Instruments give readings. Slogans give volume.

The fourth scene is the workers' cooperative, included because it tests whether the chain is a definition of capitalism in disguise. In a genuine cooperative the members own the product in common, dispose of their collective activity through their own decisions, and meet one another as co-owners rather than as competitors for a wage. On the manuscript's logic the four relations attenuate together, which is the result the theory wants: remove the sale of labour power to an alien owner and the chain loses its first link, and with it the derived links. Market pressure remains, and members may drive themselves harder than any employer drove them, a phenomenon debated under the name of self-exploitation. The chain handles the case with its usual discrimination. Self-imposed intensity is not the second relation, because the disposition of the activity remains with those who perform it; the discomfort is real and the estrangement, strictly, is absent. Whether that discrimination marks the theory's precision or its blindness is a live argument, and a good one. What the scene establishes for glossary purposes is narrower and sufficient: the four types are keyed to a specific social relation, the sale of labour power, and not to work, effort, hierarchy or unhappiness as such. Where the key relation is absent, the chain honestly reports absence, and a concept willing to report absence is a concept that can be trusted when it reports presence.


A fifth scene, the hospital nurse, belongs in the set because care work is where sympathetic readers most often misapply the chain in both directions at once. The nurse's product is an effect, the stabilised, comforted, discharged patient, and it belongs in the relevant sense to the institution that sells and schedules care, not to the hands that produced it. Her activity is doubly governed, by protocol and by professional judgement, and the chain gives a split reading that matches the reality with unusual fidelity: where protocol dictates and staffing ratios compel, the second relation stands; where clinical judgement genuinely disposes of the hour, it recedes, sometimes within a single shift. The third relation is the one most often fumbled here. Because nursing expresses recognisably human capacities, sympathy, attention, skilled tenderness, observers conclude either that no estrangement can be present, or that its presence proves the framework sentimental. The chain says otherwise on both counts. The capacities can be fully exercised and fully estranged in form when their exercise is a means commanded by an institution's throughput, exactly as the engineer's absorption did not dissolve her first relation; and where the nurse's care is self-directed in substance, the framework reports attenuation without embarrassment. What the scene finally shows is the framework doing the work of a decent instrument in untidy territory: refusing the melodrama of declaring an entire vocation alienated, refusing the complacency of declaring it exempt, and returning differentiated readings that follow the disposition of activity and the ownership of effects hour by hour.

## The premise examined: what the first two relations actually assert

Because the whole chain rests on the first pair, the pair deserves a slower audit than either usually receives. To call the first two relations asserted is not to call them arbitrary. An assertion in this context is a claim read directly off a described state of affairs, and the state of affairs here is the wage contract as the economists themselves describe it, supplemented by one precondition they mention less often. The precondition is separation. The worker who sells labour power does so because he lacks independent access to the means of production, the tools, materials and premises without which his capacity to work cannot become actual work. He is free in the double sense the later Marx would make famous: free to sell his labour power to any buyer, and free of any property in the conditions that would let him employ it himself. Given that separation, the contract's terms do the rest. The buyer acquires the use of the capacity for the contracted period, owns everything the capacity produces during that period, and directs the producing. Product alienation and activity alienation are not two discoveries added to this description from outside. They are the description itself, unfolded: the first names what the contract does to the object, the second what it does to the hours. This is why the manuscript can assert them with such economy, and why disputes about them tend to be disputes about whether the description is complete, not about whether an inference has been botched.

Seeing the first pair as an unfolded description also explains their unequal vulnerability to counter-example, a point examiners reward and lists obscure. The product relation is nearly analytic given the premises: if the contract assigns the product to the buyer, the producer's exclusion from the product follows by the meaning of the terms, and the only live questions concern the product's further career as accumulated power. The activity relation carries one more ingredient, an experiential and dispositional claim: that labour performed under another's direction, for purposes set elsewhere, is lived and structured as external, as not the worker's own. That claim is stronger than the contract's letter, since a contract might assign direction while leaving the performer absorbed, identified with the task, even joyful in it, and the manuscript meets the point not by denying such cases but by classifying them: absorption under alien direction is the activity relation with its subjective sting anaesthetised, and the theory's measure remains the locus of disposition rather than the quality of feeling. Critics who think the anaesthetised case refutes the second relation are measuring feelings. Critics who think it refutes nothing are measuring structure. The manuscript measures structure, and says so in its definitions, which is all a glossary can ask of a concept: that its measure be declared and consistently applied.

One more refinement completes the audit of the premises, concerning time. The first relation describes a stock, the accumulated product world, while the second describes a flow, the hours as they pass. Their union in a single working life produces the peculiar temporality of estranged labour, in which the worker's past works against his present: the more completely yesterday's labour was objectified in alien property, the more completely present labour is commanded by the apparatus that property composes. Estrangement is therefore not a static condition sampled at a moment but a ratchet, deepening with accumulation itself, each cycle of production enlarging the alien power that organises the next. The mature theory will give this ratchet an economic mechanics, in the analysis of accumulation and the reserve army, but the ratchet's shape is already visible in the manuscripts, and it is a shape no list of four types has ever conveyed. Lists are timeless. The passage is about a process that feeds on its own results, and the first two relations, read as stock and flow of a single estrangement, are where that process is geared together.

## Do the four types apply beyond the wage relation?

### Can the four types appear outside paid employment?

Partly. The manuscript builds all four on the sale of labour power, so they apply strictly where that sale exists. Wherever its elements recur separately, unpaid care, coerced assignments, compelled production for another's accumulation, some relations reappear in altered form, though the full chain needs the wage relation.

The strict answer comes first, because glossary precision is this article's charter. The four relations are derived from a specific transaction, the sale of labour power by a propertyless worker to an owner of the conditions of production, and they hold in their full chained form where that transaction holds. Outside it, the honest procedure is not to ask whether the types apply, as though they were a spray to be applied to any surface, but to ask which elements of the premise are present, and to let the answer determine which relations follow and in what altered shape. Applied with that discipline, the framework illuminates several territories beyond the factory gate without annexing them. Applied without it, the word estrangement becomes a universal solvent for every form of human dissatisfaction, and a concept that explains everything has quietly stopped explaining anything.

Gig work is the territory where the premise is most artfully disguised, and the chain cuts through the disguise with satisfying directness. The platform driver or courier is, in legal form, an independent contractor, an owner of means of production in the shape of a car or a bicycle, nobody's employee. Legal form, however, is not the chain's measure. Ask the structural questions. Who disposes of the activity during its performance: the worker choosing his hours, or the application allocating tasks, setting prices, prescribing routes, rating performance and disciplining by deactivation? Who owns the product, where the product is the completed service and, more consequentially, the data and the network the service continually builds? Where the answers point to the platform, the first two relations hold in substance beneath a contract that denies them in form, and the derived relations follow as far as the premises carry them. Where a genuinely independent tradesperson sets prices, chooses clients and directs the work, the chain reports the opposite, however the tax code classifies her. The framework's loyalty is to relations, not labels, which is why it remains serviceable in an economy industrious at relabelling. The fuller contemporary survey of such cases, from warehouses to platforms, is the business of the series article on [alienation at work today](/2011/06/15/alienation-at-work-today/); the present concern is the rule of application, and the rule is structural.

Unpaid domestic labour presents the harder and more interesting test, because the premise is partly present and partly transformed. The person who cooks, cleans and cares within a household produces use values consumed directly, directs much of the activity in form while being constrained in substance by necessity and by expectations not her own, and receives no wage in which a sale of labour power could be located. Feminist readers of the manuscripts have long disputed what follows. On one side, the absence of the wage transaction places the case outside the chain's strict scope, and the subordination involved is better analysed in its own terms than borrowed ones. On the other side, the elements of directed activity, of life activity consumed by necessity, of products that vanish in the using and leave the producer's position unchanged, reproduce the pattern of the first three relations closely enough to make the analogy argue rather than merely decorate. A glossary should report the dispute rather than settle it, and should mark the deciding consideration: the chain's engine is the separation of the producer from the conditions and product of production by a property relation, and the household case turns on whether an analogous separation, of the carer from independent subsistence, does the same work. Where a reader finds that it does, the relations follow in altered form. Where not, not. The concept's border is drawn by its premise, and borders drawn by premises can be argued about rationally, which is all that precision ever promises.

Compulsory and institutional production, the work exacted in prisons, in conscript labour, in the assessed exercises of students, supplies the third test, and returns the most qualified verdict of all. Coerced activity is present in extreme form, so the second relation's description fits the hours well; products are appropriated by the institution, so the first relation has a foothold; yet the absence of the labour market, of the free seller confronting a buyer, changes the mechanism at its root, since the estrangement is imposed by direct command rather than constituted by a contract between formally equal parties. Marx's analysis is of the second mechanism, the peculiar one in which domination wears the dress of free exchange, and extending the four types to command economies of labour is an analogy of structure, illuminating about the experience of the activity, silent about the specific social form the manuscripts exist to explain. The student case is the mildest version of the same pattern: assessed work is performed under external direction for a product, the essay or examination script, which the producer surrenders and which returns as a grade that governs his future, a miniature of the product confronting its maker as an alien power. The miniature is real enough to teach with. It is not the wage relation, and the chain's derived links, which run through property and species-being, should not be pronounced over it without the qualifications this section has modelled. Precision is not the enemy of application. It is what keeps application honest.


## One without the others: how the links can separate

A derivation asserts necessary connection, and the sharpest way to interrogate the chain is to ask whether its links ever come apart in experience. If the four relations always arrived together, inseparably, the derivational claim would be plausible but untestable in detail. If they separate freely, in any combination, the claim of derivation weakens towards mere association. The cases examined in this article suggest the interesting middle position, and stating it exactly is a genuine refinement of the standard account. The links separate where, and only where, the shared premise is partially absent. The absorbed software architect showed the pattern: product estrangement complete, activity estrangement partial because self-direction survived inside the working day, and the derived third relation correspondingly interrupted, not by an exception to the logic but by the failure of one of its inputs. The chain behaves, in other words, less like a row of dominoes and more like a circuit whose later lamps dim as the current through the earlier ones weakens. That behaviour is what a derivation from a premise predicts. A list predicts nothing of the kind, and the observed gradation is therefore a point in the chain's favour that its textbook presentations never claim for it.

The service economy supplies the separation case that strains the first link rather than the second. Much service labour produces no durable object: the cleaned room, the served meal, the completed call, the cared-for patient are consumed in the very act of production, and the product relation, framed for loaves and vehicles, must be translated before it can be applied. The translation is available, and the manuscripts' logic performs it without strain. What the service worker produces is an effect owned and sold by another: the room's cleanliness is the hotel's to sell nightly, the call's resolution is the centre's metric, the care is the agency's contracted output. The producer is excluded from the effect as thoroughly as the weaver from the cloth, and the effect, aggregated as ratings, contracts and brand, confronts its producers as governing power in the now familiar way. Yet a residue of strain is honest to record. The drama of the object, the thing that outlasts its maker and accumulates, is muted where the product evaporates at the moment of delivery, and the first relation in services leans harder on the aggregate, the firm's stock of reputation and contracts, than on any item a worker can point to. The chain survives the translation with a small loss of vividness and no loss of structure, which is the most that should ever be claimed for a concept carried into territory its author did not survey.

The separation can also run in the opposite direction, and the opposite case guards against a lazy assumption that the first relation is always the strongest. Imagine piece work performed at home by a skilled embroiderer for a single merchant buyer. Her activity, in its rhythms and techniques, is her own to an unusual degree; she works when and as she chooses within the deadline, mistress of her craft in the performing of it. The product relation, by contrast, is total: every finished piece is the merchant's by the terms of their arrangement, priced by his knowledge of markets she cannot reach, her dependence on his orders the governing fact of her working life. Here the second link is weak and the first is strong, and the chain, read carefully, accommodates the case because its two assertions are distinct readings of the wage relation's neighbourhood, of putting-out and contracting forms that precede and accompany the factory. What the case cannot do is arise inside the classical factory form itself, where the buyer of labour power purchases disposition over the activity along with title to the product, and buys both in a single transaction. The manuscript's home ground is that transaction. At its edges, in the penumbra of contracting, platforms, services and home work, the links separate in instructive patterns. At its centre they hold together, because a single premise generates them jointly. A concept whose unity is explained by its premise, and whose partial failures are explained by the premise's partial absence, is behaving the way serious concepts behave, and the four types, so read, are a more respectable instrument than either their devotees or their detractors usually notice.

## The four types and the division of labour: a standing confusion

One confusion survives every fence erected so far, and a precision glossary should dismantle it explicitly: the belief that the four types are Marx's theory of the division of labour under another name, so that estrangement is caused by specialisation and would be cured by job rotation. The belief has a textual excuse. In The German Ideology, written two years after the Paris notebooks, estrangement is bound tightly to the division of labour, and the famous sentence declares division of labour and private property to be identical expressions, the one affirming what the other predicates of human activity. Readers who meet that sentence first understandably import it backwards into the manuscripts and read the four relations as an anatomy of specialisation. The import is a mistake about mechanism. In the estranged labour section the engine is not the splitting of tasks but the sale of labour power: the product is alien because it is owned by the buyer of the activity, and the activity is alien because its disposition passed with the sale, and both would hold in an unspecialised workshop of generalists exactly as they hold on the line. Specialisation aggravates the second relation cruelly, since the narrower the fragment of activity sold, the less of the producer's powers the activity can engage, and it deepens the first by making the finished product unrecognisable to any single maker. Aggravation, however, is not causation, and the distinction is testable in both directions. The cooperative of narrow specialists who own their product and dispose of their activity in common shows specialisation without the chain. The unspecialised day labourer, hired for whatever tasks the day brings, propertyless and directed in all of them, shows the chain without specialisation. A theory of estrangement keyed to the division of labour cannot explain the second case. The manuscripts' theory, keyed to property and the sale of activity, explains both, which is why the glossary insists on the key. Job rotation treats a symptom the theory acknowledges. It leaves the relations the theory names exactly where they stood.

## The four after the manuscripts: Capital, fetishism and the break debate

No glossary of the four types is complete without an account of their strange afterlife, because the structure that organises the manuscripts of 1844 does not organise the work of Marx's maturity, and readers deserve to know what became of it. The vocabulary thins dramatically. Capital does not deploy the fourfold scheme, rarely uses the language of species-being, and builds its critique of the labour relation on the analysis of the commodity, of labour power and of surplus value instead. In place of estrangement from the product, the mature work offers commodity fetishism, the doctrine that under generalised commodity production the social relations between producers assume the fantastic form of relations between things, so that the product world governs its makers not because a philosopher says it confronts them, but because the value form itself inverts subject and object in daily practice. The kinship with the first relation is plain, and so is the difference: fetishism is derived from the analysis of the commodity rather than asserted from the wage contract, and it explains the inversion as a property of a whole mode of exchange rather than of the employment relation alone. Whether the later doctrine is the earlier one grown rigorous, or a replacement that quietly discards the earlier one's anthropology, is the question on which the scholarship of the break divides.

The break thesis, associated above all with Louis Althusser, holds that an epistemological break separates the ideological, Feuerbachian Marx of 1844 from the scientific Marx of Capital, and that the concepts of estrangement and species-being belong to the superseded side of the divide, humanist baggage the mature theory unloaded. On this reading the four types are a museum piece: historically fascinating, theoretically discharged. The continuity reading replies that the break is overstated, pointing to the Grundrisse of 1857 and 1858, where the language of estrangement returns in force in the analysis of money and capital, and to the fetishism chapter itself, whose argumentative shape, a human relation appearing as a power of things, is the first relation's shape generalised. It points further to the positive counter-image that closes the third volume of Capital, where the realm of freedom is said to begin beyond labour determined by necessity, and associated producers rationally regulate their interchange with nature: a vision that answers, point for point, the inversions the manuscripts catalogued, activity reclaimed as self-directed, nature reclaimed as a commonly governed body, production reclaimed as the free development of human powers. A doctrine whose negative the early work states and whose positive the late work still sketches is not obviously a doctrine abandoned. The prudent glossary verdict is the one the evidence supports: the fourfold structure as such is not carried forward, its third relation's Feuerbachian housing is dismantled, and its substance is rebuilt inside the value theory in forms, fetishism above all, that no longer need the anthropology to run. Readers who meet the four types in an examination are meeting the early form of a lifelong argument, and the derivation they are asked to reproduce is the argument's first, most legible draft.

There is a final reception fact that belongs in a precision glossary, because it governs how the four types are actually used in the wild. In journalistic and managerial circulation the word alienation has drifted almost entirely free of the manuscript, becoming a synonym for disengagement, low morale, or the employee's failure to identify with an employer's mission, conditions to be remedied by communication exercises and values workshops. Every element of that usage inverts the concept it borrows. The manuscript's relations are structural and would persist, untouched, through the most successful morale campaign ever run, since they concern ownership and disposition rather than identification and mood. A worker may identify totally with a firm's mission while standing in all four relations in their purest form, and may feel estranged from nothing while being, in the concept's strict sense, estranged from product, activity, species-being and fellows. Writers who use the word in its Marxist sense owe their readers the discipline of the chain: name the relation, show the premise, and let engagement scores fall where they may.


## What the manuscript does next: wages, property and the non-worker

The estranged labour section does not end with the fourth relation, and its continuation shows the derivation generating consequences well beyond the quartet the textbooks extracted. Having established that the worker's estrangement produces the relation of the non-worker, the owner, Marx turns the result on the concept of wages themselves. Wages, he argues, are a necessary consequence of estranged labour, not a separate institution that might be reformed while the estrangement stands: the wage is the form in which the sale of activity by the hour is paid for, and wage and estrangement are the same relation viewed from the side of payment and from the side of experience. From this he draws the section's most abrasive corollary, aimed at the egalitarian communism of his contemporaries. An enforced equalisation or raising of wages, leaving the relation itself intact, would alter nothing in the structure the four relations describe; it would be, in his deliberately shocking phrase, merely a "better remuneration of slaves", payment improved while the position of the paid remained the position the chain anatomises. Whatever a reader thinks of the corollary's politics, its logic is the chain's logic applied without sentimentality: if the disease is a form of activity and ownership, remedies addressed to amounts will not touch it, and a glossary that reports the four types without their corollary has understated how radical, and how uncongenial to simple redistribution, the young Marx's argument actually was.

The continuation also resolves, in the manuscript's own way, the puzzle of private property's origin that political economy had left as a brute premise. The economists begin from private property as a fact and derive the rest; the estranged labour section ends by deriving private property from estranged labour, as its product, result and necessary consequence, while conceding the appearance of circularity and answering it with a temporal distinction: estranged labour is the origin, private property its crystallised result, and thereafter each reproduces the other, property becoming the means by which labour estranges itself afresh in every generation. The four relations sit inside this larger arc as its mechanism. They explain how a labour relation, once established, manufactures both the property that confronts the worker and the persons, owner and worker, who confront each other through it. That is a great deal of theoretical work for ten pages of a notebook to perform, and it is work that proceeds entirely through the derivational method this article has been tracing: nothing in the continuation is a fresh list, everything is drawn out of the relations already secured. The textbooks froze the argument at its fourth step because four steps make a syllabus unit. The argument itself kept walking.

## The chain in other hands: later writers re-run the derivation

The derivation's afterlife in the hands of later writers is a useful final test of its reality, since structures that exist only in an expositor's imagination do not usually prove portable. Fromm took the chain's conclusion as his starting point and ran it in the direction of social psychology, arguing that the estrangement of activity and product in modern production and consumption produces a character structure oriented to having rather than being, receptive, marketing itself, estranged from its own powers in leisure as in labour. Critics have found Fromm's version therapeutic where the original is structural, and the criticism is just as far as it goes, yet the portability is the point: the inference from seized activity to a deformed relation to self and others survived translation into a different discipline, changed in rigour, recognisable in shape. Ollman ran the chain in the opposite direction, towards rigour, reconstructing the four relations as an internally related structure in which each is a viewpoint on the whole of estranged labour rather than a separate part of it. His relational reading complicates the linear derivation this article has defended, and the complication should be recorded honestly: if each relation contains the others from its own angle, the chain is less a sequence of dominoes than a spiral, in which the derivation's order is the order of exposition and understanding rather than of temporal or logical production. The spiral and the chain agree against the list, which is the glossary's quarrel. They disagree, interestingly, about whether the fourth relation could in principle be expounded first by a sufficiently skilled writer who then unfolded the others from it. The manuscript's connectives favour the chain. The concept's internal unity leaves the spiral open. A precise reader can hold both, using the chain for exposition and the spiral as a reminder that exposition is not ontology.

Jean-Paul Sartre, who cannot have borrowed from a text he read late and partially, reinvented the first relation's core under another name, and independent reinvention is the strongest testimony a structure can receive. In the Critique of Dialectical Reason the practico-inert names worked matter that turns back on its makers: the deforested hillside that floods the farmers who cleared it, the machine ensemble that imposes its rhythms and its unemployment on the class whose labour built and staffs it. The mechanism is the product relation generalised into a philosophy of history, past labour congealed in things and governing present labour through them, and Sartre's development of seriality, of people rendered other to one another by their common subjection to such worked matter, traverses the same ground as the fourth relation by an entirely separate road. Two thinkers, starting from different premises and vocabularies, converged on the structure product, activity, self, others because the structure answers to something in the object itself: in a society where labour is mediated by accumulated things and by the market, the path from the alien object to the estranged society is the path the phenomena themselves suggest. Convergence does not prove the derivation true. It does make the hypothesis that teachers invented it considerably harder to maintain.

The analytical tradition, finally, subjected the chain to the discipline of asking what would count as its microfoundations, and emerged with a split verdict that a balanced glossary should report. The first two relations survived the scrutiny with ease once translated into claims about property rights over products and control rights over activity, both of which can be specified exactly and observed in contracts and organisations. The third relation survived only where its defenders were willing to restate species-being in the leaner vocabulary of self-realisation, of capacities whose development is a human good, a restatement that keeps the inference while surrendering the Feuerbachian metaphysics that originally carried it. The fourth relation divided its examiners, some treating competition and mutual instrumentalisation as derivable from the labour market's structure, others as contingent pathologies that solidarity and institutions can durably correct. The split verdict is, in its way, the most respectful the chain has received: a structure examined joint by joint, found load-bearing in some places and in need of recasting in others, is a structure being treated as an argument. Which returns the discussion to the only claim this article has insisted on throughout. The four types deserve to be treated as an argument, because that is what, in the manuscript, they are.


## For students and exam candidates

This topic rewards one skill above all others, and it is not recall. Any candidate can memorise a quartet of labels in an evening, and examiners know it, which is why the marks on this question cluster around the derivation. When a question asks what the four types are, the strong answer states them in the manuscript's order, marks the first two as read off the wage relation, and then shows the second two being drawn out, naming the premise about species-being on which the third depends. That single structural gesture separates the script from the pile, because it demonstrates comprehension of an argument rather than custody of a list. Build the answer as the text builds itself: product first, with the image of the object returning as a governing power; activity second, with the locus of command as the test; species-being third, derived in the open, with the condition stated; other people fourth, grounded twice, in the shared essence and in the figure of the owner who holds the product. Examples earn their place only when they instantiate a relation rather than a mood, so choose one scene per relation and say which feature of the scene carries the classification. Keep a compact note of the chain, its order and its two inferential joints, among your revision materials; you can [save your notes and build a citation-linked reading list free on VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html), which suits a topic where the order of items is itself the thing to be remembered. Avoid the three classic mark-losers: presenting the four as a numbered classification Marx issued, treating the fourth as loneliness, and using unhappiness as evidence for any of the relations. Each is an error this article has fenced in turn, and each is the kind of error a marker can identify from a single sentence.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

Teach the sequence backwards from the habit your students arrive with. They will bring the list, absorbed from earlier schooling and revision sites, so begin by honouring it: write the four labels up, concede that each names something real, and then ask the question the list cannot answer, which step in the text is an inference and which is a report. Let the class hunt for the connectives in a translation of the estranged labour section, and let them discover that the passage flags its own deductions. The order to teach in is the manuscript's order, against the temptation to begin with the social relation because it feels accessible: beginning with other people strands the class in psychology within ten minutes, while beginning with the product keeps the argument on institutional ground until the premises can carry the ascent to species-being. A seminar exercise that reliably produces genuine argument assigns the objection in this article to one half of the room, the imported Feuerbachian premise, and the conditional-proof reply to the other half, with instructions to locate the exact sentence where the premise enters. The series companion on [teaching alienation to students](/2011/08/15/teaching-alienation-to-students/) develops sequences and discussion designs for this topic at classroom length. In assessment, ask for the derivation and mark the joints; a candidate who flags the premise has understood more than one who recites all four definitions flawlessly.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Three cautions govern serious use. First, provenance: cite the four as a structure of the estranged labour section of the first Paris manuscript, in the edition and translation used, and never attribute the numbering, the word type, or a taxonomy to Marx himself; the count belongs to the twentieth century teaching tradition documented in this article, and conflating the two provenances is the commonest scholarly slip on this topic. Second, translation: Entfremdung is rendered both estrangement and alienation across editions, and the Milligan and Livingstone and Benton translations differ in the connective passages on which derivations turn, so any argument about the inferential joints should be checked against the German. Third, scope: the relations are structural, keyed to the sale of labour power, and their extension to platforms, households or institutions is analogy disciplined by the premise, not automatic coverage. Journalists in particular should resist the drifted usage in which alienation means whatever employees tell a survey they feel; the concept classifies relations of ownership and command, and a workforce can score high on every engagement index while standing in all four relations undisturbed.

## Correct usage: the four types of alienation in summary

The rules of correct usage can now be gathered, and they are few enough to hold entire. Name the four in the manuscript's order, product, activity, species-being, other people, and mark the change of logical gear between the second and the third, since the first pair are asserted from the wage relation and the second pair derived from them. Credit the numbering to the teaching tradition and the sequence to Marx, never the reverse. Keep objectification innocent, anomie elsewhere, Seeman's five in their own scheme, and exploitation on its own axis, and let none of them borrow the four types' name. Test every proposed example by asking which relation it instantiates and which feature carries the classification, and discard examples that testify only to fatigue, pressure or discontent. When extending the types beyond the wage relation, argue from the premise, element by element, and report partial fits as partial. When disputing the types, aim at the joints: the anthropology that carries the third relation, the immediacy claimed for the fourth, since the first two stand on the comparatively plain ground of property and contract. Used this way, the four types recover the character their author gave them, not a catalogue of modern malaise but a compact argument about what the sale of labour power does, in sequence, to the things a person makes, the hours a person lives, the kind a person belongs to, and the people a person meets. The list will go on being memorised, because lists are portable and arguments are not. The reader of this article has the argument, and with it the means to rebuild the list correctly whenever it is needed, which is the only kind of knowing a glossary should certify.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What are the four types of alienation?

In Marx's estranged labour analysis the worker is cut off from four things in sequence: the object he produces, which belongs to another and accumulates as a power over him; the activity of producing, which is directed and disposed of by another; his species-being, the free conscious activity that defines human life, now shrunk to a means of survival; and other people, whom he meets as competitors or as the owner of his product. The sequence matters, since the last two are drawn out of the first two.

### Q: What is alienation from the product of labour?

It is the producer's exclusion from the thing he makes. Under the wage contract the finished object is the employer's property from the moment of its making, so the worker's own embodied labour confronts him as something foreign: an item priced against his wage in the shops, and, accumulated with all the other products, the factory, brand and capital that hire, pace and discipline him. The relation concerns ownership and command over the object world labour creates, not whether the worker happens to like or use the item.

### Q: What is alienation from the act of production?

It is estrangement within the working hours themselves rather than in their result. The activity is not the worker's own: its purpose, tempo and manner are disposed of by the buyer of his labour power, so the labour is experienced as external and coerced, a forced performance undertaken only as a means to the wage. Marx's test is the locus of command. Where the performer directs the activity, even under hardship, this relation weakens; where another directs it, the relation holds however pleasant the surroundings or contented the performer.

### Q: What is alienation from other human beings?

It names the way estranged labour reorganises relations between persons. Because each worker confronts his own nature as something foreign, he confronts other people, the bearers of that same nature, through the estrangement: fellow workers appear as rivals whose cheaper labour threatens his place, and the non-worker appears as the owner who holds the product and commands the activity. Marx calls this an immediate consequence of the other relations and grounds it practically in property, since the alien product must belong to somebody, and that somebody is another man.

### Q: Are the four types a list or a derivation?

A derivation, in the passage itself. Marx establishes the product relation and the activity relation directly from the terms of the wage bargain, then announces and performs the step to species-being, and states the relation to other people as a consequence in its turn. The parallel list is a later teaching presentation that strips out the inferential joints. Presenting the four as a derivation is therefore not an interpretation imposed on the text but a report of how the text proceeds, connectives and all.

### Q: Which type of alienation is most important?

The question assumes a catalogue whose entries can be ranked, which the structure resists. Within the derivation, the product relation carries the greatest foundational weight, since it is the premise from which the others are developed and the one tied most directly to property. The species-being relation carries the greatest philosophical weight, because it is where the argument's claim about human nature lives and where its strongest objection strikes. A reader forced to choose should say which kind of weight is meant, foundational or philosophical, and answer accordingly.

### Q: Can you experience one type of alienation without the others?

In the classical factory form the four travel together, because a single transaction, the sale of labour power, generates them jointly. At the edges of that form they separate in instructive patterns. A self-directing professional may be wholly excluded from her product while directing much of her activity, which weakens the second relation and interrupts the derivation of the third. A home-based piece worker can show the reverse pattern. The separations follow the premise: where the elements of the wage relation are only partly present, the relations appear only in part.

### Q: Are the four types Marx's own numbering?

No. Marx neither numbers the relations, calls them types, nor presents them as a finished classification. He develops them in sequence inside one section of an unpublished notebook. The count of four, the parallel layout and the label types were assembled during the twentieth century by editors, translators, textbook writers and syllabus designers who needed the passage in teachable and examinable form. The device is legitimate and useful; the error lies only in mistaking it for the author's own presentation and thereby losing the derivation it conceals.

### Q: What are examples of each type of alienation?

For the product, the assembly worker whose finished vehicles are priced beyond her wage and shipped to markets she never sees. For the activity, the service worker whose words follow a script she may not alter, timed and monitored through the shift. For species-being, the skilled maker whose craft, the free exercise of his powers, survives only as an instrument for paying rent. For other people, colleagues set against one another by a ranking system that turns each worker's diligence into the rest's disadvantage. Each example fits its relation structurally, not by depicting unhappiness.

### Q: Do the four types apply outside the workplace?

Strictly, the analysis is built on the sale of labour power, so its home ground is the employment relation. Beyond that ground the types apply by disciplined analogy: ask which elements of the premise are present in the case at hand, unpaid care, platform work, coerced institutional tasks, and let the answer decide which relations follow and in what altered form. Where none of the premise is present, the honest finding is that the types do not describe the case, however dissatisfied the people in it may be.

### Q: Why does alienation from species-being follow from the first two types?

Because species-being, on Marx's account, consists in free conscious productive activity, and the first two relations seize exactly that activity and its fruit. If what a person makes is taken as alien property, and the making itself is directed by another as a coerced means to a wage, then the activity that constitutes the human essence has been converted into a mere instrument for staying alive. Life activity inverted into a means to life is the third relation, and the inference requires the premise about what human activity essentially is.

### Q: What should a teacher use to show the derivation rather than the list?

Use the passage itself, in translation, with attention fixed on its transitions. Have students locate the sentences where the analysis turns from the object to the act, where it announces a further relation still to be deduced, and where it states a consequence as immediate. A two-column exercise works well in prose form: one column for what is reported from the wage relation, one for what is inferred, with the species-being premise named as the hinge. The chain table in this article can serve as the check against which students compare their findings.

### Q: How does alienation from other people appear outside paid employment?

It appears wherever people are made to meet one another through an estranged measure rather than directly: applicants ranked against one another for a fixed pool of places until each becomes the obstacle to the rest, carers and cared-for persons processed as units in a system neither directs, neighbours set into competition by allocations designed elsewhere. In such settings the form of the fourth relation, persons confronting persons as rivals, instruments or gatekeepers, recurs without a wage changing hands. The analogy holds at the level of the social form, while its mechanism differs from the property mechanism the manuscript analyses.

### Q: Which type of alienation does a journalist most often misname?

The third, species-being. Coverage of work routinely describes any frustrated, underused or bored employee as alienated in this sense, when the relation names something stricter: the conversion of a person's life activity into a mere means of existence, derived from the loss of product and activity. A demanding job that engages someone's powers fully may involve no species-being estrangement in Marx's structure, while a comfortable post whose holder's activity is wholly instrumentalised may involve it completely. The misnaming matters because it turns a structural diagnosis into a complaint about job design and morale.

### Q: How does control over the product differ from control over the process in the four types?

Control over the product is a question of ownership and disposition after and during making: who holds title to the finished thing, who decides its use, sale and accumulation. It defines the first relation. Control over the process is a question of command inside the activity itself: who sets the purpose, pace, method and duration while the work is being done. It defines the second relation. The two usually pass to the same buyer in a single contract, which is why they are confused, but they can separate, as the self-directing professional with no claim on her output shows.
