Uneven and combined development is the Marxist concept most often summarised wrongly, because the summary almost always stops halfway. Uneven development, the claim that societies move at different speeds and reach different levels, is a commonplace that no serious historian disputes and no reader needs Trotsky to learn. The concept earns its name, its literature and its long academic afterlife from the second word. Combined development holds that a society entering the modern world late does not repeat the sequence travelled by the pioneers. Pressed by rivals already armed with railways, steel mills, modern navies and central banks, the latecomer takes over the finished products of other histories wholesale, plants them inside social relations that are centuries older, and produces something that is neither a copy of the advanced society nor an earlier stage of it. The result is an amalgam: the most modern factory in Europe ringed by peasant communes, a central bank beside a subsistence village economy, a conscript army officered by a landed caste. That amalgam, and the distinctive instabilities it generates, is the whole subject of this article.

![Uneven and combined development, backwardness, borrowed forms and the privilege of history - Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

The governing claim can be stated at the outset, because everything that follows either expounds it or tests it. The combination is the concept. Unevenness alone explains nothing that a school atlas does not already show, since maps have always displayed rich and poor neighbours side by side. What Trotsky added, in the opening chapter of his history of the Russian revolution, was a mechanism: external necessity forces backward societies to borrow, borrowing skips stages rather than repeating them, and skipped stages do not vanish but fuse with what was already there, so that archaic and advanced forms coexist inside a single social formation and grind against each other. A reader who grasps that mechanism possesses a tool for reading Russian factories, Meiji shipyards, Ottoman debt offices and a shelf of international relations theory. A reader who stops at unevenness possesses a truism. This article therefore spends its length where the concept does its work: on combination, on the privilege of historic backwardness that makes combination possible, on the cases that display it, on the rivals that contest it, and on the second career the idea has enjoyed in a discipline far from its birthplace.

## Uneven and combined development defined without the usual shortcut

Definitions in this field usually fail by generosity, stretching the term until it covers every contact between societies. Precision requires the opposite movement. Uneven and combined development names a specific proposition about how development proceeds when it proceeds late, under pressure, inside a world already partly transformed. The proposition has four load-bearing parts, and removing any one of them collapses the concept back into banality. First, development across the world is uneven: techniques, class structures, state forms and levels of productivity differ sharply between societies at any given moment, and the differences are not noise but the normal condition of history. Second, societies do not develop in sealed containers, so the less developed are never simply left behind in an earlier century; they are taxed, invaded, traded with, lent to and preached at by the more developed, and their internal life is shaped by that contact at every step. Third, contact under pressure produces borrowing of the most advanced available forms rather than slow indigenous repetition of the pioneers' path, because a state facing foreign armies and foreign goods cannot wait three generations for its own guilds to ripen into factories. Fourth, the borrowed forms land in soil prepared by a different history, where they fuse with surviving older relations instead of replacing them, and the fusion, not the borrowing alone, generates the formation's characteristic tensions.

Each part carries a distinct burden. Unevenness supplies the gradient, the slope down which techniques and pressures flow. Interaction supplies the channels: war, trade, diplomacy, investment, missionary and military missions, the simple visibility of a richer neighbour's power. Necessity supplies the motive force, which Trotsky called the whip of external necessity, the compulsion felt by any ruling group that watches a better armed rival across a border. Combination supplies the outcome and the analytical payoff, the fused formation whose classes and crises cannot be read off either the advanced model or the backward inheritance alone. Writers who quote the phrase while retaining only the first part have kept the scenery and discarded the engine. Writers who retain all four parts hold a proposition that can be tested against cases, argued with, and refined, which is what a concept is for.

A further precision concerns scope, and it matters for everything argued later in these pages. Trotsky stated the idea as a law of historical development, but he stated it from a case: Russia, whose peculiarities his first chapter set out to explain. The original claim was therefore double. In its narrow form it explained why the first successful workers' revolution occurred in one of Europe's most backward great powers rather than in its most advanced industrial states, the standing puzzle of Marxist expectation. In its wider form it proposed that the Russian path, so far from being an exotic exception, displayed in concentrated shape what lateness does everywhere, so that the histories of the second, third or tenth rank of cultural development would show combinations of their own. Later borrowers, above all in international relations theory, stretched the wider form further still, into a claim about the existence of multiple societies as such, in every epoch. Whether that stretching preserves the concept or dissolves it is a genuine question, graded carefully in a later section. Precision begins by keeping the three scopes separate: a law stated for a case, a generalisation about late development, and a transhistorical thesis about social multiplicity. Much confused writing on the subject consists of sliding among the three without noticing.

### Why is unevenness by itself too weak to explain anything?

Unevenness by itself predicts nothing, because every known record shows societies at differing levels without any particular consequence following. Rates diverge in tranquil centuries and violent ones alike. What requires explanation is what divergence does to the societies touching each other, and that is a question about interaction and fusion, which the bare fact of difference cannot answer.

The point deserves dwelling on, since the misreading it corrects is the commonest in the literature and the one this article exists to repair. Textbooks, encyclopedia entries and a good deal of political commentary use the full phrase as an honorific label for the observation that the world is unequal. The observation is true, and Trotsky himself treated it as close to self evident; he called unevenness the most general law of the historic process in a tone suggesting that only a very sheltered theorist could miss it. But a law that merely registers difference has no cutting edge. It does not tell us why Russia industrialised in vast foreign financed plants rather than in small workshops, why its working class formed in enormous concentrated batches, why its bourgeoisie arrived too weak and too entangled with the state to lead a revolution of the classic kind, or why tasks belonging to different historical epochs landed on the same generation's desk at once. Those are the phenomena the concept was built to explain, and every one of them belongs to the combined half. Difference is the premise. Combination is the argument.

There is a disciplinary moral here as well. Concepts survive by doing work that neighbouring terms cannot do, and a concept reduced to its premise does no work at all: world inequality, core and periphery, centre and margin, development gaps, all the vocabulary of unevenness was available from other traditions, several of them older. What no neighbouring term supplied was the claim about fusion inside one formation, the insistence that the borrowed and the inherited interlock as a single structure with its own logic and its own breaking points. That is why the grading of borrowings later in this article applies a simple test. A use of the concept that draws consequences from fusion, from the coexistence and friction of forms belonging to different epochs within one society, is using the concept. A use that gestures at global inequality and moves on is borrowing a famous phrase to decorate an argument that would stand unchanged without it.

## Unevenness: the premise everyone already accepts

Since the combined half carries the weight, the uneven half can be treated briskly, but it should be treated accurately. Human societies have never developed in lockstep. Agriculture appeared in a handful of river valleys and spread unevenly for millennia. Iron, coined money, printing, gunpowder weapons, the steam engine and the joint stock company each arose somewhere particular and diffused at speeds set by geography, war, trade and resistance. Marx and Engels wrote inside that awareness from the beginning: the Communist Manifesto describes the bourgeoisie battering down national walls and drawing even the most barbarian nations into civilisation, a picture of forced and unequal incorporation rather than parallel progress. Lenin, writing in 1915 on the slogan of a United States of Europe, compressed the point into the remark that uneven economic and political development is, in his phrase, "an absolute law of capitalism". Nothing in that sentence was controversial then and nothing is now. Statesmen, geographers and merchants had always known that some lands were rich, commercial and armed while others were poor, agrarian and exposed.

Two refinements turn the truism into a usable premise. The first is that unevenness is not a fixed league table. Positions change, and they change partly through the very contacts that unevenness creates: England's early industrial lead was overtaken by Germany and the United States, whose later starts Trotsky himself cited as evidence that backwardness in capitalist development could become an advantage when the latecomer equipped itself with the newest plant instead of a museum of superseded machinery. The second refinement is that unevenness within the modern world differs from the unevenness of earlier ages in one decisive respect: density of contact. Premodern societies could remain genuinely separate for centuries, developing along paths that touched only at raid and caravan. The capitalist world market, colonial empires and the modern state system abolished that separateness. From the nineteenth century onward, no society of any size developed outside the pressure field of the others, which is why a concept about interaction and fusion became necessary for this epoch in a way it had not been for, say, the Bronze Age. Rosenberg's later generalisation deliberately pushes the concept back across all epochs, and the strain that manoeuvre creates is examined in its place. For the original argument, the epoch matters: unevenness became explosive when it became inescapable.

It is worth pausing on the word law, because the original texts use it freely and later readers divide sharply over it. Trotsky called unevenness a law and combined development a law, and he meant something definite: a tendency so deeply rooted in the material conditions of the epoch that individual cases vary around it rather than escaping it. He did not mean an iron sequence of stages through which every society must pass in identical order; the entire point of the combined half is that no such identical sequence exists. His laws describe the shape of variation, not a timetable. A law of that kind can still be argued with, and the section on the strongest objection gives the argument its full force, but critics who attack the concept as a rigid stage theory have usually aimed at the position it was built to destroy. The stage theory belonged to the orthodoxies Trotsky was fighting, within Marxism as much as outside it, and the historical materialism section below sets out that quarrel in its own terms.


## Combination: what the second word actually claims

Combined development begins where a backward society, unable to afford the pioneers' slow chronology, imports finished results. The imports are impressively concrete. A state that has never seen a joint stock company charters a central bank on a foreign model. A countryside of wooden ploughs receives a railway built with foreign rails, foreign locomotives, foreign engineers and foreign loans. Conscription on the Prussian pattern arms peasants whose grandfathers owed labour dues to a lord. Universities teach engineering in languages whose technical vocabularies have to be invented as the lectures proceed. None of this is imitation in the schoolroom sense, because the imported form does not enter an empty space. It enters a going concern: a fiscal system, a land regime, a priesthood, a set of village customs, a court. The imported form and the inherited structure then have to live together, and their cohabitation is rarely peaceful. The railway needs wage labour in a district where labour is bound by communal obligation. The central bank needs a tax base that a subsistence peasantry cannot supply. The modern army needs literate NCOs from a population the state has never schooled. Combination names that forced cohabitation and its consequences.

### What does combined add to uneven development?

Unevenness means societies develop at different rates, which is uncontroversial. Combined means latecomers adopt the most advanced techniques and forms while retaining older social structures, producing an amalgam rather than an earlier stage of the advanced societies. That amalgam generates its own distinctive instabilities.

Three features distinguish a combination from the mere presence of old and new side by side, which any traveller can observe in any old city on earth. The first is functional interdependence. In a combination, the archaic element is not a museum piece surviving at the margins; it does work inside the modern sector. The Russian peasant commune, for instance, was no picturesque residue: it underwrote industrialisation by reproducing labour power cheaply, absorbing discharged workers in downturns, and anchoring the fiscal demands of the state, while the factories it fed were among the largest and most technically advanced in Europe. Remove the commune and the factory system of 1900 becomes unintelligible; remove the factories and the commune's burdens lose their explanation. The second feature is compression. Stages that the pioneers spread across centuries, because each had to wait for its predecessor to mature, arrive in the latecomer within a single lifetime and often within a single reign, so that living people embody different epochs simultaneously: the grandfather a serf in his youth, the father a communal peasant, the son a lathe operator in a plant owned in Paris. The third feature is political simultaneity. Because the stages arrive compressed, the tasks historically attached to them also arrive together and compete for the same political moment: land redistribution, national independence, constitutional government, the organisation of labour, the emancipation of women, the secularisation of education. A politics built for one task at a time confronts a queue of tasks all demanding attention at once, and that crowding, Trotsky argued, is what made the politics of backward countries so volatile and so unlike the sequential politics of the pioneers.

### How can the most advanced and the most archaic forms occupy one society?

They occupy it because neither was chosen freely. The advanced form arrives under compulsion, selected by foreign pressure for survival rather than by internal ripening. The archaic form survives because the resources that might have dissolved it were spent meeting that same pressure. Each is the other's condition: borrowing preserves the inheritance, and the inheritance shapes the borrowing.

Understanding compression clarifies why analogies with the pioneers mislead so persistently. England's industrial revolution grew its factory system out of handicraft production through generations of workshop enlargement, putting out networks and incremental mechanisation, and its working class formed gradually inside that lengthening process, acquiring organisations, chapels, friendly societies and eventually unions stage by stage. A combined formation has no such sedimentation. Its industry appears at the frontier of scale from the start, because the borrower copies the frontier, not the pioneer's infancy: nobody building a steel industry in 1890 reproduced the puddling furnaces of 1790 when Bessemer converters were on the market. Its working class therefore appears suddenly, concentrated in huge plants, recruited directly from villages, carrying peasant habits into factory barracks, and confronting employers and a state it has had no generations to learn. Its bourgeoisie appears deformed in the opposite direction, hatched by state contracts and foreign credit rather than by slow accumulation in trade and manufacture, and consequently timid, dependent and hostile to the political risks that the classic bourgeois revolutions required. Every class in the formation is stamped by the combination, which is why class analysis calibrated on the pioneer cases kept failing in the backward ones, and why a special concept was needed at all.

A terminological caution belongs here, because a persistent misreading feeds on vocabulary. Combined development does not mean a mixed economy. A mixed economy is a policy arrangement, public and private ownership combined by governmental choice within one mode of production, and the phrase belongs to postwar debates about nationalisation and planning. Trotsky's combination is not chosen, not confined to ownership, and not an arrangement within a single mode: it is the unplanned interlocking of forms from different modes and epochs, serf agriculture with finance capital, tribal or communal landholding with export mining, absolutist bureaucracy with parliamentary facades. A second misreading treats combination as a synonym for hybridity in the cultural sense, a flavour of mixture. The concept is harder than that. It specifies which elements combine, by what pressure, with what functional roles, and it predicts friction at the joints. Where an account cannot name the pressure, the borrowed form, the retained form and the friction, it is using the word as atmosphere rather than analysis, a distinction the grading section applies to the concept's academic borrowers without favour.

The most elegant expression of the idea remains its author's own illustration. Peoples encountering firearms, Trotsky observed, discard bows and arrows for rifles all at once rather than travelling the road between the two weapons across centuries of intermediate technology. The illustration repays slow reading. It contains the whole mechanism in miniature: an external object embodying generations of foreign development; adoption at the frontier, not at the historical starting point; the skipping of intermediate stages as something done not from cleverness but because the object exists ready made and the need is urgent; and, quietly, the consequence, since a society armed in an afternoon has acquired the endpoint of a weapons tradition without the social organisation that tradition built around itself among its originators, the gunsmiths' guilds, the powder mills, the drill regulations, the fiscal machinery for arsenals. The rifle is the least of the borrowing. Everything the rifle presupposes must then be improvised, borrowed in turn, or done without, and it is in that improvisation that combinations are truly made.

## The whip of external necessity

Mechanisms need motive power, and the concept locates it outside the borrowing society. Left to its internal resources, a backward society changes slowly; Trotsky's own description of old Russia stresses slowness, the vast plain, sparse population, extensive agriculture that exhausted soil rather than improving it, a state that grew by consuming surplus rather than by fostering production. What breaks the slowness is the proximity of societies that have already broken it. Their existence confronts every backward ruling group with a standing threat that is military before it is anything else. A neighbour with rifled artillery, railways for mobilisation and a treasury able to fund long wars will, in due course, use those advantages, and the backward state that wishes to survive must acquire equivalents or perish. Peter the Great's shipyards and foundries, the Ottoman military reforms after repeated defeats, the Meiji oligarchs' frantic programme after the arrival of American warships, the Qing dynasty's self strengthening arsenals: the pattern recurs across continents because the pressure is structural rather than cultural. Trotsky named that pressure the whip of external necessity, and the metaphor is exact. A whip does not persuade; it compels motion in a direction not chosen by the one who moves, at a pace set by fear rather than readiness.

### What compels a backward country to borrow rather than grow slowly?

Survival compels it. A state ringed by rivals with superior weapons, revenues and transport faces defeat, partition or subordination if it waits for gradual internal growth. Borrowing compresses into years what organic development spreads across generations, so rulers borrow even when the imports strain every institution they rule through, because the alternative is losing the state itself.

Military competition explains the first wave of borrowing, but the whip cracks through more hands than the war ministry's. Trade exerts it continuously: once the world market reaches a backward economy, its handicrafts compete against factory goods produced at a fraction of the cost, and whole artisan sectors can be ruined in a decade without a shot fired, as Indian handloom weaving was ruined by Lancashire cloth. Finance exerts it through debt, since a state that borrows abroad to fund armies and railways accepts foreign supervision of its revenues, most starkly in the Ottoman Public Debt Administration, a foreign staffed body that collected earmarked Ottoman taxes directly for bondholders. Diplomacy exerts it through prestige and hierarchy, the grading of states into civilised and backward that governed recognition, treaty terms and colonial vulnerability in the nineteenth century, and that reforming elites from Istanbul to Tokyo studied anxiously. Ideas exert it through the demonstration effect of visibly richer and stronger societies, which delegitimises inherited arrangements in the eyes of the backward country's own educated youth. Necessity, in short, is not a single emergency but a permanent environment once contact is dense, and combined development is what permanent external pressure does to a society's internal architecture over time.

Recognising the whip also disciplines the concept against a voluntarist misreading. Combination is not a strategy that wise rulers select from a menu of development paths, and backwardness is not a clever shortcut devised by far sighted modernisers, however energetic particular reformers were. The reformers themselves, from Peter to the Meiji oligarchs to Witte, described their programmes as forced upon them, and their opponents at court accused them of betraying inherited ways precisely because the imports were experienced as alien impositions rather than native growth. Nor is the whip a guarantee of success. It compels borrowing; it does not compel successful combination. The borrowed forms may fail to fuse, producing enclaves that serve foreign markets while the interior stagnates, or the fiscal burden of imitation may break the state attempting it, or the archaic structure may capture the imported form and turn it to old purposes, as when modern weapons strengthened rather than dissolved a slaveholding or caste order. The concept claims that pressure produces combination as the characteristic outcome; it does not claim that every combination coheres, survives or progresses. That modesty matters when the strongest objection is weighed below.

One more feature of the whip deserves emphasis, because it explains the direction borrowing usually takes. Necessity is selective. A threatened state borrows first what preserves states: weapons, arsenals, military schools, railways with strategic value, the fiscal machinery to pay for them, and only later and reluctantly the civil and political forms associated with the pioneers. The result is a characteristic distortion visible across cases, a modernity of coercion ahead of a modernity of welfare or rights, armies more modern than the villages that feed them, police techniques ahead of plumbing. That distortion is itself a combination, and often the most politically charged one, since a state equipped with the newest instruments of repression while ruling through the oldest relations of deference has armed one epoch against another inside its own borders. Observers who ask why backward countries so often modernised their means of control faster than their means of life are asking a question the concept answers directly.


## The privilege of historic backwardness, stated precisely

If the whip explains why borrowing happens, the privilege of historic backwardness names the peculiar freedom that borrowing confers. Trotsky's formulation is deliberately paradoxical in dress and strictly mechanical in content. A backward country, he argued, is not obliged to repeat the history of the advanced ones, because the results of that history lie ready to hand. It may adopt what exists in advance of any given date, skipping a whole series of intermediate stages, and in skipping them it sometimes arrives at combinations the pioneers never knew, including, occasionally, forms more advanced in one dimension than anything in the countries it copies. The privilege is real, and its conditions are exact. It is a privilege of sequence, not of welfare. It permits the latecomer to choose the order of its acquisitions, to take the endpoint of a foreign development without the centuries of preliminary fumbling, and to amalgamate what it takes with what it already has. It does not exempt the latecomer from payment. The bill arrives in other currencies: foreign debt, cultural dislocation, political forms without their social foundations, industries without the surrounding ecology of skills and suppliers that the pioneers grew organically.

### Does the privilege of backwardness mean backwardness is a blessing?

No, and the confusion between a privilege of sequence and an advantage in life is the misreading Trotsky's phrasing invites. Skipping stages means adopting finished forms without the slow preparation that made them workable elsewhere. The costs reappear as debt, dislocation and institutions imposed faster than society can digest them. Backwardness remains backwardness; only its chronology is negotiable.

The mechanism behind the privilege can be stated in terms of depreciation and obsolescence, which is why it operates most visibly in industry. The pioneer's capital is embodied in plant built across many decades, much of it technically superseded yet still earning returns that its owners resist writing off. The latecomer, unencumbered, equips itself at the frontier. Trotsky noted that Germany and the United States overtook England partly for this reason: their later capitalist development let them build at the newest scale with the newest methods, while English industry carried the accumulated weight of its own priority. The point generalises beyond economics into institutions. A state founding a banking system in 1870 could copy the most advanced statutes in Europe rather than evolving through the pioneers' centuries of usury law, goldsmith banking and crisis. A university founded in a backward capital could teach the newest science without dislodging faculties committed to the old. Wherever development leaves behind a sediment of vested technique and vested interest, the latecomer's emptiness is, in that narrow sense, an asset.

But the same mechanism, followed honestly, shows why the privilege is poisoned at its root. Forms borrowed at the frontier arrive without the complementary forms that grew up around them at home. The newest factory presupposes maintenance cultures, technical schools, commercial law, a labour force habituated to industrial discipline, banks able to judge industrial risk. Import those complements and the borrowing deepens until it reorganises the whole society, which is what the whip demands and what conservative defenders of the old order resist. Refuse them, and the imported form runs degraded, adapted downward to the surrounding backwardness: Trotsky himself remarked that the backward nation not infrequently debases the achievements it borrows in adapting them, a sentence that deserves equal billing with the privilege itself. The history of late development is written in that oscillation, between adopting the form intact and mutilating it to fit, and no case escapes the oscillation. Combination is the name for the stable pattern the oscillation produces: not the form as designed, not the old order untouched, but a working hybrid whose properties neither side intended.

The privilege also has a strictly political dimension that proved decisive in the concept's home case. Because stages can be skipped economically, they can be skipped politically as well, or rather, the political tasks of skipped stages do not disappear but are inherited by whoever holds power when the compression peaks. A country that has not completed its agrarian revolution, its national unification, its constitutional settlement or its secularisation may find all four unfinished when an industrial working class appears and organises. The party or class that acts then confronts a bundle of tasks belonging to different epochs, and must either attempt them in fusion or be overwhelmed by them in sequence. That inheritance is the hinge between combined development and permanent revolution, examined in its own section below. Here it is enough to mark the dependency: permanent revolution is combined development translated into a political prognosis. Where the translation is resisted, the sociological claim can and should still be assessed on sociological evidence, a separation this article maintains throughout, in line with the discipline of keeping theory and political programme in distinct accounts.

A final clarification forestalls an error that examination candidates commit with metronomic regularity. The privilege is not the claim that backward countries develop faster, full stop, nor that they are destined to overtake the pioneers. Some do and many do not. The claim concerns the shape of their development when it occurs: derivative in content, original in combination, discontinuous in sequence. Growth rates belong to economic history's other debates. Shape is the concept's territory, and on shape it makes risky, checkable assertions: that late industry will be more concentrated than pioneer industry at equivalent output, that the state will play a larger entrepreneurial role, that foreign capital will penetrate earlier and deeper, that classes will form out of the pioneer order, weakest where the pioneers were strongest and strongest where the pioneers were embryonic. A theory that predicts the shape of a society rather than its growth rate is unfashionable in some quarters and indispensable in others, since it is shape, not speed, that determines political possibility.

## Russia: the case the concept was built from

Every generalisation in the preceding sections was reverse engineered from a single national history, and intellectual honesty requires returning to that history in detail. Trotsky composed the opening chapter of his History of the Russian Revolution in exile, publishing the work in 1930 with an English translation following in 1932, and the chapter, titled "Peculiarities of Russia's Development", performs an unusual double duty. It is a compressed history of Russia from the medieval period to the revolution, and simultaneously the statement of a general law, offered with the Russian material as its demonstration. Readers who want the author's life, exile and polemics around the text should consult the companion account of [leon trotsky intellectual biography](/2014/09/01/leon-trotsky-intellectual-biography/), which treats the man at length; here the chapter matters as an argument, and its argument begins with slowness. Russia, on Trotsky's telling, developed at a crawl: a vast, cold, thinly peopled plain, open to invasion from the steppe, poor in the urban networks and Roman inheritances that accelerated the West, its agriculture extensive and wasteful, its towns few, its middle strata stunted. Backwardness was not an episode in Russian history but its baseline condition across centuries.

Onto that baseline the whip fell early and repeatedly. The Muscovite state itself, Trotsky argued, was shaped by military pressure from both steppe and West, growing into an absolutism that devoured an extraordinary share of the social surplus to sustain armies and administration, and in devouring it, retarded the very classes whose growth might have balanced it. Peter the Great's reforms at the start of the eighteenth century form the classic early episode of forced borrowing: shipyards, foundries, a navy, a table of ranks, Western dress and Western technicians, all imposed on a serf economy by a state fighting Sweden for survival. Nothing was allowed to ripen; everything was commanded. Serfdom, far from dissolving under the impact, was intensified and extended, because the state needed a bound peasantry to feed and man its imported modernity. There, two centuries before the factories, the signature combination already appears: the newest European military and industrial technique fused with, and financed by, an archaic system of compulsory labour, each element strengthening the other in a configuration no Western country reproduced.

The nineteenth century repeated the pattern at higher voltage. Defeat in the Crimean War, administered by industrial powers with rifled weapons and steam logistics, exposed the serf economy as a military liability, and the emancipation of 1861 followed as a reform from above designed to free the state's hands. Yet emancipation was itself combined in form: the peasants received land through the commune, burdened with redemption payments, tied to the village by collective responsibility, so that the old institution was preserved and repurposed as an instrument of fiscal extraction at the very moment of formal liberation. Railways spread with state guarantees and foreign capital. The finance minister Sergei Witte in the 1890s then drove the most deliberate episode of forced industrialisation of the century: protective tariffs, the gold standard adopted in 1897 to reassure foreign lenders, a railway programme crowned by the Trans Siberian line, and an open invitation to French and Belgian capital to build the heavy industry of the south. Industrial growth rates in that decade were among the highest in the world. They were achieved not by a rising bourgeoisie investing its accumulated profits but by the fiscal squeeze of an autocratic state on its peasantry and by loans negotiated in Paris, which is to say by the archaic and the foreign jointly, the combination in its purest documented form.

### Why did giant factories rise inside an empire of peasant communes?

Because foreign capital and the autocratic state, the two builders of Russian industry, had no interest in gradualism. Investors imported the largest proven plant designs to minimise unit costs in a protected market. The state wanted output quickly for strategic reasons. Meanwhile the commune lowered the cost of reproducing labour, letting wages stay below family subsistence without starving the workforce.

The statistics of concentration, cited by historians across the interpretive spectrum, retain their power to startle. On the eve of the First World War, the proportion of Russian industrial workers employed in enterprises with more than a thousand hands was higher than in Germany or the United States, the very homelands of large scale industry. The Putilov works in St Petersburg, a sprawling complex of steel, armaments and locomotive production underwritten by French finance and state orders, employed tens of thousands and stood among the largest factories in Europe. The metallurgical south, around Yuzovka, a settlement founded by the Welsh industrialist John Hughes, ran on British capital and Russian and Ukrainian labour in a landscape of peasant villages. Baku oil, Nobel and Rothschild money, derricks beside nomadic grazing routes. Against these islands stood the ocean: well over four fifths of the population rural at the century's turn, agriculture dominated by the commune and the three field system, literacy a minority attainment, and a countryside that periodically starved. Industry employed a small fraction of the population, around three million factory workers in a realm of some one hundred and seventy million people, yet that fraction was concentrated, strategically placed in the capitals and a handful of industrial districts, and organised by the very modernity of its plants into a collective actor of a weight utterly disproportionate to its numbers.


## The Russian combination examined joint by joint

Abstract combination becomes persuasive only when the joints are shown, the points where forms from different epochs meet and transmit force. Russian industry displays the first joint with unusual clarity. Its commanding heights were built by foreign capital, which by 1914 held dominant positions in southern metallurgy, mining and oil, and its growth was financed substantially by state borrowing abroad, above all in France, where Russian bonds were sold to hundreds of thousands of small savers and underwritten by an alliance that made Russian industrialisation a department of French strategy. The joint matters because it reverses the pioneer sequence. In England, industry generated the finance; in Russia, finance, much of it foreign and political, generated the industry, so the commanding economic institution of the new order was indebted abroad and mortgaged to diplomacy from birth. A bourgeoisie produced by such a process could not resemble the independent mill owners of Manchester. Russian manufacturers depended on state orders, state tariffs and state credit, clustered in associations that petitioned the ministry rather than confronting it, and feared their own workers more than they resented the autocracy, whose police they needed. When crisis came, this class did not lead; it hid, and its hiding is a datum that stage theories of revolution could not digest and combination explains without strain.

The second joint runs between factory and village. The Russian worker of 1900 was typically a peasant in transit, holding an allotment in a commune, sending wages home, returning for harvest, counted in two worlds and secure in neither. Employers valued the arrangement because the allotment subsidised the wage; the state valued it because the commune guaranteed taxes and redemption dues; the worker suffered it because neither wage nor allotment alone could feed a family. The consequence for labour politics was paradoxical and fertile. A workforce with shallow roots in industry might be expected to organise weakly, and in craft terms it did. But concentration corrected what shallowness weakened: tens of thousands gathered in single enterprises and districts, facing the same employer, the same police and the same grievances, with the village connection supplying an additional radicalism, the land hunger that industrial disputes alone would never have generated. Strike waves fused economic and agrarian discontents that in the pioneer countries belonged to different centuries of protest. The soviet, when it appeared in 1905 and again in 1917, was itself a product of combined development in Trotsky's explicit judgement, an organisational form invented by a young working class unencumbered by the parliamentary traditions and craft narrowness of older movements, the political expression of skipping: improvisation at the frontier because no inherited institution stood in the way.

The third joint is the state itself. Russian absolutism was no mere survival, a feudal relic awaiting replacement by its bourgeois successor. It was the active organiser of the combination, the broker between foreign capital and domestic backwardness, taxing the commune to pay the bondholder, building strategic railways with French money, staffing modern ministries beside a court of Byzantine ritual. Its modernity and its archaism were the same fact viewed from two sides, since it modernised precisely in order to preserve an order that modernisation corroded. Trotsky's narrative dwells on the resulting brittleness: a state strong enough to industrialise a continent yet unable to satisfy any class it had called into being, not the peasants it squeezed, not the workers it concentrated, not the bourgeoisie it kept in tutelage, not even the landowners whose world industrialisation dissolved. When war in 1914 subjected the whole structure to the supreme test of the whip, the combination failed at every joint at once, which is why the collapse of 1917 was total rather than partial, monarchy, then liberal ministry, then the very framework of legality dissolving in eight months. The revolution's depth, the riddle the concept was built to solve, reads in this account as the depth of the combination itself: because tasks of several epochs had been fused into one formation, their settlement could not be staggered across generations, and a crisis of the whole arrived instead of a crisis of a part.

### What separates a combination from a simple mixture of old and new?

A mixture is a list; a combination is a structure. In a mixture, old and new coexist without depending on each other, and each can be removed without changing the other's behaviour. In a combination, the archaic element performs functions the advanced element requires, the advanced element transforms the archaic one's meaning, and the two fail or hold together.

That distinction, between mixture and combination, is the article's most portable analytical instrument, and it will be used to grade the concept's later borrowers. Apply it as four questions of any candidate case. Does the retained archaic form do necessary work for the advanced form, supplying labour, revenue, legitimacy or coercion that the advanced sector cannot supply itself? Does the advanced form, in return, alter what the archaic form is, converting commune into fiscal instrument, tribe into labour reserve, church into welfare ministry, caste into electoral machine? Do the two share a fate in crisis, so that shocks propagate across the joint instead of being contained within one sector? And is the resulting whole irreducible, such that describing the society as an early version of some pioneer, or as a traditional society with modern additions, misses the specific tensions generated at the seam? Four affirmative answers indicate combination. Negatives indicate the travel brochure version of mixture, old town beside new town, which no theory is needed to describe. Russia in 1900 answers yes at every point, which is why it remains the paradigm against which looser usages are measured, and why the cases section below tests Japan, Germany, the Ottoman lands and others against the same four questions rather than against a vague resemblance.

## The combination table

The concept's findable artifact compresses its claim into a form that can be checked at a glance and argued with row by row. The combination table sets out, for each illustrative case, the advanced element adopted, the archaic element retained, the instability the combination produced, and the political consequence that followed. It spans several cases rather than Russia alone, because the concept's whole wager is that the pattern is general among late developers, and a table confined to the home case would concede the wager before it was tested. Readers should use the rows as hypotheses disciplined by the four questions of the previous section: each row asserts a functional interdependence, and each can be falsified by showing that the two elements merely coexisted. The table is also the article's answer to the charge of vagueness levelled at the concept by its critics. A vague idea cannot fill its own table with checkable entries.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Russia before the First World War | Foreign financed heavy industry at the largest European scale | The peasant commune and autocratic fiscal extraction | A concentrated working class beside an insolvent countryside and a dependent bourgeoisie | Tasks of agrarian, democratic and socialist revolution fused in a single crisis |
| Meiji Japan | Conscription, modern navies, joint stock companies and technical universities | Emperor ideology, landlord tenancy and the dissolved samurai order's surviving ethos | A modern military state resting on squeezed tenant agriculture | Rapid great power ascent shadowed by militarism and rural distress |

Reading the rows together teaches more than reading any one of them. Notice first that the adopted elements cluster in predictable domains: heavy industry, transport, finance, military organisation, law and script, the equipment of state power and production. Notice second that the retained elements cluster just as predictably in the domains the state could not afford to rebuild: land, labour reproduction, legitimacy and local order. The pattern is the whip's selectivity made visible. States modernised what invasion and markets forced them to modernise and conserved what extraction and obedience required them to conserve, and the seam between the two ran through every society in the table. Notice third that the instabilities are not generic backwardness but specific frictions at the seam: classes created by one element and injured by the other, sovereignties sold to fund the imports, armies trained to defend orders their training undermined. Political consequences then follow the frictions with a logic that invites, and survives, case by case contestation. Historians will dispute individual cells, the weight given to tenancy in Japan, the characterisation of caste in India, and such disputes are the table working as intended, since each cell states a claim precise enough to be wrong.

Two rows deserve a sentence of defence each, because they test the concept's range in opposite directions. Germany appears although it stood near the frontier by 1900, because Trotsky himself treated the German and American overtaking of England as the privilege operating among the advanced, and because the German political combination, frontier industry under a semi absolutist constitution staffed by a landed service class, generated the classic puzzle of a powerful socialist movement confronting a state it could neither capture nor ignore. India appears although its development was colonial rather than sovereign, because the whip there cracked in a foreign hand: the colonial state imported railways and law for extraction and control, conserved landlordism and caste as instruments of cheap rule, and thereby manufactured a combination whose seams the national movement later tore along. A concept that handles the sovereign latecomer and the colonised territory, the near frontier power and the dissolving empire, has earned its claim to generality among late developers. Whether it has earned generality beyond that company, across epochs innocent of capitalism, is the harder question reserved for the debate section.


## The sociological foundation of permanent revolution

Trotsky did not develop the concept as an ornament for historians; he developed it as the foundation under his oldest political idea. Permanent revolution, first sketched with Alexander Parvus in the aftermath of the 1905 revolution and restated in the exile writings, held that in a backward country the tasks of the bourgeois revolution, land, democracy, national sovereignty, would not be performed by the bourgeoisie, and that a revolution beginning with those tasks would pass over, under working class leadership, into socialist measures and dependence on international extension. For decades the doctrine could be read as a daring prognosis resting on intuition about Russian peculiarities. Combined development supplied the missing sociology beneath it. If the bourgeoisie of a combined formation is the creature of foreign capital and state patronage, it cannot lead an anti absolutist revolution for the structural reason that its property and credit depend on the absolutism and the foreigners. If the working class is concentrated, modern and fused with agrarian discontent, it can be pushed beyond the station that stage theory assigns it. If tasks of different epochs arrive simultaneously, then a revolution that solves the first task inherits the rest unsolved and is driven onward or backward by their pressure. Each clause of the political doctrine maps onto a clause of the sociological one. The full exposition of the doctrine itself, its texts, its controversies and its fate, belongs to the companion treatment of [permanent revolution explained](/2014/09/15/permanent-revolution-explained/), and readers should follow the argument there; this article's concern is the dependency, which runs one way. Permanent revolution needs combined development. Combined development does not need permanent revolution, and can be true as sociology in the hands of scholars who reject the doctrine entirely, a fact its academic afterlife proves abundantly.

### Which classes does a combined formation produce, and with what weights?

It produces the pioneer classes in scrambled proportions. The bourgeoisie emerges small, foreign dependent and state nursed, weaker than its economic footprint suggests. The working class emerges concentrated beyond its numbers, strategically placed and politically heavier than its share of population. The peasantry remains the vast majority yet cannot act as an independent national force.

That scrambling of class weights is the concept's most consequential single claim, because class weight is where sociology becomes politics. Orthodox expectation, shared by most Marxists of the Second International and by liberal modernisers alike, assigned political capacities by developmental rank: a numerous and confident bourgeoisie makes bourgeois revolution, a mature proletariat eventually makes socialist revolution, and a backward country must wait its turn in the queue of history. Combined development replies that the queue is an illusion created by studying pioneers. Capacities are set not by rank but by configuration. A proletariat of three million in an empire of one hundred and seventy million sounds negligible until its concentration in the capitals, its command of railways, armaments and communications, and its alliance potential with a hundred million peasants hungry for land are entered into the ledger, at which point its weight exceeds that of far larger working classes dispersed across societies where every other class has already settled its accounts. Conversely a bourgeoisie can be wealthy and weightless at once, if its wealth is a function of concessions and contracts revocable by the state it would need to confront. Configuration over rank: the formula travels well beyond Russia, and it is the element of the concept that later sociologists of revolution, including several with no Marxist allegiance, have quietly absorbed into their own accounts of why revolutions cluster in states that are neither the most developed nor the least.

The peasantry's position in the configuration deserves its own paragraph, since agrarian majorities dominate every classic case. In a combined formation the peasantry is not a survival awaiting dissolution; it is the formation's foundation and its fuel, taxed to fund industry, conscripted to man armies, supplying the migrants who staff the factories and the grievances that ignite the countryside. Yet its very structure, dispersed across villages, bound by communal and familial horizons, disables it from independent national action of the kind the concentrated classes can undertake. It can riot, desert, seize land and destroy a regime's rural base; it cannot draft a programme for the state it helps to topple. Trotsky drew from this the conclusion that peasant war requires urban leadership to become revolution, a conclusion other traditions contest, pointing to peasant movements that generated their own political instruments. The contest matters and is honestly noted here: where a peasantry has built durable organisations of its own, the configuration claim must be revised for that case rather than defended by formula. A concept that specifies the conditions of its own revision is doing scientific work; one that cannot is doing liturgy, and the evidence section below returns to that standard.

## Stage theory complicated: the quarrel with the staircase

To feel the concept's polemical edge, a reader must see the staircase it was built against. The Marxism of the Second International, in its dominant textbook form, taught history as a succession of modes of production through which societies climb in order: slavery, feudalism, capitalism, socialism, each preparing the next, none skippable, the sequence driven by the growth of productive forces and guaranteed, in the vulgar versions, by something close to historical providence. Applied to politics, the staircase commanded patience. Backward countries stood on lower steps; their socialists must support the bourgeoisie in completing the bourgeois step before entertaining higher ambitions; premature attempts to leap would be punished by history itself. The doctrine had a certain textual warrant in the famous 1859 preface to A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy, where Marx sketched the succession of Asiatic, ancient, feudal and modern bourgeois modes as epochs of progress, though Marx's own historical writings, on France, India, Ireland and the Russian commune, repeatedly violated the staircase in practice, treating formations as entangled, externally driven and capable of divergent paths. The full architecture of that orthodoxy and its tensions is laid out in the series' treatment of [historical materialism complete guide](/2010/01/15/historical-materialism-complete-guide/), which readers may consult for the framework as a whole. Combined development intervenes at exactly the sore point: it keeps the modes, keeps material determination, keeps class analysis, and denies the staircase. Societies do not climb in single file. They are dragged, raided, financed and armed into configurations that no staircase diagram can represent, and the configurations, not the steps, explain their politics.

The denial should not be overstated into its opposite error. Trotsky did not argue that anything can follow anything, or that productive forces impose no constraints. A society cannot leap into socialism from the Bronze Age, and his texts are explicit that the possibility of skipping is by no means absolute, being bounded by a country's economic and cultural capacities. What he denied is the universal obligation to repeat: the claim that each society must internally generate each stage in full before the next becomes possible. Where the results of a stage exist abroad in transferable form, the stage can be appropriated in compressed, partial and fused shapes, and the society that results is not the stage itself but a combination containing some of its elements in alien company. Philosophically, the move replaces a philosophy of history modelled on embryology, each nation an organism maturing through fixed phases, with one modelled on ecology and conflict, each society a field of forces in contact with stronger and weaker neighbours whose pressures bend its growth into shapes no internal logic predicts. That replacement, more than any single empirical claim, is what later historical sociology found reusable, since an ecological picture accommodates the plain fact that no society in the record has ever developed alone.

There is also a quieter implication for how the modes themselves are conceived. If stages fuse, then the units of analysis blur: a single society can host relations of production belonging, by the textbook taxonomy, to several modes at once, and can do so not as a transitional embarrassment but as its durable normal state. Trotsky's Russia had displayed that plurality for centuries, serfdom beside merchant capital, communal agriculture beside finance capital, and the plurality was the structure rather than a mess on its way to tidiness. Theorists who later formalised this insight spoke of the articulation of modes of production, a literature with its own debates about whether articulated modes retain separate logics or dissolve into the combined whole. The foundational exposition of the unit concept is given in [mode of production explained](/2010/02/15/mode-of-production-explained/), and the present article takes no side in the articulation literature beyond marking the kinship: combined development is the historical and dynamic cousin of a structural idea, supplying in narrative form what articulation theory attempted in taxonomy. Both insist that the real unit of social analysis is the concrete formation, messy, dated and placed, and both treat the pure mode as an abstraction that exists nowhere unmixed, least of all where development came late and under fire.

## The international inside the national

Every element so far has pressed toward a single reorientation: the causes of a society's shape are not contained within its borders. That reorientation is the concept's deepest bequest and the bridge to its academic afterlife. Classical social theory, Marxist and liberal alike, was built predominantly as a set of national stories: England industrialises, France revolutionises, Germany unifies, each narrative internalist in structure even when it mentions trade or war. Combined development makes the international constitutive rather than incidental. The Russian factory is unintelligible without French finance and Welsh engineers; the Ottoman reform is unintelligible without Russian armies and British bondholders; the Meiji state is unintelligible without the American squadron and the spectacle of China's humiliation. In each case the external is not an influence upon an otherwise self propelled development but a co author of the development's form, present in the layout of its industries, the composition of its classes and the calendar of its crises. The series' treatment of [marxism and imperialism guide](/2017/02/01/marxism-and-imperialism-guide/) covers the theories that explained the outward drive of the advanced powers themselves; combined development complements those theories from the receiving end, describing what the pressure did to the societies that absorbed it, and the two literatures lock together as the export and import sides of one process.

Seeing the international as constitutive also corrects a habit of thought that the concept's borrowers in international relations made their explicit target: the habit of treating the existence of many societies as background scenery for theory rather than as a causal fact requiring explanation. Political science had theories of what happens inside states and theories of what happens between them, and the two storeys connected only by staircases labelled foreign influence. If, however, the inside of each state is partly made by the between, then the separation fails at the foundation, and a theory of the international must be a theory of how multiple, unevenly developed societies produce each other's internal forms through their interaction. That is the proposition Justin Rosenberg extracted from Trotsky and offered to his discipline as its missing ground floor. Whether the offer succeeds is debated in the afterlife sections below. What can be said without controversy is that the proposition could not have come from the premise of unevenness alone. It comes from combination, from the claim that interaction fuses, transforms and constitutes, and it therefore stands as the clearest demonstration of this article's governing claim: the combination is the concept, and the unevenness is the doorway through which readers enter on their way to it.

A corollary follows for the study of any single country, and it disciplines the case discussions scattered through this article. No national history written under this concept's influence can be a sealed biography. The historian of the latecomer must work in at least two archives of causation, the domestic inheritance and the external field, and must expect the decisive facts to sit at their intersection: the loan negotiated abroad that fixed the factory's location at home, the war scare that set the reform's timetable, the imported statute that reorganised the village. Conversely, the historian of an advanced power who ignores what its finance, arms and markets were manufacturing abroad misses half the consequences of its own development. Combined development, taken seriously, is thus also a research programme, an instruction about where causes live. It tells the student of any late developing society to ask first what the society was forced to borrow, from whom, under what threat, and what the borrowing fused with, before asking the questions internalist narratives ask first. Programmes are judged by the work they organise, and by that standard this one has organised a great deal, from monographs on Ottoman finance to the syllabuses of international relations degrees where Trotsky's name would once have drawn a blank.


## The rival accounts of backwardness, at full strength

A concept earns authority by surviving comparison with the strongest alternatives, and backwardness has attracted formidable rivals. Fairness requires stating each at its best before registering where combined development answers differently, since a victory over caricatures is worth nothing. Three rivals dominate the territory: modernisation theory, which reads late development as accelerated travel along a universal road; dependency theory, which reads it as subordination inside a single world economy; and the account associated with Alexander Gerschenkron, which agrees with much of the combined picture while relocating its engine. Each captures something real. The question is what each cannot see.

Modernisation theory, in the confident form given by Walt Rostow's stages of growth and the sociological literature around Daniel Lerner and others in the decades after the Second World War, held that development is a passage from tradition to modernity through identifiable phases, that the passage is driven by diffusion of capital, technology and attitudes from the advanced to the backward, and that its destination, mass consumption, plural politics, urban literate society, is broadly common. Stated at full strength, the theory had evidence and generosity on its side. Diffusion is real; technologies do spread; literacy, urbanisation and demographic transition did recur across continents in recognisable patterns; and the theory at least promised the backward a future rather than a fate, insisting that poverty was a phase rather than an essence. Its policy offspring, aid programmes, technical assistance and institution building, treated underdevelopment as a gap to be closed by transferring the missing ingredients. Against combined development the theory makes a clean target because the two disagree on the one point that matters: what transfer does to the receiver. For modernisation theory, transferred ingredients work in the receiver much as they worked at home, so development is repetition at higher speed. For combined development, transferred ingredients are transformed by the soil, fused with retained structures, and productive of configurations with no counterpart at either end of the transfer. The empirical record of the twentieth century, enclave economies, authoritarian modernisers, revolutions in the least developed rather than the most, fits the second expectation better than the first, which is why modernisation theory survives in policy rhetoric while combined development survives in scholarship. Yet the verdict should be precise rather than gleeful: diffusion, the modernisation school's central fact, is also combined development's mechanism of borrowing, and the older theory deserves credit for documenting the flows whose consequences it misread.

Dependency theory arose from the Latin American experience and from disgust at modernisation optimism, and at full strength it is the most serious of the rivals because it shares the combined concept's externalism while drawing opposite conclusions about agency and hope. In the formulations of Andre Gunder Frank, the development of the advanced and the underdevelopment of the backward are two products of one process: metropolitan accumulation actively manufactures satellite backwardness by draining surplus, distorting structures toward export enclaves, and allying with local elites whose wealth depends on the drain. Fernando Henrique Cardoso and Enzo Faletto complicated the picture with dependent development, showing that industrialisation could proceed under dependency, though in associated and exclusionary forms; Immanuel Wallerstein's world systems analysis enlarged the canvas to a single capitalist world economy stratified into core, periphery and semiperiphery across five centuries. The strengths are genuine: an explanation of why contact with the advanced so often impoverishes rather than enriches, an account of enclaves and comprador elites that fits many coasts and mining zones, and a refusal to blame the backward for their backwardness. Readers can pursue the school's full exposition in [dependency theory explained](/2016/07/01/dependency-theory-explained/), which treats its texts and internal quarrels at the length they deserve.

Set beside combined development, dependency theory differs in three connected ways. First, the direction of emphasis: dependency looks outward to the drain between societies, while combination looks inward to the fusion within the receiving society, and the two optics reveal different facts; the drain explains stagnation where it occurs, while fusion explains the specific class configurations and political explosions that occur whether the receiving economy stagnates or grows. Second, the treatment of the receiving society's interior: in the stronger dependency formulations the satellite's internal structure is largely a reflection of its external insertion, whereas combined development insists on the causal weight of the inherited interior, the commune, the hacienda, the caste order, which bends the insertion into shapes the metropolis neither intended nor foresaw. Third, prognosis: dependency in Frank's version leaves the satellite no internal road, since development within the system is the mechanism of underdevelopment itself, while combined development, precisely because fusion creates novel configurations, finds in the backward country's very distortions the materials of political novelty, concentrated classes, fused tasks, skipped institutions, which is why revolutions surprised the dependency theorists in some of the same places they surprised everyone else. Neither school refutes the other wholesale, and honest scholarship uses both optics; but where the question is why a particular backward society produced a particular politics, the combined optic resolves details that the dependency optic leaves blurred, while where the question is who appropriated the surplus across a century, the dependency optic remains indispensable. Rivalry, properly conducted, allocates territory rather than annihilating opponents.

Gerschenkron presents the subtler challenge, because his account of economic backwardness, assembled in essays published across the postwar decades and collected in 1962, agrees with Trotsky on the phenomena and disagrees chiefly on framing. Studying the industrialisations of Germany, Italy, Russia and the Balkans, Gerschenkron argued that the later the start, the more discontinuous and state mediated the spurt of industrialisation, the larger the scale of plant, the greater the emphasis on producers' goods, and the more the functions performed in England by accumulated private capital had to be performed by substitutes: the great banks in Germany, the state itself in Russia. He called these the advantages and prerequisites substitutions of backwardness, and he documented an ideology of industrialisation, Saint Simonian, nationalist, Marxist in turn, that latecomers generated to legitimise the strain. The parallels with combined development are unmistakable: skipping, frontier plant, the entrepreneurial state, the fusion of imported technique with domestic substitutes. The differences are of scope and temper. Gerschenkron wrote as an economic historian suspicious of universal laws, offering graded generalisations about degrees of backwardness rather than a theory of social formations; his actors are banks, states and ideologies of growth, not classes in conflict, and his combinations are institutional devices for achieving industrialisation, not fused structures pregnant with revolution. Trotsky's concept is Gerschenkron's pattern raised to the level of the whole society and charged with political consequence. Scholars cite the economic historian more safely and the revolutionary more fruitfully, and a complete account of the territory acknowledges both: Gerschenkron for the mechanics of substitution, Trotsky for what the substitutions do to the society that hosts them.

Behind all three rivals stands a fourth presence, less a theory than a default setting of narrative history: the assumption that each nation's story is self contained and its stages self generated, against which combined development, dependency and Gerschenkron all rebel in their different dialects. The concept's distinctiveness within that rebellion can now be fixed in a sentence. Modernisation sees transfer and expects repetition; dependency sees transfer and expects subordination; Gerschenkron sees substitution and expects spurts; combined development sees transfer and expects fusion, a third thing, a formation neither repeated nor merely subordinated nor merely spurred, but reconfigured at its joints, with politics to match. That sentence is the one to carry into the cases and the afterlife, because it supplies the grading standard for both: wherever fusion and its consequences are analysed, the concept is alive; wherever transfer is discussed in any of the other three registers while wearing this concept's name, the borrowing is loose.


## Cases beyond Russia, tested against the four questions

Generality is asserted cheaply and earned case by case. The combination table listed rows; this section works three of them in prose and glances at others, applying the four questions each time: functional interdependence between borrowed and retained forms, mutual transformation, shared fate in crisis, and irreducibility of the whole to an early version of a pioneer. Cases that pass all four extend the concept's credit. Cases that fail instructively mark its borders, and both outcomes are reported, since a concept with no borders explains nothing.

Japan passes the test with a completeness second only to Russia, which is why the Meiji restoration is the standard second exhibit in the literature. The whip fell in 1853 with Commodore Perry's squadron, and it fell on a society that had watched China's defeat in the Opium Wars as a preview of its own possible fate. The Tokugawa order, a feudal system of domains under a shogunate with the emperor as ceremonial sovereign, dissolved within fifteen years, and the oligarchs who replaced it borrowed at a tempo and breadth that still astonish: a conscript army and navy on European models, a central bank, joint stock companies, railways and telegraphs, technical universities, a constitution drafted after study of Prussia's, legal codes after France's and Germany's, even, in the first flush, ballroom dancing at the Rokumeikan as diplomatic equipment. The Iwakura Mission of 1871 to 1873, half the government touring America and Europe for nearly two years, is the privilege of backwardness institutionalised: a ruling group shopping deliberately at the frontier of every foreign achievement. Yet what the borrowed forms fused with was no blank ground. Landlord tenancy survived the land settlement and became the fiscal and social base of the new state, with rents in kind squeezing tenants whose sons filled the conscript regiments and whose daughters staffed the silk mills that earned the foreign exchange for the warships. Emperor ideology, refurbished from a marginal institution into the sacred centre of the state, supplied the legitimacy that a society being disassembled at speed required. Samurai, abolished as a class, supplied the officers, officials, teachers and entrepreneurs of the new order, converting a sword caste into a service class in one generation. Interdependence is visible everywhere: the mills needed the villages, the fleet needed the silk, the throne needed the reforms it also sanctified against reformers. When crisis came in the 1930s, it propagated along exactly those joints, rural distress radicalising junior officers whose emperor worship had been manufactured by the state they then terrorised. Japan's combination was more successful than Russia's by every economic measure and no less fused, and its very success refutes the lazy inference that combination is a theory of failure. Fusion is a structure, not a sentence.

The Ottoman case tests the concept at the point of failure to fuse, and is correspondingly precious. From the late eighteenth century the empire reformed under the whip in successive waves: Selim the Third's new army, destroyed by Janissary revolt; Mahmud the Second's abolition of the Janissaries in 1826 and the ministries that followed; the Tanzimat era's codified law, secular courts, conscription and provincial reorganisation; railways, telegraphs and a modern debt. The borrowed forms were real, and so was the archaism they were meant to coexist with: a sultanate that was also a caliphate, communal jurisdictions governing Christians and Jews, tax farming's long afterlife, an agrarian interior barely touched by the coastal enclaves. What failed was the financing of combination. The empire borrowed abroad from 1854, defaulted in 1875, and accepted in 1881 the Public Debt Administration, a foreign controlled council collecting the salt, tobacco, stamp and other revenues directly, employing thousands, a state within the state dedicated to the bondholders of Paris and London. There the combination degenerated: the advanced element, European finance, did not fuse with the retained element but subordinated and bypassed it, while the retained element, dynastic and religious legitimacy, could neither discipline the borrowing nor survive the humiliation it funded. The Young Turk officers who seized power in 1908 were products of the combination's schools and regiments turning against the dynasty those institutions existed to defend, the table's political consequence in its purest form. The Ottoman row thus grades the concept's border: combination requires that the borrowed and retained elements interpenetrate within one sovereignty; where the advanced element rules as an external enclave with its own fiscal state, the formation is better described by dependency's vocabulary, and the concept, honestly used, hands the case over.

Germany, the near frontier case, tests the opposite border, combination without deep backwardness. The Zollverein and then the Reich of 1871 unified markets under Prussian hegemony; universal banks mobilised capital for heavy industry in the manner Gerschenkron anatomised; cartels organised coal, steel and chemicals at scales England's family firms could not match; by 1900 German electrical and chemical industries led the world. Retained alongside stood the Junker estates east of the Elbe, worked increasingly by seasonal Polish labour, a monarchy with real executive power, an army answerable to the crown rather than parliament, and a bureaucracy and professoriate steeped in deference to the state that employed them. The interdependence is plain: Junker officers commanded the army that guaranteed the Reich within which industry grew; industrial tariffs and agrarian tariffs were traded as a single settlement, the marriage of iron and rye; the socialist working class, Europe's largest, was contained by a constitution that gave it votes without power. Crisis propagated along the joints in 1918 and catastrophically after 1929, when the authoritarian inheritance supplied the channels through which economic collapse became political catastrophe. Germany shows the concept stretching toward the frontier and thinning as it goes: the archaism is political more than economic, the fusion less a matter of subsistence than of authority, and some readers will prefer to describe the case with the vocabulary of authoritarian modernisation. The honest verdict is that combined development illuminates Germany partially, chiefly its political configuration, and that a concept should be praised, not blamed, for having a gradient of fit rather than a universal solvent.

Shorter glances extend the map. Michael Löwy's classic study of Latin America read the region through combined development explicitly, finding in Brazil a formation where slavery persisted inside an export economy integrated with industrial capitalism until abolition in 1888, and where coffee planters, foreign railways and an imperial state fused into configurations that neither stage theory nor simple dependency captured; his title, the politics of combined and uneven development, announced the filiation. Scholars of China have debated the treaty port era in cognate terms: Shanghai's modern mills, banks and municipal council operating under foreign privilege inside an empire and then a republic whose agrarian interior, landlordism and warlord fragmentation supplied both the labour and the tinder of the revolutionary decades, a combination whose seams the Chinese revolution followed with terrible fidelity. Kemalist Turkey, already in the table, compresses the pattern into a single generation, secular codes and a new alphabet imposed by a tutelary party on an Anatolian smallholder society, the borrowed element wielded deliberately as a lever against the retained one, which is combination conducted as policy rather than suffered as fate, with the strain showing in every decade since. In each instance the concept's value lies in the specificity it forces: name the elements, find the joints, trace the crisis along them, and the case either confirms the pattern or shows exactly where it stops.


## The second life: the concept inside international relations theory

The strangest fact about uneven and combined development is bibliographical. For most of the twentieth century the idea lived where Trotsky left it, inside the literature of a revolutionary movement, expounded in party educationals, refined by a handful of scholars on that movement's margins such as Löwy and Ernest Mandel, and ignored by the universities. From the middle of the 2000s it underwent a migration with no parallel in the history of Marxist concepts: it became a live research programme inside academic international relations, debated in the discipline's leading journals, taught in its graduate programmes, and worked into monographs on subjects from early modern state formation to the geopolitics of climate, most of whose authors hold no Trotskyist commitment and several of whom hold no Marxist one either. Explaining that migration, and grading what the migrants did with the concept, is this article's distinctive service, since the Trotskyist literature treats the academic afterlife as a footnote and the academic literature often treats the origin as an embarrassment to be acknowledged quickly. Both treatments undersell a genuinely instructive episode: a concept changing disciplines and being changed by the journey.

The migration had a named beginning. Justin Rosenberg, a scholar of international relations at the University of Sussex, devoted his Isaac Deutscher Memorial Lecture, delivered in 1994 and published in New Left Review in 1996, to the argument that his discipline had lost its own history, having forgotten that classical social theory once possessed resources for theorising the international that the twentieth century's disciplinary division of labour destroyed. A decade later he made the positive proposal in programmatic form. In "Why Is There No International Historical Sociology?", published in the European Journal of International Relations in 2006, and in a companion series of articles on the basic problems of the theory, including a second instalment in the Cambridge Review of International Affairs on unevenness and political multiplicity, Rosenberg argued that uneven and combined development supplies the missing foundation: a general theory of the international. The reasoning runs as follows. International relations as a field presupposes its object, a world of multiple societies interacting, and then struggles to explain why that multiplicity matters causally rather than decoratively. Realism treats multiplicity as anarchy and derives self help; liberalism treats it as interdependence and derives cooperation problems; constructivism treats it as a distribution of identities. None explains why social development itself is stamped through and through by the fact of coexisting, differing societies. Trotsky's concept, generalised, explains exactly that: because societies coexist at different levels and interact under pressure, the development of each is partly authored by the others, combination is universal, and the international is therefore not a separate realm between societies but a dimension of their being, present in the constitution of every one of them. On that foundation, Rosenberg proposed, an international historical sociology could finally be built, joining the insights of historical sociology, which had shown how states and classes form, to the international dimension those accounts habitually bracketed.

The proposal landed in a discipline ready for it. Historical sociology within international relations was already gathering force, in work by scholars such as George Lawson on revolutions, John Hobson on the Eurocentric construction of the field's categories, and Benno Teschke on the geopolitical origins of early modern state forms, and Rosenberg's articles gave that dispersed work a theoretical banner and a classical pedigree. Development followed along several tracks. Alexander Anievas and Jamie Allinson, in a widely cited outline of the approach's uses and misuses, defended the concept against the charge that generalising it across all history evacuates its content, arguing that its transhistorical form still generates distinctive explanations of phenomena from the emergence of capitalism to the Cold War. Anievas and Kerem Nisancioglu then attempted the programme's most ambitious historical demonstration in How the West Came to Rule, published in 2015, rereading the rise of capitalism as a product of intersocietal interactions, Ottoman pressure, Mongol trade routes, American silver, colonial plunder, rather than as an endogenous European miracle, with uneven and combined development as the book's declared framework. Anievas and Kamran Matin edited a collective volume in 2016 on uneven and combined development over the longue durée, gathering applications and criticisms across world history. Matin's own work applied the lens to Iran and to the origins of modernity debates. Neil Davidson, writing from within the Marxist tradition, probed the conditions under which combined development begins, insisting that the full phenomenon requires the specific compulsions of a capitalist world market and resisting its extension to epochs where interaction was thin and borrowing episodic. The debate, in short, became a debate: about scope, about mechanism, about whether a concept forged to explain Russia in 1917 could explain the emergence of states in the second millennium or the transition from feudalism, and the journals and edited volumes carried it because the questions were real.

### Can a concept built for capitalism travel to earlier epochs intact?

Only with its mechanism restated, which is where honest borrowers part from loose ones. Under capitalism the whip is permanent because competition is systemic and contact is dense. Earlier epochs knew conquest, tribute and trade, but rarely the continuous pressure that makes borrowing compulsory and fusion structural rather than episodic.

Three features of the afterlife deserve emphasis before the grading begins. First, the participants are explicit that the concept's origin is no obstacle: the debate is conducted as normal science, with claims, counterexamples and refinements, and Trotsky figures in it as a theorist to be argued with rather than a prophet to be cited or a contaminant to be scrubbed. Second, the traffic is two way. The international relations literature sent questions back to the Marxist tradition that the tradition had not asked itself, about pre capitalist combination, about the concept's status as law or heuristic, about its compatibility with the critique of political economy, and scholars from within that tradition, Davidson and Callinicos among them, answered in the same journals. Third, the afterlife confirms the article's governing claim from an unexpected direction. What international relations wanted from Trotsky was never unevenness, which its own textbooks already possessed in the vocabulary of power differentials. What it lacked, and took, was combination raised to a principle: interaction as constitutive. The borrowing discipline voted with its citations for the second word.

## Law, description, or theory of the international: the debate graded

The scholarly argument over the generalised concept has crystallised around three questions, and presenting them in order shows both the programme's strength and its exposure. The first question is scope. Rosenberg's generalisation detaches the concept from capitalism and from late development, proposing uneven and combined development as a transhistorical feature of human social existence wherever multiple societies coexist. Against this, critics from within the Marxist tradition, Alex Callinicos most prominently, have objected that the detachment costs the concept its mechanism: under capitalism, combination is driven by identifiable compulsions, the whip of military and market competition operating through a world market that no society escapes, whereas a transhistorical version must rest on interaction as such, which is sometimes intense and sometimes negligible, and which therefore explains combination where it occurs only by redescribing it. Davidson's intervention sharpens the point by distinguishing the general fact of intersocietal influence, as old as trade and conquest, from the specific phenomenon Trotsky named, in which development itself becomes combined because a dominant mode of production exists somewhere and exerts continuous pressure everywhere. On that reading, applying the term to, say, the interplay of Rome and its neighbours stretches an analogy past its warrant.

The second question is level. Is the concept a law, a theory, a framework or a sensibility? Trotsky's own usage, law, sits awkwardly with modern social science, and his defenders divide: some defend law in the tendential sense, a claim about what interaction under unevenness reliably does; others retreat to framework, a set of questions and expectations that organise research without dictating answers; still others, among the international relations borrowers, present it as a substantive theory of the international comparable in ambition to realism or liberalism. Each step down the ladder from law to framework purchases defensibility at the price of content, and the grading section will insist that the price be acknowledged rather than concealed. A framework that merely prompts scholars to consider interaction has value, but less than a law that risks falsification, and the concept's admirers should say which they are defending in any given use.

The third question is the Marxism question: does the generalised concept retain anything specifically Marxist, or does it become a generic interactionism available to any theoretical allegiance? The borrowers themselves divide interestingly here. Rosenberg presents the generalisation as recovering a classical sociological insight that Marxism happened to preserve, and welcomes its use by scholars of other persuasions. Critics reply that what makes the original concept Marxist is not its interactionism but its embedding in an account of capital: the whip is competition among capitals and states within an accumulating world economy, combination is a form taken by the development of the productive forces and class relations, and stripping that embedding leaves a husk, true everywhere and explanatory nowhere in particular. Anievas and his collaborators answer that the historical work done under the banner, above all the rereading of capitalism's origins as intersocietal rather than endogenous, vindicates the generalisation by its fruits, since it corrects Eurocentric narratives that treated Europe's rise as self generated, a correction Marxists and non Marxists alike have reason to want. The honest summary is that the generalised concept is Marxist in provenance and in its materialist temper, but portable in a way its originator would have found surprising, and that portability is simultaneously its triumph and its danger, the danger being dilution into the metaphorical uses graded next.

It should also be recorded, in fairness to the programme's critics outside Marxism, that mainstream international relations has hardly surrendered. The discipline's dominant theories continue without the concept; its uptake is concentrated in international historical sociology and critical theory, influential subfields rather than the whole. Presenting the afterlife as a conquest would repeat the triumphalist habits this article criticises elsewhere. What occurred is more interesting than conquest: a concept from a marginalised tradition proved indispensable to a growing subfield's self understanding, was fought over in leading journals by scholars who mostly owed that tradition nothing, and generated a shelf of historical work that stands or falls on its evidence. For an idea first stated in a chapter of revolutionary history during its author's exile, that is a remarkable fate, and it is best honoured by the same standard the concept applies to societies: trace what the borrowing fused with, and where the seams strain.


## Where borrowing becomes metaphor: a grading standard

Concepts that travel accumulate loose change, usages that spend the prestige of the term without earning it. Uneven and combined development now circulates well beyond its home tradition and its international relations beachhead: in historical sociology and development studies, in geography's accounts of variegated capitalism, in literary and cultural studies where combined formations are discerned in texts and genres, and in journalism where the phrase occasionally dignifies any juxtaposition of skyscraper and slum. Some of this is the healthy diffusion of a fertile idea. Some is metaphor wearing a concept's clothes. Since this article has praised the borrowing, it owes the reader a standard for telling the two apart, and the standard can be strict without being proprietary, because it derives from the concept's own machinery rather than from loyalty to a school.

A use counts as evidence about the concept's reach when it retains the machinery: a specifiable unevenness that matters, a pressure transmitting it, identifiable borrowed and retained forms, demonstrated interdependence between them, and consequences drawn from their friction. Rosenberg's programme, whatever its scope problems, retains every part, which is why even its critics engage it as theory. Anievas and Nisancioglu's history retains the machinery at world scale, narrating specific transmissions, gunpowder empires, colonial trades, silver flows, and specific fusions in the receiving formations. Historical sociology in the same vein, work on state formation that shows fiscal military borrowing fusing with patrimonial structures, earns its use of the term by showing joints. Geography's stronger contributions, mapping how global production networks land in and fuse with local labour regimes and welfare settlements, likewise show joints and earn the vocabulary. In each success the concept does work that would be lost if the phrase were deleted: delete it and the analysis loses its account of why the elements interlock and why their friction generates the outcomes.

A use decays into metaphor in recognisable stages. First the machinery is thinned to interaction: societies influence each other, therefore combined development, a claim no more distinctive than saying history is connected. Then unevenness is thinned to diversity: societies differ, therefore the concept applies, and the term becomes a synonym for plurality with a revolutionary accent. Then combination is thinned to juxtaposition: old and new visible in one frame, a temple beside a tower block, therefore combined development, although nothing is claimed about interdependence, transmission or consequence. At the end of the slide the phrase functions as an incantation certifying that the author knows history is complicated. Such uses are not harmless. They devalue the currency for the serious borrowers, they expose the concept to the objection that it means everything and nothing, and they invite the backlash in which a genuinely explanatory idea is dismissed because its decorative uses were empty. The discipline's own internal critics perform a service when they police the boundary, and the standard proposed here, machinery retained or machinery lost, gives that policing a criterion independent of anyone's politics.

### When does a borrowed term stop analysing and start decorating?

It stops when deleting it changes nothing. If an account can remove the phrase and lose no claim about pressure, transmission, interdependence or consequence, the phrase was ornament. Analysis begins where the term carries weight: where naming the borrowed form, the retained form and their friction tells the reader something the narrative had not already established.

Two intermediate grades deserve naming, because most real borrowing falls between the poles. The analogical use borrows the pattern for a domain the originator never considered, a literary form fusing archaic and modern genres, a legal system layering imported codes over customary practice, and argues the analogy explicitly, showing parallels in mechanism rather than merely in appearance. Legal transplants, to take the strongest example, genuinely do behave in combined ways: codes imported wholesale are bent by retained institutions, produce hybrid doctrines, and generate frictions at predictable seams, so the analogy there approaches the literal. The heuristic use, common in research design, borrows the concept as a question generator: what was borrowed here, under what pressure, what did it fuse with, where are the seams? Heuristic use makes no claim that the concept is true of the case in advance, and is therefore honest even when the answers disappoint. Both grades are legitimate. Both become illegitimate only when they borrow the assertoric force of the original law while doing the work of analogy or heuristic, a bait and switch that careful readers learn to detect by watching modal verbs: the metaphorical use says is where the honest one says works like, or asks.

The grading cuts inward as well as outward, and fairness requires a sentence on the home tradition. Trotskyist literature itself contains decorative uses, centenary invocations of the phrase as a badge of lineage, applications to new cases that assert combination without locating joints, and defences of the concept that treat criticism as heresy rather than as the normal metabolism of theory. A concept's custodians can dilute it as thoroughly as its tourists. The standard is indifferent to the user's credentials: machinery retained, machinery lost. By that standard the international relations debate, for all its abstraction, has generally served the concept better than the commemorative literature, because the debate forced its participants to specify mechanisms, scope conditions and falsifying evidence in public, under hostile review. There is a lesson in that for every tradition that owns a powerful idea: export it, and strangers will test it in ways friends decline to.

## Misreadings corrected in one place

Precision articles exist to be raided by readers in a hurry, so the corrections scattered through the preceding sections are gathered here in compact form, each stated as the error, the correction, and the reason the distinction matters. The first misreading takes unevenness for the concept, and has been the article's running target: unevenness is the premise, combination the claim, and an account that stops at differential rates has explained nothing. The second takes combined development to mean a mixed economy, a policy blend of public and private ownership; the concept concerns the fusion of historical forms under external compulsion, not a government's ownership portfolio. The third takes the privilege of backwardness to mean that backwardness is advantageous overall; it names a freedom in sequencing purchased at compounding costs, and the societies enjoying it remained poor, coerced and crisis prone by every material measure. The fourth takes the concept to be only about Russia; Russia is the paradigm and the proof case, but the claim was framed generally from its first chapter, and its career in other regions and disciplines is the working test of that generality. The fifth takes it to be a Trotskyist doctrine in the sense of a membership badge; it is a sociological proposition that stands or falls on evidence, which is why scholars with no allegiance to its author's politics could adopt, amend and fight over it in open journals. The sixth, subtler, takes skipping to mean that stages vanish; skipped stages return as fused tasks and institutional gaps, and the bill for every shortcut is presented somewhere in the formation, in its debt, its missing middle strata, or its overloaded revolutions.

A seventh misreading, flattering to the concept, requires correction as firmly as the hostile ones: the idea that combined development predicts revolution wherever it finds fusion. It does not. It predicts configurations, concentrated classes, fused tasks, brittleness at the seams, whose political outcomes depend on war, organisation, leadership and accident, as the concept's own home case demonstrates twice over, the same formation producing collapse in 1905 that stopped at reform's edge and collapse in 1917 that did not. A theory of configurations constrains what can happen and explains why surprises cluster where they do; it does not relieve historians of narrating how a possibility became an event. Readers who want a concept that issues destinies should look elsewhere, and readers who meet one in the literature should ask what work the destiny is doing that configuration could not.


## The strongest objection: a pattern in search of a mechanism

The objection that matters is not that the concept is Marxist, nor that its author was a revolutionary, nor that its vocabulary is dated. It is the cooler charge that the concept describes rather than explains. To say a society combines advanced and archaic elements, the critic observes, is to name a pattern visible in every society that has ever contacted another, which is to say every society in the record. Patterns are cheap. Explanation requires a mechanism that generates the pattern, scope conditions stating where the mechanism operates, and predictions that could fail. On all three counts the critic finds the concept wanting. Its mechanism, external pressure, is so general that it borders on the definitional: which society lacks rivals, markets or conquerors at its borders? Its scope is elastic, expanding from Russia to late developers to all history as convenience dictates, and a claim compatible with every epoch is a claim about none. Its predictions, finally, are issued retrospectively: after a revolution in a backward country, combination is found; after stability in a backward country, combination is found again, differently weighted. A vocabulary that redescribes every outcome in its own terms has immunised itself against evidence, and immunised theories, however evocative, belong to literature rather than to social science. The transhistorical generalisation, the critic concludes, completes the dissolution: what began as a sharp claim about Russia under the whip of a capitalist world market ends as the observation that societies interact, dressed in the borrowed authority of a law.

That statement of the objection is deliberately left at full strength, because the concept deserves the compliment of being attacked properly, and because weaker versions, the concept is old, the concept is partisan, answer themselves. The reply must concede what is true in it before claiming what survives. Three concessions are owed. The transhistorical version is genuinely more exposed than the original: interaction before the world market was episodic, its pressures escapable by migration, withdrawal or simple distance, and combination in Trotsky's loaded sense, compulsory, continuous and structurally fusing, is a phenomenon of epochs with dense and inescapable contact, above all the capitalist one. The law language invites misunderstanding and has sheltered lazy uses; tendential claim would have been the honester label. And the tradition's own literature does contain the retrospective vice the critic names, readings of cases that find combination because they went looking for it with no prior statement of what its absence would look like. A defence that denied these points would forfeit credibility exactly where it needs it.

What survives the concessions is the mechanism in its original scope, and the predictions it generates there. Within a world of dense contact, the mechanism is specific enough to fail: pressure from more developed rivals compels ruling groups to import frontier forms; importation at the frontier, into formations whose other elements are older, necessarily produces interdependence between the imported and the inherited; interdependence generates characteristic class configurations and seam frictions. Each link can be examined in a case, and cases exist where the chain breaks, where pressure was escaped, where imports remained enclaves, where classes formed in pioneer proportions despite lateness, and such cases count against the concept rather than being absorbed by it, provided the investigator states the expectation before admiring the outcome. The predictions about configuration are the sharpest: late industrialisers will show higher industrial concentration than pioneers at comparable output; their states will bulk larger as entrepreneurs and financiers; their bourgeoisies will be more dependent on state and foreign capital and correspondingly more timid politically; their working classes will be younger, more concentrated and more fused with agrarian grievance; their political crises will tend to bundle tasks that pioneer history separated. These are not prophecies of events; they are claims about shapes, and shapes can be measured, compared and found absent. The Russia of the paradigm case displays all five. Germany displays most, with the political ones strongest. Japan displays the configuration under a regime skilful enough to ride it for two generations. A critic who wants the concept dead should attack the five claims comparatively, across the population of late developers, and the invitation stands open in the literature; what the critic may not do is pretend the claims were never made.

### What observation would defeat a combined development reading of a case?

A durable absence of joints. Show that the imported sector reproduced itself without relying on the retained structures for labour, revenue or legitimacy; that the retained structures were untouched in function by the imports beside them; and that crises stayed sealed inside one sector. Then borrowing occurred without combination, and the reading fails for that case.

The evidence standard implied by that answer deserves a paragraph of its own, because it converts the concept from a narrative taste into a research discipline. Evidence for combination is positive and material: fiscal records showing the modern sector's dependence on archaic extraction, as Russian industrialisation depended on redemption dues and the vodka monopoly squeezes on the peasantry; labour records showing the imported plant's workforce reproduced by the village, allotment holding workers whose wages presupposed communal subsistence; institutional records showing retained forms performing new functions, caste associations operating as electoral and credit networks, churches running the welfare that industrial wages omitted, customary courts enforcing contracts for firms the state code could not reach. Evidence against is equally material: an imported sector that taxes, feeds, houses and reproduces itself from its own revenues and a fully proletarianised workforce has escaped combination whatever its skyline looks like. Investigators will dispute borderline mixtures of the two evidentiary piles, and that dispute, conducted over archives rather than over definitions, is the concept working as science. The combination table earlier in this article is best read as a docket of such disputes waiting to be held, each cell a claim whose evidence can be demanded.

One further turn of the objection should be faced, concerning the concept's two halves and their unequal risk. Unevenness, the critic notes, is unfalsifiable because it is true by observation. Combined development, properly stated, risks itself in every application, since each claimed combination can dissolve under scrutiny into juxtaposition or enclave. The concept as a whole therefore inherits the falsifiability of its stronger half, which is another way of stating this article's thesis from the philosophy of science side. A theory is only as brave as its bravest claim. Trotsky's bravest claim was never that the world is uneven. It was that lateness fuses, and that the fusions rule the politics of the societies that host them. Judged there, the concept has survived a century of hostile and friendly examination with its central assertions intact in its original scope and productively disputed beyond it, which is more than most social theories of comparable age can report.

## Why revolutions cluster where development came late

No question has carried the concept further into general curiosity than the one phrased in schoolrooms and forums alike: why did the great social revolutions of the twentieth century occur in poorer countries rather than in the advanced ones where theory first expected them? France in 1789 stands behind the question as the classical template, a revolution in the most advanced society of its continent; but the sequels refused the template's geography. Russia in 1917, China across the decades to 1949, Vietnam, Cuba: the revolutionary century unfolded in the agrarian and semi industrial world, while the richest working classes, organised in the mightiest unions and parties, settled into reformist routines within states that never approached collapse. Expectation had ranked revolutionary potential by developmental level. Experience inverted the ranking. Combined development is the explanation of the inversion that requires no conspiracy and no psychology of national character, only the configurations the concept describes.

Assemble the mechanism in sequence. A combined formation concentrates grievances that pioneer history distributed across centuries: land hunger beside factory discipline, national humiliation beside constitutional promise, subsistence crisis beside conspicuous imported luxury. It equips the aggrieved with modern instruments, railways to gather on, telegraphs to coordinate with, conscript armies that teach peasants the use of arms and the experience of collective organisation. It weakens the classes that in pioneer history absorbed shocks, leaving a bourgeoisie too dependent to lead or to concede with authority, a landed class too entangled with the state to reform itself, and a state whose modernised coercion rests on archaic finance that war or depression can snap. And it fuses the tasks, so that every partial demand, bread, land, peace, a constitution, national independence, runs immediately into every other, and movements that begin by asking for one thing discover the whole structure in their path. Revolutions, on this account, are not the wages of poverty; the poorest and most isolated societies, lacking combination, produced few. They are the wages of fusion under pressure, which is why they clustered in the great combined formations, Russia, China, the dissolving empires, rather than in either the advanced core or the untouched periphery. The concept thereby explains a distribution, not merely a set of episodes, and distributions are the currency in which social theories are ultimately paid.

The same mechanism casts light backwards on the apparent exceptions. Germany, the advanced country that came nearest to revolution in 1918 and 1923, was, in this article's earlier grading, the frontier case with the deepest political combination, frontier industry under semi absolutist authority, and its revolutionary crises followed the political seams precisely. England, the pioneer, displayed the opposite configuration, classes formed gradually, tasks settled sequentially, a state that had fused nothing because it had borrowed nothing under compulsion, and its politics, Chartism's defeat absorbed, reform by instalment, matches the concept's expectation for an uncombined formation: intense conflict, no revolutionary situation of the fused kind. Such retrospective fits are suggestive rather than probative, and are offered in that spirit. Their value lies in showing the concept's explanatory grammar at work across the range, generating the right questions about cases it was not designed for, and inviting the archival labour that alone can settle whether the grammar corresponds to the record.


## Roots of the idea before its famous statement

Famous formulations condense longer histories, and this one's prehistory matters because it shows the concept being forced into existence by events rather than invented in a study. The Russian Marxists of the generation before Trotsky had argued for decades over a precursor question: whether Russia must pass through capitalism at all. The Narodniks held that the peasant commune might serve as the embryo of a distinct Russian socialism, sparing the country the capitalist stage; Marx himself, in his late correspondence on the Russian commune, including the celebrated drafts of his letter to Vera Zasulich in 1881, refused to foreclose that possibility, treating the commune's fate as dependent on the international environment, in particular on whether a proletarian revolution in the West arrived in time to supply the material conditions Russia lacked. The environment deciding a formation's path from outside: the germ of combined development sits in those drafts, inside the founder's own hesitations about the staircase. Georgi Plekhanov and then Lenin settled the orthodox answer against the Narodniks by documenting capitalism's actual growth in Russia, Lenin's Development of Capitalism in Russia of 1899 amassing statistics to prove the stage was not coming but present. Yet presence arrived in a peculiar shape, as Lenin's own data showed: capitalist relations penetrating a countryside still governed by servile survivals, a combination his analysis registered without yet theorising as a general form.

The 1905 revolution converted registration into theory. Defeat in the war with Japan, itself a lesson in what a rival's successful combination could do to an unsuccessful one, triggered strikes, soviets and peasant revolt across the empire, and the young Trotsky, presiding over the St Petersburg Soviet and then analysing the wreckage in exile, concluded with Parvus that the configuration he had witnessed obeyed laws the staircase could not state. Results and Prospects, written in 1906, contains the doctrine of permanent revolution in full and the sociology of combination in embryo: the weakness of the Russian bourgeoisie, the disproportionate weight of the proletariat, the fusion of democratic and socialist tasks, all derived from the peculiar interlacing of Russia's backwardness with the world economy's advances. The exile writings of his later years, above all the History with its opening chapter composed in 1930, generalised the embryo into the named law, at the moment when its author needed to explain to a hostile world how the backward country had made the first workers' revolution and why its subsequent degeneration also followed from the terms of its isolation. Knowing this genesis protects readers against two symmetrical errors: treating the concept as a timeless axiom dropped from theory's heaven, and treating it as a rationalisation improvised for one man's politics. It was an induction from a witnessed configuration, extended chapter by chapter into a general claim, and it retains the strengths and the scars of that origin, empirical density at the core, polemical overreach at the edges.

The concept also had a parallel life inside the controversies of the communist movement that lay readers rarely see, and a paragraph of map is owed to them. Through the 1920s the question of stages was a fighting question across the colonial world: must the movements of China, India and Persia subordinate themselves to their national bourgeoisies in the name of the democratic stage, or did combined development license independent working class politics aiming beyond it? The Chinese catastrophe of 1927, in which the Communist Party's enforced subordination to the Kuomintang ended in massacre, was read by Trotsky and his supporters as the staircase's practical refutation and by their opponents as proof of premature adventurism, and the argument ran through the movement's journals for a generation. This article takes no side in that inheritance dispute, which belongs to political history, beyond noting its analytical residue: every participant, including the concept's enemies, was obliged to argue about configurations, class weights in backward formations, the behaviour of dependent bourgeoisies, the fusions of national and social tasks. The vocabulary won arguments even where the politics lost them, an early instance of the afterlife pattern that the international relations section documented at larger scale: the concept outworking its custodians because the configurations it names kept recurring wherever development arrived late and armed.

## Combination above the base: law, school, church and script

Most expositions, this one included so far, illustrate combination with factories, armies and banks, because its author did. A complete account must register that the fusion operates through the whole height of a society, and that some of its most durable evidence sits in the superstructure, where forms are borrowed with even less friction than blast furnaces and retained archaisms defend themselves with even greater tenacity. Law offers the clearest laboratory. The nineteenth and twentieth centuries saw wholesale legal transplants on a scale no earlier age attempted: the French civil code carried across continents, German jurisprudence received in Japan, Swiss civil law adopted entire by the Turkish republic in 1926, Soviet codes assembled from European models and revolutionary decree, postcolonial states inheriting colonial codes and administering them through customary courts. In each reception the code fused with retained institutions, religious personal law, communal land tenure, patriarchal family authority, producing hybrid orders whose actual operation bewildered the transplanters and whose seams, between code and custom, citizen and subject, became the working terrain of politics. Legal historians describe these orders in a vocabulary of layering and hybridity that maps onto combination point for point, usually without the citation, another unnamed inheritance of the pattern this article traces.

Education and script tell the same story in miniature and at mass scale. The late developer imports the university, the technical institute, the normal school, and staffs them initially with foreign instructors teaching foreign curricula to students whose homes speak other worlds; within a generation the imported institution has been captured by local purposes, nationalist historiography written in the imported seminar form, engineering turned to state projects the models never envisaged, and the resulting intellectual class is itself a combined formation, bilingual in modern technique and inherited loyalty, and chronically unstable between them. Script reforms condense the drama into a single decree: Turkey's adoption of the Latin alphabet in 1928 severed a literate culture from its archive at a stroke, an act of forced skipping in the realm of letters, whose costs and liberations historians still tally. Japan's Meiji translators coined the vocabulary in which Japanese would discuss society, economy and philosophy, importing the concepts with the words. None of these episodes is reducible to influence. Each shows borrowed form and retained structure interpenetrating, transforming each other's function, and generating the seam frictions, generational, confessional, regional, that the concept predicts in the economy and finds again, unprompted, in the culture. A theory whose pattern recurs at levels it was not designed for gains plausibility in the way that matters most, by consilience, provided the recurrence is demonstrated joint by joint rather than asserted by analogy, the grading standard again.

Religion, finally, is where retained forms most visibly refuse the role of residue. Across the combined formations, churches, mosques, temples and their networks did not merely survive modernisation; they were rearmed by it, printing presses, railways for pilgrimage, modern schools and parties reorganising inherited faiths into mass forms their premodern custodians could not have built. The archaic element here is simultaneously a product of the advanced one, since a pilgrimage organised by steamship and telegraph is not the pilgrimage of the caravan age, however venerable its destination. Observers who file religion under survival therefore misread the joint: the retained form has been transformed by the borrowed instruments into something new that continues to do the old work of legitimacy and consolation under new conditions of scale. Combination, once more, is not the coexistence of a mosque and a ministry. It is the ministry's timetable reorganising the pilgrimage and the pilgrimage's crowds reorganising the ministry's politics, each unintelligible alone.

## A worked method: reading any late developer in five passes

Readers who have followed the argument this far possess a theory; what teaching requires is a procedure, and the concept converts into one gracefully. The method below distils the preceding sections into five passes over any candidate case, ordered so that each pass disciplines the next, and it works as well for a seminar essay on Meiji Japan as for a monograph on Ottoman finance or a journalist's brief on a state industrialising under sanctions and scarcity in any era. It assumes no allegiance beyond accuracy, and it terminates, honourably, in a negative verdict where the evidence refuses the pattern.

The first pass inventories unevenness concretely: the candidate society's levels of productivity, armament, literacy, urbanisation and fiscal capacity set against the specific neighbours and rivals whose pressure it actually feels, since unevenness in the abstract is weather, while unevenness against a named rival across a contested border is climate with an address. The second pass traces the whip: the wars, defeats, debt negotiations, trade shocks and prestige competitions through which pressure entered, with dates, because the timetable of borrowing follows the timetable of fear more faithfully than any plan. The third pass lists the borrowings at the frontier and the retentions at the base in two columns, refusing abstractions: not modernisation and tradition, but this arsenal and that tax farm, this code and that court, this mill and that commune, each named, dated and sourced. The fourth pass hunts the joints, the fiscal, labour, institutional and ideological links where the two columns depend on each other and transform each other, and applies the four questions from the table section, recording negatives without flinching. The fifth pass reads the politics off the configuration: the relative weights of the classes as the combination made them, the bundle of tasks the society's history has fused, and the seam lines along which its crises have actually run or, in prospective work, along which they are likeliest to run. A case that survives all five passes with its joints documented has earned a combined development reading. A case that fails at the fourth has been honestly processed, and the failure, reported, adds to knowledge rather than subtracting from the concept, since concepts grow by their mapped borders as much as by their conquests.

Two warnings belong to the method's honest use. The first concerns sources: the joints live in fiscal records, factory inspectorates, debt commissions, consular reports and the administrative paper of states, not in the programmatic texts of movements, and a reading built only on manifestos will find the combination its authors wanted rather than the one their society had. The second concerns sympathy: the method's inventor wrote as a partisan of the class his theory crowned, and the configuration claims must therefore be checked with double severity exactly where they flatter the inventor's politics, in the weight assigned to the working class and the incapacity assigned to its rivals. Severity toward one's own side is the tax a tradition pays for the right to be believed about the other side, and this article has attempted to levy it throughout, conceding scope, relabelling laws as tendencies, handing the Ottoman case to a rival vocabulary, and marking Germany as a partial fit. Whether the concessions were sufficient, the reader, now equipped with the table, the standard and the method, is in a position to judge without assistance.


## The broker state: combination's indispensable institution

One actor appears in every case yet is easily under theorised, because the concept's vocabulary of classes and forms can make it seem a mere arena. The state in a combined formation is not the arena; it is the broker, the only institution positioned at every joint simultaneously, taxing the archaic to fund the advanced, importing the foreign to discipline the domestic, and staffing the boundary with translators, engineers, officers and inspectors who live in both epochs professionally. Understanding the broker state converts several loose ends of the argument into a single picture. It explains why late development is so often authoritarian in form regardless of the rulers' temperaments: the brokerage functions, extracting from peasants for factories they will never see, conscripting for wars of prestige, suppressing the disorders that dislocation breeds, require concentrated coercion, and representative institutions, where they appear, arrive as additional borrowed forms to be fused with, or subordinated to, the brokerage. It explains the characteristic corruption of late developers, which is less a moral failing than a structural position: brokers who allocate scarce foreign exchange, contracts, licences and exemptions stand at a valve between worlds of vastly different prices, and the valve leaks in every known century. And it explains why crises of combined formations are so often crises of the state itself, since the broker holds the whole configuration's debts in both directions, owed modernity to its creditors and protection to its traditionalists, and a shock that forces it to default on either obligation propagates everywhere at once.

The broker's instruments repay listing in prose because each is a small combination in itself. Consider the development bank and its ancestors: the Russian state banks channelling French loans into metallurgy, the Ottoman Bank, a foreign owned institution performing central banking for a sultan, the Japanese industrial banks, the Kemalist state banks founded after the foreign ones withdrew. Each fused the most advanced financial technique with political purposes no commercial criterion would have chosen, lending where strategy directed rather than where returns beckoned, and each therefore generated the soft budgets, political loans and periodic banking crises that mark combined finance to this day in the literature on state capitalism. Consider the concession and the enclave: mining and railway concessions granted to foreign companies under terms that imported not only capital but law, company towns under foreign management, extraterritorial courts, a sovereignty leased by parcels, so that the map of a combined formation often shows its joints as legal geography, zones where different centuries of law govern adjacent streets. Consider the model factory and the pilot zone, the state's habit of concentrating its borrowed modernity in exhibitable sites, Potemkin plants beside untouched provinces, a pattern recurring from Peter's Petersburg, a capital city built as a combination in stone, to the special economic zones of later industrialisers, where the zone's fence is the seam made visible. In each instrument the state is not reacting to combination but manufacturing it, deliberately and by design, and the resulting formation bears the stamp of brokerage in its very layout, capital here, barracks there, commune everywhere else, the seams surveyed and policed.

Against this picture a liberal objection naturally forms, and it should be heard: that the broker state is simply what weak states do, and that strong impersonal institutions in the pioneers performed equivalent functions without the drama. The objection concedes more than it notices. The pioneers' states also taxed, borrowed, chartered and coerced their way through industrialisation, over longer periods and with domestic resources, and the difference the concept insists on is temporal and structural rather than moral: compression. A brokerage spread over two centuries, funded from growing domestic revenues and legitimated by slowly widening representation, fuses gradually and revises as it goes. The same brokerage compressed into two reigns, funded abroad and legitimated by dynasty or emergency, fuses explosively and revises by crisis. The variable is the one Trotsky's chapter began with, tempo, and tempo, in social formations as in music, changes the character of everything played at it.

## Combination within borders: regions, sectors and the internal frontier

The concept's unit is usually the society, but its mechanism operates inside societies as faithfully as between them, and some of its most instructive evidence is regional. Every large late developer contains its own unevenness: a modernising coast against a subsistence interior, a capital of banks and ministries against provinces governed by notables, an export sector wired to the world market against a food sector wired to the village. The whip cracks inside the borders too, since the modernising region and its state tax, conscript and culturally devalue the interior, and the interior's retained forms, its kinship systems, religious authorities and customary tenures, do not merely survive the pressure but are reorganised by it, supplying the migrants, the soldiers and the remittances on which the advanced region depends. Brazil's industrial southeast and its northeast of latifundia and drought, the Italy of Turin and Milan against the Mezzogiorno of emigration and clientelism, China's coastal provinces against its hinterland across several regimes, the Russia of Petersburg and the Donets against the black earth villages: each pair is a combined formation nested inside a national one, and the politics of such states, regional parties, internal colonialism debates, separatisms and populisms of the interior against the coast, follow the seams with the same fidelity the concept predicts internationally. The nesting matters theoretically because it answers a possible objection to the international relations generalisation from the opposite side: if combination also operates below the level of the society, then the mechanism cannot depend on sovereignty or on the formal existence of an international system, but on contact under unevenness and compulsion as such, which is precisely the generalised claim, arriving this time from evidence rather than from abstraction.

Sectoral nesting tells the same story at finer grain. Within one industry, frontier and archaic production interlock: the Indian mill sector of Bombay and Ahmedabad grew beside, and partly upon, a handloom sector that colonial policy was supposedly destroying, yet handlooms survived by occupying niches the mills created, weaving mill spun yarn into cloths mills could not profitably make, so that the most advanced and the most archaic textile production in the world were functional partners for a century. Within one enterprise, the pattern repeats: the great Russian and Japanese plants housed craft shops beside assembly lines, patriarchal labour contractors beside personnel departments, company shrines beside time clocks. Within one household, the peasant worker's family divided its labour across the seam, wages from the plant, subsistence from the allotment, daughters to the mill dormitory, sons to the regiment, the household itself the smallest combined formation and the one that bore the fusion's costs in its body, diet and demography. Analysts who stop at the national aggregate miss the mechanism's ubiquity, and with it the explanation of the concept's durability: combined development is not a rare configuration of a few unlucky empires. It is the normal texture of development once development ceased to be a solitary national project, visible at every scale from the household budget to the world market, which is why a term coined for one country's peculiarities kept being reached for, by scholars who had never read its author, whenever they confronted lateness, contact and fusion together.


## The unnamed inheritance in comparative sociology

The surest evidence for a concept's reach is the work done in its pattern by scholars who arrived independently, and combined development possesses that evidence in unusual abundance. Barrington Moore's Social Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy, published in 1966 and among the most influential works of comparative history ever written, explained the divergent political routes of modern states by the configurations of landlords, peasants, bourgeoisies and states through which they entered the modern world: a bourgeois revolutionary route where a strong commercial class broke the landed order, a capitalist reactionary route from above, Germany and Japan, where landlords and a dependent bourgeoisie fused under authoritarian states, and a communist route where peasant revolution destroyed formations whose commercialisation had failed. Moore's middle route is combined development described from the political end: frontier industry and retained landlord power interlocked, with the seam frictions issuing in authoritarianism and war. Moore reached his configurations from archival comparison rather than from Trotsky, and the convergence of an independent research programme on the same structures is the kind of corroboration no citation count can buy.

Theda Skocpol's States and Social Revolutions, published in 1979, supplies the second witness, and the more striking because its author was explicitly constructing a structural alternative to Marxist accounts while reproducing their combined core. Comparing France, Russia and China, Skocpol argued that social revolutions occurred where agrarian states, pressed by military competition from more developed rivals, attempted reforms that broke their own administrative and coercive machinery while peasant communities retained the solidarity to revolt from below. The whip of external necessity stands at the head of her causal chain under the name of international pressure; the retained archaic form, the autonomous peasant community, does the indispensable work; the crisis propagates along the joint between a modernising state and the agrarian order it can neither preserve nor escape. Substitute the vocabulary and the explanation is the one this article has expounded, arrived at through comparative method by a scholar at pains to distinguish her account from the tradition that first stated it. The pattern repeats in the literature on late industrialisation in economics: studies of the developmental state, from Chalmers Johnson on Japan's ministry led growth to Alice Amsden on South Korea's learning based catching up, document states deliberately borrowing frontier technology, fusing it with disciplined domestic capital and abundant disciplined labour, and generating the characteristic strains, financial, social and diplomatic, that the combination predicts, all within an idiom of policy that cites Gerschenkron where it cites any ancestor at all.

This unnamed inheritance cuts two ways, and both cuts should be acknowledged cleanly. In the concept's favour, independent rediscovery is what truth looks like in the social sciences: investigators with different methods, politics and archives keep tripping over the same structures because the structures are there. Against a certain custodial vanity, the inheritance shows that the concept does not need its name to work, and that insisting on the name as a loyalty test adds nothing to the analysis the name abbreviates. The mature position is Rosenberg's implicit one: the filiation is a matter of scholarly record, worth teaching because it explains where the tool came from and what it was sharpened on, and worth forgetting in the act of use, when only the joints matter. Articles in this series have traced the same pattern before, a Marxist concept performing live work inside a discipline that does not foreground its origin, and the pattern itself may be the tradition's most reliable contribution to the common stock of social science: not doctrines preserved, but instruments escaped.

## What the concept cannot do

An authority article owes its readers a map of the tool's limits as carefully drawn as the map of its powers, and four limits stand out after the preceding tour. The first is microeconomic silence. Combined development says much about configurations and little about efficiency: it does not tell us whether a given fusion will raise productivity or squander it, why one latecomer's borrowed plant achieves world standard yields while another's rusts, or how firms inside combinations learn. The literatures of the developmental state and of firm level catching up exist precisely in that silence, and a student who expects the concept to answer questions about industrial policy's details will be disappointed in proportion to the questions' specificity. The second limit is timing. The concept identifies brittleness without dating the fracture: Russia's combination was brittle in 1905 and in 1914 as in 1917, and nothing in the theory specifies why the structure failed when it did rather than a decade earlier or later, a task that belongs to the narrative of war, harvest, mutiny and decision. Theories of configuration generally share this limit, and it is a reason to pair them with narrative rather than a reason to discard them, but pairing must be practised and not merely preached.

The third limit concerns agency and culture, the materials through which configurations are lived. The concept treats classes as weights and tasks as bundles, a grammar powerful for structure and thin for meaning: why one fused grievance finds a religious idiom and another a nationalist one, why particular leaders succeed in articulating a bundle that others fumble, why the same seam produces millenarian revolt in one province and clientelist bargaining in the next, these require the history of ideas, languages and organisations that the concept presupposes but does not supply. The fourth limit is normative abstinence. Combined development explains why late developers generate fused tasks and brittle states; it is silent on whether the revolutions it helps explain were worth their costs, and readers seeking a verdict, celebratory or condemnatory, import it from elsewhere, as they should, since a sociology that smuggles its verdicts into its mechanisms corrupts both. These four limits, microeconomic, temporal, cultural and normative, mark the honest frontier of the concept's competence. Within it, the concept remains the sharpest available instrument for its class of questions. Beyond it, other instruments, several of them named in this article, take over, and the mark of understanding the concept is knowing where to put it down.


## The pioneers under the concept's light

A fair test of any theory of lateness is what it says about the cases that were never late. England industrialised first, which by the concept's logic means it industrialised uncombined: no frontier existed abroad to borrow from, no rival's fleet compelled the timetable, and its forms grew endogenously across centuries, workshop into factory, merchant into banker, common law accreting precedent on precedent. The configuration that resulted bears the marks of that solitary growth in every particular the concept would predict. Its classes formed gradually and settled their accounts sequentially, the landed interest commercialising itself over generations rather than confronting industry as an alien import, the bourgeoisie rising through the interstices of an old order it increasingly staffed, the working class organising in craft layers that mirrored industry's own slow sedimentation. Its state borrowed nothing under compulsion and therefore fused nothing under pressure. And its politics, for all the violence of Peterloo or the menace of Chartism, never faced the fused bundle: land, constitution, national question and social question arrived at different centuries' doors and were answered, evaded or repressed one at a time. England is the control case, and the concept passes the control test by explaining its uneventfulness in the same terms that explain Russia's convulsions, tempo and fusion rather than national virtue.

France complicates the control instructively, since France was a pioneer in politics and a laggard in parts of its economy, revolutionary in 1789, 1830, 1848 and 1871 while its industry grew in family firms and its countryside remained a nation of smallholders into living memory. French history under the concept's light shows combination in a milder solvent: a peasantry preserved by revolution rather than by commune, becoming the ballast of republics and the despair of modernisers; a state centralised to a degree England never knew, performing brokerage functions for a society whose private capitals preferred rentes to risk; revolutions that fused tasks, most purely in 1848, when democratic, national and social demands arrived in the same spring and destroyed one another in the same summer. The mildness is itself data: combination comes in degrees, proportional to the depth of lateness and the density of compulsion, and a theory that required every case to be Russia would fail France. Gradients, again, are what a serious concept supplies. Even the United States, the case of a society founded inside modernity, displays the pattern at its internal frontier, where a slaveholding plantation complex exporting cotton to the world's most advanced mills fused archaic labour relations with frontier commerce for two generations, until the seam tore in civil war: the peculiar institution was not a survival at the republic's edge but a working component of its growth, retained, financed and politically entrenched, which is combination's signature in a settler key.

## Reading the source text and its satellites

Students meeting the concept in its author's pages should know what they are holding. The canonical statement occupies the first chapter of the History of the Russian Revolution, a work in three volumes published in Russian in 1930 and in Max Eastman's English translation in 1932, composed in exile on the island of Prinkipo and in France, from newspapers, memoirs and documents assembled by assistants, by a writer who had been a principal actor in the events narrated and made no pretence of the academic's detachment. The chapter's brevity is deceptive: within a few dozen pages it moves from geography and the medieval inheritance through serfdom, Peter, emancipation, Witte and the war, stating the law of uneven development, deriving combination as its necessary complement, and coining the privilege in passing, with the illustrative machinery, rifles for bows, Germany overtaking England by the advantage of its lateness, that later literature quotes. Its appendices return to the argument against critics. Around the canonical statement stand the satellites: Results and Prospects of 1906 for the doctrine in embryo, written in the heat of a defeated revolution; the writings on China and on the colonial world for the concept contested in application; and the polemics of the exile years, where combined development is wielded in arguments about Germany, Spain and the approaching world war, sometimes with a flexibility that its scholarly admirers decline to follow and that a careful reader should register as the difference between a theory in use by a partisan and a theory under examination. Both are legitimate objects of study. Confusing them is the one illegitimate move.

For the academic afterlife, the reading order that serves a newcomer best runs from Rosenberg's 2006 European Journal of International Relations article, the programme in its clearest single statement, through the Anievas and Allinson outline of uses and misuses, which teaches the debates by staging them, to the Anievas and Matin volume of 2016, where applications across world history can be sampled and the internal disagreements left visible rather than edited away. Davidson's essays repay attention from readers of any allegiance for their insistence on conditions and beginnings, and Löwy's earlier book remains the model of the concept worked at regional scale with full scholarly apparatus. On the paradigm case itself, the historiography of Russian industrialisation, from Alexander Gerschenkron's essays through the archival revisions of later economic historians, supplies the evidentiary floor on which any combined reading must stand, and students should be warned that the floor has shifted: research has qualified the extent of peasant immiseration, revised growth estimates, and complicated the picture of foreign capital's dominance, without dissolving the concentration and configuration facts on which the concept's Russian claims principally rest. A theory whose paradigm case is under permanent archival negotiation is in the normal condition of historical theories, and the negotiation is the sign of a live research front rather than of a corpse.

Study technique for a concept this prone to loose summary deserves a direct word. The single most effective discipline is the case card: one card per case, with the five passes of the method section as its fields, the borrowed element, the retained element, the whip's dates, the joints with their evidence, and the configuration of classes and tasks, each entry sourced and each negative recorded. A set of such cards, built for the table's seven cases and extended to any case a course examines, converts the concept from a paragraph to be memorised into an instrument to be operated, and exposes instantly the difference between a case one understands and a case one has merely labelled. Readers who keep their study materials in a structured vault will find the [Marxism study notes](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) companion a natural home for such cards, since the card format rewards exactly the cross referencing between cases, concepts and texts that the companion is built to hold. The examination answers that score, on this topic, are the ones written from cards rather than from summaries: they name elements, date pressures, show joints, and grade claims, which is to say they perform the concept instead of reciting it.

## The concept restated, with its burdens assigned

The long tour can now be gathered into a restatement compact enough to carry away and strict enough to argue with. Uneven and combined development holds that, in a world of multiple societies in dense contact at unequal levels, the development of a latecomer is shaped decisively by that contact: external pressure compels the adoption of frontier forms, adoption skips and compresses stages, and the adopted forms fuse with retained older structures into configurations whose classes, states and crises follow from the fusion rather than from any stage's script. Its burdens are therefore assigned as follows. Unevenness carries the burden of premise: real, measurable, and by itself inert. The whip carries the burden of mechanism: dated, specific pressures, without which borrowing is a metaphor. The privilege carries the burden of sequence: the latecomer's freedom to reorder and compress, with its costs itemised rather than waived. Combination carries the burden of explanation: interdependence demonstrated at the joints, configurations derived, crises traced along seams. And the namable claim of this article carries the burden of emphasis for every future summary: the combination is the concept, and a treatment that reaches combined with its machinery intact has understood Trotsky's contribution, whether it finally assents to it or not, while a treatment that stops at unevenness has understood only the doorway and mistaken it for the house.

Opponents and proponents can share that restatement, which is the mark of its precision, and then resume their proper quarrel at the proper places: whether the machinery operates outside the capitalist epoch, whether configuration claims survive comparative measurement, whether the concept's normative abstinence is honesty or evasion, whether the broker state's brokerage is a universal of lateness or a feature of a vanished world of empires and gold standards. Those quarrels are the concept's continuing life, in journals, in monographs, in seminars where a chapter written in exile by a defeated revolutionary is set, without embarrassment and without incense, beside the standard works of disciplines he never entered. Few ideas earn such a fate. Fewer still earn it by the route this one travelled, from the peculiarities of one backward empire to the foundations of a theory of the international, carrying its central insight intact through every border crossing: that societies do not grow up one by one, like children in separate rooms, but collide, borrow, fuse and fracture in one another's company, and that the fusions, not the timetables, are where their histories are decided.


## War as the concept's supreme examiner

Every mechanism in this article is tested most severely by war, and the concept's author knew it, having written his history in the shadow of one world war and on the approach of another. War is the whip in its concentrated form: it prices a society's combination without pity, discovering in months whether the borrowed modernity is fused into the social body or merely bolted onto it, whether the archaic base can feed, man and finance the advanced superstructure of violence, and whether the broker state can honour its debts in both directions under maximum strain. The First World War examined the great combined formations of the table in sequence and returned differentiated verdicts that read like the concept's own case notes. Germany's combination held longest among the losers, its frontier industry and disciplined administration sustaining four years of total war against a coalition, until the political archaism at its summit, a command structure answerable to no electorate, made the negotiated peace that might have saved the regime unobtainable and the seams gave way from the top. Russia's combination failed fastest and most completely, its railways unable to move grain and shells simultaneously, its villages stripped of men and horses for an army its industry could not equip, its broker state defaulting first on bread, then on authority, then on existence. The Ottoman combination dissolved into its components, the modernised officer corps salvaging a Turkish national state from the imperial wreck by amputating the archaic periphery it could no longer carry. Austria Hungary, a combination of combinations, an industrial Bohemia and a military frontier and a Hungarian agrarian half yoked in one dynasty, simply ceased, its joints becoming the borders of successor states.

The pattern extends beyond that single examination. Japan's combination passed the tests of 1895 and 1905 against China and Russia respectively, victories that confirmed the Meiji settlement's fusion of imported technique with domestic extraction, and then failed the greater test its own success invited, when a military built by combination acquired autonomy from the civilian order that had created it and drove the formation into a war beyond its base's capacity. The Soviet state that emerged from Russia's collapse conducted the most deliberate experiment in forced combination on record, importing entire factories from America and Germany during the first Five Year Plan, Albert Kahn's firm designing plants by the hundred, Magnitogorsk modelled on Gary, Indiana, while the countryside was collectivised into a new archaic discipline to fund the imports, a combination conscious of itself as such, whose strains and whose terrible costs have filled libraries. Defeat and victory in 1945 were then read by contemporaries, not unreasonably, as the examination results of two rival combinations. None of this licenses the vulgar inference that war is a laboratory arranged for theorists; the suffering inside these examinations was real and is not redeemed by yielding data. It licenses only the analytical observation that total war performs, involuntarily, the stress test that reveals where a formation's joints truly are, and that historians reconstructing those joints could ask for no more pitiless a source.

A corollary for method follows, and the five pass procedure absorbs it naturally. Where a case includes a major war, the war's logistics are the fourth pass's best evidence: mobilisation rates against railway capacity, shell production against peasant food deliveries, officer recruitment against the retained hierarchies, war finance against the archaic tax base. A combination that looks rhetorical in peacetime prose becomes arithmetic in wartime, tons, calories, kilometres of rail, and the arithmetic either shows interdependence or exposes its absence. Investigators who suspect a combined reading of being a literary overlay should be sent to the war records first, since those records were compiled by men trying to move armies rather than to please theorists, and their testimony about joints is correspondingly hard to argue with.

## The premise examined: how uneven was the world, and when

The concept's first premise, unevenness, was treated early in this article as a truism, and truisms deserve one examination before an article ends, because the degree and timing of unevenness turn out to matter for the generalised debates. Economic historians reconstructing long run incomes, in the tradition of Angus Maddison's statistical projects, describe a world in which regional differences in prosperity were real but modest for most of recorded history, a leading region perhaps twice or three times as rich per head as the poorest, followed by a dramatic widening across the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, the great divergence, in which industrialising regions pulled away to ratios of ten or twenty to one. Kenneth Pomeranz's influential study of that divergence argued that as late as 1750 the most developed parts of China stood comparable to the most developed parts of Europe in markets, life expectancy and commercial sophistication, and that the subsequent gap owed much to fortunate coal deposits and to the land and resources of colonised Americas, windfalls rather than essences. Other historians contest the comparability and date Europe's edge earlier, in institutions, states or science. The quarrel matters to this article at exactly one point: the transhistorical generalisation of the concept. If deep unevenness is largely a product of the last three centuries, then the premise in its strong form is modern, and the whip's permanent pressure, which presupposes large and visible gaps between neighbours, belongs chiefly to the epoch of divergence and after. That conclusion, reached from income statistics rather than from doctrinal loyalty, converges with the Davidson and Callinicos insistence that combined development in the full sense is a phenomenon of the capitalist centuries, and it shows the two literatures, economic history and Marxist theory, disciplining a concept neither owns.

The same statistics sharpen the home case rather than dissolving it. Russia's peculiarities appear in the quantitative record as a specific profile: aggregate size befitting a great power, per head income closer to the agrarian empires than to the industrialising West, growth spurts among the fastest recorded when state and foreign capital combined in the 1890s and again after 1905, and a structural composition, the shares of agriculture, modern industry and everything between, unlike any other great power's. A profile of that shape is what a combination looks like in numbers before it looks like anything in narrative: not uniform backwardness, not uniform advance, but dispersion, the frontier and the archaic coexisting at measurable distance inside one set of borders. Econometric work on other late developers finds cognate profiles, dualism in the older development literature's vocabulary, and although dual economy models lack the concept's dynamics, their measurements supply its premise with an evidentiary base independent of any historian's prose. Premise, mechanism, sequence, explanation: the article's assigned burdens can each be carried to a different evidentiary court, statistics for the first, diplomatic and military records for the second, industrial and institutional chronology for the third, archives of fiscal and labour interdependence for the fourth, and a concept whose parts answer to different courts is healthier than one that answers only to its own texts.


## Lateness after empire: the postcolonial developers under the lens

The generations after the Second World War multiplied late developers beyond anything the concept's author witnessed, as the colonial empires dissolved into states that inherited borders, bureaucracies and little else, and set about industrialising under the combined pressures of Cold War rivalry, world markets and their own nationalist mandates. The scholarly literatures that grew up around those attempts rarely cite Trotsky, and often arrived, by the unnamed inheritance traced earlier, at descriptions his vocabulary fits with embarrassing ease. The fit is worth examining in the two most studied successes, the industrialisations of South Korea and Taiwan between the 1960s and the 1990s, because success tests a theory of configurations as surely as collapse does, and because the literature on these cases, Amsden on Korea pre eminent within it, is rich enough to check the concept against rather than merely to illustrate it.

Assemble the Korean configuration from the literature's own findings. The whip fell first as war and division: a peninsula state whose survival depended on an American alliance, whose security establishment consumed the budget, and whose rulers therefore experienced development as a military necessity in the most literal sense, with a hostile twin across a fortified border industrialising under a rival patron. The retained base was an agrarian society transformed by a land reform carried out under occupation and war, which abolished landlordism more thoroughly than almost any comparable reform and left a countryside of smallholders, literate beyond the region's norms, disciplined by conscription and by a Confucian educational ethos that the developmental literature treats as cultural capital. Onto that base the broker state grafted frontier borrowings at a deliberate tempo: Japanese colonial infrastructure and administrative habits as the ambiguous first layer, American aid and technology as the second, then export discipline learned from the world market itself, with conglomerates, the chaebol, raised by state directed credit from trading and construction origins into steel, shipbuilding, automobiles and semiconductors within three decades. The joints are documented in the literature's own terms: the state banks' dependence on foreign borrowing guaranteed by the alliance, the factories' dependence on a labour force reproduced by smallholder households and educated by a state school system built ahead of the economy it served, the conglomerates' dependence on licences and credit that the state could withdraw, exercised most dramatically in the restructuring after the financial crisis of 1997, when the seams of the whole configuration were audited by creditors in public. Configuration claims check out one by one: concentration extreme, the state entrepreneurial to a degree Gerschenkron's schema predicts for very late developers, the bourgeoisie state created and periodically state disciplined, tasks fused, national division, land settlement, industrialisation and democratisation arriving not in sequence but in collision across the 1980s. A reader who finds Trotsky's name absent from this literature and his pattern present on every page has located the unnamed inheritance at industrial scale.

Taiwan's variation instructs because it differs at the joints while rhyming in structure: a refugee state carrying a mainland bureaucracy and army onto an island society, land reform imposed by rulers with no local landed ties, small and medium enterprises where Korea built giants, American aid and market access as the external skeleton, and a ruling party that was itself a combined formation, a Leninist organisational shell governing a commercialising Chinese society through a mixture of martial law's long archaism and technocratic modernity. The comparison disciplines enthusiasm. Two societies under similar whips, with similar cultural inheritances and the same patron, produced markedly different combinations, giant firm against small firm, because their broker states and inherited structures differed; the concept accommodates the variation because it claims configurations follow from the specific elements fused, not from lateness in the abstract. A theory that predicted identical outcomes from similar pressures would be refuted by the pair. A theory that predicts patterned difference, and specifies where to look for its sources, is corroborated by it, modestly, in the way comparative evidence corroborates anything.

The failures of the same era are equally part of the record, and the concept reads them without strain where simpler theories of policy error must multiply explanations. Import substitution across Latin America and parts of Africa borrowed the factory without the export discipline, fusing frontier plant with protected domestic markets, populist coalitions and agrarian structures left unreformed, so that the joint between industry and countryside, never rebuilt, transmitted every external shock directly into fiscal and political crisis, the debt collapse of the 1980s propagating along seams laid down in the 1950s. States that borrowed consumption rather than capacity, financing imported lifestyles for capitals from oil rents or aid while the productive base stood still, produced the limiting case the Ottoman row foreshadowed: the advanced element as enclave and ornament, the retained element unreformed and increasingly expensive, sovereignty mortgaged to whoever supplied the imports. Between the Korean audit and the debt crisis audit lies the whole postwar syllabus of development, and a concept that organises both ends of it, specifying in advance which joints to inspect, has earned its place on that syllabus whether or not its author's name is pronounced there. The syllabus, fittingly, is where this article's remaining pages now turn, from the concept's fortunes among researchers to its uses in the classroom and the examination hall.


## The geography of combination: capitals, ports and frontier zones

Combination has an address. In formation after formation the fusion concentrates in built space, and a reader who learns to see it there gains a sense for the concept that no definition supplies. The late developer's capital is the first exhibit: St Petersburg, raised from marshland by conscript and serf labour to Italian and French designs, a European facade administered through Russian absolutism and provisioned by an empire of villages, was a combination functioning as a city two centuries before the factories generalised the pattern. The Meiji state rebuilt Edo as Tokyo around ministries, parade grounds and a palace on the shogun's castle grounds, the new order literally housed in the old one's shell. Ankara was declared a capital in a provincial Anatolian town precisely to escape Istanbul's combined strata of empire, foreign embassies and capitulatory commerce, an attempt to found a capital with only one epoch in it, whose subsequent history, ministries of European design amid a smallholder hinterland, reasserted the pattern the founders fled. Brasilia repeated the experiment in concrete and glass a generation later, a frontier capital conjured by a developmentalist state into the interior, supplied by migrants whose retained worlds filled its unplanned periphery within years, the plan and the periphery together composing the true city. Capitals of late developers are therefore documents: read their strata and the combination's terms, what was borrowed, what was retained, what the seam costs, can be recovered from stone, zoning and commute.

Ports and treaty towns form the second exhibit, the places where the whip physically lands. The treaty ports of the China coast, the free ports and foreign concessions of the Ottoman and Persian worlds, the colonial port capitals from Bombay to Batavia, concentrated the advanced element in its purest enclave form: banks, godowns, clubs, extraterritorial courts, a modernity with a fence around it and a flag over it. What the concept adds to the familiar picture of enclave imperialism is the traffic across the fence, without which the enclave explains nothing about the host society. Compradors became brokers between legal centuries; port coolies and mill hands became the first working classes of their countries, carrying village obligations into union halls; customs revenues collected at the port by foreign administered services became the fiscal spine of states whose interiors they barely reached; and the nationalist movements that eventually expelled the enclaves were themselves educated in the ports' schools and printed on the ports' presses. The enclave, in short, fused despite its fences, because a host society cannot wall off the fiscal, human and ideological flows that a port exists to conduct. Where genuine sealing occurred, in the rare hermit episodes states attempted, development stopped rather than remaining pure, which is the experiment's control condition and its verdict: combination is what contact does, and the only alternative on offer was stagnation in isolation, chosen briefly by Tokugawa Japan and Korea in their seclusion centuries and abandoned by both under the whip's first serious blows.

Frontier zones provide the third exhibit and return the argument to the land. Where a combined formation expands into territory it rules thinly, steppe, desert, forest, the pampas, the interior plateaus, the advanced element arrives as railway, garrison and survey party while the retained element governs everything the timetable does not reach, producing the dual sovereignties and raiding economies that fill the border histories of Russia in Central Asia, the Ottomans in Arabia, Argentina in Patagonia, Brazil in the sertao. Such zones look peripheral and are diagnostic, because they display combination without the capital's cosmetic blending: the seam runs across open country, and the friction, settler against nomad, tax collector against tribe, concession hunter against communal grazing right, generates the revolts and pacification campaigns that punctuate every late developer's annals. A complete geography of the concept would map all three exhibits in every case, capital, port and frontier, and would read the political history off the map's friction lines. That no such atlas exists is a fair entry in the ledger of work the concept still organises and awaits, a reminder that a theory can be a century old, fought over in three disciplines, and still have its most obvious illustrations uncollected.


## A note on terms and abbreviations

Scholarly literatures run on shorthand, and a glossary article should hand the reader the keys to the filing system. The abbreviation UCD, for uneven and combined development, circulates through the international relations journals and conference programmes as the concept's call sign; readers searching catalogues should try it alongside the full phrase and the older variant uneven development Trotsky, under which much Trotskyist literature is shelved. The phrase law of uneven and combined development signals the strong version of the claim, tendential and general, and authors who use it are usually aligning with the home tradition's reading. Privilege of historic backwardness appears in some translations and discussions as privilege of historical backwardness; the two renderings translate the same Russian phrase and the same passage of the History, and neither signals a different doctrine. Combined and uneven development, with the adjectives reversed, occurs in Löwy's title and in Latin American literature, a word order variant rather than a rival concept. Amalgam, Trotsky's own recurring noun for the product, is worth searching in full text databases, since some of the sharpest contemporary discussions proceed under that word without the banner phrase at all. Finally, readers should be warned against a false friend in the development literature: uneven development without the combined is also the title of a large geographical literature on regional inequality under capitalism, associated with Neil Smith and others, which shares the premise and the Marxist ancestry while pursuing different questions about spatial scales of accumulation. The two literatures overlap at the edges and cite past each other in the middle, and keeping them distinct saves a student several confused afternoons. A last key opens the Russian filing system: the chapter of the History in which the concept is stated is sometimes cited under the transliteration of its title from editions that render Russia's development with variant possessives, and catalogue searches should tolerate those variants rather than concluding that a copy does not exist.

## What a skipped stage leaves behind

Skipping deserves one closer inspection, because the metaphor of the skipped stage can mislead even careful readers into imagining an absence, a blank in the record where a phase should have been. Skipped stages do not leave blanks. They leave substitutes, debts and ghosts, and the three kinds of residue organise much of what is distinctive in a combined formation's later history. The substitute is the institution that performs the skipped stage's function in alien dress: where no independent commercial class accumulated capital across centuries, the state bank and the foreign loan perform the accumulation; where no craft tradition ripened into engineering, the imported polytechnic and the foreign foreman perform the ripening; where no parliamentary century schooled a political class, the party, the army or the bureaucracy performs the schooling. Substitutes work, which is why skipping is possible at all, and they work crookedly, which is why the formations built on them lean: each substitute imports the interests of whoever staffs it, soldiers, officials, creditors, into the place where a missing class's interests would have stood.

The debt is the skipped stage returning as an obligation. A society that has not built its own capital goods sector borrows the machines and owes the interest; a society that has not educated its own technicians hires them and owes the salaries and the leverage that comes with them; a society that has not settled its land question governs a countryside whose unsettledness charges interest in every harvest failure and every recruiting season. Debts of this kind cannot be refinanced indefinitely, and the moments when they are called, a loan suspended, a war demanding the unbuilt industry, a constitution demanding the unschooled citizenry, are the crisis dates of combined formations, which is why those dates so often appear to arrive from outside, as foreign decisions or world market shocks, although the vulnerability they expose was manufactured domestically by the pattern of the skipping itself. The ghost, finally, is the skipped stage persisting as an idea: the bourgeois revolution that never happened becomes a programme endlessly reissued, the missing middle class becomes a sociological wish, the stage's literary and legal forms arrive as imports and haunt the culture as standards against which the actual formation is always found wanting by its own intellectuals. Late developers' celebrated self criticism, the novel of the superfluous man, the essay on national backwardness, the cinema of the unmodernised town, is the ghost's literature, and it cannot be understood as mere imitation of foreign genres, since its subject is precisely the wound that skipping left.

Holding the three residues together converts a slogan into an audit. For any claimed skip, the investigator can ask what performs the function now, what obligation the performance creates and to whom it is owed, and what cultural and political afterlife the absence leads. A case in which those questions have rich answers is combined at depth. A case in which the skipped stage left no substitute, no debt and no ghost probably did not skip anything, and the investigator is looking at simple poverty or simple isolation, conditions with their own histories that this concept does not claim. The audit also explains the concept's melancholy undertone, which readers sometimes mistake for its author's temperament. A theory in which every shortcut is invoiced, every borrowed form arrives with a creditor, and every missing stage persists as a haunting is not a celebration of lateness and never was. It is an account of development as it actually befell most of the world, late, armed, indebted and fused, and its sobriety is part of its evidentiary value.

One further consequence of skipping is easily missed because it concerns time rather than institutions. A stage traversed slowly generates its own memory: generations who lived inside it, customs that encode its lessons, a stock of proverbs, precedents and cautionary tales by which a society instructs itself in the stage's characteristic dangers. The pioneer societies argue with their pasts from inside an inheritance of such memory; their factory acts, banking laws and constitutional settlements are scar tissue over remembered wounds. The skipping society has the settlement without the scar tissue, the statute without the catastrophe that taught the statute's meaning to its originators, and must therefore learn the same lessons at its own expense or govern by forms whose rationale nobody locally remembers. Reformers in late developers complain perennially that imported institutions are observed in the letter and evaded in the spirit, and the complaint, translated, is about missing memory: the spirit of an institution is the deposit of the struggles that built it, and spirits are the one commodity that cannot be imported at the frontier. They must be grown, in conflicts the skipping society will have in its own idiom and on its own schedule, which is why combinations keep generating political novelty long after their economic borrowing has stabilised, and why no act of adoption, however comprehensive, has ever relieved a late developer of the necessity of having its own history after all.

## For students and exam candidates

This concept rewards a specific answering discipline, because examiners meet two stock scripts and mark both down: the summary that recites unevenness and stops, and the denunciation or celebration that substitutes allegiance for analysis. The script that scores defines both halves in the first paragraph, names the combination is the concept as its organising claim, and then operates the machinery on one case in depth, Russia by default, a second case in comparison, Japan for preference, before turning to the debate over law and generalisation. Marks concentrate at predictable points: the privilege explained as sequence rather than advantage, the whip identified with dates rather than invoked as atmosphere, class configuration derived rather than asserted, and the strongest objection, description without mechanism, stated fairly and answered with the concept's configuration predictions. Candidates should memorise the combination table's logic rather than its cells, since a table rebuilt from understanding survives any question wording, while a table recited survives only the question that was predicted. Where a syllabus pairs this topic with permanent revolution, the dependency between them, sociology beneath doctrine, is the highest value sentence in the answer, and it should be written early, defended with the bourgeoisie's configuration, and qualified by the observation that the sociology can be accepted by readers who reject the doctrine outright.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The combination table is built to be stolen for the classroom, and the most productive theft is adversarial: distribute the table with two cells blanked or falsified, a retained element that performs no function, a political consequence that does not follow, and set groups to detect and repair the damage using the four questions, which teaches the machinery faster than any lecture on it. A second exercise assigns each group a case absent from the table, Persia under the Qajars, Siam between the empires, Ethiopia under Menelik and his successors, Argentina under the export oligarchy, and requires a five pass reading defended against a rival group briefed to argue enclave or mixture. The concept's afterlife organises a strong closing seminar: set Rosenberg's programme against Davidson's conditions and let the room discover that the argument is really about what mechanisms are, with Trotsky's chapter as the shared evidence both sides raid. Teachers should insist on the dated whip throughout, since undated pressure is the first step toward the metaphorical usage the grading section warns against, and should treat every undated invocation in student writing as a request for the missing evidence. Assessment rubrics can mirror the article's burden assignment directly: premise stated, mechanism dated, sequence itemised, joints shown, objection faced, five criteria that convert a sprawling topic into a markable performance.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

For researchers, the concept's usable frontier lies where its machinery meets archives it has not yet organised: the fiscal sociology of late developers, the labour history of allotment holding and dormitory workforces, the legal history of transplanted codes operating through customary courts, and the comparative mapping of capitals, ports and frontiers proposed in the geography section. The international relations programme remains the most populated frontier, and its open problems are well defined: the scope dispute between transhistorical and capitalist readings, the status of the claim as law, framework or theory, and the accumulation of case studies dense enough to arbitrate between them, with the Anievas and Matin volume as the standing agenda. Historians approaching the concept from national archives should expect to revise its paradigm case in detail while confirming its configuration in outline, as the Russian literature has done for two generations, and should report negatives with the same prominence as confirmations, since the concept's borders are a research output and not an embarrassment. Journalists will find the framework most valuable as a question generator for states industrialising under pressure in any period they cover: what is being borrowed, from whom, under what compulsion, what is it fusing with, and where will the friction surface first? Five questions, asked of finance ministries and factory districts alike, will organise a better dispatch than any amount of development rhetoric, provided the answers are evidenced rather than asserted.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is uneven and combined development?

It is Trotsky's account of how development proceeds in societies that enter the modern world late. Uneven development means that societies stand at different levels and move at different speeds. Combined development means that a latecomer, pressed by stronger rivals, adopts their most advanced techniques and institutions wholesale while retaining older social structures, so that advanced and archaic forms fuse within one social formation. The fusion produces classes, states and crises unlike those of the pioneer countries, and explaining that fusion, rather than registering the inequality, is the concept's purpose and its lasting contribution to social theory and to the study of revolutions.

### Q: What does combined add to uneven development?

Combined adds the mechanism and the outcome. Unevenness alone records that societies differ, which nobody disputes and nothing follows from. Combination specifies what difference does under contact: the weaker society borrows the stronger one's finished forms, skips the stages that produced them, and amalgamates the imports with its inherited structures. The resulting formation has properties neither element has separately, giant factories dependent on peasant communes, modern armies officered by landed castes, bourgeoisies created by the state they would need to challenge. Those properties generate testable expectations about class weights and political crises, which is why the addition converts a truism into a theory.

### Q: What is the privilege of historic backwardness?

It is Trotsky's name for the freedom a backward country gains from the existence of readymade achievements abroad. Because the pioneers' results lie available, the latecomer may adopt whatever exists in advance of a given date, skipping a whole series of intermediate stages instead of repeating them. The privilege concerns sequence, not welfare: the latecomer chooses the order of acquisition and sometimes equips itself more modernly than its teachers, unencumbered by their obsolete plant and vested routines. The costs are charged elsewhere, in foreign debt, in institutions without social foundations, and in borrowed forms degraded by adaptation to surroundings that were never prepared for them.

### Q: Where does Trotsky set out the idea?

He sets it out programmatically in the first chapter of his History of the Russian Revolution, titled "Peculiarities of Russia's Development", published in Russian in 1930 and in English translation in 1932. That chapter states uneven development as a general law, derives combined development from it through the Russian case, and coins the privilege of historic backwardness with its illustration of peoples exchanging bows and arrows for rifles at a stroke. The doctrine's embryo stands earlier, in Results and Prospects of 1906, written with the experience of the 1905 revolution behind him, where the class configuration of backward Russia is already derived from its interlacing with the advanced world.

### Q: Is uneven and combined development a law or a description?

Its author called it a law, meaning a tendency rooted in the conditions of an epoch rather than a timetable of stages. Defenders who keep the word usually mean that, wherever societies coexist at sharply different levels in dense contact, pressure to borrow and fusion of forms follow with something like necessity. Critics answer that a law compatible with every outcome describes rather than explains, and that the generalised versions are frameworks at best. The careful position distinguishes scopes: within the capitalist epoch of inescapable contact the claim is strong and falsifiable through its predictions about class configuration; stretched across all history it weakens toward a heuristic, valuable but less exposed.

### Q: How does it relate to permanent revolution?

Combined development is the sociology underneath the doctrine. Permanent revolution holds that in a backward country the bourgeoisie cannot complete the democratic revolution and that a revolution begun with democratic tasks passes, under working class leadership, toward socialist ones. The combined account explains why: the bourgeoisie of a late developer is the creature of foreign capital and state patronage, hence too dependent to lead; the working class is concentrated and fused with peasant land hunger, hence stronger than its numbers; and the tasks of several epochs arrive together, hence cannot be settled in stages. The full doctrine has its own companion treatment in this series; the relation to remember is one way dependency.

### Q: Is the concept used in international relations theory?

Yes, and the usage is the concept's most remarkable afterlife. From the middle of the 2000s, scholars led by Justin Rosenberg argued in journals including the European Journal of International Relations and the Cambridge Review of International Affairs that the concept supplies a general theory of the international: because societies coexist at different levels and shape one another's development through interaction, multiplicity is causally fundamental rather than background scenery. Alexander Anievas, Kerem Nisancioglu, Kamran Matin and others built monographs and a collective volume on the programme. Most participants hold no Trotskyist commitments, and a genuine debate runs over whether the concept survives generalisation beyond capitalism and what remains specifically Marxist in it.

### Q: Can the concept be applied outside Russia?

It was designed to be. Trotsky framed the Russian case as the concentrated instance of a general pattern among peoples of the second, third or tenth cultural rank, and he himself pointed to Germany and the United States overtaking England as the privilege operating elsewhere. Later scholarship has applied the concept, with varying strictness, to Meiji Japan, the late Ottoman Empire, Kemalist Turkey, Porfirian Mexico, colonial India and Latin America, and, by independent routes, comparative sociologists reached matching configurations for Germany and Japan. Each application must prove its joints rather than assume them: where borrowed and retained forms interlock functionally, the concept applies; where they merely coexist or the borrowed sector rules as an enclave, rival vocabularies serve better.

### Q: What are the criticisms of the concept?

The strongest criticism is that it describes rather than explains: naming a fusion of advanced and archaic elements identifies a pattern visible almost everywhere, without by itself specifying a mechanism or risking a prediction. Related criticisms hold that its scope expands opportunistically, from Russia to all late developers to all history; that its law language overclaims; that its home tradition reads cases retrospectively, finding combination wherever it looks; and that generalising it beyond capitalism drains the specifically Marxist content, leaving generic interactionism. Defenders reply with the mechanism of external compulsion, with falsifiable predictions about concentration, state entrepreneurship and class weights, and with concessions on scope that this article records rather than conceals.

### Q: Does it explain why revolutions happen in poorer countries?

It explains the distribution better than any rival single account. The great revolutions of the twentieth century clustered neither in the richest countries, where classes formed gradually and tasks were settled in sequence, nor in the most isolated, where nothing combined, but in the great combined formations, Russia, China, the dissolving empires, where land hunger, factory discipline, national humiliation and constitutional promise arrived fused, where modern instruments of organisation fell into aggrieved hands, and where dependent bourgeoisies and brittle broker states could neither lead nor concede. Poverty alone predicts resignation as often as revolt. Fusion under pressure predicts the revolutionary configuration, which is why the surprises kept occurring in the same kinds of places.

### Q: Why does the combination matter more than the unevenness in the concept?

Because unevenness is a fact of observation while combination is a claim about consequences. Every map shows societies at different levels; the observation generates no expectations about any particular society's classes, state or crises. Combination asserts that the levels interact under pressure to produce fused formations with distinctive properties: skipped stages, scrambled class weights, bundled political tasks, brittleness at identifiable seams. Those assertions can be checked, contested and refined, and they are what later disciplines borrowed when they took the concept up. An account that retains only the unevenness could be written without Trotsky and usually has been; an account that retains the combination is using the part of the idea that was new.

### Q: What should a student separate between combined development and simple backwardness?

A student should separate the condition from the process. Backwardness is a condition, a society's distance from the frontier in technique, wealth and armament. Combined development is the process that condition triggers under contact: compulsory borrowing at the frontier, fusion with retained structures, and the emergence of a configuration that is not a younger version of the advanced societies. Two further separations protect examination answers. Combined development is not a mixed economy, which is an ownership policy within one mode of production. And the privilege of backwardness is not the claim that backwardness is beneficial; it is a claim about sequencing whose costs are charged to other accounts.

### Q: How does the privilege of backwardness work in the argument?

It works as the hinge between pressure and fusion. External necessity explains why a backward society must borrow; the privilege explains what borrowing makes possible, namely adoption out of sequence. Because finished forms exist abroad, the borrower need not recapitulate the stages that produced them: it can found the central bank before the commercial revolution, field the conscript army before the literate citizenry, build the frontier scale factory before the workshop sector. Each skip, however, deposits the adopted form in unprepared surroundings, so the privilege manufactures combination automatically. It also explains overtaking, as when German and American industry, equipped at the newest scale, surpassed an England encumbered by the plant of its own priority.

### Q: Where has the concept been borrowed outside Marxist debates?

Most substantially in international relations, where it anchors a research programme in international historical sociology and a debate in leading journals over the theory of the international. It has also been borrowed, with varying strictness, in historical sociology and development studies, in geography's discussions of variegated capitalism, in legal scholarship on transplanted codes, and in literary and cultural studies. Beyond named borrowing stands a larger unnamed inheritance: Barrington Moore's routes to modernity, Theda Skocpol's account of social revolution under international pressure, and the late industrialisation literature on developmental states all reproduce the concept's configurations from independent research, which is evidence about its reach that no citation index captures.

### Q: What evidence would show combination rather than mere borrowing in a case?

Evidence of interdependence, in records rather than impressions. Fiscal evidence: the modern sector financed by extraction from the archaic one, industrialisation paid from peasant dues, customs or monopolies. Labour evidence: the imported plant's workforce reproduced by retained structures, allotment holding workers, migrant systems, dormitory labour fed from villages. Institutional evidence: retained forms performing new functions, communes collecting for the modernising state, customary courts enforcing the new economy's contracts, religious networks running its welfare. Crisis evidence: shocks propagating across the joint, a harvest failure stopping factories, a foreign loan's suspension toppling ministries. Where such evidence is absent and the imported sector stands self sufficient, borrowing occurred and combination did not.
