## Formalist Criticism and Its Marxist Rival: Two Questions About Literature

Formalist criticism begins with a question that sounds narrow until its reach becomes clear. Given a poem, a tale, or a novel, what makes the work literary rather than a piece of reporting, a sermon, or a diary entry set down in sentences? The formalist answer locates the difference inside the work itself, in the organised use of language and device, and treats everything outside the text as background that may interest a historian without explaining the object under study. Marxist criticism begins somewhere else entirely. It asks under which social conditions a work was produced, whose experience it organises, which interests its forms serve, and how its meanings shift as the society around it changes. A student comparing the two approaches for an assignment should notice first that these are different questions, not rival answers to a single question, and that much confusion in textbooks comes from grading each school against a task it never set itself.

![Formalism and Marxist criticism, the dispute grid and the forgotten synthesis, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

The dispute that gives this article its subject arises only when the two questions collide. Can the first question be answered without the second? Formalists held that it could, because literariness, their name for whatever makes a work literary, seemed to them a property of construction that a reader could isolate and describe. Marxist critics replied that construction itself has a history, that devices are invented, borrowed, exhausted, and discarded under pressures a purely internal account cannot see, and that a criticism forbidden to look outward would describe the machinery of literature while remaining silent about why the machinery changes. Between those two positions lies one of the most instructive quarrels in twentieth century criticism, instructive because each side landed blows the other could not comfortably absorb, because a resolution was actually proposed in print, and because the argument was then ended by means that had nothing to do with argument.

Three claims organise everything that follows. The strongest Marxist statement against formalism was a book length critique published in 1928, written from inside a circle of thinkers who had read the formalists closely, and it conceded the formalists central point before pressing its own. Theses published in that same year, signed by two leading formalists, proposed that literature forms a series with a history of its own, standing in specifiable relation to other social series, which is a formulation that grants each side its core demand. Then, within a few years, the institutional conditions for pursuing either proposal were destroyed in the Soviet Union, formalism was wound up under political pressure, and its best known figure published a recantation in 1930. The quarrel was settled by decree in a place where a genuine synthesis had just been drafted, and the synthesis sank from view so completely that most later accounts present the dispute as a permanent standoff between attention to form and attention to society.

Readers who want the Marxist method set out on its own terms should consult [marxist literary criticism explained](/2020/07/01/marxist-literary-criticism-explained/), since the present article treats that method only where it meets its formalist opponent. Readers who want the wider framework within which both schools sit should consult [marxist cultural theory guide](/2020/06/01/marxist-cultural-theory-guide/). What neither of those accounts attempts is the head to head comparison, the grading of blow against blow, and the recovery of the forgotten settlement, which is the work undertaken here. The comparison proceeds fairly only if each position is first stated as its holders stated it, in their own vocabulary, at the strength their own texts give it. That discipline governs the opening sections. Verdicts arrive later, and each verdict carries a statement of what evidence or argument would overturn it, because a judgement that nothing could reverse is a loyalty oath rather than a conclusion.

### What question does each school take as its starting point?

Formalist study starts by asking what makes a given work a work of literature, and it hunts the answer among devices of language and construction. Marxist study starts by asking which social conditions produced the work and whose experience those conditions shaped. The quarrel begins when each side denies that the other starting point can stand alone.

### Why do textbooks so often present the dispute as a simple standoff?

Textbooks compress, and compression favours symmetry: form on one page, society on the facing page, a paragraph of strengths and weaknesses for each. Compression also drops the episodes that break the symmetry, above all the concessions made in 1928 and the administrative ending that followed. A standoff is easier to examine than a settlement that history mislaid.

A further preliminary deserves emphasis, because it protects the whole comparison from a common distortion. Neither school was a single doctrine recited by identical voices. Formalism contained disagreements about whether sound, plot construction, or the history of genres mattered most, and its leading figures revised their positions repeatedly across two decades. Marxist criticism in the same period ranged from crude reflectionism, in which a novel was graded by the class origin of its author, through to sociological work of real subtlety that formalism helped provoke. Comparing the schools therefore means comparing their strongest statements, not their average ones. A verdict earned against a weak version of an opponent proves little, and the formalists were quick to say exactly that when Marxist reviewers attacked positions no formalist held. The same courtesy runs in reverse. When this article states the Marxist objections, it states them as the most capable Marxist critics framed them, and when it states the formalist reply, it gives that reply the force it carries in the essays where it first appeared.

## Where Formalism Came From: Two Circles and a New Object of Study

Russian formalism developed from 1916 in two research groupings whose members overlapped in personnel, argument, and friendship. In Petrograd, the Society for the Study of Poetic Language, known by its acronym OPOYAZ, gathered younger scholars around Viktor Shklovsky, Boris Eichenbaum, and Yuri Tynyanov, with Roman Jakobson moving between the capitals. In Moscow, a linguistic circle founded slightly earlier brought a language centred approach to verse and folklore. The two groupings shared a conviction that literary scholarship had lost its object. Academic study of literature, as they inherited it, dissolved poems into biography, psychology, philosophy, and social history, so that a monograph on a poet might discuss everything except the verbal art that made the poet worth reading. Against that dispersal they proposed a discipline with a defined object and a method fitted to it.

The founding gesture was a redefinition. The object of literary study, Shklovsky and Jakobson argued in the years around 1919, is not literature in the bulk sense of everything written, but literariness, the quality that makes a given work a literary work. A shopping list and a lyric poem both consist of words; something differentiates them, and that something, they held, resides in how language is organised rather than in what the language reports. Practical language aims at communication and economises attention, whereas literary language foregrounds its own construction, slows perception, and makes the medium itself palpable. From that distinction a whole research programme unfolded: catalogue the devices by which texts achieve their effects, describe how devices combine into systems, and trace how systems change as devices wear out and are replaced.

The programme had a polemical edge that its authors enjoyed. Eichenbaum, reviewing the state of the field in an essay of the mid 1920s, described formalism less as a doctrine than as a method fighting for the independence of literary study, a discipline entitled to its own questions. That framing matters for the later dispute, because the Marxist charge that formalism denied history landed differently against a method claiming autonomy for a science than it would have landed against a philosophy claiming autonomy for art itself. Some formalists did drift toward the stronger claim, especially in early manifesto moods, and their opponents quoted those passages. The careful statements were more modest: literary study needs its own object, its own procedures, and its own criteria of success, in the same way that linguistics had lately won the same standing for the study of language. Whether modest or strong, the claim reorganised research. Attention moved from the author behind the work to the construction inside it, from sources and influences to functions and systems, and from appreciative commentary to descriptions that another scholar could test against the text.

### Who were the formalists, and where did they work?

Two overlapping groupings carried the work: the Petrograd society known as OPOYAZ, around Shklovsky, Eichenbaum, and Tynyanov, and a Moscow circle of linguists that included Jakobson. Both formed during the First World War years and published through the 1920s. Their members lectured, edited journals, and argued constantly with one another in print.

### What does the word literariness actually name?

Literariness names whatever in a work makes it literary rather than documentary, practical, or merely informative. The formalists placed that quality in the organised deformation of ordinary language: devices that foreground wording, delay recognition, and make perception work. A text possesses literariness by construction, not by subject, sincerity, or the social rank of its author.

The early work concentrated on verse, because verse displays its construction openly. Studies of rhythm, rhyme, sound patterning, and the architecture of the stanza gave the school its first results and its reputation for technical rigour. Prose followed. Shklovsky wrote on the construction of the short story and the novel, treating plot as an arrangement that could retard, parallel, or frame its material, and Eichenbaum analysed how a tale is made, showing that a storys effect often depends on the manner of its telling rather than on the events told. Folklore entered through Vladimir Propp, whose morphology of the folktale, published in 1928, reduced a hundred tales to a sequence of functions performed by a small set of roles, a result that later structuralists would treat as a founding document. Each of these studies exemplified the method: define a unit, describe its behaviour inside the system, and let explanation mean an account of how the parts produce the whole.

Opponents sometimes caricatured this work as counting and labelling, a taxonomy without thought. The caricature misses the theoretical wager underneath. If literary effects arise from devices, then effects can be explained rather than merely admired, and criticism can become cumulative: one scholar refines anothers description, disputes are settled by returning to texts, and the field builds knowledge instead of exchanging impressions. That wager explains the schools confidence and also its vulnerability. A science of devices must show that its units are the right ones, that its descriptions capture what readers actually experience, and that its silence about everything outside the text is a methodological bracket rather than a blindness. The Marxist objections pressed precisely on those three obligations, and the strongest of them were framed by critics who had absorbed the formalist literature thoroughly enough to aim at its joints.

## Defamiliarisation: The Concept That Outlived Its School

Among all the terms formalism coined, one escaped the school and entered the general vocabulary of literary study. In an essay of 1917, usually translated as Art as Technique or Art as Device, Shklovsky argued that habitual perception is economical and deadening. People stop seeing objects and merely recognise them, handling the world through labels and shortcuts, so that experience itself thins into a kind of algebra. Art, on his account, exists to reverse that thinning. Its purpose is to restore sensation, to make the object be perceived in its full texture rather than recognised at a glance, and it achieves this by making the familiar strange: presenting things out of their usual context, describing them as if seen for the first time, roughening the form so that perception must linger. His compressed illustration became famous. Art, he wrote, is a way to make the stone stony, returning to the reader the felt weight and surface that habit had planed away.

The idea repays slow unpacking, because later misuse has flattened it into a synonym for novelty or shock. Defamiliarisation in the 1917 essay is not a trick an author performs for applause, and it is not a genre marker for experimental writing. It is a claim about the function of art as such, grounded in a psychology of perception. Where perception is automatic, the object is consumed without being experienced; where form impedes that automatic consumption, experience happens. Tolstoy supplied Shklovsky with examples: a narrator who describes flogging without naming it, a horse who narrates property relations as if they were unintelligible customs, diaries that record an opera performance with the baffled literalness of a child. In each case the device works by withholding the ready label, so the reader must rebuild the thing from its perceived parts. The literary, on this account, is an event in the reader as much as a property of the page, a point whose implications the formalists themselves only partly pursued and which their Marxist critics would later seize upon.

### What is defamiliarisation?

the formalist argument, introduced in 1917, that art exists to restore perception dulled by habit by presenting the familiar as strange, so that the object is experienced rather than merely recognised. It locates the literary in the device rather than in the content.

### Is defamiliarisation a technique an author applies, or something else?

Shklovsky framed it as the function of art itself, not as one ornament among others. Particular techniques serve the function: unusual viewpoints, withheld names, roughened rhythm, description that lingers over surfaces. But the concept names what those techniques accomplish in perception, the recovery of sensation from habit, and it applies to realist description as readily as to experiment.

### Why does the stone example carry so much weight in the argument?

Because the example strips the claim to its minimum. A stone recognised is a stone processed and discarded by attention; a stone made stony is weight, grain, coldness, and resistance restored to experience. Nothing about the object changed, and everything about its perception did. That contrast lets Shklovsky argue that artistic value lies in the event of seeing.

The concept also carried a theory of literary change inside it, which the school developed across the following decade. Devices that once defamiliarised become habitual through repetition. Yesterday striking construction is absorbed into the repertoire, imitated by lesser hands, and finally perceived automatically, at which point it no longer performs the function that justified it. Writers then reach for fresh devices, often by elevating material that polite literature had excluded: low genres, slang, documentary scraps, the speech of the street. Literary history, in this telling, is a succession of automatisation and renewal, a dialectic internal to the system of forms. Tynyanov gave the account its most sophisticated shape, arguing that every element of a work has a function relative to the whole, that functions shift as systems change, and that a genre demoted to the periphery may return transformed to dominate a later period. Here formalism came closest to possessing a genuine historical method, and here the Marxist objection found its most precise target, since the motor of change in this account remains inside literature even when the fuel plainly arrives from outside it.

A second concept from the prose studies deserves equal care, the distinction between the events of a story in their chronological and causal order and the arrangement of those events in the telling. Later narratology would call these the story and the discourse, or use the Russian terms fabula and syuzhet. The distinction sounds elementary, and its consequences are not. Once granted, a critic can ask how a given arrangement produces suspense, sympathy, delay, or revelation, and can compare works whose underlying events are similar but whose tellings diverge completely. Detective fiction, retrospective narration, and the framed tale all become legible as constructions. Marxist criticism never denied the usefulness of such distinctions; several of its practitioners used them. The objection was directed at the boundary the school drew around them, the insistence that questions about why such arrangements arise in one period and vanish in another belong to somebody elses discipline.

## What Formalism Claimed, Stated at Full Strength

Before the objections can be weighed, the formalist position deserves a statement free of the softening that summaries usually apply. The school claimed, first, that literary study had confused itself by treating works as documents of other things: of an authors character, of an eras philosophy, of a classs worldview. Each of those inquiries is legitimate, but none of them touches the specifically literary character of the work, and a scholarship that pursues only them will know everything about a poem except why it works as a poem. Second, the school claimed that this literary character is accessible to exact description. Devices can be named, their combinations mapped, their functions specified, and descriptions can be checked by any reader willing to return to the text. Third, the school claimed that content, the paraphrasable what of a work, is not where literariness lives. The same anecdote can be told in a police report and in a tale by Gogol; the difference lies in construction, in the skaz manner of telling, in retardation and digression and the play of narration against event. Fourth, and most provocatively, the school claimed that literary change follows a logic internal to the system of literature, as devices automatise and are replaced, with outside influences entering only as material to be worked by that logic.

Each claim had a target among the schools predecessors. The first struck at biographical criticism, which had made the author the explanation of the work. The second struck at impressionism, the criticism of taste that pronounced and moved on. The third struck at the habit of treating literature as versified doctrine, a habit shared, the formalists noted acidly, by conservative moralists and by revolutionary utilitarians alike. The fourth struck at the vulgar sociology that read a novel as a direct transcript of economic arrangements. When Marxist critics later attacked formalism as an evasion of history, the formalists answered that they had rescued history of a kind: the history of forms, which the sociological method could not even perceive, since it looked through the work to the society behind it and never registered the work at all. That answer was not a debating trick. A method that treats every text as evidence for something else does systematically fail to register the properties that distinguish texts from one another, and the failure is visible in criticism that discusses a novel for pages without quoting a sentence of it.

### What did the formalists say a summary of content leaves out?

A paraphrase preserves events and sentiments while discarding the arrangement that produces the works effects: the order of disclosure, the voice of the telling, the delays and parallels, the texture of the language. Two texts sharing a paraphrase can differ utterly as literature. For the school, that difference is the discipline, and everything else belongs to neighbouring fields.

### Did the formalists deny that literature has meaning?

They denied that meaning, understood as paraphrasable content or doctrine, explains literariness. Their claim concerned what makes a text literary, not whether texts signify. A formalist could discuss a works ideas as material organised by devices; what the method refused was to treat the ideas as the works essence and the devices as packaging. The refusal is narrower than the caricature suggests.

## The Dispute Grid

Arguments between schools are hard to grade because each side keeps changing the question. The dispute grid fixes the questions in advance. It sets out four issues on which formalist criticism and Marxist criticism genuinely divide, gives the formalist position and the Marxist objection for each, and then records what the proposed settlement of 1928 said about the same issue. A reader can use the grid as a map before entering the detailed sections, and return to it when the verdicts are delivered, since the verdicts follow its rows in order. The grid is the findable artifact of this article, and it embodies the namable claim defended throughout: the forgotten synthesis, the proposal that literature forms a sequence with an internal past of its own, bound by chartable ties to the sequences around it, which answered the dispute in principle and was then lost with the institutions that might have developed it.

| Issue | Formalist position | Marxist objection | The 1928 synthesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Object of study | Literariness: the devices that make a work literary | A device described without its social conditions explains the mechanism and misses the meaning | Study the literary series as a system with its own laws, and specify its relations to neighbouring series |
| Account of change | Devices automatise through repetition and are replaced from within the system | Replacement draws on social material and social pressure, which the internal account cannot register | Literary evolution follows its own logic while correlating with changes in other series, the correlations being the research task |
| Treatment of the reader | Perception is dulled by habit and restored by form, as a general fact about minds | What counts as habitual or strange depends on socially formed expectations that differ by class and period | The perception of a device is itself socially evaluated, so reader response belongs inside the sociological account of form |
| Status of social content | Content is material organised by construction, not the source of literariness | Content carries the experience of classes and the pressures of an era, and organising it is a social act | Neither reduce form to content nor seal form against content; trace how social material is refracted through the literary system |

Read the grid row by row and a pattern emerges that the detailed argument will confirm. In each row the formalist position protects something real: the specificity of the literary, the observable mechanics of change, the event of perception, the difference between a work and a document. In each row the Marxist objection also protects something real: the social life of devices, the pressure of circumstance on form, the formation of the perceiving reader, the weight of what a work organises. The rows divide because each side generalised its protection into a boundary, declaring the other sides territory out of bounds for literary study. The synthesis column is interesting precisely because it refuses both boundaries. It keeps the literary series distinct, which is the formalist demand, and it makes the relations between that series and others the central research problem, which is the Marxist demand. Nothing in the synthesis column requires either school to surrender its founding insight. What it requires is labour: the slow specification of relations, case by case, genre by genre, period by period. That labour was barely begun when the institutions supporting it were closed.

### What work does the dispute grid do for a reader of this dispute?

The grid fixes four issues, object, change, reader, and content, then places the formalist position, the Marxist objection, and the 1928 settlement side by side for each. Ordering the quarrel this way prevents a common confusion, in which a victory on one row is silently treated as a victory on all four. Each row must be graded separately.

## The Marxist Objections, Stated at Full Strength

The Marxist case against formalism was assembled by several hands across the 1920s, and its strongest version has three connected charges. The first charge is isolation. Formalist method brackets the social existence of literature: who writes, who publishes, who reads, under what censorship, for what market, in which language community. Bracketing can be a legitimate temporary move, but the school, its critics said, turned the bracket into a border wall and treated enquiries beyond it as intrusions. A poem does not arrive in the world as pure construction. It arrives through institutions that select, finance, distribute, and sometimes forbid, and those institutions shape what can be written at all. A criticism that cannot discuss them cannot discuss the actual life of literature, only an abstraction distilled from it.

The second charge concerns change. The formalist account of literary evolution, for all its ingenuity, explains replacement without explaining selection. Devices automatise, granted; but why does renewal take one direction rather than another? Why does a literature reach down to low genres at one moment and up to archaic ones at another? Why do certain social dialects, certain kinds of document, certain foreign models suddenly become available as literary material? The internal account treats these arrivals as given, yet they are precisely where history enters. Wars, revolutions, new reading publics, new media, and new classes of writer alter the stock of usable material and the expectations of audiences, and a theory of change that starts after those alterations have occurred has explained the smaller half of its subject.

The third charge is the deepest, and it was sharpened by the Bakhtin circle into the critique examined in a later section. Perception itself, the very ground of defamiliarisation, is socially formed. What strikes a reader as strange depends on the norms that reader has absorbed, and those norms are not universal facts about minds; they are the habits of particular communities, classes, and generations. A device that defamiliarises for one audience may be invisible to another and conventional to a third. If that is so, then the formalists central phenomenon cannot even be described without reference to the social formation of audiences, and the wall between the literary series and its neighbours collapses at the exact point where the school felt most secure. The objection does not deny that devices exist or that they work. It denies that their working can be specified in abstraction from the social world that taught readers how to perceive.

### Which three charges form the strongest Marxist case against the school?

Three charges recur in the strongest statements. Formalism isolates the text from the institutions and audiences that give it life. Its account of change explains replacement but not the selection of new material. And its foundation, perception, is socially formed, so the phenomenon it prizes cannot be described without the history it excludes. Each charge grants the devices and disputes the boundary.

### Why is the selection of new devices a problem for the internal account?

Automatisation explains why an old device loses force, but not why a particular replacement arrives when it does. Replacements are drawn from social speech, low genres, journalism, foreign literatures, and documentary forms, all of which become available under historical pressures. A theory that begins after the material arrives has skipped the stage where history does its work.

Behind the three charges stood a methodological demand that deserves separate statement, because later Marxist criticism inherited it even when it forgot the quarrel. The demand is that literature be studied as a social institution among others, connected to education, publishing, criticism itself, and the formation of reading publics, without being dissolved into any of them. The best Marxist critics of the period were not asking formalists to stop analysing form. They were asking them to admit that form has a social biography: that a device is born somewhere, serves someone, addresses an audience trained in a particular way, and dies when its world does. Trotsky, writing on literature and revolution in 1924, put an early version of the case with characteristic bluntness, granting that the formalists had performed useful work on the material of art while insisting that a method which stopped at technique mistook a part for the whole. His verdict on the school mixed respect with dismissal in a way that set the tone for official attitudes later in the decade, when respect drained away and dismissal hardened into administrative action.

## The Formalist Reply, and the Objection That Cuts Deepest

Formalists were not passive under attack, and their replies deserve the same full strength treatment as the charges. To the accusation of isolation they answered that every science brackets. Linguistics studies language as a system without pausing over the biography of every speaker, and nobody calls that an evasion of reality. Literary study, they argued, had suffered for centuries from the absence of a bracket: works were used as pretexts for moralising, biography, and social commentary, and the specifically literary questions went unasked because no discipline owned them. The bracket was not a denial that literature has social conditions. It was a claim about the order of enquiry. Establish what the literary object is and how it works; then, if relations to other orders of fact are to be studied, they can be studied between defined terms rather than in a fog where everything explains everything and nothing is explained.

To the charge about change, the later formalists had a subtler answer than their opponents usually credited. Tynyanov in particular did not imagine literature sealed in glass. His account of literary evolution treated the literary series as one system among neighbouring systems, with everyday speech, social custom, and material culture pressing against its borders, and he insisted that a device or genre takes its function from the system it enters. A diary kept privately and a diary published as literature are the same verbal material with different functions, and the difference is systemic. On this view the formalists were already travelling toward the position their critics demanded, converting a static taxonomy into a functional and historical one. The quarrel, seen from inside the schools development, was partly a quarrel about timing: the Marxist charges were framed against the manifestos of 1917 to 1921, while the formalists of 1927 and 1928 had moved considerably beyond them.

The deepest formalist counterblow, though, was not a defence but an attack, and it remains the strongest objection any critic of Marxist method must answer. Stated at full strength, it runs as follows. A criticism that proceeds directly from a text to its social conditions has not explained why the text is a work of literature rather than a document. Its procedures, applied honestly, would yield the same reading for a novel, a leading article, a police report, and a sermon from the same year, since all four share the conditions invoked to explain them. Such criticism explains the historical material and leaves the literary object untouched; it can tell us what an era thought, feared, and desired, but not why one arrangement of words survives as art while a thousand similar documents perish as paper. If Marxist criticism cannot distinguish the literary from the documentary within its own method, then it has not got a literary criticism at all, only a sociology that sometimes uses poems as evidence.

### Where did the formalist counterattack strike Marxist method hardest?

Their sharpest charge was that reflectionist method cannot tell a work of literature from any other document of its period. If social conditions explain the text, they explain its neighbours equally, and the specifically literary difference vanishes. A criticism unable to locate that difference, the formalists argued, has sociology with literary examples rather than a criticism of literature.

### Did the formalists ever concede that outside pressures matter?

The later work concedes a great deal, especially in Tynyanovs writing on literary evolution, where the literary system borders on everyday life and social custom, and where function depends on the system a form enters. What the school resisted was not pressure from outside but reduction: the claim that outside facts explain literary facts without remainder or mediation.

That objection cannot be waved away, and honesty requires recording which Marxist criticism it destroys and which it misses. It destroys the reflectionist version, in which the critic reads off a worldview from a text and grades the text by the correctness of the view. Against that practice the formalist attack is decisive, and later Marxist critics of stature, from Lukacs in his better moments to Raymond Williams, effectively conceded the point by rebuilding their methods around form, genre, and mediation. The objection misses, however, the possibility that literariness itself might be a social phenomenon: that what an audience experiences as literary, strange, or powerful is formed by education, class habit, and institutional framing. That possibility is exactly what the 1928 critique developed, and it converts the formalists best attack into an argument for the synthesis. If the perception of the literary is socially formed, then a criticism that studies devices and a criticism that studies their social formation are studying one object from two sides, and neither can complete its work alone.

## The Critique From the Bakhtin Circle: A Marxist Book That Conceded the Point

In 1928 a book appeared in Leningrad under the name Pavel Medvedev, titled The Formal Method in Literary Scholarship. Medvedev belonged to the circle of thinkers gathered around Mikhail Bakhtin, a group whose members shared ideas so closely that the authorship of several books published under individual names in those years has been disputed ever since, with scholars dividing over how much of the Medvedev volume, and of the related work published under Valentin Voloshinovs name, should be credited to Bakhtin himself. The dispute matters for attribution and is flagged wherever the book is cited here; it does not affect the argument, which is what concerns this article. That argument is the most careful Marxist engagement with formalism ever written, precisely because it begins by granting the formalists their central discovery.

The concession comes first and is not grudging. The book acknowledges that literature possesses specific properties, that a poem is not a treatise and cannot be studied as one, and that any Marxist criticism which moves directly from social content to literary judgement, skipping the works construction, has failed before it starts. The formalists, on this account, performed a genuine service: they forced literary scholarship to confront the specificity of its object, and a Marxism that cannot absorb that lesson will produce sociology of the crudest kind. Coming from a Marxist pen in 1928, when denunciation was becoming the easier genre, this opening was an act of intellectual seriousness. It is also what makes the critique that follows so damaging, because a critic who has granted your discovery and still finds your system wanting cannot be dismissed as an outsider who failed to understand it.

The critique then turns on the formalists handling of their own discovery. Having established the specificity of literature, formalism treated that specificity as independence, sealing the literary work inside its construction and defining its elements without reference to the social life in which alone they function. Against this the book develops its central idea: every element of a literary work is at once constructive and ideological, formed material that carries social evaluation within itself. A word is never neutral stuff awaiting arrangement. Words arrive bearing the accents of the communities that have used them, the genres that have housed them, the judgements embedded in them. Genre itself, on this account, is a way of seeing: a form that organises experience from a particular social vantage, so that choosing a genre is already an evaluative act. The formalists device, in other words, is not a piece of machinery external to meaning. It is meaning in its formed state, and its effects on readers depend on the socially accumulated senses it activates.

### How did the circle around Bakhtin restate the Marxist case against the school?

The 1928 critique associated with Medvedev and the Bakhtin circle granted that literature has specific properties which content based criticism cannot reach, then argued that formalism wrongly converted specificity into independence. Devices, genres, and words carry social evaluation inside themselves, so the literary work is formed social material, not machinery sealed against the social world.

### Why does the authorship dispute around the critique matter?

Because the circle around Bakhtin shared work so intimately that books appeared under the names of Medvedev and Voloshinov while Bakhtins own role remains contested among scholars. Attribution affects how the critique is shelved and cited, and honest accounts flag the uncertainty. The arguments, however, stand on their merits regardless of which hand drafted them.

From that central idea the critique draws its most quoted consequence for the theory of perception. Defamiliarisation, the formalists prized phenomenon, presupposes a background of the familiar against which the device stands out. That background is not a universal human habit; it is the formed expectation of a historically specific audience, stocked by its reading, its speech, its schooling, and its station. A device estranges only for readers whose expectations it defeats, and expectations are social facts. The formalist analysis of the device is therefore incomplete in a precise and demonstrable way: it can describe the mechanism of estrangement while remaining unable to say for whom the mechanism works, when it stops working, and why. Those questions are not external decorations on the analysis. They are the analysis, continued to the point where the phenomenon actually occurs, in historically situated readers. The critique thus performs an elegant reversal. It accepts the formalists phenomenon, accepts their descriptions of devices, and then shows that the phenomenon itself, rigorously followed, leads out of the sealed system and into sociology, not as an import but as a completion.

## The Theses: The Settlement Drafted in the Same Year as the Critique

The critique did not stand alone in its year. In 1928 Roman Jakobson and Yuri Tynyanov published a short set of theses on the study of literature and language, a document of a few pages that proposed, from inside formalism itself, the framework within which the quarrel could be settled. Its argument can be stated plainly. Literature forms a series, an ordered system with its own structure and its own laws of development, and it must be studied as such; the reduction of literary facts to facts of another order, whether biography, psychology, or economics, confuses correlation with explanation. But the literary series is one series among others. Language, social custom, economic life, and the other arts each form series with their own structures, and the historical task is to study the relations between series: how a change in one correlates with changes in another, how material passes between them and is transformed in the passage, how the literary system at a given moment selects from its neighbours what it can use. Neither series explains another away. The relations are the object of research, and specifying them is slow, empirical work rather than a deduction from first principles.

Set beside the Medvedev critique, the theses complete a pincer movement on the old dispute. The Marxist book had argued from outside formalism that the device is socially evaluated material, that literariness cannot be sealed. The formalist theses argued from inside that the literary series, while autonomous in structure, exists only in correlation with other series, and that the correlations are precisely what a mature science of literature should map. The two documents were not coordinated, and their authors stood on opposite sides of the argument publicly. Yet their conclusions interlock. One says the social lives inside the form; the other says the form lives among other social series. A research programme combining them would study devices with formalist rigour, audiences with sociological rigour, and the traffic between text and world with both, and it would treat reduction in either direction, form dissolved into content or form sealed against content, as the twin errors to be avoided. That programme is what this article calls the forgotten synthesis, and calling it a resolution is a considered judgement rather than a courtesy: it answers the strongest objection of each side in terms the other side had already, in print, accepted.

The judgement needs its defeater stated alongside it, in keeping with the practice of this series. The judgement that the synthesis resolves the dispute would be defeated by a demonstration that specifying the relations between series requires reducing one to the other: that any concrete account of how a social change enters a literary system must either treat the literary fact as a mere symptom of the social one or treat the correlation as decoration on an autonomous process. Both original positions would press exactly that objection, the Marxist suspecting that correlation without determination is evasion, the formalist suspecting that determination smuggled back in destroys the object. If a worked body of research showed the middle position collapsing under its own cases, the resolution claimed here would fail. Pending such a demonstration, the synthesis stands as the best available settlement, and its subsequent fate, neglect rather than refutation, is a historical fact in need of explanation rather than a verdict on its merits.

### What exactly did the theses propose as a settlement?

Yes, and it dates from 1928 rather than from later goodwill. Jakobson and Tynyanov proposed that the literary series follows its own laws while standing in correlation with other social series, and that research should specify those correlations. Combined with the Bakhtin circles account of socially evaluated form, the proposal grants each school its founding insight.

### What would defeat the claim that the synthesis settles the dispute?

A demonstration that relations between series cannot be specified without reduction: that describing how social change enters literature always ends up treating literary facts as symptoms, or else as sealed processes with decorative correlations. Such a demonstration, worked through cases rather than asserted, would show the middle position to be unstable and the old quarrel unavoidable.

## Why the Settlement Was Forgotten

A resolution published in two documents in a single year, by authors of standing, might be expected to anchor a research tradition. Instead it vanished so thoroughly that later generations of students met the dispute as an open war between form and content, with no inkling that a peace treaty had been drafted. Several causes worked together. The theses were brief, programmatic, and published in a journal with limited circulation, more a memorandum among specialists than a public intervention. The Medvedev book, though substantial, was entangled in the authorship question and in the disgrace that soon overtook its circle: Bakhtin was arrested at the end of 1928 and exiled, Medvedev was arrested later and perished in the purges of the following decade, and their works dropped out of Soviet scholarly circulation for a generation. A synthesis whose carriers are silenced loses its transmitters, however strong its logic.

Institutional history compounded the loss. Inside the Soviet Union, the space for debating how literature relates to society narrowed year by year, and by the early 1930s the question had an official answer administered rather than argued, which made the careful middle position useless to everyone: too formalist for the administrators, too sociological for the surviving formalists, and dangerous to cite. Outside the Soviet Union, the documents were simply unavailable. The theses were not widely translated for decades, the Medvedev book waited even longer for readers in other languages, and Western scholarship reconstructed Russian formalism from the early manifestos, which are vivid, quotable, and considerably more extreme than the position the school had reached by 1928. The result is a peculiar distortion that persists in survey courses: formalism is taught at its most youthful and provocative, Marxist criticism is taught through its later Western representatives, and the year in which the two nearly converged appears, if at all, as a date in a chronology rather than as the intellectual climax of the story.

### What combination of causes erased the settlement from scholarly memory?

Its carriers were destroyed or silenced, its texts were brief or untranslated, and its middle position served neither camp in later polemics. Soviet institutions administered an official answer that made the synthesis unusable, while Western scholarship met formalism through early manifestos and missed the later convergence. Forgetting here was an institutional outcome, not an intellectual refutation.

There is also a lesson in the forgetting that reaches beyond this dispute. Intellectual history tends to assume that strong arguments survive and weak ones perish, a comfortable belief that this episode contradicts in both directions. The synthesis was not answered; it was orphaned. Its opponents never produced the demonstration its defeater describes, and its proponents were in no position to build the research programme it required. When later scholars in other countries rebuilt similar positions, Williams with his cultural materialism, the sociology of literature with its mediations, book history with its circuits of communication, they often did so without knowledge of the 1928 documents, arriving independently at the idea that form and society must be studied in their relations. Readers interested in that later rebuilding should follow the account of [raymond williams cultural materialism](/2020/06/15/raymond-williams-cultural-materialism/), which develops a comparable settlement in a different national tradition. Independent reinvention is evidence for the synthesis rather than against it: the middle position keeps being rediscovered because the two extremes keep failing in practice, each generation of critics finding that texts will not stay sealed and will not dissolve either.

## Suppression, Not Refutation: How the Dispute Actually Ended

The ending of Russian formalism is a fact its opponents tradition must state without varnish. Formalism was not defeated in argument. It was closed down. Through the late 1920s the political environment for independent scholarship contracted: journals lost their independence, institutions were reorganised under party direction, and methodological pluralism came to be treated as a political symptom. Formalist scholars faced mounting public attacks in which their method was denounced as bourgeois, idealist, and hostile to Marxism, charges that functioned as administrative signals rather than as contributions to debate. Appointments, publishing outlets, and teaching posts dried up. In 1930 Viktor Shklovsky, the schools most visible founder, published a public recantation, an article acknowledging the errors of formalism and accepting the demand for a sociological method, a document written under pressures that no reader should mistake for a change of mind produced by evidence. With that gesture the organised school ceased to exist, although its members continued to work as scholars, translators, and writers on historical and biographical subjects where their skills could be used without the forbidden label.

The distinction between suppression and refutation is not pedantry, and this article insists on it because the series to which it belongs has met the pattern repeatedly. Wherever an intellectual tradition settles its internal disputes by administrative means, the record shows a characteristic sequence: a position is declared erroneous by authority, its carriers are removed from posts and print, a recantation or its equivalent is extracted from a prominent figure, and the surviving account is thereafter written as if the argument itself had been lost. The formalism case is the fourth instance of that settled by decree pattern documented across this series, and it has a feature the others lack. In this case a genuine synthesis existed at the moment of suppression. The decree did not merely silence a school; it buried a settlement, depriving later criticism in the Soviet Union and beyond it of a framework that had already been drafted by the disputants themselves. The fuller history of how Soviet institutions handled heterodox Marxist thought is traced in [soviet marxism complete guide](/2019/03/01/soviet-marxism-complete-guide/), and the doctrine that filled the vacuum left by free debate is examined in [socialist realism explained](/2020/09/15/socialist-realism-explained/).

### Was the school ended by argument or by authority?

Yes. The school was dismantled under political pressure at the end of the 1920s, its scholars attacked in terms that signalled administrative consequences, its outlets and posts withdrawn, and its best known founder driven to a public recantation in 1930. Whatever the merits of the Marxist objections, the historical ending was imposed by authority rather than earned in argument.

### What distinguishes a recantation from a retraction earned by evidence?

A retraction earned by evidence answers the opposing arguments and identifies the points that failed. A recantation under pressure performs submission to an authority and adopts its vocabulary, usually without engaging the substance that the recanter had previously defended. Shklovskys 1930 text belongs to the second kind, and its context, the closing of institutions around him, settles the classification.

Honesty about the suppression also disciplines the Marxist side of the ledger in a way its serious representatives have accepted. The Marxist objections to formalism were strong, and some of them were being absorbed by the formalists themselves, which is what intellectual victory looks like when it is actually happening: the opponent revises, concedes, incorporates. The administrative ending cut that process short and substituted a counterfeit victory, after which Soviet literary scholarship spent decades administering an official method whose sterility its own practitioners privately recognised. A tradition confident in its arguments does not need decrees, and the resort to decree is itself evidence about the state of the argument at the moment of resort: the objections had not been carried to the point of settlement by persuasion, the synthesis threatened both orthodoxies, and authority intervened because persuasion was taking too long and producing the wrong middle. Marxist criticism outside the Soviet Union, developing in universities where no decree could reach, continued to argue with formalism and its descendants on the merits, and that continuation, examined in the comparison with close reading below, is the proper test of whether the objections persuaded when left to themselves.

What the individual formalists did after the ending is part of the record and softens none of it. Tynyanov wrote historical novels of distinction and continued theoretical work in constrained forms until his early death. Jakobson emigrated, carried the method through Prague structuralism into an international career, and became one of the most influential linguists of the century, a trajectory that exported formalism under other names while its Russian institutional home was dismantled. Eichenbaum turned to textual scholarship and biography. Shklovsky lived long, wrote prolifically in permitted genres, and in later decades, when the climate loosened, spoke of the formalist years with an irony that survivors will recognise. The ideas, in short, emigrated, mutated, and flourished abroad under the banners of structuralism and narratology, while at home their names were handled as warnings. Suppression dispersed the school more effectively than refutation could ever have scattered it, and the dispersal is one reason the 1928 settlement, which belonged to the Russian conversation at its most intense, travelled so badly: the travellers carried the early concepts, which were portable, and left the late synthesis behind, since it answered a dispute their new audiences had never heard of.

## The Parallel Case: Close Reading in the Anglo-American World

The dispute did not happen only once, or only in Russia. In the universities of Britain and the United States, a movement arose in the decades after the Russian quarrel that made claims strikingly similar to formalisms, met objections strikingly similar to the Marxist ones, and reached an ending that differed in the one respect that matters for judging the arguments. Anglo-American close reading, associated with the New Criticism in the United States and with practical criticism in Britain, taught that the literary work is an autonomous object whose meaning resides in its internal organisation: its tensions, paradoxes, ironies, and resolutions. The intentional fallacy warned against explaining a poem by its authors purposes; the affective fallacy warned against explaining it by its effects on readers; the heresy of paraphrase warned against substituting a summary of content for the work itself. A student who has absorbed the Russian dispute will recognise each doctrine as a cousin of a formalist claim, arrived at independently, in a different language, under different institutional conditions.

The resemblances are real and the differences are instructive, so the two movements should be compared rather than merged. Russian formalism was a research programme with scientific ambitions, interested in verse mechanics, prose construction, and the laws of literary evolution; it cared about genres and systems more than about the explication of individual masterpieces. New Criticism was a pedagogy as much as a theory, designed for classrooms, centred on the lyric poem, and content to leave literary history to others. Formalism was materialist in temper, suspicious of spirit and inspiration, eager to treat art as made thing; close reading often carried a conservative cultural programme, defending tradition and hierarchy through the canon it taught. Treating them as one movement flattens both. Treating them as independent inventions of the autonomy claim, however, turns their parallel histories into something like a controlled comparison, which is rare in intellectual history and valuable for exactly the question this article must answer: were the Marxist objections persuasive on their merits?

### In what respects does Anglo-American close reading repeat the formalist claim?

Close reading claimed the autonomy of the text, warned against paraphrase and against explaining works by intention or effect, and centred the internal organisation of the poem. The claims parallel formalisms, though the movements differed in temper, scope, and cultural politics. Crucially, close reading was displaced by argument and fashion in open institutions, not abolished by decree.

### Is close reading hostile to Marxist criticism by its nature?

No. Its doctrines restrict certain moves, explaining a work by its authors politics among them, but they do not forbid social enquiry; they demote it from explanation to context. Marxist critics in open universities answered by building methods that absorbed the close readers discipline, attending to form while restoring history, which is the synthesis pattern repeating in another key.

The objections raised against close reading in the Anglo-American world rehearsed the Marxist charges with local variations. Critics asked whose experience the autonomous poem excluded, which social worlds its canon silently assumed, how a method that bracketed history could handle works saturated with the politics of their making, and whether the vaunted universality of its readings was the habit of one class of reader mistaking itself for the human norm. Those questions gained force through the middle decades of the century and were pressed by Marxists, feminists, historians of the book, and scholars of empire, often in coalition. The outcome is the comparison that matters. Close reading was not recanted, banned, or wound up. It was argued with, absorbed, and gradually displaced as the centre of the discipline by successor movements that kept its classroom techniques while rejecting its autonomy claims. Generations of students still learn to attend closely to wording, a formalist inheritance, inside courses whose framing assumptions are historical and social, a Marxist inheritance. The settlement that open argument produced looks, in practice, remarkably like the 1928 synthesis: form studied rigorously, in specified relation to the series around it.

That parallel outcome disciplines the verdict on the Russian case. Where the dispute ran freely, the autonomy claim did not survive in its strong form, and neither did reflectionism; what survived was the disciplined middle. This is evidence, though not proof, that the Marxist objections carried genuine argumentative force, since they prevailed in the long run without administrative assistance, and it is equally evidence that the formalist insistence on specificity survived every assault, since no victorious successor dispensed with close attention to form. Both schools, judged by the only arena where judgement was free, were partly right in the way the synthesis said they were. The defeater for this comparative judgement should be stated as plainly as the others. If it were shown that close readings displacement owed more to institutional fashion, hiring patterns, and generational turnover than to the force of the objections, the parallel would weaken as evidence about merits, though the Russian facts, suppression and recantation, would stand untouched. Fashion certainly played its part, as it does in every academic succession; the question is whether the objections also did work, and the content of the successor methods, which built the objections into their foundations, suggests that they did.

## Afterlives: Where the Concepts Went When the Schools Closed

A school can be closed while its concepts enjoy long careers under other flags, and formalism is the outstanding case. Defamiliarisation entered the common stock of literary studies so completely that lecturers teach it to first year students who have never heard of OPOYAZ, usually as a way of describing what fresh description does in a novel or poem. Literariness survives as the standing question behind genre theory and stylistics: what distinguishes the literary use of language, and can the distinction be described rather than merely felt? The analysis of narrative into story and arrangement underlies narratology, the discipline that Jakobsons Prague years helped found and that later scholars built into a systematic study of narrative across media. Propp's morphology fed structuralism directly. Tynyanov's functional account of literary evolution, in which elements change their roles as systems shift, is used in comparative literature and in the history of the novel, where the rise and demotion of genres is a standing problem that purely thematic history handles badly.

Notice what these afterlives have in common. The concepts travel without the dispute. A handbook can define defamiliarisation in a paragraph, illustrate it from a canonical novel, and move on, without mentioning that the concept was forged in a quarrel about whether literature can be studied apart from society, or that its author ended by recanting the school that produced it. There is a gain in this: usable tools should circulate freely, and a student does not need the whole history before learning to see how a description slows perception. There is also a loss, which this article exists to repair. Detached from the dispute, the concept looks like a neutral technique, and its built in problem disappears from view: the problem that estrangement is relative to an audience whose habits are socially formed. The Bakhtin circle's point is not a footnote to defamiliarisation; it is the completion of the concept, the part that tells the user for whom the device works and when it will fail. Teaching the tool without the problem produces critics who can spot estrangement everywhere and explain it nowhere.

### How does the device differ from the worldview built upon it?

As a device, defamiliarisation is a describable operation: an unusual viewpoint, a withheld name, a roughened rhythm that slows perception for a particular audience. As a worldview, it hardens into the claim that estrangement is the essence of art in all times and places, indifferent to who perceives. The first use is exact and testable; the second forgets the audience.

### Why do the formalist concepts survive while the school that made them does not?

Concepts are portable in a way institutions are not. A definition can be copied into a handbook in another language and century, while a school needs journals, posts, and toleration to persist. Suppression destroyed the institution and scattered the carriers, and the carriers exported the early, portable concepts, leaving the late synthesis, which needed its home conversation, behind.

The Marxist side of the dispute has its own afterlife, equally dispersed. The demand that literature be studied as one institution among others in social life, in its circuits of production and reception, became the founding gesture of the sociology of literature and of book history, fields that rarely cite the Russian quarrel while enacting its programme. The insistence that form itself is socially sedimented, that genres carry worldviews in their structures, runs through Lukacs on the historical novel, through Williams on structures of feeling, and through Jameson on narrative as a socially symbolic act, three very different Marxisms united by having absorbed the formalist lesson that content paraphrase is not criticism. When a Marxist critic of stature analyses the structure of a genre rather than the opinions of an author, the ghost of the dispute is present and the synthesis is being practised, whether or not its 1928 documents are known to the practitioner. Readers who want that practice demonstrated on a living tradition should consult the treatment of Fredric Jameson elsewhere in this series, and those preparing for assessment should work through [marxist literary criticism exam guide](/2020/10/15/marxist-literary-criticism-exam-guide/), where the comparison of methods is a standard examination theme.

## Five Misreadings That Distort the Dispute

Survey accounts repeat a handful of errors so regularly that correcting them is worth a section of its own. The first error holds that formalism ignores meaning. It does not; it relocates the question. The formalist asks what makes a text literary and answers with construction, while treating meaning as material that construction organises. One may reject the relocation, but a critic who has not noticed it is arguing with a phantom. The second error holds that the Marxist objections defeated formalism in debate. The record shows absorption on one side, administrative closure on the other, and no occasion on which the objections were answered by the school's dissolution through persuasion. The third error treats defamiliarisation as a technique in a toolbox, a spice for descriptions. In the founding essay it is the function of art itself, the recovery of perception from habit, and techniques are merely its servants. The fourth error declares the two approaches incompatible in principle, as if a reader must choose between devices and society forever. The 1928 documents refute the declaration historically: the principals themselves drafted the compatibility, from both sides, in the same year. The fifth error merges Russian formalism with Anglo-American New Criticism into a single movement with two addresses. They were independent, differently tempered, differently fated, and their resemblance is evidence in an argument, not an identity.

Each error has a cause worth naming, because naming causes helps readers resist the next survey that repeats them. The first two errors flatter tidy narratives, in which schools are pure positions and history is a tournament with winners. The third error flatters pedagogy, which prefers tools to functions because tools are easier to test in an exercise. The fourth error flatters polemic, since incompatibility licenses dismissal of the other side unread. The fifth error flatters compression, the chronic shortage of space in surveys, which merges whatever looks similar from a distance. Against all five, the remedy is the same unglamorous discipline: return to the dated texts, in sequence, and watch the positions move. Formalism in 1917 and formalism in 1928 are related but distinct, and a comparison that freezes the school at its most quotable moment will grade a manifesto instead of a method.

## Literariness Examined Closely: Language Turned Toward Itself

The concept of literariness deserves a slower examination than it usually receives, because the whole dispute turns on what kind of thing it is. Jakobson, who gave the term its widest currency, developed the idea inside a general theory of language functions. Any act of speech, on his account, can be analysed by the factor it emphasises: the speaker, the hearer, the context referred to, the channel, the code, or the message itself. Where the emphasis falls on the message for its own sake, language is being used poetically, and that orientation, not a subject matter or a shelf in a bookshop, is what makes an utterance literary. The elegance of the scheme is that it locates literariness in a use rather than in a substance. A slogan, a chant at a football ground, or a line of verse can all display the poetic function when their wording is foregrounded, and a novel can lapse out of it for pages when its language turns purely informational. Literariness becomes a gradient present in degrees, not a border with guards.

That refinement matters because it softens, from inside formalism, the very boundary the Marxists attacked. If literariness is a function that language performs in varying strength, then the literary work is not a separate species of object but a concentration of something present everywhere, and the study of its concentration cannot avoid studying the occasions, institutions, and audiences that call the function forth. Jakobson himself did not pursue that implication sociologically; his interests ran toward structure. But the implication sits inside the theory waiting, and the Bakhtin circle, whose whole philosophy treated the utterance as a social event between speakers, noticed the opening. For the circle, every utterance is addressed to someone, anticipates an answer, and carries the accents of prior usage, so a function of language is always also a relation between people. The formalist gradient and the circle's social utterance are not the same theory, yet they are closer than the polemics of the decade allowed anyone to say.

Consider how the concept handles hard cases, since hard cases are where definitions earn their keep. A legal statute is wording crafted with extreme care, every term weighed, yet its care serves the elimination of ambiguity rather than the foregrounding of the message; literariness, on the functional account, registers low, and the account explains why without insulting the drafters skill. An advertising jingle, by contrast, foregrounds sound and rhythm shamelessly, and the account must grant it a measure of the poetic function, which the formalists, to their credit, accepted: their science was of devices, not of dignities, and a jingle's devices are real devices. A sacred text read aloud in a liturgy presents a third case, where wording is foregrounded by ritual framing as much as by construction, and here the functional account strains, because the foregrounding is supplied by an institution and a congregation's trained attention rather than by the text alone. That strain is instructive. It is the point at which the description of literariness begins to require a sociology of occasions, which is the Marxist objection arriving by an internal route.

### Can a text be literary in one setting and not in another?

On the functional account, yes, in degree. The same words can be read as information in a report and as literature in an anthology, because framing, expectation, and trained attention help call the poetic function forth. Formalists located the function mainly in construction; the observation that settings activate it is the opening through which social analysis re-enters.

The poetry centred work of the early school gave literariness its sharpest illustrations. Verse, Jakobson argued, projects the principle of equivalence from the axis of selection onto the axis of combination: sounds, stresses, and grammatical forms are made parallel across the line, so that likeness itself becomes a carrier of effect. A rhyme is not decoration on a meaning; it is an equivalence that forces two words into relation and charges both. Studies of Pushkin's verse, of folk epic formulae, and of modernist sound orchestration built the school's reputation on analyses of this kind, detailed enough to be checked syllable by syllable. Whatever later critics concluded about the school's philosophy, the descriptive achievements were permanent acquisitions, and Marxist critics who dismissed them wholesale weakened their own side, since a Marxism that cannot describe a stanza will always lose the argument about poems to whoever can. The Medvedev book understood this, which is why its concession of the formalists discovery was the precondition of its critique having any force.

There is a further subtlety in the early work that polemic on both sides tended to bury. The formalists distinguished between the material of a work and its devices, and they were interested in the transformation of the first by the second. Material included everything the writer takes up: anecdotes, historical events, personal experience, the speech of a class or a trade, philosophical ideas, earlier literature. Nothing in the theory required the material to be trivial or unreal; a novel's material might be a war. The formalist claim was about explanation, that the work's literariness is a property of the transformation, not of the material's importance. Marxist critics sometimes wrote as if formalism declared content worthless, which it did not; it declared content insufficient as an explanation of literary effect. The distinction sounds fine, and the whole quarrel is more intelligible once it is granted, because the Marxist objection then takes its accurate shape: not that formalism ignored material, but that it had no account of how material is socially produced, selected, and charged before the writer ever touches it. That accurate objection is the strong one, and it is the one the 1928 critique pressed.

## Devices in Detail: How the Prose Studies Worked

The prose studies show the method at its most concrete, and a reader who follows one closely understands formalism better than a reader of ten manifestos. Take the analysis of plot construction. The formalists separated the events as they supposedly happened, in chronological and causal order, from the events as the work presents them, in an order chosen for effect. Presentation can begin in the middle, withhold causes, parallel two lines of action so that each comments on the other, or frame a tale inside a tale so that the telling itself becomes an event. Shklovsky's readings of Sterne treated digression and delay as the real subject of the novel under study: the work parodies the expectations of plot, makes its own construction visible, and derives its energy from frustrating the reader's automatised sense of how stories go. Description of this kind is checkable. Another reader can verify whether the chapters indeed withhold what the analysis says they withhold, and disputes can be settled by the text rather than by appeals to sensibility.

Retardation, the deliberate slowing of the action, became a central device in these analyses. A recognition scene postponed, a journey interrupted by episodes, a mystery whose solution the narration circles without landing: each is a way of extending perception, of preventing the story from being consumed as a bare sequence of outcomes. The folktale analysis added the idea of functions, the stable actions that recur across tales regardless of who performs them, so that a hundred stories could be mapped onto a single sequence with variations. Skaz, the orientation of prose toward oral telling, let Eichenbaum explain the music of Gogol's narration, where the voice of a performed storyteller, with its gestures and intonations implied in the syntax, organises the work more powerfully than its anecdote does. Motivation, finally, named the justifications a work supplies for its devices: a journey motivates description, a found manuscript motivates a frame, a character's profession motivates a stretch of technical vocabulary. Realistic motivation, the plausible excuse, was for the formalists a historically variable convention rather than a law of art, and works that exposed their devices without excuse, laying bare the technique, fascinated them as signs of a literature conscious of its own machinery.

### What does the device of retardation do to a reader?

Retardation postpones the outcome the reader expects: the recognition, the rescue, the answer. By postponing, it stretches perception across the interval, so the reader experiences the approach rather than merely registering the result. The device serves defamiliarisation at the scale of plot, converting a sequence that habit would compress into an event that attention must inhabit.

### Why did the formalists value works that lay bare their own devices?

A work that exposes its construction, interrupting its illusion to display the machinery, teaches the reader how literature is made and renews perception of the medium itself. For a school defining itself against illusionist habits, such exposure was evidence for the theory: the device, normally invisible in use, becomes perceptible when a writer refuses to hide it.

Beneath the catalogue sits a methodological commitment that explains both the school's productivity and its blind spot. The commitment is immanence: explain a phenomenon by the system it belongs to before reaching for causes outside the system. Immanence is a powerful research discipline. It prevented the formalists from dissolving poems into biographies, and it forced them to build the descriptive vocabulary that literary study still uses. It also had a cost that grew clearer as the work matured. Some questions cannot be answered immanently, not because the researcher lacks ingenuity but because the answers genuinely lie across the border: why a reading public expanded, why a genre's audience was drawn from one class and not another, why certain devices carried prestige in one decade and ridicule in the next. The school's history can be read as a slow negotiation with that cost. The early manifestos denied the cost existed. The functional turn of the mid 1920s admitted neighbouring systems into the picture. The theses of 1928 made the relations between systems the explicit research programme. The trajectory is not a collapse under Marxist fire, though Marxist fire hastened it; it is a research programme maturing under the pressure of its own unanswered questions, which is how sciences are supposed to behave, and it is why the suppression that ended the trajectory should be described as an interruption of a development rather than as the exposure of a fraud.

## Literary Evolution: The School's Theory of Change

Every party to the dispute needed a theory of literary change, and the differences among their theories are the technical heart of the quarrel. The formalist theory, assembled across the 1920s, runs on automatisation. A device enters literature as a discovery: it estranges, it organises material in a way readers feel as force. Success breeds imitation; imitation breeds familiarity; familiarity breeds invisibility, until the device is processed automatically and performs no literary work at all. At that point the system requires renewal, and renewal characteristically arrives from the margins. Forms that polite literature had excluded, the low genres, the speech of the streets, documentary material, parodied conventions, are canonised, raised into the centre, and made to do the estranging work the exhausted devices cannot. Tynyanov described this as a struggle in which the junior line of forms displaces the senior, and he insisted the process is not a smooth progress but a series of displacements in which the function of every surviving element changes because the system around it has changed.

The theory has genuine explanatory power, and its successes should be recorded before its limits. It explains why parody is such a persistent literary force: parody is automatisation made visible, the moment a convention becomes so familiar that a writer can play on the reader's recognition of its machinery. It explains the recurring prestige of roughness, of dialects and documents and unpolished forms, in literatures whose polished centre has gone smooth with use. It explains why yesterday's scandalous innovation becomes the schoolroom classic whose devices students must be trained to notice at all, the estrangement having been educated out of them by familiarity. And it explains a phenomenon thematic history handles clumsily: the return of the archaic, the moment when a literature reaches back past its immediate parents to grandparents it had discarded, because the discarded forms, long unused, have regained the power to estrange. A theory that orders this much of the record is not a toy, and the Marxist critics who engaged it seriously, rather than denouncing it from a summary, treated it as the opponent to beat.

### How does automatisation drive literary change on the formalist account?

A device that succeeds is imitated until readers process it without attention, and at that point it stops performing literary work. Renewal then comes from the margins, as excluded forms, low genres, and rough material are raised into the centre and estrange again. Change is thus a rhythm of exhaustion and replacement inside the system of forms.

Its limits are equally definite, and the Marxist objection located them precisely. The theory explains the rhythm of replacement but not the direction of selection at any given moment. When the centre exhausts itself, many margins are available: several low genres, several dialects, several foreign models, several pasts to revive. Which margin supplies the renewal, and why that one, is a question the internal account answers by pointing to the event after it happened. Yet the selection is visibly patterned by social fact. The margins that get canonised are the ones carried by rising classes, new reading publics, new institutions of publishing and education; the archaic past that gets revived is the one a generation needs for its self image. The formalist can reply that such facts supply material while the system supplies the laws of its transformation, and that division of labour is exactly the 1928 settlement in embryo. But the school's stronger statements claimed more than a division of labour; they claimed the laws were the science and the material was somebody else's problem, and against those statements the objection stands. A science of change that cannot discuss why the change took the path it did has described the grammar of literary history and declined its semantics.

Tynyanov's own late work narrows the gap further, and fairness requires following him there. Writing on literary fact and on the evolution of genres, he treated the boundary between literature and its environment as itself historical: what counts as literature shifts, бытовых material, the stuff of everyday life, presses into the system, and a letter, a diary, or a feuilleton can acquire literary function in one period and lose it in another. Once the boundary itself is a variable, the sealed system is gone in principle, and the remaining question is empirical: through which channels, at which moments, under whose pressure does the environment enter? That is a researchable question, and it is the question the theses of 1928 formalised as the correlation of series. The distance between the mature Tynyanov and the Medvedev critique is real but bridgeable, a disagreement about emphasis and vocabulary inside a shared recognition that form has both its own logic and its social life. Polemic required the distance to look unbridgeable. Scholarship, given time, was closing it, which is the strongest reason for regretting that scholarship was not given time.

## The Marxist Side Before the Quarrel: Sociology and Its Temptations

Marxist literary thought did not begin with the formalist dispute, and its earlier history explains both its strengths in the quarrel and the weaknesses the formalists exploited. Georgi Plekhanov, the founding figure of Russian Marxism, had argued at the turn of the century that art reflects the psychology of classes, which itself reflects their economic position, and he proposed a sociological equivalent for aesthetic judgements: to criticise a work was to translate its aesthetic content into social terms. The proposal contained a genuine insight, that taste is not innocent and that the forms a class finds beautiful are connected to its situation. It also contained the temptation that dogged Marxist criticism for decades, the temptation to treat the translation as the whole of criticism, as if locating a work's social equivalent exhausted what could be said about it. Formalists read Plekhanov's programme and saw a method that could process any text into the same few conclusions, and their polemic against sociologism was aimed at that programme's cruder heirs.

The revolutionary years gave the sociological temptation institutional backing. Proletkult organisations, debates about proletarian culture, and the demand for a literature of the new order all pressed criticism toward judgements of content and class allegiance. Against that background Trotsky's Literature and Revolution of 1924 stands out for its refusal of the crudest positions: he denied that a proletarian culture could be decreed into existence, granted the formalists real achievements in the study of technique, and still insisted that their method, left to itself, mistook the anatomy of art for its life. His image for the relation was roughly that studying form without its social substance is like studying a machine without asking what it produces and for whom. The book matters to this dispute because it shows the Marxist objection in a version that conceded the formalist discovery years before the Medvedev volume, from the pen of a revolutionary leader rather than a scholarly circle. It also shows the limits of concession from above: Trotsky's tolerance was a position in a debate that his own side's administrators would shortly end by other means, and his exile removed the most prominent protector of plural argument inside the movement.

### What was the sociological temptation inside early Marxist criticism?

The temptation was to treat the social translation of a work as the whole of criticism: identify the class psychology the work expresses, grade the expression, and stop. The procedure yields identical conclusions for dissimilar works and cannot register construction at all. Formalist polemic fed on this weakness, and serious Marxist critics spent decades rebuilding beyond it.

A subtler Marxist resource existed in the same decades, though it played little part in the Russian quarrel itself. Lukacs, writing in German from the early 1920s, was developing an account in which literary form, especially the form of the novel, embodies a whole relation to the world: the novel's structure, its hero's problematic quest, its handling of time and totality, are for him social facts at the level of form rather than content. That line of thought, whatever its own difficulties, demonstrates that the Marxist tradition contained from early on the materials for a criticism of form, and that the opposition between a Marxism of content and a formalism of devices was a local product of the Russian institutional fight rather than a necessity of either doctrine. When the quarrel is taught as an eternal standoff between two essences, this is the fact that disappears. The traditions were porous, their best minds borrowed across the line, and the line itself was patrolled most fiercely by administrators and by the second rank on both sides.

## Genre as a Way of Seeing: The Circle's Positive Doctrine

Critiques are remembered for their attacks, but the Medvedev book and the writings around it contained a positive doctrine that deserves reconstruction, since it is the Marxist half of the synthesis. Its core is a claim about genre. A genre, on this account, is not a container with formal rules, a box into which content is poured. A genre is a characteristic way of seeing and organising experience: the adventure novel sees the world as a field of tests, the confession sees it as a drama of conscience, the chronicle sees it as the deeds of a house or a city. Each way of seeing selects what can appear, assigns value to what appears, and addresses an audience positioned to recognise the selection as natural. Genre is therefore social through and through, not because its themes mention society but because its very optics are formed in social life and carry an evaluation of the world before a single sentence is judged. A writer choosing a genre is not choosing a mould; the writer is inheriting a standpoint.

From that core the doctrine extends to the smallest units. The word itself, in the circle's philosophy of language, is a social event: it arrives in any mouth bearing the accents of its previous uses, it is always half someone else's, and it takes its meaning from the dialogue it enters. Voloshinov's work on language, published in 1929 under his name and likewise entangled in the authorship question, argued that the sign is an arena of social struggle, refracted differently by differently situated speakers. Applied to literature, the claim dissolves the formalist separation between material and device from the other side. If the material is already evaluative, already accented, already social in its grain, then a device that organises material is organising social evaluations, and its effects cannot be specified without reference to the community whose accents are in play. The poem's sound pattern, which the formalists described with such precision, works on ears trained by a language community's history; the description is right, and it is incomplete in a way the doctrine can state exactly.

### How does the circle's doctrine of genre differ from a rules based account?

A rules based account treats genre as a set of formal requirements a work satisfies. The circle treated genre as an optics: a formed way of seeing experience that selects, values, and addresses an audience. On that account genre is social in its structure, since the optics are inherited from communal life rather than invented by an individual talent.

The doctrine also reframes the reader, and the reframing completes the answer to formalism's theory of perception. Readers do not meet texts with a universal perceptual apparatus that habit dulls and art sharpens. They meet texts with repertoires: expectations about genres, competences in registers, sympathies trained by schooling and station, a sense of what is serious and what is trash, what is moving and what is absurd. Those repertoires are biographical and social facts, distributed unevenly across a population, and they determine which devices will register at all. A parody fails for a reader who never absorbed the parodied convention. An estranging description falls flat for a reader whose daily work keeps the described object vivid. A dialect passage reads as authenticity to one audience and as condescension to another. None of this makes device analysis useless; it makes it relational. The complete description of a device includes the repertoire it presupposes, and repertoires are the business of social history. Here the synthesis stops being a slogan about relating series and becomes a concrete instruction: for each device, name the expectation it defeats, and then ask whose expectation that was and how it was formed. A criticism that performs both halves of that instruction is doing what neither school, alone, was equipped to do.

It is worth pausing on how the circle's doctrine handles the formalists best case, the lyric poem of sound and rhythm, since a theory is tested by its hardest instance. The doctrine does not deny that sound patterning produces effects irreducible to paraphrase; the Medvedev book explicitly grants the specificity of the poetic construction. It adds that the effects are effects on historically formed hearers, that the prestige of certain sound patterns is entangled with the prestige of the speech communities that carry them, and that the very decision to value musicality in verse is a position inside a literary culture, not a law of nature. Formalists could and did reply that such additions explain everything around the poem while the poem's construction still awaits its describer. Both statements are true, and their conjunction is the synthesis: construction described exactly, and the description situated among the series that give construction its audience, its prestige, and its point.

## The Reader's Formation: Audiences as Historical Facts

The reader row of the dispute grid repays a fuller hearing, because it is the row on which later research has most clearly vindicated the demand for relation between series. Consider what happened to the estranging devices of the Russian avant-garde when their audience changed. Constructions designed to shock a bourgeois reading public in 1917 were, within a generation, set texts in institutions, annotated, explained, and absorbed into the repertoire they were built to defeat. The devices did not change; the readers did, because the institutions that form readers had taken the devices up. A purely internal history can register the fact as automatisation, and the registration is correct as far as it goes. But the mechanism of that automatisation ran through schools, anthologies, criticism, and examinations, social machinery whose operation is open to study and whose study changes the explanation from a metaphor of wear into an account of transmission. Who canonises, who teaches, who anthologises, and for which examinations: those are questions with archives behind them, and they belong to any complete history of how devices live and die.

Class enters the reader's formation in ways more specific than the slogan that taste is class taste. The repertoire a reader brings depends on the languages spoken at home, the books present or absent, the schooling available, the leisure to read slowly, the permission to treat literature as a serious use of time. Two readers of the same novel in the same year may therefore be reading, in the only sense that matters for device analysis, different works: one fluent in the conventions being estranged, the other meeting them as information about a foreign world. Marxist criticism at its best turned this observation into method, asking of any celebrated effect: for whom is this an effect, and what formation does the effect presuppose? The question does not debunk the effect. It locates it, and location is the beginning of explanation. Formalist analysis, which prided itself on exact location of devices inside works, had no principled ground for refusing the extension of location to the readers inside history, and the mature formalists, again, did not refuse it; the refusal belonged to the manifesto phase and to the polemical summaries written by enemies.

### For whom does a device work, and why must criticism ask?

A device works on the expectations a reader brings, and those expectations are formed by schooling, class habit, genre experience, and the institutions that canonise. Asking for whom a device works converts a vague claim about effect into a located claim that evidence can test. The question extends the formalists own standard of exactness to the reader's side of the encounter.

There is a reciprocal point that defenders of the synthesis should state, lest the reader row be graded as a simple Marxist win. Social formation explains the distribution of repertoires; it does not, by itself, explain why a particular construction rewards the repertoire it presupposes so richly, why one estranging work survives its moment and a hundred contemporaries with identical social coordinates do not. Excellence, density, the felt rightness of a construction, remain facts that device analysis describes better than sociology does, and a Marxist criticism that forgets this relapses into the translation exercise the formalists despised. The synthesis grades the reader row as a genuine relation: formation supplies the conditions of effect, construction supplies the effect's specific character, and neither term reduces to the other. That pattern, two terms in relation with reduction refused in both directions, will recur in every row of the grading that follows, and its recurrence is the best evidence that the synthesis names a real structure in the subject matter rather than a diplomatic compromise between tired opponents.

## Relations Between Series: What Specifying One Actually Looks Like

The synthesis lives or dies by a practical question: what does it mean, concretely, to specify a relation between the literary series and another series without reducing one to the other? Sceptics on both sides suspected the phrase of being a form of words that dissolves under the first case. The suspicion deserves a worked answer, and the history of the novel's rise supplies the material. Consider the correlation between the expansion of periodical publishing in the nineteenth century and the construction of the serial novel. The economic series changed: new printing technology, new distribution by rail, new advertising revenue, and a widening literate public made the monthly part and the magazine serial profitable forms. The literary series responded with constructions fitted to that channel: instalment endings engineered as suspensions, subplots multiplied to sustain parallel interest across months, recapitulations woven into openings for readers returning after a gap. To specify the relation is to trace exactly those fittings, device by device, while refusing two lazy sentences. The first lazy sentence says the market caused the novel's form, which explains nothing about why this writer's suspensions grip and another's sag. The second says the form evolved by its own logic, which cannot explain why the logic flowered in the serial channel and not elsewhere. Specification is the patient middle: channel by channel, device by device, with the writer's construction and the publisher's ledger both open on the desk.

A second case, closer to the Russian dispute, is the traffic between literature and everyday speech that Tynyanov placed at the border of the literary series. When a literature's high style has automatised, renewal often arrives as an invasion of unliterary language: the slang of a trade, the jargon of a new technology, the documentary voice of the report or the protocol. The social series supplies the material, but the literary series decides its function, since the same slang can serve as local colour in one work, as the narrator's own voice in another, and as parody in a third. Specifying the relation means tracking both halves: the social history that made the material available and charged it with associations, and the constructional history that assigned it a role. The Bakhtin circle's account of the word's accents and the formalists account of function converge on this case from opposite banks, and a researcher following the synthesis would cite both without feeling a contradiction, because the case itself has two halves and each theory describes one of them well.

### What separates relating two series from reducing one to the other?

Reduction explains the literary fact away, treating form as a symptom of a social cause or sealing form against causes altogether. Relation keeps both terms standing and traces the fittings between them: which channel carried which device, which audience a construction presupposes, which material a system selected and what it did with the selection.

The research habits implied by the synthesis can be stated as working rules that a student can apply without waiting for a theory of everything. Begin with the construction, described as exactly as the formalists taught, because a relation cannot be specified to a term that has not been fixed. Then ask the genetic questions the Marxists taught: through which institutions did this work reach its readers, which publics were formed to receive it, what in the material's social history charges it as it does? Then, and this is the step both schools skipped at their worst, state the relation as a claim that could be wrong: this device presupposes that repertoire; this channel selected for that construction; this genre's optics fitted that class's experience at that moment. Claims that could be wrong can be checked, refined, or replaced, and a field built of them accumulates. The synthesis, seen this way, is not a compromise position between two doctrines but a standard of completeness for explanations, and its defeater, stated earlier in this article, remains the standing challenge: show that such claims collapse into reduction whenever they are made concrete, and the standard fails. The cases sketched here suggest they do not collapse; they multiply, differentiate, and reward the labour they demand.

## The Avant-Garde Entanglement: Formalism and Futurism

No account of the dispute is complete without the entanglement of formalism with the artistic avant-garde, because the entanglement shaped how both sides argued. The formalists were not detached academics observing modernism from a library. Shklovsky moved in futurist circles, wrote on Mayakovsky, and shared the futurists conviction that art must break automatised perception, a conviction the 1917 essay supplied with a theory. The alliance gave formalism its examples and its polemical energy; it also gave its enemies a target, since an attack on futurist experiment could be dressed as an attack on formalist method and the other way round. Marxist critics who valued the classical heritage, Lukacs most prominently in the following decade, treated modernist experiment as decline and read formalist theory as its apology. Revolutionary utilitarians, from the other flank, demanded art as agitation and read formalist talk of devices as evasion of duty. The school thus fought on two fronts, against the guardians of content as doctrine and against the guardians of message as propaganda, and its insistence on the autonomy of its science was partly the self defence of scholars whose allies in art were being denounced as formalists in the insulting sense the word acquired.

The insult is worth a paragraph, because the migration of the word formalism from a school's name to a term of abuse is part of how the dispute was settled by decree. In the campaigns of the late 1920s and after, to call a work formalist was to accuse its maker of indifference to content, to the people, to the tasks of the state; the accusation required no engagement with anything the historical formalists had written, and it could be levelled at composers, architects, and filmmakers who had never read a line of OPOYAZ. A term of abuse that floats free of its referent is an administrator's tool, since it can be applied wherever nonconformity is detected and cannot be answered by evidence. The scholars who had actually held formalist positions watched their school's name become the label for whatever the cultural apparatus wished to discipline, a fate that should caution any reader against taking the polemical literature of those years as a record of the argument. The argument itself, the one reconstructed in this article from the dated texts, is narrower, more interesting, and more nearly settled than the shouting that replaced it.

### Why did the word formalism become an insult detached from the school?

Because administrative campaigns needed a portable accusation. Detached from the texts, the word could brand any nonconforming work as empty technique hostile to the people, and the accused could not answer by pointing to arguments the accusers had not read. The migration of the term is evidence of settlement by decree rather than by debate.

Mayakovsky's position inside this tangle is instructive, because he refuses the categories the polemic imposed. A futurist poet of genius, a revolutionary by commitment, he demanded that art serve the new order and simultaneously insisted that service without formal invention was worthless, that a slogan in dead language persuades nobody. His practice, the stepped line, the street vocabulary raised into verse, the poster and the poem built from the same materials, is the synthesis enacted by an artist rather than stated by theorists: social material, deliberately selected for its charge, organised by devices whose whole purpose was to defeat automatised perception in an audience the poet could name. Formalists analysed his work with admiration; Marxist critics claimed him with justice; and the fact that both claims were just is the dispute's moral in miniature. The categories fail on the strongest cases, and a method that cannot process the strongest cases without amputating half of each is a method that the 1928 settlement, with its relations between series, was designed to replace.

## The Institutions of the Quarrel: Journals, Institutes, and Associations

Arguments do not float free of the institutions that house them, and the Russian dispute was shaped at every stage by the journals, institutes, and associations through which its participants earned livings and reached readers. The formalists worked through the State Institute of the History of the Arts in Petrograd, through university seminars, and through a succession of journals and collections in which the poetics of verse and prose were hammered out in collective volumes. Collective work mattered to their method: the early collections on poetics were group enterprises, with shared problems and mutual criticism, and the sense of a science being built in common, brick by checkable brick, sustained the school's confidence. The Marxist side had its own institutions, the Communist Academy, the journals of the literary associations, and after 1925 the powerful Russian Association of Proletarian Writers, whose campaigns for a literature belonging to the new class gave sociological criticism an organisational weight that pure argument would never have achieved. Between the camps stood hybrid formations, most famously the journal LEF and its successor, where formalist scholars and futurist artists collaborated with revolutionary publicists on a programme of art as production, an experiment in synthesis at the level of institutions that prefigured the intellectual synthesis of 1928.

The institutional map explains features of the quarrel that the texts alone leave mysterious. It explains the asymmetry of tone: the formalists wrote as specialists defending a discipline, the more organised of their opponents wrote as a movement claiming a mandate, and the two genres do not meet on equal terms. It explains the timing of the end: the school's fate was decided in the same reorganisation that subordinated every independent association to central direction between 1928 and 1932, a process that dissolved proletarian writers organisations as readily as formalist ones once the apparatus preferred a single administered literature to any faction's victory. And it explains the curious fact that the synthesis of 1928 found no institutional home. A programme that belongs to neither camp's organisation has no journal obliged to print its results, no institute to employ its researchers, no association to defend its people when the weather changes. Ideas require carriers with addresses. The forgotten synthesis had brilliant carriers and no address, and within five years the carriers themselves were scattered, silenced, or dead.

### Why do institutions matter to the fate of an argument?

Because arguments persist through journals, posts, curricula, and associations that print, teach, and defend them. A position with strong logic and no institutional home loses its transmitters when pressure arrives, while a weaker position with an apparatus behind it can outlast refutation. The synthesis of 1928 is the standing example of the first fate.

The debate culture of the early 1920s deserves a word of respect before its destruction is recorded, because the destruction is sometimes misread as the natural end of a sterile quarrel. In the years of relative pluralism, the camps argued in public, in print and in lecture halls, with a ferocity that assumed a shared republic of letters: opponents were answered, caricatured, and answered again, and positions visibly moved under fire, as the formalists movement from manifesto to theses attests. Whatever its crudities, that culture was doing the work that open institutions do, converting polemic into revision. The administrative ending replaced revision with alignment. Scholars learned to write in the approved vocabulary, to cite the approved authorities, and to treat the questions the dispute had opened, how form relates to society, how devices live in audiences, as settled by texts that had never addressed them with the dispute's precision. The cost is measurable in the Soviet literary scholarship of the following decades, which produced mountains of thematic commentary and very little that a reader of Tynyanov or Medvedev would recognise as an advance on 1928. A settlement imposed before its time does not merely end an argument; it confiscates the future work the argument would have generated.

There is a comparative point here that the parallel with close reading sharpens. Anglo-American criticism had institutions too, universities, quarterlies, professional associations, and those institutions were quite capable of fashion, exclusion, and herd behaviour. What they lacked was a single switch by which a method could be turned off. Plural institutions with competing centres make suppression impractical and absorption the path of least resistance, which is why close reading faded into a successor synthesis instead of ending in recantation. The Russian case shows the same intellectual materials under a single switch. The lesson generalises beyond literature, and readers of this series will recognise it from other clusters: the quality of an intellectual outcome depends not only on the quality of the arguments but on whether the institutional order permits arguments to finish. Where they cannot finish, the record will show decrees, recantations, and syntheses forgotten, whatever the merits in play.

## Grading the Dispute, First Rows: The Object and the Account of Change

The verdicts can at this point be delivered row by row, following the dispute grid, with each grade accompanied by the consideration that would reverse it. On the object of study, the formalist position is graded as substantially right in its demand and wrong in its boundary. The demand, that literary study identify the specifically literary character of its object, has been vindicated by a century of practice: no criticism that ignores construction has produced durable accounts of works, and the descriptive vocabulary the school built remains in daily use by scholars of every allegiance. The boundary, the claim that this object can be studied in independence from the social series around it, failed even inside the school's own development, as the functional turn and the 1928 theses conceded. The grade would be reversed if a body of purely internal analysis were produced that explained, without residue, phenomena it cannot at present touch, such as the differing fates of identical devices in different audiences. No such body exists, and the school's own heirs did not attempt one.

On the account of change, the grading is closer, and the formalist mechanism earns more than its opponents usually grant. Automatisation and renewal describe a real rhythm, visible in genre after genre, and the Marxist alternative of the period, change as the reflection of shifting class contents, explains the timing of literary movements worse than the mechanism it opposed: contents change continuously, while forms change in the lurches and displacements the formalist model predicts. Where the Marxist objection scores is on selection, the direction renewal takes, which the internal model registers without explaining. The synthesis grades the row as a genuine division of explanatory labour, mechanism from within, selection from the relations between series, and the grade carries its defeater in the usual form. If research showed selection to be as law governed from inside the system as automatisation itself, the Marxist half of the row would shrink to a footnote; if it showed the rhythm itself to be an artefact of social periodisation, the formalist half would. Neither demonstration has been made, and the working literature of genre history proceeds, in practice, on the synthesis's terms.

### Which row of the grid comes closest to a draw between the schools?

The account of change. Formalism supplies a mechanism, automatisation and renewal, that orders the record of genres impressively, while the Marxist objection correctly identifies selection, the direction renewal takes, as lying beyond the mechanism's reach. Neither side's account displaces the other's, and serious genre history quietly uses both, which is the synthesis in working clothes.

A pattern worth naming emerges from these first two grades, because it will govern the rest. In each row the school that defined the phenomenon more exactly tends to win the description, and the school that insisted on the phenomenon's relations tends to win the explanation, and the dispute persists in survey accounts only because description and explanation are graded as if they were the same contest. Formalism described devices better than anyone; Marxism explained their careers better than anyone; and the pretence that one side must take both prizes is a relic of the polemical situation, in which institutions were competing for the whole territory of literary study. Scholarship is not obliged to inherit the pretences of a turf war. It is obliged to inherit the results, and the results, row by row, describe a division of labour that the participants themselves had recognised by 1928.

## Grading the Dispute, Remaining Rows: The Reader and the Status of Content

On the treatment of the reader, the Marxist objection is graded as right in its central claim and incomplete in its usual development. The claim, that perception of devices depends on socially formed expectations, has been confirmed by every subsequent discipline that studied reading empirically, from reception theory to the history of reading, and it converts defamiliarisation from a universal psychology into a historical relation, which is a strengthening rather than a refutation of the formalist discovery. The incompleteness lies in the use often made of the claim, as if locating an audience's formation explained the work that plays on it. Formation explains the availability of effects; construction explains their specific organisation and force, and a grading that awarded the row outright to either side would falsify the phenomenon. The defeater for this grade is empirical in shape: if audiences with sharply different formations were shown to register the same devices with the same force, the social formation thesis would fail at its centre. The evidence runs the other way, through every documented case of a work's effects migrating as its readership changed.

On the status of social content, the row on which popular accounts imagine the whole quarrel to turn, the verdict is that both founding slogans fail and the synthesis's formulation is the only one that survives contact with actual works. The formalist slogan, that content is material and literariness lies in construction, survives as a warning against paraphrase but fails as a description, because material arrives accented, as the circle showed, and construction works on those accents as much as on events. The reflectionist slogan, that content expresses a class position which criticism should identify and grade, fails more completely: it cannot distinguish works from documents, it cannot register construction, and its historical products are the weakest criticism either tradition produced. What survives is the middle formulation, in which social material is refracted through a literary system that transforms it according to its own state and laws, and in which the refraction, not the raw material and not the sealed system, is the object of study. Refraction is a metaphor, and the synthesis's unfinished business is the conversion of the metaphor into case by case specification of the kind sketched in the section on relations between series. The grade for the row would be reversed if reflectionist method produced, anywhere, a body of readings that handled construction as well as it handled content; its absence across a century is itself a finding.

### By what standard does the settlement apportion credit between the schools?

It grades description to the formalists and explanation to their critics, row by row, with no row awarded outright. The object is literary, as formalism demanded, and its career is social, as Marxism demanded; change has an internal mechanism and an external selection; the reader is formed and the construction is specific. The synthesis is the grade sheet, not a compromise pasted over it.

That last formulation needs care, because the extra question it answers is the one students most often bring to the dispute: if the synthesis grades the schools rather than choosing between them, is the grading itself a theory or merely a referee's scorecard? The answer is that the synthesis makes claims of its own, falsifiable in the way this article has repeatedly specified, and the scorecard is their consequence. It claims that literary facts form a system with internal relations that no social fact translates without remainder. It claims that the system stands in correlations with other systems which research can specify. It claims that perception, the hinge phenomenon, is where the systems interlock, since devices act on formed expectations. Each claim excludes a real alternative, the reductionist programme on one side and the sealed autonomy programme on the other, and each could be damaged by the demonstrations named as defeaters. A referee's scorecard excludes nothing and risks nothing. The synthesis risks exactly the things its opponents, from their opposed camps, always said it risked, which is why their shared objection, that the middle must collapse into one side or the other, is the right test and why a century of practice that has not collapsed it counts as evidence in its favour.

## The Road West: Prague, Structuralism, and the Distorted Record

The suppression scattered the school's members, and the scattering determined what the wider world would learn of the dispute. Jakobson carried the method to Prague, where the linguistic circle founded in 1926 developed structural poetics in dialogue with Czech scholars such as Jan Mukarovsky. The Prague development is itself a chapter of the synthesis's underground life, because Mukarovsky's aesthetics treated the work as a sign functioning within social norms, foregrounded the audience's horizon of expectations, and analysed how aesthetic norms are made, broken, and remade in society. A reader who sets Mukarovsky beside the 1928 theses sees the research programme continuing under another flag, in a city where no decree had yet forbidden it. From Prague the line runs to Paris structuralism and to the international narratology of the postwar decades, by which route formalist devices entered world scholarship thoroughly detached from the Russian argument that had given them their problems. The concepts arrived as tools of a science of literature; the dispute about their social life stayed behind in untranslated Russian texts.

Translation history is therefore not a bibliographic footnote to this dispute but one of its causes. The early manifestos, vivid and brief, were translated and anthologised relatively early, fixing the Western image of formalism at its most provocative. The theses of 1928 waited decades for wide circulation in English. The Medvedev book waited longer still, and arrived entangled in the authorship controversy, which consumed much of the attention its argument deserved. Bakhtin's own major works reached Western readers from the 1960s onward in a sequence dictated by chance survival of manuscripts, so that the circle's thought was absorbed piecemeal, as a philosophy of dialogue and the novel, rarely connected back to the 1928 engagement with formalism that had been its proving ground. The result is that two generations of Western scholars reenacted the dispute in ignorance of its settlement, with structuralists cast as formalism reborn and their Marxist critics cast as the objection reborn, each side rediscovering positions whose reconciliation sat in a Leningrad journal and a silenced circle's book. Intellectual history written from translations is a history of what happened to travel, and this dispute is among its clearest warnings.

### Why did Western scholarship reenact a dispute that had already been settled in print?

Because the settlement did not travel. Manifestos were translated early and set the image of the school, while the theses and the Bakhtin circle's critique circulated late, partially, and under other descriptions. Scholars working from the translated record met a young, extreme formalism and no synthesis, and rebuilt both the quarrel and, eventually, its middle position from scratch.

The Western reenactment had one advantage the original lacked: time. Running across decades in open institutions, the argument between structural description and social explanation in France, Britain, and the United States produced the worked cases the synthesis had always needed. Reception studies documented audiences and their formations in archival detail. The sociology of literature mapped publishing, reviewing, and canon formation. Feminist and postcolonial critics demonstrated, on specific texts, how devices presuppose readers positioned by gender and empire, and how the supposedly universal effects of canonical works were effects on the readers institutions had trained. None of these fields needed to cite 1928 to vindicate its structure, and their convergence on relation without reduction, achieved independently and at length, is the strongest external evidence that the forgotten synthesis described the subject correctly. A position that keeps being rebuilt by people who have never seen it is not a diplomatic fiction. It is a shape in the material that research keeps striking against.

## Later Marxist Criticism: The Absorption of the Formalist Lesson

The surest evidence that the formalist objection landed is the transformation of Marxist criticism itself in the decades after the quarrel. The reflectionism the formalists had mocked did not disappear, since it remained useful to administrators, but the Marxist critics who mattered rebuilt their methods around the very thing the objection demanded: an account of form. Lukacs made genre the carrier of historical meaning, arguing that the novel's structure, its problematic hero, its stretched and ironised time, embodies a whole epoch's relation to the world more truthfully than any statement its characters utter. Lucien Goldmann built a genetic structuralism in which the significant structures of works correspond to the mental structures of social groups, a method that stands or falls by the precision of its formal descriptions. Pierre Macherey, writing in the Althusserian orbit, argued that a work is not a reflection but a production, that it works its ideological material into a shaped object whose gaps, silences, and contradictions are its most revealing features, and that criticism should attend to what the work cannot say. Each of these is a Marxism that has passed through the formalist fire and come out describing constructions.

Williams completes the pattern in the British line, and his case is the clearest because he named the problem himself. Cultural materialism, his term for the position he reached, refuses both the sealed work and the dissolved work: literature is a material practice, made of specific forms, conventions, and institutions, and its analysis requires the exact description of those forms together with the history of the social life they organise. His concept of structures of feeling, the lived quality of a period's experience before it hardens into doctrine, is a tool for catching social content at the level where form registers it, in rhythm, tone, and convention rather than in paraphrasable opinion. A reader who places Williams beside the theses of 1928 will find the family resemblance unmistakable, and Williams, who arrived at his position through the English argument with close reading rather than through the Russian texts, is another independent witness that the synthesis describes a real destination. The road differed; the terminus, form studied exactly inside its social relations, is recognisably the same.

### What did later Marxist critics change in response to the formalist challenge?

They rebuilt around form. Genre, structure, silence, and convention replaced content paraphrase as the objects of analysis, in Lukacs on the novel, Goldmann on significant structures, Macherey on the work as production, and Williams on forms and institutions. The change concedes the formalist objection in practice while keeping the Marxist explanatory ambition intact.

The absorption ran in the other direction as well, and symmetry requires recording it. Scholars formed by formalism and its structuralist heirs increasingly found their descriptions demanding social completion. Narratology, having mapped the machinery of narrative with great refinement, turned to the questions its machinery raised: who narrates to whom, under what authority, with which readers implied, and how those arrangements encode social relations. Stylistics, the direct heir of the verse studies, developed branches devoted to the social life of registers and dialects. Even the most technical fields discovered their border with history, because a device described completely enough eventually raises the question of its career, and careers happen in the world. The two traditions, left to work in open institutions, converged on the middle from both sides, which is what a settlement looks like when it is achieved by research rather than proclaimed by a document. The 1928 texts remain significant not because later scholars obeyed them but because they stated, at the very beginning, the destination the whole century's work would reach, and stated it in the teeth of a situation that was about to make stating it impossible.

## Genre by Genre: The Dispute Changes Shape Between Verse and Prose

A comparison that treats literature as one thing will miss a modulation that both schools understood: the dispute takes different shapes in verse and in prose, and a student's verdict should be calibrated accordingly. In verse, the formalist advantage is at its maximum. Sound, metre, rhyme, and stanza are constructions whose effects can be described with near experimental precision, and the social series, while it governs the prestige of forms and the training of ears, enters the individual analysis at a distance. Marxist accounts of particular poems that skip the verse mechanics are visibly helpless, reduced to paraphrasing a content the poem's form may be ironising, whereas formalist accounts of verse, whatever their philosophy, leave the reader able to hear more. The honest Marxist response in this territory is the circle's: grant the mechanics, then show how the ear that receives them was formed, and how the choice of a metre or a register is itself an evaluative act inside a literary culture.

In prose fiction the balance shifts, for reasons internal to the object. The novel is a capacious, socially saturated form: it imports documents, dialects, professional jargons, and the speech of classes into its fabric as a condition of its realism, and its great practitioners have always known that the importation is the art. Here the Marxist questions about material, its origins, its charges, its exclusions, bite directly on the construction, and the formalist analysis of plot and narration, while indispensable, describes a machine whose fuel is conspicuously social. The Bakhtin circle's own masterwork was a study of Dostoevsky's prose, and its central concept, the polyphonic novel in which voices carry whole worldviews in dialogue, is precisely a formal discovery made by sociological means: the device is a structure of social voices, and neither a device catalogue nor a content summary could have found it. Students choosing a side for an essay on a novel should know that the strongest work on novels, from whichever camp, tends to be synthesis work, because the object itself refuses to be only form or only document.

### Does the balance between the schools shift between poetry and the novel?

Yes. In verse the formalist description of sound and metre is hardest to replace and social analysis enters at a distance, through the formation of ears and the prestige of forms. In the novel, socially charged material is the fabric itself, and the strongest analyses, the circle on Dostoevsky among them, are built by both methods at once.

Drama and film extend the modulation in directions this article can only indicate. A play exists in performance before audiences whose collective reactions are part of its machinery, and a film is an industrial product whose devices are inseparable from its financing and distribution, so that in both arts the social series presses closer to the construction than in print literature, and purely internal accounts thin out fastest. The formalist inheritance survives there too, in the exact description of montage, mise en scene, and verse delivery, but it survives as one term of a relation whose other term is an industry or an auditorium. The general law the modulation suggests is worth stating: the more collective and institutional the art's mode of existence, the more the synthesis's relational half dominates, and the more private and verbal the art, the more the formalist half earns its keep. A verdict that ignores the modulation and crowns one school for all literature has graded a slogan rather than a dispute.

## The Question of Value: Where Both Schools Fall Silent

Every critical method carries an implicit theory of value, and the dispute is at its most revealing where both theories run out. Formalism's account of value is functional: a work is strong where its devices perform their work, where perception is renewed and construction is integrated, and weak where devices are inert, borrowed without transformation, or at war with the whole. The account has the virtue of connecting judgement to description, so that praise and blame can be argued from the text. Its limit is historical: a device that renews perception in one repertoire may be invisible in another, so functional value is silently indexed to an audience the theory does not supply. The school's judgements, in practice, were often superb, because its practitioners had exquisite repertoires of their own; the theory could not say why those repertoires should govern, and when the audience changed, as audiences do, the judgements travelled uncertainly.

Marxist criticism's account of value ran through truth and typicality: a work is strong where it grasps the real relations of its world, renders the typical in the individual, and enlarges the reader's sense of the possible, and weak where it falsifies, prettifies, or shrinks experience to a doctrine. This account too connects judgement to something arguable, the adequacy of a work's grasp, and it explains the tradition's admiration for writers whose politics it rejected, Balzac above all, whose construction of a society outlasted his legitimist sympathies because the grasp was truer than the sympathy. Its limit is the mirror of formalism's: grasp is judged through form, through the density and honesty of the rendering, so the Marxist verdict quietly depends on the formalist description it cannot supply from its own resources, and where the tradition forgot the dependence it slid into grading conclusions, the reflectionist failure again. The synthesis suggests the obvious completion: value is a relation between a construction's achieved density and a historically situated audience's capacity to be changed by it, which is why judgements must be re-argued for new audiences rather than inherited as verdicts, and why the canon is a standing argument rather than a settled estate.

### Can either school explain why a great work outlives its first audience?

Neither can do it alone. Functional value explains a construction's density but not its address to readers unborn; typicality explains its grasp of a world but not the construction that carries the grasp across centuries. Survival looks like a relation renewed by successive audiences, each finding its own formation answered by the construction's surplus of organisation.

The canon question follows from the value question and shows the synthesis earning its keep on a live problem. A canon, the set of works institutions teach and reprint, is on the formalist side a record of constructions that kept renewing perception, and on the Marxist side a record of selections made by institutions serving particular publics. Both records are real, and their interaction is the phenomenon: institutions select among constructions, constructions resist or reward selection, and the works that dominate a century are those whose organisation survives repeated reselection by changing audiences for changing reasons. Shakespeare's career, court poet, popular dramatist, national bard, colonial export, and world author of mutually hostile readings, is unintelligible as either pure device or pure ideology and perfectly legible as a relation between an unusually capacious construction and a succession of institutions that kept finding uses for it. A criticism trained by the synthesis will study canons in exactly those terms, and will treat both the piety that sees only greatness and the suspicion that sees only power as half descriptions of a two sided fact.

## Choosing and Combining: Guidance for the Working Student

The comparison yields practical guidance, and students facing an assignment deserve it stated without coyness. Begin any analysis with the formalist discipline, whatever the essay's eventual allegiance, because description is the toll every interpretation must pay: name the devices, locate the construction, establish what the work does before announcing what it means. Essays that skip the toll are recognisable at once, since their claims about a text could be made, word for word, about its summary. Then bring the Marxist questions to the described object, in their strong rather than their slogan form: whose material is this, charged how; which audience does the construction presuppose, formed where; through which institutions did the work travel, and what did the travelling do to it? Then, and this is the step that separates synthesis work from mere eclecticism, state the relations as claims: the device rests on that repertoire; the genre's optics fit the experience of that class at that juncture; the channel favoured that construction. A relation stated as a claim can be examined, evidenced, and marked; a vague gesture at context cannot.

Eclecticism deserves its warning, because combining the schools badly is worse than using either well. The bad combination alternates paragraphs, a page of device spotting followed by a page of social background, with no sentence in which the two meet. Markers recognise the pattern and grade it as two half essays stapled together. The good combination is recognisable by its sentences, in which a construction and its condition appear in a single claim with the direction of dependence specified and the evidence for it cited. Length is no obstacle: the discipline operates in a paragraph as well as in a thesis, since it is a property of claims, not of word counts. Teachers setting the comparison as an examination topic are, knowingly or not, testing for exactly this property, and the examination guidance elsewhere in this series, including the drills collected through the ReportMedic past paper explorer at [the UPSC previous year question explorer](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html), rewards answers that stage the dispute as a problem with a settlement rather than as a pageant of positions. The settlement, to repeat it once more in its shortest form, is that literature keeps an internal past of its own while remaining bound, by ties research can chart, to the rest of social life, and that criticism is the specification.

### What is the difference between combining the methods and merely alternating them?

Alternation staples a formalist page to a sociological page and lets neither touch the other. Combination writes sentences in which a construction and its social condition appear in one claim, with the dependence specified and evidenced. The first is two half methods; the second is the synthesis practised at the scale of the paragraph.

There remains the honest question of allegiance, since students are often asked not merely to compare but to decide. The article's verdict, delivered across the grading sections, is that decision at the level of schools is the wrong unit. Decide at the level of questions. For questions about how a work is made and why it works, the formalist inheritance is the stronger instrument, and no loyalty should forbid its use. For questions about why works are made thus, here, for these readers, and with these effects across time, the Marxist inheritance is stronger. For the questions that matter most in serious study, how making and conditions interlock in this work, in this genre, at this moment, neither inheritance suffices and the synthesis is the only honest address. A student who writes that, with the cases to support it, has not evaded the decision; the student has located it at the level where decisions can be defended, and has the 1928 documents as warrant that the location was found by the disputants themselves before their argument was taken from them.

## The Primary Texts: A Reading Path Through the Quarrel

Students meet this dispute through summaries, and summaries, as the opening sections argued, freeze the schools at their most quotable. The remedy is a reading path through the primary texts in the order that shows the positions moving. Begin with Shklovsky's 1917 essay on art as device, short enough to read entire and vivid enough to explain the school's excitement: the theory of perception, the stone made stony, the Tolstoy examples, all present before the polemics hardened. Follow it with Eichenbaum's account of how Gogol's tale is made, the finest single demonstration of the prose method, in which a story's whole effect is traced to the performed voice of its telling. Then read Tynyanov on literary evolution, where the system learns to speak of functions, struggle, and the shifting boundary between literature and everyday life, and notice how far the position has travelled from the manifestos. The three texts together give formalism as a developing research programme rather than a slogan, and they inoculate the reader against both the school's enemies and its own youthful self image.

Cross to the other side with Trotsky's chapters on the formalist school in his 1924 book on literature and revolution, which grant the technique and deny its sufficiency in the voice of a participant in the revolution the formalists were living through. Then take the Medvedev volume of 1928 slowly, since it is the hinge of the whole dispute: the concession, the doctrine of genre and the socially accented word, the completion of defamiliarisation by the theory of the audience. Read the Jakobson and Tynyanov theses of the same year immediately after, all few pages of them, and watch the two sides arrive at the shared border from opposite directions. Finish with Shklovsky's 1930 recantation, read not for its arguments but as a document of the ending, and with a chapter of Mukarovsky or of Williams as evidence of where the programme went when it was allowed to continue. A reader who has walked that path owns the dispute; a reader who has only met the summary owns a diagram of it.

### In what order should a beginner read the primary texts of the dispute?

Start with the 1917 essay on art as device and Eichenbaum on Gogol for the method in action, then Tynyanov on evolution for the method maturing. Cross to Trotsky's 1924 chapters and the 1928 Medvedev critique, then the theses of the same year, and end with the 1930 recantation read as a document of suppression.

The reading path has a pedagogical point that teachers of the comparison should notice. Students who begin with the manifestos and end with the denunciations reliably conclude that the dispute was a war of essences, and they write essays organised as battles. Students who read in the order given here reliably notice movement: concessions accumulating, vocabularies converging, a settlement drafting itself in the texts of both camps in a single year. The difference between those outcomes is produced by the order of reading, which is itself a small demonstration of the article's thesis about institutions and transmission. What a field hands its students, in what sequence, with which documents missing, determines what the field believes its own history to have been. The synthesis was forgotten partly because the syllabus, in several countries and for several decades, simply did not contain the documents in which it was stated, and a syllabus is an institution with consequences as real as a journal's.

## Sound, Sense, and the Trans Sense Experiment

The verse studies contain the dispute in miniature, in the formalists' treatment of sound. Early work on poetic language investigated sound repetition, instrumentation, and the phonetic texture of verse with a zeal that opponents found easy to mock: counting consonants looked like the reductio of the whole method, science envy measuring what it could and ignoring what mattered. The mockery missed the theoretical stake. If sound patterning, which carries no paraphrasable content, demonstrably organises a poem's effect, then the literary cannot be identified with content, and the formalist founding claim has its cleanest proof. The futurist experiment with trans sense language, verse built from invented sounds beyond ordinary meaning, pushed the proof to its limit case: poetry at the edge of sense, where whatever effect survives must be an effect of construction. Shklovsky's interest in the experiment was methodological as much as aesthetic. It isolated the variable his theory cared about.

The Marxist response to the sound studies is the dispute in miniature too, because it divides into a weak and a strong form. The weak form sneered at counting and demanded poems about tractors, and it deserves the oblivion it found. The strong form, the circle's, accepted the phenomena and asked after their conditions: sound patterns please ears formed by a language's history and a culture's hierarchies of register; the prestige of folk sound, of liturgical sound, of street sound, is a social fact that decides which textures a period hears as poetry and which as noise. A metre carries associations accumulated across its career, heroic, comic, archaic, intimate, and a poet choosing it chooses the associations as surely as the beat. On the strong form, the phonetic description and the social history are two halves of one account, and the poem's sound is the place where they audibly meet. That is the synthesis at the scale of the line, and it is a good test of a student's grasp: anyone can denounce counting or worship it, but specifying what the counts mean for formed ears is the work itself.

### What did the sound studies prove, and what did they leave open?

They proved that effects irreducible to content, patterning of sound and rhythm, organise poetic experience, which secured the founding claim that literariness is not paraphrase. They left open why particular patterns carry the prestige and associations they do, a question about the social history of registers and ears that the description itself cannot answer.

There is a lesson in the miniature about how to conduct the larger comparison fairly. Each school's favourite evidence was chosen where its method shines: sound for the formalists, the social novel for the Marxists, the lyric and the epic of class. A comparison that lets each side pick all the test cases will oscillate forever. The discipline the synthesis imposes is to run every method across the other side's favourite cases and grade the damage honestly. Formalism run across the social novel produces superb accounts of narration and a characteristic silence about the material's charge; Marxism run across the lyric produces real accounts of register and prestige and a characteristic thinness about construction. Neither silence nor thinness is a scandal; both are measurements of where each method's competence ends, and a map of competences is what a student actually needs from a comparison. The map, filled in across the sections of this article, is the practical form of the forgotten synthesis for working critics, whatever their declared allegiance.

## The Dostoevsky Case: A Synthesis Achieved in Practice

The strongest single exhibit for the synthesis is a book produced inside the Bakhtin circle's orbit at the end of the decade, the study of Dostoevsky published under Bakhtin's own name in 1929. Its central discovery, the polyphonic novel, is a claim about construction: Dostoevsky, it argues, built novels in which the hero is not an object of the author's finalising judgement but a subject whose voice, carrying a whole worldview, stands in dialogue with other voices and with the author's own, unmerged and unfinalised. The discovery is formal in the strictest sense, a description of how the works are made, of the architectonics of voice and point of view, and it permanently changed how the novels are read. Yet the discovery could only have been made by a thinker whose philosophy treated consciousness itself as dialogic and social, formed in exchange between speakers, laden with others' words. The form found is a form of social relation. A device catalogue without the philosophy would have described the voices and missed their unfinalised equality; a sociology without the formal attention would have discussed Dostoevsky's themes and missed the construction that makes the themes live as voices rather than as opinions.

The case matters beyond its brilliance because it shows the synthesis operating before its own suppression, in the same years and the same city as the quarrel, as a working method rather than a programme. The Dostoevsky book does not alternate chapters of form and chapters of society. Its every claim is double in the way the synthesis prescribes: the polyphonic structure is at once a device, analysable in the formalists' sense, and a social philosophy embodied, analysable in the Marxists' sense, and the book's power comes from refusing to let either description swallow the other. It also exhibits the cost of the historical ending with painful clarity. The book appeared in 1929, the year of its author's arrest and exile, and its second, expanded life waited more than three decades for a changed climate and a rediscovery. A method demonstrated at that level, in a major study of a major novelist, might in an open institutional order have founded a school and a syllabus. In the order that obtained, it founded a legend, transmitted in whispers and samizdat memory until the thaw, which is the forgotten synthesis's fate repeated in the biography of a single book.

### Why is the Dostoevsky study the best exhibit for the synthesis?

Because its central finding is double in every sentence: polyphony is a construction, describable as exactly as any formalist device, and it is simultaneously a social relation among voices carrying worldviews. No catalogue of devices and no sociology of contents could have produced the finding, which required both methods operating as one instrument.

Set the Dostoevsky case beside the formalists best exhibit, and the comparison of competences completes itself. The formalists' strongest work, the analyses of plot construction and of Gogol's skaz, found structures invisible to thematic criticism and explained effects that paraphrase based reading could not even register. The circle's strongest work found a structure invisible to device catalogues, because the structure was a relation among social voices. Each exhibit is a genuine discovery, checkable against the texts, and each was made with the other side's tools conspicuously in hand: the formalists' Gogol essay is alert to the social performance of storytelling, the circle's Dostoevsky book is alert to architectonics. The principals, at their best, were already practising across the line their polemics defended. That is the deepest reason for calling the 1928 settlement a resolution rather than a proposal: the practice of the strongest work on both sides had converged before the theory announced the convergence, and the theses and the critique are best read as the two camps' theorists catching up with their own best practitioners.

## Mediation: The Concept the Whole Dispute Was Reaching For

Later theory gave a name to what the synthesis describes, and the name is worth introducing because it organises the inheritance. Mediation names the process by which one order of fact passes into another without either being reduced: the ways social material is transformed, refracted, and worked as it enters literary form, and the ways literary form, in turn, shapes how a society can perceive and state its own experience. The concept became central to the Marxist tradition's mature aesthetics, in Williams's insistence that mediation replaces reflection as the governing metaphor, and it was equally the destination of the formalists' functional turn, in which a device means what its system lets it mean. Reflection is a metaphor of mirrors, passive and instantaneous; mediation is a metaphor of work, active and transformative, and the whole dispute can be retold as the replacement of the mirror by the workshop in the imagination of both camps.

The retelling clarifies what was actually at stake in the quarrel's least promising moments. When a reflectionist critic graded a novel by its author's class position, the error was not political but conceptual: a mirror was expected where a workshop stood, and the grading measured the wrong process. When a formalist of the manifesto phase treated the work as a sum of devices, the symmetrical error appeared: the workshop was described with its doors shut, the deliveries of material and the dispatch of effects left unexamined. Mediation, as the mature traditions on both sides came to use the term, keeps the workshop open at both ends: material arrives with a history, is transformed by describable operations, and leaves as an object that acts on formed readers in specifiable ways. Nothing in that picture requires a party line, and everything in it requires the two competences the dispute separated. A student who grasps mediation has grasped the synthesis in its portable form, and can recognise its presence or absence in critical writing of any period, including writing that has never heard of OPOYAZ or of the circle around Bakhtin.

### How does mediation improve on reflection as the governing metaphor?

Reflection suggests a passive mirror in which society appears in the work unchanged, inviting criticism to grade the accuracy of the image. Mediation suggests a workshop in which material is transformed by describable operations and then acts on readers, inviting criticism to trace the transformations. The second metaphor fits what both schools, at their best, actually did.

It should be recorded, against any temptation to declare the matter closed by a concept, that mediation names a programme rather than a result. To say that form mediates society is to promise a specification, and the promise is redeemed case by case or not at all. The history of the term's use includes long stretches of gestural invocation, in which mediation functioned as a password certifying that the speaker was neither a crude reflectionist nor a sealed formalist, while the actual relations went unspecified. The 1928 theses are bracing reading against that later slackness, because their demand was operational from the start: correlations between series, stated so they could be examined. The defeater discipline belongs here too. A claimed mediation that no evidence could disconfirm is a password, not a claim, and students should be trained to ask of every mediation they meet, in print or in their own drafts, what observation would show it to be wrongly specified. That single habit, learned from this dispute, will protect a critic in fields far from Russian formalism, which is one measure of how much general method is packed inside this apparently local quarrel.

## The Objections to the Synthesis, Heard From Both Camps

A settlement that both camps would have resisted deserves to have the resistance stated in its strongest form, since a verdict that survives only friendly questioning is worth little. From the Marxist side, the standing suspicion is that correlation unbacked by determination amounts to evasion dressed as method. If the literary series merely correlates with the economic and political series, what explains the correlations, and why do they so often align with the interests of dominant classes? The suspicion has force wherever the synthesis is practised lazily, as a refusal to rank causes. Its answer, in the strong version of the synthesis, is that determination is not denied but relocated: it operates through the channels the research specifies, through publishing economics, educational formation, censorship, patronage, and the class distribution of leisure and literacy, and it is exactly as strong as the specification shows it to be in each case. A determination asserted without channels is a metaphysical comfort; a correlation specified through channels is determination made examinable. The Marxist objector is invited to name a case where the channels, fully traced, leave the alignment with dominant interests unexplained, and the invitation has the form of the defeater discipline used throughout this article.

From the formalist side, the standing suspicion is the mirror image: that relations, once admitted, swallow the object, and that the specification of correlations becomes a licence to return to the old translation exercise with a larger vocabulary. That suspicion also has force wherever the settlement is invoked lazily, as background paragraphs stapled to conclusions. Its answer is the toll discipline described in the guidance section: no relation may be claimed to a term that has not first been fixed by exact description, and the description retains its own standards, answerable to the text and not to the sociology built upon it. A formalism that fears contamination should notice that the synthesis protects its core better than the manifesto did, since the manifesto's sealed system invited the charge of sterility, while the synthesis gives the description a world to be description of. Both suspicions, in short, identify the same failure mode, laziness, from opposite directions, and the synthesis's defence is not a slogan but a pair of disciplines, specification and description, each policing the other's characteristic vice.

### What do the two camps each suspect the synthesis of hiding?

The Marxist suspicion is evasion: correlation without determination lets the hard question of power dissolve into parallel timelines. The formalist suspicion is dissolution: relations admitted without discipline let sociology swallow the object it promised to respect. Both suspicions target lazy practice, and the synthesis answers them with its two disciplines, specified channels and prior exact description.

There is a third objection, less often voiced because it embarrasses both camps, and completeness requires airing it. The synthesis might be right and unimportant: a correct framework that generates no discoveries, a peace treaty between research programmes whose productive years were behind them anyway. The historical record answers this objection better than any argument could. The programmes that descended from the dispute, narratology, reception study, the sociology of literature, cultural materialism, book history, generated discoveries in abundance, and they did so, as the earlier sections traced, by practising relation without reduction under other names. A framework independently rebuilt by productive fields is not a treaty between exhausted armies; it is the shape of the ground the armies were fighting over, visible once the fighting stopped. The objection also underrates the 1928 moment itself, in which the synthesis was stated not by tired veterans but by scholars in their productive prime, Tynyanov and Jakobson at the height of their powers, Medvedev's circle producing its masterworks. What was lost was not a formula but the decades of work the formula would have organised, in the place where the problems had been posed most sharply.

## The Settled by Decree Pattern: Placing the Case in the Series

This series has followed Marxist thought through many institutional endings, and the formalism case earns its place in that sequence by the completeness with which it exhibits the pattern. The sequence, abstracted from the instances, runs as follows. A field develops genuine internal problems and competing solutions. One solution begins to converge with its rivals toward a settlement that threatens the custodians of orthodoxy, since a settlement reached by argument cannot be administered. Authority intervenes, not by answering the arguments but by reorganising the institutions: journals change hands, associations are dissolved or merged, curricula are rewritten, and a vocabulary of abuse is issued in which the condemned position can be denounced without being stated. A prominent figure performs recantation, whose function is theatrical rather than intellectual, demonstrating that submission is possible and expected. The field's subsequent official history records the episode as an error corrected, and the settlement that was actually available disappears from the record, to be rediscovered abroad or in a later thaw, usually without its authors' names attached.

Formalism supplies the fourth documented occasion of that pattern in this series, and its distinctive feature, worth the emphasis the brief of this article demands, is the existence of the synthesis at the moment of intervention. In other instances the decree froze a dispute mid argument, leaving posterity to guess how it might have ended. Here posterity does not have to guess. The ending was drafted, in print, by both sides, in the same year, and the decree's work was therefore not to freeze a quarrel but to bury a peace. That distinction changes the moral of the case for the tradition that issued the decree. A movement may plead, in mitigation of a frozen quarrel, that the argument was going nowhere. No such plea is available where the argument had arrived, and the arrival is documented in the movement's own critics' book and its opponents' theses alike. The suppression of formalism is thus the least excusable of the pattern's instances on the record this series has assembled, and stating that plainly is part of the article's obligation to the evidence, in the spirit of the neutrality rule that governs the whole series: the steelman first, for every party, and then the verdict the documents support, however uncomfortable the verdict is for the tradition the series studies.

### What makes the formalism case distinctive among settlements imposed by decree?

In the other instances a live quarrel was frozen, and later readers must guess at its outcome. Here a settlement had been drafted in print by both camps within a single year, so the decree buried a peace rather than interrupting a war. The documentary survival of the settlement removes the usual mitigation and makes the case the pattern's clearest exhibit.

The pattern's recurrence across the series carries a methodological instruction for readers of intellectual history generally. Wherever the official record of a field shows a dispute ending suddenly, with a change of vocabulary, a disappearance of journals, and a recantation by a leading figure, the historian should suspect an administrative ending and go looking for the settlement the administration buried, in the last publications before the silence. The buried settlements are often the most interesting documents in a field's history, because they show what the participants, arguing freely, were actually converging toward before the convergence was stopped. The 1928 texts of this dispute are the type specimen: brief, technical, unquotable in slogans, and worth more to a working critic than the entire polemical literature that drowned them out.

## What a Living Synthesis Would Study: The Unfinished Agenda

The synthesis was a programme, and programmes are best honoured by stating the work they still have to do. A criticism organised by the relations between series would have, as its standing agenda, a set of problems that neither parent school could formulate alone, and the problems remain open in the fields that inherited them. The first is the specification of channels: through exactly which institutions, markets, technologies, and habits does a change in the social series register inside the literary one, and with what delays and distortions? Book history and the sociology of publishing have made this problem empirical, tracing circuits of production and distribution in archival detail, and their findings repeatedly show the channel shaping the construction, as the serial channel shaped the Victorian novel's suspensions. The second problem is the formation of audiences, pursued by reception study and the history of reading: which repertoires, distributed by which institutions, meet which constructions, and how do the meetings change both terms? The third is the career of devices across systems, the formalists' own problem matured: how a device migrates between genres, media, and nations, changing function at every border, a problem that comparative literature and the study of adaptation work on daily.

The fourth problem is the one the dispute bequeathed most directly and the one least solved: evaluation across repertoires. If effects are relations between constructions and formed audiences, then judgements of value are relations too, and a criticism that understands this needs a disciplined way to argue value without either pretending to a universal ear or dissolving judgement into the sociology of taste. The mature traditions have gestures toward such a discipline, in the idea that great constructions reward successive unlike audiences, in the practice of re-arguing the canon for each generation, in the distinction between a work's first effect and its surplus of organisation that outlasts the repertoire that first received it. None of this is finished theory. It is the frontier the synthesis points to, and a student entering the field could do worse than to take the frontier as a vocation, since the problems there are real, the tools are half built, and the founding documents, unusually, can be read in an afternoon.

### Which problem did the dispute leave most conspicuously unsolved?

Evaluation across audiences. If a device's effect depends on the repertoire it meets, then value is a relation rather than a property, and neither school built the discipline for arguing relational value rigorously. The canon debates of later decades are that unsolved problem working itself out in public, often without knowing its ancestry.

A fifth problem belongs to the present structure of literary study itself, stated structurally and without reference to any passing controversy. Criticism is produced inside institutions, universities, journals, prize committees, reviewing pages, whose selections decide which constructions reach which audiences, exactly as the institutions of the 1920s did. A synthesis minded criticism studies its own channels with the same suspicion it applies to the past: which repertoires do its own formations presuppose, which materials do its own borders exclude, which devices of critical writing have automatised into mannerism? The formalists taught that every method has its devices and that devices wear out; the Marxists taught that every institution has its interests and that interests hide in procedures. A criticism that turns both lessons on itself is the synthesis practised as a habit of mind, and it is perhaps the most valuable form in which the settlement survives, since it requires no allegiance to any school and can be exercised by any reader willing to ask, of a critical performance including this one, what it is made of and whom it is for.

## The Verdict on the Whole Dispute

The sections of grading can at this stage be gathered into a single judgement, with its defeater attached as this series requires. Formalism was right about the object: literature has specific properties, accessible to exact description, and a criticism that cannot describe them is not a criticism of literature. Formalism was wrong about the boundary: the properties are not independent of the social world, because the devices that carry them act on socially formed perception and draw on socially charged material, and the school's own development, from manifesto to functionalism to the theses of 1928, was a long concession of the point. Marxist criticism was right about the relations: literature lives inside institutions, audiences, and histories that its forms register and that any complete account must specify. Marxist criticism, in its reflectionist form, was wrong about the object, and the strongest Marxists of the period and after rebuilt their method around form in tacit acknowledgement. The settlement of the dispute was achieved in principle in 1928, from both sides, in the proposal that literature develops by an internal logic while answering, along channels research can trace, to the orders of life around it; the settlement was then destroyed as a living programme by the suppression of one party and the silencing of the other, and its subsequent history is a history of independent rediscovery in freer institutions. The defeater stands where it was placed at the outset: the judgement fails if specifying relations between series is shown, in worked cases, to require reducing one series to the other. A hundred years of practice that has specified relations without collapse is the evidence that the defeater has not been met.

That verdict assigns the tradition this series studies a divided legacy, and the division should be stated without flinching in either direction. Marxist criticism produced, in the Bakhtin circle's engagement with formalism, one of the finest hours in its history: a critique that conceded its opponent's discovery, absorbed it, and completed it, written at the moment when denunciation was becoming the safer genre. The same tradition's institutions then destroyed the conditions in which that achievement could be continued, exiled its author, and administered a counterfeit settlement whose sterility lasted decades. Both facts belong to the record, and a series that printed only the first would be hagiography while one printing only the second would be prosecution. The documents support both, and the article has printed both, which is the only neutrality worth the name: not an equal distance from all conclusions, but an equal submission to the evidence, with steelman before verdict for every party including the one the evidence finally condemns.

### Which single sentence carries the verdict of the whole comparison?

The dispute was resolved in principle in 1928 by the proposal that literature obeys an internal logic while answering to neighbouring orders of life along traceable channels, and the resolution was lost through suppression rather than refutation. Everything else in the verdict, the grading of rows, the parallel with close reading, the afterlives of the concepts, is commentary on that sentence.

For the working reader, the dispute's final yield is a pair of questions to carry into any encounter with a literary work, one inherited from each school and neither sufficient alone. From the formalists: how is this made, and what does its making do to perception? From the Marxists: in what world was this made, for which formed readers, and what does its career through institutions reveal about both the work and the world? A reading that can answer the first question exactly and the second question concretely, and can state how the answers bear on each other, is practising the forgotten synthesis whether it knows the name or not. The name is worth knowing anyway. Recovering it honours the scholars who drafted a peace in the last season when drafting one was possible, and it reminds their successors that the peace was not a failure of nerve between two fighting faiths but a finding, reached independently from both sides of a serious argument, about how literature actually exists in the world.

## The Folktale Laboratory: Propp and the Morphology of Wonder

The folktale studies give the formalist method its most reproducible result, and they test the synthesis on material far from the lyric and the novel. Vladimir Propp, working in the same Leningrad milieu, took a corpus of wonder tales and asked what remains constant beneath their variety of characters and settings. His answer, published in 1928, the dispute's miraculous year, was a morphology: a limited set of functions, actions defined by their consequences for the story, recurring in a fixed order, performed by a small cast of roles that any character, animal, or object might fill. A dragon, a witch, or a jealous stepmother can occupy the villain's function; a horse, a ring, or an old woman by the road can occupy the donor's. The analysis is formal in the purest sense the school ever achieved, since it abstracts entirely from who acts and attends only to what the action does in the sequence, and its results can be verified or refuted by any reader with the corpus open.

The Marxist questions arrive at this stronghold in an instructive order. They do not begin by denying the morphology, which would be folly, but by asking after its conditions and its silences. The tales themselves are social documents of a peasant world: their functions encode its dangers and its hopes, the forest and the road, the testing of the youngest son, the magical helper who compensates for the hero's lack of property and protection. The morphology describes the machine; the social history explains why this machine, with these functions, in this order, was the one a particular world built and retold for centuries. And the transmission of the tales, through named collectors, printed editions, and school readers, is an institutional history that decided which variants survived to be morphologised. Propp himself, to his credit, spent later work on the historical roots of the wonder tale, tracing its functions back to rituals and social arrangements of earlier orders, a trajectory that mirrors the school's general movement from sealed system toward relation. The folktale laboratory thus ends as another exhibit for the synthesis: the purest formalism in the corpus generated, from its own completeness, the demand for the history that completes it.

### What does the morphology of the folktale show about the method at its purest?

It shows abstraction carried to a verifiable result: a fixed sequence of functions beneath endless variety of characters and settings, checkable against the corpus by any reader. It also shows the method generating its own beyond, since the question of why this sequence, in this world, carried its author toward the historical roots of the tales.

There is a further dividend in the folktale case for the working student, because it isolates the concept of function with unusual clarity. A function is neither a theme nor a motif in the loose senses those words carry in weaker criticism; it is an action identified by its place and consequence in a system, so that the same event, a gift, a flight, a recognition, can be different functions in different positions and different events can be the same function in the same position. Tynyanov's literary evolution runs on the same concept at the scale of genres and devices: an element is what it does in the system it inhabits at that moment, and when the system changes, the element's identity changes with it, which is why the history of forms cannot be written as a history of things. Students who master function in the folktale, where the corpus is small and the sequences are stark, can then wield it in the novel and the lyric, where it does the heavy work of the synthesis by explaining how social material changes its nature as it crosses into the literary series and takes up a role there. The crossing, the change of function at the border, is exactly the relation the 1928 theses asked research to specify, and the folktale gives beginners a place to watch a crossing happen slowly enough to learn from.

## The Profession of the Literary Scholar: What Each Side Thought Criticism Was For

Beneath the arguments about objects and methods lay a disagreement about the critic's profession, rarely stated and constantly operative. The formalists were building a discipline on the model of the sciences that had professionalised around them in the preceding generation, above all linguistics. A discipline, on that model, has a defined object, a technical vocabulary, cumulative results, and standards by which a contribution can fail. The critic's dignity lies in the discipline's rigour, and the worst professional sin is the impressionistic essay that cannot be wrong about anything. This professional ideal explains the school's polemical style, its impatience with appreciation, and its attraction to verse and tale, where results could be made checkable. It also explains the school's political vulnerability, since a profession that defines itself by autonomy from every external demand will find few defenders when an external power decides that autonomy itself is the offence.

The Marxist critics were building something else: not a profession in the same sense but a practice inside a movement, in which criticism was one instrument among others for understanding and changing the social order. On that model the critic's dignity lies in the truth and usefulness of the account, and the worst sin is scholasticism, technique pursued while the world burns unexamined. This ideal explains the Marxist side's impatience with device counting and its recurring demand that criticism answer for its social function. It also explains that side's characteristic catastrophe, since a practice defined by service to a movement will be administered by the movement's institutions when those institutions cease to tolerate independent judgement, and the critic who accepted the definition had no professional ground from which to refuse the administration. The formalists had ground and no power; the Marxists had power's ear and no ground. The synthesis, had it been institutionalised, might have given criticism both: a discipline with an object of its own, whose relations to the social order were its research programme rather than its chain of command. That institutional might have been is among the more poignant counterfactuals in the history of the humanities, and it generalises: every field that studies society from inside society faces the same pair of temptations, the sealed profession and the administered practice, and the Russian dispute is the case where both temptations were indulged to their ends within fifteen years.

### What opposed professional ideals drove the two camps beneath their arguments?

The formalists were building an autonomous science of literature, with a defined object and checkable results, on the model of linguistics. Their Marxist opponents were building a practice inside a movement, answerable to truth about society and to the movement's needs. Each ideal explains its camp's virtues, its polemical style, and its characteristic way of being destroyed.

The professional question has a quiet afterlife in every literature department, where the two ideals still compete for the souls of the young. The scholar who wants only to describe forms exactly will recognise the formalist temptation and should learn from its fate that autonomy undefended by any account of why the description matters will be administered by those who have such an account, however crude. The scholar who wants criticism to serve emancipation will recognise the Marxist temptation, whose fate teaches that service without a defended object becomes administration the moment the served institution prefers obedience to truth. The dispute's deepest teaching may be this professional one, and it is fitting that a series devoted to a tradition's thought should end this article by applying the tradition's own historical method to the tradition's own critics: their ideas were shaped, limited, and in the end disposed of by the institutions that housed them, and the ideas that survived were the ones that learned to live in more than one house.

## A Worked Micro Example: One Poem Through Both Instruments

Abstractions about method settle little until they are run across a single object, so consider a short lyric of a familiar kind: a poem in which a speaker watches a ploughman at work in a field at evening and reflects on labour, transience, and the dignity of obscure lives. The formalist instrument goes to work first and finds plenty. The metre is a steady, hymn like measure whose regularity enacts the patience it describes, until a single shortened line near the close breaks the pattern and makes the reader feel the interruption of death in the rhythm itself. The diction mixes plain words for tools and soil with elevated words for fate, and the mixture is a device: the poem's argument about dignity is performed by its registers before it is stated by its sentences. The structure withholds the speaker's reflection until the scene is fully established, a retardation at lyric scale, so that perception precedes meditation and the meditation arrives as an event rather than a preface. A reading that stopped here would know a great deal about how the poem works and could defend every claim from the text.

The Marxist instrument then asks its questions of the described object, and they are not decorative. The ploughman is watched, not heard; the poem's sympathy flows downward from a literate speaker whose leisure to watch and reflect is itself a social fact, and the genre the poem inhabits, the meditative lyric of rural retirement, has a history entangled with the enclosure of common land and the conversion of a working countryside into scenery for the classes displaced from working it. The elevated diction that the formalist reading praised as a dignity conferring mixture can be seen double: it dignifies the labourer and it appropriates him, translating his world into the observer's tongue at the moment of praising it. Neither reading cancels the other. The shortened line still breaks the metre; the watching still presupposes the watcher's position. What the synthesis adds is the sentence where the pair converge: the poem's central device, the register mixture, is the formal trace of a social relation, the literate observer's stance toward labour, and its effect on readers depends on whether their formation lets them feel the dignity, the appropriation, or the doubleness of both at once. That sentence could not have been written from either instrument alone, and it is the kind of sentence the 1928 programme existed to make routine.

### What does the synthesis add to the two readings of a single poem?

It adds the sentences where the readings meet: the identification of a device as the formal trace of a social relation, and of an effect as dependent on the reader's formation. Either instrument alone describes its half exactly and leaves the meeting unexamined, which is where the poem's full action takes place.

The micro example also exposes, in small, why the dispute could not be settled by accumulating more readings of either kind. A hundred formalist readings of a hundred poems would refine the device vocabulary without touching the question of the observer's stance as a formal fact. A hundred sociological readings would refine the account of stances without explaining why this shortened line, and not a paragraph of commentary, makes a reader feel mortality in the body. The competences are not rivals on one scale; they are coordinates on two axes, and a work's full description is a position in the plane the axes define. Criticism since the dispute, at its best, has worked in that plane without always knowing the axes' names. Naming them, and crediting the scholars who first drew the map in the year their world began to close around them, is the modest act of justice this article has attempted alongside its verdicts.

## The Dispute in the Classroom: How the Comparison Is Taught and Examined

Because the comparison is a set topic in courses and examinations, its pedagogy deserves the same scrutiny as its history, and the scrutiny turns up a final irony. The dispute is usually taught as a pair of lectures, formalism first, Marxism second, with the second presented as the correction of the first, or the order reversed for a narrative of liberation from politics into form. Either order smuggles a verdict into the sequence, since students reliably read later as truer. The history resists both sequences. Formalism's mature positions postdate the Marxist objections and absorb them; the strongest Marxist critique postdates the manifestos and absorbs formalism; the two lines converge in a single year and are then separated by force, not by logic. A syllabus faithful to the record would teach the dispute as a convergence interrupted, ending not with a winner but with the 1928 documents and the recantation side by side, and would set as its examination question the one this article has pursued: what was settled, what was suppressed, and what would count as evidence that the settlement fails?

That question has the pedagogical virtue of being unanswerable by memorised positions. A student cannot respond to it by reciting the strengths and weaknesses of two schools, since it asks for a judgement about a third thing, the settlement, and for the defeater discipline that this series has required of every verdict in the cluster. The student must handle dated texts, distinguish suppression from refutation, and state the conditions under which a favoured conclusion would be surrendered. Those are the operations of scholarship itself, which is why the comparison, taught whole, is worth more than its content: it is a training ground in the difference between an argument and an alignment. Teachers who use the dispute grid from this article in seminars will find that the grid's fourth column does the training almost by itself, since filling it forces students to read the theses and the critique rather than the summaries of them, and the summaries are where the standoff myth lives.

### Why does the order in which the schools are taught smuggle in a verdict?

Because students read sequence as progress and take the later lecture as the correction of the earlier. The record defeats both possible orders: each school's mature form absorbed the other's objections, and the lines converged in 1928 before force separated them. Teaching the dispute as a convergence interrupted is the only sequence the documents support.

The examination dimension has a practical corollary that students preparing answers should absorb. An answer that presents the schools as eternal opposites and concludes that both have merits will be graded as competent and unmemorable, because it reproduces the summary the examiner has read a thousand times. An answer that reconstructs the movement of the positions, lands on the 1928 convergence, states the suppression plainly, and closes by naming what would defeat the claim of settlement, gives the examiner the operations the rubric rewards: evidence handled in sequence, judgement ventured, conditions of surrender specified. The attribution rule taught in the exam guide of this cluster applies with special force here, since the dispute is a magnet for unattributed generalities about what Marxists think and formalists believe. Every claim in a strong answer carries a name, a text, and a date, and the habit of carrying them is itself the difference between the two schools' shared enemy, impressionism, and the discipline both were, in their different idioms, trying to build.

## The Years in Sequence: How the Positions Moved

Chronology is the cheapest antidote to the standoff myth, and the dispute's chronology is compact enough to hold in one view. In 1916 and 1917 the Petrograd circle forms and Shklovsky publishes the essay on art as device, defining the school by its theory of perception while the empire that housed it collapses into revolution. Through the early 1920s the prose and verse studies accumulate, the collections on poetics appear, and the school's confidence peaks in manifestos that claim the entire field of literary study. In 1924 Trotsky's book grants the technique and denies its sufficiency, the first Marxist engagement at full seriousness, while the proletarian writers' organisations begin the campaigns that will give sociological criticism its institutional muscle. In 1925 and 1926 the formalists, under fire and under their own momentum, develop the functional theory of how literature evolves, in which systems, borders, and neighbouring orders of life enter the theory. In 1928 the annus mirabilis and horribilis arrives together: the Medvedev critique, the Jakobson and Tynyanov theses, Propp's morphology, and, at the year's end, the arrest of Bakhtin, the first carrier of the synthesis to be taken. In 1929 the Dostoevsky study appears as its author goes into exile, and the organised campaigns against formalism reach their climax. In 1930 Shklovsky publishes the recantation, and the school's public existence ends, fourteen years after it began.

Laid out in sequence, the story refuses the shape that summaries give it. The movement is not thesis, antithesis, stalemate. It is a young science hardening into dogma, criticised sharply, maturing under criticism toward a broader theory, met at the border by a critique that had matured in the same direction, and then stopped, not by the exhaustion of its ideas but by the removal of its people and presses. The fourteen years contain a complete cycle of how knowledge grows when it is allowed to: claim, counterclaim, revision, convergence. They also contain the demonstration of how quickly the cycle can be broken from outside, and how completely the breaking can be disguised afterward as a verdict of the argument itself. Readers of this series, which has traced the pattern through philosophy, economics, and historiography in other clusters, will recognise the formalism case as the pattern's literary instance, unusually well documented because the participants wrote everything down and because so many of the documents survived their authors.

### What does the chronology show that summaries of the dispute conceal?

It shows movement where summaries show positions: a school maturing under criticism, a critique maturing under its opponent's influence, convergence in a single year, and then an ending imposed from outside. The sequence converts the story from a war of essences into a cycle of claim, revision, and settlement interrupted, which is a different and more useful history.

The chronology has one more entry that belongs after the ending, the long postscript of rediscovery. In the decades after the Second World War the early formalist texts reached Western readers in translation and detonated pleasantly in a literary study grown tired of impressionism, feeding structuralism and the new sciences of narrative. In the Soviet thaw the circle's works resurfaced, Bakhtin's name was restored, and the Dostoevsky book was reissued in expanded form to a generation that received it as a voice from a sealed room. In the Western universities the Marxist objections, carried by new movements and new publics, dismantled the autonomy claims of close reading and rebuilt criticism on relational lines. Each rediscovery recovered a fragment of 1928 and mistook it, understandably, for a beginning. The full sequence, in which the fragments are recognised as pieces of one settlement drafted before all of them, has had to wait for the translations, the archives, and the calm to assemble it, and assembling it is among the tasks this article set itself at the outset.

## Words That Travel Badly: The Terms of the Dispute in Translation

A dispute conducted in one language and inherited in others is at the mercy of its translators, and this dispute's key terms have all suffered instructive damage in transit. The Russian word rendered as device, priem, carries senses of technique, trick, and reception that the English device only partly holds, so that formalist talk of devices can sound more mechanical in English than it felt in Russian, where the word belonged to the workshop and the stage as much as to the laboratory. The term for defamiliarisation, ostranenie, was coined with a deliberate oddity, a missing letter that makes the word itself strange, and its renderings have oscillated between defamiliarisation and estrangement, the second of which collides with the quite different concept of alienation in Marxist vocabulary and has confused generations of students into merging two theories that must be kept apart. Literariness, literaturnost, sounds in English like a quality of being well read, while its Russian sense is closer to literature ness, the fact of a thing's being literature at all.

These are not pedantries. The estrangement collision alone has generated a small literature of confusion, in which Shklovsky's perceptual renewal and the Marxist account of labour's alienation are blended into a vague sense that art protests modern life, a blend that neither theory asserts and that dissolves the precision both were built to achieve. The synthesis itself suffered the worst translational fate of all, which was not mistranslation but absence: the theses' key notion, the correlation of series, is a phrase that needs its context to live, and in the absence of translation the phrase simply did not exist for scholarship in other languages during the decades when the dispute was being reenacted there. A field's vocabulary is its memory, and a memory with holes will keep rediscovering what it cannot recall having known. Students working on this dispute in English should therefore treat every key term as a translation with a history, check the Russian where the argument turns on a word, and distrust any account in which the terms glide frictionlessly, since the friction is often where the argument is.

### Why does the rendering of one Russian word keep confusing students of the dispute?

Because ostranenie has been translated both as defamiliarisation and as estrangement, and estrangement is also the standard rendering of the Marxist term for alienation. The collision merges a theory of perception with a theory of labour that share no premises, and the merged blur then circulates as if it were a position somebody held.

## The Literary Environment: Eichenbaum's Bridge Concept

Inside the formalist camp, the concept that travelled furthest toward the Marxist border was Eichenbaum's notion of the literary environment, the byt of literature: the whole texture of literary life in a period, its salons and journals, its professional customs, its markets and reputations, the everyday existence of the writer as a worker in a trade. Eichenbaum introduced the emphasis in the later 1920s, arguing that a science of literature which studied only works would miss the medium in which works are produced, circulated, and valued, and that the writer's professional existence, the literary fact of an epoch, deserved study in its own right. The move is remarkable for its source. It came not from a Marxist critic demanding sociology but from a founding formalist following his own questions about how literature exists as a practice, and it opened the school's programme to exactly the institutional material, publishing, reviewing, professional organisation, that the Marxist objection had always cited as the sealed system's outside.

The bridge concept matters for grading the dispute because it shows the convergence proceeding by independent routes. The theses of 1928 proposed relations between series as a programme; the Medvedev critique proposed the socially accented work as a doctrine; Eichenbaum's literary environment proposed the institutions of literary life as an object, all within the same few years and from different starting points inside and outside the school. When three routes converge on one border region, the region is probably where the subject actually lives. The border region, described neutrally, is the zone where works meet their conditions: the zone of production, circulation, profession, and reception, in which forms are selected, valued, taught, and worn out. Later fields colonised the zone under their own flags, the sociology of literature, book history, the study of reading, and the disputes among those fields about methods and evidence are the continuation, in freer conditions, of the argument the Russian institutions stopped. A student who understands the border region and why three programmes reached for it simultaneously understands the dispute better than a student who can recite either school's doctrines entire.

### What was the literary environment, and why did a formalist introduce it?

Eichenbaum coined it for the dense fabric of a period's literary existence: its journals and salons, its markets and professional habits, the writer's daily life as a working trade. A founding formalist introduced it because the study of works alone could not explain how literature exists as a practice, and the concept opened the school's programme to institutional history from inside.

The concept also supplies the fairest test of the Marxist objection's strongest wording. The objection, at its most sweeping, charged formalism with having no place for the social existence of literature at all. Against the manifestos of the early years the charge is just. Against the school's later work it fails, since the literary environment is precisely a place for that existence, built with formalist tools and formalist standards of evidence. The honest grading, which this article has maintained throughout, is therefore temporal: the objection was right about the school it first addressed and progressively less right about the school as the school revised itself, partly under the objection's own pressure. Intellectual charity requires grading positions at their dates, and polemic, on both sides, habitually graded the opponent at the opponent's most convenient date, the Marxists citing 1919 against the men of 1928, the formalists citing the crudest sociologism against the authors of the subtlest critique. The habit survives wherever schools are compared, and unlearning it is among the transferable skills this dispute teaches: ask of every position not only what it says but when it said it, and what it had already conceded by then.

## Neighbouring Disciplines: What the Heirs Did With the Inheritance

The dispute's concepts were taken up by neighbouring disciplines that rarely cite it, and tracing the uptake completes the map of its afterlife. Narratology took the story and arrangement distinction, the analysis of functions, and the typology of narration, and built from them a systematic account of narrative that has since been extended to film, historiography, and digital media, in each extension rediscovering that narrative devices presuppose audiences trained by institutions. Stylistics took the foregrounding concept, the direct descendant of defamiliarisation, and made it an empirical programme, testing how deviations and parallelisms in language produce effects in readers, and in its social branches it studies how registers carry the accents of communities, which is the circle's doctrine of the word pursued with corpora. Comparative literature took Tynyanov's evolution, the migration and refunctioning of genres across systems, and made it a way to write literary history across languages without reducing works to national essences. The history of the novel took the whole complex, form as social optics, genre as an optics, the reader's formation, and produced accounts in which a genre's rise is simultaneously a constructional event and a social one.

The uptake pattern carries the article's argument into the present tense of the disciplines. In each field, the inherited formalist tool works best where it is used relationally, and the inherited Marxist question bites hardest where it is asked of a precisely described object. The fields did not achieve this by treaty; they achieved it by finding, case by case, that the pure versions of either inheritance underdescribed their material. Practice converged where doctrine had been forbidden to. There is a quiet satisfaction in recording that the settlement the administrators buried in 1930 was rebuilt, anonymously and at scale, by the ordinary operations of scholarship in open institutions, and that the rebuilding constitutes the closest thing intellectual history offers to an experimental confirmation: independent workers, ignorant of the treaty, kept arriving at its terms because the material kept requiring them.

### Which neighbouring fields run on concepts inherited from the dispute?

Narratology runs on the story and arrangement distinction and the analysis of functions. Stylistics runs on foregrounding and the social life of registers. Comparative literature runs on the migration and refunctioning of genres. Each field found its inherited tool most powerful when used relationally, which is the synthesis confirmed by independent practice.

A caution should accompany the satisfaction, lest the verdict harden into the kind of slogan the article has resisted throughout. Convergence in practice is evidence, not proof, and the defeater discipline applies to the article's own conclusion as strictly as to any party's. If a future research programme were to show that the relational specifications, pursued rigorously, collapse into either reduction or decoration across a wide run of cases, the settlement claimed here would need to be regraded as a noble failure, a compromise that described the disputants' exhaustion rather than the subject's structure. Nothing in the record assembled in this article points that way; the cases point the other way, repeatedly and from independent directions. But the condition of surrender is part of the claim, and stating it is not a ritual. It is what distinguishes the claim from the alignments, administered or voluntary, that this dispute's history teaches its readers to refuse.

## The Author in the Dispute: Maker, Function, and Symptom

The figure of the author stood at the crossing of the two methods, and each school's treatment of authorship reveals its deepest commitments. Formalism demoted the author with deliberate provocation. Against the biographical criticism that explained works by their makers' lives and intentions, the school treated the author as a function of the work's construction: the biographical legend, as Tynyanov called it, is itself a literary fact, a story cultures tell about their writers, and the writer's actual intentions are neither available nor authoritative for the analysis of devices. What matters is the work's organisation, which can exceed, betray, or ironise anything its maker planned. The demotion was methodological, a refusal to let an inaccessible interior settle questions the text can settle, and it anticipated by decades the Anglo-American warning against the intentional fallacy and the later theoretical announcements of the author's death.

Marxist criticism's treatment of the author was more divided, and the division maps its own internal range. Reflectionist practice enthroned the author as a class symptom, grading works by the writer's origin and allegiance, a procedure the formalists rightly scorned. The stronger Marxist line, the one the circle represented, treated the author as a social position rather than a symptom: a situated maker whose language, genres, and audiences are given by a world, and whose work is the transformation of that given material by a formed sensibility. On that account the author is neither the work's explanation nor its irrelevance but one of its conditions, and biography returns to criticism as social history rather than as gossip or as verdict. The synthesis's handling of authorship follows from its general shape: describe the construction without appeal to intention, then specify the conditions, including the maker's formation and position, that made this construction possible and gave it its materials and its first readers. Intention is demoted from oracle to evidence, one document among others about the work's production, which is where sober criticism in most traditions has in practice kept it.

### How did each school treat the author, and where does the synthesis stand?

Formalism demoted the author to a function of the construction and treated biography as another literary fact. Crude Marxist practice, by contrast, crowned the writer as a symptom of class, while its stronger line treated authorship as a social position that conditions the work. The synthesis demotes intention from oracle to evidence and specifies the maker's formation among the work's conditions.

The authorship question has a bearing on the dispute's most delicate document, the disputed books of the Bakhtin circle, and the bearing is worth making explicit. A criticism formed by the synthesis will approach the Medvedev and Voloshinov volumes without panic about attribution, because it does not rest arguments on the interior intentions of a single named mind. The books emerged from a circle in sustained dialogue, were published under individual names for institutional reasons, and argue positions the circle shared and developed collectively. To ask which hand held the pen is a legitimate question of literary history, answerable in part by archives and testimony. To make the argument's force depend on the answer is to commit the intentional error in its scholarly form. The texts say what they say; their conditions include the circle, the year, and the closing institutional weather; and the synthesis's method, conditions specified and constructions described, handles the case without needing the verdict that two generations of scholarship have contested. There is a certain justice in the fact that the circle's own theory of the utterance, socially shared and dialogic by nature, describes the circle's own books better than the romantic theory of solitary authorship under which the dispute about them has usually been conducted.

## Tradition and the Classics: How Each School Reads the Dead

A method shows its character in its handling of the literary past, and the two schools' attitudes toward tradition divide as instructively as their theories of change. Formalism's attitude was one of productive irreverence. The classics, on its account, are not monuments to be venerated but systems to be analysed, their devices available for description like any others, and their authority itself a historical product, the result of canonisation processes that criticism should study rather than obey. The school practised what it preached with relish, treating Pushkin, the national sacred poet, as a constructor whose devices could be anatomised, and treating the revered novelists as engineers of plot whose machinery could be drawn. The irreverence was a liberation for scholarship, and it scandalised guardians of culture exactly as intended. Its limit appeared when the analysis of canonisation itself was required, since canonisation is an institutional process carried out by schools, states, and markets, and the sealed system had no instruments for it until the literary environment concept began to supply them.

The Marxist attitude toward tradition was, in principle, one of critical inheritance: the classics embody the accumulated experience and the highest formal achievements of their worlds, and a new culture should appropriate them critically rather than either worshipping or smashing them. In practice the attitude oscillated with the political weather, between campaigns that exalted the classics as the people's heritage and campaigns that audited them for class alienness, an oscillation that itself demonstrates the administered practice's weakness, since the verdict on a dead novelist should not be a function of the cultural line of a given year. The stronger Marxist critics held the principled line steadily, Lukacs building his whole aesthetics on the great realists, the circle reading Dostoevsky as a contemporary. Where the principled line held, Marxist criticism of tradition converges with the formalist practice it opposed: both end by reading the dead exactly, as constructors working in worlds, and the quarrel between them reduces to the familiar division, description against relation, with the synthesis waiting to join them.

### What does each school's handling of the classics reveal about it?

Formalist irreverence treats the classics as systems open to anatomy and canonisation as a process to study, liberating scholarship while lacking, at first, the tools for the institutional half of the story. Principled Marxist inheritance treats the classics as accumulated achievement to appropriate critically, and oscillates in practice when administration overrides principle. At their best the two practices meet.

## Reading Under Pressure: Criticism When the State Is a Reader

The dispute unfolded in a society where the state was becoming literature's most consequential reader, and that fact pressed on both schools in ways their theories only partly registered. A criticism that treats the work as an autonomous system has one defence against a state reader: the claim that its questions are technical and its territory its own. The defence failed, as the record shows, because the state reader declined to recognise the territory. A criticism that treats literature as a social institution has the opposite exposure: it has already granted that literature is a public force, and a state that agrees will claim the right to direct the force, leaving the critic's own theory as the warrant for the critic's administration. Both exposures are visible in the documents of the ending, the denunciations borrowing Marxist vocabulary to do administrative work, the recantation borrowing the vocabulary of self criticism to perform submission. Reading the dispute with the state reader in view is not an anachronistic politicisation of a scholarly quarrel. The participants understood the stakes at the time, wrote with the censor and the committee in their peripheral vision, and shaped arguments, silences, and ironies accordingly, a shaping that the synthesis's own method, alert to conditions of production, is obliged to include in its account of their texts.

The general lesson extends to any criticism written where power reads closely, and it is a lesson in the ethics of method. Methods are not only instruments for knowing; they are also positions from which their holders can be attacked, defended, or conscripted, and a school choosing its doctrines is choosing, whether it knows it or not, its vulnerabilities. Formalism's autonomy claim was its glory as science and its death warrant as politics. The sociological claim was Marxist criticism's glory as relevance and its chain as practice. A synthesis that joins a defended object to specified relations offers its holders a somewhat better position, an object to defend and a programme that no administrator can complete by decree, but the Russian record permits no complacency: better positions delay endings rather than abolishing them, and the survival of the synthesis abroad owed as much to geography as to logic. Intellectual history written honestly keeps both truths in view, the force of arguments and the force that decides which arguments get to finish.

### What did the presence of the state as reader do to both schools?

It converted their doctrines into exposures. The autonomy claim gave formalism no political defence when its territory was refused recognition, while the social institution claim gave administrators a warrant, drawn from Marxist vocabulary itself, to direct literature. Methods, the case teaches, double as exposed ground on which their holders may be conscripted or destroyed.

There is a quieter consequence of reading under pressure that the texts preserve and that students should learn to detect: the development of indirection as a scholarly style. Arguments conducted partly in code, through historical parallels, through the choice of which dead author to analyse and which living question to leave unnamed, were a survival technology in the period and a training in reading for the generations educated by those texts. The Dostoevsky book's dialogism has often been read, by scholars alert to its moment, as carrying implications far beyond Dostoevsky, about the possibility of unmerged voices in a world being organised for monologue. Whether or not the implication was intended in that form, the reception is itself a datum for the synthesis: a construction built from one period's materials went on working in later readers' formations, finding in them the repertoire it needed. Devices and doctrines alike have careers that their makers cannot schedule, which is the final reason the history of this dispute cannot be written as an account of intentions, and the final confirmation that its object, literature in its relations, is the right one.

## Studying the Dispute: Notes, Archives, and the Working Scholar

The dispute rewards organised study, and a few practical instruments deserve naming for readers who intend to work on it seriously rather than merely to pass an examination on it. The first instrument is the grid itself, kept as a living document: a scholar comparing any two critical methods can build the same four rows, object, change, reader, and content, and force each method to declare itself on each row before any verdict is attempted. The second instrument is the dated dossier, the habit of arranging positions chronologically before comparing them, which this article has argued is the single most effective defence against the standoff myth in any field. The third is the defeater file, in which every verdict the scholar entertains is stored together with the observation that would overturn it, so that reading becomes a test of verdicts rather than a search for confirmations. Students who keep their notes in a structured vault can hold the grid, the dossier, and the defeater file for this dispute in a single linked workspace; the [VaultBook study notes for this series](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) are arranged for exactly that discipline, with the dispute grid as a reusable card and the primary texts indexed by year so that the chronology, and not the textbook order, governs the reading.

Archival reality imposes a humility that the scholar should build into every claim. The record of the dispute is incomplete in patterned ways: the circle's papers suffered from arrests and exiles, the formalists' institutional archives reflect the victors' filing, and whole conversations that the participants treated as common knowledge were never written down at all. Claims about the synthesis in particular must carry their evidence grading openly, distinguishing what the printed texts of 1928 establish, which is a great deal, from what memoir and hindsight suggest about intentions and influences, which is less. The grading habit is the defeater discipline applied to sources, and it protects the article's central claim rather than weakening it, since that claim rests on the strongest stratum of the evidence, two printed documents and a printed critique, whose existence and content no archival accident can disturb. What the archives might still change is the story around the claim, the routes of influence, the drafts, the conversations in Petrograd kitchens, and a scholar should hold the story loosely while holding the documents fast.

### Which three instruments organise serious study of the dispute?

A grid that forces every method to declare itself on object, change, reader, and content before verdicts. A dated dossier that arranges positions chronologically, defeating the standoff myth. A defeater file that stores each verdict beside the observation that would overturn it. Together they convert reading from a search for confirmations into a test of claims.

## The Critique of the Critique: Trotsky, the Associations, and the Road Not Taken

One episode inside the Marxist camp deserves separate treatment, because it shows that the road to the synthesis had political as well as scholarly signposts, and that the road was blocked politically before it was lost intellectually. In the middle 1920s the question of literary policy was genuinely contested inside the ruling party: whether proletarian writers' organisations should be granted hegemony over literature, whether fellow travellers, the non party writers who supplied most of the period's serious prose, should be tolerated or disciplined, and whether the party should issue a line on artistic method at all. The resolution adopted in 1925, under the influence of the more pluralist leadership of that moment, declined to grant any single association hegemony and refused to bind the party to one literary method, a settlement that left the field open for the argument this article has reconstructed. Trotsky's literary writings belonged to that pluralist moment, and his insistence that art must make its own road, judged by its own laws while situated in the revolution's history, is recognisably a cousin of the synthesis arrived at from the political side. The cousinship matters because it shows the middle position was not a scholars' eccentricity; it was, for a few years, close to the operative policy of a revolutionary state toward literature.

The closing of that settlement is the political precondition of everything that followed. As the decade turned, the pluralist resolution was first undermined by the campaigns of the proletarian associations, which behaved as if hegemony were theirs, and then superseded by the centralising reorganisation that dissolved all associations into a single union and prepared the doctrine under which literature would be administered for a generation. Formalism's fate was sealed inside that larger process, as one item in a general settlement of cultural accounts, and the Medvedev circle's fate likewise. The point for the grading of the dispute is that the synthesis lost its political shelter at the same moment it lost its institutional homes, and for connected reasons: a state organising culture for administration has no use for a method whose essence is to specify relations case by case, since specification cannot be hurried, audited, or quoted in a resolution. Administration requires verdicts without defeaters, and the synthesis is a machine for producing verdicts with defeaters attached. Its incompatibility with the administered order was therefore structural, not accidental, and its survival anywhere required institutions of a different kind, plural, slow, and tolerant of unfinished arguments.

### Why was the synthesis structurally incompatible with an administered culture?

Because its method produces specified, revisable claims whose defeaters are stated in advance, while administration requires portable verdicts that can be applied without case by case enquiry. A cultural order organised for command will always find such a method useless or suspect, whatever the method's conclusions in any particular case happen to be.

The road not taken can be sketched without romance. A literary scholarship organised on the 1928 programme, housed in the institutes that already existed, continuous with the pluralist policy of 1925, would have spent the 1930s specifying relations: the sociology of the new reading publics, the careers of devices across the revolutionary divide, the formation of the Soviet reader, the traffic between literature and the new media of radio and film. Some of that work was eventually done, decades later, in other countries and in the thaw, by scholars who had to reconstruct the programme from its fragments. The sketch is not nostalgia; it is a measurement of cost, the standard by which the suppression should be judged. What decree settlement costs a culture is not only the books unwritten in the year of the decree but the research tradition that does not accumulate, the students not trained, the questions that wait two generations to be asked again by people who believe they are asking them first. The formalism case lets that cost be stated with unusual precision, because the programme that was stopped survives in print, dated, brief, and legible, a fossil of a future that did not occur.

## Irony and Parody: The Devices That Testified for Both Sides

Certain devices served as witnesses for both camps, and none more than parody, whose career in the dispute deserves a section of its own. For the formalists, parody was a central mechanism of literary evolution: the moment a form becomes automatised, parody exposes its machinery, and the exposure is both a criticism of the exhausted form and a laboratory for its successor. Tynyanov's work on parody treated it as a dialectical device, at once destruction and construction, and the school's own prose, Shklovsky's above all, practised parody as a critical instrument, deflating solemn conventions by performing them badly on purpose. Parody, in this account, is the literary system thinking about itself, the clearest evidence that literature has an internal life of forms commenting on forms.

The circle's account of parody begins from the same phenomenon and arrives at the complementary half. Parody is double voiced discourse: two intentions, two accents, two social voices occupy a single utterance, the parodied style and the parodist's attitude toward it, and the effect depends entirely on the reader's ability to hear both voices and the distance between them. That ability is a social competence, stocked by the reader's formation in the genres and registers being parodied, so the device that formalists cited as proof of the system's internal life is simultaneously proof that the system's life runs through socially formed hearers. A parody of a bureaucratic circular lands for readers who have suffered circulars; a parody of a sentimental novel lands for readers raised on sentimental novels. The two accounts do not compete. They describe the mechanism and its conditions, and the fact that the dispute's most contested device requires both descriptions to be intelligible is a compact argument for the synthesis, small enough to carry into any seminar.

### Why does parody testify for both schools at once?

For formalism, parody is the system exposing its own automatised machinery and generating successors, evidence of an internal life of forms. For the circle, parody is double voiced discourse whose effect depends on the reader's socially stocked competence in the parodied registers. The device is unintelligible without both descriptions, mechanism and condition together.

Irony, parody's quieter sibling, divides the schools' competences in the same pattern. Formalist analysis locates irony in construction, in the gap between what a narration says and what its arrangement shows, and can map that gap with precision across a work. The Marxist question asks whose irony it is: which social position makes this gap visible, which readers are invited inside it and which are left as its objects, how a period's dominant ironies encode its settlements about what may not be said directly. The history of irony in the literatures of administered societies, where indirection was a survival technology, gives the second question an archival richness the first cannot supply, while the first question keeps the second honest by refusing to let irony be discussed as a free floating attitude. A criticism that can hold both, locating the gap and naming its social geometry, is again the synthesis in working order, and the frequency with which the pattern recurs, device after device, is the practical ground on which this article's verdict rests.

## The Short Story and the Sketch: Minor Forms as Evidence

Major genres attract the manifestos, but minor forms often carry the cleanest evidence, and the short prose forms of the Russian tradition repay the dispute's instruments particularly well. The sketch, the ocherk, sat between literature and journalism in the period's hierarchy: a documentary account of observed life, factories, villages, courts, and streets, prized by editors and distrusted by aesthetes. For the formalists, the sketch was a problem in the theory of literary fact, since its literariness could not rest on invention and had to be located in selection, arrangement, and the pressure of a perceiving voice on reported material, exactly the operations their prose studies had catalogued in fiction. Eichenbaum's and Tynyanov's interest in such border forms was methodological: a theory of the literary that cannot process the sketch, the feuilleton, the diary, and the memoir will fail on a large share of what periods actually read. For the Marxist critics, the sketch was evidence of literature's social metabolism in near laboratory purity, since its material arrived from reportage with its charges visible, its audience was the newspaper public, and its career tracked the demand for knowledge of a society transforming itself.

The border forms thus became a shared laboratory in the late 1920s, one of the places where the two programmes were visibly converging before the ending. The fact literature movement, associated with the LEF circle and with writers such as Sergei Tretyakov, proposed to dissolve the boundary deliberately, building literature from documents, biographies of objects, and reportage, and the proposal drew formalist analysis and Marxist purpose into a single practice: the document's social charge was the point, and the construction that organised the document was the art. Whatever the movement's artistic yield, its theoretical significance for this dispute is large, because it shows practitioners of the period treating the border between the literary series and its neighbours as a workplace rather than a wall, which is the theses of 1928 translated into a literary programme. The administered culture that followed had no use for border work of any kind, preferring its genres classified and its documents and fictions in separate ministries, and the laboratory closed with the rest.

### Why do minor and border forms give the dispute its cleanest evidence?

Because in the sketch, the feuilleton, and the documentary forms, the two halves of the phenomenon are both visible at once: construction operating on material whose social charge has not been disguised by invention. Major genres let each school hide in its favourite half; border forms force the meeting that the synthesis describes.

The lesson generalises to the student's own reading. When a comparison of methods is in doubt, take it to a border case, a text whose status is mixed, a memoir that reads as a novel, a report that behaves as literature, a lyric built from a document. Pure cases flatter pure methods, since each method defined purity in its own image. Border cases expose the joints, showing where description runs out and relation must begin, and where relation, in turn, must stop and let description speak. The habit of testing methods on border cases is another of the transferable disciplines this dispute teaches, and it has the incidental virtue of directing attention to the minor forms themselves, which are usually better reading, and always better evidence, than the monuments over which methods prefer to fight.

## Two Temperaments of Criticism: The Laboratory and the Forum

At the deepest level the dispute opposed two temperaments of criticism, and naming them helps a reader recognise the quarrel's recurrences in other periods and other languages. The laboratory temperament wants criticism to be a cumulative inquiry: defined objects, shared procedures, results that stand until better results replace them, and a professional community as the arbiter. Its prose is technical, its pleasures are those of exactness, and its characteristic failure is sterility, the refinement of procedures on objects too small to matter. The forum temperament wants criticism to be a public reasoning about the life a society leads in its imagination: verdicts argued before the common reader, stakes stated in the open, and history as the arbiter. Its prose is rhetorical, its pleasures are those of significance, and its characteristic failure is the verdict untethered from the object, opinion in the costume of knowledge. Russian formalism is the laboratory temperament's purest literary instance, and the Marxist criticism of the period is the forum temperament armed with a theory of history. Their quarrel was therefore never only about literature. It was about what kind of activity criticism is, and every subsequent age, including the ages of close reading, of theory, and of the various returns to history, has restaged the quarrel under new names because the temperaments are permanent possibilities of the critical mind.

The synthesis, seen in this light, is a proposal about the temperament of a mature criticism: laboratory standards for claims about works, forum standards for claims about their lives, and the discipline of knowing which standard governs which sentence. That proposal is harder to institutionalise than either temperament alone, since laboratories and forums have different rewards, different audiences, and different clocks, and a critic practising both will be suspected by each camp of betraying it to the other, as the mediating figures of the Russian dispute were suspected in their time. The suspicion is the tax on the middle position in every generation, and the Russian case suggests the tax is worth paying, since the alternatives on record are a laboratory that the state can close for irrelevance to its purposes and a forum that the state can staff. Between those fates, the taxed and suspected middle, with its grid, its dossier, and its defeaters, is the only position from which criticism has ever accumulated knowledge and kept its conscience at the same time.

### Are the two schools best understood as methods or as temperaments?

As both, in layers. Their methods are teachable and testable, but beneath the methods sit temperaments, the laboratory's desire for cumulative exactness and the forum's desire for public significance, which recur in every period's criticism under new names. The synthesis is a temperament as much as a method: it is the discipline of applying each standard where it governs.

## The Czech Contribution: Mukarovsky and the Aesthetic Norm

The Prague development deserves a fuller hearing than the earlier summary allowed, because Jan Mukarovsky's work shows a mind formed by formalism growing, under no political compulsion, toward the relational position. Mukarovsky began from the formalist inheritance: the work of art as a structure of components, foregrounding as the violation of an automatised norm, the aesthetic function as one function among others that an object can serve. His decisive step was to treat the norm itself as a social fact. A norm exists only where a community holds it, enforces it through taste, schooling, and ridicule, and feels its violation as a shock or a liberation. Foregrounding, therefore, cannot be described as a relation between a text and language in the abstract; it is a relation among a text, a norm, and the collectivity whose norm it is. The aesthetic object, in his mature formulation, resides neither in the physical artefact nor in any single consciousness but in the consciousness of a community, where the artefact's structure is realised as a value. With that step the sealed work is gone, replaced by a structure that exists socially, while the structure itself, its components and their tensions, remains as describable as any formalist could wish.

Mukarovsky also supplied a dynamics that the Russian debate had lacked. Norms, on his account, are not merely broken by strong works; they are perpetually renegotiated, as violations succeed, are absorbed, and become the background against which fresh violations register. The canon is the sediment of that negotiation, and its authority at any moment reflects a settlement among institutions, generations, and classes of readers rather than a verdict of eternity. He wrote these things in a democratic republic in the 1930s, in dialogue with the Russian inheritance and with Czech structuralism's own resources, and the freedom of the setting is part of the evidence this article has been assembling: given open institutions, the formalist programme developed toward society by its own logic, without needing a decree in either direction. The Prague school's later fate, overtaken by war and then by a regime that preferred its aesthetics administered, repeats the Russian pattern in a minor key and confirms that the pattern is about institutions, not about nations or doctrines.

### What did Mukarovsky add to the inheritance he received?

He socialised the norm. Foregrounding became a relation among a text, a standard held by a community, and the readers formed by that community, so the aesthetic object lives in collective consciousness while remaining structurally describable. The move completes, from the formalist side, the same journey the theses had sketched, and it was reached in open institutions by argument rather than by pressure.

The Czech case also refines the article's verdict about what suppression cost. Prague shows the programme's growth points under freedom for roughly a decade longer than Russia allowed, and the growth was rapid: from structure to norm, from norm to value, from value to the social life of the canon. Extrapolating that decade across the half century the Russian programme was denied is speculation, and the article has avoided speculation where documents exist. But the direction of the Prague work is a document, and it points the same way as the Dostoevsky study and the literary environment concept: toward a criticism in which structure and society are two descriptions of one object, each incomplete without the other, and each sharpened by the friction between them. Three independent lines, Leningrad, the circle, and Prague, converging on that single shape within fifteen years, is as close to a controlled demonstration as the history of criticism provides.

## Verse Drama and the Stage: A Third Test of the Instruments

Drama tests the two instruments differently from either verse or prose, because a play's text is a score for an event that happens in a room, before a gathered audience whose reactions are part of the machinery. Formalist analysis of drama found its devices readily enough: the aside and the soliloquy as conventions for managing knowledge, the well made plot's retardations and recognitions, verse forms whose elevation or plainness positions the speakers. Yet every one of those devices is calibrated to a live collectivity in a way that a lyric's devices are not. A soliloquy works because an audience accepts the convention of overhearing thought, and the convention is a social contract renewed nightly, variable across periods and publics. The Marxist questions therefore enter dramatic analysis at the ground floor rather than through a later supplement: who gathered in that room, under whose patronage, at what prices, with which expectations stocked by which earlier performances? The history of theatre audiences, their composition and discipline, is not background to the history of dramatic form. It is one of its moving parts, since dramatists write for the house they expect.

The synthesis's handling of drama follows the established pattern and gains a vivid illustration from the fate of verse drama itself. Elevated verse on stage estranged and dignified in periods when audiences were trained by liturgy, school, and rhetoric to hear it; as that training thinned, the same verse registered as remoteness, and dramatists who kept it had to rebuild its contract with the house or accept a coterie audience. Nothing inside the verse changed. The relation between the construction and the formation of its hearers changed, and the change decided the device's career more completely than any internal exhaustion could. A purely internal history can narrate the decline as automatisation, and the narration is not false, but it mistakes the location of the wear: the wearing surface was the audience's schooling, an institution, and its history is written in curricula and chapels as much as in playhouses. Drama thus supplies the reader row of the grid with its most concrete case, and students who grasp the verse drama example rarely relapse into treating perception as a constant of human nature.

## The Essay and the Feuilleton: Criticism's Own Minor Forms

The dispute's instruments turn profitably on criticism's own genres, which neither school examined with the same zeal it brought to poems and novels. The review, the essay, and the feuilleton are the forms in which literary life actually conducts its business, and each is a border form in the sense developed earlier: part document, part construction, addressed to a public whose formation the writer can presume upon in detail. A review's devices are as analysable as a tale's: the delayed verdict, the quoted passage made to testify, the plot summary that smuggles judgement in as description, the closing sentence engineered to be repeated. A formalist criticism of criticism, applying the school's own tools to the school's own practice, is a bracing exercise that the historical formalists largely spared themselves, and its absence is a small irony in their record, since no literature is more device ridden than the literature about literature.

The Marxist questions meet the review on its home ground, because a review is an institutional act before it is an aesthetic one: it appears in a journal with a line, addresses subscribers of a describable kind, and participates in the allocation of reputation, which is a currency convertible into sales, posts, and prizes. To specify a review's relations is to map a small economy of prestige, and the mapping explains features of critical prose that device analysis alone leaves mysterious, such as the distribution of severity across a reviewer's career or the synchronised enthusiasms of a season. The synthesis's programme, applied reflexively, yields a sociology and a rhetoric of criticism in one account, and the reflexive turn is more than a parlour game. A field that cannot describe its own forms and institutions will keep mistaking its fashions for discoveries and its exclusions for judgements, which is the professional form of the disease both schools, at their worst, suffered from.

### What happens when the dispute's instruments are turned on criticism itself?

The review and the essay become border forms open to both analyses at once: their devices yield to exact description, while their appearance, timing, and severity belong to an economy of reputation run by journals and publics. Reflexive application exposes a field's fashions and exclusions as facts about its institutions, not only about its objects.

There is a historical dividend in this reflexive section, because the Russian dispute was itself conducted in these minor forms and cannot be understood apart from them. The manifestos were feuilletons in spirit, brilliant, quotable, and built to provoke a season's argument; the denunciations were reviews in the administered style, verdict first and evidence as decoration; the theses were a memorandum, a form addressed to colleagues rather than to the public, which partly explains their faint echo. Genre decisions shaped the quarrel's transmission as surely as doctrine did. The settlement lost partly because it was stated in the least portable genres, a scholarly monograph and a set of theses, while the extremes were stated in manifestos and slogans, the most portable genres there are. Intellectual history that attends only to doctrines will miss that mechanical fact, and the fact generalises grimly: in any field, the positions that travel are the positions whose genres travel, and a syllabus assembled from travelling genres will systematically overrepresent the extremes of every dispute it teaches.

## What the Dispute Teaches About Reading Disputes

A long comparison should end by surrendering its method, and this dispute's method, assembled across the preceding sections, can be stated as a small set of transferable rules for reading any quarrel between schools. Date every position before grading it, since schools move and polemic grades them at their most convenient moments. Distinguish a position's defeat from its disappearance, and ask, whenever a school vanishes suddenly, which institutions changed hands in the season of its vanishing. Look for the settlement documents in the last publications before any silence, since convergence, where it happens, happens in print and is usually brief, technical, and unquotable. Attach a defeater to every verdict, including the verdict that a settlement exists. Test each method on the rival camp's favourite cases and on border cases, where pure methods show their joints. Attend to genres and translations, the unglamorous machinery by which positions travel or fail to. None of these rules mentions Marxism or formalism, and all of them were learned from watching Marxism and formalism collide, converge, and be parted by force.

The rules also describe, in retrospect, the standard this series has applied across its clusters, in which theories and their state records are kept separate, objections are stated at full strength before verdicts, and every verdict carries the observation that would reverse it. The literary dispute is the cluster where that standard is easiest to teach, because the documents are short, the convergence is datable to a single year, and the suppression is documented in a single recantation. A reader who masters the standard here can carry it to harder cases, to disputes whose documents run to volumes and whose settlements, if any, are still being negotiated. That portability is the final answer to the student who asks why a quarrel among Russian critics of a century ago deserves forty thousand words. It deserves them because it is the clearest available specimen of how criticism knows things, loses them, and finds them again, and because its settlement, drafted and buried and rebuilt anonymously across a century, is still the best available map of the ground on which any serious criticism stands.

## The Short Century of the Word Device: A Term and Its Career

The career of a single critical term can summarise a dispute, and no term in this one has had a stranger career than device. In the formalists' hands the word was a precision instrument: a device is an identifiable operation performed on material, a retardation, a framing, a shift of voice, whose presence can be pointed to and whose effect can be argued about. The precision was the point. A criticism armed with devices could disagree productively, because the parties to the disagreement were looking at the same identifiable thing. When the term passed into general critical usage, it thinned. Device became a compliment or a complaint, a way of calling a passage clever or mechanical, with the pointing and the arguing quietly dropped. Textbooks speak of a writer's devices the way catalogues speak of features, and the word that once demanded demonstration survives as decoration. The thinning is a small instance of automatisation operating inside criticism itself: a term that estranged critical prose in 1920 is processed without attention a century later, its work done, its edge gone.

The Marxist side had its own travelling term with a parallel fate. Mediation entered the dispute's aftermath as the name of a research obligation, the tracing of transformations between orders of fact, and in much later usage it thinned into a password, a word whose utterance certified that reflection had been avoided and reduction disowned, while the tracing itself went unperformed. Both terms thus illustrate the law their owners discovered in literature: success breeds imitation, imitation breeds invisibility, and the instruments of a living method decay into the mannerisms of a school. The remedy the formalists prescribed for literature applies to criticism as well. Terms must be periodically made strange again, returned to the operations they named, tested against cases where they might fail. A reader who performs that renewal on device and mediation, asking of each use what operation is being pointed to and what transformation is being traced, holds the dispute's two inheritances in their working rather than their decorative form.

### What happened to the dispute's key terms as they entered general usage?

They automatised. Device thinned from a demand for demonstration into a vague label for technique, and mediation thinned from a research obligation into a password certifying good intentions. The decay follows the law the formalists found in literature itself, and the remedy is theirs as well: return each term to the operation it names and test it on cases.

The career of a third term completes the set. Literariness, the school's founding coinage, travelled least of the three, remaining a specialist's word, and its confinement is itself instructive. Terms travel when they name something neighbouring fields need; narratology needed function and took it, stylistics needed foregrounding and took it, while literariness named a question, what makes a work literary at all, that neighbouring fields preferred to leave unasked once their own objects were defined. The unasked question did not disappear. It waits inside every syllabus decision, every prize shortlist, every act of shelving that separates literature from information, answered implicitly by institutions that would struggle to state their criteria. The formalists' most provocative legacy may be that they asked the question in a form that cannot be unasked, even by fields that have forgotten who posed it, and the Marxist completion of the question, whose criteria, formed where, in whose interest, waits inside the same decisions with equal patience.

## Hospitality and Hostility: The Personal Texture of the Quarrel

Intellectual history written only from doctrines misses the texture that decided many outcomes, and this quarrel's texture included genuine hospitality alongside the hostility. The Petrograd and Moscow scholars argued in one another's seminars, published in one another's collections, and shared the material hardships of the revolutionary years, when a heated room and a ration of paper were scholarly infrastructure. Shklovsky's memoirs of the period move between futurist studios, formalist meetings, and the fronts of the civil war with a picaresque energy that reminds the reader how young the participants were and how improvised their institutions remained. The Bakhtin circle's gatherings in Nevel and Vitebsk had the same character, philosophy conducted in provincial rooms by men whose formal posts were precarious or nonexistent. Polemic in such a world was not the warfare of established faculties; it was the noise of a small company that dined together, and its ferocity coexisted with debts, dedications, and the mutual knowledge that the other side's best work was worth stealing from.

The hardening of the late 1920s is therefore a story about the destruction of texture as much as about doctrine. As associations were reorganised and journals changed hands, the personal channels through which concessions had travelled, the seminar argument, the shared collection, the review that an opponent would actually read, were replaced by channels designed for alignment, in which a text's function was to signal its author's position in a campaign. Concessions are made to persons; campaigns receive only submissions or defiance. The synthesis required the older texture, since it asked each side to publish its surrender of a cherished boundary in the expectation that the other side would read it fairly, and by the time the theses and the critique appeared, the texture that could have received them as contributions was already being dismantled around their authors. Timing, in intellectual history, is an institutional fact before it is a personal tragedy, and the tragedy here is precise: the settlement arrived in the last season in which it could have been heard, and was heard by almost nobody, because the hearing apparatus was being rebuilt for other purposes in the same months.

## Children, Tales, and the Schoolroom: The Dispute at Its Most Concrete

One arena brought the dispute's abstractions down to decisions that can be pictured, the literature written and selected for children. The folktale, which the formalists had made a laboratory of pure form, was in the same years a contested inheritance in the schoolroom, where educators argued over whether wonder tales should be given to children of the new order at all. Detractors of the tales, speaking a sociological idiom, held that their kings, miracles, and magical helpers stocked young imaginations with the furniture of a discarded world. Defenders answered from several directions at once, and the formalist analysis armed the strongest of them: the tale's hold on children could be described as a constructional fact, a machinery of testing, helping, and reward whose satisfactions did not depend on believing in tsars, and whose functions could be traced in the child's own play. The defence did not deny that tales carry a world with them. It denied that the carried world was the whole of the tale's action, and it demanded that any policy toward children's reading reckon with the machinery as well as with the furniture.

The schoolroom case is worth dwelling on because it reverses the usual prestige of the two methods. In the seminar room, sociological criticism often sounds like the serious adult and formalism like the game. At the point where a decision must be made about actual books for actual children, the formalist description becomes the practical instrument, since it predicts what a tale will do to its readers, while the sociological inventory of furniture, detached from the machinery, predicts almost nothing, as the period's own experience showed when approved literature of correct furniture and inert construction failed to hold its audience. The synthesis's formula earns its keep in such cases with unusual clarity: keep the machinery analysis, keep the account of the carried world, and let the decision be argued where the two meet, in the formed expectations of child readers and the institutions that stock their shelves. The case also left a residue in the period's best writing for children, where poets trained in the formalist milieu built verses whose sound play and constructional wit treated young readers as perceivers to be delighted rather than as vessels to be filled, a practice that outlasted the polemics on every side.

### Why did children's tales become a testing ground for the whole dispute?

Because there the abstractions met a decision. The tales' furniture could be inventoried sociologically, while their hold on readers could only be explained constructionally, through the machinery of functions the formalists had mapped. Policy made from either half alone failed in practice, and the case showed the synthesis working as an instrument of judgement rather than as a slogan.

## The Memoir as Evidence: What the Survivors Chose to Remember

The dispute's later memory was shaped by its survivors, and the shaping deserves the same source criticism this article has applied to the doctrines. Shklovsky lived long enough to become the school's memorialist, and his retrospective writings, witty, elliptical, and self protective in the manner of a man who had survived by indirection, fixed an image of the formalist years as a heroic youth of discovery betrayed by philistines. The image is not false, but it is a survivor's composition, foregrounding the manifestos and the friendships while passing lightly over the school's own dogmatisms and over the concessions of its maturity, which sat awkwardly with the heroic frame. Jakobson's retrospections, delivered from the summit of an international career, composed the past differently, as a genealogy of structural science in which the Russian years figure as a founding chapter of a world discipline. That composition foregrounds continuity and method, and passes lightly over the quarrel with Marxism, which in the genealogy becomes a local disturbance rather than the pressure under which the method matured.

Against both compositions the documentary record assembled in this article stands as a corrective, and the corrective illustrates a general rule for the history of criticism: memoirs tell the truth about what survival required their authors to believe, while the dated texts tell the truth about what the arguments were. The Bakhtin circle left almost no memoir at all, its memory carried instead by students, by the survival of manuscripts in unlikely hands, and by the posthumous publication that restored its works to circulation in an order dictated by accident. A tradition whose memory arrives by accident will be misremembered in patterned ways, its systematic works read as isolated brilliance, its engagements with opponents forgotten, its 1928 critique shelved as a curiosity of attribution. The recovery of the synthesis in recent scholarship is therefore not a fashion but a repair, the slow substitution of the documentary record for the survivors' compositions, and this article has tried to perform the substitution in full view, grading its central claim on printed texts of a single year and flagging every place where the evidence thins into memoir, inference, or hindsight.

## The Bibliography as a Battlefield: Editions, Anthologies, and What Got Reprinted

Wars between schools are also fought in bibliographies, and the material history of editions decided much of what later readers could think about this dispute. The formalists' early collections were products of small Petrograd publishing ventures, printed in modest runs during years of paper shortage, and many copies perished with the libraries that held them. The Medvedev volume appeared under a state publisher's imprint and was withdrawn from circulation as its circle fell, surviving in scattered holdings. The theses occupied a few pages of a journal devoted to the theory of literature and language, a venue few libraries outside the Soviet Union received. When Western scholars after the Second World War set out to recover Russian formalism, they worked from what emigration, chance, and a handful of Slavic collections had preserved, and the recovery's shape, manifestos prominent, late work faint, reflects the archive's accidents as faithfully as it reflects the school's development. Anthologies then canonised the recovery's shape, reprinting the vivid early essays in volume after volume while the documents of convergence waited for their editors.

Editors and translators, in this story, are unacknowledged legislators of intellectual history. The decision to translate a manifesto rather than a monograph, to anthologise the essay of 1917 rather than the theses of 1928, to publish the circle's work under the heading of one man's name, each decision quietly rebuilt the dispute in the receiving culture, and the rebuilt version, a young school of pure form assaulted by pure sociology, was the version against which two generations of scholars defined their own positions. The repair work of recent decades, critical editions, complete translations, the patient reconstruction of the circle's bibliography, has been unglamorous and decisive in equal measure. It demonstrates a proposition the synthesis itself would endorse: the life of ideas is a material life, conducted through paper, print runs, and library shelves, and a history of criticism that ignores its own bibliography is formalism of the worst kind, a story of pure meanings floating free of the channels that carried them.

### Why does the bibliography of the dispute matter to its interpretation?

Because survival was selective. Small print runs, withdrawn volumes, and untranslated journals decided which texts later scholarship could build on, and anthologies canonised the accident as if it were the school's essence. The received image of a young, extreme formalism is partly a shelving fact, and repairing the bibliography has been a precondition of seeing the convergence at all.

The point extends to the digital afterlife of the texts, stated without reference to any particular platform or fashion. Works that are scanned, indexed, and assigned to courses multiply; works that exist only in decaying holdings recede, whatever their arguments. The 1928 documents have benefited from the same machinery that buried their rivals' pamphlets in obscurity, and a scholar alert to channels will treat the settlement's rising visibility not as the automatic triumph of a better argument but as a change in its material conditions, welcome, fragile, and reversible. Arguments do not win by themselves. They win when institutions carry them to readers formed to receive them, which is, in the end, the whole doctrine of the dispute applied to the dispute itself.

## Silence, Evasion, and the Unwritten: Reading the Dispute's Gaps

A final instrument deserves a place in the comparison, the reading of silences, because both schools practised it on literature while rarely applying it to themselves. The formalists taught readers to notice what a construction excludes, the road not taken by a plot, the voice a narration cannot admit. The circle taught readers to hear the unspoken inside the spoken, the evaluation a word carries without announcing. Turned on the dispute's own texts, the instruments find instructive gaps. The formalist manifestos are silent about the audience's formation at exactly the points where their theory of perception most needs it. The sociological criticism of the associations is silent about construction at exactly the points where its verdicts on particular works most need it. The theses and the critique are eloquent precisely where the others are silent, which is one more way of stating the convergence: each settlement document speaks into the other camp's characteristic gap.

The silences of the ending are of a different order, and they should be named without melodrama. After 1930 the questions this quarrel had opened did not get answered in Soviet print; they got avoided, and the avoidance is visible in the period's criticism as a set of shaped absences, topics approached and swerved from, names unmentioned in contexts that cry out for them, a whole generation's work organised around a centre it could not discuss. Readers trained by this dispute learn to map such absences rather than merely deplore them, since an absence with a shape is evidence about the pressure that produced it. The skill transfers to every administered literature and to some unadministered ones, where fashion rather than fear draws the map of the unsayable. In either case the critic's task is the synthesis's task once more: describe the construction exactly, specify the conditions that formed it, and compose the sentence in which the two accounts join, even when, especially when, the sentence concerns the conditions under which sentences themselves are permitted.

## The Lecture Hall and the Little Magazine: Two Rooms Where Criticism Lived

Criticism inhabits rooms, and the dispute's rooms shaped its voices as surely as its doctrines did. The seminar room rewarded the formalist manner: a text on the table, a claim that could be checked against the page in front of everyone, a premium on the colleague who noticed what others had walked past. Arguments in that room ended when the passage was read aloud and the description either fitted or failed to fit, a discipline that bred exactness and a certain impatience with any consideration that could not be brought back to the words. The little magazine rewarded a different manner: the manifesto, the review that drew blood, the programme stated in slogans sharp enough to survive quotation. Arguments in that room ended when the season turned, and they bred brilliance, cruelty, and a taste for positions that could be recognised at a distance. The quarrel between the schools was partly a quarrel between these rooms, each mistaking its own acoustics for the nature of truth.

A third room overshadowed both by the decade's end, the committee room, where criticism was discussed as a branch of administration and where the operative questions concerned loyalty, utility, and the management of reputations at scale. The committee room's acoustics deformed every voice that entered it: descriptions became denunciations or certificates, programmes became lines, and scholars learned to write sentences that could survive being read aloud by an enemy. The settlement of 1928 was composed for the first two rooms and perished in the third, which had no procedure for receiving a claim whose defeater was attached, since a defeater, in committee acoustics, sounds like a confession of doubt, and doubt was the one contribution the room could not use. Later ages rebuilt the first two rooms in other countries and other languages, and the settlement was rebuilt with them, anonymously, by scholars who believed they were inventing a method. The rooms explain the reinvention better than any history of influences: give criticism a seminar and a magazine and enough years, and it will rediscover that works are made things with social lives, because the rooms themselves teach the two halves of that sentence in alternation.

## Paper, Print Runs, and the Price of Books: The Material Floor of the Argument

Every claim in this dispute was printed on paper that somebody allocated, in a country where paper was rationed by war, revolution, and scarcity in succession, and the material floor of the argument is not a metaphor. The early formalist collections appeared in editions whose smallness reflected the economics of Petrograd publishing in the years of collapse, when printers lacked stock, fuel, and sometimes premises. Journals lived from issue to issue, and a journal's death, by bankruptcy or by administrative decision, could orphan a whole line of research more completely than any refutation. The circle around Bakhtin published through provincial and precarious channels, in Vitebsk and Leningrad imprints that distributed unevenly, so that a book could be famous in two cities and unknown in a third. When later scholars ask why the settlement of 1928 found so few readers, part of the answer is embarrassingly physical: the documents existed in small numbers, in few libraries, in a distribution system that war and reorganisation kept breaking.

The material floor cuts both ways as evidence, and the article's method requires noticing the second direction as well. Scarcity also explains the intensity of the period's polemics, since a small world of print, in which every participant read every journal, made each essay an event and each review a public act with personal consequences. It explains the speed with which reputations formed and the completeness with which they could be destroyed, there being few alternative channels through which a banned scholar could still reach readers. And it explains a feature of the texts themselves that purely doctrinal histories miss, their compression: the theses are brief because journal pages were scarce and because their authors wrote for colleagues who shared the context that a longer statement would have had to supply. Brevity, in the settlement documents, is a material fact before it is a stylistic one, and the settlement's later invisibility is partly the invisibility of short texts in small journals, a fate that bulkier and worse books escaped by the simple advantage of being harder to lose.

### Why should a history of this dispute count print runs and paper?

Because the arguments travelled in physical objects whose scarcity decided their audiences. Small editions, dying journals, and broken distribution limited the settlement's readership in its own time, while the same scarcity concentrated the polemical world and made reputations quick to form and quick to destroy. The material floor is part of the explanation, not scenery beneath it.

## A Note on Fairness: Reading Opponents at Their Strongest

The procedure followed throughout this comparison deserves a closing statement, because it is itself one of the dispute's lessons. Each school has been quoted from its own best texts, dated, and graded at its mature rather than its juvenile form. Each objection has been given the force its ablest author gave it, and each verdict has been paired with the evidence that would overturn it. That procedure is not decoration. It is the only known antidote to the two diseases that ruined the original quarrel's reception, the grading of opponents at their weakest and the conversion of administrative outcomes into intellectual ones. A reader who has watched the procedure operate across forty thousand words on a single dispute owns a tool that transfers: to other schools, other centuries, other arts. State the rival case so well that its holders would sign it. Date it. Find the document where the rivals converged, if one exists. Ask what ended the argument, and whether the ending was an answer. Attach the defeater. The Russian critics who taught this procedure did so partly by violating it, in polemics their better work rebukes, and partly by exemplifying it, in a critique and a set of theses that remain, nearly a century on, the fairest pages either side produced. Honouring them means using the procedure on them as rigorously as on their enemies, which this article has attempted, and accepting its verdicts where they fall, including the verdict that falls hardest on the tradition this series exists to study.

## The Small Courtesies of Scholarship: Footnotes, Acknowledgements, and the Recovery of Names

Recovery is also a matter of courtesy, and the dispute's aftermath shows how small scholarly acts repair large administrative damage. A footnote that attributes the 1928 critique to its circle rather than to a single convenient name, an edition that restores a dropped chapter, a translation that prints the theses alongside the manifestos, a syllabus that puts the recantation beside the argument it retracted: each is a small courtesy, and together they are the mechanism by which a buried settlement returns to circulation. Scholarship often imagines itself as the production of new knowledge, and it is, but much of its real work is custodial, the patient maintenance of a record against the institutions that would simplify it. The names matter here in a way the article's own argument about authorship might seem to deny. Attribution is not the same as intention worship; it is the record of who did the work, under what conditions, at what cost, and a field that loses its attributions loses its capacity to honour its debts and to recognise its martyrs. The formalism case, whose carriers were exiled, silenced, and in one case erased, is among the places where getting the names right is not a pedantry but a debt, and the debt is discharged in the unglamorous currency of footnotes, reissues, and careful editions.

The courtesy extends to opponents. To cite a formalist argument in the form its author finally gave it, rather than in the manifesto form its enemies preferred, is a courtesy with argumentative consequences, since the grading changes when the position is dated correctly. To cite the circle's critique with its concession intact, rather than as the denunciation its decade preferred, is the same courtesy in the other direction. Whole distortions of the dispute can be traced to discourtesies of citation, repeated until they hardened into the received account, and whole repairs can be effected by reversing them, one accurate reference at a time.

## For students and exam candidates

Hold the dispute as a story with dates, not as a pair of statues. The examinable core is compact: formalism asks what makes a work literary and answers with devices and literariness; Marxist criticism asks under what circumstances the work was made and whose lived experience its forms digest; the Bakhtin circle's 1928 critique conceded the first question and socialised the second; the theses of the same year proposed the literary series in relation to other series; suppression, not refutation, ended the argument. Build answers on the dispute grid, one row at a time, and attach a defeater to every verdict, since the attribution rule and the defeater standard are what separate strong scripts from summaries. The companion drills in The exam guide for this cluster rehearses the comparison in examination form, and the study notes linked earlier in this article keep the grid, the chronology, and the primary texts in one place for revision. Practise the micro example method on any poem set for comment: describe the construction first, then specify its conditions, then write the sentence where the two meet.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

Teach the comparison as a convergence cut short, and let the documents do the work. Set the 1917 essay beside the 1928 theses and ask the seminar to measure the distance travelled; set the Medvedev critique beside the manifestos and ask whose concessions came first. The dispute grid makes an effective seminar instrument when students must fill its fourth column from the primary texts rather than from summaries, and the recantation of 1930, read aloud at the end of the sequence, teaches the difference between an argument's end and its administration more efficiently than any lecture on method. Guard against the two classroom myths, that formalism ignored meaning and that Marxism refuted it, by returning whenever they surface to the dated page. The comparison also trains transferable habits, chronological grading of positions, defeaters attached to verdicts, border cases used as tests, which students will carry into every other topic on the syllabus long after the Russian names have faded.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

The open problems are stated in the agenda section, and the archives still have evidence to give on routes of influence, drafts, and the circle's internal division of labour. Researchers should hold the central claim, the 1928 settlement, on its printed stratum, two documents and a critique whose content is not in doubt, while treating memoir based reconstructions of intention with graded confidence. The translation record needs continued work, since key texts reached other languages late and the dispute's Western reenactment cannot be understood without the bibliography of what was available when. Historians of scholarship will find in this case a compact instance of a general phenomenon, settlement by decree, with the unusual advantage that the buried settlement survives entire. Journalists covering the politics of universities and culture ministries in any country will recognise the pattern's instruments, the floating term of abuse, the reorganised association, the extracted recantation, and may find in the formalism case a documented precedent for asking, whenever a field's argument ends suddenly, what was about to be settled when the ending arrived.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is the difference between Marxist and formalist criticism?

One tradition begins by interrogating a work's construction, hunting for the operations that make its language literary rather than documentary. The other begins by interrogating a work's circumstances, the society that financed, printed, censored, and read it, and the experience of classes that its forms digest. Because the starting questions diverge, the two methods often talk past each other, and the historical quarrel ignited when each denied that the rival starting point could support a complete criticism by itself.

### Q: What is defamiliarisation?

It is Shklovsky's 1917 proposal about the purpose of art. Habit, he argued, turns perception into shorthand, so people register objects without truly encountering them. Artistic form interrupts the shorthand by rendering the habitual object odd: an action described without its name, a scene narrated by an unlikely witness, a rhythm that refuses to settle. Encounter replaces registration. The notion describes what art does for perception in general, and particular techniques matter only as the servants of that recovery.

### Q: What is literariness?

The formalists coined the term for the property that separates a literary work from every other kind of writing, and they made that property the defining object of their discipline. A laundry list and a lyric may share a vocabulary; what they cannot share is organisation. Sound, structure, and device are arranged in the lyric so that language itself becomes palpable. Jakobson later softened the boundary into a matter of degree, treating the literary as a function that speech performs whenever wording is prized for its own sake.

### Q: What did Marxists object to in Formalism?

Three complaints recur wherever the objection is made carefully. A method that seals the work inside its construction cannot discuss the publishers, censors, markets, and schools through which literature actually lives. A history of forms that runs on wear and replacement cannot say why fresh material floods in from one direction at one moment and from another direction at another moment. And a psychology of perception that treats habit as universal ignores that readers are trained, unevenly and socially, into the very expectations a device exists to frustrate.

### Q: What did Formalists object to in Marxist criticism?

Their countercharge targeted method rather than politics. Take any procedure that reads a work straight off into the circumstances of its age, they said, and apply it honestly: a novel, a leader column, and a court transcript from the same season will yield indistinguishable readings, because the explanatory machinery never touches what separates literature from paperwork. A criticism blind to that separation may be excellent social history, but it has surrendered the object that makes it literary criticism, and its verdicts on individual works are unearned.

### Q: What was the Bakhtin circle's critique of Formalism?

A 1928 volume issued under Pavel Medvedev's name, written within the orbit of Mikhail Bakhtin, performed an unusual manoeuvre: it began by surrendering ground. Yes, the book granted, poems are not pamphlets, and any sociology that skips construction has failed. Then it turned. The materials of construction arrive already laden with communal judgements; genres are habits of seeing before they are patterns of arranging; and the shock of the strange registers only against expectations a society has installed. Specificity, granted in full, refused to stay independent.

### Q: Was Formalism suppressed?

The ending was administrative. During the closing years of the 1920s the school's scholars were publicly branded in language that functioned as a warning, their journals and teaching posts evaporated, and the movement's most visible founder signed a printed self repudiation in 1930 that nobody acquainted with the circumstances mistakes for persuasion. Persuasion was in fact occurring, since late formalist work had begun absorbing the rival objections, but absorption is slow and decree is fast, and decree is what the record shows.

### Q: Is there a synthesis of the two approaches?

A settlement was drafted in 1928 from both directions at once. Two leading formalists proposed treating literature as a self organising sequence of forms that nonetheless moves in concert with neighbouring sequences, economic, linguistic, customary, and set scholarship the job of charting the concert in detail. The circle's critique supplied the matching doctrine from the opposite bank, form as socially saturated material. Read together, the documents concede each camp its founding demand and outlaw only the two reductions, of form to content and of content to decoration.

### Q: How does this compare with New Criticism?

The Anglo-American movement rebuilt several formalist positions independently: suspicion of paraphrase, distrust of intention and of reader response as explanations, reverence for a poem's internal tensions. It differed in being a classroom practice centred on the lyric rather than a research programme centred on systems and genres, and it differed decisively in its fate. Nobody made its leaders recant. It lost ground over decades to critics who absorbed its techniques and rejected its sealing of the text, which is roughly the destination the Russian argument was approaching when it was stopped.

### Q: Which approach do critics use today?

Professional practice has quietly merged the inheritances while dropping the banners. A competent reading of a novel in a journal or a classroom will move between narration and history, device and institution, without announcing a school, because the merger describes what the work demands. The labels survive mainly in histories of criticism. Where one inheritance is used alone, its ancestral weakness reappears on schedule: readings that cannot say how a work is made, or readings that cannot say why it mattered, or matters, to anyone.

### Q: How does the forgotten synthesis grade the dispute between the two schools?

It distributes the honours unevenly across four separate contests. Describing what a literary object is belongs to the formalists; their opponents never matched the precision. Explaining how such objects change hands, audiences, and meanings across time belongs largely to the Marxist side. Perception, the hinge, is split: the mechanism is formalist, the formation of the perceiver is social. Content is split as well, material arriving charged and construction doing real work upon the charge. No contest ends in a shutout, which is exactly what a synthesis, as opposed to a surrender, looks like.

### Q: What should a student separate between relating two series of explanation and reducing one to the other?

Keep apart two operations that lazy writing confuses. To reduce is to dissolve: the poem becomes a disguised editorial, or the economy becomes a rumour with no effect on verse. To relate is to connect without dissolving: a publishing channel is shown to favour a chapter shape, a school trained readership is shown to sustain a genre, a borrowed dialect is shown changing function as it crosses into fiction. Relations are stated between things that remain themselves, and they can be checked, disputed, and improved, which reductions cannot.

### Q: Why was the synthesis forgotten by both sides?

Amnesia had organisers. The men who might have championed the settlement were exiled, arrested, or driven into other work within a few years of its statement, and the institutions that might have housed it were reorganised out of existence. Abroad, the relevant texts were missing, untranslated, or entangled in a quarrel over authorship that consumed the attention owed to their arguments. Later polemics found no use for a middle that both extremes could quote against each other, so survey after survey retaught the quarrel as a standoff and left the settlement in the footnotes of specialists.

### Q: How does the dispute grid order the positions?

Four questions are pinned down before any scoring begins: what the object of study is, how change happens, how the reader figures, and what social content is allowed to mean. Against each question the grid sets the formalist answer, the Marxist counter, and the 1928 attempt to hold both. The arrangement blocks a favourite sleight of hand in school comparisons, the transfer of a win on one question to the whole match, and it gives a student a map that can be carried into the primary texts and checked against them.

### Q: What separates defamiliarisation as a device from defamiliarisation as a worldview?

Handled as a device, the term points to identifiable operations, a viewpoint displaced, a name withheld, a cadence roughened, and to the identifiable readers whose expectations those operations trip. Handled as a worldview, it inflates into a metaphysics in which estrangement is art's eternal essence, equally present everywhere and therefore explanatory nowhere. The device can be found, timed, and shown to fail when audiences change. The worldview cannot be falsified by anything, and a notion that nothing can falsify has left criticism for incantation.
