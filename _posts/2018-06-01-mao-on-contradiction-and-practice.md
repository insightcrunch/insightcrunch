In the summer of 1937, at a military and political college in the loess hills of Yan'an, Mao Zedong delivered two courses of lectures that became the most widely read philosophical texts produced by any Chinese Marxist. The first, On Practice, argued that knowledge begins in doing and is proved in doing. The second, On Contradiction, argued that every situation contains many conflicts, that the conflicts are never of equal weight, and that one of them, the principal contradiction, determines how all the others can be handled. That second claim is the subject of this article, because it is not merely a description of how the world is arranged. It is an instruction to whoever acts in the world: identify the conflict that governs the rest, subordinate your efforts to it, and expect its identity to change as the situation changes. Read in that way, as a decision procedure rather than as a picture of reality, the essay explains both its own extraordinary influence and the suspicion it has attracted from critics who ask whether a procedure so flexible can ever be shown to have been applied wrongly.

![A lecturer's desk holding the two essays, an annotated Soviet textbook and a hand-drawn propositions table, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-56.webp)

The two works are short. On Practice runs to a few thousand words in translation, and On Contradiction, the longer of the pair, can be read in an evening. Their brevity is part of their history: they were teaching texts, prepared for cadres who had marched thousands of miles and who needed a philosophy that could be carried into a meeting, a village investigation or a battle plan. Yet their afterlife has been anything but brief. The vocabulary of the second essay entered the formal doctrine of a ruling party, where it was restated at congresses across six decades. Its central distinctions were taken up in Paris a quarter of a century after composition, when Louis Althusser built his concept of overdetermination partly on this text and acknowledged the debt in print. And the essays became a standard exhibit in an argument about originality, because Mao composed them while working through Soviet philosophical textbooks in Chinese translation, annotating his copies in the margins as he went. The annotated copies survive. The revisions he later made for the collected works are documented. Almost everything about these two short texts, their sources, their editing and their reception, can be checked, and this article proceeds on that basis: the texts are treated as philosophy to be assessed, neither as doctrine to be repeated nor as a curiosity to be patronised.

A reader who has met the phrase principal contradiction in political writing has usually met it as a slogan, detached from the argument that gives it meaning. The argument matters, because the phrase does no work on its own. In the essay it is the third of five connected propositions about contradiction, and it depends on the two that precede it: contradiction is universal, and every contradiction is particular. Strip away those foundations and the ranking doctrine becomes a bare permission to call whichever conflict one prefers the important one. Keep the foundations in view and the doctrine becomes something more disciplined and more interesting: a claim about the structure of complex situations, joined to a demand that the claim be earned by investigation in each case. Whether the discipline survives the permission is the question the critics press, and it is the question with which this article ends. The route there passes through the theory of knowledge in On Practice, through the five propositions and the table that organises them, through the Soviet sources and the textual history, and through the French reception that carried one part of the argument into a different philosophical world.

## Two essays from a lecture hall

The setting explains the form. Yan'an in 1937 was the headquarters of a party that had survived the Long March and was preparing, under a new united front with the Nationalists, for war against Japan. The Anti-Japanese Military and Political College, known by its abbreviated Chinese name Kangda, trained officers and political workers in short intensive courses. Mao lectured there on philosophy as part of a campaign against two habits of mind he believed were costing the movement dearly. One habit was dogmatism: the treatment of texts, whether Marxist classics or directives from above, as answers that could be applied without investigating the situation in front of one. The other was a narrow empiricism that trusted only immediate experience and refused to generalise from it. On Practice takes aim at both habits through a theory of knowledge. On Contradiction takes aim at the first habit in particular, by insisting that no situation can be understood through a formula, since each situation's contradictions have their own character and their own hierarchy.

The editorial note attached to the essay in the Selected Works states the occasion plainly: the text began as lectures at the college, written with the object of overcoming dogmatist thinking in the party, and was revised by the author when the Selected Works were prepared. Both halves of that note deserve emphasis. The lectures were an intervention in an internal argument about how the party should think, delivered to an audience of practitioners rather than scholars. And the text that the world reads is a revised text, edited in the early nineteen fifties, a fact with consequences for citation that a later section examines in detail. The companion essay on practice, delivered in July 1937, and the essay on contradiction, delivered the following month, belong to a larger body of lecture notes on dialectical materialism that Mao prepared across the same period, a set of notes that circulated internally for decades before scholars could study them in full.

### Why were two philosophy essays written at a military college?

Because the college trained the officers and political workers who would have to make judgements in the field, and Mao held that their errors were philosophical before they were tactical: dogmatists applied formulas without investigation, empiricists trusted impressions without generalising, and a course on knowledge and contradiction addressed both failures at their root.

That answer identifies the audience, and the audience identifies the genre. These are not treatises written for a republic of letters. They are closer to a field manual for judgement, composed by a political leader who believed that the way his movement thought was a material force in whether it survived. The prose reflects the purpose. It proceeds by numbered problems, by examples drawn from war, agriculture and everyday life, and by repeated returns to the practical question of what follows for action. Readers who approach the essays expecting the texture of European academic philosophy often find them elementary. Readers who approach them as an attempt to compress a method into teachable form find the compression itself instructive, because what Mao chose to keep, when everything else was cut, was the machinery of ranking conflicts and testing knowledge. The machinery is the philosophy.

The lectures also had a second, quieter function. A party school curriculum built around these texts established a shared vocabulary in which disagreements could be conducted. To call a comrade's proposal dogmatist, or to describe a dispute as a contradiction among the people rather than between the people and an enemy, was to make a move inside a common language, one whose terms carried instructions about how the matter should be handled. Vocabulary of that kind is never neutral equipment. It sorts the world before argument begins, and part of assessing these essays is noticing how much sorting their terms perform in advance: a conflict classified as non-antagonistic has already been assigned a method of resolution, and a conflict classified as principal has already been assigned the organisation's resources. The essays teach a way of seeing that is simultaneously a way of allocating attention, effort and, in the limiting case, coercion.

## What the two texts set out to do

On Practice is a theory of knowledge in miniature. Its claim is that knowing is a process with a structure: it begins in perceptual knowledge, the impressions gathered through direct engagement with things; it advances, when those impressions are worked upon, to conceptual knowledge, the grasp of connections and laws; and it is completed, provisionally, when concepts are carried back into practice and tested against the reality they claim to describe. The movement then repeats at a higher level, since practice continually generates new impressions and corrects old concepts. Two errors correspond to the two halves of the process. Rationalism, in the essay's usage, leaps to concepts without the perceptual groundwork, mistaking book learning for knowledge. Empiricism refuses the leap, clinging to fragmentary impressions and denying that theory can guide action. Mao's targets were party members guilty of each error in turn, but the schema itself is a general epistemology, and it has been read as one ever since.

On Contradiction is a theory of situations. Its claim is that conflict is not an accident that befalls otherwise harmonious things but the internal condition of every process, that each conflict has a specific character requiring specific study, and that complex processes contain a hierarchy of conflicts in which one dominates the others for a time. Where the first essay tells the reader how knowledge is made, the second tells the reader what the world is like such that knowledge of it must be made in that testing, revising way: a world of uneven, shifting, interlocked oppositions cannot be known by a formula, because the formula would have to know in advance which opposition governs, and that is exactly what changes. The two essays therefore belong together, and the order in which they were delivered is the order in which they should be read, a point the closing sections return to. The epistemology supplies the method; the ontology supplies the reason the method is necessary.

Between them, the essays also stake out a position inside the Marxist tradition they inherited. The general category of contradiction in Marxist method, its career from Hegel through Marx and Lenin, has its own treatment elsewhere in this series, in the study of [contradiction in Marxist method](/2012/08/01/contradiction-in-marxist-method/), and the broader system of dialectical materialism is mapped in the [dialectical materialism complete guide](/2012/07/01/dialectical-materialism-complete-guide/). What belongs to the present article is narrower and more specific: Mao's formulation, the additions he made to the inherited material, and the uses to which the formulation was put. The distinction matters because the most common misreading of these essays treats them as a summary of dialectical materialism in general, when their historical significance lies in the places where they depart from the textbook account: the demotion of the other dialectical laws beneath the single law of the unity of opposites, an elevation of particularity over universality in the order of study, and above all the doctrine of a governing contradiction, which has no real counterpart in the Soviet manuals Mao was reading.

The biographical setting, Mao's formation as a reader and the years in which a village schoolteacher became a party leader with a library in a cave dwelling, belongs to the companion study of [Mao Zedong's intellectual biography](/2018/05/15/mao-zedong-intellectual-biography/) and is drawn on here only where the argument requires it. What the argument requires, chiefly, is the reading programme of the winter and spring before the lectures: between November 1936 and April 1937, Mao worked through the Soviet philosophical literature available in Chinese translation with unusual intensity, filling the margins of his copies with close to thirteen thousand characters of annotation. The lectures of that summer were the public form of a private study campaign. To assess the essays is therefore also to assess a reading: what a gifted and ruthless student took from the textbooks of another country's orthodoxy, what he rearranged, and what he added that the textbooks did not contain.

## On Practice: knowledge begins in doing

The essay on practice opens with a refusal. Before Marxism, it says, knowledge was commonly treated as something separate from the activity of the knower, a contemplative relation between a mind and a world it observed from outside. Against that picture the essay sets a single insistence: people know things by intervening in them. Whoever wants to know a thing, Mao writes, has no way of doing so except by coming into contact with it, by living and practising in its environment. The claim is illustrated at several scales. A person who wants to understand a pear's taste cannot get there by speculation about pears. Whoever wants to understand a society cannot get there by reading about societies alone. Anyone who wants to understand revolution, the essay concludes, must take part in revolution. The examples descend from the homely to the world-historical without changing their logic, and the logic is that direct experience is not an optional prelude to knowledge but its only source.

The most quoted sentence in the essay carries the point in its most compressed form. "If you want to know the taste of a pear," Mao writes, "you must change the pear by eating it yourself." Nine words of that sentence, the clause about changing the pear, do the philosophical work. Knowing is presented as a species of changing: the knower alters the object in the act of encountering it, and the knowledge gained is knowledge of the object as it responds to intervention. This is why the essay treats practice as a technical term rather than a synonym for experience in general. Practice names the deliberate, socially organised activity by which people transform their circumstances: production first of all, then class struggle, political life and scientific experiment. Experience that merely washes over a person is raw material. Practice is experience taken up with an end in view, and it is only in that taken-up form that experience educates.

### What does the pear illustration actually claim?

It claims that some knowledge exists only on the far side of intervention: the taste is not a fact stored in the pear awaiting inspection, but an event produced when the pear is changed by being eaten, so that a knower who refuses to intervene has not found a purer route to the same knowledge but declined the knowledge itself.

From that starting point the essay builds its two-stage account of how knowledge grows. The first stage is perceptual knowledge. Through practice, the senses accumulate impressions of a thing's outward features, its separate aspects, its apparent connections with other things. A new recruit in a village learns its paths, its households, its tempers; an investigator in a factory learns its shifts, its machinery, its grievances. At this stage knowledge is rich but unordered, a heap of particulars. The second stage begins when the mind works the heap over: impressions are compared, sifted and connected, and concepts form that grasp the thing's internal relations rather than its surface. Mao calls this the leap from perceptual to rational knowledge, and he treats it as a genuine leap, a qualitative change in the knower's position, not a mere tidying of notes. Concepts formed in the leap are not copies of impressions. They are reconstructions, and precisely because they are reconstructions they can be wrong in ways impressions cannot, which is why the essay refuses to let the process rest there.

### How does knowledge move from perception to conception in the essay?

By accumulation and then by a leap: practice multiplies impressions of a thing's separate aspects until the mind, comparing and sifting them, forms concepts that grasp internal connections the senses never registered directly, after which those concepts must return to practice, where their truth or falsity is settled by whether action guided by them succeeds.

The return journey matters as much as the outward one. A concept that never goes back into practice remains, for this essay, unfinished business, because the criterion of truth is located in the testing. Mao states the criterion without ornament: an idea applied in practice that achieves the anticipated result is true; an idea that fails to achieve it is false, and must be corrected or discarded. The standard is pragmatic in structure, though the essay embeds it in a materialist frame, since what does the testing is not private satisfaction but the resistance of an objective world that does not rearrange itself to suit the investigator. Social practice, the essay insists, is the criterion of the truth of knowledge of the external world, and the insistence is aimed at every school that proposed a different tribunal: scripture, authority, introspection, or the say-so of a classic text.

## The spiral: knowledge that returns to its starting point changed

Neither stage is visited once. The essay describes a movement that repeats: practice, knowledge, again practice, again knowledge, each cycle raising the content of knowledge to a higher level. The image is sometimes called a spiral, and the metaphor earns its place, because the investigator who returns to a village after forming concepts about it does not see the village of the first visit. The paths and households are the same; what they are evidence of has changed. Concepts direct attention to relations that impressions alone had not disclosed: the debt behind the harvest, the lineage behind the dispute, the market behind the migration. Practice at the second cycle is therefore not a repetition of practice at the first. It is informed practice, and the knowledge it generates tests the concepts that informed it while extending them.

This cyclical structure gives the essay its answer to a standing problem in theories of knowledge, the problem of how finite knowers can claim to know a world that exceeds any single encounter with it. The answer is that no single encounter is asked to bear the weight. Knowledge is a social and historical process, distributed across many practitioners and many cycles, in which error is not a scandal but a stage: a concept falsified in practice is replaced by a better one, and the replacement is itself a product of the practice that exposed the failure. Mao presses the point against any notion of finished truth. Even a correct concept, he argues, is correct for a stage of the process it describes; as the process develops, yesterday's adequate account becomes the next stage's partial one, and the knower who treats it as final has converted a living instrument into a relic.

There is a consequence here that connects the epistemology to the essay on contradiction. If knowledge advances by cycles of testing, then the investigator's first task in any situation is to discover what the situation actually contains, and that discovery cannot be shortcut by importing conclusions from other situations. The demand for concrete investigation, which runs through Mao's writing from the rural surveys of the late nineteen twenties onward, receives its philosophical grounding in this essay: since concepts are only as good as the practice that produced and tested them, a concept carried from one county to another arrives untested in its new home, whatever its credentials elsewhere. The party worker who quotes a resolution instead of examining a village is not merely lazy. On the theory of this essay, he does not yet know the thing he is pronouncing upon, because the knowing has not happened.

### Why does the essay refuse to let a true concept rest?

Because the process a concept describes keeps developing after the concept is formed, so an account adequate to one stage becomes partial at the next, and the knower who stops testing treats a stage-bound instrument as a permanent possession, which is how correct ideas harden into the dogmas the essay was written against.

The refusal cuts in two directions, and the essay is careful to mark both. Against the dogmatist, it says that no text, however authoritative, certifies its own application; the application must be earned by investigation each time. Against the complacent empiricist, it says that experience hoarded without conceptual work remains blind, a pile of impressions that cannot guide the next action because nothing in it distinguishes the essential from the accidental. The two errors are mirror images: one has concepts without fresh practice, the other has practice without formed concepts. The spiral is the discipline that corrects both, since it requires the concept to be risked in action and the action to be harvested into revised concepts, endlessly. It is a demanding picture of intellectual life for an organisation at war, and the demand was the point. Mao was not describing how knowledge happens to grow in favourable conditions. He was prescribing a regimen for an organisation whose survival depended on the quality of its judgements about terrain, allies, enemies and timing.

## Two errors the essay was written against

Dogmatism receives the sharper treatment, because it was the error of the educated. The essay describes comrades who treat the writings of Marx and Lenin as a pharmacy from which prescriptions can be dispensed for any ailment, who study the phrases of the classics while declining to study the situation the phrases must be applied to. Mao's phrase for the habit, in the polemics of the period, was book worship, and the essay on practice gives the habit its epistemological diagnosis: the book worshipper has performed only half of the knowing process, and the easier half. He possesses concepts, often accurate ones, that were formed and tested in other people's practice, in other countries and other decades. What he lacks is the perceptual groundwork in his own situation and the test of application in his own practice, and without those the concepts are, in the strict sense of the essay's theory, not yet his knowledge at all. They are borrowed instruments whose calibration in local conditions nobody has checked.

### What is book worship, and why does the essay attack it?

Book worship is the treatment of doctrine as self-certifying, the habit of answering questions about a situation by citing texts rather than investigating it. The essay attacks the habit because its theory of knowledge denies that concepts formed in others' practice become knowledge for a reader who has neither grounded them in fresh perception nor risked them in action.

Empiricism, the second error, belonged to a different temperament: the veteran practitioner, rich in experience, suspicious of theory, who trusts the feel of a situation and resents the seminar room. The essay honours the experience and refuses the conclusion. Fragmentary experience, it argues, sees the phenomena of one locality and one period and mistakes them for the whole; it cannot rise to the internal connections that link localities and periods, and so it leaves its possessor repeating responses that worked before in conditions that no longer obtain. The practitioner who will not generalise is as disabled as the reader who will not investigate, because action at any scale above the immediate requires exactly the conceptual grasp the empiricist disdains. Between the two errors the essay stakes its middle position, which is not a compromise but a sequence: begin in practice, rise to concepts, return to practice, and treat any stopping point, whether the book or the impression, as a failure of method.

The polemic had a precise institutional target. In the years before Yan'an, the party's leadership had included a group of Moscow-trained cadres whose authority rested substantially on their command of the Russian texts and the Russian example, and whose strategic judgements Mao blamed for catastrophic defeats. The essays of 1937 are, among other things, the philosophical brief for the prosecution in that internal case: if knowledge requires local grounding and local testing, then authority imported from a foreign centre, however orthodox its vocabulary, is epistemologically void until it has been through the local cycle. The argument's elegance is that it does not contest the classics. It contests the shortcut. Marx and Lenin remain authorities, but their authority is made to pass through the reader's own investigation before it can be spent, and that single requirement redistributed intellectual power inside the party more effectively than any direct attack on the Moscow-trained group could have done.

A modern reader, meeting the essay outside that quarrel, can still feel the force of the standard it sets, because the standard is portable. Any organisation that acts on the world, a ministry, a company, a newsroom, contains both errors in modern dress: the consultant's framework applied without a site visit, and the veteran's hunch defended against every dataset. The essay's claim is that these are not opposite virtues requiring balance but the same failure at different stations of one process, the failure to complete the circuit between doing and knowing. Whether the circuit, completed, delivers truth as reliably as the essay promises is a question philosophers have pressed, since practice can mislead as well as instruct, and success in action is an imperfect witness to the truth of the concepts that guided it. The essay has no elaborate defence against that worry. Its confidence rests on the repetition of the cycle: a single test may deceive, but a discipline of perpetual testing, conducted by many hands over many cycles, is offered as the only tribunal available to beings who know the world by changing it.

## Practice as the criterion: what the first essay promises

The boldest commitment in On Practice is its location of the criterion of truth. Truth is not correspondence contemplated from outside the process of knowing; it is established inside that process, when ideas formed from practice are applied in further practice and the world answers. The essay's formulation deserves care, because it is easily caricatured as the doctrine that whatever works is true. Mao's actual claim is narrower and more demanding. An idea is tested by being carried out, and the test is genuine only where the outcome was anticipated in advance on the basis of the idea: the harvest that follows the agronomy, the engagement that follows the estimate of the enemy, the policy that produces the predicted shift in allegiance. Where the anticipated result is achieved, the idea has been verified for the conditions in which it was applied. Where it is not, the idea must be revised, and the revision re-enters the cycle. The criterion is thus closer to a discipline of prediction and audit than to a celebration of success, and its severest implication falls on the powerful: a leadership's concepts are on trial in every campaign it orders, and the results are the minutes of the trial.

Two qualifications keep the doctrine from naivety, and the essay states both. The first is that verification is stage-bound. A concept may be true of a process at one phase and false at another, not because truth is relative but because the process has changed under the knower; the map was accurate, and the river moved. The second is that the process of knowing a complex thing is never finished, so the stock of verified concepts at any moment is a working capital, not a treasury. Both qualifications prepare the ground for the companion essay. A world in which situations develop through phases, in which the relations among their elements shift as they develop, is a world whose conflicts will not keep the same ranking for long. The epistemology of cycles requires an ontology of uneven and changing tensions. On Contradiction supplies it.

It is worth pausing on what the first essay does not attempt. It offers no account of mathematics, no treatment of the social character of language, no discussion of how institutions store knowledge between generations of practitioners. Its examples are drawn from production, war and investigation, and its knower is collective as often as individual: classes and parties, not only persons, move through the cycle of practice and knowledge in its pages. Philosophers who assess the essay as a contribution to epistemology generally find it schematic, a forceful restatement of the Marxist theory of knowledge rather than an advance in its details. Its historical force lay elsewhere. It armed a political movement with a theory of its own learning, one that dignified the investigator's notebook as highly as the classic text and made the refusal to investigate into a philosophical error with a name. When the second essay then argued that every situation must be analysed in its particularity, the first essay had already explained why: because knowledge of the particular is the only kind there is, until practice has done its work.

## On Contradiction: the architecture of the argument

The second essay is built like a courthouse. It opens with a contest between two world outlooks, the metaphysical and the dialectical, and awards the dialectical outlook the field: things develop not because external forces push them but because they contain oppositions within themselves, and the task of thought is to find the oppositions and follow their movement. It then proceeds through a sequence of problems that the text itself enumerates: the universality of contradiction, the particularity of contradiction, the principal contradiction together with the principal aspect of a contradiction, the identity and struggle of the aspects of a contradiction, and the place of antagonism in contradiction. The sequence is not a miscellany. Each problem narrows the one before. Universality establishes that conflict is everywhere; particularity establishes that each conflict is its own; the ranking doctrine establishes that the many conflicts within one process are ranked; the principal aspect establishes that within a single conflict the two sides are ranked as well; identity and struggle describe how the sides relate; and antagonism closes by marking the special case in which the relation turns violent and open. The essay's architecture is a series of funnels, from the most general claim about the world to the most operational claim about a situation an actor must decide how to handle.

That architecture explains the essay's peculiar double character, which every serious reader remarks on. At the level of its opening sections it is a metaphysics, asserting a law of all processes whatsoever. At the level of its closing sections it is a manual of attention, instructing the reader where to look first in a crowded field of tensions. The passage from one register to the other is made by the middle sections, and above all by the doctrine of ranking, which converts a universal law into a local instruction. A law that says only that everything contains contradiction tells an actor nothing about what to do on Monday. A law that adds that among the contradictions one is principal, and that the principal one can be identified by study, tells the actor exactly what to do on Monday: study, rank, and concentrate. The essay's defenders regard this conversion as its genius. Its critics regard the same conversion as the point where philosophy hands the actor a blank authority, since the ranking step is where the theory's discipline is thinnest. Both judgements fasten on the same joint in the structure, which is a sign that the joint is where the essay lives.

### Why does the essay begin with two world outlooks rather than with its definitions?

Because its author wanted the law of contradiction to be received as a claim about how things develop, not as a verbal formula: by contrasting the outlook that locates change outside things with the outlook that locates change within them, the essay makes every later distinction a consequence of choosing the internal account of development.

The metaphysical outlook, as the essay presents it, sees things as isolated, static and one-sided, and explains change by external causes: climate, geography, the push of neighbouring things. The dialectical outlook replies that external causes operate only through internal ones, a point the essay fixes with a homely example: warmth will hatch an egg into a chicken, but no temperature will hatch a stone, because the two objects differ in their internal constitution. The example does more work than its simplicity suggests. It establishes that conditions matter only as they engage a thing's own oppositions, which means that the analyst's attention belongs on the inside of the situation under study, on the tensions that constitute it, rather than on the scenery around it. Every later proposition presupposes this orientation. When the essay eventually instructs the reader to find the principal contradiction inside a complex process, it is applying the egg's lesson at the scale of a society.

The essay's treatment of its authorities is also architectural. Lenin is quoted at the threshold, calling this law the essence and kernel of dialectics, and the essay proceeds as an expansion of that sentence. Marx and Engels supply the pedigree, Hegel the repudiated ancestor whose dialectics stood on its head, and the Soviet debates of the decade supply the polemical weather: the essay notes the criticism directed at the Deborin school in Soviet philosophical circles and aligns its own anti-dogmatist campaign with that criticism. A reader who knows the tradition will recognise how much of the essay's furniture is inherited. A reader who does not will still be able to follow the argument, because the essay was designed for an audience that had the furniture described to it as the lectures proceeded. The inheritance and the additions are separated in a later section of this article, where the sources are examined directly. What matters at this stage is the design: a universal law, a demand for particular study, a doctrine of rank, and an operational conclusion.

## The first movement: contradiction is universal

The universality proposition asserts that contradiction exists in every process from beginning to end, in natural processes as much as social ones, in the simplest phenomena as much as the most complex. The essay gives the proposition a twofold meaning. Contradiction is universal first in the sense that no process is exempt: there is no thing whose development is not driven by internal oppositions. It is universal second in the sense that it persists through every stage of a process: a process does not begin in harmony, suffer a temporary disturbance of conflict, and end in harmony again, but carries opposition through each of its phases, the old contradictions giving way to new ones as the process develops. The essay's examples range across mathematics, mechanics, physics, chemistry and biology before arriving at society, a deliberate procession meant to establish that the law is not a metaphor borrowed from politics and projected onto nature, but a pattern the author takes to be visible wherever anything changes.

As philosophy, the proposition is the essay's most exposed flank, and the critics treated in a later section have concentrated their fire accordingly: a law illustrated by examples chosen from every science, but stated at a level of generality that no finding could embarrass, risks explaining everything by explaining nothing in particular. The essay's own defence is built into its structure. Universality is not offered as a finding to be used directly; it is offered as the premise that makes the second proposition necessary. If contradiction were rare, the analyst could wait for it to appear. Since it is everywhere and always, the analyst's problem is never whether a situation contains contradictions but which ones it contains and how they stand to one another. The universal claim functions less as a description than as a standing instruction to look, coupled with a warning against the two symmetrical mistakes: imagining a situation without tensions, and imagining that naming the tensions in general terms has advanced the analysis at all.

### Why does universality not make every contradiction equal?

Because the proposition asserts only that opposition is present in every process at every stage, not that every opposition carries the same weight: the essay pairs the universal claim immediately with particularity and with ranking, so that the discovery of contradictions everywhere is the beginning of an analysis that must still determine which of them governs the rest.

There is also a polemical edge to the proposition that its first audience would have felt. To say that contradiction persists through every stage is to deny that any victory, including the victory of one's own side, brings the age of contradiction to an end. New opposites arise inside the new order, including inside the revolutionary camp itself, and a leadership that expects harmony after triumph will misread its own internal tensions as contamination from outside. The essay returns to this thought in its treatment of identity and struggle, and the 1957 sequel draws the political moral explicitly. In 1937 the moral was aimed at comrades who attributed every difficulty to enemy agents and every internal dispute to insufficient loyalty. The universality proposition, read in its setting, is a rebuke to that reflex: the tensions are ours, they are structural, and they will not be abolished by vigilance.

## The second movement: every contradiction is particular

If the first proposition is the essay's most exposed, the second is its most characteristic. Particularity asserts that the contradiction in each thing has its own specific character, that the oppositions driving one process differ from those driving another, and that the methods for resolving them differ accordingly. The essay insists that this is where study must concentrate, and it supports the insistence with a survey of domains: the contradiction between productive forces and relations of production takes one form in one society and another form elsewhere; the contradiction between classes expresses itself differently under different conditions; even within the sciences, each field's governing oppositions have their own texture, so that a method adequate to mechanics cannot be transferred wholesale to biology or to politics. The universal law, the essay argues, resides in the particular and can only be known through it, a formulation that reverses the usual order of textbook exposition, in which the general is mastered first and applied afterwards. Mao's order is investigation first, generality as its harvest.

The practical maxim drawn from the proposition is the one the essay repeats in several forms: concrete analysis of concrete conditions. The phrase is Lenin's, and Mao adopts it as the living soul of Marxism, the point at which the doctrine touches the ground. Its content, unfolded, is a research programme. Before acting in a situation, study the situation's own contradictions in their specificity: which groups stand in which relations, which grievances are ripening and which are stale, which alliances the structure of the moment makes possible. Refuse the transfer of conclusions between situations until the comparison has been done term by term. Treat the failure to investigate as a disqualification from judgement. The maxim had been Mao's signature long before 1937; the rural investigation reports of the previous decade are its earlier exercises, complete with the declaration that no investigation means no right to speak. The essay gives the maxim its philosophical warrant by deriving it from the structure of things: because each contradiction is particular, knowledge that skips the particular is not knowledge of the thing at all.

### What does concrete analysis of concrete conditions demand in practice?

It demands that the analyst establish, for the situation actually at hand, which oppositions it contains, what stage each has reached, and how they condition one another, before any conclusion is drawn or any method chosen; and it forbids the substitution of conclusions earned in other situations, however similar they appear, for that labour of establishment.

The proposition also carries the essay's theory of stages. A process develops through stages, and the contradictions within it do not merely persist but change their configuration as the stages succeed one another: oppositions that were marginal become central, alliances invert, and the whole process changes character when the governing opposition changes. The analyst is therefore required to periodise, to say which stage a process has reached, because the correct handling of a contradiction depends on its stage as much as on its nature. A method fitted to one stage, applied in the next, becomes its opposite in effect. This stage-sensitivity is the hinge between the particularity proposition and the doctrine that follows it, for if configurations change across stages, then the identity of the governing contradiction can change too, and a doctrine of rank that ignored stages would freeze the analyst's attention on yesterday's centre of the situation. The essay's third proposition builds the ranking doctrine with the stage doctrine already inside it.

## The five propositions table

The argument of On Contradiction can be laid out as five propositions, each with the operation it licenses for an actor, the objection it invites, and an anchoring example. The table below is this article's findable artifact: it compresses the essay into a form that can be checked against the text, and the sections that follow it unpack each row in turn.

### What does the table show about the location of the essay's originality?

It shows that four of the five propositions systematise material the Soviet textbooks also contained, while the middle proposition, the ranking of contradictions under a principal one, is the essay's own construction, so that the originality of the whole text can be located in a single row.

Two features of the table deserve notice before the rows are unpacked. The first is that only the middle row, the principal contradiction, has no real precedent in the textbook tradition Mao inherited; the other four rows systematise material that Soviet manuals also contained, in more scattered form. The table therefore locates the essay's originality precisely. The second feature is the third column. Each proposition licenses an operation, something an organisation does differently because the proposition is believed, and the operations grow more consequential down the table, from an attitude toward tension at the top to a decision about how human beings are to be treated at the bottom. The essay's philosophy and its politics are not two subjects. The table shows them as one subject read at two speeds.

## The third movement: the principal contradiction

The doctrine at the essay's centre begins from an observation about complexity. A complex process, a society, a war, a revolution, never contains a single contradiction. It contains many, of different ages and weights, developing unevenly: some sharpening, some easing, some newly born, some in decline. The doctrine asserts that these many do not form a parliament of equals. In Mao's sentence, among the many contradictions in a complex thing, "one of them is necessarily the principal contradiction" whose existence and development determine or influence the existence and development of the others. The principal contradiction is the one whose resolution would transform the configuration of the rest, the knot whose loosening or tightening moves every other strand. Secondary contradictions are real, and they can wreck a policy that ignores them, but they are conditioned by the principal one: their prospects, their tempo and their possible resolutions are set within limits the governing contradiction establishes.

### What is a principal contradiction?

in Mao's account, the one conflict within a complex situation whose resolution determines the others, and whose identity can change as circumstances change. It functions as a decision procedure, telling a political actor which of many conflicts to treat as determining at a given moment.

The essay illustrates the doctrine from the situation its audience inhabited. In a semi-colonial country such as China, Mao argues, the contradiction between the nation and imperialism could become principal, outweighing the internal contradictions among classes; when that happened, classes otherwise opposed to one another could be drawn into a common front, and the internal class contradiction, without disappearing, was temporarily relegated and mitigated. In other configurations the ranking inverted: when imperialism prosecuted its domination through domestic agents and the domestic reaction sharpened its rule, the contradiction between the masses and the feudal order could move to the centre. The examples are not decorations on the doctrine. They are the doctrine in use, and they display its two essential features. The ranking is relational, depending on the whole configuration rather than on the intrinsic importance of any single conflict. And the ranking is temporary, liable to reversal when the configuration shifts, so that yesterday's correct concentration becomes an error if it is clung to after the shift.

A companion distinction completes the apparatus. Within any single contradiction, the two opposed aspects are themselves unequal: one is the principal aspect, and it determines the character of the whole contradiction for as long as it holds its position. A thing's nature, the essay argues, is fixed mainly by the principal aspect of its governing contradiction, which is why a process can change its character without changing its identity all at once: the secondary aspect strengthens, the principal aspect weakens, the positions exchange, and the thing emerges transformed, as when a colony becomes independent or a revolutionary movement becomes a government. The two rankings, among contradictions and within them, give the analyst a grid of four questions to put to any situation. Which contradiction governs? Which aspect of it dominates? Are the two stable, or exchanging places? And what follows for action in each case? The grid is the essay's bequest to its readers, and everything in its later career, the illuminating uses and the abuses alike, can be described as answers given to those four questions.

### What is the principal aspect of a contradiction?

It is the dominant side within a single contradiction, the aspect whose position determines the character of the whole for as long as it holds: a process keeps its nature while the same aspect rules, and changes its nature when the subordinate aspect grows strong enough to exchange places with the ruling one.

## The decision procedure: the article's central claim

The five propositions can be read as a description of the world, and generations of summaries have read them that way. The reading this article defends is different. The doctrine of the principal contradiction is best understood as a decision procedure: an instruction issued to a political actor about which conflict in a crowded field is to be treated as the governing one for the time being. The distinction between description and procedure explains features of the text that the descriptive reading leaves puzzling. A description aims at truth and is embarrassed by exceptions. A procedure aims at guidance and is embarrassed only by uselessness. The essay's insistent returns to what the analyst must do, study this, distinguish that, concentrate on the other, are the marks of a text whose propositions are load-bearing because they direct attention and effort, not merely because they depict a structure. When the essay says that one contradiction is principal, the cash value of the sentence is an allocation: of forces, of propaganda, of patience, of compromise. Other contradictions are to be postponed, softened, or deliberately left unresolved, and the procedure is what authorises the postponement.

Read as a procedure, the doctrine also shows why it proved so powerful in the hands of the movement that produced it. A revolutionary organisation faces a permanent surplus of possible struggles and a permanent shortage of strength. Every framework it might adopt must answer the question of priority, and most answer it by habit, by sentiment or by the preferences of leaders. The ranking doctrine answers it by analysis, or claims to: the priority is read off the structure of the situation, discovered by investigation rather than chosen by inclination. The claim to have discovered, rather than decreed, the priority is the doctrine's authority. It converts a strategic choice, which could be argued with, into a finding, which must be argued against on the terrain of the analysis itself. Whoever controls the identification of the principal contradiction therefore controls the organisation's agenda with the force of objectivity behind the control, a consequence the essay's first readers can hardly have missed, and one that gives the doctrine its double edge.

The same reading explains the doctrine's characteristic abuse, which the brief for this article names and which the criticism section examines in full. If redefining the principal contradiction redefines everything that follows, then an actor who wishes to reverse a policy has the strongest possible incentive to announce that the situation has changed and a new contradiction has become principal. The announcement, dressed in the procedure's vocabulary, presents the reversal as a discovery compelled by the facts. Alliances can be dissolved, campaigns halted, yesterday's enemy embraced and yesterday's ally reclassified, each move certified by the ranking doctrine. The procedure cannot by itself distinguish a genuine re-ranking, forced by a real transformation of the situation, from a convenient one, because the identification step is exactly where the procedure's discipline runs out. That is not a peripheral flaw. It is the price of the doctrine's operational power, and an honest assessment of the essay has to hold both halves of the sentence together: the procedure is powerful because it allocates, and it is abusable because the allocation rests on an identification the theory does not fully govern.

There is a test available for grading applications of the procedure, and this article proposes it as the standard the text itself implies. An identification of the principal contradiction earns its authority when the investigation behind it is displayed: when the analyst can show the other contradictions that were ranked below it, state the evidence for their subordination, and specify what change in the situation would promote one of them. An identification that arrives without its working, that cannot say what it outranked or what would displace it, has used the vocabulary as a stamp rather than as a procedure. The distinction between a method of investigation and a licence for any conclusion lies exactly there, in whether the working is shown. Later sections apply this grading to the doctrine's own career, including its restatements at party congresses, where the identifications were sometimes argued in detail and sometimes simply promulgated.

## The fourth movement: identity and struggle

The proposition on identity and struggle is the essay's most compressed piece of metaphysics. The two aspects of a contradiction, it argues, stand in a double relation. They are identical, in the specific senses that each exists only in relation to the other, that each contains elements of the other, and that under definite conditions each can transform itself into the other: war into peace, peace into war, the ruler into the ruled. And they struggle, absolutely: the opposition between them is not a temporary disturbance of an underlying harmony but a constant, present even inside their unity, and it is the struggle, not the identity, that admits no conditions and no intermission. Identity is conditional and relative. Struggle is unconditional and absolute. The asymmetry between the two adverbs is the proposition's whole content, and the essay builds the asymmetry into a theory of change: because struggle never pauses, no equilibrium is final; because identity is conditional, every unity is a temporary settlement whose terms can be renegotiated by the movement of the struggle itself.

The examples the essay marshals are deliberately miscellaneous. Life and death in an organism, the two sides of a war, the proletariat and the bourgeoisie, the old and the new in a developing process: each pair is said to exhibit the same double relation, each side inconceivable without the other, each pair locked in a contest whose outcome, at some stage, is the transformation of the whole. A critic may observe that the examples illustrate the proposition rather than test it, since a pair that failed to exhibit the pattern could always be redescribed until it did. The observation is fair, and it belongs to the general case against the essay's metaphysical sections. What can be said for the proposition, as with universality, is that its function in the system is regulative rather than descriptive. It instructs the analyst to treat every apparent settlement as provisional and every opposing camp as internally related to its adversary, instructions that had immediate consequences for a movement perpetually tempted to treat its alliances as permanent and its enemies as external to itself.

### How can identity between opposites be conditional while struggle is absolute?

Because identity, in the essay's usage, names the interdependence and possible interconversion of the two sides, which hold only under definite conditions that can lapse, while struggle names their opposition itself, which requires no conditions and never pauses: unities are settlements with terms, whereas the contest that produced them continues inside the settlement.

The proposition also does quiet work against a temptation the essay elsewhere encourages. A doctrine of ranking might suggest that situations are stable once ranked, that the analyst who has found the principal contradiction and its principal aspect has finished the intellectual task and may proceed to administration. Identity and struggle forbid the suggestion. The principal aspect rules conditionally; its rule is itself a phase of the struggle; and the subordinate aspect is not a defeated quantity but a living side whose growth is the mechanism by which the whole will change its character. The analyst is therefore sent back to the situation continuously, which is where the ontology of the second essay locks into the epistemology of the first. Knowledge must spiral because the object spirals. A single act of identification, however brilliant, has a shelf life, and the text says so in its own vocabulary.

## The fifth movement: antagonism and its absence

The final proposition distinguishes among contradictions by their manner of resolution. Some contradictions are antagonistic: the opposition between their sides is open and hostile, and they are resolved by the overthrow of one side by the other, as the essay takes the opposition between the working class and the capitalist class to be resolved. Others are non-antagonistic: the sides share a fundamental interest beneath their disagreement, and the contradiction is resolved by criticism, discussion and adjustment rather than by destruction. The distinction sounds taxonomic, a sorting of conflicts into two boxes. Its consequences were anything but taxonomic, because the sorting prescribed the treatment. A contradiction classified as antagonistic licensed confrontation and, in the conditions of a revolutionary state, coercion. A contradiction classified as non-antagonistic prescribed persuasion and protected its parties from the methods reserved for enemies. The classification was therefore among the most consequential acts an authorised analyst could perform, and the essay's calm presentation of the distinction, as one more feature of the world's furniture, understates the power it lodges in whoever classifies.

### Why does the antagonistic distinction decide how people are treated?

Because the distinction is prescriptive as well as descriptive: to classify a contradiction as antagonistic is to license confrontation and the overthrow of one side, while to classify it as non-antagonistic is to prescribe criticism and persuasion, so the act of classification settles in advance the methods that may legitimately be used against the people involved.

Mao himself developed the distinction twenty years later, in the 1957 work on the correct handling of contradictions among the people, which separated the contradictions between the people and their enemies from the contradictions among the people themselves and assigned each category its methods. That later text, a speech delivered in February 1957 and published in revised form the following June, is treated in the next section. What belongs here is the seed in the 1937 essay and the structural point the seed already contains. The boundary between the two kinds of contradiction is not given by the contradiction itself in any way the essay specifies. The same parties can stand in a relation classified one way in one period and the other way in another, and the essay's own examples acknowledge the mobility: a contradiction non-antagonistic in form can sharpen into antagonism if mishandled, a warning the 1957 text repeats with emphasis. The classifier's judgement, informed by investigation but not replaced by it, is thus built into the machinery at the point of maximum consequence. Readers who find the principal-contradiction doctrine troubling on grounds of unchecked discretion should find this distinction more troubling still, since the discretion it confers is exercised not over priorities but over persons.

## The sequel among the people

The 1957 speech extended the fifth proposition into a doctrine of government. Its premise was that contradictions do not end with the victory of a revolution or the establishment of a socialist order; they persist inside the new society, between the government and the governed, between workers and peasants, between regions, between the leadership and the led. The speech divided these persistent contradictions into the two great categories its title assumes. Contradictions between the people and the enemy were antagonistic in foundation and were to be handled by the methods of dictatorship. Contradictions among the people were non-antagonistic in foundation, and were to be handled by what the speech called the democratic method: persuasion, criticism, education, the formula of unity, criticism, unity, in which discussion begins from a desire for unity and arrives, through the airing of differences, at a new unity on a corrected basis. The speech warned, with a precision that later events made poignant, that contradictions among the people could become antagonistic if they were handled clumsily or if vigilance against real enemies relaxed, and it insisted that the distinction between the categories was a matter for concrete judgement in each case.

As a document in the career of the 1937 essay, the speech matters for three reasons. It demonstrates that the fifth proposition was not a scholastic ornament but the germ of a governing doctrine, the point at which the lecture-hall vocabulary acquired administrative force. It displays the doctrine's mobility in the author's own hands, since the categories of people and enemy are defined politically and shift with the political situation, so that the classification of a group could be, and was, revised. And it supplies the clearest case for the grading test proposed earlier in this article: the speech displays its working. It argues at length about which contradictions belong in which category, acknowledges borderline cases, and names the conditions under which a case changes categories. A reader may dispute the classifications while recognising that the text performs the procedure rather than merely invoking it. The distance between that performance and later uses of the vocabulary, in which classification arrived as a verdict without displayed reasoning, is the gap between the method and its degeneration, and the 1957 text is valuable partly because it lets that gap be measured.

The speech also complicates any simple verdict on the essays of 1937, because it shows their author applying their machinery to the problem every revolutionary government faces and few revolutionary theories address: what to do with disagreement after the enemy has been defeated. The answer the speech gives, that disagreement among the people is a normal and even healthy condition requiring institutional patience, is not the answer history most associates with the decades that followed, and the tension between the text and the subsequent record is part of the evidence a serious assessment must weigh. The text's categories were available for the patient use it recommended. They were also available, by reclassification, for the opposite use, and the availability was not an accident of reception but a property of the machinery, which lodges the decisive judgement in the classifier. The essays of 1937 built the machinery. The speech of 1957 showed it running at full scale. The assessment of both must reckon with what the machinery made easy as well as with what it made possible.

## Where the framework came from: the Soviet textbooks

No part of the essays' history is better documented, or more often mishandled, than their sources. The mishandling takes two opposite forms. Admirers long presented the essays as creations from nothing, the philosophy of a revolutionary genius elaborated from Chinese conditions alone. Detractors, discovering the sources, have presented the essays as transcriptions, philosophy by copying. The documentary record supports neither portrait. It shows a reader working through the standard Soviet philosophical literature of the nineteen thirties in Chinese translation, annotating it closely, arguing with it in his margins, and then lecturing from the resulting synthesis, in which the inherited framework is rearranged and one doctrine, the ranking of contradictions, is developed well beyond anything the sources contain.

The reading programme can be reconstructed in unusual detail because the books survive with their marginalia. In the months from November 1936 to April 1937, Mao read and annotated the Chinese translations of Soviet works on dialectical materialism, including the textbook tradition associated with Mark Mitin and his collaborators, whose Dialectical and Historical Materialism circulated widely in translation, and a Soviet course on dialectical materialism whose margins he filled with comments. The annotations, published in scholarly editions and studied most thoroughly by the Australian scholar Nick Knight, run to some thirteen thousand characters across the surviving volumes. They are not the notes of a copyist. They argue: approving a distinction here, objecting to a formulation there, connecting the text to Chinese examples, and occasionally sketching the formulations that would appear, reworked, in the summer lectures. Alongside the Soviet books stood the work of Chinese intermediaries, above all Ai Siqi, the party's most popular philosophical writer, whose expositions of dialectical materialism for general readers Mao read, praised and drew upon, and whose explanations mediated the Soviet material for a whole generation of Chinese communists.

### What survives in Mao's annotated copies of the Soviet textbooks?

Thousands of characters of marginal commentary in his hand across the translated volumes, approving, objecting, connecting the arguments to Chinese conditions and drafting formulations that reappear in the lectures, evidence that lets scholars reconstruct the reading programme of the months before the essays and test claims about their originality against a paper trail.

The lecture notes themselves confirm the dependence and the distance. Mao's Dialectical Materialism lecture notes, the long draft from which the two essays were drawn, follow the organisation of the Soviet textbooks across long stretches: the two world outlooks, the laws of dialectics, the theory of knowledge. Knight's edition of the notes, published in English in 1990, made the relationship impossible to deny and equally impossible to simplify, since the same notes that track the textbooks also depart from them at the decisive points. The departures are systematic. Where the Soviet manuals treated the three laws of dialectics as a co-ordinate set, the unity of opposites, quantity passing over into quality, and the negation of the negation, Mao subordinated the second and third to the first, treating them as aspects of the single fundamental law. Where the manuals moved from the universal to the particular, Mao reversed the emphasis, insisting that universality resides in particularity and is known only through it. And where the manuals mentioned the uneven development of contradictions in passing, Mao built from the remark the doctrine of ranking contradictions, the essay's centre of gravity and the tradition's most consequential borrowing from a hint.

A word is needed on the intermediaries, because the transmission was not a private line from Moscow to Yan'an. Chinese Marxist philosophy in the nineteen thirties was a busy field. Li Da, one of the party's founders, had written a large systematic treatise on dialectical materialism. Ai Siqi's popularisations reached readers the textbooks never touched, and Ai was in Yan'an, in conversation with Mao, during the period of the lectures. Qu Qiubai, a earlier party leader executed in 1935, had introduced elements of the Soviet philosophical vocabulary a decade before. The essays of 1937 are best pictured as the most influential product of this collective enterprise rather than as an isolated invention, the point at which a whole literature of translation, popularisation and debate was condensed by its most powerful reader into a teachable system. Condensation is a real intellectual act, and the history of philosophy is full of systems whose originality lies in selection and arrangement. But it is a different act from creation out of nothing, and the essays' reputation suffered, in the long run, from having been credited with the second when the evidence supports the first.

## The charge of copying, and the scholarly verdict

The dependence on Soviet sources became a public charge in the early nineteen seventies, when the Soviet philosophers M. Altaisky and V. Georgiyev published a critique arguing that the lecture notes were lifted wholesale from Mitin's textbook and from a Soviet encyclopaedia article, whole passages reproduced with little or no change. The charge was made in the heat of the Sino-Soviet split, by scholars of a state engaged in ideological war with Mao's, and its purpose was demolition: to show that the celebrated philosopher of Yan'an had never held an original philosophical thought. Western scholarship, arriving at the sources independently as Chinese editions of the notes and annotations became available, reached a more differentiated verdict that has remained broadly stable since. The verbal dependence in the lecture notes is real and extensive. The two published essays are a different matter from the notes: they are compressed, reorganised and directed at Chinese problems, and their central doctrine is not in the sources. The relationship is that of a student who mastered the set texts so thoroughly that he could rewrite them for his own purposes, which is neither the plagiarism of the Soviet charge nor the immaculate originality of the earlier cult.

The episode carries a lesson about how the essays should be cited in arguments about originality. Three distinct objects circulate under their names: the lecture notes of 1937, expansive and closely dependent on the textbooks; the essays as revised for the Selected Works, compressed and reordered; and the ranking doctrine itself, which belongs to the essays and has no textbook source. A claim that is true of one object may be false of the others. The notes are derivative in texture. The essays are derivative in framework and original in emphasis and in their central doctrine. The doctrine is an invention, though an invention assembled from materials, Lenin's remarks on uneven development and the concrete analysis of conditions, that lay ready to hand. Precision about which object is under discussion dissolves most of the quarrel, and what remains is a genuine question about how much originality a rearrangement can claim, a question philosophy asks about many of its canonical texts and answers, usually, in favour of the rearrangers who changed what their readers did next.

### Did the textbooks contain the doctrine of the governing contradiction?

They contained its ingredients without the doctrine: remarks on the uneven development of contradictions, on the distinction between principal and secondary contradictions in passing, and on the need for concrete analysis, which the lectures gathered into a single ranking procedure with the naming of a governing contradiction at its centre and the conduct of policy as its output.

It is also worth recording what the sources explain about the essays' silences. The Soviet textbooks of the nineteen thirties were written under the shadow of the Deborin controversy, in which a school of Soviet philosophers had been condemned for excessive Hegelianism, and the victorious orthodoxy prized a dialectics of clear laws and materialist guarantees. Mao's essays breathe that air. Their confidence that the law of contradiction admits no exceptions, their procession of examples from the natural sciences, their demotion of the negation of the negation, which smelled of Hegel, all belong to the intellectual climate of the textbooks he was reading. The climate explains the essays' dogmatic surface, the tone of settled science, better than any hypothesis about their author's temperament. What the climate does not explain is the operational turn, the conversion of the laws into a procedure for ranking and deciding, which answers to the needs of a headquarters rather than a seminar. The essays are the textbooks passed through a war. Their philosophy is inherited; their use is the author's own.

## A text with two lives: the revisions

The essays that the world reads are not the lectures that Yan'an heard. Both texts were revised by their author before their publication in the Selected Works of the early nineteen fifties, On Practice appearing in print first and On Contradiction following in the first volume of the collected works, and the revisions were documented once scholars gained access to the earlier versions preserved in the lecture notes and in wartime printings. The documentation matters because the differences are occasionally substantive rather than merely stylistic. Knight's edition of the lecture notes records over ninety variations between the notes and the later texts at the level of the translated extracts alone, and the study of the variants has become a small scholarly industry, since each variant is potential evidence about how the author's thought, or his sense of his audience, had changed between the cave classrooms of 1937 and the capital of a new state.

The character of the revisions can be described in general terms. Passages closely following the Soviet textbooks were pruned or recast, which had the effect, intended or not, of making the published essays appear more independent of their sources than the lectures had been. Examples tied to the immediate polemics of 1937 were softened or removed. Formulations were tightened, and some were strengthened: claims that the lectures had advanced as teaching points returned in the published text with the polish of doctrine. The direction of the editing is consistent. The lectures were the working papers of a movement still fighting for power, addressed to insiders and frank about their borrowings. The published essays were the canonical texts of a ruling party, addressed to a nation and to posterity, and edited to stand as self-sufficient statements of a philosophy. Neither version is the false one. They are the same argument at two stations of its author's career, and the scholarly rule that follows is simple to state and widely ignored: a citation of these essays should specify which version it uses, because an argument about what Mao thought in 1937 cannot be settled by a sentence he polished in 1952, and an argument about the doctrine of the party cannot be settled by a draft its author superseded.

### What did the editors of the collected works change?

The author himself, revising for the collected works, pruned passages that had followed the Soviet textbooks closely, softened examples tied to the polemics of the lectures' own moment, and tightened formulations into doctrinal form, so that the published essays stand more independently of their sources and speak with a finish the wartime drafts did not attempt.

The textual situation has a further wrinkle that students of the essays should know. The lecture notes from which the essays derive circulated for decades only as an internal party document, and their full scholarly publication came late, through Japanese and then Chinese and English editions, so that the study of the essays' sources is younger than the study of their influence. For a generation, the essays were assessed in the West from the published texts alone, in translations made from the revised versions, and judgements about their originality were formed in ignorance of the paper trail. The judgements formed since the trail opened have been more measured in both directions, less inclined to worship and less inclined to dismiss, which is what usually happens when a text acquires a documented childhood. The essays shrank slightly as philosophy and grew considerably as historical evidence: evidence of how a revolutionary movement taught itself to think, from what materials, and with what edits applied when the movement became a state.

There is a parallel here with the doctrine the essays contain, and it is too neat to be accidental. The published text is the principal aspect of the work's identity, the version that governed its effects in the world. The lecture notes are the subordinate aspect that scholarship has been strengthening ever since, and with it the character of the whole work, what the essays are taken to be, has changed as the subordinate aspect grew: from scripture to document, from a philosophy to be believed to a record to be studied. The essay on contradiction teaches that such exchanges of position are the normal fate of complex things. Its own fate is a well-documented instance.

## The doctrinal afterlife: the vocabulary at the congresses

The principal-contradiction doctrine did not retire into the library after 1949. It remained in formal doctrinal use as part of the operating vocabulary of the Chinese Communist Party, and its content was restated, with new identifications, at the party's great formal occasions. This section records that usage as documented fact, with the congresses and dates attached, and offers no assessment of any formulation: the record itself is the evidence for the doctrine's institutional career, and it is the career, not the merits of any single restatement, that belongs to an account of the essay's afterlife.

The Eighth National Congress of the party, meeting in 1956, declared that the contradiction between the proletariat and the bourgeoisie inside China had been basically resolved, and identified the principal contradiction facing the country as the one between the people's demand for rapid economic and cultural development and the inability of the economy and culture of the time to satisfy that demand, a formulation also expressed as the contradiction between an advanced socialist system and backward productive forces. The identification had operational consequences of the kind the doctrine promises: it ranked economic construction above class struggle in the party's order of business. Within a few years the ranking was contested and then reversed in practice, as class struggle was restored to the centre, an episode that belongs to the history of the party rather than to this article, but which a reader of the 1937 essay will recognise as the doctrine's machinery running in both directions within a single decade.

The Sixth Plenary Session of the Eleventh Central Committee, in 1981, adopted the Resolution on Certain Questions in the History of the Party, which restated the principal contradiction for the era of reform: the contradiction between the ever-growing material and cultural needs of the people and backward social production. That identification, promulgated with the authority of a formal resolution on history itself, underwrote the reorientation of the party's work toward economic development, and it held as the official formulation for more than three decades. At the Nineteenth National Congress in 2017, the political report announced that the principal contradiction had evolved, identifying it as the contradiction between unbalanced and inadequate development and the people's ever-growing needs for a better life. The report presented the change of identification as a judgement about a changed situation, in the doctrine's own grammar: the configuration had shifted, the governing conflict had shifted with it, and the party's tasks were to be re-ranked accordingly.

### Which congresses restated the doctrine, and in what words?

The Eighth Congress in 1956 named the contradiction between the people's demand for economic and cultural development and the means available to meet it; the 1981 resolution named the contradiction between growing material and cultural needs and backward social production; the Nineteenth Congress in 2017 named the contradiction between unbalanced, inadequate development and the people's need for a better life.

Three observations about this record belong to an assessment of the essay, as distinct from an assessment of the formulations. The first is longevity: few philosophical coinages of the twentieth century remained in continuous official use, as a load-bearing term of a governing party's self-description, for so long. The second is fidelity of form: each restatement follows the 1937 grammar exactly, naming a pair of poles, declaring their contradiction principal, and deriving the party's central task from the identification, which suggests that what the institution preserved was the procedure even more than any of its outputs. The third observation is the one the critics seize on, and it is recorded here without adjudication: the identifications changed at moments when the party's course was changing for reasons that can be described in other vocabularies, of succession, of economic failure and success, of coalition and control, so that the doctrine can be read either as the instrument by which the changes were thought through or as the language in which changes decided elsewhere were announced. The 1937 essay, read carefully, predicts both readings, since it casts the naming of the governing contradiction as an act of analysis performed by the leadership and offers no external check on the performance. The grading test set out earlier in this article applies here with full force: the identifications that displayed their working, ranking the rival candidates and stating the evidence, used the doctrine as its author described it. The identifications that arrived as formulae used it as a stamp.

## The French reception: Althusser reads the essay

In December 1962, the journal La Pensée published an essay by Louis Althusser, a philosopher at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris and a member of the French Communist Party, under the title Contradiction and Overdetermination. The essay, later collected in For Marx in 1965, announced a programme for re-reading Marx against both the humanist interpretation then fashionable and the mechanistic dialectics of the orthodox textbooks. Its central exhibit was not a European text. Althusser had read On Contradiction in the French translation published a decade earlier in the Cahiers du Communisme, the theoretical review of his own party, and he returned to it as the place where the Marxist conception of contradiction appeared, in his phrase, "in a quite un-Hegelian light." The judgement was the seed of one of the most influential concepts in late twentieth-century Marxist theory, and it carried a Yan'an lecture text into the centre of Parisian philosophy.

What Althusser took from the essay can be stated precisely. Hegel's contradiction, as he read it, was simple: a single opposition, expressive of one essence, unfolding toward its resolution. The contradiction in Mao's essay was nothing like that. It was complex from the start, a structured whole of many contradictions, unevenly developed, in which one contradiction dominated the others and the aspects within each contradiction stood in relations of dominance as well. For Althusser this structure dissolved the picture of a society organised around one simple opposition, capital against labour read as an essence expressing itself in every sphere. In its place stood a whole in which economic, political and ideological instances each had their own contradictions and their own tempo, bound together in a structure in dominance where the economy was determinant in the last instance but rarely the directly dominant instance on the stage. He named the condition of such a contradiction overdetermined, borrowing the term from Freud's account of dream formation, in which a single image is the meeting point of several chains of association. Every contradiction, on this account, reflects in itself the whole structure of the complex whole to which it belongs.

### What did Althusser find in the essay that Hegel could not give him?

A contradiction that was complex and structured from the outset: many contradictions within one process, developing unevenly, ranked under a dominant one, so that no single opposition could be treated as the simple expression of an essence, which was the resource he needed against both Hegelian readings of Marx and mechanistic ones.

The acknowledgment was explicit and repeated. In the 1962 essay and in the companion piece of the following year, On the Materialist Dialectic, Althusser credited Mao's text with having posed, in practical and theoretical form, the problem of the specificity of contradiction: the idea that a contradiction is never the generic opposition of textbooks but always this determinate contradiction, in this structured whole, at this stage of its uneven development. Étienne Balibar, Althusser's student and collaborator, later recalled the encounter with the essay as a revelation for his teacher, the discovery that a leader of a victorious revolution had also produced a philosophy of the first order, and that the philosophy broke with the Hegelian inheritance at exactly the point where Althusser wished to break with it. The transmission had an irony the participants savoured. The French party's own theoretical journal had carried the translation as an act of international solidarity, expecting an edifying document from a fraternal party. What arrived instead was the lever with which the party's most formidable philosopher prised French Marxism loose from its official dialectics.

The connection matters beyond the biography of one concept, because it joins two clusters of this series that are usually kept apart. The Chinese texts of 1937 are commonly studied inside the history of the Chinese revolution, and the French theory of the nineteen sixties inside the history of European philosophy, and the corridor between them is walked mainly by specialists. Yet the corridor is load-bearing. Overdetermination became a term of art across the human sciences, in literary studies, in political theory, in the analysis of ideology, and at the end of the corridor stands a lecture delivered to officers in a cave-region college, whose author cannot have imagined the destination. The fuller treatment of the concept itself belongs to the study of [overdetermination explained](/2016/01/01/overdetermination-explained/), and the comparison of Mao's doctrine with the Soviet orthodoxy it partly inherited belongs to the study of [Maoism and Soviet Marxism compared](/2018/09/01/maoism-vs-soviet-marxism/). What belongs here is the fact of the transmission and an assessment of what it preserved.

## What the reception kept, and what it changed

Fidelity in transmission is a matter of degree, and Althusser's use of the essay was transformative as well as faithful. He kept the structure: the complex whole, the ranking of contradictions, the unevenness of their development, the refusal to reduce the whole to one simple opposition. He changed the purpose. In Mao's text the ranking doctrine is a procedure for an actor who must decide where to strike, ally and wait; identifying the principal contradiction is the prelude to a campaign. In Althusser's hands the same structure becomes an instrument of theoretical anti-humanism, a way of describing how social formations hold together and reproduce themselves, in which the actor recedes and the structure occupies the foreground. The decision procedure becomes a descriptive anatomy. Something is gained in the exchange, a rigour about the relative autonomy of politics and ideology that the original text gestures toward but does not develop. Something is also lost, the operational edge, the sense that the ranking exists to be acted on before the configuration changes. Readers who meet the principal contradiction first in the French literature often miss that the doctrine was built for use under time pressure, by people for whom a misidentification was measured in lost counties rather than in lost arguments.

The reception also redistributed the essay's reputation in a way its author could not have foreseen. In the West, On Contradiction acquired a second life as a philosophical text at exactly the moment when its standing as a political charter was becoming harder to separate from the record of the state its author founded. Philosophers could read it through Althusser as a contribution to the theory of complex wholes, bracketing the doctrinal career described in the previous section. Historians of China could read it through the congresses as an instrument of rule, bracketing the philosophy. The two readings rarely meet, and each is impoverished by the separation: the philosophical reading forgets that the ranking doctrine was designed to allocate real resources and real coercion, and the institutional reading forgets that the doctrine's durability owed something to a genuine analytical structure that a first-rate philosopher found worth rebuilding his system around. This article has tried to keep the two readings in one frame, because the essay itself is in one frame: a metaphysics that becomes a procedure, a procedure that becomes an institution, an institution's vocabulary that becomes, in other hands, a philosophy again.

A further station of the reception deserves a sentence. The vocabulary of the essays travelled downward as well as outward, into the study materials of parties and movements far from China, where the principal contradiction was learned as a catechism answer rather than as the conclusion of an investigation. The catechism use is the degeneration the grading test identifies: the identification without the working. That the same text could serve as a research programme in one setting, a state doctrine in a second and a catechism in a third is not a paradox to be explained away. It is the normal biography of a successful procedure, whose authority survives the discipline that originally earned it. The essay's own theory predicts the pattern, since it holds that the character of a thing is set by whichever aspect of it is principal, and the principal aspect of this text has been, in different settings, its method, its authority and its slogans in turn.

## The strongest objection: a framework that cannot be wrong

The objection that any honest assessment must state at full strength is not that the essays are crude, or foreign, or old. It is that the framework they build cannot lose. Consider the machinery as the critic describes it. Every situation contains contradictions, so the analyst who announces contradictions has said something that cannot fail to be so. Among them one is principal, but the identity of the principal one is fixed by an analysis whose criteria the text does not state in a form that could convict an identification of error. The principal contradiction can change identity as stages succeed one another, so an identification overtaken by events is not refuted but updated. A contradiction classified as non-antagonistic can become antagonistic if mishandled, so a failed classification is reclassified rather than falsified. At every joint where the framework might be caught in a mistake, the framework supplies a category that absorbs the mistake as a further instance of itself. A theory so equipped, the objection concludes, cannot be wrong about anything, and a procedure built on it lends whatever policy is adopted the appearance of theoretical derivation: the policy comes first, in the order of decision, and the naming of the principal contradiction arrives after it, in the order of presentation, wearing the costume of a finding.

The objection has a distinguished pedigree, and the brief for this article requires it to be attached to names. Karl Popper, in Conjectures and Refutations, laid down the criterion the objection invokes: "A theory which is not refutable by any conceivable event is non-scientific," and he added that irrefutability is not a virtue of a theory but a vice. Popper's target was the Marxist theory of history as he believed its followers had come to practise it, a theory whose early predictions had been testable and falsified, and which its adherents had rescued, in his account, by reinterpreting both theory and evidence until agreement was restored at the price of testability. The application to this ranking doctrine writes itself, and critics have not been slow to write it: a ranking that can be revised without a stated rule of revision is Popper's rescue operation built into the theory's foundations, not practised as an occasional evasion but prescribed as a virtue under the name of concreteness.

Leszek Kołakowski, in Main Currents of Marxism, pressed the companion charge against the dialectical laws themselves, holding that they consist partly of "truisms with no specific Marxist content," partly of philosophical dogmas that cannot be proved by scientific means, and partly of statements that shift between the two conditions depending on how they are interpreted. Applied to the universality proposition, the charge is exact. That every process contains oppositions may be a truism if opposition is defined loosely enough to be found anywhere, and a dogma if it is defined strictly enough to be interesting, and the essay's procession of examples from the sciences does not settle which of the two it is, since the examples are chosen by the theory's own lights. Between Popper's criterion and Kołakowski's taxonomy, the objection stands complete: the framework's propositions are either empty or unprovable, and its central procedure, the naming of the governing contradiction, is a judgement dressed as a discovery, whose real function is to certify decisions taken on other grounds. A framework in which the principal contradiction can change identity with no stated criterion cannot be wrong about anything, and what cannot be wrong about anything cannot be the reason a policy is right.

### Where does the procedure leave a policy it cannot justify?

It leaves it dressed in the procedure's authority: since the identification of a principal contradiction presents a strategic choice as an analytical finding, a policy adopted for other reasons can be announced as the conclusion the situation compelled, and the vocabulary supplies no internal test by which an audience could separate the finding from the costume.

## What the text answers, and the concession that stands

The answer must begin by identifying, exactly, what the text does say about how the principal contradiction is identified, because the objection is entitled to its full force only against what the text actually contains. The essay does not leave the identification unregulated. It specifies, first, the object of the search: in a complex process, the analyst is to find the contradiction on which the existence and movement of the others depend, a functional definition that directs attention to causal weight rather than to noise or moral salience. It specifies, second, the method: the identification is to be reached by the concrete analysis of the particular situation, the study of each contradiction in its specificity, its stage of development, and its relations with the rest, the whole apparatus of the particularity proposition conscripted into the ranking step. It specifies, third, the signs of a change in rank: the transformation of the configuration when a secondary contradiction sharpens, when an external assault reorganises the field, when the principal aspect of the governing contradiction weakens, the stage doctrine supplying the occasions on which a re-ranking is to be expected rather than improvised. And it specifies, fourth, the discipline of the first essay standing behind the second: an identification is a concept, and concepts are tested in practice, so that a ranking that leads action into repeated failure stands condemned by the criterion of On Practice, which allows no concept, however orthodox, to survive its own results indefinitely.

That is a real answer, and it should be given its weight. The identification is not, in the text, a free choice. It is a judgement with a defined object, a prescribed method, stated occasions for revision, and an external audit in the outcomes of the action it guides. Many respectable procedures share exactly that shape. A physician's diagnosis, a general's estimate, a court's assessment of the decisive issue in a case: each is a judgement that cannot be reduced to an algorithm, each can be done well or badly, and each is answerable to results. The doctrine's defenders are entitled to the analogy, and to the further point that Popper's criterion was designed for universal theories in the sciences, not for procedures of situated judgement, which are assessed by the quality of their track record rather than by their refutability in the abstract. A method of investigation is not a hypothesis. Demanding that it be falsifiable may be a category error, and the demand, pressed without discrimination, would convict every craft of judgement that human beings practise in complex situations.

The concession must then be stated as plainly as the answer, because the text earns it. What the essay does not supply is a criterion by which an identification, as distinct from the policy built on it, could be shown mistaken at the time it is made. The functional definition tells the analyst what the principal contradiction does, determines or influences the others, but influence is a matter of degree and of interpretation, and two competent analysts, each faithful to the method, can rank the same configuration differently with no procedure in the text for deciding between them except further argument and eventual outcomes. Outcomes arrive late, and they judge the whole intervention, not the ranking step in isolation: a campaign can fail because the ranking was wrong, or because the execution was poor, or because the adversary adapted, and the framework provides no instrument for separating the causes. The stage doctrine, likewise, explains re-rankings after they occur more readily than it disciplines them in advance, since the boundary of a stage is itself a judgement of the same unruled kind. The honest verdict is therefore double. Against the charge that the doctrine is an empty licence, the text's specifications are a genuine defence: the procedure has a method, and the method can be followed well or badly, displayed or faked. Against the charge that the doctrine cannot certify its own identifications, there is no defence in the text, and this article concedes it without qualification: no criterion is supplied that would allow an identification of the principal contradiction to be shown mistaken, and every use of the doctrine that presents an identification as a finding rather than as a judgement has claimed more than the essay can underwrite.

### Where exactly does a method end and a licence begin?

The display of the working: a method shows the candidates it ranked, the evidence on which it ranked them, and the change that would alter the ranking, while a licence invokes the vocabulary to certify a conclusion reached elsewhere, and the difference is visible in whether the analysis could, in principle, have come out the other way.

The double verdict locates the essay's real position in the landscape of methods. It stands with the crafts of judgement, not with the sciences of law, and its propositions are best assessed as the maxims of a craft: universality as the maxim never to expect a tension-free situation, particularity as the maxim to investigate before prescribing, ranking as the maxim to concentrate on the governing conflict, the antagonism distinction as the maxim to match methods to the kind of conflict in hand. Maxims can be wise or foolish, fertile or sterile, and the historical evidence about these maxims is unusually rich, since they were practised for decades by a large organisation under conditions of extreme consequence, and then restated, borrowed and catechised across the world. The record shows the maxims organising brilliant campaigns of investigation and alliance, and it shows them certifying reversals and reclassifications that the maxims themselves could not check. Both halves of the record are the doctrine's biography. An assessment that keeps only one half has not assessed the essay but enlisted it.

## Unevenness: the premise beneath the ranking

Beneath the ranking doctrine sits a premise the essay states almost in passing and then relies on everywhere: contradictions do not develop evenly. In any complex process, some contradictions are intensifying while others are relaxing, some are moving toward their resolution while others have barely formed, and the aspects within each contradiction are likewise out of step, one waxing as the other wanes. The premise sounds modest. Its consequences are not. If development were even, a situation could be read from any of its parts, since each would mirror the state of the whole. Because development is uneven, no such shortcut exists: the analyst must survey the whole field of contradictions and estimate the trajectory of each, because the one that matters is determined precisely by the unevenness, by which tension is gathering force fastest at the point where the others intersect. Ranking is the formal recognition of unevenness. A doctrine that ranked nothing would be a doctrine for a world of uniform processes, and the essay's world is not uniform.

Unevenness also supplies the doctrine's account of timing, the dimension of politics that formulas handle worst. A contradiction correctly identified but addressed at the wrong stage is a practical error of the first order: the alliance that would have held during the stage when the national contradiction was principal becomes a capitulation in the stage when the class contradiction resumes its rank, and the confrontation that a later stage requires becomes adventurism when attempted early. The essay's stage doctrine, its insistence that configurations transform and that the analyst must periodise before prescribing, is the ranking doctrine extended through time. Althusser, whose reading fastened on exactly this feature, made unevenness the signature of the whole theory, the point at which the Marxist conception of a historical moment breaks with every philosophy of an expressive present in which all elements of a society march in step. On that reading, which this article has no cause to resist, the essay's most durable philosophical contribution may prove to be neither the universality claim nor the antagonism distinction but the picture of a present that is never contemporaneous with itself, a knot of processes running at different speeds, which action must untangle in the right order or not at all.

### Why does uneven development make ranking unavoidable?

Because if every contradiction in a process developed at the same pace and with the same force, attention could be distributed evenly without loss, whereas unevenness means that force concentrates at particular points and moments, so an actor who refuses to rank has not declined a choice but made one blindly, by default and by drift.

## The principal aspect at work: how things change their character

The doctrine of the principal aspect rewards a slower exposition than it usually receives, because it carries the essay's theory of qualitative change. A contradiction persists through a process, but its two aspects do not hold their positions. The aspect that was subordinate gathers strength through the very development the contradiction drives, while the dominant aspect, its conditions eroding, weakens. For a long period the exchange of strength is quantitative, a matter of degree registered in the balance between the sides. At a definite stage the positions invert: the formerly subordinate aspect becomes principal, and the thing whose character the aspect determined changes its nature, though its name and much of its material persist. The essay's world is full of such inversions patiently described. A colony straining against an empire is one thing while the imperial aspect dominates and another when the national movement takes the principal place. A party in opposition and the same party in government share a name, a membership and a programme, and differ in character because the aspect of their situation that rules has exchanged with its opposite.

The doctrine gives the analyst a second set of questions to set beside the ranking questions, and the two sets together form the essay's complete instrument. For the governing contradiction: which aspect dominates, how secure is its dominance, and what is strengthening the subordinate side? For each secondary contradiction: is its development being accelerated or retarded by the state of the principal one, and could its aspects invert in a way that promotes it? The instrument is diagnostic rather than predictive, and its honesty about that is a virtue the critics sometimes miss. It does not promise to say when an inversion will occur. It promises to say what an inversion would look like, what evidence would announce its approach, and why the character of the whole depends on it. Judged as a contribution to the crafts of judgement, that is a substantial promise, and the generations of analysts who kept the vocabulary in service were, whatever else they were doing, using an instrument that directed their attention to the balance within conflicts as well as to the ranking among them.

There is a reflexive application the essay invites and its later custodians rarely performed. A doctrine, too, is a thing with aspects, and the principal-contradiction doctrine has had its own exchanges of position: the aspect of method principal in the lecture hall, the aspect of authority principal in the doctrinal decades, the aspect of historical evidence principal in the scholarship since the sources opened. The character of the essays in the world's eyes changed at each exchange, from instrument to charter to document. Noticing the pattern does not settle the assessment of the doctrine, but it disciplines the assessor, who is reminded that the object under judgement is still in process, and that the verdict appropriate to one stage of its career may embarrass the verdict appropriate to the next.

## War as the essay's laboratory

The examples by which the essay teaches are drawn disproportionately from war, and the choice is methodological before it is biographical. War presents contradictions in their most legible form: two sides whose opposition is explicit, a field of secondary tensions among allies, neutrals, logistics and morale, and a premium on correct ranking so immediate that error is punished within the season. The essay's military illustrations do specific work. The discussion of attack and defence, of advance and retreat, of victory and defeat, exhibits the identity of opposites in a domain where the interconversion of the pair is a commonplace of the art: the defensive that prepares the offensive, the retreat that preserves the force that will advance. The discussion of strong and weak armies exhibits the principal aspect, since an army inferior in the whole may be superior at the chosen point, and the art of command consists in arranging the local inversion. When the essay generalises from these cases to all processes, the generalisation can be questioned, and philosophers have questioned whether the social world is as legible as a battlefield. But the choice of laboratory explains the doctrine's temper. It was built where the cost of misranking was annihilating and the feedback was fast, and its confidence about the possibility of correct identification, the confidence the concession in this article's criticism section qualifies, is the confidence of a headquarters that had repeatedly survived by ranking well.

The war examples also clarify the doctrine's relation to its author's wider strategic writings of the same years, in which the ranking of contradictions is performed rather than theorised: the analysis of the stages of a protracted war, the ranking of the national contradiction above internal ones for the duration of the resistance, the insistence that the aspects of strength and weakness between the belligerents would exchange places across the stages. The philosophical essay and the strategic essays are the same mind at two altitudes. Reading them together is the fastest way to see what the decision procedure looks like when its working is fully displayed, since the strategic texts argue their rankings at length, with the rival candidates named and the evidence marshalled. They are, by the grading test this article proposes, among the doctrine's best applications, and they supply the standard against which its thinner applications, the promulgated formulae of later decades, can be measured and found wanting.

## Contradiction is not a quarrel, and not a logical fault

A persistent misreading, encouraged by the English word, takes contradiction in these essays to mean either a logical inconsistency, a statement that cannot be true together with another, or a mere quarrel, a disagreement between people that firmer leadership might compose. The essay's term means neither. A contradiction, in the usage the text inherits and refines, is a relation between opposed aspects within a single process, aspects that depend on each other for their existence and character while pressing against each other in their development. The relation is ontological before it is verbal: it is found in things, not in sentences about things, and it is not a defect to be regretted but the engine by which the thing moves. A logical contradiction signals an error in the thinker. A contradiction in Mao's sense signals a structure in the world, and the thinker's error lies in missing it, or in flattening it into a simple opposition between separate things rather than an interdependence within one thing.

The distinction has practical teeth. If contradictions were quarrels, they could be ended by reconciliation, by the removal of misunderstanding, by goodwill. The essay denies the premise: the opposition between the aspects is constitutive, and while its form can be managed, softened, or prevented from turning antagonistic, the opposition itself lasts as long as the process lasts. If contradictions were logical faults, they could be ended by better definitions. The essay denies that premise too, since the aspects are not definitions but forces, classes, tendencies, materials, whose mutual pressure no clarification dissolves. What analysis can do, on the essay's account, is locate the contradictions accurately, rank them, and choose the manner of their handling appropriate to their kind and stage. The modesty of that offer, set against the grandeur of the universal law that frames it, is one of the text's most instructive tensions. The law promises a key to all development. The practice it actually teaches is a discipline of attention, closer to diagnosis than to prophecy, and the essay is at its best when the discipline is remembered and at its most dangerous when the key is believed.

### What separates contradiction in this essay from contradiction in logic?

A logical contradiction is a relation between statements, which cannot both be true, and its discovery convicts a thinker of error, whereas contradiction in the essay names opposed aspects bound within one process, whose interdependence and struggle drive the process forward, and whose discovery is the beginning of an analysis rather than the end of one.

## The schoolroom career: how the texts were taught

The essays reached most of their readers not as books but as curriculum. Within the party's schools and study campaigns, the two texts became standard material, read in groups, glossed by lecturers, and applied in the ritual of self-examination in which members analysed their own work in the vocabulary the texts supplied. The Rectification Movement of the early nineteen forties, the great campaign of ideological consolidation centred on Yan'an, placed the essays near the heart of its reading lists, and the habits of thought they taught, investigation before judgement, the ranking of contradictions in every situation, the classification of disagreements by their kind, became the default grammar of internal discussion for a generation of cadres. A text's meaning is partly what its institutions do with it, and the institution that took up these essays did with them something specific: it made their vocabulary the medium in which loyalty and error were described, so that to be fluent in the terms was to be legible as a member, and to be caught misclassifying a contradiction was to be marked as a thinker of the wrong kind.

The schoolroom career explains a feature of the essays that puzzles readers who meet them in translation as philosophy. Their repetitions, their numbered problems, their parade of examples from the familiar to the abstract, are the equipment of texts designed to be taught to adults of very unequal schooling in conditions of scarcity, and to be retained after a single hearing. Judged as literature the equipment can seem crude. Judged as instructional design for its setting, it is exact. The five propositions survive in summary, in the table this article prints, because they were built to survive in memory. The same design had a shadow. A text built for retention is also built for recitation, and the distance between understanding a ranking procedure and reciting its vocabulary is the distance the doctrine's later career travelled in many settings, from the seminar to the examination hall to the wall poster. The essays cannot be blamed for every use of their sentences, but their form invited the use that required the least thought, and a complete assessment registers the invitation.

There is a further institutional fact that belongs to the record. The essays' author supervised the system that taught them, and the interpretation of the texts was never left to the market of readers. Authoritative glosses accompanied the curriculum, study questions fixed the points to be extracted, and the application of the vocabulary to live disputes was itself supervised by the organisation whose disputes they were. The procedure for identifying the principal contradiction was thus, in practice, embedded in a hierarchy that reserved the final identification to the centre. Nothing in the 1937 text commands that arrangement, and its theory of knowledge, with its honouring of investigation at every level, can be read against it. But the arrangement governed the doctrine's career, and it sharpens the concession recorded in the criticism section: the text supplies no criterion by which an identification could be proved wrong, and the institution supplied a substitute for a criterion, the authority of the identifier, which is precisely the substitution the grading test in this article is designed to detect.

## The vocabulary abroad: translation and circulation

The essays travelled. Translations into Russian, English, French and dozens of other languages carried the vocabulary of the principal contradiction into movements and universities on every continent, often in cheap editions produced for study groups, and the term entered the working language of organisations that had no other contact with Chinese conditions. The French reception through Althusser, treated in its own section, was the most philosophically consequential of these journeys, but it was not the most typical. The typical journey ended in a party school or a guerrilla encampment, where the five propositions were learned as the method for analysing the local situation, and where the doctrine's demand for concrete investigation competed, with mixed success, against the temptation to import the Chinese rankings along with the Chinese vocabulary. The essay itself supplies the verdict on that temptation: a ranking earned in one situation and transferred untested to another is book worship in a new binding, and the history of the doctrine's export is in part a history of its own first essay being disobeyed by its admirers.

Translation left marks on the doctrine that specialists continue to note. The Chinese term rendered as principal contradiction carries the sense of the main or chief contradiction, and the adjective does quiet work in every language: it suggests a ranking by importance in the eyes of a judge, where the original argument aims at a ranking by causal governance in the structure of the process. The slippage between importance and governance is the slippage between the licence and the method, and it operates in Chinese as well as in translation, since a leadership's sense of what matters and a situation's structure of determination are easy to confuse when the leadership is doing the describing. Careful readers in any language learn to ask, of every announced principal contradiction, whether the claim is that this conflict governs the others or merely that it preoccupies the announcer. The essay's functional definition supports the first claim. The doctrine's career is largely the story of the second claim travelling under the first claim's passport.

## The analysis performed: China as the essay's worked example

Abstract statements of the doctrine come alive in the one extended application the essay itself performs, its analysis of the contradictions of Chinese society in the period of its composition. The performance deserves attention because it shows the procedure with its working displayed, the standard this article has proposed for grading every later application. The analyst begins by refusing the single-cause accounts on offer. Chinese society in the nineteen thirties is presented as a knot of contradictions of different orders: between the nation and the imperialist powers pressing upon it; between the masses and the feudal relations that bound them; between the peasantry and the bourgeoisie; between sections of the ruling camp themselves, whose rivalries opened spaces the revolution could use. Each contradiction is given its history and its trajectory. Some are sharpening under the pressure of invasion, some are being blunted by the very emergency that sharpens others, and the configuration as a whole is in motion because the invasion is reorganising it month by month.

The ranking the essay defends follows from the survey. With the deepening of Japanese aggression, the contradiction between China and Japan rises to the principal place, and the internal contradictions, without vanishing, are re-ranked beneath it: the class contradiction is to be handled by adjustment and negotiation rather than by confrontation, the contradictions within the national camp are to be managed for the sake of the resistance, and the energies of the movement are to be concentrated on the governing conflict. The identification is argued, not announced. The rival candidates are named, the evidence for their subordination is given, and the condition that would reverse the ranking, a settlement or withdrawal that returned the internal contradictions to the centre, is visible in the structure of the argument. Whatever a reader thinks of the politics, the performance satisfies the doctrine's own standard, and it did so before an audience in a position to test the identification in the hardest available way, since the united front it justified was the strategy the movement then staked its survival upon.

Set beside that performance, the thinner applications of later decades measure themselves. A restatement of the principal contradiction that names the new pair of poles and derives the central task, without displaying the survey of the contradictions outranked or the conditions that would promote one of them, has kept the doctrine's conclusion and dropped its method. The distinction is not pedantic. The method is the only part of the procedure that disciplines the conclusion, and a tradition that preserves the conclusions while letting the method lapse has preserved the licence and lost the investigation. The essay's text, which spends most of its length on the disciplines of study and only a fraction on any particular ranking, distributes its own emphasis in the opposite proportion, and a reader who follows the text's emphasis rather than the tradition's will know which part of the inheritance is the valuable part.

## What the procedure asks of an organisation

A decision procedure is also a constitution in miniature, since it settles who may decide what, and the doctrine's organisational implications deserve the same scrutiny as its logic. To run the procedure, an organisation must perform three acts. It must investigate, gathering the material from which a ranking can be argued, which requires channels through which unwelcome information travels upward intact. It must deliberate, entertaining rival rankings and the evidence for each, which requires that disagreement about the ranking be treated as a contribution rather than as disloyalty. And it must commit, concentrating resources on the chosen contradiction and subordinating the rest, which requires the discipline to leave real grievances unaddressed and real opportunities untaken while the concentration lasts. The doctrine's text speaks eloquently to the first act and adequately to the third. Its silence about the second is the silence the institution filled with hierarchy, and the quality of a ranking, in the doctrine's long career, tracked the quality of the deliberation behind it more closely than it tracked the cleverness of the ranker.

Postponement is the procedure's least celebrated output and its most constant one. To rank a contradiction as secondary is to tell the people who suffer it that their conflict will be handled later, by gentler methods, or not at all in its sharpest form, and the doctrine asks organisations to make that announcement routinely and asks the postponed to accept it as analysis rather than as betrayal. Sometimes the acceptance was earned by the visible accuracy of the ranking, as when the united front's subordination of class demands was repaid by survival and growth. Sometimes the announcement exhausted the credit of the announcers, as postponed contradictions accumulated interest in the form of resentment that later stages collected. The essay's stage doctrine contains the acknowledgement in embryo: a contradiction relegated is not abolished, its aspects continue their struggle, and the ranking that ignores its trajectory will be overturned by it. The procedure therefore demands of its users a quality rarer than decisiveness, the memory of what has been postponed and the readiness to re-rank before the postponed contradiction re-ranks itself by force.

Compromise, likewise, is reclassified by the procedure from a vice to an instrument. Within a ranking, the handling of secondary contradictions by concession and adjustment is not softness but method, the means by which the configuration is kept from generating new fronts while the principal conflict is engaged. The 1957 doctrine of handling contradictions among the people by persuasion is the same thought at the scale of a state. What the procedure cannot abide is the confusion of the registers: compromise offered on the principal contradiction, where the doctrine demands concentration, or confrontation applied to a secondary one, where the doctrine demands management. Much of the internal polemical literature of the movement that used this vocabulary consists of accusations of exactly those two confusions, which suggests that the procedure, whatever its defects as a theory, supplied its users with a genuine diagnostic language for their own errors. A vocabulary in which an organisation can say, precisely, that it has mistaken a secondary contradiction for the principal one, or handled a non-antagonistic one by antagonistic means, is a vocabulary that has earned its keep in the only currency the first essay recognises: it has been tested in practice, and its distinctions have survived the testing often enough to be reached for again.

## The essays and the older Chinese tradition

The essay opens its account of the two world outlooks with a gesture toward antiquity, remarking that the dialectical outlook emerged in ancient times in China as in Europe, though in a spontaneous and naive form that metaphysics later displaced. The gesture is brief, and the essay does not develop a genealogy of Chinese dialectics, but the gesture did work in its setting. It naturalised the imported doctrine, presenting dialectical materialism not as a foreign contrivance but as the systematic form of a way of thinking with native roots, and it answered, in advance, the charge that the philosophy of the lectures was a Russian garment on a Chinese body. The classical saying the essay quotes against the metaphysical outlook, that heaven does not change and the Way does not change, is drawn from the Han dynasty synthesis it opposes, and the choice of an ancient target performs the same naturalisation in reverse: the enemy, too, is given a Chinese pedigree, so that the contest of outlooks is staged as a drama internal to Chinese thought rather than as an import against a tradition.

Later scholarship has treated the gesture with reserve, noting that the ancient materials, the Book of Changes with its interplay of yin and yang, the military classics with their reversals of fortune, the Mohist and Legalist debates, resemble the dialectics of the essay mainly at the altitude from which all doctrines of change resemble one another. The reserve is justified, and the essay's own standards justify it, since a claim of kinship across two thousand years is exactly the kind of unexamined transfer the particularity proposition forbids. What can be said without strain is that the essays were received by many of their first readers as continuous with habits of thought already familiar from the classical education of the older cadres: the expectation that situations invert, that strength carries the seed of weakness, that the wise actor waits for the configuration to ripen. Whether that familiarity was a discovery or an effect of the teaching, it eased the doctrine's passage into the movement's common sense, and it forms part of the explanation for the vocabulary's durability in Chinese political language long after the textbooks from which it was drawn had been forgotten by everyone except historians.

## Grading the applications: the procedure turned on its users

The grading test proposed in this article, that an application of the doctrine earns its authority by displaying the investigation behind the identification, can now be applied to the doctrine's own career, and the exercise is the fairest summary of the essay's strengths and limits that the evidence allows. Three cases mark the range. The first is the ranking performed in the late nineteen thirties, when the national contradiction was identified as principal and the united front built upon the identification. The working was displayed at length, in the essay itself and in the strategic writings around it: the rival contradictions were surveyed, their trajectories argued, the condition for reversal stated. The identification was then tested in the hardest available practice, and the movement that acted on it survived a war it had entered as the weaker party. By the doctrine's own criterion, the test of practice, and by this article's criterion, the display of the working, the application stands at the head of the class.

The second case is the sequence of official restatements, from the Eighth Congress through the resolution of 1981 to the Nineteenth Congress. These identifications were promulgated through the forms of collective decision, and the documentation around them varies. Some were accompanied by extensive argument about the state of the society being ranked; others arrived as formulae whose supporting analysis, if it existed, was conducted inside the leadership and presented to the wider organisation as a conclusion to be studied rather than as an argument to be weighed. The grading test does not ask whether the identifications were correct, a question this article has declined throughout, but whether they were earned in the doctrine's own manner. On that question the record is mixed, and the mixture is instructive: the procedure survived as a form while its discipline, the displayed investigation, was observed in proportion to the deliberative health of the institution using it. The doctrine, in short, graded its users as reliably as they graded their situations.

The third case is the catechism use, the learning of the vocabulary as a set of answers. In the study materials of movements and in the examination cultures that grew around the doctrine, the principal contradiction could be produced as a recitation, the five propositions as a list, the antagonism distinction as a pair of definitions, with no situation investigated and no ranking performed. The grading test returns its lowest mark here, and the failure is not the students'. A procedure taught as a catechism has been stripped of the one element that made it a procedure, the obligation to investigate the case in hand. The irony is exact and was available to any reader of the first essay: the doctrine of the man who laid down that the right to speak must be earned by investigation became, in the catechism, a thing spoken without investigation. The text contains the standard by which that use stands condemned, which is the strongest reason for continuing to read the text rather than only its reputation.

### By what standard does the procedure judge its own applications?

By the working displayed behind the identification: an application grades highly when the rival contradictions are surveyed, the evidence for the ranking is argued, and the condition that would reverse it is stated, and it grades as a mere use of the licence when the identification arrives as a formula, however orthodox its vocabulary, with no investigation shown.

## The scholarly assessment: what the specialists concluded

The academic study of the essays, conducted over half a century by scholars with access to progressively better sources, has converged on a set of judgements that an assessment can report with some confidence. Stuart Schram, the dean of Mao studies in the West, treated the essays as genuine philosophy of a distinctive kind: not contributions to the technical debates of the discipline, but the considered worldview of a revolutionary leader, whose originality lay in the synthesis and in the operational doctrines rather than in the framework. John Bryan Starr's study of Mao's political thought traced the career of the principal contradiction through the policy record and documented, with a care that neither worship nor polemic could have managed, the occasions on which redefinitions of the principal contradiction preceded and certified reversals of course, supplying much of the evidence on which the unfalsifiability objection, in its scholarly form, rests. Nick Knight's work on the sources, already cited, settled the question of dependence and thereby changed the question of originality into the more interesting question of what the rearrangement accomplished.

The convergence is worth stating as a balance sheet. On the framework, the verdict is dependence: the essays systematise the Soviet dialectical materialism of their decade, with the rearrangements noted. On the epistemology, the verdict is restatement with a purpose: On Practice adds little to the inherited theory of knowledge and deploys it with unusual polemical precision. On the central doctrine, the verdict is originality of the consequential kind: the ranking procedure, assembled from hints in Lenin and the textbooks, became in Mao's hands an instrument that organised the strategy of a revolution, the doctrine of a state and the theory of a Parisian school. On the texts as published, the verdict is caution: they are revised documents, and arguments built on their sentences must reckon with the revisions. A reader who holds the balance sheet in mind is equipped to resist both of the lazy receptions, the one that treats the essays as the unmediated voice of a genius and the one that treats them as a translation exercise with a famous name attached. The truth the scholarship supports is more interesting than either: a gifted reader, working in a cave-region headquarters with borrowed books, built from those books the one doctrine in the Marxist tradition that tells its user, in so many words, that the doctrine's own identifications must be earned again in every situation, and then watched the doctrine be used, for decades, in every way except that one as often as in that one.

## Misreadings this article corrects

The first misreading takes the essays as original inventions, philosophy struck from the author's head in a cave. The documentary record refutes it: the reading programme of 1936 and 1937, the annotated textbooks, the lecture notes tracking the Soviet manuals, all establish an engagement with an existing literature so close that the interesting question is not whether the essays depend on it but how. The correction matters because the invention story disables criticism in both directions. A text believed to be invented can only be worshipped or debunked, while a text known to be a rearrangement can be assessed, joint by joint, for what the rearrangement did. The second misreading is the mirror image, taking the dependence as a refutation, as though a doctrine assembled from sources were thereby disposed of. Most working philosophies are assemblies. The question the record actually poses is whether the assembled doctrine, the ranking procedure, did work in the world that the unassembled materials had not done, and the answer given by the doctrine's career, in Yan'an, in Paris and at the congresses, is that it did.

The third misreading takes contradiction to mean logical contradiction, and then finds the essay either trivial or confused. The correction is in the section on the term itself: contradiction names a relation of opposed and interdependent aspects within a process, and the essay's claims are about that relation, not about the laws of statement. A reader who imports the logical sense will find the universality proposition absurd, since a world in which every statement is inconsistent is not a world but a muddle, and will miss the proposition the essay is making, that every process is constituted by internal oppositions whose struggle drives its development. The fourth misreading takes the principal contradiction to be fixed, a permanent feature of a society or of history, the class contradiction enthroned forever or the national contradiction installed for a century. The text forbids the reading in terms: the identity of the principal contradiction changes with the configuration and the stage, and an identification treated as permanent has already ceased to be an application of the doctrine and become a relic of one. The fifth misreading assumes that the published essays are the lectures of 1937 word for word. The revision history refutes it, and the correction is practical: cite the version you mean, and do not build an argument about the Yan'an period on sentences polished for the Collected Works without checking what stood there before.

A sixth misreading, subtler than the rest, takes the antagonistic distinction to be a description of kinds of conflict found in nature, so that the classifier merely reports which kind a given conflict is. The record of the distinction's use corrects the picture. The classification was an act with consequences, assigning methods and fates, and the boundary between the categories moved with political judgement, as the 1957 text's own warnings acknowledge. To read the distinction as descriptive is to miss where the power in the machinery sits. A seventh misreading, common in the literature of movements, takes the doctrine's flexibility as its vindication, proof that the framework can accommodate every development. The criticism section has given the answer: accommodation without a criterion is the objection, not the reply, and a defence of the essay that rests on its ability to absorb any outcome has conceded the critics' case while imagining it was refuting it. The essay's genuine defences are narrower and stronger: a defined object for the identification, a prescribed method, marked occasions for revision, and an audit in practice. They should be used, and the wider claim should be dropped.

## How to read the two essays

The order of reading is the order of delivery: On Practice first, then On Contradiction. The sequence is not a courtesy to chronology. The epistemology of the first essay is the licence for the method of the second: only a reader who has absorbed the claim that knowledge is made in cycles of practice, and that concepts untried in a situation are not yet knowledge of it, will feel the full weight of the second essay's demand for concrete analysis, or notice when later users of the vocabulary skip the investigation the first essay makes obligatory. Read in the reverse order, the doctrine of ranking contradictions floats free as a piece of metaphysics, and its character as a procedure, an instruction to an investigator, is easy to miss. Read in the delivered order, the second essay is the first essay's method applied to the structure of situations, and the pair coheres as a single teaching: how to know, and what sort of world it is in which knowing must proceed in that way.

The versions require a decision. A reader coming to the essays for the first time should read the published texts, the versions that acted in history, in a translation made from the Selected Works, and should read them whole, since each is short and the architecture is part of the meaning. A reader arguing about the essays, about their sources, their originality or their author's development, owes the argument a consultation of the earlier stratum: the lecture notes in Knight's edition, where the dependence on the textbooks is visible on the page and the revisions can be compared line by line. The two readings answer different questions, and confusing them produces the characteristic muddles of the secondary literature, in which the published text is praised for an independence its drafts did not have or blamed for a derivativeness its published form had edited away. The discipline is the one the essays themselves teach: specify the object, in the version and stage appropriate to the question, before pronouncing on it.

A study method suggests itself from the texts' own pedagogy. Read each essay once for its architecture, marking the five propositions and the two stages of knowledge. Read it again for its examples, asking of each whether it illustrates the proposition or tests it. Then take the procedure out of the book and run it, on paper, against a situation the reader knows well, a workplace, a city, a historical episode studied in another connection, performing the full sequence: survey the contradictions, argue the ranking, name the principal aspect, state what would reverse the identification. The exercise is the essays' own criterion applied to the reader: the concepts are not known until they have been practised, and the respects in which the exercise is difficult, the points where the ranking resists argument and the evidence runs thin, are exactly the respects in which the doctrine's limits, conceded in the criticism section, stop being abstract. A reader who has felt the identification step resist discipline from the inside has understood the essay better than a reader who can recite its propositions, and has also understood why its author, who had performed the step under fire, trusted it further than his critics think he should have.

## The decision procedure restated

The argument of this article can be gathered into a single paragraph of assessment. On Practice and On Contradiction are two short teaching texts of 1937, built from the Soviet philosophical literature their author was annotating, and revised by him into canonical form in the early nineteen fifties. The first supplies a theory of knowledge as a cycle of practice and testing. The second supplies a theory of situations as knots of unevenly developing contradictions, and at its centre a doctrine the sources did not contain: that among the contradictions of a complex process one is principal, determining the others, and that its identity changes as the process changes. That doctrine is the article's subject because it is best read not as a description but as a decision procedure, an instruction to identify the governing conflict and to allocate everything else in relation to it. So read, it explains the doctrine's career: its power in the hands of a movement that investigated before it struck, its restatement at the congresses of a ruling party across six decades, its rebuilding by Althusser into the concept of overdetermination, and its characteristic abuse, the certification of reversals by redefinition, against which the text provides a method but, as this article has conceded in the critics' own terms, no criterion by which a ranking could be shown mistaken at the moment it is made. The essays survive their concession. A procedure that displays its working can still be followed, graded and learned from, and the texts that contain it remain what they were in the lecture hall: the most consequential short course in the Marxist tradition on the discipline of deciding what matters most.

## The two outlooks reconsidered: metaphysics as a habit

The essay's opening contest between the metaphysical and dialectical outlooks is usually read as a ceremonial first chapter, a genuflection to the tradition before the original work begins. Read again, it is a diagnosis of a habit that institutions fall into independently of their doctrines. The metaphysical outlook, in the essay's portrait, treats things as isolated, finished and self-explanatory, and explains their changes by forces outside them. Translated out of philosophy, that is the outlook of every headquarters that explains a failure by the weather, the terrain, the enemy's numbers or the treachery of allies, and of every ministry that explains a policy's collapse by the ingratitude of its beneficiaries. The dialectical reply, that change is driven from inside the situation by its own oppositions and that external forces act only through internal ones, is in the first instance a rule of inquiry: when something has gone wrong, look for the contradiction inside the arrangement before looking for the accident outside it. The egg and the stone make the rule unforgettable. A policy, like an egg, hatches only what its internal constitution permits, and the administrator who blames the temperature has declined to examine the egg.

The rule cuts against the analyst's own side as readily as against the enemy, which is why the essay insists that the defeats of the movement in the preceding decade be explained internally, by the opportunism and adventurism within its own ranks, rather than by the strength of its opponents. That insistence, repeated across the author's writings of the period, is the two outlooks doing practical work: an organisation trained to seek internal causes will audit its own line and structure when it fails, while an organisation trained in external causes will purge messengers and multiply vigilance. Neither habit guarantees wisdom. Internal explanation can curdle into the hunt for hidden wreckers, a pathology the movement also exhibited. But the essay's version of the internal turn is disciplined by its pairing with investigation: the internal cause is to be established by study of the actual contradiction, not assigned by suspicion. The distance between the disciplined version and the pathology is the distance between the method and its degeneration, the same distance this article has measured at every other joint of the doctrine.

There is a second, quieter point in the opening chapter that repays attention. The essay grants the metaphysical outlook a history and a social basis: it was the thinking of an earlier stage of knowledge, serviceable when the sciences were young and things could plausibly be treated in isolation, and it persists because it is the spontaneous philosophy of common sense in every age. The concession matters for the essay's pedagogy. The student is not asked to despise the metaphysical habit as a moral failure but to recognise it as the default setting of untrained thought, his own included, against which the dialectical discipline must be practised deliberately and repeatedly. A doctrine that understands its rival as a stage of its own audience's development teaches differently from one that understands its rival as an enemy camp, and the difference in teaching temper between the opening chapter and the later doctrinal uses of the same vocabulary is another measure of the distance between the text and its career.

## The subordinated laws: quantity, quality and the missing negation

The essay's rearrangement of the inherited laws of dialectics is easy to pass over, since it occurs inside the exposition of the first law and announces itself as a clarification. Its significance emerges in the comparison with the textbooks. The Soviet manuals presented three laws in co-ordination: the unity and interpenetration of opposites, the transformation of quantity into quality and its reverse, and the negation of the negation. The essay demotes the second and third, treating the transformation of quantity into quality as a form of the unity of opposites, the point at which the struggle of the aspects passes a threshold, and leaving the negation of the negation almost entirely to one side. Balibar, in the recollection cited earlier, fastened on the omission as a philosophical event: the negation of the negation was the most visibly Hegelian of the laws, the one that guaranteed history a direction and a reconciliation, and a dialectics without it is a dialectics without a built-in destination. What remains is struggle without a promised synthesis, development without a script, which is precisely the austere version of the doctrine that the ranking procedure requires. A process whose destination were guaranteed would not need its contradictions ranked by fallible investigators; it would need only to be awaited. The essay's universe, by contrast, has outcomes that depend on the balance of aspects and the quality of the intervention, and its philosophy is the more honest for the omission.

The demotion of quantity into quality has a practical corollary the essay's audience would have applied at once. If qualitative change is the threshold form of the struggle of opposites, then the accumulation of small changes, of forces, of grievances, of organisational strength, is never merely quantitative in its significance: it is the approach toward an inversion, and the analyst's task includes estimating the distance to the threshold. The strategic writings from those years are saturated with such estimates, of the relative strength of the belligerents across the stages of a long war, each stage defined by the approaching exchange of the principal aspect between the invader and the resistance. The philosophy and the strategy illuminate each other here with unusual clarity. The law, demoted in the textbook sense, becomes in the headquarters sense a doctrine of timing: accumulate where accumulation is invisible, strike where the threshold is near, and never mistake the quiet phase of a contradiction for its absence.

### Why did the essay set the negation of the negation aside?

Because its author subordinated every other law to the unity of opposites and treated the remaining laws as forms of that one, with the effect, whether intended or not, of removing the guarantee of a final reconciliation from the doctrine: struggle continues without a scripted destination, and outcomes depend on how the aspects balance and on the quality of intervention.

## The doctrine among its rivals: other machines for ranking

The problem the doctrine solves is not unique to Marxism, and placing the procedure beside its rivals from other traditions sharpens the assessment of both its power and its exposure. Military theory offers the closest analogue. The Prussian tradition that runs through Clausewitz locates, in any enemy system, a centre of gravity, the hub of power and movement on which everything depends, against which the attacker's forces are to be concentrated. The resemblance to the principal contradiction is structural and striking: a complex whole, a survey of its elements, the identification of the one element whose fate decides the rest, and the concentration of effort upon it. The differences are equally instructive. The centre of gravity is identified within a bounded problem, a campaign against a known adversary, and its identification is disciplined by a professional literature of cases and by the rapid verdict of battle. The principal contradiction is identified within an unbounded problem, a whole society across an open future, where the verdict of practice arrives slowly and ambiguously. The doctrine extends a sound military intuition beyond the conditions that discipline it, and the extension, not the intuition, is where the critics' case takes hold.

Lenin's image of the weakest link in a chain offers a second comparison, this time from inside the tradition. The chain breaks at its weakest link, and the revolutionary's art is to find the link at which the whole imperialist system can be snapped. The image shares with Mao's doctrine the commitment to unevenness and to concentration at a chosen point. It differs in its object: the weakest link is a place, a country or a moment, whereas the principal contradiction is a relation, a ranked tension within a process, and the difference gives Mao's version a wider jurisdiction, since relations exist at every scale from the village to the world system, each with its own ranking to be performed. The wider jurisdiction is the doctrine's glory and its temptation, for a procedure that can be applied at every scale can also be invoked at every scale, and the history of its use includes no shortage of village disputes and office quarrels solemnly analysed into principal and secondary contradictions by parties to the quarrel itself.

A third comparison belongs to the ordinary world of administration, where triage, the ranking of cases by urgency and consequence, is practised in hospitals, in engineering maintenance and in the management of any portfolio of problems under scarcity. Triage shares the doctrine's premise that attention is scarce and must be allocated by a ranking, and its practitioners would recognise the doctrine's insistence that the ranking be argued from the state of the whole. What triage possesses, and the doctrine lacks, is a professional consensus about the criteria of rank, built from outcomes recorded across thousands of cases, so that a triage officer's judgement can be checked against the protocol and the protocol revised against the outcomes. Mao's doctrine of the principal contradiction never acquired its protocol. Its criteria remained at the level of the functional definition and the exemplary cases, which is why its applications varied so widely in quality and why the grading test used in this article, the display of the working, has to serve as a substitute standard. The comparison is not a dismissal. Crafts before their protocols are still crafts, and some of the doctrine's performances rank with the best situated judgement of its century. It is a location of the doctrine on the map of methods: nearer to the war room and the clinic than to the laboratory, and to be assessed by the standards of those rooms, which are track record, displayed reasoning and the audit of results.

## A guided tour of the second essay, section by section

A reader who wants the essay's argument in its own order can walk its seven stations with profit, since the order is itself an argument. The first station, the two world outlooks, establishes the rule of internal explanation: development is self-movement driven by internal contradiction, and external causes act only through internal ones. Everything later depends on the rule, because the search for a principal contradiction is a search inside a process, and an analyst still hunting for external causes will rank the scenery instead of the structure. The second station, universality, then guarantees the search an object: there will be contradictions, in every stage, without exception. The third station, particularity, is the essay's longest act of instruction and its methodological heart. It argues that each contradiction and each aspect has its specific character, that the universal is known only through the particular, that processes must be divided into stages, and that the failure to study the particular is the root error of the dogmatists the lectures were convened to correct. A reader in a hurry can measure the essay's priorities by its proportions: the station that teaches how to look is given more room than the stations that announce what will be found.

The fourth station contains the centre: the principal contradiction and the principal aspect. Its argument proceeds by the analysis of complexity, the observation that a complex process contains many contradictions developing unevenly, from which the ranking doctrine is drawn, and then descends within the single contradiction to the ranking of its aspects and the exchange of their positions as the engine of qualitative change. The fifth station, identity and struggle, supplies the dynamics: the aspects interdepend and interconvert under conditions, while their struggle is absolute, so that every unity is provisional and every process carries its transformation inside itself. The sixth station, the place of antagonism, performs the classification on which so much later depended, distinguishing the contradictions that resolve by the overthrow of a side from those that resolve by adjustment, and noting that antagonism is one form of struggle rather than its synonym. The seventh station is a conclusion of a few paragraphs, and its brevity is eloquent: having built the machine, the essay does not linger over it, but sends the reader back to the analysis of concrete things, where alone, on its own theory, the machine can be learned.

Two features of the tour deserve emphasis for the student. The first is the essay's habit of ending each station with a direction to practice: the universality station closes by returning to particularity, the particularity station by demanding investigation, the ranking station by warning that the principal contradiction changes. The text is restless in a way its summaries are not, and the restlessness is the method enacting itself, refusing to let any proposition become a resting place. The second feature is the distribution of certainty. The metaphysical stations speak in the tone of settled law. The operational stations, where the doctrine touches decisions, are hedged with the vocabulary of stages, conditions and transformations, the grammar of a fallible craft. Readers who attend to the distribution will locate the essay's confidence and its humility in the right places, and will be less surprised than most by the concession this article has recorded: the humility was in the text from the beginning, at exactly the joint where the critics later pressed.

## Who identifies? The subject of the procedure

Every procedure implies a subject, someone authorised and equipped to run it, and the essays are less explicit about their subject than about their steps. The implied identifier of the principal contradiction, across the two texts, is a collective investigator: the party, the army, the organisation that studies its situation through surveys, reports and deliberation, and whose leadership pronounces the ranking that the study supports. The first essay's theory of knowledge is social in the same way, its cycles of practice and knowledge turning at the scale of classes and movements as readily as at the scale of persons. There is a strength in the collectivity. A ranking produced by an organisation with investigators in every village and reports from every front draws on a breadth of perception no individual analyst commands, and the doctrine's best performances, the rankings of the war years, were collective products in exactly that sense, argued through a headquarters that functioned as a genuine centre of study.

The weakness is the obverse. A collective subject requires a mechanism for resolving its internal disagreements about the ranking, and the mechanism the movement possessed was authority: the ranking that issued from the centre bound the whole, and the theory of knowledge that honoured investigation at every level coexisted with a discipline that made the centre's identification final. The essays do not address the tension, because in 1937 the centre and the lecturer were the same, and the identification being taught was the lecturer's own. Later readers inherit the tension unresolved. When the identifier is a leadership whose pronouncements cannot be appealed, the standard used in these pages, the display of the working, becomes the only check available to those who must act on the identification, and the historical record shows how unevenly even that check could be applied. A complete assessment of the doctrine must therefore include its sociology: the procedure presupposes an investigator who can be wrong in public, corrected by results and by argument, and it degrades in exact proportion as the investigator is placed beyond correction. The text's own criterion, the verdict of practice, was meant to supply the correction. Institutions that muffle the verdict, by controlling the reports of results as tightly as the pronouncements of rankings, disable the criterion while continuing to cite it, and the doctrine's saddest applications are those in which both the identification and its audit were monopolised by the same hands.

### Why must existence and rank be kept apart?

Existence is guaranteed by the universality proposition and costs nothing to assert, whereas rank is a judgement earned only by investigating how the contradictions in a situation condition one another, so every claim that a contradiction exists should be treated as the beginning of an analysis and every claim about which one is principal as its conclusion.

That separation, existence from rank, is the single most useful discipline a student can carry away from the essay, because most abuses of the vocabulary consist in collapsing it. To announce that a situation contains a contradiction between this and that is to say something the theory guarantees in advance and therefore something with no informational value. To announce that the contradiction is the principal one is to make the only claim in the system that can be wrong, and it should be priced accordingly, in the currency of displayed evidence. Examiners of the doctrine, friendly and hostile alike, have often failed to enforce the separation, crediting identifications with the certainty that belongs only to existences. The essay, read with the separation in mind, becomes a sterner and more interesting text than either its catechists or its dismissers describe: a text whose guaranteed claims are trivial, whose valuable claims are risky, and whose author, whatever his later custodians did, built the risk into the centre of the machine and labelled it with the demand for investigation.

## The pear and the planet: the essay's rhetoric of examples

The examples in the two essays repay study as rhetoric, because they reveal the intended epistemology of the reader. They are drawn, in descending order of scale, from the cosmos to the kitchen. The essay on contradiction ranges across the sciences for its universality illustrations, differential calculus with its positive and negative, mechanics with its action and reaction, physics with its charges, chemistry with its combinations, biology with its life and death, before arriving at classes and parties. The essay on practice descends the other way, from the laws of revolution to the taste of a pear. The two movements meet in the middle, in the village and the battlefield, and the meeting is the message: no scale is exempt from the law, and no scale is so grand that it cannot be interrogated by the methods that work at the smallest scale, contact, intervention, test. A philosophy that begins with a pear and ends with a planet is making a claim about the uniformity of knowing that its examples perform rather than merely state.

The natural science examples have aged unevenly, and honesty requires the point to be registered. Several were drawn from the popular science of the decade and from the textbooks' stock illustrations, and a few, examined by later specialists, turned out to be loose: the mathematics of infinitesimals does not exhibit contradiction in the essay's sense without considerable charitable reconstruction, and the biology of the period has been superseded in ways that leave some illustrations stranded. The damage to the doctrine is real but bounded. The universality proposition, as the criticism section argued, was never carried by its examples; they illustrate a claim whose support, such as it is, lies in the metaphysical frame, and a reader who discards the illustrations has lost the essay's colour but not its centre. The centre, the ranking procedure, draws its examples from war and politics, domains where the author's experience was first-hand and the illustrations correspondingly exact. It is a peculiarity of the text that its most checkable examples are its least important and its most important are drawn from the domain where checking is hardest, the judgement of situations, and the peculiarity is one more reason the assessment of the essay cannot be conducted as an audit of its illustrations.

There is a lesson in the pear that outlasts the philosophy around it. The illustration has entered the language of fields far from Marxism, in the pedagogy of experiential learning, in the methodology of fieldwork disciplines, in the management literature's suspicion of the report that substitutes for the visit. In each borrowing, the point preserved is the essay's own: some knowledge lies past the point of intervention, and the observer who declines to intervene has not purified his knowledge but refused it. The borrowing fields rarely preserve the second half of the doctrine, the obligation to return the concept formed from the intervention to further practice for its audit. The whole circuit is the essay's contribution; the pear is merely its most portable part.

## One divides into two: the doctrine argued over at home

The essays' vocabulary remained a live instrument of internal argument long after it became a curriculum, and one episode shows the instrument being fought over with a precision that illuminates the identity proposition better than any commentary. In the early nineteen sixties, the philosopher Yang Xianzhen, a senior figure in the party's own school system, taught that the unity-of-opposites law could be summarised in the formula that two combine into one, emphasising the moment of unity in the relation of opposites. In 1964 the formula was attacked in the party's press as a revision of the dialectic, and the counter-formula, that one divides into two, was upheld as the correct summary, emphasising the moment of struggle. Mao's authority stood behind the second formula. The controversy, conducted in newspapers and journals with an intensity outsiders found disproportionate to its verbal stakes, was in substance an argument about the fourth proposition of the 1937 essay: whether identity or struggle is the senior partner in the relation of opposites. The essay's own answer, identity conditional and relative, struggle unconditional and absolute, was the text to which the winning side appealed, and the episode demonstrates that the text's asymmetries were load-bearing in the movement's own reading of it. A formula that made unity senior would have licensed an politics of harmony and consolidation. The formula that made division senior licensed the continuation of struggle as the normal condition, with consequences for the campaigns of the following decade that historians continue to weigh.

The episode also displays, in miniature, the sociology of identification examined earlier. The correct reading of the law was settled not by the text alone, since both formulas could quote the tradition, but by authority speaking through the text, after which the settled reading bound the school system that Yang himself had served. A student of the doctrine should hold the episode in mind as the domestic counterpart of the Althusser reception: in Paris the same text was used to dissolve orthodoxies, in Beijing to settle one, and the difference lay not in the sentences but in the institutions reading them. Texts of this kind, procedures for judgement compressed into portable formulas, are unusually dependent on their institutional weather, because the formula travels and the discipline must be re-supplied in every new setting by whoever teaches it. Where the teacher is a seminar, the formula opens arguments. Where the teacher is a hierarchy, it closes them. The essay of 1937 cannot be acquitted of having been written for the second setting as well as the first, since its author built the hierarchy that taught it, but neither can the first setting be denied, since the text's own demands, investigation, displayed reasoning, the audit of practice, are the equipment of open argument and were repeatedly used as such by readers the hierarchy did not control.

## Lenin behind the text: the notebooks and the fragment

The essay on contradiction has a named ancestor inside the tradition that its exposition treats with a deference verging on concealment, and identifying the ancestor clarifies both the dependence and the departure. In 1914 and 1915, in Swiss exile, Lenin filled notebooks with excerpts and comments on Hegel's Logic, and in a short fragment known as On the Question of Dialectics he set down the sentence the 1937 essay is built to expand: dialectics, in the proper sense, is the study of contradiction in the very essence of objects. The fragment sketches the whole programme in a page: the splitting of a single whole and the cognition of its contradictory parts as the essence of dialectics, the interdependence and struggle of the opposites, the demand that knowledge grasp a thing in its relations and its self-movement rather than in its isolation. Mao quotes the sentence at his threshold and presents the essay as its unfolding, and the presentation is accurate as far as it goes. The universality and identity material of the essay is Lenin's fragment systematised, with the textbooks' furniture arranged around it.

What the fragment does not contain is the ranking doctrine in its developed form. Lenin's writings on strategy are full of ranked judgements, of links in chains and moments seized, and the fragment's insistence on the concrete is the seed of the particularity proposition. But the explicit apparatus, the principal contradiction named and defined, the principal aspect, the exchange of positions as the mechanism of qualitative change, the stage-wise re-ranking as a standing obligation, is the work of the 1937 lectures. The genealogy therefore runs in three strands that an assessment should keep separate: the metaphysical frame from Lenin's fragment and the Hegelian tradition behind it; the textbook system from the Soviet manuals of the nineteen thirties; and the operational doctrine from the lectures themselves, answering to a headquarters and its urgencies. The strands are braided so tightly in the text that generations of readers took the whole for a single cloth, and the scholarly unbraiding of the past half-century, whatever it has cost the text in aura, has returned a more usable object: a doctrine whose parts can be assessed separately, the frame by philosophy, the system by the history of textbooks, and the procedure by the record of its applications.

## Investigation as a genre: the reports behind the philosophy

The essays of 1937 codified a practice their author had been performing for a decade, and the earlier performances belong in any account of the philosophy because they show the method before it had a vocabulary. In 1927 Mao reported on an investigation of the peasant movement in Hunan, walking the counties, interviewing participants, and classifying the actors of the rural drama by their relation to the upheaval he was assessing. In 1930, in the essay later known as Oppose Book Worship, he laid down the maxim that no investigation confers no right to speak, and conducted the survey of Xunwu county that remains a model of the genre: a commodity-by-commodity, street-by-street inventory of a small city's economy, assembled from interviews with shopkeepers, officials and peasants, from which the social structure of the county is made to emerge. The genre has rules that the philosophy of 1937 can be read as generalising. Begin from the thing itself, in its place. Classify by relations, not by appearances. Let the categories be corrected by the material. Conclude with the ranking of the forces found, and the disposition that follows from the ranking.

Reading the reports beside the essays dissolves a puzzle about the philosophy's tone. The essays sound, to a reader formed by academic philosophy, strangely confident for texts that preach fallibilism and perpetual revision. The confidence is the investigator's, not the metaphysician's: the tone of a man who has repeatedly watched a well-conducted survey overturn the assumptions of a headquarters, and who trusts the genre the way a craftsman trusts a tool that has never yet failed to cut. The trust was not always repaid, and the later record contains investigations commissioned to confirm rankings already decided, the genre's form preserved and its discipline inverted. But the genre itself, at its best, remains the doctrine's strongest exhibit, stronger than any of its propositions, because it shows the procedure producing knowledge that its rivals, the imported resolution and the veteran's hunch alike, demonstrably lacked. A philosophy is entitled to be judged partly by the practices it trains, and the investigation meeting, the notebook, the classified inventory of a county town, are practices these texts trained in thousands of practitioners who never read a page of Hegel.

### Why does the investigation genre matter to the philosophy?

Because it is the philosophy in its working clothes: the survey that classifies a county's actors by their relations, corrects its categories against the material, and ends in a ranking of forces performs, without the vocabulary, exactly the sequence the essays later formalised as perception, conception, concrete analysis and the identification of the governing contradiction.

## Determination and initiative: the tension at the doctrine's heart

A final tension runs through the two essays and through the scholarship on them, and an assessment should name it rather than resolve it. The ranking doctrine is, on one face, a doctrine of determination: the ranking is in the situation, placed there by the structure of the process, and the analyst's task is discovery. On the other face, the doctrine is an instrument of initiative: the identification is an act, performed by a subject, under uncertainty, with consequences that reshape the situation identified, since to treat a contradiction as principal is to pour resources into it and thereby to make it more nearly principal. The text leans on the first face when it speaks of laws and on the second when it speaks of method, and it nowhere supplies a theory of their junction. Critics have exploited the seam from both directions, determinists charging the doctrine with licensing voluntarism, the will dressed as analysis, and voluntarists of a later school charging it with a fatalism that waits upon structures. The author's own practice exhibited both faces in alternation, the patient investigator of the survey years and the gambler of the later campaigns, and the doctrine's elasticity, its capacity to underwrite patience and audacity in successive seasons, is plausibly connected to the seam.

The honest treatment of the tension is to present it as the doctrine's open problem rather than as a scandal. Every craft of situated judgement lives at the same junction: the clinician's diagnosis is both a discovery about the patient and an intervention that changes the patient's course, and the maturity of a craft is measured by whether its practitioners are trained to hold the two faces together, to act on a ranking while remembering that the ranking was theirs. The essays train the first half of that maturity superbly and the second half hardly at all, since the rhetoric of law and discovery, inherited from the textbooks, continually upstages the rhetoric of judgement and risk that the method requires. A reader who supplies the second rhetoric from the criticism the essays have attracted will possess the doctrine in its most defensible form: a ranking that is mine, argued from the situation, answerable to the results, and revisable when the configuration moves. Nothing in the text forbids that form. Much in the text's career was an evasion of it.

## The essays in the world's classrooms

The two texts have been assigned reading for three distinct populations, and each population has met a different book. In China, across the decades of the People's Republic, the essays formed part of the standard philosophical curriculum in party schools and universities, the canonical statement of dialectical materialism for generations of students who encountered the doctrine first as an examination subject, with the five propositions and the stages of knowledge set out for memorisation and commentary. The examination use rewarded exactly the catechism performance the grading test marks lowest, and Chinese philosophers of the reform era, revisiting the texts their education had made too familiar to read, have been among the most interesting of the essays' later interpreters, recovering the investigation ethic of the first essay as a resource against the dogmatism the curriculum itself had instilled. Abroad, in universities where modern Chinese thought or Marxist philosophy is taught, the essays are met as primary sources in translation, read beside Lenin's fragment and the Althusser essays, in seminars where the questions are those of this article: dependence and originality, description and procedure, the criterion problem. In the study circles of movements, the third population, the essays are met as manuals, in cheap editions, with the ranking doctrine extracted for application to local struggles and the rest of the apparatus honoured in summary.

The three receptions rarely inform one another, and a student moving between them can feel that three different texts are under discussion. The catechism text is a list. The seminar text is a problem. The manual text is a warrant. The argument of this article has been that all three are abridgements of a fourth text, the one written in 1937 and revised in the fifties, in which a method of investigation is joined to a doctrine of ranked contradictions, and that the abridgements can be corrected by returning to the whole. That is the use of a long article on two short essays: not to multiply commentary, but to restore the proportions the texts themselves observe, in which the disciplines of study occupy most of the pages and the famous identifications occupy few, and in which every identification is presented as the conclusion of an investigation rather than as a charm against the need for one.

## The doctrine in other hands: movements abroad

The export of the vocabulary beyond China produced a natural experiment in the procedure's behaviour under different institutions, and its results belong to the evidence. Insurgent movements in Asia, Latin America and elsewhere adopted the ranking doctrine as their method of analysis during the decades when Maoist parties formed an international current. In Peru, the movement that launched a people's war in 1980 framed its strategy in the doctrine's grammar, identifying the contradictions of Peruvian society and ranking them under a principal contradiction whose resolution its campaign was to force. In Nepal, the Maoist movement that fought a decade-long war from 1996 conducted its strategy in the same vocabulary, and its eventual turn to a negotiated settlement and competitive politics was argued, by its own leadership, as a re-ranking compelled by a changed configuration, the doctrine supplying the language in which a reversal of the first magnitude could be presented as continuity of method. The two cases stand at opposite poles of the doctrine's career abroad: in one, the ranking certified an escalation maintained long after the configuration that might have justified it had dissolved; in the other, the ranking legitimated a de-escalation that the movement's own doctrine of stages could plausibly describe. The grading test separates the cases more by the display of working than by the outcomes, which in both instances were mixed, and the experiment's lesson is the one this article has drawn from the Chinese record: the procedure amplifies the deliberative quality of the organisation that wields it, disciplined where the organisation argues its rankings in the open, dangerous where the ranking is the property of a centre that audits itself.

A quieter export deserves a sentence, because it reached further than the insurgencies. The vocabulary of principal and secondary contradictions passed, through the Althusserian corridor and through the general prestige of the Chinese example in the nineteen sixties and seventies, into the ordinary language of political analysis far beyond Marxist organisations: into journalism, into the strategy papers of parties of many colours, into the speech of activists who could not have named the essay the terms came from. Few philosophical coinages achieve that passage into unmarked common use. It testifies to the genuine vacancy the doctrine filled, the absence, in most political vocabularies, of a compact term for the governing conflict of a situation and for the obligation to subordinate the rest to it. The vacancy remains, and the term remains in occupation of it, long after the doctrine's official and insurgent careers have passed their peaks.

## What remains open

An assessment that ends with the open questions is more useful than one that ends with a verdict, and the essays leave four that continue to organise research and argument. The first is the criterion problem in its general form: whether any procedure for ranking the tensions of a complex situation can state, in advance, the conditions under which its ranking would count as mistaken, and whether the crafts of judgement that lack such a criterion are thereby disqualified or merely classified. The question reaches far beyond this text, into every discipline that must act on a reading of a whole, and the essay's value to the question is that it presents the problem in an unusually pure form, a procedure that knows it must rank and cannot prove its rankings. The second open question is textual: the relationship between the lectures, the notes and the published essays continues to be refined as editions improve, and the full scholarly digestion of the annotations, of what the reader of 1936 and 1937 argued with in his margins, is not complete. The third question is comparative: how the ranking procedure stands beside the other great instruments of situated judgement the century produced, in strategy, in statecraft and in the sciences of complexity, a comparison this article has sketched with the centre of gravity and with triage and which a fuller study could press much further. The fourth question is the reflexive one the essay invites and its custodians declined: what the principal contradiction of the doctrine's own situation now is, between its method and its authority, between the seminar and the catechism, and which aspect of the text will be principal in the next stage of its career. The essay's theory predicts that the exchange of aspects will continue. Its history gives no reason to doubt the prediction.

## The five propositions re-read

Return, at the end of the analysis, to the table where the exposition began, and read its rows with the whole argument in mind. The universality row survives the critics in the diminished but useful form this article has defended: not as a finding about the world that evidence could disturb, but as a standing instruction to expect tension and to distrust any report of a situation from which tension is absent. The particularity row survives intact and emerges as the essay's most durable teaching, the one whose violation explains the largest share of the doctrine's failures in other hands: every disaster of application in the record can be written as a skipped investigation, a transferred conclusion, a stage misread. The principal contradiction row survives as a procedure under the double verdict recorded in the criticism section, powerful as an allocation of attention and effort, unproven as a certification of its own outputs, and dependent for its honour on the displayed working of whoever invokes it. The identity and struggle row survives as the doctrine's theory of time, its guarantee that no settlement is final and no ranking permanent, the row that sends the analyst back to the field. The antagonism row survives under the heaviest qualification, since its classifications assign fates, and the record of its use obliges any honest restatement to attach the warning the 1957 text itself attached: that the boundary between the categories is a judgement, that mishandling moves cases across it, and that the classifier's power is the point of maximum danger in the entire apparatus.

Read in that order, the table also discloses the essay's own ranking of its propositions, the meta-lesson a student should not miss. The rows are not equally load-bearing. The system's centre of gravity sits in the middle row and in the second row that disciplines it, particularity and rank in their tension: rank without particularity is the licence, particularity without rank is the heap of impressions the first essay warned against, and the doctrine lives only where the two are practised together, the survey that earns the concentration and the concentration that justifies the survey. The other rows frame the pair, guaranteeing the object, supplying the dynamics, prescribing the handling. A tradition that had kept the middle pair in their tension would have had a sterner and a smaller doctrine than the one that history transmitted, in which the framing rows, the laws that could be recited, travelled more easily than the disciplines that had to be practised. The table, restored to its proportions, is this article's answer to the question of what in the essay deserves to survive: not the catechism of five propositions, but the working relation between the two at its centre.

## The doctrine as a theory of disagreement

One further application of the essays deserves record, because it governed the daily life of the movement that produced them and because it translates, with surprising ease, into settings far from Yan'an. The vocabulary of contradictions supplied a theory of disagreement: a way of classifying the disputes that arise inside any cooperative enterprise and of matching the manner of their handling to their kind. A disagreement over method among people who share an aim is a contradiction among the people, to be handled by argument, evidence and the patience of the unity, criticism, unity formula, in which the aim of criticism is a better unity rather than a victor. A disagreement that reveals an aim opposed to the enterprise itself is a contradiction of the other kind, to be handled by separation. Between the two stands the standing temptation the doctrine names and every organisation recognises: to reclassify a stubborn internal critic as an enemy, converting a disagreement that argument was losing into a disloyalty that discipline can settle. The 1957 text warns against the conversion explicitly, and the history of the movement is in part a record of the warning's observance and its breach.

The theory of disagreement is the point at which the essays touch the experience of readers who will never rank the contradictions of a society. Committees, faculties, firms and families all conduct the classification implicitly whenever they decide whether a dissenter is a colleague in error or an opponent in disguise, and the decision, as in the doctrine, settles the methods that follow: persuasion and inclusion in the one case, exclusion in the other. The essay's contribution to that universal predicament is the insistence that the classification is a judgement to be argued, from the evidence of the case, with the presumption set in favour of the milder category and the burden of proof on whoever would move a case across the boundary. That presumption, stated in the 1957 text and honoured unevenly since, is among the most humane sentences in the whole corpus, and it deserves to be quoted in paraphrase wherever the doctrine is taught: contradictions among the people are to be resolved by the democratic method, and only an analysis that has failed to persuade, applied to a case that has shown itself antagonistic in fact, may reach for the other register. Organisations that have internalised the presumption argue better and purge less. The doctrine cannot make them do it. It can only, as in everything else, display the standard by which their practice will be graded.

## The style as an argument

The essays' prose is an instrument of their doctrine, and a reading that ignores the style misses part of the teaching. The sentences are short by the standards of the tradition, the paragraphs proceed by assertion and illustration rather than by qualification, and the characteristic movement of the whole is a descent from the abstract term to the homely case and back: law, then egg; category, then pear; the ranking of the contradictions of a subcontinent, then the management of a meeting. The descent is the epistemology enacted at the level of composition. A text that argues knowledge must touch the ground at every stage writes sentences that touch the ground at every paragraph, and the famous plainness of the style, its freedom from the terminology in which the Soviet textbooks swam, is not a concession to the audience's limitations but a demonstration of the method: any proposition that cannot survive the descent to an example the audience can test has not yet completed the circuit the first essay prescribes.

The style has its dangers, which the reception made visible. Plainness lends an air of self-evidence to claims that are, at the metaphysical stations, anything but self-evident, and the procession of examples can simulate a proof where only an illustration has occurred. A reader carried by the descent may reach the bottom of the page feeling that the universality of contradiction has been established, when what has been established is the author's skill at finding instances. The rhetorical analysis therefore converges with the philosophical one at the same joint: the text is most persuasive exactly where its argument is thinnest, at the passage from the law to the case, and the vigilance the criticism section recommends toward the identification step applies equally to the reading step. The remedy is the one the text itself supplies. Read it as it teaches: take each proposition back into a practice of one's own, a situation one knows from inside, and watch where the descent to the case succeeds and where it strains. The strain points are the essay's true boundaries, and they differ instructively from the boundaries its critics announce from outside.

## Among the philosophers of praxis

The essays belong to a family of twentieth-century Marxist writings that share a conviction and differ in almost everything else: the conviction that philosophy is to be judged by its service to a practice that transforms the world. Antonio Gramsci, writing in a fascist prison in the same decade, built his philosophy of praxis around the unity of theory and practice and the figure of the intellectual formed inside a movement rather than imported into it. The parallels with the Yan'an lectures are structural and striking, two party intellectuals, unknown to each other, arguing in the same years that knowledge is made in engagement, that the separation of leaders who think from followers who do is a defect to be overcome, and that a doctrine's truth is bound up with its capacity to organise the understanding of those who act on it. The differences are equally instructive. Gramsci's central category, hegemony, directs attention to the slow construction of consent across a whole society, a war of position measured in decades. Mao's central category directs attention to the ranking of conflicts at a moment, a war of manoeuvre measured in campaigns. The Italian's instrument is a theory of cultural leadership; the Chinese commander's instrument is a procedure for decision. That the same conviction about practice produced instruments so different is itself a confirmation of the particularity proposition, since each instrument was shaped by the contradiction its author took to be principal: the stability of a bourgeois order that had survived its crisis, in the one case, and the fluidity of a social order dissolving under invasion, in the other.

Set beside Lenin as a philosopher, the essays' position is clearer still. Lenin's philosophical writings are interventions of a different temper: the combative materialism of his book against the empirio-critics, and the private, exploratory notebooks on Hegel, in which the ranking and concreteness that Mao would systematise appear as flashes in a reader's margin. Mao's essays take the flashes and the textbook system of the intervening decades and produce from them something Lenin never attempted, a teachable doctrine of method for an entire organisation. The production was an act of pedagogical statesmanship as much as of philosophy, and it should be assessed in both currencies. As philosophy, the essays systematise more than they discover, with the one great exception of the ranking doctrine. As pedagogy, they are among the most successful philosophical texts ever written, if success is measured by the number of practitioners who learned from them a way of approaching situations and by the durability of the vocabulary they installed. The two assessments are not rivals. They are the aspects of a single contradiction in the text's identity, and the exchange of their positions across the decades, pedagogy principal in the age of the movements, philosophy principal in the age of the seminars, is a further instance of the process the text describes.

## The criterion of practice under strain

The first essay's criterion of truth deserves a harder examination than it usually receives from either its devotees or its dismissers, because it is the load-bearing wall of the whole structure: the ranking doctrine's only audit, on the text's own account, is the verdict of the practice the ranking guides. The criterion's difficulty is familiar to philosophers of science in another dress. A test never examines a single idea alone. The agronomy that fails may convict the seed theory, or the soil survey, or the weather reading, or the organisation of the labour, and the failure itself does not say which. A campaign that collapses may convict the ranking of contradictions, or the estimate of forces, or the execution, or the adversary's counter-move, which the ranking had correctly anticipated and the command had mishandled. Practice returns a verdict on the whole intervention, and the intervention is a bundle. The essay's confidence that the verdict can be distributed among the bundle's parts, that the concept at fault can be identified and revised while the rest stand, is an act of methodological faith. It is the same faith every experimental discipline practises, and in the sciences it is underwritten by the possibility of isolating variables and repeating trials. In politics and war, isolation is rare and repetition rarer, and the audit the essay promises is correspondingly weaker than its rhetoric suggests.

The weakness does not void the criterion; it demotes it, from a tribunal to a discipline. A leadership that expects its concepts to be audited by results, that records its anticipations in advance and reviews them against outcomes, will learn faster than one that treats results as weather, even if the attribution of each result to its causes remains partly a judgement. The historical record contains both postures in the movement these essays served, seasons in which the review of failed anticipations was searching and public within the organisation, and seasons in which failure was assigned in advance to wreckers and the audit was a ritual. The criterion's fate in practice thus repeats the doctrine's general pattern: a discipline that works when its conditions, honesty of reporting and freedom to revise, are institutionally protected, and that degenerates into its opposite, a machinery for certifying the centre, when they are not. The essay states the criterion and not the conditions. Supplying the conditions is the work its readers, in every generation and setting, have had to do for themselves, and the unevenness of their success is the truest measure of the gap between the text and the world it hoped to govern.

### Can practice acquit a ranking that deserved to fail?

It can, and the possibility is the criterion's weakness: outcomes judge the intervention, not the ranking alone, so a fortunate result can certify a misidentification and an unlucky one can convict an identification, which is why the audit of practice must be supplemented by the audit of the working, the investigation by which a ranking is assessed before results arrive.

## Why these two essays became the canon

The author's writings fill volumes, and the movement he led produced mountains of documents, yet the philosophical reputation of the whole enterprise came to rest on two lectures from a single summer. The canonisation was not accidental, and its causes are worth identifying because they shaped what later generations took the philosophy to be. The essays were short, in a corpus whose characteristic documents were long. They were systematic, in a corpus dominated by the occasional and the strategic. They were early, composed before the victory that made their author a head of state, and so could be read as the movement's mind in its heroic age. And they were, by the standards of the tradition, unusually complete in themselves: a theory of knowledge and a theory of situations, bound in a single course, requiring no other text to be intelligible. A movement selecting a philosophy to teach would have chosen these from the shelf by the same criteria a publisher would, and the party's editors, assembling the Selected Works in the early nineteen fifties, placed the two essays in the first volume with the prominence that fixed their status for the decades of the curriculum.

Canonisation has the usual double effect. It preserves, and it freezes. The essays were preserved through every subsequent turn of the doctrine's fortune, reprinted, translated and taught when other texts of their author were being revised or withdrawn, and their preservation carried the investigation ethic and the ranking doctrine intact into settings their author never imagined. It also froze the philosophy at its 1937 station, so that the tradition's philosophical identity remained anchored to two lectures while the author's own later writings, on the handling of contradictions among the people, on the continuation of revolution, on the dialectics of the socialist period, pushed into territory the lectures had not mapped and sometimes against the lectures' own emphases. Readers who know the philosophy only from the canon meet a tidier thinker than the record contains. The untidiness matters for assessment, because some of the charges laid against the essays, of rigidity, of a confidence the situations did not warrant, belong more properly to the frozen canon and its custodians than to the working texts of the summer school, which were written in a hurry, for a war, by a man who revised them when he could and whose whole doctrine instructed his readers to revise everything else. The canon is the principal aspect of the essays' public identity. The working texts are the aspect that scholarship keeps strengthening, and the character of the whole, on the essay's own theory, continues to be decided by their struggle.

## The essays and the making of a Chinese Marxism

A year after the lectures, at the Sixth Plenary Session of the Central Committee in 1938, Mao called for the sinification of Marxism, for the doctrine to be given a Chinese form, stamped with the characteristics of the nation and carried in a style the common people could understand. The essays of the previous summer were the programme's first fruits and its standing exhibit. Their claim to that status does not rest on any doctrine of national essence, and the texts themselves are innocent of one: the framework is the international doctrine of the textbooks, the authorities are Marx, Engels and Lenin, and the examples move freely between China and the world. The sinification visible in the essays is of a different and more defensible kind. It consists in the selection of problems by a Chinese agenda, the subordination of the inherited system to the needs of a Chinese headquarters, the illustration of every law from Chinese materials, and the installation, at the centre of the system, of a procedure whose whole point is that no identification earned elsewhere can be imported. A doctrine that forbids the import of conclusions is, by that prohibition, nationalised in its operation wherever it is practised, since every application must be produced on the spot from local material. The essays are thus the least exportable of texts in their method and the most exported in their vocabulary, a paradox their career illustrates at every station.

The larger series to which this article belongs treats the Chinese career of the doctrine in the round, in the study of [Marxism in China: The Complete Guide](/2018/05/01/marxism-in-china-complete-guide/), and the present article's narrower claim can be left in a single sentence: the essays of 1937 are the philosophical charter of that career, the point at which an imported system was re-founded as a method for producing local knowledge, and every subsequent debate about the doctrine's identity, orthodox or heterodox, faithful or revisionist, is a debate about whether a given use honoured the charter's single non-negotiable clause, that the ranking be earned by investigation in the situation where it is to be spent.

## A short shelf: the texts around the essays

A reader who wants to go further has a well-marked shelf. Begin with the essays themselves in the first volume of the Selected Works, in the standard English translation, and read the speech of 1957 on contradictions among the people as their sequel. Place beside them Lenin's fragment On the Question of Dialectics, a few pages that show the programme the essays executed. For the sources and the strata, Nick Knight's edition of the writings on philosophy of 1937, with its translations of the lecture notes and the marginalia, is the indispensable instrument, and his later studies of Marxist philosophy in China supply the field in which the essays were one product among several. For the life and the intellectual formation, the biographies and studies associated with Stuart Schram remain the standard point of entry, and John Bryan Starr's analysis of the political thought traces the doctrine through the policy record with the documents in view. For the French corridor, Althusser's For Marx, in Ben Brewster's translation, contains the two essays in which the encounter with On Contradiction is worked out, and Balibar's later recollections supply the human setting of the encounter. For the critics, Popper's Conjectures and Refutations states the falsifiability criterion in its author's own terms, and the third volume of Kołakowski's Main Currents of Marxism gives the case against the dialectical laws its most considered form. A reader who works through even half the shelf will have met every party to the argument this article has staged, in their own words and at their own length, which is where an assessment should finally be conducted.

## Stages: the doctrine's calendar

The stage concept, which the ranking doctrine presupposes, deserves a section of its own, because it is where the essay's philosophy of history is decided. A process, on the essay's account, does not develop by smooth accumulation but by stages, within each of which a definite configuration of contradictions holds, and between which the configuration transforms, sometimes gradually and sometimes, at the moment the principal aspect changes places with its opposite, all at once. The analyst's periodisation is therefore not a convenience of exposition but a claim about the object: to say that a process has entered a new stage is to say that its contradictions have been re-ranked and its character altered, and the claim is as consequential, and as contestable, as identifying the principal contradiction itself. The two claims are in fact one claim stated twice, since a stage's boundary is nothing but the moment of re-ranking, and a doctrine that cannot certify its rankings cannot certify its calendar either. The concession of the criticism section extends to the calendar without remainder.

What the stage doctrine gives the practitioner, in exchange for its uncertainty, is a discipline of expectations. Within a stage, the configuration is stable enough to plan against; the alliances it permits and the methods it prescribes can be relied on for the stage's duration. At the boundary, everything must be re-earned: the survey repeated, the ranking re-argued, the alliances re-examined for the partners whose position the transformation has inverted. The costly errors in the doctrine's career cluster at the boundaries, and they are of the two kinds the doctrine predicts: the stage declared over before its configuration had transformed, licensing a confrontation the situation could not sustain, and the stage clung to after its transformation, licensing an alliance with a partner whose aspect had already changed. The essay cannot protect its users against either error, since both are errors of the uncertified judgement at its centre. It can and does name them, in its vocabulary of premature and belated action, of adventurism and tailism, and a vocabulary that names the characteristic errors of its own application has done more for its users than a criterion it does not possess could have done. The names are tripwires. They do not stop the fall, but they mark the edge, and generations of practitioners learned to feel for them in the dark.

## The essay's silence about itself

A last observation concerns what the essays never discuss: their own status as texts within the process they describe. The doctrine holds that every unity is provisional, every aspect liable to exchange, every formulation stage-bound. The essays, as promulgated, exempt themselves. They are presented in the curriculum as settled doctrine, their propositions as laws, their identifications of method as final, and the machinery of revision that the first essay prescribes for every other concept is not applied, institutionally, to the concepts of the essays themselves. The exemption was the author's to grant and his successors' to maintain, and it stands in visible tension with the spiral of knowledge the first essay describes, in which no concept is finished and every truth is the truth of a stage. A reader formed by the essays will notice the exemption without prompting, and will draw the conclusion the texts make available: that the essays, taken at their word, demand to be read as the philosophy of a stage, powerful within it, corrigible beyond it, and that the most faithful reading of Mao's doctrine of the principal contradiction may be the one that treats the doctrine itself as a contradiction whose aspects, method and authority, continue their struggle in every new setting where the vocabulary is taken up. The text cannot perform that reading on itself. Its readers, in the seminar and in the study group, have been performing it for decades, and the performance is the essay's afterlife at its most honourable: the procedure turned, at last, on the text that taught it.

## The first circulation: print, classrooms and the war years

Between the lectures and the canon stands a decade of wartime circulation that shaped what the texts could mean to their earliest readers. The longer lecture draft, the notes on dialectical materialism from which the two essays were quarried, was printed in full within a year of delivery and appeared again in fragments across the following years, in journals and in editions issued far from Yan'an, while the essays themselves travelled in the baggage of the party's expanding school network. A text encountered in that form, as a duplicated course book in a base-area classroom or as a journal fragment in an occupied city, met readers for whom its doctrines were not examination material but equipment for decisions being taken under bombardment. The audience's circumstances did interpretive work no editor could control. Passages on the ranking of conflicts were read against the week's actual dilemma, whether to confront a rival force or to preserve it for the common resistance, and the reading was tested, in the manner the first essay prescribes, by what happened next in the county where the reader sat.

The circulation also fixed a habit of excerpting that the texts have never escaped. Course books and study outlines lifted the portable elements, the definitions, the fivefold scheme, the stages of knowledge, and left the connective argument behind, so that for many wartime readers the philosophy arrived as a set of detachable propositions before it arrived as a book. The habit had a long future. It is the ancestor of the catechism use this article has graded lowest, and it began not as a degeneration imposed by later bureaucrats but as a practical response to scarcity, of paper, of time, of teachers, in a movement schooling itself while fighting. Understanding that origin tempers the judgement without reversing it. Excerpting under fire is forgivable. What the grading standard condemns is the survival of the excerpt habit into conditions of leisure, where the whole text sits on the shelf and the summary is still preferred, because the summary can be recited and the whole must be understood.

A second feature of the war years deserves emphasis, since it complicates the picture of a doctrine imposed from a centre. The base areas were numerous, communications among them were slow and often cut, and the schools that taught the essays operated with wide local latitude. Surviving syllabi and memoirs show the same texts being glossed differently in different places, applied to land policy in one classroom and to army discipline in another, argued over in study groups whose conclusions no headquarters reviewed. For a season, in other words, the procedure circulated the way its own epistemology recommends, as an instrument taken up by many hands and corrected in many local practices, with the centre's authority present more as prestige than as supervision. The tightening came later, with victory, with the move to the capital, with the editorial work that produced the canonical versions and the curriculum that fixed their reading. The doctrine's biography thus runs from dispersion to consolidation, and its admirers and critics alike tend to mistake the consolidated form for the original one. The lectures were born in the dispersed phase, addressed to a movement that still had to persuade itself county by county, and something of that persuasion survives in their rhetoric, in the patience of their examples and the absence, notable in texts of their later reputation, of any appeal to the author's authority as a reason for belief. The argument is made to stand on the survey and the example. Where it cannot, the text falls silent and moves on, a reticence its canonisers did not always imitate.

## Reading the essays in translation

Most of the world reads these texts in translation, and the translations are not a transparent window. The standard English versions, prepared in Beijing for the Selected Works and issued by the Foreign Languages Press, render the key term as principal contradiction, where the Chinese speaks of the main or chief contradiction, and the choice among renderings is not innocent. Principal suggests rank within an order, a first among peers in a settled hierarchy. Main suggests weight, the load-bearing member of a structure. The essay's functional definition supports the second image more readily than the first, since what qualifies a conflict for the title is the dependence of the others upon it, and a reader working from the English should keep the structural image in mind against the hierarchical one the adjective smuggles in. Older editions compound the distance with the Wade-Giles spellings of their period, in which the author's name and the place names wear a vanished orthography, and a student collating editions across the decades needs the equivalents at hand to avoid mistaking a change of spelling for a change of doctrine.

The French version through which Althusser read the essay had its own history, printed in a party journal as an act of solidarity and read, a decade later, as a philosophical event, and the episode generalises into a caution about all the texts' journeys. A translation is itself an identification performed by other hands: the translator decides which of the original's tensions is governing, in passages where the original sustains two renderings, and the decision propagates silently into every argument built on the translated page. Scholars who work on the essays' reception therefore treat the translations as part of the evidence, dating them, naming their sponsors, and checking the load-bearing sentences against the Chinese where an argument turns on a word. The caution is the doctrine's own discipline applied to its transmitters, one more instance of the general rule these pages have enforced: wherever a claim arrives with authority, ask to see the working, in whatever language the working was done.

A second rendering deserves the same scrutiny. The word translated as practice in the first essay's title carries, in the Chinese, a stronger flavour of enactment than the English word suggests, closer to putting into effect than to rehearsing, and the essay's argument leans on that flavour at its decisive points. Knowledge is to be carried back not into activity in general but into the deliberate transformation of the material the knowledge concerns, and a reader who hears practice as mere experience, as time spent in the vicinity of a subject, will find the essay's severity about book learning harder to follow than a reader who hears it as intervention with a stake in the outcome. The two titles together encode the pairing this article has stressed throughout: one essay about the world as a field of ranked tensions, one about the knower as an agent inside that field, each unintelligible at full strength without the other. Translation, which must choose renderings one word at a time, cannot hold the pairing in view the way an argument can, and the student who reads the essays in any language does well to restore it deliberately, asking of each doctrine in the second essay what act it instructs, and of each stage in the first what tension it presupposes. The exercise costs little and returns the texts from the shelf of systems to the workbench of methods, which is where their author, whatever else is said of him, built them to be used.

## A doctrine for a world of scarce attention

Strip the vocabulary of its period dress and the procedure answers a problem that no organisation escapes: attention is the scarcest resource any collective possesses, and it is allocated well or badly whether or not the allocation is ever made explicit. A headquarters, a ministry, a firm, a newsroom, each confronts a field of simultaneous tensions that exceeds its capacity to act, and each drifts, by default, toward the tensions that are loudest, nearest or most flattering to its habits. The ranking doctrine is, in this light, an early and unusually explicit theory of agenda-setting. It insists that the allocation be argued rather than drifted into, that the argument proceed from a survey of the whole field rather than from the volume of its parts, and that the winner of the argument receive not a plurality of the organisation's energy but its concentration, with the losers consciously managed at a lower intensity rather than unconsciously neglected. Modern management literature rediscovered fragments of this discipline under other names, in the language of priorities, of the vital few, of strategy as the choice of what not to do. The Yan'an version remains more demanding than most of its descendants, because it attaches two obligations the modern versions drop: the obligation to re-argue the ranking when the field changes, and the obligation to remember the postponed items as live claims rather than as settled discards.

The attention reading also clarifies why the doctrine's failures take the form they do. An agenda argued in the open can be corrected by the same process that set it. An agenda announced as a discovery about the world can be corrected only by a counter-discovery, which subordinates are rarely positioned to supply. The slide from the first form to the second is the slide this article has tracked under several names, from method to licence, from investigation to catechism, and the attention reading gives the slide a mechanism: whoever frames the field's governing tension frames every subsequent decision, so the framing step attracts exactly the political pressure that a merely analytical step would escape. Organisations that understand this protect the framing step procedurally, by rotating its performers, by publishing the rival framings considered, by scheduling its revision in advance. The movement that produced the doctrine protected it hardly at all, relying on the investigator's virtue and the audit of outcomes, and the doctrine's history is in large part the record of what an unprotected framing step undergoes across decades of use by the powerful.

There is a corollary for readers in quieter settings. The procedure scales down as faithfully as it scales up. A scholar with a field of open problems, a clinician with a ward of competing needs, an editor with a book of possible stories, each performs the ranking whether or not the performance is acknowledged, and each is served by the doctrine's central demand in its modest form: name the governing problem, say aloud what has been subordinated to it, and fix the event that would entitle a subordinated problem to promotion. The demand sounds administrative. Its neglect is the commonest cause of the drift by which capable people and institutions spend their strength on the second problem while the first one ripens unattended, and the essay of 1937, whatever its metaphysical liabilities, remains one of the sharpest instruments ever written for refusing that drift.

## What a criterion would have required

The concession recorded in the criticism section can be made more precise by asking what a criterion for the ranking would actually look like, if a tradition had set out to build one. Three components suggest themselves, each familiar from disciplines that did build them. The first is registration in advance: the ranking, the evidence for it and the developments that would overturn it committed to record before action begins, so that later revisions can be distinguished from later rationalisations. The second is a measure of dependence that does not depend on the ranker's say-so: some observable trace of the governing relation, the way a fever chart traces an infection, by which a conflict's grip on the others could be compared across cases. The third is a stock of settled cases, rankings whose outcomes are agreed, against which a new ranking can be benchmarked and a ranker's track record compiled. Medicine built all three, slowly, out of case records and professional discipline. Strategy built partial versions, in staff colleges and after-action reviews. The doctrine's tradition built none of them systematically. Its cases were celebrated or condemned as wholes, its rankings were registered in resolutions that recorded conclusions rather than reasoning, and dependence was asserted in the vocabulary of the doctrine itself, so that the measure and the thing measured shared a language and could not check each other.

The reasons for the omission are not mysterious, and naming them completes the assessment more fairly than another round of reproach. A criterion binds the hands of whoever ranks, and the ranking in this tradition was the prerogative of a leadership that experienced binding as a cost rather than as a protection. Registration in advance creates a record against which the leadership can be audited by its own followers, and the institution preferred its audits retrospective and narrative. A stock of settled cases requires agreement about outcomes, and outcomes in politics are the very thing under dispute. Each component of a criterion, in short, presupposes a distribution of authority that the doctrine's home institution did not practise, in which the ranker is one party among others and the record belongs to all. The absence of a criterion is therefore not an oversight in the text that a cleverer author would have repaired. It is the signature of the procedure's birthplace, a headquarters at war, where speed and unity of command outranked corrigibility, and where the audit of outcomes was entrusted to the starkest of tribunals, survival. That the procedure outlived its birthplace, and was asked to govern in settings where survival no longer audited anything, is the circumstance that turned a wartime expedient's missing safeguard into a standing invitation to abuse.

A reader who grasps this will also grasp the limit of the criticism. Demanding that the essay supply the criterion is demanding that a text of 1937 invent, inside a revolutionary headquarters, the institutional culture of a self-auditing profession. The demand is not absurd, since the text's own epistemology gestures toward it, but it is a demand on the tradition that carried the text rather than on the text itself. The essay can be faulted for the confidence of its legal rhetoric, which disguised a judgement as a law and thereby discouraged the building of the safeguards judgements need. It cannot fairly be faulted for the safeguards' absence in institutions that had every incentive to leave them unbuilt. The grading standard used throughout these pages distributes the praise and the blame accordingly: the method is the text's, the licence is the tradition's, and the distance between them is the measure of what the text's readers, in every setting since, have owed it and failed, as often as not, to pay.

One practical consequence follows for anyone examined on these essays, and it is worth stating before the persona sections take up their separate audiences. An answer that recites the five propositions in order, however accurately, demonstrates acquaintance with the catechism and nothing more, and examiners who know the literature mark it accordingly. An answer that earns its grade does three further things. It shows the propositions at work on a single concrete situation, tracing how a ranking is reached and what would dislodge it. It keeps the two essays in conversation, letting the epistemology discipline the metaphysics at the points where the metaphysics grows confident. And it registers the objection that shadows the whole construction, in the strong form given it by the critics named in these pages, rather than in a weakened version arranged for easy rebuttal. None of this requires agreement with the doctrine, and the strongest answers in the record include several that conclude against it. What they share is the refusal to let the text be smaller than it is, neither the relic its detractors describe nor the oracle its custodians proclaim, but an argued instrument whose powers and whose missing safeguard can both be exhibited on the page.

## For students and exam candidates

Treat the two essays as a pair and learn them in the order they were delivered: the theory of knowledge first, then the theory of contradiction. The examinable core is the five propositions table in this article, and the highest-return preparation is to be able to reproduce each proposition with its operation and its objection, since questions on this material reward the candidate who can state the criticism as precisely as the doctrine. Separate existence from rank in every answer: that a situation contains contradictions is the guaranteed claim, and the claim that one of them is principal is the risky one that must be argued from evidence. Keep the five propositions, the congress restatements and the Althusser connection in a structured note you can revise from, using the [Marxism study notes on VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) to hold the table and the key distinctions in one place. Practise the grading test on past identifications: state the ranking, name what it outranked, and say what would reverse it. An answer built in that shape demonstrates the method instead of merely reciting it.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The principal contradiction is an unusually clear example of a theoretical concept with immediate practical consequences, and it teaches well because students can run the procedure themselves within a single session. Set the two essays a week apart, On Practice first, and open the second seminar by asking the room to rank the contradictions of a situation every member knows, a university, a city, a workplace, before the doctrine is named; the disagreements the exercise produces are the seminar's real text, since they exhibit the identification step and its missing criterion more vividly than any commentary. Then read the essay's own worked example, the analysis of Chinese society in the nineteen thirties, against the exercise, and ask where the displayed working in the text exceeds the working the room produced. Close with the critics: Popper's criterion and the concession it extracts make a better final hour than any summary, because they leave students with the doctrine's strength and its limit in a single grip. Guard the room against the catechism use, in which the vocabulary is recited as a charm, by grading every application on its displayed investigation.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

The research frontier on these texts is documentary, and the first discipline is version control: cite the stratum you mean, lecture notes, wartime printings or the revised Selected Works text, and treat any argument that slides between strata as unsound until the variants are checked. Knight's edition of the 1937 writings, with the marginalia to the Soviet textbooks, is the control instrument for questions of dependence and originality, and the annotations repay direct study as evidence of a reading in progress. For the doctrine's institutional career, the congress documents of 1956, 1981 and 2017 supply the primary record of the restatements, and they should be quoted as formulations, with their occasions, rather than paraphrased into a general claim about doctrine. Journalists covering the vocabulary's appearances in official language serve their readers best by reporting the identification as a judgement with a history, naming the formulation it replaced and the occasion of the replacement, instead of treating the term as a transparent description of the situation it names. The Althusser corridor, finally, is the live connection to European theory, and researchers who follow it in both directions will find the transmission still generating questions neither side has finished with.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is On Contradiction about?

It is the longer of the two philosophical essays Mao delivered as lectures at Yan'an in 1937 and afterwards reworked for his Selected Works. The essay builds a single argument in stages. Conflict is present inside every process; no two conflicts are alike, so each must be studied on its own ground; where many conflicts coexist, a single one of them governs the remainder; inside any one conflict the two poles are unequal, and the stronger pole fixes the character of the whole; the poles depend on each other yet never stop contending; and some conflicts are settled by adjustment while others end only when one side prevails. The ranking claim at the centre is what the present article calls a decision procedure.

### Q: What is On Practice about?

The shorter essay, delivered a month before its companion, asks how knowledge is produced and how it is checked. Its answer runs in a circuit. People learn by intervening in the world, and the impressions gathered through intervention are at first scattered and superficial. Worked over by thought, those impressions condense into concepts that seize the inner relations of a thing. Concepts then have to be carried back into action, where they stand or fall by the results they forecast. The circuit repeats, each turn correcting the last. Against this standard the essay indicts two failures: the reader who trusts books in place of inquiry, and the practitioner who hoards experience and refuses to generalise from it.

### Q: How does Mao define the principal contradiction?

He defines it by the work it does inside a process. A complicated process holds a crowd of conflicts, and the principal one is the conflict on which the others depend: settle it, and the prospects of every lesser conflict alter; leave it untouched, and their movement stays bounded by it. Two consequences follow from defining the thing by its function. The title can pass from one conflict to another as the process changes stage, because dependence is a relation, not a property. And the title cannot be claimed by loudness or by moral urgency, since a conflict may dominate attention while governing very little. Only a survey of how the conflicts condition each other can award it.

### Q: What is the difference between antagonistic and non-antagonistic contradictions?

The difference lies in how the opposition can end. Where the two sides rest on irreconcilable foundations, the essay calls the contradiction antagonistic: it closes through the defeat of one side, as in the opposition it draws between labour and capital. Where the sides share an underlying interest and differ over means, timing or understanding, the contradiction is non-antagonistic: it closes through argument, criticism and mutual adjustment. Because the label prescribes the handling, confrontation for the first kind and persuasion for the second, the act of labelling carries practical force. A later text of 1957 rebuilt the distinction as one between the people and their enemies on one side and disagreements within the people on the other.

### Q: Did Mao take these ideas from Soviet textbooks?

The scaffolding did, and the proof sits in his own margins. In the months running up to the lectures he worked through Chinese versions of the standard Soviet manuals of dialectical materialism, among them the exposition associated with Mark Mitin's school, covering the pages with thousands of characters of comment that editors have since published, Nick Knight's edition being the fullest. His lecture drafts track those manuals for long stretches. The addition that made the essays consequential, the worked-out procedure for ranking a process's conflicts under a governing one, appears in no manual he is known to have used. Dependence in the frame, invention at the centre: that is the proportion the documents support.

### Q: Were the texts revised after they were written?

Both were, by their author, before they entered the Selected Works at the start of the nineteen fifties. The lectures of 1937 survive in draft notes and in wartime printings, and collating them against the canonical versions shows a consistent pattern of editing: stretches that had leaned closely on the Soviet manuals were cut or recast, allusions to the factional quarrels of the lecture hall were dropped, and loose teaching formulations were tightened into doctrine. Some changes altered emphasis enough to matter to interpretation. Scholars therefore treat the two strata as distinct witnesses and insist that an argument about the Yan'an period cannot rest on sentences polished fifteen years afterwards without checking what stood in their place.

### Q: How did Althusser use On Contradiction?

He made it the chief exhibit in his campaign to reread Marx without Hegel. The French translation had appeared in his party's theoretical journal a decade before he wrote Contradiction and Overdetermination in 1962, the essay later gathered into For Marx, and in it he credited the Chinese text openly. What he seized on was the picture of a whole made of many unequal conflicts, one dominant among them, none reducible to a single essence expressing itself everywhere. Borrowing a word from Freud, he named that condition overdetermination and built from it an account of social formations in which economy, politics and ideology keep their own tempos under a structure of dominance. A lecture text from Yan'an thereby entered the foundations of French structural Marxism.

### Q: Is contradiction theory falsifiable?

Measured against the criterion Karl Popper proposed, the universal claims are not, and the concession should be made cleanly. A scheme that finds conflicts everywhere, permits the governing conflict to exchange places with a lesser one, and allows a classification to be revised after the event can accommodate any outcome, and Popper's verdict on such schemes is that a theory no conceivable event could refute fails as science. The defence available to the essay is of a different order: it offers a discipline for forming judgements, with a prescribed survey, a defined object, and outcomes as a delayed audit, rather than a set of predictions. What it nowhere offers is a test that could convict a particular ranking of error at the moment the ranking is announced.

### Q: Is principal contradiction still official doctrine?

The term holds a place in the party's formal language, and the occasions on which its content was reset are matters of public record. A party congress in 1956 fixed it as the gap between what the population required by way of economic and cultural advance and what the country could then supply. A resolution of 1981 recast it as popular material and cultural requirements outrunning a backward productive base. The congress of 2017 announced a further change, setting the population's rising expectations of a good life against development described as uneven and insufficient. Those three promulgations, each attached to a turn in policy, are the documented career of the term inside the institution that coined its use.

### Q: In which order should you read On Practice and On Contradiction?

Take them in the sequence in which they were composed, the epistemology before the ontology. The practice essay establishes the rule that a concept earns its standing only through inquiry and trial in the situation it describes. A reader carrying that rule into the second essay will grasp why its central doctrine is an instruction to investigate rather than a licence to pronounce, and will notice at once when later users skip the inquiry the rule demands. Reversing the order leaves the ranking apparatus looking like a piece of speculative machinery. For a first reading the canonical Selected Works versions serve best; for arguments about origins, the lecture drafts printed in Knight's edition are the necessary control.

### Q: How does the decision procedure grade an application of the text?

It grades the reasoning on show, not the luck of the outcome. A strong application lays out the field first: the conflicts present in the situation, the grounds for subordinating most of them, the evidence that one of them governs the rest, and the development that would entitle a different one to the governing place. The wartime ranking behind the united front earns its standing that way, since the reasoning was published and argued before it was acted on. A weak application presents the governing conflict as a settled formula and moves directly to the policy it is meant to authorise. Between the two lies the whole distance this article has marked, between a method that can be checked and a vocabulary that can only be repeated.

### Q: What should a student separate between a contradiction's existence and its rank?

Two claims that the vocabulary makes easy to blur. That conflicts exist in a situation is the cheap claim: the theory promises them in advance, so announcing them adds nothing and risks nothing. Which conflict governs the others is the expensive claim: it can be mistaken, it commits whoever acts on it, and it stands in need of the survey that alone can support it. The student who keeps the price tags attached will read the doctrine's history more shrewdly, since its abuses almost always consist in borrowing the certainty of the first claim to decorate the second. In an examination answer the same separation earns credit, because it shows the marker a candidate who knows where the argument's weight actually rests.

### Q: Why does the principal contradiction change between stages?

Because rank follows the configuration, and the configuration is what a stage is. Within a stage the balance among a process's conflicts is steady enough to plan against. Across a boundary the balance gives way: a subordinate conflict gathers force, an outside shock rearranges the field, the weaker pole of the governing conflict grows until it trades places with the stronger. At that point the conflict that had set the terms for the others no longer does, and clinging to its title turns a once-accurate ranking into a source of error. The essay's own illustrations come from the war years, when invasion promoted the national conflict over the internal ones and diplomacy or defeat could demote it again.

### Q: How does the five propositions table order the argument?

The table follows the essay's own descent from the widest claim to the most practical one. Its opening row promises that no process will be found free of internal conflict. The next row qualifies the promise by insisting that every conflict is a special case requiring its own study. The middle row performs the selection among conflicts, awarding the governing place to one of them. The fourth row describes the traffic between the poles of a conflict, their mutual dependence and their unceasing contest. The last row sorts conflicts by the manner of their ending and so by the treatment each is owed. Arranged that way, the table also shows where the essay's weight falls: the selective middle row, disciplined by the investigative row above it.

### Q: What separates a method of investigation from a licence for any conclusion?

Visibility of the steps. A method leaves its steps where others can inspect them: the alternatives weighed, the grounds for the order chosen, the circumstance that would change the order. Anyone retracing the steps can find the point at which they would have branched differently, and the conclusion stays corrigible for exactly that reason. A licence hides the steps and exhibits only the destination, borrowing the method's vocabulary to give a decision taken elsewhere the complexion of a discovery. The texts themselves draw the line, since the first essay denies a hearing to whoever has not investigated, and a ranking announced without its investigation is, by that standard, speech without the inquiry that entitles it.
