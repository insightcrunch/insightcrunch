Marxist feminism is examined across sociology, politics, and gender studies papers more reliably than almost any other theoretical perspective, and it is marked in a way candidates consistently misunderstand. Assessment on this topic rewards one skill above all others: the ability to state what distinguishes the traditions from each other in terms of the system each treats as primary. It penalises, with equal consistency, the answer that ranks oppressions, placing class above gender or gender above class as though the perspectives were entries in a league of harms. A marker reading the ranking formulation learns within two sentences that the candidate has absorbed a slogan about the position rather than the position itself, and the script is graded accordingly for the remaining pages. Everything in this guide follows from that asymmetry. The distinction between a primacy claim and a hierarchy of suffering is the single most valuable sentence pattern available on this topic, and the candidate who builds an opening around it has already separated the script from the majority of the cohort.

The second reward is smaller but nearly as reliable. Markers credit reading that runs past the standard revision resources, and on feminist perspectives those resources largely stop with the debates of several decades ago, as though the tradition had been wound up once the domestic labour argument had run its course. It had not. The position exists in a developed contemporary form, social reproduction theory, which restates the older claims about unpaid work with greater precision and answers several objections that the older form invited. Naming that form, accurately and in its strongest version, performs two services in a single sentence: it demonstrates currency, and it supplies the evaluation of the earlier literature that the top band asks for. This guide calls that manoeuvre the current-form move, and treats it as the highest-return single addition a candidate can make to any answer in this field.

This guide teaches the writing, not the doctrine. The doctrine itself, the arguments in full and the history behind them, is set out in the series article on the [marxist feminism complete guide](/2016/09/01/marxist-feminism-complete-guide/), and each component topic has its own treatment, linked where a candidate needs depth rather than technique. What follows is organised around the question types examiners actually set on feminist perspectives, gathered into the feminism answer matrix, and around the small set of errors that cost the most marks year after year. General essay technique, the transferable kind, belongs to its own guide and is not repeated here; the wider sociology paper, where feminist perspectives appear as one theory among several, has its own treatment as well. The territory of this guide is narrower and more valuable: the formulations that survive a knowledgeable marker, the theorists worth naming and the claims worth attaching to them, and the discipline of evaluating through named critics rather than through unattributed complaint.

## What the assessment rewards on feminist perspectives

Examination bands, across institutions and levels, describe operations rather than opinions. The lower bands describe identification and narration: the candidate can say what a perspective holds and can retell its history in order. The middle bands describe explanation: the candidate can show how a claim is built, which premises carry it, and where its evidence sits. The upper bands describe evaluation performed on material that has been stated correctly, by a candidate who can also state the strongest version of the position being evaluated. Feminist perspectives are an unusually pure test of this ladder, because the material is a set of rival frameworks rather than a body of agreed findings. There is no neutral summary to hide behind. Every sentence of exposition already commits the candidate to a characterisation of some tradition, and a marker who knows the literature can tell, from the characterisation alone, whether the candidate has read the tradition or a caricature of it.

That is why exposition carries unusual weight here. On a topic where the facts are settled, a shaky opening can be recovered by later accuracy. On this topic the opening is the accuracy. The candidate who writes that Marxist feminists subordinate women's interests to the class struggle has not made a weak start to a good answer; the candidate has stated a different position from the one named, and everything built on the statement inherits the error. Markers are trained to notice precisely this, because examination boards return to it in their guidance: the perspectives differ in their account of which structure generates the pattern, not in their estimate of how much the pattern matters. A script that grasps the point writes differently from its first line, defining each tradition by the system it treats as foundational and only then turning to what follows from the foundation.

### What does a marker learn from the opening sentence of a feminism answer?

A great deal, and quickly. An opening that defines the perspective by its primary system tells the marker the candidate understands the field's architecture. An opening that announces which oppression is worse tells the marker the candidate has memorised a ranking the traditions themselves reject. First sentences set the ceiling for everything that follows them in the script.

The penalty structure deserves equal attention, because candidates usually imagine the risks wrongly. The danger on this topic is not controversy. Scripts are not marked down for concluding against a perspective, and a later section of this guide takes up that question in full, since it is the one candidates ask most anxiously. The danger is imprecision wearing the costume of confidence: the confident misattribution, the theorist named without a claim, the criticism voiced without an author. Each of these tells the marker that the candidate's knowledge is secondhand. Secondhand knowledge is not worthless, and it earns the middle bands honestly. But the distance between the middle and the top of the mark range on feminist perspectives is almost exactly the distance between knowing that criticisms exist and being able to say who made them, against which version of the position, and with what force.

### Why is unattributed criticism worth so little on this topic?

Because evaluation in the top band is a transaction between named positions. A criticism without an author cannot be weighed, since the marker cannot tell whether it strikes the strong form of a theory or a weak summary of it. Attaching a critic and a specific argument converts complaint into scholarship, and markers reward the conversion directly.

There is also a matter of fairness internal to the assessment, and candidates who understand it write better evaluations. A perspective stated in its strong form and then criticised is a perspective the candidate has demonstrably understood; the criticism lands on something real. A perspective stated weakly and then criticised proves nothing except that weak statements are easy to defeat, and markers are alive to the difference. The discipline this guide teaches, stating each position in its strongest present form before any verdict is attempted, is therefore not politeness toward the material. It is the mechanism by which evaluation becomes creditable at all. The phrase present form matters as much as the word strongest. Traditions develop. The Marxist feminism of the domestic labour debate is not identical to the social reproduction theory that succeeded it, and an evaluation aimed at the earlier form, delivered as though nothing had been written since, dates the script as surely as a superseded statistic would.

## The distinction that carries the marks

Every field has a sentence or two on which the marking quietly turns, and on feminist perspectives the sentence concerns primacy. Radical feminism, in its classic statements, treats male domination as a system in its own right, historically prior to class society and capable of surviving changes in economic organisation; the name patriarchy is given to that system, and the claim is that it must be analysed and fought on its own terms. Marxist feminism treats gender relations differently: not as unimportant, not as a distraction, but as taking their distinctively modern shape inside a particular organisation of production and reproduction, so that the form women's subordination wears in a capitalist society is explained by that society's need to renew its workforce daily and generationally. Liberal feminism, the third member of the standard comparison, locates the problem neither in a system of male domination nor in the structure of capital, but in unequal rights, discriminatory practices, and blocked access, which reform can remedy piece by piece.

### What is the mark-carrying distinction between Marxist and radical feminism?

which system is primary. Radical feminism treats male domination as prior to and independent of economic organisation; Marxist feminism treats gender relations as taking their modern form within capitalist production and reproduction. Stating this rather than ranking oppressions is what earns the marks.

Notice what the distinction is not. It is not a disagreement about whether women's subordination is real; all three traditions affirm it. It is not a disagreement about whether it should end. It is a disagreement about causal and analytical architecture: what explains what, and therefore what would have to change for the pattern to change. A candidate who frames the comparison this way can generate genuine analysis from it, because each tradition's account of the family, of work, and of the state follows from its answer to the primacy question, and an answer can trace those consequences one by one. A candidate who frames the comparison as a contest of concern can generate only emphasis. Markers know the difference on sight, and the examiners' literature on this topic says so in almost those words.

### How should a candidate phrase the primacy claim in an opening paragraph?

State the architecture, not the allegiance. Write that Marxist feminism locates the modern form of gender relations within the capitalist organisation of production and reproduction, and contrast that location with radical feminism's prior and separate system of male domination. The formulation commits the candidate to nothing except accuracy, and it can open an answer arguing in any direction.

The ranking error deserves a autopsy of its own, because it is not a random mistake but a taught one. Many revision resources compress the primacy distinction into a memorable formula, class before gender, and the formula is memorable precisely because it is simpler than the truth. Candidates then reproduce it in good faith, believing they are summarising. What the formula destroys is the actual content of the Marxist feminist claim, which concerns the reproduction of labour power: the daily and generational work, overwhelmingly performed by women and overwhelmingly unpaid, through which workers are fed, rested, raised, and replaced. That claim can be assessed, evidenced, and criticised. The ranking formula can only be asserted. A marker who meets the formula in a script knows the candidate has been badly served by a resource, and marks the script in front of them rather than the resource behind it. Recovering from that inheritance is the first task this guide sets, and the answer matrix below is built to make the recovery mechanical.

## The feminism answer matrix and how to use it

The matrix gathers the five question types that exhaust nearly everything examiners ask on this material. Each row pairs a question type with the definition that should lead the answer, the theorist whose name does the most work, the evaluation that lifts the band, and the trap that the question type sets for the unwary. Candidates should learn the matrix as a planning instrument rather than as content: in the examination room its function is to convert a question, within the first minute of reading it, into a sequence of moves the candidate has rehearsed. The rows are ordered by frequency rather than by difficulty, and the comparison row is the one that repays the closest study, since comparison questions expose the primacy distinction most directly and punish the ranking formulation most reliably.

| Question type | Definition to lead with | Theorist to cite | Evaluation that lifts the band | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Explain Marxist feminism | Gender relations acquire their modern shape inside a capitalist organisation of production and reproduction | Margaret Benston on the material basis of housework | The dual systems objection, named and stated fairly | Defining the position by a ranking of harms |
| Compare feminist perspectives | Each tradition named by the system it treats as primary | Sylvia Walby on the structures of patriarchy | The charge that comparison grids flatten each tradition | Treating radical feminism as a temperament rather than a theory |
| Evaluate the account of the family | The household as the place of labour power's unwaged renewal | Christine Delphy on the domestic mode of production | The objection that the account explains too much by function | Slipping into a summary of family diversity research |
| Apply feminist theory to work or education | Unpaid and low-paid care as the subsidy beneath the wage economy | Heidi Hartmann on job segregation by sex | The precision objection against an overextended concept of labour | Quoting figures whose measurement the candidate cannot defend |
| Assess whether Marxism neglects gender | A charge pressed most forcefully from inside the Marxist feminist tradition itself | Michèle Barrett on the limits of the early literature | The reply drawn from the social reproduction literature | Answering a different question about Marx the man |

Reading the matrix well requires a habit the table cannot supply: each cell is a compressed instruction, and the candidate's task in preparation is to expand every cell into two or three sentences of their own prose, written out in advance and revised until the expansion is fluent. A definition cell expanded gives the opening sentence of the answer. A theorist cell expanded gives the named claim that anchors the middle. An evaluation cell expanded gives the turn into the top band. A trap cell expanded gives the sentence the candidate must not write, and knowing forbidden sentences is as useful as knowing required ones, since under time pressure candidates fall back on the formulations they have heard most often, which on this topic are precisely the formulations the matrix marks as traps.

### How does the matrix change what a candidate does in the first five minutes?

It replaces improvisation with recognition. The candidate sorts the question into a row, takes the lead definition and the anchoring theorist from memory, and spends the planning minutes on the evaluation rather than on deciding what the answer is about. Scripts planned this way open cleanly, and clean openings are what markers remember when bands are assigned.

The matrix also disciplines length. Candidates commonly lose marks not through ignorance but through distribution, writing three quarters of an answer on exposition because exposition feels safe, and leaving evaluation to a hurried final paragraph that the band descriptors cannot credit. A row of the matrix is a rationing device: definition, theorist, development, evaluation, in proportions the candidate has decided beforehand. When the clock presses, the candidate who is following a row can compress the development and still deliver the evaluation entire, which is the correct sacrifice. The candidate writing without a row compresses whatever happens to be last, and what happens to be last is usually the part the marks were waiting for.

## Explain questions: stating the position so a marker can credit it

The explain question looks like the easiest item on the paper and is marked like the hardest, because its simplicity is a filter. Asked to explain Marxist feminism, weaker candidates produce a paragraph of gestures toward inequality and a closing sentence about capitalism and patriarchy working together, a phrase that papers over the exact junction the examiner wants examined. A creditable explanation has a skeleton. It begins from the canonical definition: women's subordination takes its modern form inside the capitalist organisation of production and reproduction, particularly through unpaid labour that renews labour power. It then identifies the mechanism, the unpaid work of the household, and shows why the mechanism matters to the system, since a workforce must be produced and maintained before it can be employed. Only then does it name the contrast with neighbouring traditions. Explanation in that order is analysis in embryo, because each step depends on the one before it and the whole has a direction.

The mechanism deserves the candidate's closest care, since it is the part of the position most often gestured at and least often stated. Labour power, the capacity to work, is unlike other commodities in that it is not produced in a factory. It is produced in households, through cooking, cleaning, laundering, nursing, comforting, and childrearing, work that restores the worker each evening and manufactures the next generation of workers across two decades. Where that work is unpaid, capital receives its central input without paying the full cost of producing it, and the wage of the employed worker quietly understates what the workforce costs to maintain. Margaret Benston gave this argument its early rigorous form, observing that women constitute a group whose relation to production is mediated through the household, and that the material basis of their status lies in that arrangement rather than in attitudes alone. Walking a marker through the mechanism in four sentences does more than define a term. The candidate has shown the position from inside.

### Which theorist should anchor an explain answer, and with what claim?

Benston, attached to the claim that housework, though it yields the commodity labour power, stands outside the wage relation, and that women's material position follows from that exclusion. One theorist carrying one precise claim outweighs a procession of surnames, because the marker can credit the claim and has nothing to credit in a list of names unaccompanied by arguments.

Engels belongs in an explain answer, but under supervision. His account of the family tied the subordination of women to the rise of private property and the need to transmit inheritance through a controlled line of descent, and it remains the tradition's founding text. The supervision required is the caveat: the anthropology on which Engels built, drawn from the scholarship available to him, has not survived the scrutiny of later specialists, and a candidate who retails his sequence of family forms as established fact invites correction. The disciplined move is to present Engels as the origin of the materialist framing, the relocation of women's status from nature to history, while flagging that the specific prehistory he relied on is contested ground. That single flag converts a liability into evidence of reading, and the full treatment in the series article on Engels and the family, within the cluster this guide serves, gives the candidate the detail the flag summarises.

### What separates an explanation that earns the middle band from one that reaches higher?

Development through consequences. The middle band answer states the definition and the mechanism and stops. The higher answer follows the position into one of its implications, showing for instance how the reserve of unpaid domestic labour also shapes women's position in paid employment, and then acknowledges the objection the implication attracts. Consequence plus objection is the signature of the upper bands wherever this topic is set.

Explain answers also benefit from a sentence most candidates never write: the sentence that says what the position is not. Marxist feminism is not the claim that sexism is a trick played by employers, though some of its critics have parodied it as such; nor is it the claim that every feature of gender relations can be read off the needs of accumulation, a functionalism its own best writers resisted. Delimiting a position against its parodies is a recognised scholarly operation, and examiners reward it because it demonstrates that the candidate knows where the defensible frontier of the theory runs. The candidate should choose one parody, name it as a parody, and replace it with the stronger claim the literature actually defends. Done once, briefly, that move does the work of a page of additional exposition.

## Compare questions: the three traditions and their primary systems

Comparison is the question type on which this topic is won or lost, because the comparison is the field. A compare answer should be organised by the primacy axis from the first paragraph: three traditions, three candidate foundations, and every subsequent paragraph testing how each foundation reorganises the same phenomenon. The family serves well as the test phenomenon, since each tradition has a developed account of it. Work serves equally well. What does not serve is the procession structure, in which the candidate describes one tradition completely, then the second, then the third, and appends a paragraph observing that they differ. Procession answers earn description marks and forfeit comparison marks, because comparison is an operation performed inside paragraphs, not a summary stapled to their end. The marker should be able to point at any middle paragraph and say which traditions are being set against each other in it, and on what point.

Liberal feminism, stated in its strongest form, is not the feeble position of the caricatures. Its claim is that the formal and informal barriers excluding women from education, professions, property, and political voice are the substance of the problem, and that their removal, through law and through changed practice, dissolves subordination without requiring the transformation of either the economy or the relations between the sexes at their root. Its strength is its record: the barriers were real, and their removal changed lives measurably. Its stated weakness, from the other traditions, is that formal equality leaves the material organisation of care untouched, so that women enter public life still carrying the household. Radical feminism, stated strongly, holds that male domination is the foundational system, older than capitalism and visible inside every class, and that its institutions include the family, sexuality, and the control of women's bodies; its strength is that it explains the persistence of the pattern across radically different economic systems, and its stated weakness, from the Marxist side, is that a system defined so broadly struggles to explain historical variation in women's position.

Marxist feminism enters the comparison with its own strength and its own exposure. Its strength is specificity: it can say why subordination takes the particular form it takes in a wage economy, why the housewife of one century and the care worker of another occupy structurally similar positions, and why employers benefit from arrangements they did not invent. Its exposure is the mirror image. A theory tuned to the wage economy must work harder to explain the subordination that predates that economy and the forms that persist where the economy has been transformed, and its critics have pressed exactly there. A comparison answer that can state all three strengths and all three exposures, in the traditions' own terms, has done the analytical work the question exists to elicit, and the verdict the candidate then reaches can be modest without costing marks, because the marks were in the stating.

### How many traditions should a comparison answer handle, and in what order?

Three, ordered by the logic of the argument rather than by chronology. Open with the tradition the question names, establish its primary system, then bring in each rival as a challenge to that foundation. Ordering by foundation lets every paragraph perform a comparison; ordering by date turns the script into three short essays that never meet, and markers mark the meeting.

The comparison grid found in many textbooks, rows of traditions against columns of slogans, is the specific artefact the current-form move exists to correct. Grids freeze each tradition at the moment of its textbook summary, which for Marxist feminism is usually the domestic labour debate and for radical feminism a simplified doctrine of patriarchy. Real traditions argue with themselves across decades. Radical feminism contains materialist currents that borrow freely from Marxist method, and the Marxist tradition contains the dual systems proposal, associated with Heidi Hartmann, which conceded that patriarchy might be a system in its own right standing in partnership and in tension with capital. A candidate who knows that the boundary between the traditions is itself disputed, and can show one place where it is disputed, writes a comparison of a different order from the grid. The series treatment of [marxist radical liberal feminism](/2016/11/15/marxist-radical-liberal-feminism/) works through the three positions in the depth a comparison answer draws on, and this guide assumes the candidate will mine it during revision rather than reproduce it under examination conditions.

### What is the trap inside the standard comparison grid?

Fixity. The grid presents each tradition as a finished doctrine with a date of manufacture, so the candidate reproduces the doctrines and never notices that each has a later form answering the standard objections. Answers built on grids read as summaries of summaries. The remedy is to attach a development to at least one tradition, showing the marker a position that moved.

One further discipline governs comparison answers: symmetry of charity. Candidates routinely state their favoured tradition in its sophisticated form and its rivals in their crude form, and markers read the asymmetry as advocacy rather than analysis. The rule of the current-form move applies to all three columns equally. If Marxist feminism is to be represented by social reproduction theory rather than by its earliest statements, then radical feminism must be represented by its most developed account of patriarchy as a set of structures, the account Sylvia Walby constructed, distinguishing forms of patriarchal organisation rather than treating domination as a single unchanging fact. Symmetry is not neutrality of conclusion. A candidate may conclude firmly for one tradition. Symmetry governs only the quality of the statements from which the conclusion is drawn, and it is the quality the band descriptors describe.

## Evaluate the family: the household as an economic institution

Questions inviting evaluation of the Marxist feminist account of the family are a gift to the prepared candidate, because the account is concrete enough to evaluate precisely. The claim under examination is that the household under capitalism is an economic institution before it is an emotional one: the place where labour power is produced and renewed outside the wage relation, and therefore the place where a large share of women's work disappears from the category of work altogether. Evaluation should begin by acknowledging the claim's explanatory victories. It explains why housework is unpaid without treating that fact as natural. It explains why the work is assigned to women through a mechanism, the organisation of reproduction, rather than through prejudice alone. And it explains why campaigns that won women entry to employment did not by themselves equalise the household, since the institution the account describes was left standing.

From that acknowledgement the candidate turns to the objections, and the family question has a well stocked armoury. Christine Delphy's intervention is the most instructive, because it came from within materialist feminism and displaced its centre of gravity: on her account the household is a mode of production in its own right, in which husbands appropriate wives' labour directly, so that the primary exploiter of a housewife is not capital at a distance but the head of her own household. The objection matters for examination purposes because it cannot be dismissed as an outsider's misunderstanding; it is a rival materialism, and the candidate who deploys it demonstrates that evaluation on this topic is a family quarrel among serious positions. Alongside it stands the functionalism charge: that the classic account explains the family by the services it renders to capital, and that explaining an institution by its benefits risks assuming the benefits caused the institution. And alongside that stands the diversity objection, that households vary by class, region, and household form far more than a single model of the nuclear unit allows.

### Which objection lifts a family answer into the top band most reliably?

The functionalism charge, stated precisely. It targets the logic of the classic account rather than its sympathies, it can be answered from within the literature by distinguishing benefit from cause, and it lets the candidate show a theory being repaired rather than merely denounced. Markers consistently reward objections that the theory's own defenders had to work to answer.

The strongest family answers end with a repair rather than a wreck. Social reproduction theory restates the household claim in a form that survives the standard objections better: it treats the work of renewing life and labour as a sphere with its own dynamics, bound to the economy without being a simple reflection of it, and it widens the site of reproduction from the nuclear household to schools, hospitals, and neighbourhoods. A candidate who closes a family evaluation by showing the objection landing on the older form and the newer form absorbing part of the blow has performed the complete evaluative arc the question was designed to test. The treatment of the neglect charge and its literature, in the series article devoted to whether Marxism neglected gender, supplies much of the raw material for that arc, and the candidate preparing this question type should know its argument in outline.

Proportion is the remaining skill. Evaluation questions are failed by candidates who evaluate in every paragraph and establish nothing, leaving the marker unsure the candidate could state the account being judged. The reliable proportion is a first half that states and develops, a turn in which the strongest objection is introduced in its author's terms, a reply where the literature offers one, and a short verdict that follows from the exchange rather than preceding it. Candidates sometimes fear that stating a position fully before criticising it wastes time that advocacy could use. The marking logic runs the other way. The statement is what the criticism is worth, and a verdict that arrives after a fair fight is credited as judgement, while a verdict announced in the introduction and repeated at the end is credited as opinion.

## Apply questions: work, education, and the units of the syllabus

Feminist perspectives rarely appear only as a theory topic. They travel across the specification, surfacing in units on the family, on education, on work, and on crime, and the apply question tests whether the candidate can carry a framework into unfamiliar material without dropping it. The carry has a technique. The candidate selects the element of the framework that touches the item in the question, states that element in one sentence, and then works it against the particulars given, rather than reproducing the whole theory in hope that some of it lands. For Marxist feminism the portable elements are few and strong: the renewal of labour power without wages, the segregation of paid labour markets by sex, the ideological work of the family in reconciling workers to their position, and the reserve function by which women's paid employment expands and contracts with the needs of employers.

In the work unit the sharpest instrument is Hartmann's analysis of job segregation. Her argument, that the exclusion of women from better paid occupations is a joint product of capitalist labour markets and male workers' organised defence of advantage, gives the candidate a mechanism to apply wherever an item mentions occupational distribution. It also models the dual systems proposal in miniature, since Hartmann refused to let either system swallow the other. In the education unit the portable claim concerns the hidden curriculum of care: schooling sorts girls toward subjects and dispositions that prepare them for the caring roles the economy requires, and the Marxist feminist version of that claim ties the sorting to the reproduction of labour power rather than to teacher prejudice alone. In the family unit the application usually runs through the reserve argument and the subsidy argument, the household's unpaid output lowering the wage at which labour can be offered. Each application is a paragraph, not a chapter, and the candidate's economy in making it is itself marked, since application questions carry many marks in short compass.

### How should a candidate handle figures in an apply answer?

Describe the pattern and decline the number. Pay differentials and the measured hours of unpaid work shift with definitions, populations, and dates, and a candidate who cites a figure cannot defend its provenance under examination conditions. A stated pattern, women clustered in lower paid occupations across the economies the literature examines, is defensible. An orphaned statistic is a hostage the marker is free to discount.

Crime and deviance units reward a different element, the ideological one. The Marxist feminist contribution there concerns whose behaviour is policed and whose is excused: the double standard by which female offenders are judged against ideals of femininity as well as against law, and the way welfare and family policy discipline mothers in particular. Candidates should present that contribution as an application of the reproduction framework, since the regulation of mothers is, on this account, part of how the renewal of labour power is supervised. The wider paper's demands are treated in the series article on [marxist sociology exam answers](/2021/08/15/marxist-sociology-exam-answers/), which maps the perspectives onto the standard units in a way this guide deliberately does not duplicate.

### What makes an application paragraph score rather than merely mention?

Contact with the item. A paragraph that names the framework and then discusses the question's material in the framework's vocabulary, drawing one consequence the material supports and noting one it resists, scores. A paragraph that describes the framework beside the material, like two exhibits in adjacent cases, mentions. Markers can feel the difference in a single reading, and so can candidates who practise the contact deliberately in revision.

The revision practice for apply questions is therefore specific. Take past items from across the units, and for each, write the single sentence that connects framework to item before writing anything else. Candidates who drill that sentence find the paragraphs follow from it; candidates who begin by writing out the theory find the item still waiting, unaddressed, when time expires. Application is the question type where planning most visibly converts into marks, and it is also the type where the matrix earns its keep outside the theory paper, since the trap column for application warns against the two failures markers see constantly: the treatise that ignores the item, and the figure quoted without a defensible source.

## Assess whether Marxism neglects gender

The neglect question is the most philosophically loaded of the five types, because it asks a candidate to evaluate a tradition's self understanding rather than one of its doctrines. The charge, as it should be stated, is that classical Marxism theorised class exploitation with precision while treating women's subordination as derivative or incidental, leaving a gap that its own categories were slow to fill. The crucial point for the examination room, and the point that lifts the mediocre answer above the rest, is that the charge was pressed most forcefully from inside the Marxist feminist tradition itself. It was not an accusation hurled by opponents and defended against by loyalists. It was the position from which a generation of Marxist feminists worked, and the tradition they built is in large part the record of its attempt to answer it.

Michèle Barrett's contribution supplies the anchor for this question type. Her examination of the early literature's treatment of women, and of the silence about gender in categories built for class analysis, gave the charge its documented form. A candidate citing Barrett should attach the specific point: that the classical theory supplied the tools to analyse women's subordination, in the analysis of reproduction and of the household economy, without supplying the account itself, and that the gap had to be closed by work the tradition did not initially perform. The evaluation then runs both ways. On the one side stands the weight of the silence, documented in the texts; on the other stands the tradition's own capacity to generate the missing analysis once the question was asked, which is evidence that the neglect was historical rather than structural. That distinction, between a tradition that cannot answer a question and a tradition that had not yet answered it, is the analytical move the question rewards.

### How does a candidate avoid turning the neglect answer into biography?

By judging the tradition's categories rather than its founder's attitudes. The question is whether the theory contains the resources, and the answer is built from the texts and their development, not from anecdotes about any individual's household. Biographical answers score nothing in the upper bands, because the examiners set a question about a framework, and anecdote answers a different one.

The reply from the social reproduction literature belongs in the second half of the answer, as the evidence that the tradition has developed a systematic account of reproduction that its critics acknowledge as its own achievement. Candidates should present that development honestly: it answers the charge in part, by supplying a theory of gendered reproduction that the classical texts lacked, and it leaves a residue, since the theory's core categories were still built around the wage relation and the extension to reproduction remains contested even among its defenders. That residue is where a final judgement earns its marks, provided the judgement is framed as a judgement about categories and not as a verdict on a movement. The article on [did marxism neglect gender](/2016/12/15/did-marxism-neglect-gender/) handles the historical record at the depth this question type draws on, and a candidate who has revised it can afford to be brief here without seeming evasive.

The neglect question also tests a candidate's handling of internal criticism. A tradition's own critics are not the same kind of evidence as its external critics. Internal criticism demonstrates that the position is alive and self correcting; external criticism demonstrates that it is contested. The answer that can hold both kinds of evidence at once, showing the neglect charge as an internal motor of the tradition's development and the external objections as a separate set of pressures, writes at the level the question was designed for. Most scripts present one kind and ignore the other. The candidate who presents both has already moved ahead of the cohort, and the marker's recognition of that move is visible in the band assigned.

## The theorists worth naming, and the claims that make the names matter

Names are the currency of the upper bands on this topic, and the rule governing them is simple: a name is worth its place only when a claim travels with it. Examiners' reports return to this with weary regularity, noting scripts that deploy half a dozen surnames in a paragraph and say nothing any of them argued. The discipline is to know, for each theorist a candidate intends to use, the single claim that justifies the mention, phrased tightly enough to reproduce under pressure. The directory below supplies that discipline for the names most useful in this field, in the order a revision course would meet them.

Engels supplies the founding materialist framing of the family and the proposal that women's subordination is a historical product tied to private property, and the candidate's responsibility with him is the caveat about the anthropology he borrowed. Clara Zetkin and Alexandra Kollontai supply the early socialist feminist claim that women's emancipation is inseparable from the transformation of the economy, and that the household itself must be reorganised rather than merely exited; their usefulness lies in showing the tradition's early practical radicalism, and their limitation is that neither supplied a systematic theory of reproduction. Margaret Benston supplies the argument that housework produces the commodity labour power outside the wage relation. Mariarosa Dalla Costa and the wages for housework literature supply the campaigning translation of that argument, the demand that the work be paid, and the theoretical claim that housework is productive of surplus in a sense the classical categories underplayed; a candidate citing the campaign should present it as a demand and a diagnosis rather than as a slogan.

Heidi Hartmann supplies the dual systems thesis and the analysis of occupational segregation as a product of patriarchy and capital acting together, the single most valuable synthesis for comparison and application answers alike. Christine Delphy supplies the domestic mode of production, the claim that the household is itself a site of extraction, which lets a candidate evaluate Marxist feminism from inside materialist feminism. Michèle Barrett supplies the documented account of classical Marxism's neglect and the demand that the tradition close its own gap. Sylvia Walby supplies the structural account of patriarchy as a set of structures operating across the household, paid work, the state, violence, sexuality, and culture, and the distinction between private and public forms of patriarchal organisation, which is the strongest available form of radical feminism for comparison purposes.

### What distinguishes a theorist cited well from a theorist merely listed?

The claim. A name plus a specific proposition, in one sentence, is a credit. A name alone, however distinguished, is decoration, and decoration is not marked. Candidates should drill their theorists as name and claim pairs until the pairs are automatic, because under time pressure the names survive and the claims are what disappear first.

The later tradition supplies the names that demonstrate currency. Tithi Bhattacharya, whose work on social reproduction has done much to restate the tradition's claims in a form that answers the standard objections, and Susan Ferguson, whose formulation of reproduction as the labour of renewing life supplies the most teachable version of the current form, are the two names a candidate should be able to attach to a precise formulation each. Bhattacharya's contribution can be attached to the claim that social reproduction theory is best understood as a theory of the system as a whole rather than a supplement to the theory of production, and Ferguson's to the insistence that the work of reproduction includes the social and cultural labour through which persons and communities are sustained, not only the biological renewal of workers. Both formulations are strong, and both are evaluable, which is why markers credit them. Naming the precision objection as well, the charge that the concept of labour expands until it loses its edge, shows the tradition in argument rather than in procession.

The theorists a candidate should not name are instructive too. Figures from other fields, quoted for a sentence that sounds relevant, dilute an answer rather than enriching it. Names from the liberal tradition belong in the comparison, attached to the rights and access claim, not scattered as garnish. And theorists the candidate cannot attach to a claim should be left out of the answer entirely, however well they were revised, since a name without a claim is a promise the script does not keep. The discipline feels harsh in revision and generous in the examination room, because it converts a long list into a small arsenal, and small arsenals are easier to fire accurately.

## Social reproduction theory and the current-form move

The current-form move is this guide's namable claim, and it deserves its own anatomy. Social reproduction theory takes the classical question, how is labour power produced and renewed, and gives it a systematic modern answer. Its claim is that the activities renewing life and labour, carried on in households, schools, hospitals, and communities, form a sphere integral to the capitalist economy rather than a remainder outside it, and that this sphere is gendered in its organisation, so that the analysis of gender is not an addition to the analysis of capital but a dimension of it. Lise Vogel's earlier restatement, which distinguished the reproduction of labour power from the production of goods and showed how the two were articulated, laid the foundation the later literature built on. The literature's own term of praise for the restatement is precision: where the older theory spoke loosely of housework serving capital, the newer theory specifies the relation between the spheres and allows each its own dynamics.

Why does citing this earn marks out of proportion to its length? Because a single accurate deployment does three examinable things at once. It updates the tradition, proving the candidate knows the position did not freeze in the decade of the domestic labour debate. It evaluates the older form, since the move to social reproduction was itself driven by the objections the older form attracted, and the candidate who makes the move can name the objection it answers. And it strengthens the candidate's own verdict, whichever way the verdict falls, because a conclusion about the strongest form of a position is worth more than a conclusion about a superseded one. The series article on [social reproduction theory explained](/2016/09/15/social-reproduction-theory-explained/) sets out the material at full length; in the examination room, two or three sentences of it, placed well, carry the effect.

### How should the current-form move be phrased inside a timed answer?

As a development, not as a correction of the examiner. Write that the tradition's later literature restates the claim through social reproduction theory, specify one thing the restatement sharpens, and continue the argument from the sharpened version. The move takes two sentences. Candidates who announce that textbooks are outdated spend marks on meta-commentary; candidates who simply write the stronger theory collect them.

Placement matters. The strongest position for the move is the pivot of the answer, the point where exposition turns to evaluation, because there it does its double work: closing the account of the tradition at its strongest, and opening the evaluation from a position of demonstrated knowledge. Placed at the very end, it reads as an afterthought, a name dropped for credit. Placed in the introduction, it promises a sophistication the answer may not sustain, and markers discount promises. Candidates should also know the move's limit. It does not rescue an answer that has misstated the basic position, and it does not substitute for the primacy distinction, which remains the load-bearing wall. The current form is the roof; a script still needs its foundations in the right order.

### What is the strongest objection the current form itself attracts?

The precision objection. By widening reproduction to cover the whole renewal of life, the theory risks stretching the concept of labour until it covers everything and explains nothing in particular. Sophisticated defenders answer by specifying levels and relations rather than by denying the risk, and a candidate who can state both the risk and the answer writes the theory as a living argument rather than as a monument.

There is a final, quiet advantage in teaching the move as technique rather than as allegiance. A candidate of any conclusion can use it. The candidate arguing against Marxist feminism needs its strongest form even more than the candidate arguing for it, since a refutation of the textbook version persuades nobody who knows the literature, and examiners know the literature by definition. Teaching the move neutrally, as the craft of stating positions in their strongest present form, is therefore also the honest way to teach it: the move is what scholarship does, and assessment at its best is scholarship under a clock.

## The evaluation library: named critics and the arguments they carry

Evaluation is a library, not a mood, and this section shelves the volumes a candidate needs. Each entry is a named critic, a specific argument, and the reply the literature offers, since an objection without its reply is only half a paragraph. The candidate who can draw three of these entries, chosen to fit the question, and run each through its full arc of statement, reply, and residual force, has an evaluation section of top band quality. The candidate who instead writes that the theory has been criticised for ignoring this and oversimplifying that has written the table of contents of an evaluation and none of its pages.

The dual systems objection, carried by Hartmann, holds that the marriage between Marxism and feminism, in her famous characterisation, was an unhappy one, because Marxism's categories are sex blind: they can analyse capital but not the relations between men and women, which require a second theory of patriarchy operating alongside the first. Stated at full strength, this is the most consequential criticism the tradition has faced, since it concedes the analysis of capitalism and denies its sufficiency. The reply from within the unitary tradition is that two systems explains nothing about how the systems interact, and that the phenomena Hartmann assigns to patriarchy are better explained by the organisation of reproduction itself. The residual force of the objection is real: the interaction problem cuts both ways, and a candidate may fairly conclude that neither the dual nor the unitary form has fully solved it. That conclusion, reached by that route, is what evaluation marks look like when they are earned.

The materialist objection from Delphy cuts from the opposite side. Where Hartmann found Marxism insufficiently attentive to patriarchy, Delphy found it insufficiently attentive to the household as a direct relation of exploitation, arguing that wives' labour is appropriated by husbands within a domestic mode of production that capitalism did not create and does not exhaust. The reply notes that Delphy's own framework remains materialist and historical, so that her work extends the tradition's method even while correcting its object. For the candidate, the value of the entry is its demonstration that the sharpest evaluations of Marxist feminism have often been written by feminists working with its own tools, a fact that complicates any lazy narrative of a theory refuted from outside.

### Which two objections should every candidate in this field be able to run entire?

The dual systems objection and the functionalism charge. Between them they test the tradition's architecture and its logic, one asking whether a single system can explain the pattern and the other asking whether explanation by benefit is explanation at all. A candidate fluent in both arcs, objection and reply and residue, is equipped for nearly every evaluation question this topic generates.

The functionalism charge, already met in the family section, generalises across the whole literature. Its form is always the same: the theory observes that an arrangement benefits capital, and infers that the arrangement exists because it benefits capital, and the inference is a fallacy unless a mechanism connects benefit to persistence. The literature's mature reply is to supply mechanisms, the state's interest in a reproduced workforce, employers' interest in a divided labour market, households' interest in survival, and to let arrangements persist through the actions of those pursuing their own ends. To convert the charge into a demand for mechanisms, and then to judge whether the mechanisms offered are plausible, is to do philosophy of social science at examination level, and markers recognise the altitude.

The liberal objection deserves its place, stated as its proponents state it. On that view the Marxist feminist framework systematically undervalues the gains available within existing institutions, treats reforms that transformed women's legal and educational position as superficial, and offers a total transformation whose absence becomes an excuse for discounting actual progress. The reply from the Marxist side distinguishes between gains and their distribution, noting that access to professions has not dissolved the organisation of care. The residue is a genuine question about pace and strategy that an honest candidate may leave open. And the objection from the study of intersecting inequalities, associated with Kimberlé Crenshaw's analysis of how categories interact, holds that any single axis framework, class first or gender first, misdescribes lives lived at the crossing of several. The social reproduction literature's reply is that a theory of the whole can incorporate intersecting relations without surrendering its architecture. Stating that exchange fairly, objection at full strength and reply in its own terms, is among the most creditable moves available in an evaluation section.

## Examples that work: the evidence base a candidate can defend

Examples in this field fail in a characteristic way: they are vivid, particular, and indefensible. A candidate reaches for a statistic half remembered from a documentary, or a workplace anecdote from the news, and the marker, who cannot verify either and suspects the candidate cannot either, discounts both. The examples that work are of a different kind. They are institutional and historical, drawn from the literature itself, and their function is to embody a mechanism rather than to decorate a claim. The wages for housework campaign is the model example: a real movement, emerging from the theory, whose demand made the theory's central claim visible by insisting that the work of the household be recognised as work through the wage it was denied. A candidate using the campaign can show theory generating politics, and can evaluate the demand itself, since its critics within the movement argued that wages would entrench the role they were meant to abolish. An example that contains its own evaluation is worth three that merely illustrate.

The historical depth of the tradition supplies the second class of safe examples. The early socialist organisation of communal households and public childcare, as proposed by Kollontai and attempted in the revolutionary period, embodies the claim that the household is an institution that can be reorganised, and its difficulties embody the evaluation, since the experiments faltered on resources and on the resistance of the very arrangements they targeted. The nineteenth century factory legislation debates, in which the hours of women and children were regulated before those of men, let a candidate discuss the state's role in reproduction with a documented case. The wartime expansions of female employment, followed by organised pressure to return women to the home when the emergency passed, embody the reserve argument in a sequence any marker will recognise. Each of these is checkable, each is in the literature, and none requires the candidate to assert anything about the present day.

### What property makes an example safe under examination conditions?

Provenance. The example comes from the scholarship the candidate has read, it illustrates a named mechanism rather than a mood, and its limits can be stated in a sentence. Examples borrowed from journalism fail on all three counts at once. Candidates revising this field should build a small stock of provenanced cases, four or five deep, rather than a long list of striking ones shallowly held.

The third class is the thought experiment the literature itself uses, and candidates may borrow it openly. Ask what would happen to the wage economy if the work of the household stopped for a month: no meals cooked, no children minded, no sick tended, no worker restored. The stoppage thought experiment, used by the tradition's writers to make invisible work visible, converts the reproduction claim into a scene a marker can test. Used sparingly, once in an answer, it performs the same service in the examination room that it performed in the pamphlets. The discipline is to return immediately to analysis, since a scene held too long becomes rhetoric, and rhetoric is what the bands are designed to see through.

## The errors clinic: six mistakes and their corrections

Errors on this topic are not random. They form a small repertoire, transmitted by weak resources and by the pressure of the clock, and a candidate who can name the repertoire is halfway to avoiding it. The first and costliest has been treated throughout this guide: the ranking formulation, which mistakes a claim about analytical primacy for a claim about relative importance. Its correction is mechanical. Wherever the draft of an answer says that one thing matters more than another, rewrite the sentence so that one system explains the form of another. The rewritten sentence is nearly always the analytically respectable one, and the exercise takes seconds once it becomes habitual.

The second error treats Marxist feminism and socialist feminism as identical labels for a single position. The terms overlap in ordinary usage, and some writers use them interchangeably, but the examination rewards the distinction where a specification draws it: socialist feminism is often used for the broader current that seeks to hold class and gender in a single frame, sometimes by dual systems means, while Marxist feminism names the stricter claim that gender relations are analysed within the theory of capital and its reproduction. The safe technique is to follow the question's own vocabulary, define the term as the question uses it, and note the neighbouring usage in a clause. The third error defines radical feminism by its manner, militancy or separatism, rather than by its theory. Radical in the tradition's name refers to the root, the claim that male domination is the root system of oppression, and a definition by temperament earns nothing because it cannot be compared with anything.

The fourth error presents the tradition as a closed historical episode, a debate of a particular decade after which nothing was written. Its correction is the current-form move itself, and the two belong together in revision: the candidate who has learned social reproduction theory as the tradition's present form cannot write the closed episode, because the present form is the answer's destination. The fifth error cites Engels without the anthropological caveat, retailing his sequence of family forms as though later scholarship had confirmed it. The sixth error, unattributed criticism, has been treated as a failure of evaluation and appears here as a failure of craft: the candidate knows objections exist and writes as though their existence were the evaluation. Its correction is the name and claim discipline of the earlier sections.

### Which single correction yields the largest mark gain for the least revision time?

Replacing the ranking formulation with the primacy formulation. It is one sentence pattern, it can be drilled in an afternoon, and it repairs the opening of every answer type in the matrix at once. Candidates with limited time before a paper should fix that pattern first and let the rest of the revision follow its logic.

Two further errors belong to the approach of the examination itself rather than to the content. The first is the answer that writes the theory paper inside every other unit, reproducing the full account of reproduction in an education question where one mechanism was required. Its correction is the portable element technique of the application section. The second is the answer that hedges its verdict into vapour, concluding that each perspective has strengths and weaknesses and that the truth lies somewhere between. Examiners do not forbid balanced conclusions; they forbid unearned ones, and a balance reached without the arguments having been run is unearned by definition. A modest verdict with a route is worth more than a grand verdict without one, and the route is built from named claims and named objections, nothing else.

## Anatomy of a strong answer: the four movements

Every strong answer in this field, whatever its question type, performs four movements in sequence, and candidates revise more effectively when they can name them. The first movement is location: the answer places the perspective according to the system it treats as foundational, in the opening sentences, so that everything later hangs from a stated foundation. The second movement is construction: the answer builds the position's central mechanism, showing how the foundation generates the claims about family, work, and state that the question will ask about. The third movement is pressure: the answer brings the strongest objection it knows against the construction, attributed to its author and stated at full strength. The fourth movement is judgement: a verdict proportioned to the pressure the construction withstood, which may range from endorsement through repair to rejection, and which the marker credits in proportion to the honesty of the third movement rather than the direction of the fourth.

Weak answers characteristically collapse the movements. The commonest collapse merges pressure into construction, sprinkling doubts through the exposition so that neither the position nor the objection is ever fully stated, and the marker is left with a mist of reservations that cannot be weighed. The second commonest omits pressure altogether and replaces judgement with summary, ending by restating the exposition in a tone of conclusion. The third, rarer in good candidates and fatal when it appears, performs judgement first and spends the answer defending it, which reverses the order assessment exists to reward. The four movements also give the candidate a diagnostic tool in revision: a specimen answer can be marked by movement, and the movement that is missing or stunted identifies exactly what the next practice answer must repair.

The location movement deserves a specimen, since it is where the primacy distinction does its work. Consider two openings to the same question, an invitation to assess the Marxist feminist account of women's position. The first opens by declaring the account powerful but limited, promising balance. Nothing in it is creditable yet, because the marker cannot tell what the account is. The second opens by stating that the account locates the modern form of women's subordination in the organisation of reproduction under capital, so that the unpaid renewal of labour power, rather than prejudice or law alone, carries the explanatory weight. The second opening can already be marked for accuracy, and its promise, that the assessment will test whether that location succeeds, tells the marker the remaining movements are coming. The difference between the two openings is not style. It is the presence or absence of the field's architecture in the first sixty words.

### How long should each movement be in a full length answer?

Location takes a paragraph, construction the largest share, pressure roughly a third, judgement a short closing passage. Proportions flex with the question type: explain questions enlarge construction, evaluate questions enlarge pressure. What never flexes is the order. A judgement that arrives before the pressure has been heard reads as prejudice whatever its content, and markers are trained to receive it that way.

Construction, the second movement, is where specimen answers most often underperform relative to their authors' knowledge. Candidates know more than they show because they summarise mechanisms they could demonstrate. The repair is to slow down at the mechanism and spend sentences on it: how the wage conceals the household's contribution, how the separation of home from workplace was itself a historical construction, how the category of the housewife was produced by that separation. Three sentences of mechanism outperform a paragraph of assertion, because the mechanism is what objections attach to, and an answer whose construction is thin gives its own pressure movement nothing solid to press on. The movements are load bearing for each other, which is why the anatomy is worth teaching as a single structure rather than as four tips.

## A worked comparison: one question answered three ways

Abstract advice about comparison becomes concrete when a candidate watches the same question handled at three levels of competence. Take the standard invitation to compare feminist perspectives on the family. The weakest competent handling proceeds by procession: a paragraph on the liberal view of the family as a site of unequal rights, a paragraph on the radical view of the family as the primary site of male domination, a paragraph on the Marxist view of the family as an economic unit, and a closing observation that the perspectives emphasise different factors. Every sentence in that answer may be true, and the answer still earns only description credit, because at no point do the perspectives meet. The marker learns what each says and nothing about how the claims collide, and collision is the substance of comparison.

The middle handling organises by issue. It takes the questions the family poses, who performs its labour, whose interests its form serves, what would change it, and lets each tradition answer each issue in turn, so that within any paragraph at least two traditions are in contact. That answer earns comparison credit, and its ceiling is set by the strength of the statements it compares: if the Marxist account is represented by the ranking formulation, or the radical account by temperament, the contact is between caricatures and the credit is capped accordingly. The strongest handling adds the axis and the development. It announces the primacy distinction in its opening, conducts the issue by issue comparison along that axis, represents Marxist feminism partly through its social reproduction form and radical feminism partly through the structural account of patriarchy, and closes by observing that the traditions disagree less about the family's facts than about which of its features are load bearing. That closing observation is analysis of a kind no procession can reach, because it is a claim about the comparison itself.

### What does the marker see in the strongest handling that the others lack?

A governing idea. The primacy axis selects the material, orders the paragraphs, and generates the conclusion, so the answer reads as one argument rather than as notes arranged in sequence. Markers describe such scripts as having a line, and the line is the single best predictor of the top band across all five question types in the matrix.

Candidates should practise the three levels deliberately. Take a comparison answered in revision, and rewrite it one level up: first convert procession into contact by reorganising paragraphs around issues, then strengthen the statements being compared by replacing one slogan with its theorist's actual claim, then add the development by bringing in the later form of one tradition. Each upgrade is a mechanical operation on an existing script, which makes the exercise fast, and after two or three upgrades the habits transfer to unseen questions. The exercise also teaches the candidate to read their own work as a marker reads, which is the deepest skill this guide can impart and the one that survives after the content of any particular topic has faded.

There is a warning attached to the strongest handling, and it concerns honesty about the axis itself. The primacy axis is a tool for organising a comparison, not a discovery that the traditions themselves announce in those words; sophisticated scripts acknowledge, in a clause, that the axis is the examiner's frame for the field rather than any tradition's self description. The acknowledgement costs four seconds of writing time and buys protection against the marker who prizes nuance, since it shows the candidate knows the difference between a map and the territory it organises. Maps are not diminished by being named as maps. They are diminished only when they are mistaken for the ground.

## The domestic labour debate: the argument candidates must be able to reconstruct

Behind the explain questions stands a scholarly episode that candidates should know as an argument with stages, because examiners draw on it when they set questions about housework, value, and the wage. The episode opened when Benston and others posed a deceptively simple problem: the labour performed in the household produces use values and reproduces the worker, yet it is performed outside the market and paid nothing, so what is its status within a theory built on the wage? One line of response held that housework produces the special commodity labour power and is therefore woven into the production of value indirectly, through the commodity it delivers to the market each morning. Another line, associated with the wages for housework current, pressed further, treating domestic labour as directly productive for capital and drawing the political conclusion that a wage was owed. A third line resisted both, arguing that housework, being exchanged against revenue rather than capital and producing use values for direct consumption, stands outside value production strictly conceived, while remaining indispensable to the system's survival.

The candidate does not need to settle that dispute, and examiners do not reward attempts to settle it by fiat. What earns marks is the ability to show why the dispute mattered: because each answer redistributed the theory's centre of gravity. If housework is value producing, the housewife stands inside the central relation of capitalism and her exploitation is of a piece with the worker's. If it is not, the theory requires an account of how a sphere outside value production is nevertheless organised by it, which is precisely the problem social reproduction theory later formalised. Tracing a debate forward into the theory it forced into existence is the narrative form of the current-form move, and an explain answer that contains three sentences of that tracing reads as scholarship rather than summary.

### Why do examiners keep returning to the housework problem?

Because it is the point where the tradition's categories meet ordinary experience and either illuminate it or fail audibly. Every candidate has lived in a household; the question tests whether a theory of production can see the labour that produced the candidate. Questions with that property discriminate reliably between scripts that have understood a mechanism and scripts that have memorised a description of it.

The debate also teaches an evaluative lesson candidates should absorb. The participants disagreed sharply while sharing a framework, and their disagreement was productive because each side specified the exact premise it rejected. When candidates evaluate, they should imitate that specificity: not the theory is wrong about housework, but the theory's treatment of the wage conceals a premise about which labour counts as productive, and the premise does the concealing. Premise level criticism is the strongest currency in the evaluation sections of every question type, and the domestic labour debate is the cheapest place to learn to mint it, because its premises are few, named, and still argued over in the literature the candidate is revising.

## The state, the family wage, and the reserves of labour

Three further mechanisms recur across question types often enough to deserve a candidate's deliberate preparation, since each converts readily into a paragraph in an apply or evaluate answer. The first is the family wage: the historical demand, pressed by male trade unionists as well as conceded by employers, that a man's wage should suffice to maintain a household. Hartmann's analysis treats that demand as evidence for the dual systems view, since it shows male workers defending an arrangement that subordinated women within the home while presenting itself as a defence of the working class family against capital. The Marxist reply reads the same episode through the reproduction of labour power, the wage form that paid for a household's maintenance through a single earner. Either reading gives the candidate a documented episode in which the traditions interpret the same facts differently, which is comparison gold.

The second mechanism is the reserve army in its gendered form. Marx's category of the relative surplus population, workers held in reserve and drawn into production as accumulation requires, was applied by feminist writers to women's paid employment: women constitute a flexible reserve, recruited when labour is scarce and expelled when it is not, with the household serving as the institution that maintains them during expulsion. The wartime employment cycle, expansion followed by organised return, is the standard evidence. The evaluation writes itself once the mechanism is stated, since critics ask whether the reserve pattern reflects capital's requirements or the choices and constraints of households themselves, and the candidate who can stage that exchange has a self contained evaluative paragraph usable in several question types.

The third mechanism concerns the state. Marxist feminism treats the state neither as a neutral arbiter nor as a simple committee of employers, but as an institution with its own stake in reproduction: it requires a maintained population, a future workforce, and social order in households, and its policies on schooling, welfare, and family law can be read as interventions in the reproductive sphere. That reading equips candidates for the state questions that appear in politics papers, where feminist perspectives are examined alongside other theories of power. Walby's account of the shift from private to public forms of patriarchal organisation, in which exclusion from public life gives way to segregation and subordination within it, is the comparison candidate's best bridge into the same territory, since it describes the state as a structure of patriarchy in its own right.

### Which of the three mechanisms travels best across question types?

The reserve argument. It is a claim about a pattern rather than about an institution, so it can be carried into work questions, family questions, and state questions without distortion, and it generates its own evaluation in the asking. Candidates short of revision time should secure the reserve mechanism first, then the family wage, then the state material, in that order of return.

Each mechanism also has a characteristic misuse the matrix's trap column exists to prevent. The family wage is misused when it is presented as a conspiracy rather than as an outcome of bargaining among parties with divergent interests. The reserve argument is misused when every fluctuation in female employment is attributed to it, flattening the differences between sectors and periods that the evidence insists on. The state material is misused when policy is read straight off economic function, as though a ministry had consulted the theory before drafting. Naming the misuse beside the mechanism, in revision, inoculates the candidate against producing it under pressure, and an answer that itself flags the misuse in a clause earns the marker's confidence in the way this guide has described from its opening pages.

## Liberal and radical feminism in their strongest forms

The current-form move, honestly taught, is a rule about all the traditions in the comparison, and this section pays the debt the symmetry principle incurred. Liberal feminism's strongest form is not a plea for politeness about inequality. It is a theory of institutions: formal rules and informal gatekeeping allocate education, occupation, property, and office, and they allocate them unequally by sex in ways that can be documented rule by rule and changed rule by rule. Its classic strength is diagnostic precision about mechanisms of exclusion, the examination that was closed, the credit that required a husband's signature, the profession whose informal sponsorship networks reproduced its own composition. Its developed form extends the analysis from rules to structures of opportunity, asking why formally open competitions produce patterned outcomes, and it has answers involving the distribution of time and care that bring it closer to its rivals than the textbook grid allows. Granting liberal feminism that development before criticising its limits produces a comparison the liberal tradition's own scholars would recognise, and recognition by the imagined authors is a fair test of whether a statement is strong.

Radical feminism's strongest form likewise exceeds its summary. In Walby's construction, patriarchy is not an attitude or a conspiracy but a system of structures, each with its own logic: the household, where labour is appropriated; paid work, where women are segregated into subordinate positions; the state, whose policies are biased in patterned ways; male violence and its regulation; sexuality, organised around norms not of women's choosing; and cultural institutions that represent the resulting order as natural. The construction matters for examinations because it is directly comparable with the Marxist architecture: both are system theories, both explain persistence, and they differ over whether the systems are two or one, and over which, if either, is foundational. A candidate who stages the comparison at that level, structure against structure, has located the genuine intellectual contest the topic contains, and the verdict, whichever way it falls, is an answer to a real question.

### What does symmetry of charity cost a candidate, and what does it buy?

It costs the easy victories, the paragraphs that defeat a slogan and call the defeat analysis. It buys the only verdicts a knowledgeable marker can credit, since a conclusion drawn from strong statements survives the marker's own knowledge of the literature. Candidates sometimes fear that strengthening a rival weakens their own side; in marked assessment the opposite holds, because the strength of the statements is itself the evidence of the candidate's quality.

Marxist feminism's strongest form, for symmetry's completeness, is the social reproduction restatement already described, and it should be compared against the developed forms of its rivals rather than against their summaries. The honest result of that three way comparison at full strength is a more interesting field than any textbook admits: three system theories, overlapping in their diagnoses of particular institutions, diverging on foundations and therefore on strategy. A candidate who can sketch that field in an opening paragraph, before descending to the question's particular demand, has demonstrated the synoptic grasp that distinguishes the strongest scripts in every examination system this topic appears in, from school sociology to civil service optionals and eligibility tests, where the same perspectives recur in different dress.

## The marking reality: verdicts are free, statements are graded

This guide now states, in its own voice and without qualification, the fact around which its advice on evaluation is organised. A model answer concluding against any feminist perspective, including Marxist feminism, can score full marks. A candidate may argue that the Marxist feminist account fails, that the radical account of patriarchy explains the evidence better, that the liberal programme has delivered more than either, or that the whole framework of primary systems should be abandoned, and any of those conclusions, reached through accurate statement and named evaluation, satisfies the highest band descriptors of the assessment systems that examine this topic. Markers are not grading allegiance, and on this topic they cannot, because the examiners themselves are divided among the traditions: the board that sets a feminist perspectives question contains scholars formed by different schools, and the only currency that circulates among all of them is accuracy of statement joined to fairness of evaluation.

The converse also holds, and candidates find it the harder lesson. A model answer concluding in favour of Marxist feminism, written by misstating the radical alternative and ignoring the dual systems objection, scores poorly, and its sympathy with the subject is not a mitigating circumstance but an aggravating one, since the defence was available honestly and was not taken. An answer that states Hartmann fairly, states the unitary reply fairly, and concludes that the Marxist account of gender should be set aside in favour of a dual systems analysis has given every marker exactly what the descriptors describe. An answer that defends the tradition by caricaturing Hartmann has given none of them anything. The candidate's conclusion is the least important choice in the script; the quality of the statements from which it is drawn is the most important, and this guide's whole apparatus, matrix, current-form move, name and claim discipline, exists to raise that quality.

### Can an answer that concludes against Marxist feminism score full marks?

Yes, and this guide states it as marking fact. The bands reward the accuracy of the statements, the specificity of the evaluation, and the proportion between evidence and verdict. A rejection built on the strongest form of the position, pressed by its most serious named critic, meets every descriptor a defence must meet, and markers apply the descriptors to the script in front of them rather than to the conclusion it reaches.

Why, then, do candidates persist in believing that agreement is safer? Partly because classrooms reward fluency in a tradition's vocabulary, and fluency is easily mistaken for loyalty. Partly because revision resources are often written by partisans of one school and model advocacy as the genre of the good answer. And partly because disagreement feels exposed: the candidate who concludes against a perspective imagines a marker of that persuasion reading with hostility. The institutional reality is duller and more reassuring. Marking is moderated, descriptors are public in their logic, and a script's defence in any dispute about its grade is the accuracy of its statements, which is entirely within the candidate's control. The rational response to that reality is not caution but precision: conclude where the arguments lead, and make the arguments good.

## Is it safe to criticise feminism? The candidate's real question answered

Beneath many of the queries candidates bring to this topic sits one anxious question, and the brief for this guide names it as the strongest objection to be addressed head on: whether criticising feminist perspectives risks marks. The fear is understandable. These perspectives concern live questions of justice, candidates know that markers are people, and the examination room offers no feedback until the grade arrives. The answer this guide gives is the one the marking evidence supports. Assessment rewards evidenced evaluation regardless of direction. What it punishes is not criticism but the counterfeit of criticism: the dismissal without an author, the objection aimed at a version nobody defends, the verdict that substitutes the candidate's temperature for the literature's arguments. A candidate who criticises through named critics, Hartmann on sex blind categories, Delphy on the household as direct extraction, the functionalism charge against explanation by benefit, is doing the thing the top band names, and examination systems protect that activity because it is the activity they exist to certify.

The counter-view must be stated fairly, because it contains a real subtlety. Some specifications and some question forms require the perspectives to be presented as perspectives: an item may ask for the contribution of feminist theory to an understanding of education, or for an exposition of the Marxist feminist view of the family, and in such items the candidate's task is representation before it is adjudication. The tell is in the command words and the mark tariff. Items built on explain, outline, or examine in the representational sense, especially at lower tariffs, are buying accurate statement, and a candidate who spends the item prosecuting a verdict has misread the purchase. Items built on assess, evaluate, or to what extent, especially at higher tariffs, are buying judgement, and withholding it forfeits the goods. Reading the command word as a contract, and delivering the operation it names, is the professional habit that dissolves the anxiety: the question is never whether the candidate is permitted a view, but which operation the item is paying for.

### How can a candidate tell whether an item wants representation or adjudication?

By the verb and the tariff together. Representational verbs at modest tariffs ask the candidate to be a faithful witness for the perspective, stating it as its holders state it. Evaluative verbs at the highest tariffs ask the candidate to sit as a judge among perspectives. Candidates who announce, in their opening, which operation they are performing show the marker a reader who understood the contract, and that understanding is itself credited.

There is a second reassurance, drawn from how moderation works. Scripts are sampled, second marked, and compared against exemplars precisely so that a marker's private sympathies cannot govern outcomes, and the exemplars used in training markers characteristically include high scoring scripts reaching opposed conclusions on contested topics. The candidate's protection is therefore structural, not a matter of hoping for a sympathetic reader. What the structure cannot protect is inaccuracy, because inaccuracy is the one fault every marker is trained to find. The candidate who wants safety should seek it where it lives: in statements that a scholar of any school in the field would pass as fair.

## Timed conditions: planning the answer the matrix makes possible

The matrix was described as a planning instrument, and this section cashes the description out in minutes and paragraphs. A full length answer in this field should be planned in four passes. The first pass, under a minute, sorts the question into its matrix row and fixes the lead definition. The second pass lists the theorists the answer will use, no more than three or four, each with the claim that justifies the name, and candidates should write the claims in compressed form, since the claim is the part that evaporates under stress. The third pass selects the evaluation: which named objection, which reply, and what residue the verdict will acknowledge. The fourth pass allocates paragraphs to the four movements, marking where the turn into evaluation happens, because the turn is the moment timed answers lose. A candidate who has made the fourth pass can write with the plan as a rail, and the writing itself, which is where the remaining time goes, becomes execution rather than invention.

Compression strategy under time pressure follows from the movement anatomy. If the clock demands cuts, cut construction before pressure, and cut examples before mechanisms. A shorter construction with its mechanism intact still supports evaluation; a long construction that squeezes the objection into a final sentence forfeits the band the whole answer was built to reach. Candidates should also protect the verdict's final sentences: a conclusion cut off mid-argument reads as an unfinished script, and a complete short verdict outperforms an ambitious one cut off in its prime. The practical technique is to draft the closing sentences in the plan, in miniature, before writing the body. Knowing the destination disciplines the route, and the marker meeting a deliberate close credits the script with control.

### How should a candidate handle a question that fits two matrix rows at once?

Decide which operation dominates and declare the decision in the opening. A question inviting evaluation of the family account through the neglect charge, for instance, is an evaluation question wearing a historical one, and the plan should allocate its weight accordingly. Hybrid questions reward candidates who sort them visibly; markers read the sorting as comprehension of the paper's design.

Shorter items demand a compressed version of the same discipline. For a brief explain item, the matrix row reduces to definition, mechanism, one contrast: three short paragraphs or one substantial one, with no evaluation unless the verb asks for it. For a mid tariff item, add one theorist with a claim and, where the verb permits, one objection with its author. The discipline scales because it is a discipline of selection rather than of volume: at every length, the candidate is choosing the highest value moves from the row and executing them cleanly, instead of writing until the time expires and hoping the value is somewhere inside.

## Revising the perspectives together: a method

Revision for this topic fails in a predictable pattern: candidates revise each tradition separately, from separate notes, and arrive at the examination with three monologues and no comparisons. The method this guide recommends reverses the unit of revision. The unit is the issue, not the tradition. Take the family, and set each tradition's claim about it beside the others, in one's own words, together with the scholar answerable for each. Take paid work, and do the same. Take the state, and do the same. A revision file organised by issue produces comparison answers almost automatically, because the material is already in contact before the examination begins, and the primacy axis runs down the file's spine, each issue showing the foundations generating different readings of the same institution.

The second element of the method is the claim drill. For each theorist in the directory, the candidate writes the name on one side of a card and the single claim on the other, and rehearses until the claim arrives with the name unbidden. The third element is the objection drill, the same cards built for criticisms: Hartmann and sex blind categories, Delphy and the domestic mode, the functionalism charge and its demand for mechanisms, the precision objection and its answer. The fourth element is timed practice on past questions, sorted by matrix row, so that the candidate discovers the reassuring regularity that the five rows exhaust nearly everything the examiners ask. The past questions gathered through the previous year papers tool at [ReportMedic](https://reportmedic.org/tools/upsc-pyq-explorer.html) let a candidate collect the topic's appearances across years and boards and sort them against the matrix, and the answer matrix itself should be transferred into the candidate's notes system as a set of revision cards, each row held as a card the candidate can annotate with specimen sentences. The order in which topics are revisited across the whole subject is set out in the [marxism revision guide](/2022/01/15/marxism-revision-guide/), whose sequence this method plugs into.

### How many past questions should a candidate work through before the paper?

Enough to have met each matrix row twice in the wild, which in practice means ten to fifteen questions read and five or six answered under time. Reading widely across rows teaches recognition; answering under time teaches execution. Candidates often invert the proportions, answering many and reading few, and then meet a row in the examination they cannot sort.

The final element is the error audit. After each practice answer, the candidate checks the script against the six errors of the clinic: ranking language, conflated traditions, temperament definitions, the closed episode, the uncaveated Engels, the unattributed objection. The audit takes minutes and catches the faults that content revision cannot, because the faults are habits of phrasing rather than gaps of knowledge. Candidates who keep a tally of their own recurring error find it concentrates instructively: most candidates have one signature fault, repeated across scripts, and naming it is usually enough to retire it. Teachers can run the same audit on a class set of scripts and will find the distribution of faults across a cohort maps closely onto the resources the cohort was given, which is a finding about resources as much as about candidates.

## Openings, pivots, and verdicts: the sentences that frame an answer

Certain sentences carry disproportionate weight in a script, and candidates should compose theirs in revision rather than improvise them under pressure. The opening sentence has been treated: it locates the perspective by its primary system. The second framing sentence is the roadmap, which tells the marker the route the answer will take, and on this topic the best roadmaps name the objection in advance: the answer will state the account, test it against the dual systems objection, and judge what survives. A roadmap that names the evaluation earns confidence, because it promises the movement candidates most often fail to deliver. The third framing sentence is the pivot, the sentence at the turn from construction to pressure, and it should be a hinge with an author on it: the strongest pressure on this construction comes from Hartmann, who argues that its categories cannot see the relations they most need to explain. Hinges with authors are the visible joints of top band scripts.

Verdicts have their own grammar. A strong verdict is short, specific, and proportioned: it says what the account explains, what it fails to explain, and what the later literature repaired, in three clauses or fewer. Vague verdicts gesture at complexity; strong verdicts distribute praise and blame across named components. The candidate should also learn the verdict of rejection in its honourable form, since this guide has insisted that rejection can earn full marks: the account fails at its foundation, because the reproduction it describes is governed by relations its categories treat as secondary, and the dual systems alternative, for all its own unresolved problems, describes the evidence with less remainder. That is a model rejection. It names the point of failure, concedes the alternative's problems, and gives a criterion, remainder, by which the comparison was judged. A marker can disagree with it and must still credit it.

### What is the difference between a verdict and a summary wearing a verdict's clothes?

Direction of travel. A summary looks backward over the answer and restates it; a verdict looks at the position and disposes of it, accepting, repairing, or rejecting it for stated reasons. Scripts end weakly when the final paragraph could be moved to the introduction without loss. A verdict cannot be moved, because it depends on everything the answer has done.

Candidates should also prepare the sentence that handles the question's wording when the wording is loaded. Examiners sometimes embed a contestable premise in the stem, asking candidates to assess a claim that the tradition neglects gender, or that the family serves capital, and the professional response is to mark the premise as a premise in the opening: the answer treats the embedded claim as the proposition to be tested rather than as common ground. That small act of framing separates candidates who read questions from candidates who obey them, and on the neglect question in particular it is the difference between an answer that evaluates the charge and an answer that merely pleads to it.

## The assessment contexts: one topic, many papers

Feminist perspectives are a traveller among examination contexts, and the candidate benefits from knowing how the same material is dressed in each. In school sociology, the perspectives appear as a named theory topic and then recur inside the substantive units, so the candidate meets them twice: once as doctrine, once as application. The doctrine questions reward the matrix in its pure form. The unit questions reward the portable elements, and markers in the units are often marking dozens of scripts quickly, which raises the value of a crisp framework sentence at the head of each application paragraph. In gender studies and women's studies papers, the expectations invert in an instructive way: the traditions are the home ground rather than a visiting theory, exposition is assumed to run deeper, and the discriminating work shifts almost entirely to evaluation and to the handling of internal disputes, the dual systems controversy above all. Candidates crossing from sociology into such papers should deepen the theorist directory and expect questions that presuppose the domestic labour debate rather than asking for its summary.

Politics and political theory papers examine the perspectives as theories of power, and their questions lean toward the state, toward ideology, and toward the comparison with other accounts of domination. There, the Marxist feminist material should be prepared with its state mechanism at the front, and the comparison partners widen beyond the feminist traditions to include pluralist and elite accounts of where power sits. Civil service optional papers in sociology examine the topic at greater length and with an appetite for the scholarly literature in its own right; the candidate there is expected to know the debate's stages, to handle Vogel and the restatement, and to write at a length where the four movements can be given their full proportions. National eligibility tests, which select for teaching and research, examine the topic through its texts and controversies, and reward precisely the name and claim discipline this guide teaches, since their questions often turn on attributing a position correctly to its author.

### How does a candidate adapt one revision stock to several examination contexts?

By changing the proportions, not the stock. The primacy distinction, the mechanisms, the theorists with their claims, and the objections with their authors serve every context named here. What varies is the depth of exposition expected and the weight of evaluation demanded, and the candidate who knows the context's proportions can reallocate a single well built stock of material across papers without new learning.

One adaptation deserves emphasis because it is so often missed. In contexts where the topic is examined alongside empirical studies, candidates are tempted to lead with findings and let the theory trail behind as commentary. The perspectives questions, even in empirical dress, are marked as theory questions: the findings are the occasion, and the credit sits in the framework brought to them. The candidate should therefore reverse the instinct, stating the framework's claim about the domain first and admitting the findings as evidence for or against it. That order also protects against the orphaned statistic problem, since findings admitted as evidence arrive with their sources in the literature attached, while findings quoted as decoration arrive naked.

## Depth study: the family in the literature

Because the family row of the matrix is examined so heavily, candidates benefit from a deeper map of the literature that row compresses. The Marxist feminist account of the family was assembled in layers. The founding layer is Engels: the family as a historical institution, its monogamous form bound to property and inheritance, its internal relation described in the vocabulary of class. The second layer is the domestic labour analysis, which relocated the family's economic significance from inheritance to production, arguing that the household's unpaid work is the hidden foundation on which the wage economy rests. The third layer is the ideological analysis, associated with Barrett and others, which examined how the family forms consciousness: how it naturalises hierarchy, teaches the acceptance of authority, and reconciles its members to the order outside its doors, while also being, as its defenders insist, a site of genuine care and resistance to market values, a doubleness the sophisticated literature holds in a single frame.

Evaluation of the family account draws on tensions among the layers themselves. The ideological analysis sits uneasily beside the economic one when either claims priority, and the history of the debate shows writers negotiating the junction in different ways. Delphy's domestic mode of production challenged the second layer's treatment of the household's beneficiary. Feminist historians challenged the founding layer's sequence and its inattention to women's work outside the home in earlier economies. Each challenge is a paragraph in an evaluation answer, and the candidate who can present the challenges as the literature's internal development, rather than as a pile of external refutations, writes the family answer at its most creditable.

### Why does the family attract more examination questions than any other institution in this field?

Because every tradition in the comparison has a developed doctrine of it, every mechanism in the theory passes through it, and every candidate has experience of some version of it to be disciplined by the literature. It is the field's natural examination ground, the place where doctrine, mechanism, and experience can be tested against each other in a single answer.

The family's doubleness, as both an economic institution and a lived refuge, is the point at which evaluation answers most often become crude, and it is worth a candidate's deliberate care. Accounts that reduce the family to a function for capital cannot explain why its members defend it; accounts that celebrate it as a haven cannot explain the distribution of labour and authority inside it. The literature's strongest writers hold both truths, and the strongest answers imitate them: the institution can be, at once, the site where labour power is produced at no wage and the site where people experience solidarities they find nowhere else, and a theory is not refuted by the second truth nor excused by the first. A verdict that acknowledges the doubleness and then judges the account's handling of it has the texture of the scholarship itself, which is the deepest compliment a marker can pay a script and the quality the top band descriptors are groping to name.

## Depth study: paid work and the segregated labour market

The work unit gives Marxist feminism its most empirical examination territory, and candidates should command the argument's shape in detail. The starting observation is the patterning of paid employment: women clustered in a narrow band of occupations, disproportionately in caring, clerical, and service roles, and underrepresented where pay and authority concentrate. The liberal explanation reaches for barriers and choices. The Marxist feminist explanation reaches for structure: the labour market is segmented, the segments are sex typed, and the typing is maintained by interests. Employers benefit from a divided workforce and from the availability of labour whose wage can be set against the assumption of a household maintained by another earner. Male workers, on Hartmann's account, have historically defended the better segments against female competition, through union rules, apprenticeship controls, and the family wage demand. The household completes the circuit, producing workers already sorted by the expectation of their adult roles.

Evaluation of that account has its own named stations. The human capital reply holds that occupational patterns reflect accumulated choices about training and continuity of employment, and must be answered at the level of why the choices are patterned as they are. The dual systems reading and the unitary reading diverge over the male workers' role: beneficiaries of a separate patriarchal system, or participants in a single system whose reproduction assigns them their defensive part. The historical evidence, craft exclusions and marriage bars and their abolition, is the ground on which that divergence can be argued concretely, and concrete argument is what the work questions reward. Candidates should prepare the marriage bar as a specimen episode: a formal rule, in documented employments, requiring women to resign on marriage, whose existence is difficult for purely choice based accounts to digest and whose abolition tracks both feminist campaigning and labour market need.

### How should a candidate present occupational segregation without citing figures?

As a patterned distribution described in words: clustering, concentration, and the direction of the gradient between the sexes' typical occupations and their rewards. The literature's qualitative claims are strong and stable across decades of measurement disputes, and a candidate who states the pattern qualitatively, then names the mechanisms proposed to explain it, writes an answer no measurement quarrel can touch.

The caring occupations deserve a paragraph in any work answer of ambition, because they are where the theory's two spheres visibly interlock. The skills exercised in paid care are the skills of the household, transferred into employment and discounted in both places by the same logic: work coded as natural to women is priced as though it required no training and carried no scarcity. The Marxist feminist account reads that discount as the wage form of the reproduction relation, the market importing the household's valuation of the labour it gets free. A candidate who can make that connection in an answer has unified the work unit with the family unit in a single move, and synoptic connections of that kind are the currency of the highest tariffs. The evaluation follows from the same paragraph: critics ask whether the discount reflects the organisation of reproduction or the bargaining weakness of the occupations' workers for other reasons, and the candidate who stages the question has again manufactured an evaluative paragraph from the exposition itself.

## Depth study: education and the preparation of carers

In the education unit, feminist perspectives are examined through the sorting of pupils and the content of what is taught, and each tradition brings a distinctive instrument. The liberal instrument is access: who gets in, who is steered away, which subjects close. The radical instrument is the analysis of schooling as a patriarchal institution, its hierarchies and its discipline of girls. The Marxist feminist instrument is the connection between schooling and reproduction: education as part of the apparatus by which labour power is produced at the standard the economy requires, differentiated by class and sex together. On that account, the curriculum's division, academic against caring and technical against domestic, rehearses the division of adult labour, and the school's hidden lessons in punctuality, deference, and care prepare different pupils for different stations in the twin spheres of production and reproduction.

Candidates applying the framework to education should select the mechanism that touches the item. An item on subject choice calls for the rehearsal argument. An item on achievement calls for the account's explanation of why girls' success in schooling does not convert into equivalent positions in employment, which runs through the segregation of the labour market and the continuing assignment of care. An item on the curriculum's content calls for the ideological analysis, the representation of women's work in the materials from which children learn. Each application is strengthened by the comparative gesture: a sentence acknowledging what the liberal account would notice in the same item, access and its formal removal, before the Marxist feminist claim is pressed. The gesture costs little and signals to the marker that the candidate is applying a framework by choice among frameworks, which is the operation the unit examines.

### What is the commonest way education applications go wrong?

By writing the family answer in a school setting. The candidate reproduces the reproduction account entire and mentions a classroom in the final sentence. The repair is the contact discipline: every paragraph must contain the item's material and the framework's vocabulary in conversation, and a paragraph that could be transplanted to another unit without alteration has failed the application test whatever its quality as exposition.

Education also supplies the topic's most accessible evaluation exchange. The reproduction reading of schooling predicts a correspondence between the school's organisation and the economy's needs, and critics have long objected that correspondence is too easily asserted and too loosely specified: schools resist, pupils negotiate, and outcomes diverge from any simple functional script. That objection, the resistance critique, transfers directly from the wider Marxist sociology of education, and candidates who know it from that context should redeploy it here, since examiners reward the candidate who can carry an objection across the units of a specification. The reply, that reproduction is a tendency contested in practice rather than a script performed without friction, is equally portable, and the pair together give the candidate an evaluation engine that runs in several units on a single tank of revision.

## Depth study: crime, welfare, and the regulation of mothers

The crime unit is where feminist perspectives are examined least often and where a prepared candidate can therefore gain unusual ground, since the cohort's answers thin out noticeably. The Marxist feminist contribution has two strands. The first concerns the treatment of women who offend: the double standard by which female lawbreaking is judged as a failure of femininity as well as a breach of law, drawing sanctions and commentary that male offenders escape, and the related pattern by which women's victimisation is discounted when the victim departs from the expected script of respectable life. The second strand concerns the welfare state's supervision of reproduction: benefits, housing, and child welfare administered through judgements about mothers, so that the state's support for the renewal of labour power arrives with discipline attached. Both strands are applications of the reproduction framework, and the candidate should present them as such, showing the framework generating findings in a domain its founders did not address, which is evidence of a theory's fertility and a sophisticated point in any evaluation of it.

The evaluation in the crime unit runs through the framework's silences as much as through its claims. Offending by women, on the occasions when the literature addresses it directly, has sometimes been read through the same economic lens as everything else, and critics ask whether the framework can accommodate the expressive and situational character of much crime without flattening it. The candidate who raises that question, and answers it by distinguishing the framework's institutional claims, where it is strong, from its motivational claims, where it is thin, demonstrates the discriminating use of a theory rather than its wholesale acceptance or rejection. Discriminating use is the disposition the entire topic examines under its various question forms, and the crime unit, precisely because it is peripheral, tests whether the candidate's discrimination is a habit or a performance staged only on the well rehearsed central ground.

## The tradition's self criticism: a short history candidates can use

A candidate's evaluation improves when the tradition is understood as a sequence of self corrections rather than as a monument with critics outside it. The sequence, in compressed form, runs as follows. The founding texts located women's subordination in history and property. The generation of the domestic labour debate relocated it in the household's relation to the wage, and argued about the exact form of that relation with a precision that still instructs. The dual systems proposal then challenged the relocation from within, arguing that the household's economics could not explain the whole pattern and that patriarchy required its own theory. The materialist feminists of the French tradition challenged it from another side, treating the household as a direct system of appropriation. Barrett and others pressed the question of ideology and of the tradition's own neglect. And the social reproduction restatement gathered the surviving claims into a wider frame, the production of life itself, in which the earlier mechanisms find their places as parts of a larger organisation.

Presented this way, the history is itself an evaluation resource. Each stage exists because the previous stage attracted an objection it could not fully answer, so a candidate who knows the sequence possesses the objections in their biographical form: to know why dual systems was proposed is to know the objection to the unitary account, stated by the person who felt its force most sharply. Examiners reward that form of knowledge because it cannot be faked by a list of criticisms; it shows the theory as a going concern, argued over by its own adherents, and it supports the sophisticated verdict that the tradition's history is evidence both of its difficulties and of its vitality.

### Why is the internal history worth more marks than an external list of flaws?

Because it demonstrates that the candidate understands what each objection was for. An external list records that criticisms exist; the internal history shows the candidate which claim each criticism amended and what the amendment cost. Markers read the second as scholarship and the first as inventory, and they mark the difference.

The sequence also protects candidates against a subtle examination trap: the question that invites a verdict on the tradition as a whole. Candidates who know only the founding texts deliver a verdict on a theory its own side amended decades ago, and the verdict, whatever its merits, is addressed to an absent defendant. Candidates who know the sequence can specify the defendant precisely: the verdict falls on the dual systems settlement, or on the restatement, or on the founding claim about property, and specificity of address is once again the quality the descriptors are reaching for. The tradition's self criticism, properly learned, is therefore not an ornament on the answer. It is the answer's targeting system.

## Advanced evaluation: the objections from method

Beyond the named objections shelved in the evaluation library stand two objections of method that operate across the whole field, and the strongest candidates should be able to deploy either. The first is the objection from functionalism in its general form, already met in the family context: a theory that explains institutions by the needs they serve must show the feedback that keeps them serving, or stand convicted of storytelling. Its general application to Marxist feminism asks whether the account of reproduction explains the variation among capitalist societies, whose households and labour markets differ considerably while their modes of production are shared. Variation is the functionalist's hardest test, and a candidate who puts the theory to it, and reports honestly where the theory answers with mediating institutions and where it gestures, writes evaluation of a genuinely scholarly order.

The second objection concerns the theory's handling of desire and identity: that an account built on labour and its organisation explains the distribution of work better than it explains the meanings attached to femininity and masculinity, the wants people form, and the identifications through which subordination is lived from inside. The ideological layer of the literature was built partly in response, and the candidate can present the exchange as another instance of the tradition repairing itself under pressure. The residual question, whether a materialist framework can in principle generate an account of subjectivity or must always borrow one, is a fitting summit for an evaluation answer, since it is open, it is real, and it divides scholars whose names the candidate can cite on both sides.

### What makes an objection methodological rather than merely contrary?

Its target. A contrary objection disputes a finding; a methodological objection disputes the instrument that produced the finding, and therefore every finding the instrument will ever produce. Candidates who can aim one objection of each kind in an evaluation answer show range as well as depth, and range at altitude is the profile of the very strongest scripts in this field.

Candidates should be warned against a misuse of methodological objections: deploying them as universal solvents. A functionalism charge sprayed over every paragraph dissolves the candidate's own exposition along with the theory, since the exposition was built from the theory's claims. The craft is dosage. One methodological objection, aimed at the answer's most developed claim, at the point where evaluation properly begins, with the literature's reply given its due. Dosage is what separates the scholar's scepticism from the debater's, and markers, who read hundreds of scripts, develop an exact sense for the difference.

## A worked evaluation: following one answer from plan to verdict

Walkthroughs teach what precepts cannot, so this section follows a single evaluation answer at the level of decisions. The question invites assessment of how Marxist feminism accounts for the family. The plan, made in the first minutes, selects the matrix's third row and fixes its sequence: lead with the household as the place where labour power is produced and renewed outside the wage relation; anchor with Benston for the mechanism and Delphy for the internal challenge; evaluate through the functionalism charge and the diversity objection; close with the social reproduction repair and a verdict that distributes credit. Already the plan has done the answer's hardest work, because every paragraph now has an assignment and the evaluation is guaranteed its share of the script.

The opening executes the location movement in two sentences, stating the account by its mechanism and signalling the test to come. The construction movement then spends its paragraphs on the mechanism itself: the production of labour power in the household, the wage's concealment of that production, and the ideological work by which the arrangement is naturalised. A specimen sentence from the middle of the construction shows the register the movement aims at: the household in this account is not a refuge from the economy but one of its departments, distinguished by paying no wages and therefore by rendering its labour invisible to the categories the economy uses to see itself. That sentence is analysis in miniature, because it contains the claim, the mechanism, and the sting, and a marker underlines sentences of that density.

The pivot arrives on schedule and carries its author: the sharpest pressure on this construction comes from inside materialist feminism, from Delphy's argument that the account looks past the household's immediate beneficiary. Two paragraphs run the objection and the exchange it provoked, and the functionalism charge is then aimed at what remains standing, with its demand for mechanisms answered in part. The verdict, when it comes, is three clauses long. The account explains the invisibility of domestic labour better than any rival; it underexplains the household's internal distribution of power, where Delphy's correction stands; and its successor theory repairs the first weakness by widening the frame while inheriting the second as an open problem. Nothing in that verdict could have been written before the answer's work was done, which is the test this guide proposed for distinguishing verdicts from summaries.

### If the evaluation runs long and the verdict is squeezed, what then?

Protect the verdict by abbreviation, not by omission. A two clause verdict honestly reached outscores a missing one, and the movements already completed carry the evidence the short verdict points to. Candidates should practise closing in fifty words as a separate drill, since the skill of the compressed verdict is distinct from the skill of the full one and examinations reward both.

The walkthrough generalises. Every evaluation answer in this field is built by the same decisions: row, anchors, objection, reply, residue, verdict. Candidates who have made those decisions once, slowly, in revision, find they can make them in minutes under examination conditions, and the answers they produce have the finished quality that markers describe as assured. Assurance is not a temperament. It is the visible residue of decisions taken in the right order, and it can be trained by exactly the walkthrough method this section models, applied to each row of the matrix in turn.

## Command words: the contract written on the paper

The guide has treated command words as contracts, and the treatment deserves a section of its own, because misreading the contract is the one error that can waste an otherwise strong stock of knowledge entire. The representational family, explain, outline, describe, identify, asks for the perspective stated faithfully and in its strong form; the candidate's views are not merely unwelcome there, they are unmarked, consuming time that statement needed. The analytical family, analyse, examine, discuss, asks for the material taken apart: mechanisms distinguished, premises exposed, consequences traced, with judgement present but held in reserve. The evaluative family, assess, evaluate, to what extent, critically examine, asks for judgement as the deliverable, built on statement and objection in the proportions the movements prescribe. A candidate who sorts the verb before sorting the content has performed the single highest value act of the first minute, ahead even of the matrix row, because the row supplies material while the verb supplies the operation the material must undergo.

Specifications differ in their habits, and part of revision for a specific paper is learning its board's dialect: which verbs it uses for which operations, what its tariffs signal, how its exemplars behave. Candidates preparing for several boards should build the translation explicitly, since a verb that buys judgement in one dialect may buy only analysis in another. The guide's rule survives translation: read the item as a purchase order, deliver the operation named, and let the matrix supply the goods.

### Why do strong candidates still misread command words under pressure?

Because preparation stocks content more thoroughly than operations, and under pressure candidates deliver the stock they have. The repair is to drill operations directly: take one body of material and write three openings to three verbs on it, so the hands learn the difference between stating, dissecting, and judging the same position. Fifteen minutes of that drill teaches what hours of additional reading cannot.

There is a courtesy embedded in the contract reading that candidates should not miss. The examiner who set an evaluative item at the highest tariff has built the paper's summit, and markers approach those scripts expecting the field's best arguments. Arriving at the summit with the strongest objection in the literature, fairly stated and fairly answered, is a form of respect between strangers that the marking recognises instantly. Arriving with a survey of everything the candidate knows, by contrast, tells the marker the summit was mistaken for a warehouse. The matrix exists to prevent that mistake, and its rows, studied against the verbs, are the whole method in miniature.

## The paragraph clinic: sentences before and after the repair

Abstract standards become usable when candidates watch them operate on actual sentences, so this clinic takes the faults of the earlier sections at sentence level. The ranking fault appears in the wild as follows: Marxist feminists believe class is the main oppression and gender comes second. The repair does not polish the sentence; it replaces its logic. Marxist feminism holds that gender relations acquire their contemporary shape from the way reproduction is organised under capital, so that explanation runs through that organisation rather than through a separate hierarchy of harms. The repaired sentence is longer, and its length is the point: the primacy claim contains an explanation, and explanations take more words than rankings. Candidates who find their prose growing as they revise should take encouragement, since on this topic compression is usually the sign of a slogan displacing an argument.

The temperament fault appears as the claim that radical feminists take a more extreme approach to men. The repair restores the theory: radical feminism treats male domination as a system prior to class society, with its own institutions and its own history, which is why its adherents hold that transforming the economy alone would leave the system standing. Note what the repair does to the comparison: the repaired sentence can be set beside the Marxist claim and the two can be argued, while the original could only be agreed or disagreed with emotionally. Examiners mark arguable sentences, because arguable sentences are the units of analysis.

The garnish fault appears as a sentence listing Benston, Hartmann, Barrett, and Walby as leading Marxist feminists who have written on the family. Two of the four are misfiled, all four are claimless, and the sentence earns nothing while announcing that its author cannot be trusted with the directory. The repair spends the same names properly: Benston's account of housework producing labour power outside the wage; Hartmann's objection that the categories achieving that account remain blind to sex; Walby's rival architecture of patriarchal structures. Three sentences replace one, and the paragraph that results is the difference between a bibliography and an education.

### What single habit produces most of the repairs in this clinic?

Attaching. Attaching a claim to every name, a mechanism to every institution, and an author to every objection. The faults are all forms of detachment, names without claims, institutions without mechanisms, criticisms without authors, and the habit of attachment, once formed, prevents the faults at the moment of writing rather than at the cost of revision.

The clinic's final patient is the verdict that concludes that both perspectives have valid points and the truth is somewhere in between. Its repair names the ground of decision. The account of the family stands where its mechanism is specified, in the renewal of labour power, and falls where its beneficiary is misidentified, in the household's internal relations; between Delphy's correction and the restatement that absorbed it, the candidate's judgement is that the theory survives as a research programme rather than as a settled doctrine. That is a verdict with an address, and a marker can locate it, weigh it, and credit it, none of which could be done for the fog it replaces.

## Longer work: essays, projects, and the dissertation seed

Not every assessment in this field is a timed paper, and the guide's method transfers to the longer forms with adjustments worth stating. In the coursework essay, the movements expand and the library deepens: the candidate is expected to read the theorists in the original where feasible, to quote sparingly and exactly, and to let the evaluation range across the whole library rather than the two or three objections a timed answer can carry. The primacy distinction remains the load bearing wall, and the current-form move remains the highest return addition, since markers of coursework are even likelier than examination markers to know the recent literature and to notice its absence. The contested figures protocol applies with greater force in coursework, because the candidate has the library open and no excuse of the clock: figures must arrive with their sources and their measurement choices discussed, or be replaced by the patterns they were borrowed to illustrate.

For the project or dissertation seed, this field offers unusually tractable subjects, and the guide's advice mirrors its advice elsewhere in the series: choose a dispute that evidence or text can settle at the scale the project commands. The measurement disputes are tractable, since a candidate can recompute a published account of domestic labour's value under rival conventions and report how much of the disagreement is bookkeeping. The textual disputes are tractable, since the restatement literature's claims about the classical texts can be checked against the texts. The historical disputes are tractable at small scale, a single industry's marriage bar, a single campaign's archive. What is not tractable at project scale is the primacy question itself, and candidates should be steered away from dissertations that promise to settle which system is primary, toward dissertations that show what follows from each answer in a domain narrow enough to examine whole.

### How does the marking reality change when the work is coursework rather than a timed paper?

It does not change in direction, only in evidence. Coursework markers have the same descriptors and the same divisions among schools, and they have more time to check statements against the literature. The freedom of verdict is identical; the scrutiny of statement is greater. Candidates sometimes treat coursework as the place where allegiance can be shown safely, and the truth is the reverse: it is where allegiance is most easily detected and least rewarded.

Supervisors and teachers reading project work in this field will recognise the guide's apparatus in a student's draft and should welcome it, since the apparatus is simply the scholarly method wearing examination dress: state positions in their strongest form, attribute every objection, and let the verdict arrive when the evidence has been heard. A project built that way can conclude anywhere, including against every perspective it examines, and be defended in a viva without embarrassment, because its author can show the working at every step. That defensibility, in the end, is what all the assessment forms in this field are purchasing under their different tariffs, and the candidate who understands the purchase has understood the topic's examination as completely as the topic itself can be understood in advance of meeting it.

## Marxist feminism and socialist feminism: policing a boundary the questions exploit

The second error of the clinic, the conflation of Marxist and socialist feminism, deserves a fuller treatment because examiners exploit the boundary deliberately. The terms descend from a real divergence. In the debates from which both labels come, socialist feminism was the wider banner, gathering those who held that women's subordination could not be explained by capital alone nor by patriarchy alone and who therefore sought a unified or dual account of the two. Marxist feminism, in the stricter usage, names the position that stays within the theory of capital and locates gender relations inside its reproduction. Many writers, including distinguished ones, use the labels loosely or interchangeably, and candidates are not expected to police usage the literature itself leaves untidy. What candidates are expected to do is notice when a question's wording commits to one usage, and to answer within it while flagging the neighbour.

The technique is a single sentence of framing. Where the specification treats the terms as distinct, the answer opens by adopting the specification's distinction and noting that the labels travel together in much of the literature. Where the question uses one term and the candidate's best material sits under the other, the answer imports the material with its passport shown: the dual systems proposal, usually filed under socialist feminism, enters a Marxist feminism answer as the internal challenge it historically was, attributed and placed, rather than smuggled in as though the filing were uncontested. Markers reward the passport sentence because it demonstrates command of the field's geography, and they penalise the smuggling because it suggests the candidate cannot tell the map's regions apart.

### When a question uses a label loosely, should the candidate correct the examiner?

Never in tone, and once in substance. The candidate adopts the question's usage for the answer's duration, noting the stricter usage in a subordinate clause if the distinction affects the argument. Correction as framing is scholarship; correction as reproof is a misunderstanding of the examination's etiquette, and markers respond to the etiquette as well as to the scholarship.

The boundary matters beyond terminology because it marks the field's central open question in miniature. Whether the explanation of gender requires one system or two is the primacy dispute restated at the level of research programmes, and the labels are its flags. To explain why the flags multiplied, and what each camp feared losing, the Marxist fear of dissolving a material explanation into a general theory of domination, the socialist feminist fear of explaining women's lives with categories built without them, is to have understood the dispute as its participants understood it. Understanding a dispute from inside its participants' fears is the deepest form the primacy distinction can take in an answer, and examiners who set boundary exploiting questions are fishing for exactly that depth.

## Ideology and the naturalisation of the household

One further component of the literature must be in the candidate's stock, because questions on ideology and the family recur and because the component completes the theory's architecture. The claim is that the arrangement by which labour power is renewed without wages is sustained partly by being made to seem natural: the division of labour in the household presents itself as an expression of nature or of love rather than as an economic organisation, and that presentation is functional for the arrangement's survival. Barrett's work is the anchor here, with its argument that the ideology of familialism, the ideal of the family as a haven organised around a male breadwinner and a devoted homemaker, does work in the world: it shapes what women expect, what employers assume, and what policies presume, and it survives the decline of the household form it idealises, continuing to organise expectations after the single earner household has become a minority arrangement.

Candidates should handle the ideology claim with the precision the functionalism section taught. The claim is not that someone invented familialism to deceive, nor that every expression of attachment to family life is false consciousness in disguise. The claim is that ideals have effects: an ideal that defines good motherhood as full time presence raises the cost of every alternative arrangement, and an ideal that defines the wage as a family wage when paid to a man and as supplementary when paid to a woman organises the labour market it merely purports to describe. Stated at that level, the ideology argument is testable against the historical record of policy and practice, and the candidate can evaluate it there, noting where ideals lagged behind arrangements and where they led them.

### How does the ideology component change the comparison with radical feminism?

It narrows the distance on one flank and widens it on another. Both traditions analyse how domination is made to seem natural, so their accounts of consciousness converge; they diverge on what the naturalisation serves, a system of male domination in its own right or the organisation of reproduction within capital. A comparison answer that finds that convergence and that divergence in the same paragraph writes at the level the topic's best examiners hope for.

The ideology material also arms the candidate for the media and culture questions that appear in some specifications, where representations of women are the item. The Marxist feminist handling of such items refuses to treat representation as free floating: images and ideals are connected to the material organisation they naturalise, and the analysis asks what arrangement a representation makes seem inevitable. Candidates should resist the temptation, common in media items, to catalogue representations without the connection, since the catalogue without the connection is description of the kind any perspective could produce, and the marks sit in the distinctiveness of the framework's question: not what is shown, but what the showing secures.

## Reading the question paper: how the topic announces itself

Experienced candidates develop a feel for how a paper is built, and this field's papers are built legibly enough to reward the feel. The perspectives topic usually announces itself in a family of stems whose shapes recur: an invitation to explain a named perspective's view of a named institution; an invitation to compare two or three perspectives on shared ground; an invitation to evaluate a claim attributed to a perspective, often in quotation; and an invitation to assess a charge against a tradition, neglect being the standing example. Each shape maps to a matrix row, and the mapping can be practised until it is reflexive. The attributed claim shape deserves a word of its own, because it conceals a decision: the candidate must first decide whether the quotation states the perspective fairly, and the strongest answers spend their opening on that decision, accepting the claim as stated or qualifying it before proceeding. Markers treasure the qualification when it is just, since it shows the candidate reading the question rather than merely answering it.

Tariffs tell the second half of the story. The highest tariff items on a paper are its summits, and on this topic they are almost invariably evaluative in verb and comparative or critical in shape. Candidates allocating revision effort should therefore weight the evaluation library and the comparison method above additional exposition, once exposition is secure: the summit questions cannot be answered from exposition alone, while the foothills questions can be answered from the exposition the summit preparation contains. That asymmetry is the rational core of the matrix's design, and candidates who grasp it stop experiencing the paper as a lottery and start experiencing it as a structure with a known centre of gravity.

### How should the reading minutes before any writing begins be spent?

Sort every question on the paper into its matrix row, mark the summits, and note beside each question the theorist and the objection it will use. The sorting converts the paper from a source of surprises into an inventory of rehearsed operations, and candidates who perform it report the same discovery across cohorts: the paper they feared contains no question they have not, in substance, already answered in revision.

The reading minutes are also where the candidate should catch the paper's deliberate traps, which on this topic are standardised enough to enumerate. The trap of the loose label, where the stem uses socialist and Marxist interchangeably and waits to see whether the candidate notices. The trap of the embedded premise, where the stem asserts the neglect or the service to capital and waits to see whether the candidate tests it or assumes it. The trap of the anachronism, where a question about the classical theory invites an answer that forgets the restatement and judges a superseded form. Each trap, once named in revision, loses its power in the room, and the candidate's opening sentences, which quietly decline each trap, are read by markers as the signatures of the best prepared scripts in the cohort.

## The whole method on one page, in prose

Gather the guide's apparatus into a single statement, since candidates revising in the final days need the method entire and portable. Introduce every perspective through its primary system, never through a ranking of harms. Attach a claim to every theorist named, and an author to every objection raised. Represent each tradition in its strongest present form, which for Marxist feminism means carrying the argument through to social reproduction theory, and grant the same currency to its rivals. Plan every answer from the matrix row the question belongs to, in the four movements of location, construction, pressure, and judgement, and protect the pressure movement when time runs short. Describe patterns rather than quoting figures whose provenance cannot be defended. Read the command word as a contract and deliver the operation it purchases. And conclude wherever the arguments lead, in the knowledge this guide has stated as marking fact: the verdict is free, the statements are graded, and a conclusion against any perspective in the field, reached by the method above, can score full marks.

That paragraph is the guide in miniature, and a candidate who can expand each of its sentences into a paragraph of practice has internalised the apparatus. The expansion is the revision, and it is finite: nine sentences, each with its drill, each drill an afternoon at most. Fields that appear boundless from the library steps resolve, at the examination door, into a small number of moves executed well, and this topic, for all the passion of its literature and the depth of its disputes, is no exception. The passion and the depth are what the moves carry. The moves themselves can be learned, and learning them is the candidate's whole task.

## Before capital, beyond capital: the scope objection and its handling

Every candidate in this field eventually meets the scope objection, and should meet it first in revision, on friendly terms. The objection observes that women's subordination is documented in societies that precede capitalism by millennia and persisted in societies that abolished private capital in the last century, and asks what a theory of capitalist reproduction can say to either fact. It is the strongest of the external objections, because its evidence is the whole of recorded history, and candidates who wave at it with a sentence about feudalism or state policy lose the exchange in front of the marker. The literature's serious handling has three parts, and the candidate should be able to reproduce all three and then judge their sufficiency.

The first part is a distinction of claims. The theory, carefully stated, explains the form subordination takes within capitalism, not the existence of subordination in every social order; a theory of the modern form is not refuted by the antiquity of the genus, any more than a theory of the modern state is refuted by ancient kingship. The second part is a historical claim in its own right: that capitalism reorganised an inheritance, taking a subordination it found and rebuilding it around the separation of home from workplace and the wage form, so that the modern pattern is genuinely new in its mechanisms even where its outcomes rhyme with older ones. The third part concedes the residue: the persistence of subordination where capital was abolished is the harder datum, and the tradition's answers, that those societies retained the household's organisation and much of the wage form's surrounding structure, are available but contestable, and a candidate may honestly conclude that the residue stands. That three part handling, distinction, reorganisation, concession, converts the field's most dangerous objection into the candidate's most impressive paragraph, and the conversion is available to candidates of every verdict, since the concession can be made the ground of rejection or the boundary of a defended theory with equal legitimacy.

### Why is conceding a residue worth more than defeating an objection outright?

Because examiners mark judgement, and a concession correctly placed is judgement in its purest visible form. The candidate who concedes the residue shows the marker exactly where their allegiance to the evidence overrides their sympathy for any side, and that showing is the disposition the whole assessment exists to detect and reward.

The scope objection also disciplines the candidate's use of the founding texts. Engels wrote as though the origin of subordination and its modern form were a single problem, soluble by a single history, and the modern literature's separation of the two problems is one of its genuine advances. Candidates who present Engels should therefore present him as the founder of the question rather than as the supplier of its answer, a framing that honours the text, satisfies the caveat requirement, and aligns the candidate with the literature's own mature judgement. Origin questions and form questions require different evidence, and an answer that keeps them separate writes history of ideas at a level most scripts never attempt.

## A glossary in prose: the terms the markers listen for

Certain terms function in this field as shibboleths, words whose correct handling tells a marker the candidate has read the literature rather than its summaries, and candidates should be able to deploy each in a sentence that defines it by use. Labour power is the capacity to work, sold by the worker and consumed by the employer, and the hinge of the whole theory is that its production happens elsewhere than its consumption. Reproduction, in the theory's special sense, is the set of processes by which labour power and life itself are renewed, daily and across generations. The domestic labour debate is the argument over the status of household work within the theory of value. Dual systems names the settlement in which patriarchy and capital are analysed as two interacting systems; unitary names the refusal of that settlement. The reserve army is the pool of potential workers held outside employment and drawn on as accumulation requires. Familialism is the ideology that represents a particular household form as natural and universal. Social reproduction theory is the restatement that gathers these terms into a single frame centred on the production of life.

The glossary matters because markers listen for terms the way examiners in every field listen for the vocabulary of the discipline: not as decoration, but as evidence that concepts have been acquired with their relations intact. A candidate who writes reproduction and means childbirth has failed a listening test; a candidate who writes it and means the renewal of labour power and life, in households and institutions, has passed one. The drills of the earlier sections should therefore include the glossary as sentences, each term carried in a clause that uses it correctly, until the usage is as automatic as the names and claims.

### How many technical terms should an answer carry?

As many as the argument needs and no more. A paragraph dense with terms and empty of mechanisms reads as incantation. The test is substitution: where a term can be replaced by its definition without loss, the candidate understands it; where the replacement collapses the sentence, the term was doing decorative work, and markers have a practiced ear for the collapse.

Candidates should also know the terms' contested edges, since the edges are where evaluation lives. Reproduction itself is contested between a narrow sense tied to labour power and a wide sense covering the renewal of life and community, and the precision objection is precisely the charge that the wide sense buys comprehensiveness at the price of analytic edge. A candidate who can state that contest in a sentence, naming it as the field's live internal question, possesses the glossary at the level the summits require: not a list of definitions but a map of where the definitions are fought over.

## What the examiners' guidance teaches, read as a candidate should read it

Across the systems that examine this topic, the published guidance to markers and the reports on candidates' performance repeat a small set of observations with remarkable consistency, and a candidate who treats those observations as a syllabus for revision gains an advantage the textbook cannot supply. The reports praise scripts that define perspectives by their analytical claims rather than by their attitudes. They lament the procession comparison, in which perspectives are described in turn and compared nowhere. They note the theorist named without a proposition and the criticism voiced without an author, in language so regular that candidates can set the phrases beside the clinic section of this guide and watch them match. They reward, in the reports' own recurring formula, answers that show awareness of debate within and between traditions, which is the bureaucratic phrasing of this guide's insistence on internal history and the current form. And they return, year after year, to the observation that evaluation is attempted by assertion in most scripts and performed by argument in few, which is the whole doctrine of the named objection stated from the marking side of the desk.

Reading the reports teaches a further lesson about proportion that candidates resist and should not. The reports consistently indicate that the difference between the middle and the top of the range is rarely more content; it is the same content, organised by an axis, stated at full strength, and evaluated through names. Candidates respond to mediocre practice marks by learning more material, when the reports are telling them to organise the material they have. The matrix is this guide's answer to that instruction. A candidate with a modest stock, perfectly organised, will outscore a candidate with a large stock in procession order on every question type this topic sets, and the reports, read carefully, say so in terms.

### Where should a candidate obtain the examiners' perspective if reports are not to hand?

From the literature's own controversies, which teach the same lesson in a different voice. A field whose participants insist on attributing objections and stating rivals fairly is training its students in exactly the habits its examiners reward, because the examiners were formed by the same literature. Reading Hartmann against her respondents is a marking seminar conducted by other means.

There is a final report observation worth banking, concerning the scripts that fail outright on this topic. They fail, overwhelmingly, not by arguing the wrong side but by not arguing at all: by retelling a perspective's history as though the question had asked for a chronicle, or by answering a neighbouring question the candidate had prepared instead of the one set. The discipline of the reading minutes, sorting the actual question into its row and verb, is the prophylactic against both failures, and it costs less time than any other practice this guide recommends. Candidates who do nothing else differently after reading this guide should do that: read the paper as a set of purchases, and sell it exactly what it orders.

## The French materialist detour: Delphy and the household as a system of appropriation

Delphy's work has appeared in this guide as an objection, and it rewards a section of its own, because candidates who know it only as a name in the evaluation library underuse the most distinctive materialist alternative the field contains. Her starting point was a refusal: the refusal to treat the household as a unit with a single interest, as both common sense and much Marxist writing treated it. Inside the household, she argued, labour is performed under relations as definite as any in the factory: the wife's work, in production for the household's use and often in family enterprises, is appropriated by the husband as head of the household, and the marriage contract is the instrument of the appropriation. From that refusal she built the domestic mode of production, a sphere with its own relations, coexisting with the capitalist mode and articulated with it, in which the exploitation of women is direct rather than mediated by capital's distant requirements.

The theoretical yield for examinations is considerable. Delphy's construction explains what the reproduction account leaves vague: the beneficiary. Where the classic account traces housework's product to capital through the wage, Delphy's stops at the household's door and names the man who receives the meals, the laundered clothes, and the raised children, and whose labour power is therefore renewed at a price he does not pay. The construction also generates a distinctive politics and a distinctive prediction: that the arrangement survives changes in the surrounding economy as long as marriage's labour relation survives, which the scope evidence can be mustered to support. The replies from the reproduction tradition are equally definite: that a mode of production requires more than a relation of appropriation, that the domestic sphere produces no surplus in the technical sense and accumulates nothing, and that the household's terms are themselves set by the wage economy outside it. The exchange is a complete evaluative engine, and it is entirely internal to materialist feminism, which is why examiners prize it.

### How should a candidate position Delphy relative to the tradition she criticised?

As a materialist correcting materialism. Her method is the tradition's own, historical and concerned with labour and appropriation, and her quarrel is with its object: she relocates exploitation from the system's summit to the household's interior. Candidates who file her as an external critic misplace the field's most instructive internal border.

The French tradition around Delphy also supplies the candidate with the concept of sex classes, the proposition that men and women constitute classes in a strict sense within the domestic mode, defined by their opposed relations to domestic labour. Whether or not the candidate endorses the proposition, knowing it sharpens the primacy discussion, since it is the boldest available attempt to give patriarchy the analytical form of a mode of production rather than the looser form of a system of attitudes. Set beside Hartmann's dual systems, it shows the non unitary options differing among themselves: two systems interacting, or two modes articulated. A comparison answer that can distinguish those options has a second storey, and second storeys are what distinguish the merely excellent scripts from the celebrated ones in markers' training sets.

## Difference within the theory: class, household form, and the limits of the single model

A standing evaluation of the whole tradition, which candidates should be able to state and answer, is that its classic model describes one household, the industrial working class household of a male earner and a full time homemaker, and generalises from it beyond its warrant. Households in the record are various: households where women have always worked for wages alongside their domestic labour, households organised around extended kin rather than the nuclear pair, households headed by women, households whose relation to capital runs through land, trade, or profession rather than through a factory wage. The objection from diversity asks whether a theory built on the wage and the homemaker can see those households at all, or whether it mistakes a historically specific arrangement, itself partly an ideal rather than a universal reality, for the structure of gender under capital as such.

The tradition's resources for an answer are real and should be stated as strongly as the objection. The theory's defenders reply that the model names a tendency and a norm, not a census: the breadwinner household functioned as the arrangement employers, states, and unions organised around, and its ideological force, treated in the section on familialism, shaped households that never matched it, including households where women's wages were indispensable and were nonetheless classified as supplementary. On that reading, the diversity of practice is compatible with the unity of the organising norm, and the theory explains both the norm and the strain of living at a distance from it. The candidate's judgement can then be genuinely discriminating: the reply succeeds for the ideology and the labour market, and is weaker for the organisation of kin based production, where the wage is not the pivot and the theory must borrow from anthropology it has not always handled well.

### What does the diversity exchange teach about evaluating a theory generally?

That the unit of evaluation is the claim, not the theory. A framework is a bundle of claims with different scopes, and the discriminating verdict assigns each claim its fate separately: this one holds as stated, this one holds as a tendency, this one fails outside the cases it was built from. Verdicts on whole theories are the examination's version of the ranking error, a single judgement where the material supports several.

Candidates revising the diversity exchange should attach it to the family row of the matrix, where it serves as the second objection after functionalism, and to the neglect row, where it connects with the charge that the tradition universalised from a narrow base. Connected objections are worth more than isolated ones, because a marker watching a candidate carry an objection across question types sees a mind with a map of the field rather than a shelf of separate notes. The map, once more, is the thing being marked, and every section of this guide has been an attempt to draw it from a different side.

## Specimen openings: five rows, five first paragraphs

Candidates learn openings by imitation more readily than by precept, so this section supplies the first paragraph of an answer for each row of the matrix, in the register the guide has been teaching, for study and adaptation rather than memorisation. The explain row opens with the architecture: Marxist feminism holds that the modern form of women's subordination is produced within the capitalist organisation of reproduction, where the unpaid work of renewing labour power assigns women a position at once indispensable and invisible, and the account is best understood by following that work from the household to the wage it makes possible. The comparison row opens with the axis: the feminist traditions agree that women's subordination is systematic and disagree about the system, liberal feminism locating it in unequal rights and blocked access, radical feminism in male domination as a prior and independent order, and Marxist feminism in the organisation of production and reproduction under capital, and the comparison that follows is organised by that disagreement about foundations.

The family row opens with the institution: Marxist feminism's account treats the household as an economic department of the capitalist order, the place where labour power is made and remade with no wage paid, and its evaluation turns on whether that treatment illuminates the household's interior or obscures it. The application row opens with the portable element: the segregation of the labour market by sex is the point at which the reproduction framework touches the world of paid work, since the household that renews labour power also sorts it, delivering workers to employment already marked by the expectations of the sphere that raised them. The neglect row opens with the charge's origin: the claim that Marxism neglected gender was pressed most searchingly not by the tradition's enemies but by its own feminist adherents, and assessing it means assessing what a tradition owes to the questions it did not ask itself.

### What should a candidate steal from these specimens, and what should be left behind?

Steal the moves, leave the sentences. Each specimen performs location by primary system, signals its evaluation in advance, and commits to a mechanism; those moves transfer to any question in the row. The sentences themselves, memorised and reproduced, would collide with the repetition of a thousand other candidates' reading, and markers discount what they have read before in identical form.

Study the specimens for their economies. None exceeds three sentences before the answer's real work begins. None contains a biographical preamble, a definition copied from a glossary, or a promise of balance. Each could be followed by an answer arguing in any direction, which is the property this guide identified at the outset as the mark of a professional opening. Candidates should write their own specimens for the stems they most fear, in revision, and submit them to the clinic's tests: does the opening rank or locate, does it name a mechanism, does it signal the evaluation to come. An opening that passes those three tests will not guarantee the answer that follows it, but it guarantees the answer a hearing, and in a stack of scripts the hearing is the scarce resource.

## Reading beyond the specification: the bookshelf that builds the current form

The current-form move presupposes reading, and candidates reasonably ask what reading, in what order, with what return. The shelf this guide recommends is deliberately short, since breadth without digestion is the disease the name and claim discipline exists to cure. Begin with the founding text on the family, read for the framing rather than for the anthropology, with the caveat in hand. Continue with Benston's essay on the material basis of housework, which is brief and which contains the mechanism in its purest early form. Hartmann's essay on the unhappy marriage of the two traditions comes next, because it is the objection the whole subsequent literature answers, and no candidate understands the restatement who has not felt the objection's force in the original. Barrett's study of women's oppression in the tradition supplies the ideology material and the neglect charge in their documented form. Vogel's restatement is the shelf's summit for the ambitious, the point where the reproduction argument achieves its rigorous shape, and the later social reproduction essays, Bhattacharya's and Ferguson's among them, bring the shelf to the tradition's present.

Walby's theorising of patriarchy belongs on the shelf beside the Marxist texts rather than opposite them, since the comparison questions require her architecture at full strength, and Delphy's work on the domestic economy completes the internal opposition. A candidate who has read that shelf, eight or nine texts, possesses the field as a set of arguments in conversation, and can staff every row of the matrix from primary reading rather than from summaries of summaries. The difference shows in the texture of scripts: propositions arrive with their qualifications attached, objections arrive with their occasions, and the marker senses a candidate who has been in the room where the arguments happened.

### How should a candidate with very little time triage the shelf?

Read Hartmann and Benston entire, and the restatement literature's clearest single essay, and take the remainder as located references: know where each text sits, what it argues in one sentence, and which row of the matrix it staffs. Triage of that kind is not a confession of failure but the normal condition of examination preparation, and markers reward the honest architecture of a little reading deeply held over the panorama of much reading thinly spread.

The shelf has a discipline of use that matters as much as its contents. Candidates should read with the matrix open beside the text, entering under each row the claims and phrases the text supplies, so that reading converts immediately into examination capital. Reading without the conversion produces the familiar tragedy of the well read candidate whose scripts show none of it, because nothing was ever reformulated as an answer's move. The reformulation is the study. A claim becomes capital when the candidate has written, in their own words, the sentence in which it would appear in an answer, and the bookshelf's purpose is to manufacture those sentences at the highest quality the literature allows.

## Handling texts and quotations: the evidence of having read

Quotations in examination answers are a currency with a strict exchange rate: short, exact, and load bearing, or worthless. The rule of this series, that a quotation must be brief and must do work no paraphrase can do, applies with special force in this field, where the literature's phrases are vivid and therefore tempting. Hartmann's characterisation of the marriage between the traditions is the model case: the phrase is famous, two or three words of it carry the whole objection's flavour, and quoting more than the phrase adds nothing but risk. Candidates should quote only where the wording itself is the evidence, a definition whose terms are disputed, a characterisation whose sting is the point, and should paraphrase everywhere else, since paraphrase demonstrates the understanding that quotation merely borrows.

Textual location is the quieter skill and the more rewarded one. Locating a claim in its work, the essay on the political economy of housework, the chapter on the family in the study of women's oppression, the restatement's opening distinction between the production of goods and the reproduction of labour power, performs the same office for this field that chapter numbers perform for the study of Marx himself: it proves handling. Candidates cannot memorise locations for everything, and should not try; three or four located references, deployed where the argument turns, outweigh a script's worth of floating summary.

### Why is paraphrase usually worth more than quotation in this field?

Because the field's disputes are about concepts, and a paraphrase that preserves a concept's exact scope shows the marker the concept has been understood, while a quotation shows only that it has been encountered. The exception proves the rule: where the scope of a concept is itself the dispute, the author's own words become the evidence, and then the candidate should quote, briefly, and say why the wording matters.

Tone is the last of the textual skills, and the one candidates neglect because nobody teaches it. Scripts in this field should be written in the register of the seminar rather than of the rally or the obituary: level, exact, willing to state any position in its own terms and to judge any position on the evidence rehearsed. Markers do not penalise warmth, but they discount heat, because heat is the stylistic signature of the ranking error and its cousins, the places where feeling has replaced architecture. The candidate's prose should be able to say, in the same level tone, that an account is the most powerful the field possesses and that it fails at a named point, since holding those two judgements in one register is the stylistic form of the whole method this guide has taught.

## Foundations and strategies: what each primary system tells its holders to do

Comparison answers gain a dimension when the candidate follows each foundation into the strategy it implies, since the traditions' practical conclusions are among the clearest evidence of their theoretical difference, and examiners set questions on the connection more often than revision resources admit. If subordination is produced by unequal rights and blocked access, the remedy is reform pursued institution by institution, and the liberal tradition's history is a record of such remedies won. If subordination is the working of a prior system of male domination, the remedy must reach the system's own institutions, the family, sexuality, and the organisation of intimacy, and radical feminism's practical history is a record of attempts to build alternatives in those territories. If subordination is shaped by the way capital organises reproduction, the remedy must transform that organisation: the socialisation of care, the reorganisation of the working day, and the refusal to treat the household's labour as a natural resource, and the Marxist tradition's practical history, from the early communal experiments to the wages campaign, is a record of attempts to name and to change the reproductive arrangement itself.

Stating the strategies does two kinds of examination work. It tests the candidate's grasp of the foundations, since a strategy misassigned, separatism attributed to the liberal account, socialisation of care to the radical, betrays a confusion no amount of exposition can hide. And it supplies a fresh axis for the comparison's second half, where answers otherwise run dry: having compared the traditions on an institution, the candidate compares them on what each would do about it, and the second comparison often reveals differences the first concealed, as when two traditions that diagnose the family similarly diverge entirely on whether it can be reformed or must be superseded.

### Why do examiners value the strategy dimension of a comparison?

Because it is the proof that the candidate understands a theory as a guide to the world rather than as a catechism. A foundation that implies nothing is a label; a foundation that implies a strategy is an argument, and markers can examine an argument at the joint where theory meets consequence, which is where the strongest and weakest scripts part company.

The strategy dimension also provides the honest candidate with a balanced evaluation instrument. Each tradition's strategy has a characteristic embarrassment, and naming the three embarrassments in parallel is a verdict generating exercise: reform's embarrassment is the pattern that survives formal equality; the radical strategy's embarrassment is the difficulty of building alternatives at scale within the order being refused; the Marxist strategy's embarrassment is the historical record of transformations that reorganised production and left the household's division of labour substantially intact. Stating those three embarrassments, in the traditions' own hearing, without relish and without flinching, produces an evaluation section that no marker will mistake for advocacy, and the conclusion that follows from the three embarrassments, whichever tradition it favours, arrives with the authority of a survey completed.

## Connecting the topic outward: feminism within the wider Marxism paper

Candidates meeting Marxist feminism inside a general Marxism paper, rather than inside a sociology specification, face a different embedding, and the guide closes its content sections by marking the connection. In the wider paper, the topic is the standing test of the parent theory's completeness: the question whether a theory of capital can also be a theory of gender is one form of the question whether the theory's categories are as universal as they claim. The candidate in that setting should present the feminist literature as the parent theory's most consequential internal development, continuous with the series' other clusters: as the domestic labour debate stands to the theory of value, so social reproduction stands to the theory of the system as a whole, an attempt to describe the totality the founder's categories approached from the side of production alone. Links of that kind, drawn explicitly, are the substance of synoptic credit where specifications award it, and even where they do not, they organise the candidate's own revision into a single structure rather than a shelf of topics.

The connection runs in the other direction as well. The skills this topic teaches, primacy rather than ranking, the strongest present form, the attributed objection, are the skills of the whole Marxism paper wearing feminist dress, and the candidate who has mastered them here owns them for the questions on the state, on ideology, and on culture that the wider paper sets. Revision invested in this topic is therefore not a diversion from the rest of the syllabus but a concentration of its method, which is one reason the examiners return to it so faithfully: it is the place where the paper's method and the paper's content are the same thing.

### What is the synoptic sentence a candidate should be able to write about this topic?

That the feminist development of Marxism is the clearest case in the whole tradition of a theory being extended by its own adherents to a domain its founder left undertheorised, and that the extension's success and its residues are therefore evidence about the parent theory's powers, not merely about its politics. A candidate who can write that sentence, and then support it from the debate's history, holds the topic at the level the wider paper rewards.

## A fortnight of drills: converting this guide into a timetable

Guides are read in an evening and forgotten in a week unless their contents are converted into exercises, and this section performs the conversion for the candidate with a fortnight of preparation available. The first days belong to the foundations: the primacy distinction written out from memory until it is exact, the three traditions' primary systems stated in one sentence each, and the ranking formulation hunted through the candidate's old essays and expelled wherever it nests. The middle days belong to the stock: the theorist directory drilled as name and claim cards, the evaluation library drilled as objection and reply cards, one matrix row per day worked through a past question in outline. The later days belong to execution: two full answers under time, one comparison and one evaluation, each audited against the six errors and the four movements, and the openings for the five rows composed and tested against the clinic's three questions. The final day belongs to the reading minutes: a whole paper sorted into rows and verbs without a word of answers written, so that the first skill the examination demands is the last one practised.

Candidates with less time scale the fortnight by its logic rather than by its letter. The order is the content: foundations before stock, stock before execution, execution before the reading drill, because each stage presupposes the one before and a candidate who inverts the order, writing timed answers before the distinction is secure, practises the ranking error at speed. Candidates with more time repeat the cycle at a deeper level of the bookshelf, replacing outline answers with full ones and the clearest restatement essay with Vogel herself. The method's claim is not that a fortnight suffices for mastery. It is that a fortnight spent in this order produces a script whose faults are the honourable faults of a developing scholar rather than the transmitted faults of the revision industry, and markers know the difference on sight.

### What is the single most common way candidates waste the final week on this topic?

Learning new content. The final week's marginal hour buys more in drills on material already held, openings composed, objections rehearsed aloud, timed verdicts practised, than in any addition to the stock, because the examination will test the conversion of stock into script, and conversion is a trainable skill distinct from the knowledge it converts.

## Tutorials, vivas, and the spoken defence

Some candidates meet this topic aloud, in tutorials, seminars, and oral examinations, and the guide's method has a spoken form worth stating. The primacy distinction is even more valuable in speech than on paper, because discussion rewards the participant who can locate a disagreement exactly: the tutorial that has been circling the question of whether class or gender matters more dissolves when someone observes that the traditions differ over which system is analytically primary, and that the ranking question may be the one thing none of them is asking. The attributed objection is likewise a spoken discipline: the participant who can say that Hartmann's charge was sex blindness, and that the unitary reply turns on interaction, raises the level of the whole room, and tutors remember the raiser. The current-form move, spoken, is the observation that the debate did not end where the textbook stopped, offered with the restatement's claim in a sentence.

Oral examiners probe differently from paper markers, following a claim to its edge to see whether the candidate knows where it ends. The guide's habits are the defence: concede residues promptly and precisely, since an oral examiner reads a prompt concession as strength and a defended overclaim as the exact fault the viva exists to find. Candidates should prepare, for each of their three favourite claims, the sentence that marks its boundary: this holds for the wage economy's core cases and weakens at the kin based margin; this objection stands against the founding texts and is partly answered by the restatement. Boundary sentences are the spoken form of the discriminating verdict, and a candidate armed with them can enjoy a viva, which is the outcome tutors quietly hope for and so rarely get to see.

### What should a candidate do when an oral examiner presses a figure or a fact not in the stock?

Decline it gracefully and return to the pattern. The protocol that governs written figures governs speech: a candidate who says that the measurement is contested and the direction of the pattern is what the literature agrees on has answered as a scholar, and examiners, who know exactly how contested the measurements are, credit the refusal more than a confident invention would have earned.

## Why this topic endures on the paper

It is worth closing the content of this guide by asking why examiners, across systems and decades, keep setting feminist perspectives with such fidelity, since the answer tells the candidate what the topic is for. The perspectives endure because they test, in a single topic, the complete set of operations assessment exists to certify. They require accurate statement of positions a candidate may not hold. They require comparison along an axis the candidate must construct rather than copy. They require evaluation through the arguments of named scholars, some of whom were the positions' own adherents. And they require a verdict that the candidate must own without the protection of the marking's sympathy, since the marking has none to give. A topic that tests all of that, on material of genuine intellectual and human consequence, will be set as long as examinations are set at all.

There is a further reason, visible in the literature itself. The feminist development of Marxism is one of the places where a great tradition can be watched thinking: posing a question its founder left open, quarrelling over the answer in print, correcting itself across generations, and producing, in social reproduction theory, a restatement that even its critics treat as the position to beat. Candidates are being examined on that spectacle whether they know it or not, and the ones who know it write about the field as participants in an ongoing inquiry rather than as tourists in a museum of doctrines. That difference of stance, more than any single technique in this guide, is what the highest bands have always been describing, in their awkward bureaucratic vocabulary, and it is available to any candidate willing to state each position as though its holder were reading over the marker's shoulder. On the evidence of the literature, several of them may as well be.

## Decoding the stems: the questions behind the questions

Each recurring stem in this topic conceals a specific demand, and candidates who can hear the demand inside the wording gain the minutes other candidates spend deciding what the question wants. The stem that asks for the contribution of Marxist feminism to an understanding of some institution is asking for mechanisms, not for a summary of the perspective followed by a gesture at the institution: the contribution is the mechanism, the renewal of labour power for the family, segregation for work, naturalisation for ideology, and the answer should be organised as a tour of mechanisms with the institution in every paragraph. The stem that asks whether a perspective gives an adequate account is an evaluation purchase at the full tariff, whatever its mild wording, and it expects the strongest objection in the literature to be produced and answered before any verdict is offered. The stem that quotes a claim and invites discussion is asking first for the claim's provenance and fairness, whose claim it is and whether the perspective's holders would sign it, and only then for the argument around it.

The stem that asks candidates to compare two named perspectives on a named topic is the matrix's second row with its terms filled in, and its hidden demand is the axis: the marker is waiting to see whether the candidate constructs the comparison on the primary systems or defaults to the procession. The stem that asks for the strengths and limitations of a perspective is the most frequently mishandled in the topic, since it invites a ledger, strengths on one side and limitations on the other, with no transaction between the columns. The professional handling converts the ledger into an argument: each strength is stated with the objection it survives, each limitation with the repair the literature attempted, so that the two columns interlock and the verdict can be read off the pattern of survival and repair.

### Which stem most reliably separates the strongest scripts from the rest?

The adequacy stem. Its mild wording disguises the highest demand on the paper, and cohorts divide cleanly into candidates who hear a request for summary with opinions attached and candidates who hear a purchase order for the literature's strongest objection, its reply, and a judgement. The second hearing is learnable in an afternoon and pays out for an entire examination career.

Candidates should build, during revision, a personal concordance of the stems their board actually uses, gathered from past papers and matched against the matrix rows, with the hidden demand noted beside each in a phrase. The concordance is a small document with an outsized effect, because it relocates the examination's difficulty from the room to the desk, where it can be met at leisure. In the room, the candidate no longer interprets; the candidate recognises, and recognition is faster and calmer than interpretation by exactly the margin that separates a planned answer from an improvised one.

## Fairness as technique: the ethic beneath the method

The method of this guide can be summarised as a set of techniques, but it rests on a disposition that the candidate should understand, because techniques decay under pressure and dispositions hold. The disposition is fairness as a craft habit: the determination to state every position as its holder would wish it stated, to attribute every objection to the scholar who earned it, and to let one's own verdict be the last thing the evidence meets rather than the first. Assessment rewards the disposition because scholarship is the disposition, institutionalised. The candidate who writes fairly is not performing a courtesy for the marker's benefit. The candidate is doing the thing the certificate certifies, in the only place it can be done, on the page, under time, with the traditions watching.

Fairness has a selfish dividend that candidates should be told about plainly, since idealism is not the only motive that survives an examination season. The fair script is the defensible script. It cannot be embarrassed by a second marker who holds the opposite view, because it has already stated that view at its strongest and answered it in the open. It cannot be embarrassed by the literature, because its claims are the literature's claims with their qualifications attached. And it cannot be embarrassed by its own author on rereading, which matters more than candidates expect, since the habits formed in examination rooms persist into the essays, reports, and arguments of the working life beyond them. The traditions examined here were built by writers who argued with each other ferociously and, at their best, fairly. To be examined on them fairly is to be inducted, briefly and under invigilation, into their company.

## The wage as a hiding place: the mechanism examined once more

Because every question type in the matrix depends on it, the guide returns once more to the central mechanism, this time at the level of the wage itself, since whoever can narrate the wage's concealment possesses the explain answer's core in portable form. A wage appears, in the payslip and in the theory that takes the payslip at face value, as the price of the labour performed in the workplace during the hours recorded. What it quietly purchases, on the Marxist feminist analysis, is labour power whose production costs were borne elsewhere: the meals that restored the worker, the sleep made possible by a kept household, the childhood that produced a literate and socialised adult, the care in illness that returned the worker to the bench. None of those inputs appears in the employer's accounts as a cost of the labour consumed, because they were supplied by unpaid work in the household and by public services the wage earner did not purchase at their full value. The wage is therefore, in the analysis's precise sense, an incomplete price, and the incompleteness is gendered, because the unpaid suppliers have been overwhelmingly women.

The mechanism's elegance is that it requires no villain. Employers need not conspire to underpay for reproduction; they buy labour power in a market where its price has already been set by the existence of the unpaid sphere. Households need not be deceived about their own arrangements; they are solving the problem of survival with the resources the wage provides. The pattern reproduces itself through the ordinary rationality of everyone inside it, which is why the tradition regards exhortation as an insufficient remedy and why its analyses keep returning to organisation rather than to attitude. A candidate who can state that self reproducing quality has grasped something examiners probe for directly, since it separates the structural claim from the moral one and displays the theory's actual explanatory wager.

### How does the concealment argument answer the charge that the theory blames families?

By relocating the object of criticism from persons to a price form. The analysis indicts the wage's incompleteness and the organisation that produces it, not the households that live inside the organisation, whose arrangements it treats as rational responses to constraint. Candidates who make that relocation explicit disarm the commonest sentimental objection to the theory and demonstrate command of its register at the same time.

The mechanism also explains a datum that rival accounts handle awkwardly: the persistence of the housework burden among households where women hold full time employment. If the burden were a survival of pre modern attitudes, its distribution should track belief; where it tracks instead the structure of wages, hours, and the price of purchased substitutes, the reproduction account claims the evidence. Critics reply that time use is also shaped by preference and by bargaining inside households, and the mature literature absorbs the reply by treating the household as a site of negotiation conducted on ground the wage relation has already tilted. That image, negotiation on tilted ground, is a fair emblem of the theory's present form, and a candidate who ends an explain answer with it has travelled from mechanism to sophistication in a single paragraph.

## Two households in the archive: a comparative vignette

Abstraction persuades markers most when it has just touched ground, and the guide offers a paired vignette, drawn from the literature's standard cases, that candidates may adapt as an illustrative passage in family and work answers alike. Consider first the industrial household of the classic model at the height of the factory system: a male earner in mill or mine, a wife whose labour, cooking, washing, mending, childrearing, the management of credit and of sickness, fills longer hours than his, and a wage negotiated in a labour market that treats his earnings as the household's foundation and hers, when she earns, as an addition. The vignette's analytical content is the visibility of the circuit: her unpaid production is consumed by the same household that sells his labour power, and the employer's wage bargain silently assumes her work. Every element of the mechanism is present in one street of terraced houses.

Consider beside it the household of the professional salariat in a later economy: two earners, purchased childcare, cleaning bought by the hour, meals partly prepared by an industry, and grandparents or the state supplying portions of the renewal that the first vignette's wife supplied entire. The reproduction has not vanished; it has been redistributed, marketised in part, and collectivised in part, and the theory's question becomes the sharper one of who now performs it and on what terms. The workers supplying the purchased care are themselves overwhelmingly women, frequently maintaining their own households after their shifts, so that the burden has been transferred along lines of class as much as abolished. The social reproduction literature's widening of the frame, from the single household to the whole organisation of life's renewal, was built for exactly this comparison, and the candidate who stages it shows the theory working on the world the candidate actually inhabits, without asserting a single contemporary statistic.

### What should a candidate avoid when using a vignette in an answer?

Two things: mistaking the vignette for evidence, and letting it sprawl. A vignette illustrates a mechanism whose statement must precede it and whose evaluation must follow it; given more than a paragraph it becomes the answer's centre of gravity and the analysis is displaced by narrative. One paragraph, mechanism before, consequence after, is the professional dosage.

The vignette pair also teaches the theory's historical sense better than any summary, because the comparison between the households is itself the theory's claim about change: the organisation of reproduction is historical, its forms alter with the economy's development, and what persists is not a household shape but a relation, the renewal of labour performed under terms set by the wage. Candidates who grasp that distinction, between the persistence of a relation and the persistence of its costume, are immunised against the crudest version of the diversity objection and equipped for its sophisticated version, which asks whether the relation itself, and not merely its costume, has dissolved in economies where care is purchased and collectivised at scale. That question is genuinely open, and an answer that poses it clearly has found the frontier where this field's research and its examinations meet.

## The syllabus question: enrichment, core, and the candidate who asks what counts

One query reaches teachers of this topic in some form every season: whether the restatement literature is actually examinable, or whether it belongs to the enrichment margin where interested students browse while the prudent revise the set texts. The honest answer has two halves, and candidates deserve both. The first half is that no board examined here requires the restatement by name; a script can reach a respectable band on the classical material alone, accurately stated and fairly evaluated through the objections of its own period. The second half is that respectable is not the summit. The descriptors at the top of every system examined here speak of range, of development, of awareness that positions have histories, and the restatement is simply the most efficient evidence of those qualities available in this field. Treating it as enrichment mistakes how bands are built: the summit descriptors are not decorative, and material that answers them directly is core material for any candidate aiming at them, whatever the specification's reading list says.

The practical consequence is a two tier revision stock, and candidates should build it consciously. The ground tier holds the classical positions, the mechanisms, the founding texts with their caveats, and the great objections, mastered to the point of fluency. The summit tier holds the restatement, one developed form of each rival tradition, and the methodological objections, mastered to the point of deployability rather than fluency: fewer items, each available in two or three exact sentences. Scripts are then assembled by drawing the frame from the ground tier and the discriminating moves from the summit tier, which is precisely how the strongest answers in the examiners' exemplars are in fact assembled. Confusing the tiers in either direction is costly: summit material without the ground reads as name dropping, and ground material without the summit reads as a well kept museum.

### How can a candidate tell whether a piece of reading belongs to the ground tier or the summit tier?

Ask what breaks without it. Remove the item in imagination: if the answer's exposition collapses, the item is ground; if the answer survives but loses its claim on the highest descriptors, the item is summit. Both tiers earn their place, and the examination's tariff decides the proportion in which a given answer should draw on each.

Teachers fielding the syllabus question from anxious students should answer it in the same two halves, since the anxiety usually conceals a workload fear rather than an intellectual one. The summit tier for this topic is genuinely small: one restatement essay read carefully, two formulations learned exactly, and one objection pair rehearsed. Presented at that size, as an afternoon's work with a measurable return, the material stops looking like an open ended demand to read everything and becomes what it is, the cheapest band insurance on the paper. Students who still decline it are making a legitimate choice about their target band, and the teacher's duty is to make the price of the choice legible rather than to disguise it.

## Marking your own work: a self assessment protocol

The final skill this guide teaches is the one that makes the others self sustaining: reading a finished practice script the way a marker reads it, in one pass, with the descriptors in hand and no tenderness. The protocol has five checks, performed in order, each keyed to a gate in the marking this topic receives. Read the opening alone and ask whether the perspective has been located by its foundation or merely introduced. Read the theorists and ask of each whether a proposition travels with the name. Read the objections and ask of each whether its author is present and whether it was stated strongly enough to frighten the position it attacks. Read the proportions and ask whether evaluation received the share the verb purchased. Read the verdict and ask whether it could have been written before the answer began; if it could, it is a summary wearing the verdict's clothes, and the script's last paragraph must be rewritten from the exchange the answer actually staged.

Score the script by movements rather than by impression. Give location, construction, pressure, and judgement each a candid grade, and let the lowest movement set the revision target for the next practice answer, since scripts improve fastest at their weakest joint and markers, whatever their private arithmetic, respond to scripts as chains whose strength is the strength of the movement that fails. Keep the graded scripts. Watching the pressure movement grow across a month of practice gives a candidate evidence about their own development that no reassurance from a teacher can match, and walks into the examination with the calm of someone who has already met the marker's standards in private and satisfied them there.

### What is the most useful question to ask of a practice script once the five checks are done?

Whose voice is missing. Every strong answer in this field is a populated room: the theorist carrying the claim, the critic carrying the objection, the successor carrying the repair. Weak scripts are soliloquies. Asking which scholar should have been heard, and at which paragraph, converts a vague sense of thinness into a specific revision instruction, and it trains the ear that will hear the same absence in the examination room while there is still time to repair it.

Self assessment has one corruption to guard against, and the guide ends its teaching by naming it. Candidates grading their own work drift toward grading the effort rather than the artefact, crediting what a paragraph meant to do. Markers have only the artefact. The protocol's severity, its insistence on reading what is on the page in one pass without annotation or apology, is therefore not pedantry but rehearsal for the exact conditions under which the real script will be read. Practise being read as you will be read, and the examination loses its power to surprise, which is the last and most general form of the preparation this guide has been recommending from its first page.

## Confidence and its sources: what the prepared candidate knows that others do not

Examination confidence is usually discussed as a temperament, something candidates either bring or lack, and the discussion is useless to everyone in it. In this field confidence has identifiable sources, each of them a piece of knowledge rather than a feeling, and listing them is a fitting close for a guide that has treated preparation as a set of decisions. The first source is the knowledge that the paper is finite: five shapes of question, recurring across years, each with a rehearsed response. Nothing on the day will be a sixth shape in disguise for long, because the reading minutes exist to expose disguises, and the candidate who has sorted a dozen past papers knows the wardrobe by heart. The second source is the knowledge that marking is an institution rather than a lottery: descriptors, moderation, exemplars reaching opposed conclusions at the highest band. The third source is the knowledge that one's own faults are named and therefore manageable: a signature error identified in practice is an error already half retired, and the audit habit converts the fear of unknown weaknesses into a short list of known ones.

The fourth source is subtler and worth the candidate's trust. It is the knowledge that the field itself rewards the prepared disproportionately, because its material cannot be improvised. Nobody bluffs the domestic labour debate; nobody invents, in the room, the difference between a unitary and a dual systems architecture, or the name of the scholar who charged the classical categories with blindness to sex. Where a topic can be bluffed, preparation and improvisation compete on level terms and confidence is rational to doubt. Where it cannot, the prepared script announces itself in its first paragraph, and the marker's recognition, the sense that this candidate has been somewhere real, settles over the reading like good weather. Candidates feel that settling from the other side of the desk as the sensation of writing fluently, and mistake it for inspiration. It is recognition, earned in the library weeks before.

### What should a candidate do with the nerves that remain after preparation is complete?

Spend them on the reading minutes. Nerves are attention without an assignment, and the sorting of the paper gives attention its assignment at the moment of maximum need. Candidates who begin writing instantly to escape discomfort sacrifice the minutes that convert a frightening paper into an inventory; candidates who read first discover, almost every time, that the inventory was kinder than the fear.

One more source deserves naming, because candidates discover it late and value it out of proportion once found: the companionship of the texts themselves. A field studied through its arguments becomes, after some weeks of honest reading, a set of voices whose positions the candidate can summon and interrogate at will, and the examination room is noticeably less lonely for the candidate who arrives with Hartmann's impatience, Delphy's rigour, and the restatement's patience all available for consultation. Teachers observe this as the moment a student's writing changes tense, from reporting what authorities say to conversing with what they argue. Nobody can schedule the moment, and no drill in this guide guarantees it, but every drill here was designed to make the room in which it happens: named claims, attributed objections, positions stated strongly enough to talk back. Candidates who notice the change in their own practice scripts should trust it as the truest signal that preparation has become understanding, and should protect the final days for the light work of ordering rather than for any desperate addition to the stock.

There is a final confidence, the deepest one available on this topic, and it belongs to the candidate who has understood what the whole apparatus is for. The matrix, the movements, the drills, and the audits are scaffolding around a single achievement: a mind that can hold several serious positions at once, state each as its holder would state it, and judge among them on evidence it has rehearsed. That achievement does not expire with the examination. It is the working equipment of every profession the candidates in this field are heading toward, and of citizenship in any society where arguments matter. The examination, for once, is examining the real thing, and the candidate who has felt that, even briefly, in a practice script that suddenly populated itself with scholars arguing in good faith, has already received the guide's whole message. The marks are the receipt. The achievement is the purchase. Candidates who finish their preparation with that order of values settled tend to write the calmest scripts in the cohort, because nothing in the room can threaten what they actually came for, and markers, who are connoisseurs of calm, reward it in the only coin they have. It is a fitting place for a study guide to end: with the discovery that the technique was always a way of protecting something simpler, a reader's respect for the positions on the page and for the evidence that must judge between them.

## For students and exam candidates

Your whole task on this topic reduces to a handful of moves, and they are all in your reach. Learn the primacy distinction until you can write it in your sleep: the traditions differ over which system is primary, not over which oppression matters more, and the sentence that ranks harms is the sentence that costs you the band. Drill your theorists as name and claim pairs, Benston with the production of labour power, Hartmann with the charge that the classical categories are blind to sex, Delphy with the household as a direct relation of appropriation, Barrett with the documented neglect, Walby with the structures of patriarchy, and the social reproduction writers with the renewal of life as the theory's present centre. Practise the current-form move until it is reflexive: state the position, then state its later restatement, and let your evaluation begin from the strongest version rather than from the textbook's stopping point. Convert the feminism answer matrix into revision cards, one row per card, with the lead definition on one face and the trap on the other, using the study notes at [VaultBook](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html) to hold the cards where you can annotate them with your own specimen sentences. Sort past questions by row, using the previous year paper collections described in the revision section to gather the topic's appearances, and answer at least one question from each row under time. And when the paper is in front of you, conclude wherever your arguments lead. This guide has stated it as marking fact, and it is true: an answer concluding against any feminist perspective, including the one this guide teaches, can score full marks, provided the statements are accurate and the objections are named. Your sympathies are your own. Your sentences belong to the evidence.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The ranking formulation is the central teaching problem in this topic, and it arrives in your classroom pre-installed by the revision industry, which teaches class before gender because the phrase is memorable and the truth is not. Your first task is diagnostic: have students write the distinction between the traditions in one sentence, early, and read the sentences for the ranking error, since it will appear in a majority of scripts and it predicts the rest of each student's difficulties with unusual fidelity. The repair lesson writes itself from the clinic in this guide: take the ranking sentence apart in front of the class, show what it claims, show what the position claims, and let students perform the replacement on their own sentences until the primacy formulation is the one their hands produce unbidden. The second task is the attributed objection. Students arrive believing that evaluation means having reservations; teach them that evaluation means knowing whose reservations, and build the seminar around objection and reply pairs, Hartmann against the unitary response, Delphy against the reproduction account, so that the names become associated with arguments rather than with positions in a list. The third task is the current form. A single seminar on the social reproduction restatement, taught as the tradition's answer to its critics, updates the whole topic for a cohort and gives your strongest students the material with which they will distinguish themselves, while giving every student the salutary lesson that a literature is a moving thing. The comparison article devoted to the three traditions will serve as the seminar's common text, since it states the three traditions at the strength this guide insists on.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

This guide inverts the relationship its readers usually have with its subject: the research frontier is treated here as examination capital, and the examination is treated as a compressed form of the scholarly transaction. Researchers will recognise the standards being taught, since they are the field's own: attribute objections, state positions in their strongest form, distrust the orphaned figure, and treat the restatement literature as the position's current address. Historians of the tradition may note the guide's wager that the internal history, the sequence of self corrections from the founding texts through the domestic labour debate to the restatement, is the most efficient carrier of the field's content for assessment purposes; the wager is testable in any marking room. Journalists covering the periodic rediscovery of these debates in public life will find the primacy distinction a serviceable instrument for sorting the claims made upon it, and the guide's insistence that the traditions be stated before they are judged is offered as a professional courtesy the public discussion could borrow with profit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: How do you write an exam answer on Marxist feminism?

Begin by fixing the answer's foundation: the position locates the modern shape of gender relations within capital's organisation of reproduction. Plan from the question type, since explain, compare, evaluate, apply, and assess questions each purchase a different operation. Choose a maximum of four theorists and write down the claim each one carries before drafting anything. Develop the mechanism of unpaid renewal before reaching for illustrations. Then introduce the most serious objection you know, with its author's name on it, allow the literature its response, and close with a judgement whose proportions match the exchange you have actually staged.

### Q: What is the mark carrying distinction between Marxist and radical feminism?

It concerns foundations rather than sympathies. One tradition explains women's position through a self standing order of male power that predates class society and would survive its abolition; the other explains the contemporary pattern through the way a wage economy organises the renewal of its workforce in households. Everything else follows from that divergence: the account of the family, the reading of paid employment, and the remedy each side proposes. Candidates lose the thread when they translate the divergence into a contest of concern. Candidates hold it when they ask, of any claim in the field, which foundation is doing the explaining underneath it.

### Q: Which feminist theorists should you name?

Fewer than you think, and each with luggage. Benston for the argument that domestic work manufactures the worker while escaping the wage. Hartmann for the proposal that two systems, not one, organise the pattern, and for the analysis of segregated employment. Delphy for the claim that the household extracts labour directly. Barrett for the record of the classical theory's silence about gender. Walby for the rival architecture of patriarchal structures, indispensable in comparison. Vogel and the social reproduction writers for the position's developed shape. A name without its proposition is ballast; six names with propositions are an education the marker can grade.

### Q: How do you use social reproduction theory in an answer?

Deploy it as the position's developed shape at the moment your answer turns from exposition toward judgement. Two or three sentences suffice: the restatement widens the older housework argument into an account of how life and labour are renewed across households and institutions, specifies the relation between that sphere and production instead of assuming it, and absorbs part of the force of the standard objections. From that point, evaluate the restated version rather than the superseded one, and mention which objection the restatement was built to answer. Used there, it signals currency and supplies evaluation simultaneously. Used as a decorative name in an introduction, it signals neither.

### Q: What are the common mistakes on Marxist feminism?

Six recur across cohorts. Translating a claim about analytical foundations into a league table of harms. Fusing two neighbouring labels into one position when the question depends on their difference. Defining the radical tradition by temperament instead of by its doctrine of a prior system. Freezing the literature at the decade of the housework argument, as if nothing had been written since. Retailing the founding text on the family without flagging that its borrowed anthropology has not held up. And voicing objections in the abstract, unattributed to any scholar, which converts potential evaluation into mere mood. Every one of the six is a habit of phrasing rather than a gap in knowledge, and habits can be retrained in days.

### Q: How do you evaluate Marxist feminism in an essay?

Assemble the evaluation from the literature's own exchanges instead of inventing a stance. Give the position its mechanism first, in full, because a judgement is worth only what the statement beneath it is worth. Then select objections with authors: the dual systems charge that the categories cannot register relations between the sexes; the domestic mode argument that the beneficiary of housework is nearer home than the theory admits; the functionalist demand that benefits be connected to causes by mechanisms. Supply each objection's published reply, record what force survives the reply, and let your verdict distribute itself across those survivals. Evaluation built that way persuades markers of every persuasion, because it resembles the scholarship they were trained in.

### Q: Which examples work for feminist theory questions?

Choose specimens whose provenance you can state and whose mechanism you can name. The demand that housework be waged, raised by a campaign the theory itself generated, shows doctrine becoming politics and contains its own internal critics. The communal household experiments of the revolutionary period show the tradition attempting to reorganise reproduction rather than merely describe it, and faltering on resources. The employment cycle of the war years, expansion followed by organised expulsion, embodies the reserve argument in a documented sequence. Episodes from schooling and occupational history serve the applied questions. Journalism's striking anecdotes and half remembered statistics fail the provenance test and should be left outside the examination room.

### Q: How do you compare the feminisms in an answer?

Build the comparison on a declared axis and conduct it inside paragraphs rather than between them. Announce at the outset that the traditions divide over foundations: rights and access, a prior order of male domination, or the organisation of reproduction within a wage economy. Then take shared ground, the family or paid work, and let each foundation reorganise that ground in turn, so that any middle paragraph holds at least two traditions in contact. Represent each side by its developed scholarship rather than its slogan, and close by observing what the comparison has revealed about the axis itself. Procession, one doctrine after another with a summary stapled on, earns description credit only, however accurate its parts.

### Q: Is it safe to criticise feminism in an exam?

Yes, provided the criticism is scholarship rather than temperature. Assessment in this field rewards evidenced evaluation in either direction, and a conclusion against any of the traditions, reached through accurate statement and objections carrying their authors' names, satisfies the highest descriptors. What examiners penalise is the counterfeit: dismissal without a source, objections aimed at versions nobody defends, verdicts substituted for arguments. One qualification matters. Where an item's verb asks for representation rather than judgement, deliver representation and keep the verdict for the items that purchase it. Read the command word as the contract it is, and criticism becomes not merely safe but the most creditable activity on the paper.

### Q: How do you revise the feminist perspectives together?

Revise by issue rather than by doctrine. Take the household, and lay the three traditions' claims about it side by side in your own sentences, each claim paired with its scholar. Repeat for paid employment, for schooling, and for the state, until the material exists in your notes already in contact, which is the form the examination will demand of it. Drill names against claims and objections against replies on cards. Sort previous questions by type and answer a selection under time, auditing each attempt against the standard repertoire of errors. The candidate who revises comparatively arrives with comparisons half written; the candidate who revises doctrine by doctrine arrives with monologues and has to improvise the meetings between them.

### Q: How does the current-form move earn marks in a comparison answer?

By upgrading the objects being compared. A comparison conducted between textbook summaries can only rank summaries, and markers cap its credit accordingly. When one tradition is represented by its developed restatement, and the others are granted their strongest formulations in fairness, the comparison becomes an encounter between live positions, and the verdict, whichever it favours, is a judgement about the field as it stands. The move also smuggles evaluation into the exposition, since presenting the restatement requires noting the objection that provoked it. Two sentences accomplish the upgrade; their absence is one of the most reliable ways markers date a script's preparation.

### Q: What should a candidate separate between stating a primacy claim and ranking oppressions?

Separate a proposition about explanation from a proposition about importance. The primacy claim answers a question of architecture: which structure, on this theory, generates the pattern the others describe. The ranking answers a question of moral weight: whose suffering counts for more. The traditions dispute the architecture fiercely while mostly refusing the ranking, and a candidate who merges the two questions misrepresents every party to the dispute at once. In practice the separation is maintained by a discipline of verbs. Write generates, organises, explains, and mediates for the architectural claim, and reserve outweighs, matters more, and comes first for the ranking you are declining to make.

### Q: Why do examiners reward the distinction over the verdict?

Because the distinction can be marked and the verdict mostly cannot. Two examiners formed by rival traditions will disagree about which perspective should win a comparison, and assessment systems are built to survive that disagreement: they grade the operations a script performs, among which stating the field's architecture accurately is the first and most portable. A verdict is one candidate's destination; the distinction is the map every marker shares. Rewarding the map keeps grades defensible across a divided profession, and candidates benefit from understanding that their conclusions are free precisely because their statements are what is being bought.

### Q: How does the feminism answer matrix order a timed answer?

It front loads the decisions that improvisation makes badly. Reading the question, the candidate assigns it a row, which fixes the opening definition and the theorist whose claim will anchor the middle. The row's evaluation cell selects the objection and its author before a word of prose exists, protecting the turn to judgement from being squeezed out by exposition. The trap cell names the sentence to avoid, which matters because pressure resurrects exactly the formulations a candidate has heard most often. What remains for the writing itself is execution in four movements, location, construction, pressure, and judgement, in proportions the row has already set. Order imposed early is time returned later.

### Q: What separates a describing answer from an evaluating one in this field?

Transactions with named opponents. The describing answer reports positions in sequence, however elegantly, and its criticisms float free of any author. The evaluating answer stages encounters: a claim is built, a scholar's objection is brought against it at full strength, the literature's reply is heard, and a residue is acknowledged before judgement is pronounced. The difference is visible in the grammar, since evaluation requires names, verbs of argument, and concessions, where description requires only report. Markers are trained to feel that grammatical difference within a page, and the top band is reserved for scripts in which something is genuinely at stake between the positions on it.
