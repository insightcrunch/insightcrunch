A functional explanation accounts for something by pointing to what it does rather than to what produced it. The explanandum is held in place, on this pattern, because of its effects: a feature persists since the consequences it generates favour its own continuation. Functional explanation therefore reverses the direction a reader instinctively expects from causal reasoning, and the reversal is either a legitimate form of understanding or a sophisticated way of mistaking a benefit for a reason. Which of those verdicts applies to Marx's theory of history is the question Gerald Allan Cohen forced his tradition to answer with unusual precision, and the answer he eventually gave against his own earlier defence is the centre of this article.

![G. A. Cohen's books and the argument and the retreat table on a study desk, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

Marx's theory, as Cohen reconstructed it, explains the character of a society's production relations by their effect upon the development of its productive powers. Feudal arrangements, bourgeois property forms, and their successors enter the account because of what each made possible for the growth of human productive capacity at a given stage. Readers who want the theory itself set out in full should begin with the [historical materialism complete guide](/2010/01/15/historical-materialism-complete-guide/), which treats the doctrine as a whole; the concern here is narrower and more exacting. The concern here is the logical shape of one kind of claim inside that doctrine, the philosopher who rebuilt the claim in its strongest form, the colleague who identified the missing piece in the rebuilding, and the slow, documented withdrawal of confidence that followed.

Cohen's achievement deserves to be stated before any criticism is allowed near it. Before his book of 1978, defences of Marx's historical theory tended to proceed by quotation, by appeal to the authority of the founders, or by a looseness of formulation that let the theory absorb any evidence offered against it. Cohen proposed instead to state the theory as a small set of distinct propositions, each exposed to argument, and to defend those propositions with the tools of analytic philosophy: distinctions drawn sharply, objections entertained in their most formidable version, conclusions limited to what the premises could carry. Whatever the fate of the defence, the standard of statement changed permanently. Historical materialism after Cohen had to say exactly what it claimed, and claims stated exactly can be examined exactly, which is why the examination that damaged the defence came from inside the same project and carried a weight no external polemic had managed.

## Functional explanation and the theory it was built to defend

The theory Cohen defended is usually summarised in the vocabulary of base and superstructure, and the summary conceals the feature that interested him. Beneath the familiar picture of an economic foundation conditioning law, politics, and ideology sits a prior claim about the foundation itself: the relations of production that constitute it are explained, in part, by the level and growth of the productive forces they house. A set of production relations is not treated as an arbitrary arrangement that history happened to deposit. It is treated as the arrangement under which the available forces could be employed and extended, retained while it served that end and destabilised when it began to obstruct the end it had served.

Stated in that form, the theory's central historical claim has the unmistakable profile of an explanation by effects. Relations are said to obtain because of something they bring about, namely the development of the forces. The bringing about is not incidental colouring added to a claim that really rests elsewhere. Remove the effect, and the explanation loses its content: nothing in the bare inventory of a society's property forms explains why those forms, rather than neighbouring alternatives, were the ones consolidated. Cohen saw that this was no peripheral manner of speaking in Marx. The pattern recurs in the classic statements of the theory, in the celebrated preface of 1859 and in the analyses of particular transitions, and a defence of historical materialism that declined to defend the pattern would be defending a theory with its engine removed.

Defending the pattern required defending a form of explanation that many philosophers of science regarded with suspicion. Explanation by consequences had a poor reputation outside biology, where it was entrenched, because social scientists who used it often slid from observing that an institution had useful effects to assuming that the usefulness explained the institution, without any account of how the effect could reach back and sustain its own source. Cohen's response was not to deny the danger. His response was to isolate the logical form, show that the form is respectable where its conditions are met, argue that Marx's theory asserts those conditions, and accept the burden that came with the argument. The burden, as a colleague would soon point out, was a mechanism.

The stakes extended beyond one theory's internal health. If explanation by effects is illegitimate in social science unless a sustaining process is supplied, then a large family of familiar social explanations stands condemned alongside Marx's, including many offered by writers hostile to Marxism who explain institutions by the order they maintain or the coordination they achieve. If the form is legitimate under stated conditions, then Marx's theory possesses a kind of explanatory architecture that its critics had dismissed as mere teleology, the attribution of purposes to history itself. Cohen's defence therefore mattered to the philosophy of social science generally, a point the brief for this article registers in its account of adjacent disciplines, and it explains why the subsequent debate is still assigned to students who have no particular commitment to Marx in either direction.

### How is a consequence explanation different from an ordinary causal explanation?

An ordinary causal explanation cites antecedent conditions that produce the phenomenon, while a consequence explanation cites effects that the phenomenon reliably generates. The first looks backward along the chain of production; the second looks forward to what the item does, and claims that doing it is implicated in the item being there at all.

The difference is easy to state and surprisingly hard to keep hold of in practice, because ordinary language lets the two forms share sentences. To say that a valve exists in an engine because it releases pressure can report a designer's intention, a causal history of manufacture, or a claim about why engines of this type retain valves across generations of redesign. Only the third reading is a consequence explanation in Cohen's sense, and it is the third reading that needs a connecting process if it is to be more than a suggestive redescription. Cohen's terminology marked the point precisely. He spoke of consequence explanations and of the consequence laws that would underwrite them: generalisations to the effect that when a type of event would have a certain type of consequence, an event of that type tends to occur.

A consequence law, so stated, has an odd logical physiognomy that Cohen refused to disguise. It licenses an explanation before anyone knows how the consequence manages to secure the occurrence. In the history of science that posture is not absurd. Physicians explained the protective effect of certain practices before immunology existed, and breeders selected for traits long before genetics described the machinery of inheritance. Cohen's contention was that an explanation can be genuine while its elaboration is outstanding, provided the underlying generalisation is true and the pattern it names is real. The opposing contention, which the following sections take up, is that in the social case the outstanding elaboration is not a detail awaiting completion but the very thing that would distinguish an explanation from a coincidence dressed in explanatory grammar.

### Why does the primacy thesis need the functional form in particular?

Because the primacy thesis ranks the forces above the relations in explanatory order, and ranking requires a connection running from the subordinate term's effects back to its presence. Without explanation by effects, primacy would be a bare correlation between levels of development and types of property form, impressive perhaps, but silent about why the correspondence holds across epochs.

A correlation invites a shrug where an explanation demands an account. Societies with broadly similar productive equipment do tend to exhibit broadly similar relations of production, and a sceptic can grant the tendency while denying that it explains anything, in the way that a sceptic can grant that barometers fall before storms without supposing the barometer explains the weather. Cohen's primacy thesis was built to refuse the shrug. It asserts that the correspondence holds because relations of a given type are selected, retained, and reproduced in virtue of their suitability for the forces at hand, and that assertion is functional through and through. The functional form is not an optional garnish on the primacy thesis. It is the connective tissue that turns a ranking into a theory.

This is also why the debate could not be settled by accumulating historical illustrations, however rich. Illustrations show the correspondence; they cannot by themselves show the direction of explanation. A transition in which old relations visibly fettered growing forces, followed by a convulsion and a new set of relations under which growth resumed, fits the theory beautifully, yet the fit is exactly what a consequence explanation predicts and exactly what a sceptic about the form will call question-begging. Cohen understood that the contest would be decided at the level of explanatory logic, with history supplying the test cases rather than the verdict, and he wrote the defence accordingly, as a work of philosophy that historians could use rather than as a work of history that philosophers might admire.

## A formation inside the tradition, and a training against its habits

Gerald Allan Cohen was born in Montreal in 1941, into a household where the doctrine he would later test was not an academic speciality but a lived allegiance. His parents were active in the communist movement, and his schooling took place at an institution associated with that movement, so the vocabulary of historical materialism reached him as a native language reaches a child, prior to any decision about its truth. Biographical facts of that kind explain nothing about the validity of his arguments, and this article follows the brief's instruction to keep formation and validity apart. What the formation does explain is the peculiar combination that defined his career: an insider's knowledge of what the tradition actually claimed, joined to a refusal, acquired through philosophical training, to let the tradition claim anything loosely.

The training was formidable. Cohen studied at McGill and then at Oxford, where he came under the influence of Isaiah Berlin and absorbed the discipline of a philosophical culture that prized clarity about concepts above loyalty to systems. He taught at University College London before returning to Oxford in 1985 as Chichele Professor of Social and Political Theory, the chair he held for the rest of his career. The trajectory matters for one reason only: the defence of Marx he produced was written by a man whom the strongest available philosophical education had equipped to dismantle it, and who chose instead to rebuild it. Sympathetic readers sometimes treat the book as an act of piety. Its texture refutes them. Page after page proceeds by conceding the obvious objections, narrowing the claim, and rebuilding on the narrowed ground, which is the working method of a philosopher who expects hostile scrutiny and intends to survive it.

The intellectual setting of the nineteen seventies sharpened the project. Marxist scholarship in the English-speaking world was divided between a humanist reading centred on alienation and praxis, a structuralist reading imported from Paris that treated individuals as bearers of structures, and a long tail of exegetical commentary on the canonical texts. Cohen found each of these unsatisfying in a different way, and the dissatisfaction converged on a single demand: that the theory of history, the part of Marxism that made large claims about the direction and motor of social change, should be stated as a theory, with premises that could be listed and inferences that could be checked. The September Group, the gathering of like-minded scholars that began meeting annually and gave analytical Marxism its informal institutional home, formed around exactly that demand. Cohen was its presiding intellect, and the group's internal criticisms, offered by friends committed to the same standards, were the criticisms his work had to survive. The most damaging of them is treated in its own section below; its author receives full treatment in the companion profile of [Jon Elster and his Marxism](/2020/02/01/jon-elster-marxism/).

Cohen's book of 1978, Karl Marx's Theory of History: A Defence, announced its method in its subtitle. A defence, in the legal resonance the word carries, does not assert that the client is admirable or that the prosecution is wicked. A defence tests whether the charges, properly specified, succeed against the claims, properly specified. Cohen accordingly spent the early chapters fixing what the theory says: what the productive forces are, what relations of production are, what it means for the one to explain the other. Only after the specification did the advocacy begin. Critics who disliked the conclusions rarely denied the gain in precision, and even the book's eventual internal critics treated its statement of the theory as the one any subsequent argument had to start from. That is a rare achievement for a work of advocacy, and it is why the later retreat, when it came, could not be dismissed as a change of fashion. The man retreating was the man who had built the position, and he itemised what he was retreating from.

## The primacy thesis and the development thesis, stated as Cohen stated them

The defence rests on two theses that Cohen kept carefully distinct, because conflating them is the commonest route to misunderstanding the whole construction. The development thesis holds that the productive forces tend to grow over the course of history. The primacy thesis holds that the character of a society's relations of production is explained by the level of development its forces have reached. The first is a claim about a tendency operating across time. The second is a claim about explanatory order at a time and through transitions. Cohen's functional explanation belongs to the second thesis, but it borrows credibility from the first, since a ranking of relations by their service to development presupposes that development is indeed the direction in which the system tends.

The development thesis was grounded, in Cohen's presentation, on three sober premises about human beings and their circumstances. People confront scarcity: the material means of life are not given in effortless abundance. People possess rationality of an instrumental kind: they can recognise means to ends and adopt them. And people command intelligence applicable to production: they can accumulate knowledge of nature and embody it in tools, techniques, and forms of organisation. Given those premises, Cohen argued, an expansion of productive power across long stretches of history is what one should expect, since knowledge once gained is rarely lost wholesale and since each generation inherits an improved stock of means to work with. The thesis is deliberately modest in its modality. It asserts a tendency, not an escalator; it allows reversals, stagnations, and catastrophes; it claims only that the underlying pressures push one way, so that over the long run the level of productive power rises.

The modesty matters because the primacy thesis needs the tendency without needing a guarantee. If the forces merely tended to develop, then at any stage there would be relations under which that tendency could proceed and relations under which it would be throttled, and the primacy thesis explains the prevalence of the first sort by the service they render to the tendency. Cohen was explicit that the forces' primacy is explanatory rather than chronological or moral. He was not saying that tools matter more than laws in every respect that interests a historian, still less that technical change is the only causal factor worth studying. He was saying that when one asks why a society has the production relations it has, the deepest available answer runs through the requirements of its productive forces, whereas the reverse explanation, of forces by relations, holds only locally and derivatively.

### What is a functional explanation?

an explanation in which something is accounted for by its effects rather than its causes, as when a trait is explained by its contribution to survival. Marx's theory of history appears to explain relations of production by their effect on productive forces, which is why the form matters.

That compact statement, which serves as this article's reference definition, compresses the entire controversy into two sentences. The trait in biology is explained by its contribution to the organism's prospects, and the explanation is accepted because a process exists that connects contribution to prevalence. The relations in Marx's theory are explained by their contribution to the development of the forces, and the explanation's fate depends on whether an analogous connection can be exhibited or legitimately presumed. Everything Cohen wrote in defence of the form, and everything his critics wrote against his use of it, is commentary on the gap between those two cases: in one, the connecting process is the best-confirmed mechanism in the life sciences; in the other, the connecting process is the very thing under dispute.

It is worth pausing on the reconstruction point, because a persistent misreading treats the primacy thesis as a quotation from Marx rather than as Cohen's disciplined restatement of him. Marx nowhere sets out two numbered theses with these names. Cohen extracted them from texts written across four decades, in genres ranging from journalism to systematic economic critique, and he was candid that extraction involves judgement: passages must be weighted, metaphors disciplined, and occasional contrary suggestions set aside as lapses or as belonging to different questions. The candour is part of the defence's strength. A reconstruction that announces itself can be challenged at the points of judgement, and Cohen's readers have challenged it there, arguing over whether the 1859 preface deserves the constitutional status he gave it within the corpus. But the challenge proceeds on ground he cleared. Before the reconstruction, disputes about what Marx's theory of history even was consumed the energy that the reconstruction released for disputes about whether the theory, so stated, is true.

### Where did Cohen locate the functional pattern in Marx's own writing?

Chiefly in the preface of 1859, where relations of production are said to correspond to a definite stage of the forces and to turn from forms of their development into fetters upon them, and in passages where legal and political arrangements are explained by the material conditions they serve.

The preface's vocabulary does the theoretical work, and Cohen's reading fastened on its verbs. Correspondence suggests a fit between relations and forces that is not accidental; fettering suggests a prior period in which the same relations were not fetters but forms of development, which implies that their tenure depended on the service they rendered. Read flatly, the passage narrates a sequence. Read as Cohen read it, the passage asserts an explanatory principle: relations persist while they develop the forces and are swept aside when they cease to, so their persistence across the first period is explained by the developing they did. The reading is contestable, and historians of Marx's thought have contested it, but it is not an imposition on an innocent text. The functional grammar is present in the source, waiting for a philosopher either to legitimate it or to expose it, and Cohen chose legitimation as the harder and more interesting task.

The same grammar appears in Marx's treatment of particular institutions, where Cohen found supporting instances rather than the principle itself. Legal forms are characterised by the transactions they stabilise; political arrangements are traced to the class relations they protect; ideologies are assigned the function of reconciling people to arrangements whose basis lies elsewhere. Each instance can be redescribed in purely causal terms by a determined critic, and Cohen knew it. His claim was that the redescription loses something the original possesses: an explanation of why the institution is there, sustained across changes of personnel and circumstance, rather than a chronicle of the events that happened to install it. Whether that something is a genuine explanatory surplus or a mirage produced by grammar is, once again, the whole dispute in miniature.

### What work does the development thesis do inside the larger defence?

It supplies the direction that primacy then exploits. A tendency for productive power to grow gives the theory a standing gradient against which relations can be assessed as promoting or obstructing, so that selection among relations has a criterion and transitions have an intelligible trigger when obstruction accumulates.

Without the gradient, the functional claim would float. To say that relations obtain because they promote the development of the forces presupposes that development is a real propensity of the system and not a value the theorist projects onto history. Cohen's three premises about scarcity, rationality, and intelligence were designed to anchor the propensity in facts about human beings that a critic could accept without accepting anything distinctively Marxist. The anchoring is ingenious and also exposed: a critic who denies that the premises generate the tendency, pointing to long epochs of technical stagnation or to rational agents who prefer leisure, ritual stability, or equality over expanded output, attacks the defence at its foundation rather than at its superstructure. Cohen accepted that exposure as the price of a theory that says something definite enough to be wrong.

There is a further subtlety in the relation between the two theses that students routinely miss and examiners routinely reward. The development thesis explains why there is pressure for relations to change; the primacy thesis explains which relations relieve the pressure. Fettering is the junction between them: forces pressing against relations that once housed them comfortably. A transition, on this account, is not the irruption of an external accident into a stable order but the resolution of a strain the theory itself predicts will accumulate wherever the tendency operates. The elegance of the junction is part of what made the defence formidable. It is also what made the mechanism objection so pointed, because the junction describes a resolution without describing a resolver, a strain relieved without an account of the relieving process at the level where processes actually operate, in the decisions and conflicts of situated people.

## The anatomy of a functional explanation, taken slowly

Because the entire controversy turns on a logical form, the form deserves a slow exposition of its own. A functional explanation, in the usage Cohen codified, explains the occurrence or persistence of an item by reference to its disposition to produce a specified effect. The item might be an anatomical trait, a behavioural routine, a legal institution, or a set of production relations. The effect might be survival, coordination, legitimation, or the development of productive power. The explanation asserts that the item is there, or remains there, because it has that disposition. Four components can be distinguished in any such assertion, and separating them clarifies where agreement ends and dispute begins.

The first component is the pattern claim: items of this type are regularly accompanied by effects of that type. Hearts pump blood; certain property forms do in fact facilitate the adoption of improved techniques. Pattern claims are empirical and often uncontroversial. The second component is the benefit claim: the effect serves some system, organism, or process whose continuation is in question. Pumping serves circulation; facilitating technique serves the growth of productive power. Benefit claims introduce the teleological flavour that makes critics uneasy, since benefit is always benefit for something, and naming the beneficiary exposes the theory's commitments. The third component is the explanatory claim proper: the item's presence is explained by the benefit, not merely accompanied by it. The fourth component, usually left implicit, is the sustaining claim: some process exists by which the benefit feeds back to maintain the item that produces it. Cohen's defence can be read as an argument that the first three components can constitute a genuine explanation while the fourth remains unspecified. The objection can be read as the insistence that the fourth is where explanation either happens or fails to happen.

Cohen had technical resources for his side of that division. He developed the notion of an elaboration: an account of the process through which the functional pattern is maintained. Elaborations come in recognisable species. A purposive elaboration cites agents who perceive the benefit and act to secure it, as legislators preserve a law whose usefulness they understand. A selection elaboration cites a filtering process that eliminates items lacking the benefit, as markets eliminate firms whose organisation wastes resources, or as differential reproduction eliminates traits that handicap their bearers. A reinforcement elaboration cites learning loops, in which beneficial consequences strengthen the behaviour that produced them. Cohen's crucial move was to argue that a functional explanation does not stand or fall with the availability of its elaboration. The explanation asserts the consequence law; the elaboration, when found, enriches and confirms it, but its absence leaves the explanation incomplete rather than counterfeit.

That move had precedent on its side, and Cohen deployed the precedent with care. Darwin explained the prevalence of traits by their contribution to fitness before any mechanism of inheritance was known, and the explanation was not treated as counterfeit in the interim; it was treated as a research programme with a conspicuous vacancy. Mendelian genetics and then molecular biology filled the vacancy, and the filling vindicated the form. Cohen's suggestion was that social science might stand where biology stood between Darwin and Mendel: possessed of genuine consequence laws, awaiting their elaborations. The suggestion reframed the burden of proof. The critic could no longer win by pointing at the vacancy, since vacancies of that kind have been filled before. The critic would have to show that in the social case the vacancy is of a different and fatal kind, that no elaboration could in principle connect benefit to persistence for items like production relations. Showing that is harder than pointing, and for a while the defence held the field.

Yet the analogy concealed an asymmetry that the objection would eventually prise open. Darwin's vacancy concerned the mechanism of inheritance, while the selection process itself, differential survival and reproduction of heritable variants, was specified in the theory from the start. What Darwin lacked was the chemistry of the copying, not the logic of the filtering. Cohen's vacancy, the objection would argue, concerns the filtering itself. Between relations that promote the forces and relations that obstruct them, what does the eliminating? Wars, revolutions, imitative adoption, competitive extinction of states and firms, the deliberate reforms of far-sighted elites: candidates exist, and Cohen gestured toward several. But a gesture toward a family of unlike processes is not yet a mechanism in the sense that makes the biological case secure, and the difference between a named process and a family of gestures became the seam along which the defence split.

### How does an elaboration differ from the explanation it supports?

An explanation asserts that an item persists because of its beneficial effect; an elaboration describes the process by which that benefit maintains the item, whether through deliberate action, competitive filtering, or reinforcement. Cohen held that the assertion can be true and explanatory before the process is described.

The distinction is the hinge of the whole debate, and both sides accepted its terms while disputing its moral. For Cohen, the distinction protected functional explanation from an unreasonable demand: sciences routinely explain before they can elaborate, and a demand for the process in advance would have strangled Darwinism in its cradle. For the objector, the distinction marked the exact place where social functional explanation cheats, because in the social realm the elaboration is not a later refinement but the only thing that could distinguish a real pattern from a story told backwards from outcomes. A historian who knows which relations prevailed can always compose a plausible account of the development they promoted. The composition costs nothing and risks nothing. What would make it knowledge is a process, specified independently of the outcome, that discriminates in advance between relations that will persist and relations that will not. On that standard the elaboration is not the explanation's ornament. It is the explanation's collateral.

## The analogy with natural selection, and the load it was asked to carry

Every defence chooses its exemplar, and Cohen chose the most successful functional explanation in the history of science. Traits in biology are explained by their effects with a confidence no one finds mysterious: the heart's valves are explained by what they prevent, camouflage by what it avoids, the peacock's extravagant tail by the mating success it purchases at the price of survival. Cohen's question was why an explanatory form that biology uses without apology should be ruled illegitimate when Marx uses it, and his answer was that it should not be, provided the form's conditions are honoured in both cases. The analogy did double duty in the defence. It legitimated the form by association with a science whose credentials nobody in the dispute questioned, and it supplied the template of what a completed functional explanation looks like, against which the Marxist version could be measured and found, at worst, unfinished rather than fraudulent.

The template deserves precision, because the objection eventually turned on exactly what the template contains. In the biological case, three elements interlock. Variation generates a spread of traits in a population. Heredity ensures that traits are copied across generations with high fidelity. Differential success ensures that traits contributing to survival and reproduction are copied more often than their rivals. Given the three, the prevalence of a beneficial trait is explained by its benefit through a process that no step of which requires foresight, purpose, or a beneficiary who notices the benefit. The explanation is functional in form and mechanical in foundation. Cohen knew all of this and cited the biological case in full awareness of its foundation; his claim was that the foundation's presence in biology shows what an elaboration is, not that the explanation waits upon it. Darwin, after all, proposed selection while ignorant of heredity's machinery, and the theory explained before the machinery arrived.

Pressed hard, the analogy also reveals the disanalogy that this article is required to state plainly wherever the comparison is made: biology supplies a mechanism and Marxism does not. The sentence is not a rhetorical concession but a structural description. In biology the mechanism is internal to the theory; natural selection is not an auxiliary hypothesis bolted onto functional explanation but the engine that the explanation cites. In Cohen's Marxism, by contrast, the consequence law floats above a set of suggested processes, none of which the theory itself specifies with comparable determinacy. Defenders sometimes respond that the demand for comparable determinacy is an unfair standard imported from a science with simpler materials. The response has force, and Cohen himself explored versions of it. But it concedes the descriptive point while disputing its significance, and the history of the debate is the history of that significance being argued upward by the objectors and downward by the defenders until the chief defender changed sides on the question of what his own theory required.

There is a second asymmetry, less often noticed, that deepens the first. Biological selection operates on populations with many variants and many generations, so weak advantages compound into prevalence through sheer iteration. Production relations, by contrast, are few in type, change rarely, and are entangled with the political and military fortunes of the societies that bear them. A type of relation might persist for centuries because the state enforcing it wins its wars, while its contribution to productive development is mediocre or negative; a promising variant might vanish because its bearers lose a single battle. The statistical texture that makes selection explanations robust in biology is largely absent in the history of social forms, where the sample is small, the generations are long, and confounding causes are enormous. Cohen's consequence law therefore had to claim a regularity across a domain where regularity is hardest to establish, which raised the evidential price of the claim at the very moment the objection was raising its logical price.

### How does natural selection connect an effect to the persistence of its source?

Through differential reproduction of heritable variants: traits that raise the prospects of their bearers are copied into later generations more often than rivals, so the benefit itself, mediated by heredity and competition, explains why the trait becomes prevalent rather than remaining a curiosity.

Each element of that answer marks a place where the social analogy must find a counterpart or confess a gap. Heredity's counterpart would be the faithful reproduction of relations across time, through law, custom, education, and coercion, which exists but is partly deliberate and therefore partly purposive rather than blind. Variation's counterpart would be the generation of alternative relations, through experiment, borrowing, and revolt, which exists but is sparse and often suppressed by the very relations it might replace. Differential success has the least secure counterpart of all, since the success of a society is measured on many scales besides productive development, and the scales interfere. Cohen's defenders assembled the counterparts as best they could, and the assembly is not contemptible. What it never achieved was the interlocking necessity of the original, the sense that given the three elements the outcome follows with something like the reliability of a proof. The objection's enduring power lies in that gap between assembly and interlock.

A comparison with another discipline sharpens the point. Economics explains the prevalence of certain firm structures by their efficiency, and the explanation is respectable exactly to the extent that competitive markets supply a filtering process with some of selection's properties: many variants, rapid iteration, a single dominant metric of success. Where markets are monopolised, protected, or politically rigged, the efficiency explanation decays into the same backward storytelling that afflicts social functionalism generally. The pattern generalises into a principle the debate made visible: functional explanation earns its keep in proportion to the determinacy of the feedback process behind it, and it degenerates into narrative wherever the process is vague. That principle, which neither side stated in so many words at the outset, is close to a summary of where the argument ended, and it is the principle this article names, following the brief, as the mechanism requirement.

## The objection from inside the house

The objection arrived in 1982, in an article by Jon Elster published in Theory and Society, and its provenance is essential to its force. Elster was not an external critic ransacking Marxism for weaknesses from a rival camp. He was a member of the same analytical project, a participant in the September Group's discussions, committed to the same standards of clarity and to the same hope that Marxist theory could survive being stated precisely. His charge was that, stated precisely, its central explanatory device failed a test that the device itself invited. Functional explanation in social science, he argued, is legitimate only where a feedback loop can be demonstrated through which the beneficial effect maintains its cause. In biology the loop is natural selection. In Marx's theory of history, no loop of comparable standing had been exhibited, and the invocations of benefit that filled the gap were, in his assessment, instances of a pattern he diagnosed across the social sciences: the slide from consequence to cause without the intervening machinery that alone makes the slide valid.

Elster's argument had a diagnostic as well as a logical edge, and the diagnosis stung because it was accurate about a real habit. Marxist writing did contain passages, and not only in its vulgar versions, where the fact that an institution served the reproduction of a social order was treated as sufficient explanation of the institution, as though the order's need for the service summoned the servant into existence. Needs do not summon. A social order has no hands with which to procure what it requires, and any explanation that proceeds as if it did commits what Elster treated as a category error, the attribution of purposive agency to an abstraction. The remedy he proposed was methodological individualism in a specific and limited sense: explanations of large patterns must be capable of elaboration through the actions, beliefs, and opportunities of the individuals whose conduct constitutes the pattern. That demand, generalised, became the microfoundations programme, treated in the companion article on [Marxism and microfoundations](/2020/03/15/marxism-and-microfoundations/), and it set the agenda for the school through the following decade.

The strongest form of the objection, which this article is required to state at full strength before answering it, can be put as a single tightening sequence. To say that a set of relations exists because it promotes the development of the forces is to cite a benefit. A benefit is not a cause unless some process selects for it, discriminating between relations that confer the benefit and relations that do not, and eliminating or marginalising the latter. Unless that process is specified, the explanation asserts the very connection it was supposed to establish: it observes that the relations and the development coincide, decorates the coincidence with the word because, and calls the decoration a theory. Elster pressed the point with examples of functionalist reasoning elsewhere in social science that Cohen himself would not have defended, inviting the reader to notice that the logical form was identical and the respectability different only in the company it kept. The invitation was difficult to decline, because Cohen's own standards forbade special pleading for a favoured theory.

Cohen's initial reply defended the consequence law on the ground already surveyed: an explanation may be sound while its elaboration is outstanding, and the demand for a mechanism in advance confuses the order of discovery with the order of justification. He added a distinction between knowing that a functional pattern holds and knowing how it holds, arguing that Marxists could possess the first kind of knowledge on historical evidence even while the second eluded them. The reply was not a evasion; it drew on genuine precedents in the sciences and on a defensible philosophy of explanation. But its effect over time was paradoxical. The more vigorously the right to defer elaboration was asserted, the more conspicuous the deferral became, and the question shifted from whether a mechanism was required in principle to why, if the pattern were real, decades of sophisticated effort had produced no agreed candidate. Deferral is a wasting asset in science. Each year of vacancy raises the probability that the vacancy is permanent, and the objection's force grew with the calendar in a way that purely logical objections rarely do.

### Why is a benefit not yet a cause of the thing that confers it?

Because benefits are cheap to attribute after the fact and the attribution discriminates against nothing. Any persisting institution confers some benefit on someone or something, so citing a benefit explains persistence only if a process exists that favours benefit-conferring arrangements over their rivals and weeds the rivals out.

The cheapness of post hoc benefit is the empirical face of the objection, and it can be felt in miniature by considering explanations nobody accepts. A rain dance persists in a community, and an observer notes that it confers the benefit of social cohesion at planting time; the cohesion is real, yet few would accept cohesion as the explanation of the dance without a process by which cohesive practices outlast their competitors. Traffic congestion confers benefits on tow-truck operators and roadside diners, and nobody explains congestion by them. The difference between these cases and the Marxist case cannot be the presence of a benefit, which is common to all three. It must be either the scale and systematicity of the benefit, which is a matter of degree, or the existence of a sustaining process, which is a matter of kind. Elster's demand was that Cohen choose, and that if he chose degree, he should say how much systematicity suffices and how he knew the Marxist case possessed it. The demand was fair, and its fairness is why it could not be waved away as the misunderstanding of a hostile reader.

### What exactly did the objection ask the defence to produce?

A feedback mechanism: a specified process by which relations that fail to promote the development of the forces are selected against, and relations that succeed are retained, operating with enough regularity to underwrite the consequence law the defence asserted across historical transitions.

Specification is the operative word, and it separates the objection from a generic call for more detail. A mechanism is specified when its components and operations are identified independently of the outcomes it is meant to explain, so that the mechanism could in principle be found absent where the theory would then be embarrassed. Natural selection passes that test with room to spare: variation, heredity, and differential success can each be investigated without assuming the prevalence they jointly explain. The candidates offered for the historical case struggled against the test. Class struggle, the most frequently proposed candidate, names a real and pervasive process, but its outcomes are notoriously ambivalent for the theory's purposes: classes fight over the distribution of the product as readily as over the development of the forces, and a class whose position is threatened by development may fight successfully against the relations that would promote it, entrenching the fettering the theory expects to be temporary. A mechanism that as often blocks the predicted outcome as delivers it does not yet qualify as a mechanism in the required sense. It is a name for the arena in which the outcome, if it occurs, will occur.

Other candidates fared no better under specification. Competition between states might select for relations that maximise extractable surplus and military capacity, but military capacity tracks productive development only loosely and sometimes inversely, and states compete by imitation and alliance as well as by internal transformation. Competition between firms operates at the wrong level, selecting techniques and organisational forms within relations rather than selecting the relations themselves. Deliberate reform by enlightened rulers converts the explanation into a purposive one, which abandons the functional form for the cases where it applies and leaves the rest unexplained. Each candidate captures something real about historical change. None captures it with the determinacy the analogy promised, and the accumulation of near misses itself became evidence: if a mechanism of the required kind existed, the most talented defenders of the theory, working for two decades with every incentive to find it, might have been expected to converge on it.

## The argument and the retreat: the documentary record

What gives this episode its unusual standing in the history of the theory is that the retreat is not an interpretation imposed by critics but a sequence of texts by the defender himself, each citable, each more qualified than the last. Cohen replied to Elster in print in the early nineteen eighties, defending consequence explanation while granting that elaborations were desirable and that Marxists had been too casual in presuming them. Through the following decade, in papers on the primacy thesis and on the structure of the theory, the qualifications accumulated: the functional claim was hedged about with conditions, the confidence that the consequence law held across history was expressed with increasing caution, and the demand for mechanisms was absorbed into the statement of what a satisfactory version of the theory would have to contain. Then, in the preface to the expanded edition of the defence published in 2000, Cohen placed on record the sentence that settled the matter for most readers: he was no longer confident that historical materialism is true. The phrase matters because of its precision. He did not announce that the theory was worthless, or that his book had been a mistake, or that the questions it posed were misconceived. He withdrew confidence from the theory's truth, which is the exact coin in which a philosopher of his discipline deals, and he did so in the front matter of the book that had made the theory's reputation for rigour.

The table below, which this article calls the argument and the retreat, organises the sequence so that a reader can find each stage and test the characterisation against the texts.

| Component | Original claim | Objection | Concession made | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The primacy thesis | Relations of production are explained by the level of development of the productive forces | The ranking asserts an explanatory direction that the evidence of correspondence alone cannot establish | The claim requires the functional connection and cannot rest on correspondence by itself | Standing as a hypothesis whose support depends on the functional claim beneath it |
| The development thesis | The productive forces tend to develop through history, given scarcity, rationality, and inventive intelligence | The tendency is asserted across epochs whose record includes long stagnation and deliberate restraint of technique | The tendency is weaker and more interruptible than the defence's transitions require it to be | Partly standing as a long-run generalisation, insufficient alone to drive the theory |
| The functional explanation | Relations are explained by the development they promote in the forces, a consequence explanation needing no mechanism in advance | A benefit does not become a cause without a feedback process selecting for it, as selection does in biology | An elaboration of the mechanism is required for the explanation to be fully satisfactory, and none has been supplied | The form survives in biology and in pockets of social science with real filters; its Marxist application remains unelaborated |
| The mechanism requirement | Initially resisted as a confusion of explanation with its elaboration | Pressed from within the analytical project from 1982 onward, with the demand for specification independently of outcomes | Accepted in substance by the defender, whose later preface recorded his lost confidence in the theory under test | The requirement is the debate's durable residue and the standard any revived defence must meet |

Reading the table vertically teaches the lesson the prose argues. The objection did not refute a proposition; it raised a standard. The concession did not abandon a position under fire; it acknowledged the standard and recorded that the position had not met it. That is why the episode is better evidence about the theory than any critic's declaration of victory could be. Critics have incentives to overstate. A defender itemising his own retreat, in the preface to his own defence, has every incentive running the other way, and the itemisation therefore carries the credibility that attaches to statements against interest in any court of inquiry. Students meeting the debate for the first time sometimes ask why so much weight is placed on one scholar's change of mind, as though the truth of historical materialism were a matter of Cohen's biography. The weight is not biographical. It is evidential in the specific way just described, and it would attach to any comparably rigorous defender who performed the same withdrawal on the record.

The retreat also had a structure that the table's rows, read horizontally, reveal. Cohen conceded the periphery before the centre, and the centre last of all. The development thesis was qualified early and often, since its empirical exposure was obvious. The primacy thesis was defended longer, by being restated in forms that leaned harder on the functional connection. The functional explanation itself was the final redoubt, defended on philosophical grounds about explanation after its historical supports had been trimmed, and surrendered only in the qualified form the preface records: not declared false, but no longer held with confidence. Readers who describe the concession as total misdescribe it, and the misreading is one this article is charged with correcting. A withdrawal of confidence is not a recantation. Cohen continued to regard the theory as the most powerful framework of historical explanation on offer, continued to identify as a socialist, and continued to think the questions the theory answers are the right questions. What he ceased to do was assert that the answers had been established, and in a discipline where assertion is cheap, the cessation is the significant act.

### Which part of the defence did Cohen qualify first, and which last?

The development thesis was qualified first, since its tendency claim faced the historical record directly, while the functional explanation was qualified last, defended on grounds from the philosophy of explanation after its supporting theses had already been trimmed and hedged.

The ordering is instructive for readers learning how research programmes actually decay. They rarely collapse at the centre under a single blow. They lose their outer claims to accumulating exceptions, retreat to inner claims that are more abstract and less exposed, and finally discover that the inner claim, however defensible in isolation, no longer supports the edifice it was built to carry. A functional explanation of production relations, vindicated as a logical form, does not by itself establish that any particular set of relations was ever explained that way in fact. Cohen's trajectory follows the pattern with unusual fidelity and unusual honesty, which is why the episode is used by philosophers of social science as a case study quite apart from its Marxist content: it shows a first-rate mind following the evidence of its own standards to a conclusion it did not want.

There is a temptation, which the brief for this article explicitly resists, to read the retreat as a defeat inflicted by Elster and suffered by Cohen, two scholars reduced to winner and loser. The record supports a more interesting reading. Elster's objection succeeded because Cohen's standards were already Elster's standards; the demand for mechanisms was implicit in the analytical project's founding promise to state Marxism so clearly that its truth could be assessed. Cohen conceded ground to an argument he recognised as binding because he had helped write the rules that made it binding. The episode is therefore less a duel than a demonstration: a research community applied its own criteria to its own flagship theory and published the result, which happened to be negative. Whatever one thinks of the theory, the demonstration is a credit to the community, and the contrast with traditions that protect their flagship claims from their own criteria is a large part of why the September Group's work is still read by people who share none of its original hopes.

## What the concession did not touch, and what it changed for good

Precision about the retreat requires equal precision about its limits, and Cohen himself supplied the materials for both. The concession did not touch the value of the reconstruction. Karl Marx's Theory of History remains the clearest statement of the theory available, and critics of historical materialism routinely rely on Cohen's formulation when they want a target definite enough to criticise, which is a backhanded tribute the book continues to collect. Nor did the concession touch the legitimacy of functional explanation as a form. Cohen continued to hold, and the philosophy of biology continues to hold with him, that consequence explanations are genuine explanations where their patterns hold. The concession concerned the Marxist application: the claim that this pattern, at this scale, with these relata, had been shown to hold. Distinguishing the form's legitimacy from the application's establishment is the single most important analytical habit this article can pass on, because conflating the two produces both the crudest defences, which treat the form's respectability as the application's proof, and the crudest dismissals, which treat the application's failure as the form's disgrace.

What the concession changed permanently was the location of the burden of proof. Before 1982, a defender of historical materialism could treat the functional idiom as innocent until proven guilty, a natural way of speaking about institutions that any reasonable reader would accept. After the objection and the retreat, the idiom is guilty until elaborated: any writer who explains relations by their service to the forces owes the reader a mechanism, or owes the reader an acknowledgement that the mechanism is outstanding and the explanation provisional. That shift in the default is the debate's most concrete legacy, visible in the practice of writers who never mention Cohen or Elster by name. Textbooks state the theory with a caution their predecessors lacked. Critics press the mechanism question as a matter of routine. Defenders either attempt the elaboration in earnest, through class agency, state competition, or institutional selection, or quietly rebuild the theory on non-functional foundations, grounding transitions in the interests and capacities of classes without claiming that outcomes are explained by their benefits. The survey of the case against the theory at [the case against historical materialism](/2010/04/15/case-against-historical-materialism/) shows how thoroughly the mechanism question has been absorbed into the standard critical repertoire.

Attempts at elaboration deserve a fair hearing before the assessment closes, since the defeater this article carries is an invitation and not a tombstone. The most promising family of attempts works through the purposive route: relations are explained by the actions of classes and states that perceive, however imperfectly, where their interests lie and possess the power to install arrangements serving those interests. Where a rising class understands that new property forms would enrich and empower it, and fights for them on that understanding, the explanation of the forms runs through intentions, and intentions are mechanisms of a kind. The difficulty is scope. Purposive elaboration explains transitions that classes consciously willed, and the theory's grandest claims concern transitions nobody willed in their eventual form, across centuries in which the relevant actors could not have foreseen the arrangements their struggles consolidated. A mechanism that covers the deliberate cases and falls silent over the inadvertent ones has elaborated a fragment of the consequence law, and the fragment's boundaries coincide suspiciously with the cases that needed the functional form least.

A second family works through selection among polities: states whose relations strangle production lose wars, revenues, and eventually existence to states whose relations permit growth, so the map of surviving relations comes to reflect productive suitability the way a coastline reflects erosion. The image is powerful and the process is real, but its metric is military-fiscal success, and the correlation between that metric and the development of the forces is exactly as loose as the earlier discussion of the analogy suggested. Polities have prevailed through geography, disease, alliance, and luck; productive laggards have endured for centuries behind mountains and oceans; productive leaders have been conquered and their relations extinguished by horsemen. A selection process this noisy can explain a tendency only if the tendency is correspondingly gentle, and a gentle tendency cannot carry the primacy thesis in the strong form the defence required. The elaboration attempts thus converge on the same fork: either the mechanism is strong and the evidence for its operation is thin, or the mechanism is real and its strength is insufficient for the claim. Cohen's withdrawal of confidence can be read as his verdict on that fork after twenty years of watching the attempts, his own included.

### How should a reader weigh a concession made by a theory's own defender?

More heavily than a critic's parallel claim, other things equal, because the defender's incentives run against the concession and his knowledge of the position's resources exceeds any outsider's. A defender who withdraws confidence has usually exhausted the replies a critic has not thought of.

The weighing rule generalises beyond this debate, and students of any contested doctrine can apply it profitably. When the most resourceful advocate of a position announces, on the record and with itemised reasons, that the position's central claim has not been established, the announcement is evidence of a special grade. It does not settle the question, since defenders can lose heart for bad reasons and later work can repair what one generation could not. It does shift the rational default for everyone who lacks the time to re-run the entire inquiry: presume the claim unestablished until the repair is exhibited. Cohen's case is the paradigm because the itemisation is so exact. He did not gesture at doubts. He identified the missing mechanism as the point of failure, in a debate where the missing mechanism had been named by a colleague two decades earlier, and the convergence of the defender's diagnosis with the objector's is the strongest signal the episode emits.

## Functional explanation is not functionalism, and other separations that matter

A reader arriving from sociology brings a neighbouring doctrine that shares the vocabulary and must be separated from Cohen's topic before the two contaminate each other. Sociological functionalism, associated with Durkheim's heirs and with the systems theory of the mid-twentieth century, explains institutions by their contribution to the stability and integration of the social whole, and it typically treats the whole as the beneficiary whose needs organise the parts. Cohen's functional explanation is a narrower and more austere device. It explains a specified item by a specified effect, within a theory that names the beneficiary precisely: the development of the productive forces. It makes no appeal to social integration, posits no organismic whole, and would be embarrassed by the suggestion that society has needs in the way an organism does. The separation matters because the standard objections to functionalism, that it is conservative in temper, that it reifies society, that it cannot explain conflict or change, do not transfer automatically to Cohen's device, which was built inside a theory of conflict and change and was designed to explain why orders dissolve as much as why they persist. Elster's objection, by contrast, does transfer, because it targets the logical form the two doctrines share at the point where both move from benefit to explanation. Keeping the separation clean lets a reader discard the irrelevant objections and concentrate on the relevant one, which is a fair summary of what analytical Marxism existed to teach.

A second separation concerns teleology. To explain by effects sounds, to an ear trained on the physical sciences, like explaining by purposes, and purposes in history suggest a subject who purposes: a providence, a world spirit, a class acting as a single mind. Cohen's device requires no such subject, and he was careful to say so. A consequence explanation attributes no intentions to history. It asserts a pattern in which items with certain effects persist, and patterns can obtain without anyone intending them, as the biological case proves on every page of every textbook. The care was necessary and also, in the end, insufficient, because the objection never depended on attributing intentions. Elster's point was not that Cohen believed in a purposive history but that, intentions or none, a pattern asserted without its sustaining process is a promissory note, and the note had been outstanding for a century. Readers who defend Cohen by refuting the teleology charge are answering an objection weaker than the one that damaged him, a substitution this article flags because it remains the commonest error in the secondary literature's treatment of the debate.

A third separation distinguishes explanation of persistence from explanation of origin, a distinction the biological case observes automatically and the social case chronically blurs. Natural selection explains why a trait already present becomes prevalent; it does not explain the mutation that first produced the trait, which is a matter of variation's blind chemistry. Cohen's functional explanation, charitably read, likewise addresses persistence and prevalence: relations of a type, having arisen somehow, are retained and generalised because of what they do for the forces. The charity is often withheld, by critics who demand that the device explain origins too and by defenders who let it claim them. Both errors inflate the stakes. Origins in history are typically conjunctural, a braid of accident, borrowing, conquest, and improvisation, and no theory of Cohen's kind was ever positioned to explain them. Persistence is the theory's proper explanandum, and the mechanism objection, properly aimed, aims there: not at where relations come from, but at the process by which the beneficial ones, having come, stay and spread while their rivals wither. Even aimed at its proper target, the objection stands, since the staying and spreading are exactly what no specified process has been shown to govern.

### What separates a functional explanation from a claim about origins?

Its explanandum. The functional device addresses why an item persists and becomes prevalent once present, not how the item first arose, which belongs to the history of variation, accident, and invention. Confusing the two inflates the device beyond what any selection-style explanation attempts even in biology.

The separation repays attention in the classroom, where the slide between origin and persistence silently generates a large share of confused essays on this material. A student writes that feudal relations arose because developing agriculture required them, and the sentence fuses a claim about the circumstances of emergence with a claim about the ground of retention, attributing to requirement a generative power that no mechanism has been found to exercise. Cohen's own prose rarely commits the fusion, which is one reason his text rewards close reading as training. He writes of relations obtaining, persisting, and being reproduced, verbs of tenure rather than birth, and the discipline of the verbs is the discipline of the theory. Writers who inherit the theory without inheriting the verbs inherit its vulnerabilities in concentrated form.

## The consequence law under the microscope

Return to the logical core, because the debate's later stages are unintelligible without a firm grip on what Cohen claimed a consequence law is. In his formulation, a consequence law has the shape of a conditional linking an effect's would-be occurrence to an event's actual occurrence: if an event of a certain type would bring about a consequence of a certain type, then events of the first type tend to occur. The law is dispositional and counterfactual in structure. It does not say that the consequence causes the event in the manner of a billiard ball, travelling backward through time. It says that the world is so arranged that events are, as it were, filtered by their consequences, so that the ones with the specified consequences are the ones that show up and stay. Cohen's philosophical claim was that such laws can be true, can be known to be true, and can explain, independently of anyone's ability to describe the filter. His opponents' claim was that the filter is the whole content of the law, so that asserting the law while disavowing knowledge of the filter is asserting a grammatical shadow.

The dispute connects to a wider quarrel in the philosophy of science about whether explanation requires mechanisms at all, and situating Cohen's debate in that quarrel explains its longevity in the curriculum. One tradition, descending from the covering-law model, treats explanation as subsumption under generalisations and is correspondingly relaxed about mechanisms: if the generalisation is true and the case falls under it, explanation has occurred. A rival tradition, gathering strength through the period of Cohen's debate and dominant in the philosophy of biology and the social sciences since, treats mechanisms as the currency of explanation and generalisations without them as descriptions awaiting promotion. Cohen began closer to the first tradition and was pushed, by Elster's argument and by the temper of the times, toward the second. His concession can be read as a local instance of a general migration in the philosophy of explanation, which does not diminish its significance for Marxism but does explain why the migration's destination, a mechanism-demanding methodology, feels like common sense to readers who never witnessed the journey. The school that formed around the demand, whose founding text the series guide to analytical Marxism treats in full, made the migration its identity.

There is a technical refinement of Cohen's position that specialists regard as his most ingenious and that a complete account must include: his treatment of what he called the elaboration's dispensability through the analogy of the unknown drug. A physician in an earlier century might know that a preparation relieves fever, know it reliably and on good evidence, while knowing nothing of its pharmacology. The knowledge is real, the explanation of the patient's recovery in terms of the preparation is genuine, and the missing pharmacology is a gap in a different body of knowledge than the one the prescription draws on. Cohen argued that Marxists might know, on historical evidence, that relations persist when they develop the forces and collapse when they fetter them, with the same structure of justified ignorance about the intervening process. The analogy is the strongest card the defence holds, and the objection's answer to it is correspondingly the strongest card on the other side: the physician's ignorance was temporary and was known to be temporary because the domain, human physiology, was independently understood to run on mechanisms of the relevant kind. The social domain offers no comparable independent assurance. Whether history runs on feedback processes of the required determinacy is precisely what is in question, so the analogy borrows from biology the very assurance the social case lacks. The exchange between the drug analogy and that answer is, in miniature, the whole debate at its highest level, and it is the exchange a serious student should be able to reproduce before forming a view.

### Why must influence run from the effect back to the source?

Because the explanation claims that the effect is implicated in the presence of its own cause, and only a process running from the effect back to the maintenance of the cause can make that implication true rather than verbal. Without the loop, the effect and the presence merely coincide.

Feedback is a term of art that repays literal reading. A loop must close: the benefit produced by the relations must alter the environment in which relations are retained or discarded, in a direction that favours the benefit-producers. In the biological case the closure is exact, since the benefit is reproductive success and the environment of retention is reproduction itself; cause and effect share a currency. In the social case the benefit is the development of productive forces, while the environment of retention is woven from war, law, legitimacy, custom, and class power, currencies that exchange into productive development at rates nobody has stabilised. The looseness of the exchange is the quantitative face of the mechanism problem. Defenders who speak of feedback in the social case owe an account of the exchange rates, and the history of the debate is littered with feedback claims that dissolve, on inspection, into the observation that successful societies often have growing productive forces, which is the pattern restated rather than the loop closed.

One more distinction completes the logical survey, between strong and weak readings of the primacy thesis, because Cohen's later qualifications can be tracked as a migration between them. On the strong reading, the level of the forces explains the character of the relations with something approaching sufficiency: given the forces, the relations are broadly determined. On the weak reading, the forces constrain the relations, ruling out forms incompatible with their operation and development while leaving a range within which politics, culture, and accident decide. The weak reading is far easier to defend and far less distinctive, since almost every historian accepts that material constraints bind social forms somehow. The retreat's trajectory runs from the strong reading toward the weak one, and the interest of the trajectory is that the weak reading no longer needs the functional explanation in its full dress: constraint can be causal and forward-looking, a matter of what a given technology permits and forbids, without any claim that relations are explained by their developmental benefits. A theory can therefore survive the retreat in weakened form, and much contemporary historical sociology is, in effect, the weak reading with the functional scaffolding removed. Whether the remainder deserves the name of historical materialism is a question Cohen's later work leaves provocatively open.

## The turn to moral philosophy: why a defender of Marx went looking for a theory of justice

The second half of Cohen's career looks, from a distance, like a change of subject, and the appearance deceives. The philosopher who had spent two decades on the logic of historical explanation spent the following two on distributive justice, the concept of self-ownership, the moral demands of egalitarian commitment, and the question of what socialism would even be if its feasibility were granted. The continuity lies in the diagnosis that drove the move. Cohen came to believe that the socialist tradition he belonged to had made a fateful error in its theory of itself: it had supposed that moral argument was dispensable, a decoration for pamphlets, because history was on socialism's side and because the critique of capitalism could proceed on putatively scientific grounds, exploitation exposed as a mechanism rather than condemned as an injustice. If historical materialism were true and its predictions reliable, that self-denial might have been affordable. A movement guaranteed its destination by the laws of history can treat ethics as ballast. A movement whose guarantee has been withdrawn, by the very philosopher who stated the guarantee most rigorously, cannot. The normative turn is the retreat's other face: having ceased to be confident that history would deliver socialism, Cohen set about asking whether socialism deserved to be delivered, and what exactly it was that deserved it.

The turn had begun earlier than the public withdrawal of confidence, in a famous series of lectures and papers engaging Robert Nozick's libertarianism, and its first major fruit was the book Self-Ownership, Freedom, and Equality of 1995. The engagement is a model of the steelmanning this article's brief demands as a general standard, because Cohen took the libertarian's central premise at full strength rather than in the caricatured form convenient for refutation. The premise is self-ownership: each person is the rightful owner of her own body and powers, entitled to their fruits, and taxation of those fruits for redistribution is on a moral par with forced labour. Cohen saw that Marxists had historically relied on this very premise in their critique of exploitation, since the charge that the capitalist steals the worker's labour presupposes that the labour was the worker's to steal. The tradition thus stood in an embarrassing position, wielding a premise against capital while officially disavowing the moral vocabulary the premise belongs to. Cohen's response was to confront the premise directly and ask whether self-ownership is compatible with the equality socialists want, concluding that it is not, and that socialists must therefore choose their foundations knowingly rather than borrow the enemy's. The companion article on [whether exploitation is a moral or a technical notion](/2011/02/01/is-exploitation-moral-or-technical/) pursues the fork Cohen opened.

From that confrontation grew the work for which Cohen is most widely read outside Marxist studies: his contribution to the debate over the site and currency of egalitarian justice. Against the view that justice governs only the basic structure of institutions, leaving personal choices within just institutions morally unexamined, Cohen argued that an egalitarian ethos must inform the choices people make in daily economic life, since a society of formally just institutions inhabited by citizens who maximise without restraint will reproduce inequality through the accumulated grain of those choices. The argument was aimed at a sophisticated liberal egalitarianism that Cohen admired and had learned from, and it carried the same signature as the earlier work: take the opponent's framework seriously enough to find the point where it fails by its own lights. His book of 2000, If You're an Egalitarian, How Come You're So Rich?, turned the argument on its author's own class of affluent believers, himself included, and asked what consistency requires of people who profess equality while enjoying the fruits of inequality. The question's discomfort is deliberate and the book refuses the usual anaesthetics, neither excusing the affluent egalitarian as a helpless product of structure nor demanding a sainthood that would empty the position of adherents. It locates the obligation in between, in the reasons a person can honestly give for the distance between her principles and her practice.

### Why did Cohen turn from the theory of history to moral philosophy?

Because the retreat from historical materialism removed the guarantee that had made moral argument seem dispensable to socialists. If history would not deliver socialism by its own laws, then socialism had to be justified as just, and justified to people free to refuse it.

The sequence deserves emphasis because it reverses the usual story of intellectual disappointment, in which a scholar abandons a doctrine and drifts into unrelated work. Cohen's later subjects are the earlier subjects continued by other means. The theory of history had answered the question of socialism's possibility with a mechanism; when the mechanism failed specification, the question of socialism's desirability, which the mechanism had allowed the tradition to postpone, returned with compound interest. Even the style persisted: the later work distinguishes premises with the same patience, concedes the opponent's strongest points in the opponent's own terms, and treats the willingness to state what would change one's mind as a condition of being taken seriously. The defeater discipline that the verdict in this article carries is Cohen's own habit generalised, learned from the episode in which he practised it on himself at the highest cost.

There is also a biographical honesty in the later work that readers register without always naming. The child of Montreal communists, formed in a movement that expected history to vindicate it, spent his maturity constructing the two things the movement had lacked: a rigorous statement of its historical theory and, when that statement failed its own tests, a rigorous statement of its moral claim. The second construction is not a consolation prize. In the judgement of many philosophers who never shared the first project, the work on justice is the more durable achievement, the one that will be read when the September Group is a footnote. Cohen himself resisted rankings of that kind, treating the two projects as a single life's work under a single discipline. The discipline can be stated in one sentence, and students could do worse than copy it onto the flyleaf of their notebooks: say exactly what you claim, grant the objection its full force, and publish the result even when the result is that you were wrong.

## The camping trip, and the most honest sentence in the later work

Cohen's shortest book became his most famous, and its argument can be carried in a paragraph, which is part of its design. Why Not Socialism?, published in the year of his death, 2009, asks the reader to consider a camping trip among friends. On the trip, nobody owns the equipment privately in a way that lets them charge the others for its use; food is shared according to need and appetite rather than purchased; tasks are divided by ability and inclination; the strong do not leverage their strength into privilege, and the person who catches the most fish does not eat the most fish. The arrangements are not imposed by an authority. They are what the campers spontaneously regard as decent, and most readers, Cohen wagered, recognise the decency and would find its violation, a camper hoarding the best provisions or demanding payment for the use of a pan, faintly disgusting. Two principles do the work: a principle of equality of opportunity robust enough to cancel the advantages of luck and strength, and a principle of communal reciprocity, under which people serve one another not for payment but because the serving is part of the shared enterprise. The wager is that the reader endorses both principles at the scale of the trip. The question of the book's title then becomes precise: if the principles are right for the trip, and the trip is recognisably good, why are they wrong for the society?

Cohen's answer is the formulation this article's brief singles out as unusually honest, and it is worth giving slowly. The obstacle to socialism, he concluded, is not human nature. The campers demonstrate that human beings can operate by these principles gladly, without coercion and without sainthood, when the setting makes the principles practicable. The obstacle is that we do not know how to design the machinery that would let the principles govern at the scale of a whole society: the informational, motivational, and organisational apparatus that markets supply crudely and that no one has yet supplied well by other means. Socialism's problem, in the famous compression, is a design problem, not a verdict on the species. Every element of Cohen's career converges on that sentence. The philosopher of functional explanation, who had learned that a benefit without a mechanism is an assertion rather than an explanation, refused in his last book to assert the benefit of socialism without confessing the missing mechanism. The camping trip shows the benefit. The confession names the vacancy. The parallel with the earlier debate is exact, and Cohen, who never commented on the parallel in those terms, built it into the structure of his thought too deeply for it to be coincidence.

### What does the camping trip argument actually claim, and what does it not claim?

It claims that socialist principles command assent at small scale and that their non-adoption at large scale reflects an unsolved design problem rather than a defect in human nature. It does not claim that organising a society is as easy as organising a trip.

The disclaimer matters because the argument is routinely attacked in its inflated form, as though Cohen had proposed to run an economy on goodwill. He proposed nothing of the kind, and the book says so: the trip works partly because the campers know and monitor one another, because exit is easy and stakes are low, because the stock of goods is given rather than produced under uncertainty across decades. Scaling destroys each of those conditions, and the machinery question is, in the end, a question of what could replace them. Cohen's candour about the replacement's absence separates his socialism from the two varieties that dominate public argument: the variety that treats the machinery as a detail history will supply, which his early work had already ruined for him, and the variety that treats the desire for the machinery as a failure of commitment, which his moral philosophy existed to refuse. What remains after both refusals is a position of unusual spareness: socialism is desirable, its desirability can be argued rigorously, its feasibility is an open engineering problem in institutional design, and honesty requires holding all three clauses at once.

The reader who wants the entry point to Cohen that the brief recommends should start with that short book, for reasons beyond its brevity. It contains the whole later Cohen in miniature: the moral seriousness, the refusal of historical guarantees, the insistence that an attractive destination does not conjure a road. From there the path runs backward to the justice work for those whose interests are philosophical, and backward to the defence for those whose interests are historical, and the two paths illuminate each other at every step. The defence explains why the short book contains no prophecy. The short book explains what the defence's author thought the argument had been for, once prophecy was gone: not the management of an inevitable future, but the clarification of a choice that people remain free to make badly.

## The replies in detail: how the defence adjusted under fire

Between the objection of 1982 and the preface of 2000 lies a sequence of adjustments that specialists reconstruct from Cohen's papers of the intervening years, and the sequence repays reconstruction because each adjustment was reasonable in itself while the accumulation was fatal. The first adjustment concerned the status of elaborations. In the book, their absence had been a minor matter, a gap in the commentary rather than in the theory. In the replies, Cohen granted that a functional explanation unsupported by any known elaboration carries a standing obligation, and that Marxists had too often treated the obligation as discharged by the explanation's plausibility. The grant sounds small. It relocated the debate from logic to evidence, from whether mechanisms are required in principle to whether the historical record, searched diligently, discloses them, and on evidential ground the defence was weaker than on logical ground, since the record is exactly where fettering episodes refuse to behave uniformly.

The second adjustment concerned the development thesis and its premises. Critics had pressed the premise of human rationality with awkward cases: societies that possessed the knowledge for productive advances and declined them, ruling strata that rationally preferred a smaller product they controlled to a larger one they would not, cultures that priced stability above growth without any failure of intelligence. Cohen's responses narrowed the tendency's scope conditions, specifying the circumstances under which scarcity, rationality, and intelligence could be expected to issue in development and conceding that the circumstances are themselves historical products. Each narrowing was defensible. Together they transformed a law of history into a conditional generalisation whose antecedent conditions do much of the explanatory work, and a primacy thesis built on a conditional tendency inherits the conditionality. The strong theory of 1978, in which the forces' development drives the succession of epochs, became by degrees a framework in which development, when it occurs and is not blocked, constrains and selects among relations, a claim no sensible historian denies and no distinctively Marxist theory needs.

The third adjustment was the deepest and the least announced. Cohen increasingly presented the functional explanation as a claim about what he called the character of the theory rather than about the credentials of each of its applications, as though historical materialism were a functional theory in the way a map is a Mercator projection: globally committed to a form, locally approximate. The presentation is attractive and it concedes the objection's substance, since a theory globally committed to a form it cannot locally elaborate has precisely the standing Elster assigned it, that of a heuristic programme rather than an established explanation. By the time the preface withdrew confidence in the theory's truth, the withdrawal ratified a position the adjustments had already reached. The documentary sequence is therefore best read not as a sudden capitulation but as a long, honest negotiation between a philosopher and his own standards, conducted in public, at the pace of academic publishing. Readers who want the wider story of the school that hosted the negotiation should consult the companion survey of the case against the theory alongside this one, since the mechanism debate is the load-bearing wall of that larger case.

### What would a genuine feedback process in history have to look like?

It would have to operate independently of the outcomes it explains, discriminate reliably between relations that develop the forces and relations that fetter them, and eliminate or marginalise the fettering kind within a timescale the theory's transitions can tolerate, all without presupposing the development it is meant to secure.

Each clause of that specification corresponds to a failure mode of the candidates actually proposed. Independence excludes processes described only through their successes, the historiographical habit of finding the mechanism wherever the outcome was favourable and its absence wherever the outcome was not. Discrimination excludes class struggle taken neat, since struggle serves distribution as readily as development and can entrench fettering relations behind victorious classes whose rents depend on them. Timescale excludes the appeal to the very long run, in which any failure can be reclassified as a delay; a selection process that takes a millennium to eliminate an obstructive form explains the millennium badly and the form's eventual fall trivially, since everything falls eventually. Non-presupposition excludes purposive reform by rulers who already understand the theory, since a mechanism that requires its operators to believe the theory cannot explain the epochs that preceded the theory's formulation. Stating the specification this plainly is the article's way of keeping its own defeater honest: the invitation issued in the assessment below is not a rhetorical flourish but a checklist, and any future defender who believes the mechanism has been found should be directed to the checklist and asked to initial each clause.

It is equally important to record what the specification does not demand, lest the standard be caricatured as impossible by design. It does not demand a single process; biology itself supplements selection with drift, constraint, and exaptation, and a family of historical processes could jointly satisfy the clauses if their combined operation discriminated reliably. It does not demand determinism; selection in biology is statistical, and a historical mechanism could be statistical too, provided the statistics are stated and the sample is large enough to bear them. It does not demand that the process be invisible to the actors; purposive elements can belong to a genuine mechanism when their distribution and effects are themselves explained rather than assumed. What it demands is determinacy of the same kind natural selection possesses: components named in advance, operations described without reference to particular outcomes, and a track record of discriminating correctly in cases the theorist did not choose. Measured against that demand, generously interpreted, the historical candidates remain gestures toward a mechanism rather than the thing itself, and the gesture's inadequacy after forty years of effort by able people is itself a datum the assessment must weigh.

## The debate's second life in the philosophy of social science

Ideas that lose at home sometimes win abroad, and the functional explanation debate has enjoyed a long second life in disciplines that never cared about Marx's theory for its own sake. Philosophers of biology cite the exchange when teaching the difference between etiological accounts of function, which ground what a trait is for in the history that selected it, and dispositional accounts, which ground it in the trait's contribution to a system's capacities. Cohen's consequence explanation maps instructively onto the border between those accounts, and his insistence that explanation can precede elaboration is quoted by dispositionalists while Elster's demand is quoted by etiologists, so that a debate staged inside Marxism became a standard exhibit in a controversy about explanation as such. Sociologists meet the exchange in methods training as the canonical warning against functionalist reasoning about institutions, and the warning's canonical status owes much to the fact that the target conceded. A warning illustrated by a living defender's retreat teaches differently from a warning illustrated by a dead doctrine's embarrassment. Political scientists meet it in the literature on institutional persistence, where the question of why inefficient institutions endure is precisely the question of what feedback processes fail to eliminate them, and where Cohen's framework, inverted, supplies the research agenda: find the mechanism of persistence, since benefit alone will not explain it.

The uptake in political philosophy has already been described through Cohen's own later work, but its reach extends beyond his pen. The debate about whether justice applies to personal conduct as well as institutions, the debate about whether self-ownership can be squared with equality, and the debate about what feasibility constraints do to ideal theory all run through channels Cohen dug. Feasibility is the connecting theme, and it is the theory of history returning in normative dress. A theory of justice that ignores the mechanisms by which its principles could be realised repeats, in Cohen's mature judgement, the error of a theory of history that asserted benefits without mechanisms: it confuses the attractiveness of an outcome with an account of its attainment. His insistence that socialists state their design problem honestly is the mechanism requirement applied to the future instead of the past. The unity of the career, visible from this altitude, is a unity of methodological conscience, and it is why the profile of Cohen belongs in a series on Marxism even though its subject ended by doubting the series' founding theory. He is the tradition's severest internal examiner, and a tradition is known by the examiners it produces and survives.

### How does the mechanism requirement apply beyond the borders of Marxism?

Wherever an institution is explained by the good it does, the requirement asks what process connects the good to the institution's tenure. It disciplines functionalist habits in economics, sociology, and institutional theory exactly as it disciplined the theory of history, because the logical form, and its characteristic gap, are the same.

Consider the explanation of a legal doctrine by the certainty it brings to commerce, or of a bureaucratic procedure by the errors it prevents, or of a platform's design by the engagement it maximises. Each explanation cites a benefit. Each is respectable in proportion as a feedback process can be exhibited: litigants selecting forums, agencies punished for visible failures, users deserting designs that waste their attention. Where the process exists, the explanation graduates from narrative to mechanism and can be tested against cases where the benefit persists but the institution falls, or the institution persists while the benefit lapses. Where the process is absent, the explanation remains a story told from the winner's podium. The requirement's portability is the surest sign that the debate was never only about Marx. It was about a temptation built into social explanation itself, the temptation to read purposes off outcomes, and Cohen's career is the record of a man who first gave the temptation its most rigorous defence and then, finding the defence incomplete by his own lights, said so in print and moved his life's work to ground where the same discipline could be honoured in full.

## Misreadings that survive, and the corrections the record supports

Four misreadings circulate persistently enough to warrant explicit correction, and the brief for this article lists them as part of its charge. The first is that Cohen abandoned Marxism wholesale and ended as a critic of the tradition from outside. The record shows a man who withdrew confidence from one theory within the tradition, the theory of history, while continuing to identify as a socialist, to defend egalitarian conclusions by moral argument, and to regard Marx's questions as the right ones. Abandonment of a theory is not abandonment of a tradition, and the distinction is not pedantry: the later work's entire point is that the tradition's commitments survive its science and must be re-founded as ethics. The second misreading is that the concession was total, a surrender on every front. The concession was specific: confidence in the truth of historical materialism, withdrawn for a specified reason, the missing mechanism, while the reconstruction's value, the functional form's legitimacy, and the socialist conclusion were all expressly retained. A reader who reports total surrender has read the headline of the preface and none of its sentences.

The third misreading reverses the direction of authorship on the primacy thesis, treating it as Marx's own formulation that Cohen merely transmitted. Marx supplied the materials and the functional grammar; the two-thesis architecture, the named primacy, and the defence's argumentative order are Cohen's construction, openly offered as a reconstruction and debatable as one. The misreading flatters neither man: it denies Cohen his originality and burdens Marx with a precision he never attempted, making the theory's failure look like a failure of transcription rather than what it was, the failure of the best achievable statement. The fourth misreading inflates the camping trip into a proposal, as though Cohen had recommended organising production on the ethos of a holiday. The book recommends nothing organisational; it isolates a moral datum, that people endorse socialist principles where those principles are practicable, and a confession, that practicability at scale is an unsolved design problem. Inflating the book into a blueprint makes it refutable by logistics and misses its actual force, which is to relocate the argument about socialism from human nature, where socialists win the camping trip and lose the sneer, to institutional design, where the honest work remains undone.

### Which misreading of the primacy thesis causes the most confusion in practice?

The reading that treats primacy as a claim about importance or chronological priority, as though Cohen had ranked technology first among historical causes in every inquiry. Primacy names an explanatory direction between two specified relata, forces and relations, and claims nothing about the many inquiries where the direction reverses.

The confusion is understandable because the word primacy invites ranking in the loose sense, and because textbooks compressing Cohen into a paragraph reach for the loose sense to save space. The cost of the compression is that students then refute a claim Cohen never made, pointing to episodes where law, war, or religion plainly drove events, and believe they have damaged the defence. Cohen's claim survives those episodes untouched, since it concerns the explanation of production relations by productive forces and is silent about the explanation of battles, sermons, and statutes. What damages the defence is not the plurality of historical causes, which Cohen never denied, but the absence of the one mechanism his own claim required. Teaching the distinction between the claim made and the claim imagined is the fastest route to teaching the debate itself, and teachers who build a session around the substitution report that the mechanism objection, once aimed at the real target, needs no further advocacy from them.

## Why philosophers take Cohen seriously, including those who reject his conclusions

A useful test of a thinker's standing is the quality of the attention paid by opponents, and by that test Cohen's standing is exceptional. Libertarians treated his engagement with self-ownership as the one they had to answer, because he granted their premise its full force and located the disagreement at the exact premise where it lives, rather than at the caricature where it is easily beaten. Liberal egalitarians treated his critique of the basic-structure restriction as the objection their view had to absorb, and the literature on the site of justice that followed is unintelligible without his intervention. Historians of Marxism who reject the reconstruction still teach it, because no rival statement of the theory of history offers students so clean a surface to argue against. Even the mechanism objection, the instrument of his own theory's demotion, raised his standing: a philosopher whose work provokes a criticism that precise, from a colleague that able, has built something worth criticising precisely. The September Group's internal culture, in which Cohen was the senior figure and Elster the insurgent, modelled a way for a school to be serious: shared premises, no shared conclusions guaranteed, and prestige allocated to whoever's argument survived the room. Schools that allocate prestige to loyalty produce apologetics; the September Group allocated it to survival under fire, and its two most famous products are a defence and the objection that undid the defence, both still in print, both still assigned, bound together in the curriculum as a single lesson in how inquiry is supposed to feel from the inside.

There is a further reason for the seriousness, less often articulated, concerning the relation between Cohen's two careers. Political philosophy in the late twentieth century contained many brilliant technicians of distributive principle who had never asked whether the social world could be made to run on their principles, and many theorists of history who had never posed the question whether the destination their history promised was worth wanting. Cohen asked both questions with the same mind and let each discipline audit the other. The theory of history taught the moral philosopher that principles without mechanisms are wishes; the moral philosophy taught the theorist of history that mechanisms without justification are merely predictions, and that a prediction of victory is not a reason for allegiance. The double audit is the career's real unity, and it explains why readers who care about neither Marxism nor analytical method still find the books bracing: they watch a first-rate intelligence refuse, twice and at cost, the comforts its own side offered.

### Did Cohen prove Marx right, and what is the accurate short answer?

He proved that Marx's theory of history could be stated as a rigorous, examinable theory, which no one had done before, and he established the terms on which its truth would have to be judged. On those terms, by his own later assessment, the theory's central explanatory claim remains unestablished.

The short answer matters because the long literature invites two lazy summaries, that Cohen vindicated Marx and that Cohen refuted him, and both are wrong in instructive ways. Vindication fails because the defence's central device was left unelaborated and its author withdrew confidence. Refutation fails because no critic demonstrated the theory false; the objection demonstrated it incomplete at the decisive joint, and incompleteness at a joint is compatible with the theory being, in some repaired form, true. The accurate summary is the one the short answer gives: Cohen's achievement was to convert a doctrine into a theory, with the conversion's full price attached, the price being that theories can fail tests doctrines never sit. Traditions that resist conversion protect themselves from failure at the cost of never being known to succeed either, and Cohen's career is the standing argument that the conversion is worth its price. Students who grasp that point have grasped why the analytical school mattered beyond its conclusions, and why its history is taught as method and not only as Marxism.

## The texts in sequence: a reader's map through the defence and the retreat

Because the episode is documentary, the order of reading matters, and a reader who follows the sequence experiences the argument as its participants did, which no summary can reproduce. The defence comes first, in the edition of 1978 or its expanded successor, with attention to the chapters where the functional interpretation is introduced and where the primacy thesis receives its canonical statement. The reader should note, as an exercise in the article's method, how much of the book is specification rather than advocacy: definitions of the forces that exclude what cannot belong to them, definitions of relations that separate ownership's legal dress from its economic substance, and the careful quarantining of the superstructure's explanation from the base's, so that the functional claim is made exactly where Cohen wants it and nowhere else. The objection comes second, in Elster's article of 1982 and in the relevant chapters of his book of 1985, Making Sense of Marx, where the demand for mechanisms is set inside a general programme for the social sciences and the reader can judge whether the demand is a local weapon against Marxism or a universal standard its author applies impartially, including to positions he favours. The evidence supports impartiality, and impartiality is much of the reason the objection could not be dismissed as factional.

The replies and qualifications come third, scattered through Cohen's papers of the nineteen eighties and nineties, several collected in the volume History, Labour, and Freedom of 1988, where the defence is maintained with increasing explicitness about what it needs and has not got. The preface of 2000 comes fourth and should be read entire rather than in the excerpted sentence that circulates, because the sentence's force depends on its surroundings: a scholar reviewing his life's first work, acknowledging its virtues without flinching, and recording the precise respect in which he can no longer stand behind its central hope. The later work comes last and in the order of its own logic: the self-ownership book for the foundations, the egalitarian-ethos papers and the book of 2000 for the demands of consistency, the essays gathered in Rescuing Justice and Equality of 2008 for the mature statement on justice and feasibility, and the camping trip book of 2009 as the coda that a reader who has followed the sequence will hear in its full resonance, as the work of a man who had stopped expecting history to agree with him and had decided to be worth agreeing with anyway.

Publication facts in that map are given as the historical record they are, in keeping with this article's low decay risk: the texts are fixed, their sequence is fixed, and the debate they compose is closed in the sense that its principals have stated their final positions, Cohen's death in 2009 having ended a career whose last decade was spent, characteristically, on the subject his first decade's failure had opened. What remains open is only the defeater, the standing possibility that a mechanism of the required determinacy will be specified by some future defender, and the assessment section below states the terms of that possibility as precisely as the record allows.

## The assessment: a verdict, its grounds, and the condition that would reverse it

The verdict this article is charged to deliver can at last be stated with the precision the whole discussion has been building toward. Historical materialism, in the strongest statement its ablest defender could give it, lacks a feedback mechanism connecting the benefits it cites to the persistence of the arrangements it explains. The lack is not an incidental omission that ordinary scholarly progress can be expected to repair in due course; it persisted through two decades of concentrated effort by the very school best equipped and most motivated to repair it, and it was finally acknowledged by the defender whose reputation was staked on the repair. The judgement that follows is therefore not that the theory is false, which the record does not show, but that its central explanatory claim stands unestablished at exactly the point where the claim does its distinctive work, and that a reader deciding how much confidence to invest in the theory should invest it accordingly: high confidence in the reconstruction as an account of what the theory says, moderate confidence in the weak, constraint-based reading of primacy as a historiographical heuristic, and low confidence in the strong functional theory of history as an established explanation of why epochs succeed one another.

Every verdict in this cluster of articles carries the statement of what would reverse it, and this article's defeater is neither vague nor safely impossible. The judgement that historical materialism lacks a feedback mechanism stands unless one is specified with the determinacy of natural selection: a process, or family of processes, whose components are named independently of the outcomes to be explained, whose operation discriminates reliably between relations that develop the productive forces and relations that fetter them, whose timescale fits the transitions the theory narrates, and whose existence can be checked in cases the theorist did not select. The specification of such a mechanism, by any defender, at any future date, defeats the judgement, and this article issues the invitation in earnest. The invitation is earnest because the standard is the one the theory's own best defender came to accept, because meeting it would be a genuine intellectual event worth more than the verdict it overturned, and because a criticism that cannot say what would answer it has degenerated into the kind of unfalsifiable posture the analytical school existed to cure. Cohen's ghost, if the metaphor may be forgiven in an article that has policed metaphors strictly, would insist on the invitation's sincerity: he spent a decade hoping someone would meet the standard, and his preface records the hope's expiry, not its insincerity.

Pending that event, the mechanism requirement stands as the debate's bequest to every reader who explains anything by its benefits. It is a small instrument, easily carried: whenever an account says that an arrangement exists because of what it achieves, ask what process achieves the arrangement's survival when it ceases to achieve, or before it began to. If the account can answer, it has graduated from pattern to explanation. If it cannot, it has located the exact point where its work remains to be done, and honesty consists in marking the point rather than papering it with the word because. Cohen's defence and Cohen's retreat, read together, are the finest available training in the use of that instrument, which is why this article has treated a controversy inside Marxist theory at a length its importance to the philosophy of explanation, and to the conduct of intellectual honesty, fully justifies.

There is a final symmetry worth recording before the article turns to its guides for particular readers. The young Cohen set out to defend Marx against the charge that his theory was not a theory at all, merely a philosophy of history in the pejorative sense, a narrative with pretensions. The defence succeeded in its formal aim: after 1978, nobody could say the theory had not been stated as a theory. The old Cohen recorded that the theory, stated as a theory, had not been shown true. Between those two sentences lies a complete education in what rigour gives and what it costs, and the tradition that produced both sentences, the tradition of Marxism examined by its own most demanding adherents, emerges from the episode with its dignity altered but intact: diminished as a science of history, enlarged as a school of argument. The school's other products, the microfoundations programme and the egalitarian philosophy among them, are treated in their own articles in this series, beginning with the guide to the [analytical Marxism school as a whole](/2020/01/01/analytical-marxism-complete-guide/), to which readers whose appetite this profile has whetted are directed, along with the study companion described in the sections that follow.

## Before Cohen: the philosophy of functional analysis he inherited and revised

Cohen did not invent the suspicion of functional explanation, and his defence gains depth when read against the philosophical literature that preceded it, because he wrote with that literature open on the desk. The logician Carl Hempel had subjected functional analysis to a celebrated critique two decades earlier, arguing that to explain an item by its function is, strictly, to explain it by one of its effects, and that such explanations derive their apparent force from smuggled assumptions: that the system in question has needs, that the item is indispensable to meeting them, and that no functional equivalent could do the job instead. Where equivalents exist, and in social life they nearly always do, the explanation fails to explain why this item rather than its equivalent is present. Hempel's critique set the terms in which a generation of philosophers received functional talk in social science: as a heuristic for generating hypotheses, legitimate as a prelude to causal inquiry, illegitimate as its conclusion. His defence reads, on inspection, as a direct reply to that settlement. He accepted Hempel's demand for rigour and rejected Hempel's conclusion, arguing that the indispensability assumption is not required where a consequence law holds, since the law itself, if true, explains the prevalence of the type even when tokens vary. Whether the reply succeeds is the debate this article has followed, but its address is unmistakable: Cohen was not a naïve functionalist surprised by philosophy's objections. He was a philosopher answering the strongest extant version of the objection he knew, which makes the later objection's success, against a defence built to withstand Hempel, all the more significant.

The anthropological tradition supplied a second inheritance. Malinowski had explained institutions by the needs they satisfied for individuals; Radcliffe-Brown, by their contribution to the continuity of the social structure; and Merton, in the most sophisticated statement of the sociological version, had complicated the picture with latent functions, consequences unintended and unrecognised that nonetheless sustain the practices producing them. Cohen's device differs from all three in the identity of its beneficiary and the austerity of its logic, as the earlier separation from functionalism established, but Merton's latency is the closest cousin, since a consequence law in Cohen's sense typically operates behind the backs of the agents whose conduct sustains it. The kinship clarifies what the mechanism objection demands in Mertonian cases too: a latent function explains only when some process translates the unrecognised benefit into the practice's retention, and Merton's own examples often smuggle the process in through the plausibility of the narrative. Elster's demand, generalised, is that the smuggling stop, in Marxism first because Marxism's claims are largest, but in principle everywhere the latent-function idiom is used. A reader who sees the debate in this longer lineage understands why it could not be contained within Marxist studies: Cohen had volunteered his theory as the test case for a question the whole human sciences had been deferring.

There is a third inheritance, from the Marxist tradition itself, that Cohen's reconstruction quietly revised. The classical statements of historical materialism in the generation after Marx, in Engels's letters and in the codifications of the Second International, had oscillated between a deterministic reading, in which the economic base produces its effects with something like natural necessity, and a softer reading in which the economy determines only in the last instance, a phrase that licensed indefinite postponement of the question of how. Cohen's functional reading cut through the oscillation by specifying the sense of determination at issue: not production, not last-instance influence, but explanation by developmental benefit, with the consequence law as its form. The specification was a genuine clarification of the tradition's own muddiest concept, and it is one of the reconstruction's permanent gains, independent of the claim's truth. After Cohen, a Marxist who says the base determines the rest owes an account of which determination is meant, and the menu of respectable options is shorter and clearer than the one the tradition inherited. Clarification of that order does not expire when the clarified claim fails; it remains as the map on which the failure is charted.

## The Brenner test: what happened when the theses met a concrete transition

Abstract debates about explanatory form acquire their sharpest test when a concrete historical controversy forces the forms to compete over the same evidence, and the controversy over the transition from feudalism to capitalism in Europe supplied that test during the very years of Cohen's defence. The demographic and commercialisation accounts of the transition explained the dissolution of feudal relations by population movements and market growth, treating the relations as adjusting, in effect functionally, to pressures from the development of exchange and technique. Robert Brenner's intervention argued that the same pressures produced opposite outcomes in different parts of Europe, strengthening serfdom in the east while dissolving it in the west, and that the variable explaining the divergence was the prior structure of class relations and the balance of class forces, not the level of the productive forces or the pressure of the market. The implication for Cohen's architecture was direct and uncomfortable. If relations of production explain divergent developments under identical material pressures, then the explanatory arrow between forces and relations runs, in the best-documented transition the theory possesses, substantially in the direction primacy forbids, or at least with a strength primacy cannot accommodate.

Cohen's defenders responded along lines the framework made available: primacy is a claim about long-run explanation rather than about each episode; class structures themselves require explanation, and their divergence may trace to earlier differences in productive development; Brenner's cases concern the timing and form of transitions rather than their eventual direction. Each response has merit, and the debate they compose is among the most instructive in modern historiography. But the episode illustrates, in concrete dress, the abstract difficulty the mechanism objection names. The functional reading predicts that relations track the requirements of development; the record shows relations tracking the outcomes of class struggles whose relation to development is contingent; and the bridge between the prediction and the record is built, case by case, from auxiliary hypotheses about lags, blockages, and eventual corrections. Auxiliary hypotheses are not sins, and every mature science owns a stock of them. The question the objection presses is whether the stock is growing faster than the theory's confirmed content, which is the classic symptom of a programme in its degenerating phase. Cohen's later movement between primacy's strong and weak readings can be tracked, in part, as his response to exactly this historiographical pressure, arriving from the archives to reinforce the pressure Elster applied from logic. The defence was being squeezed from both ends, by the philosophy it had invited and by the history it claimed to explain, and the squeeze, more than any single argument, is what the retreat records.

The test also clarifies what a successful elaboration would have to accomplish in practice, which the abstract specification can leave vague. It would have to explain, through one process or a coherent family, both the western dissolution and the eastern intensification of serfdom under similar demographic and commercial conditions, showing how the process discriminated between the outcomes by reference to the forces' development rather than by reference to the victors' rents. No extant account does this without remainder, and the remainder is invariably filled by the class-power variables that belong to the rival explanation. A defender might reply that the demand is unfair, since no theory explains its domain without remainder. The reply is sound and insufficient: the objection never demanded completeness, only a mechanism whose discriminating variable is the one the theory names. In the best-studied transition in the theory's home territory, the discriminating variable the evidence keeps returning is not that variable. That is the missing mechanism viewed from the archive's side, and it deserves a place in any honest assessment beside the logical face Elster polished.

## The moral argument the tradition made while denying it made one

A thread runs between the theory of history and the theory of justice, through a specific embarrassment that Cohen did more than anyone to expose, and following the thread shows the normative turn to be a reckoning rather than a defection. Marxists had always condemned capitalism, and the condemnation's emotional and rhetorical centre was exploitation: the worker produces more than she receives, the difference accumulates as another's property, and the arrangement is renewed daily behind a façade of free exchange. Cohen asked a simple question about that condemnation: is it a moral judgement or is it not? If it is not, if exploitation names a technical transfer with no more normative charge than evaporation, then the tradition's indignation is a category error sustained for a century. If it is, then the tradition possesses a theory of justice in fact while disavowing one in name, since a judgement that a distribution is exploitative and therefore wrong is a judgement about what people are owed. The disavowal had historical roots Cohen knew intimately: Marx's own polemics against moralising socialists, the scientific self-image of the later movement, the suspicion that justice talk is ideology in judicial dress. Cohen's conclusion was that the roots did not justify the disavowal's continuation. A movement that condemns must be able to state its standard, and stating the standard is moral philosophy, to be done well or badly but not to be evaded by pretending the condemnation is a measurement.

The question ramified. If exploitation is unjust, unjust by what standard: by the standard that each should receive the full product of her labour, which presupposes a right of self-ownership the tradition elsewhere scorned; or by a standard of equality, which condemns many transfers besides exploitation and some that no capitalist is involved in; or by a standard of need, which condemns arrangements exploitation never touches? Cohen's self-ownership work, surveyed earlier, is his sustained attempt to force the tradition to choose its standard knowingly, and his own choice fell on equality disciplined by community, the principles the camping trip later dramatised. The point for this profile is the continuity of method. In the theory of history, Cohen demanded that the tradition state its explanation exactly and accept the examination exactness invites. In moral philosophy, he required the tradition to state its standard exactly and accept the same examination. The demands are one demand, addressed to a movement that had survived for a century on the fertility of its ambiguities, and the examiner's willingness to publish the examination's results, in both cases unfavourable to the position he started from, is the career's through-line and its claim on the reader's respect.

The thread also explains a feature of the later work that puzzles readers who arrive from the justice literature alone: its preoccupation with feasibility and with the psychology of actual egalitarians. A moral philosopher who had never been a theorist of history might treat feasibility as an afterthought, a matter for engineers once the principles are fixed. Cohen could not, because he had watched a theory of history founder on exactly the gap between an attractive pattern and the mechanism that would sustain it, and he recognised the same gap in normative theory wearing different clothes. A principle of equality that no institutional arrangement can implement, and no human ethos can stabilise, stands to justice as a consequence law without elaboration stood to history: it may be true as a pattern of the good, but it does not yet explain or guide anything in the world where guidance is needed. His mature work therefore refuses the separation between the ideal and its machinery that much contemporary theory practises as a division of labour. The refusal culminates in his treatment of principles themselves, where he argued that fundamental principles are insensitive to facts, that facts ground rules of regulation rather than principles, and that confusing the levels produces exactly the mush his whole career opposed. Readers need not accept the metaethical claim to learn from the discipline beneath it: know which of your claims are principles, which are mechanisms, and which are patterns, and do not let any of them borrow the credentials of the others.

## The incentives argument and the ethos: the later work's centre of gravity

The single most discussed argument of Cohen's later period deserves a fuller statement than the survey above could give it, because it displays the mature method at its most formidable and because students of this series will meet it wherever egalitarian theory is taught. The target is the difference principle in its most influential liberal form, under which inequalities are justified when they work to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged. The standard application permits high rewards to talented producers on the ground that the rewards elicit efforts whose fruits, taxed and transferred, leave the worst off better than strict equality would. Cohen's question is disarmingly simple: better by whose choice? The talented could produce the same fruits without the premium if they were themselves committed to the principle they invoke, working at ordinary rewards in the knowledge that the arrangement already benefits the least advantaged as much as equality permits. The inequality, on inspection, exists not because justice requires it but because the talented withhold their effort unless bribed, and a principle that ratifies the bribe as justice has confused a fact about people's willingness with a requirement of fairness. Kidneys concentrate the point in Cohen's celebrated analogy: a society that paid donors would elicit more kidneys and benefit the worst off thereby, yet something has gone wrong if the resulting inequality of sacrifice is pronounced just rather than merely expedient under regrettable motivational facts.

The argument's force is that it is internal. It does not import a socialist premise into a liberal theory; it takes the theory's own commitment, that inequalities stand in need of justification to those who end up worst under them, and asks whether the justification survives being addressed, in the second person, to an actual talented person who could act otherwise. Justifications that can only be stated in the third person, as facts about what people in general will do, fail a test Cohen regarded as fundamental: a principle of justice should be capable of governing the deliberation of the agents it describes, not merely of describing the equilibrium those agents reach when none of them is governed by it. The parallel with the earlier work is exact and worth stating. A consequence law that no mechanism sustains describes a pattern without explaining it; a principle of justice that no agent's deliberation sustains describes an equilibrium without justifying it. In both cases Cohen's demand is for the missing middle term, the process or the ethos through which the pattern is produced by the beings the theory is about. The demand made him the most inconvenient ally egalitarianism has had, since it is addressed not to the doctrine's enemies but to its comfortable adherents, the readers of his book of 2000 who recognised themselves in its title and were meant to.

Critics of the ethos argument have replied that it proves too much, moralising every market choice and dissolving the division between institutional design and personal virtue that makes liberal societies livable. The replies are serious, and Cohen engaged them with the same patience he had given Elster, conceding the need for rules of regulation that fall short of principle and govern daily conduct permissibly. The concession structure is familiar by this point in the article: periphery first, centre last, the centre being the claim that justice itself, as distinct from the rules we adopt under non-ideal conditions, cannot bless an inequality whose only ground is that the better off would otherwise decline to cooperate. Whether the centre holds is a live question in political philosophy, and this article takes no side beyond noting that the argument exhibits, in normative dress, the identical intellectual virtue as the defence and the retreat: it states a claim so exactly that its opponents must meet it exactly or not at all.

## The objector's programme, and why its universality mattered

Elster's objection persuaded in part because it was visibly not manufactured for the occasion. The demand for mechanisms belonged to a general methodology its author applied across the social sciences, to rational-choice theory's complacencies as readily as to Marxism's, and the book that carried the demand into Marxist studies also criticised functionalist habits in sociology, teleological habits in history writing, and the loose invocation of class interests wherever an outcome happened to suit a class. A standard applied to friend and foe alike has a credibility that a standard forged for one target cannot achieve, and Cohen, who knew the author's range at first hand from the September Group's meetings, never attempted the dismissal that a factional reading would have permitted. The profile of the objector belongs to another article in this series, and the present one confines itself to the objection's career, but the universality deserves its paragraph here because it explains the retreat's timing and completeness. A defender can outwait a polemic. A defender cannot outwait a methodology he himself shares, applied by a colleague he himself respects, to a gap he himself can see. The three conditions together made the concession, in retrospect, overdetermined, and they make the episode a poor parable for readers who want intellectual life to be a contest of wills. It was a contest of standards, and the standards won against both men: against Cohen's theory, which failed them, and against Elster's hopes for a fully mechanistic social science, which the subsequent decades have honoured only in part.

The programme that grew from the objection, the demand for microfoundations, reshaped analytical Marxism's agenda in ways that outlasted the theory it was aimed at. If large patterns must be elaborated through individual action, then the theory's key transitions had to be rebuilt as the outcomes of situated choices, and the school's energies migrated accordingly: toward game-theoretic models of class compromise, toward accounts of ideology as the beliefs that make certain equilibria stable, toward the study of how norms and organisations solve or fail to solve the collective action problems that the old theory had dissolved into the movement of structures. Some of that work is excellent and much of it quietly abandons the primacy thesis, explaining outcomes by the strategic landscape rather than by the forces' requirements, which is the objection's victory continued by other means. A companion article in this series, devoted to the microfoundations debate, traces that migration in full. For the purposes of this profile, the migration's significance is that it constitutes the school's own verdict on the debate, delivered in the currency scholars actually use, which is their choice of problems. A research community that believed the mechanism would be found would have kept looking for it at the level of epochs. It looked instead at the level of agents, and the looking has not returned.

There is an irony in the destination that neither principal emphasised. Microfoundations explain patterns by the choices of individuals, and among the choices that most need explaining, on the theory's own ground, is why individuals so often act against the class interests the old theory assigned them, organising weakly where it predicted strength and consenting where it predicted revolt. The explanations that emerged, turning on information, identity, selective incentives, and the strategic structure of collective action, are mechanisms of exactly the kind Elster demanded, and they elaborate a social science in which the grand functional pattern is not vindicated but replaced. Cohen watched the replacement with characteristic fairness, praising the rigour and regretting the loss of scale, and his later work can be read as an attempt to recover at the level of principle the ambition the school had surrendered at the level of explanation. If history could no longer be shown to move toward justice, justice could at least be defined with a rigour the movement had never previously achieved, so that whenever the movement of history became anyone's business again, the destination would be specified. The two projects, the failed explanation and the successful definition, stand as the twin monuments of the analytical episode, and a reader of this series should hold both in view when the school's exam guide asks what the whole experiment achieved.

## A parallel from within: the exploitation theory's similar ordeal

The mechanism debate did not exhaust the analytical school's self-examination, and a brief parallel with its other flagship illustrates how general the discipline of specification proved to be. John Roemer's reconstruction of exploitation, built in the same years and the same rooms, replaced the labour theory of value with a property-relations definition: a group is exploited if it would do better by withdrawing from the economy with its per capita share of assets. The definition is a model of the analytical virtues, exact, testable in models, and free of the value theory's embarrassments. Yet it faced, in due course, its own version of the specification problem: the definition classifies states of affairs without yet explaining the processes that produce and reproduce them, and its critics pressed the gap between classification and explanation with arguments structurally identical to Elster's against Cohen. Roemer's response differed from Cohen's in temper, treating the definition as a diagnostic instrument whose explanatory work was always meant to be done by accompanying accounts of asset accumulation and political power, but the structural parallel is the point. Analytical Marxism's characteristic achievement was to state the tradition's claims so cleanly that their missing middles became visible, and its characteristic fate was to discover missing middles in claim after claim. A movement less honest would have experienced the discoveries as a series of defeats. The school's own retrospective literature tends to describe them as the work itself: the tradition, examined at last by its own best standards, turned out to contain less established theory and more research programme than its adherents had supposed, and knowing that is knowledge.

Cohen's place in that collective story is first among equals for a reason the parallel throws into relief. Roemer's ordeal concerned a definition; Cohen's concerned the theory of history itself, the load-bearing claim of the entire Marxist edifice, the one whose failure reclassifies every other claim from science to proposal. That is why his retreat, among all the school's concessions, is the one the curriculum preserves and the one this series gives a full article. It is also why the retreat's manner matters as much as its matter. A concession extracted by enemies can be discounted; a concession volunteered by the position's architect, itemised in his own preface, accompanied by a continued defence of everything in the position that he believed the evidence still supported, sets the standard for how intellectual surrender should be conducted when surrender is what the evidence orders. The standard is transferable to every field this series touches, and the article's closing guides for students, teachers, and researchers each draw on it in their own way.

## What biology actually teaches: the vacancy that was filled and the one that was not

The defence's favourite precedent deserves a closer audit than it usually receives, because the precedent's details decide how much comfort it can honestly give. Darwin's theory, as published in 1859, explained adaptation through selection operating on heritable variation, while the mechanism of heredity remained unknown and Darwin's own conjecture about it, pangenesis, was wrong. Critics of the period seized on the vacancy, and one objection in particular, that blending inheritance would dilute favourable variations before selection could accumulate them, identified a genuine threat that only particulate inheritance, Mendel's factors, could remove. The vacancy was therefore real, acknowledged, and dangerous, and it was filled within two generations by a research tradition that the theory itself had told investigators where to look: find the copying machinery. Cohen's appeal to the precedent trades on that happy ending, and the appeal is legitimate as far as it goes. It establishes that a science can rationally retain an explanation through a period in which its elaboration is missing, provided the pattern the explanation names is independently well evidenced and the search for the elaboration is progressive. The question the objection presses is whether the Marxist case satisfies the proviso, and there the precedent turns from comfort to indictment. The pattern in biology, adaptation's fit between organism and environment, was evidenced by a mountain of independent observation that no rival theory organised. The pattern in the Marxist case, the correspondence of relations to forces, is evidenced substantially by the transitions the theory itself selects and narrates, and rival organisations of the same record, centred on class power, state formation, and war, organise it at least as economically. A vacancy tolerated in a theory whose pattern is secure becomes, where the pattern itself is contested, one liability stacked on another.

The philosophy of biology's own later development sharpens the indictment further. The dominant etiological theory of function, associated with Larry Wright and Ruth Millikan, grounds a trait's function in its selection history: the function of the heart is to pump blood because pumping blood is what hearts were selected for doing. On that theory, function talk is legitimate precisely because and only because a selection process stands behind it, which is Elster's requirement wearing biological dress. The rival dispositional theory, associated with Robert Cummins, grounds function in a trait's contribution to a system's analysed capacities and requires no history at all, but it purchases that freedom at a price Cohen could not pay: dispositional functions explain how a system works, not why the trait is there, and the primacy thesis needs the why. Cohen's consequence explanation aspired to etiological force with dispositional evidence, to explain the presence of relations by their contribution while exhibiting no process of selection that would make the contribution explanatory of the presence. Philosophers of biology who have examined the construction tend to deliver the same verdict as the objector from within Marxism, in their own vocabulary: the construction is a promissory etiology, and the note is unpaid. That convergence across disciplines, reached independently and stated in unrelated technical languages, is among the strongest reasons for treating the mechanism requirement as a discovery about explanation rather than as a factional victory inside a small school.

A fair hearing requires the other side of the biological ledger to be entered as well. Selection itself, in contemporary biology, is a family of processes rather than a single mechanism, supplemented by drift, developmental constraint, niche construction, and selection at multiple levels, and its explanations are routinely statistical, partial, and contested in application to particular traits. A defender of Cohen might argue that the determinacy this article's defeater demands is an idealisation even of biology, and that a historical mechanism of comparable messiness, class struggle interwoven with state competition and purposive reform, should qualify under a standard honestly drawn from the exemplar. The argument deserves its day, and the defeater as stated is designed to give it one: the standard asks for determinacy of the same order, not identity of structure, and a family of processes qualifies if its combined operation discriminates reliably and can be specified in advance of outcomes. Where the defence continues to fail is not messiness but discrimination. The biological family's mess still issues in predictions that risk falsification, about which traits will spread under stated conditions. The historical family's mess issues, in practice, in retrodictions that accommodate whatever occurred, and accommodation without risk is the signature the whole debate taught its participants to distrust. Cohen's own later standard in moral philosophy, that a claim must expose itself to the considerations that would defeat it, is the same signature recognised from the other side of his career.

## Is the primacy thesis defensible? The question in its strongest surviving form

Strip the thesis of its functional engine and ask what remains that a reasonable historian could defend, since the question on the query ladder deserves a direct answer rather than a gesture at the assessment already given. What remains is a two-part heuristic of genuine power. The first part directs attention to the material capacities a society commands when explaining the menu of social forms realistically available to it: relations that presuppose a surplus cannot precede the techniques that generate one, relations that presuppose literacy, coinage, or fossil energy cannot stabilise before their substrates exist, and the negative discipline of those impossibilities eliminates large regions of historical fantasy. The second part directs attention to strain: when a society's productive capacities outgrow the relations housing them, the mismatch generates characteristic pathologies, shortages beside capacities, innovations suppressed by incumbents, energies diverted into circumventing the rules, and the pathologies are diagnostic even where no resolution is foreordained. A historian armed with the heuristic will ask questions a purely political narrative never raises, and will sometimes find the answers that matter most. Cohen's thesis, demoted to a heuristic, remains the best single prompt for those questions that the Marxist tradition produced, and its demotion is a fall from the rank of law to the rank of instrument, not from sense to nonsense.

What the heuristic cannot do is what the theory promised: explain the succession of epochs as a directional process with a motor. Heuristics guide inquiry; they do not close it. The transitions still require their local explanations, in terms of the struggles, accidents, and decisions the Brenner debate placed at the centre, and the direction of the whole, if the whole has a direction, becomes an empirical summary rather than a theoretical guarantee. Many working historians live contentedly inside that settlement, and some would say the settlement is simply what maturity looks like in a discipline whose grand theories have all been demoted in turn. Cohen's significance is that he made the demotion undeniable from within, by first making the theory undeniable in its strong form. A theory that was never stated strongly can linger indefinitely in the twilight of plausible suggestion. Cohen dragged historical materialism into the noon of exact statement, where it could be seen whole, admired whole, and judged whole, and the judgement, when it came from the man who had done the dragging, closed the twilight for good. That is the sense in which the primacy thesis's fate is inseparable from its defender's distinction: lesser defences would have left the theory vaguer, longer-lived, and less known.

## The superstructure's parallel fate, and the asymmetry that partly excuses it

The defence's functionalism did not stop at the base, and the extension deserves examination because its fate differed instructively from the original's. Cohen also explained the superstructure functionally: legal, political, and ideological forms obtain, on his account, because they stabilise the relations of production, securing the property arrangements and authority patterns the base requires. A legal system's protection of contract, a state's enforcement of ownership, an ideology's reconciliation of the subordinated to their subordination, each is explained by its stabilising effect on the economic structure beneath it. The mechanism objection applies to this extension with the same logic, and Cohen knew it, yet the extension has weathered the objection better, for a reason that illuminates the original's failure. Stabilisation is a service that identifiable agents often deliberately purchase. Rulers design law to protect the arrangements that enrich and empower them; elites fund the ideologies that legitimate their position; institutions are reformed, consciously, when they cease to stabilise. The purposive elaboration that was a fragmentary guest in the explanation of the base is a resident in the explanation of the superstructure, because the feedback loop there runs through the perceptions and decisions of people with the power to adjust the item to the benefit. Where the loop is purposive and the agents are identifiable, a functional explanation can be cashed, case by case, into intentional history, and its claims become checkable in archives rather than promissory in principle.

The asymmetry teaches the debate's most portable lesson in its cleanest form. Functional explanation is not a single device with a single fate; it is a family of claims whose credibility varies with the availability of their elaborations, and the work of assessment is to sort the family member by member rather than to bless or condemn the surname. Cohen's superstructural functionalism survives, in qualified form, because its mechanisms are often findable. Cohen's basal functionalism failed, by its author's verdict, because its mechanism was not. The sorting discipline is the mature residue of the whole affair, and it converts the mechanism requirement from a slogan into a practice: for each functional claim, demand the loop; where the loop is purposive, name the agents and their perceptions; where it is selective, name the filter and its metric; where it is neither, mark the claim as a pattern awaiting explanation and price it accordingly. Social science conducted under that discipline would retain everything true in the functionalist tradition while surrendering its characteristic fraud, and the discipline is teachable in a single seminar, which is more than can be said for most methodological reforms.

There is a coda to the asymmetry that Cohen's critics within the tradition pressed and that a balanced profile should record. If the superstructure is explained by its service to the base, and the base by its service to the forces, the theory's explanatory weight ultimately rests on the basal claim, the one that failed. A chain of functional explanations is only as strong as its final link, and the final link is the primacy thesis's. Defenders who retreat to the superstructural cases as evidence that the method works therefore argue from the theory's periphery to its centre, and the centre is where the objection was always aimed. Cohen's own writing respects the order of strength; his concessions begin, as this article noted, at the periphery of the system and end at its centre, which is another way of saying that he sorted his own claims by how readily their elaborations could be found and surrendered them in ascending order of importance as the evidence required. The sorting is the method. The surrender is the method's price. Both are the legacy.

## The book's reception, and the strange career of a defence that became a standard

Karl Marx's Theory of History was recognised on publication as an event in its field, receiving the Isaac Deutscher Memorial Prize and entering syllabuses with a speed that works of Marxist scholarship rarely achieve, a reception explained by the vacuum it filled. The theory of history had been the tradition's most asserted and least examined possession, defended by citation and attacked by slogan, and Cohen's book was the first to treat it as a set of propositions in the philosophy of social science, with the full apparatus of that discipline brought to bear. Reviewers who rejected the conclusions used the formulations; lecturers who taught against the book taught from it; the vocabulary of primacy and development, of consequence explanation and elaboration, passed into the field's common stock within a decade. That kind of reception creates a peculiar authorial trap, which Cohen's later career can be read as escaping with exemplary honesty. The book's success gave its author every incentive to become its custodian, defending the text against each new objection with the ingenuity that had built it, and academic life rewards custodianship with a quiet, honoured stagnation. Cohen declined the role. He treated the book as a position he had occupied rather than a property he owned, revised his occupancy in public as the arguments required, and finally recorded his departure from its central confidence in the building's own front hall, the preface to its new edition. The contrast with the ordinary career of a successful academic book, defended to the last footnote by an author whose identity has fused with it, is the biographical form of the article's methodological lesson, and it is not lost on the students who meet the episode in seminars. They are being shown, in one career, the difference between a theory as an achievement and a theory as an estate.

The reception had a second act in the justice literature, where Cohen arrived as an already-formidable figure whose interventions carried the authority of the earlier rigour. The self-ownership book and the ethos argument were received with the seriousness described earlier, and their author found himself, in his fifties and sixties, at the centre of a field he had entered as an insurgent, much as the young defender of Marx had been an insurgent in his. The pattern of the two insurgencies is the same: identify the premise the field is coasting on, state it exactly, and follow it past the point where its holders become uncomfortable. In the first insurgency the premise was that Marx's theory could not be stated exactly; in the second, that justice could be confined to institutions while personal conduct escaped it. Both premises fell. The difference is that the first fall damaged Cohen's own position and the second damaged other people's, and the equanimity with which he presided over both is the quality his obituaries, in 2009, returned to most often: a philosopher for whom the argument's destination outranked the arguer's comfort, including and especially when the arguer was himself.

## The currency question: Cohen's other famous argument and its place in the profile

No profile of Cohen can omit the paper of 1989 that quietly reorganised egalitarian theory, because it shows the same mind performing the same operation on a different field's complacency. The question was what egalitarians should seek to equalise: welfare, resources, capabilities, or something else. Ronald Dworkin's influential answer, resources, came equipped with an auction and an insurance market designed to handle the difference between brute luck, the misfortunes that befall a person without her choice, and option luck, the outcomes of gambles she chose to take. Cohen's intervention accepted the distinction's importance and then relocated the theory's centre of gravity onto it: the fundamental aim of equality, he argued, is to eliminate involuntary disadvantage, disadvantage a person suffers through no fault or choice of her own, and any currency is acceptable only as an instrument toward that aim. His proposed metric, equality of access to advantage, was designed to capture the aim directly, advantage being broad enough to include welfare and resources while access registers the involuntariness that does the moral work. The paper's effect was to found, with a handful of contemporaries, the family of views later labelled luck egalitarianism, for which the brute luck and option luck distinction is the load-bearing wall.

The label became a target, and the criticisms of luck egalitarianism, that it is harsh toward the imprudent, that it humiliates the disadvantaged by auditing their responsibility, that it abandons people whose needs arise from choices, constitute a literature of their own in which Cohen participated with his usual willingness to concede the points that landed. For this profile the episode matters less for its doctrine than for its method's recognisability. Once again Cohen found the distinction a field was using without examining, the distinction between chosen and unchosen disadvantage, and rebuilt the field's central question around it, accepting the reconstruction's costs in public. And once again the reconstruction connected to the career's buried unity: the theory of history had foundered on an unspecified mechanism, and the egalitarian theory was being rebuilt, by the same hand, with its mechanism of moral discrimination specified to the last distinction. Readers who work through both episodes in sequence acquire the article's deepest lesson without needing it stated: that intellectual seriousness is a transferable habit, that it looks the same in every field it enters, and that its signature is the willingness to let a distinction reorganise a life's work rather than protecting the work from the distinction.

The currency argument also completes the answer to the forum question this article's brief requires it to address, why philosophers take Cohen seriously. They take him seriously because, three times in three different literatures, he identified the load-bearing vagueness and replaced it with a structure others had to build on or argue against: the theory of history became two theses and a consequence law; the critique of libertarianism became a confrontation over one premise, self-ownership; the theory of equality became a question about involuntary disadvantage. Fields remember the scholars who change their questions. Cohen changed the questions of every field he entered, including the field, Marxism, whose answers he ended by doubting, and the doubt itself, arrived at by his own method and published in his own preface, changed that field's questions most of all.

## Studying the argument: how to keep the whole debate in view

The debate this article has reconstructed is unusually well suited to structured study, because its documents are few, short by the standards of the tradition, and arranged by history into a natural syllabus: a defence, an objection, a sequence of replies, a preface, and a coda in another field. Students who keep notes on the sequence face a characteristic difficulty, which is that the argument's stages answer one another across decades and volumes, and a note made on the defence in January needs to be findable when the objection qualifies it in March. The companion study notes for this series, maintained as a [VaultBook study card on the argument and the retreat](https://vaultbook.net/tools/marxism-study-notes.html), exist for exactly that discipline: the four components of the table above, primacy, development, the functional explanation, and the mechanism requirement, each with its claim, its objection, and its concession kept in one view, so that the reader's own judgement about what would count as a defeater can be recorded against the standard the debate itself supplies. The card is a tool for the habit this article has praised throughout, the habit of holding a claim and the condition of its surrender in a single field of vision.

Teachers building a session on the material will find that the debate teaches itself once the documents are placed in the students' hands in the order given, because the objection's force is best felt by readers who have first been persuaded by the defence, and the preface's force is best felt by an audience that has watched the defence adjust for a decade. The pedagogical sequence mirrors the historical one, and attempts to shortcut it, by presenting the objection first as a refutation to be memorised, produce students who can recite the mechanism requirement without understanding what it cost. The cost is the content. A seminar that spends its first hour inside the defence's elegance, its second inside the objection's tightening sequence, and its third inside the preface's single sentence will have taught more philosophy of social science than a term of methodological generalities, and the students will remember the sentence for the reason the field remembers it: because they watched the position it withdrew confidence from being built, by its own builder, at full strength.

## The taxonomy of elaborations: purposive, Darwinian, and Lamarckian routes

Cohen's reply to the objection, published in the journal Inquiry in 1982 under a title that joined functional explanation, consequence explanation, and Marxism, contains a taxonomy that later discussions often flatten, and restoring it shows the defence at its most resourceful. If a consequence law holds, Cohen reasoned, the world's arrangement that sustains it might take several forms, which he illustrated by biological analogy. A Darwinian elaboration sustains the pattern by blind selection among variants, as in the canonical case. A Lamarckian elaboration sustains it by directed adaptation, in which the organism, or by analogy the society, adjusts itself in response to its circumstances and transmits the adjustment. A purposive elaboration sustains it through the intentions of agents who see the advantage and move to protect it. The taxonomy mattered because it widened the defence's options: the objection had been framed against the Darwinian template, natural selection or nothing, and Cohen could reply that social processes might elaborate consequence laws in the Lamarckian or purposive modes, through societies learning from their failures and through classes and states pursuing perceived interests, without any blind filter of the biological kind. The reply was the defence's high-water mark, the moment at which its resources looked deepest.

The taxonomy's subsequent fate is the retreat in miniature. Each non-Darwinian route, examined closely, either changes the explanation's character or inherits the original gap in a new place. The purposive route, as the superstructure discussion showed, works where agents can perceive benefits and command the means to secure them, and fails across the long, unintended stretches of the theory's narrative where no agent perceived the eventual arrangement or could have commanded it. The Lamarckian route requires a mechanism of social learning and transmission, institutions that register failure, encode the lesson, and bind successors to it, and while such institutions exist, their lessons are as often the wrong ones, since what a society learns from a crisis is filtered through the interests the crisis left in place. A fettering order is typically very good at learning how to persist. The Darwinian route, finally, is the one the objection had already audited and found unspecified at the level of relations. The taxonomy thus redistributed the difficulty without dissolving it, and Cohen's later writings show him circling the three routes with decreasing confidence that any of them, or all in harness, could carry the consequence law at the scale the theory required. Watching a first-rate mind exhaust its own taxonomy is the scholarly equivalent of watching a siege engine test every wall; the walls that stand after such a testing have earned their standing, and the walls here are the objection's.

There is a methodological moral in the taxonomy's fate that generalises beyond the debate, and it concerns the difference between multiplying options and discharging burdens. A defence confronted with a missing mechanism can always respond by enumerating the forms a mechanism might take, and the enumeration feels like progress because the space of possibilities has been mapped. But a map of where a mechanism might be is not a mechanism, and the burden is discharged only by occupation, by showing that this process, with these components, operated in those cases. Cohen's taxonomy mapped brilliantly and occupied partially, in the superstructural cases where purposive elaboration could be documented. The basal cases remained unoccupied, and the preface of 2000 is, among other things, the surveyor's honest report that the map's most important territory was still empty. Younger scholars sometimes treat the taxonomy as the defence's answer to Elster. The documentary sequence corrects them: it was the defence's research programme in response to Elster, and the preface is the programme's final report.

## Definitions under pressure: what counts as a productive force, and why the boundary is the theory

A quieter controversy ran beneath the famous one, concerning the definitions on which the whole construction rests, and no complete account of Cohen's defence should omit it, since the definitions are where the theory's empirical content is decided. The productive forces, for Cohen, comprise labour power, with its skills and knowledge, the means of production, instruments and materials, and the spaces in which production occurs. Relations of production are constituted by the powers persons have over forces and over one another's labour, ownership in the economic rather than the narrowly legal sense. The boundary between the categories does heavy work. If an item is classified as a force, its growth counts as development and the primacy thesis gains content; if the same item is classified as a relation, its change is what needs explaining. Critics pressed the boundary at its most sensitive point, the organisation of work itself. A factory's division of labour looks like a technique, hence a force, and also like a structure of command, hence a relation, and the classification chosen can determine whether a given historical change confirms the theory or embarrasses it. Cohen addressed the pressure by distinguishing the material content of a labour process from its social form, the same operations performed under different ownership and authority counting as the same force under different relations. The distinction is defensible and was defended with ingenuity, but its necessity reveals the construction's fragility at the foundation: a theory whose central claim can be insulated by reclassification must police its classifications with unusual vigilance, and the policing is itself a theoretical act that sceptics may audit.

The audit matters for the mechanism debate because the two controversies interact. A consequence law whose relata can be redefined under pressure risks the very unfalsifiability that the analytical school existed to expel from Marxism, and Cohen's care with the definitions was his prophylactic against that risk. He fixed the classifications in advance, by criteria independent of the transitions to be explained, and held them steady across the argument, which is exactly the discipline the defeater in this article demands of any future mechanism: specification in advance, steadiness under pressure, exposure to the cases that hurt. The definitions largely survived their audit; scholars still dispute the boundary cases, but the construction's terms are not generally accused of the elasticity that doomed cruder versions of the theory. The asymmetry is poignant and instructive. The defence succeeded completely at the level of statement, substantially at the level of definition, and failed at the level of the connecting process, the one level where the theory touched the world's actual machinery. A reader assembling the whole profile should resist the temptation to average the levels into a mixed verdict. The levels are the verdict: statement, exemplary; definition, defensible; mechanism, absent; and the theory's truth, by its author's final account, not established. Precision about which level failed is what separates the assessment this article has built from the vague epitaph, often pronounced, that Cohen's Marxism did not work out.

## Not technological determinism: the label Cohen refused and the distinction he drew instead

Few labels in the field are applied more loosely than technological determinism, and Cohen's relation to it requires care because the primacy thesis is routinely mistaken for the label's content. Technological determinism, in the loose usage, holds that technology develops by its own logic and drags social arrangements behind it, with human choice reduced to applause or obstruction. Cohen's forces are not technology in that sense. They include labour power, which is to say human beings with their skills, knowledge, and capacities, and the development thesis grounds their growth in human rationality confronting scarcity, which makes the theory's motor anthropological rather than mechanical: people, facing a niggardly world with inventive minds, tend over time to expand their productive powers, and relations are explained by their hospitality to that expansion. The distinction does not rescue the theory from the mechanism objection, since a tendency grounded in dispersed human rationality still requires a process connecting the tendency to the selection of relations, and dispersed rationality might as readily explain why relations fail to track development, each agent's local rationality summing to collective irrationality, as why they succeed. But the distinction does acquit Cohen of the cruder charge, and students who deploy the label against the primacy thesis reveal that they have read the summaries rather than the book. The book's forces are a society's total productive capacity, embodied in its people as much as in its machines, and its theory is a claim about the explanation of social forms by that capacity's growth, not a claim that machines make history while people watch.

The refusal of the label connects to a larger feature of the defence that the summaries also miss: its treatment of class. A reader who knows only the two theses might imagine a theory in which classes are decorative, summoned to ratify transitions the forces have already decided. Cohen's actual construction assigns class struggle the role of the theory's proximate machinery, the process through which fettering is experienced, contested, and sometimes overcome. Classes form around relations, perceive their interests within them, and fight; the fights settle which relations survive a crisis of the old order. The functional claim sits above that machinery, asserting that the fights' long-run outcomes are biased toward the relations that develop the forces. The objection, seen from this angle, is that the bias is exactly what the machinery does not supply, since classes fight for their interests as they perceive them, and a class whose interest is served by fettering, a rentier stratum battening on scarcity, may win and keep winning. Cohen never denied the possibility; the theory asserts a tendency strong enough, across enough cases, to constitute a lawlike pattern, and the empirical question of the tendency's strength is where the defence's fate was always going to be decided. The preface records his judgement that the strength had not been shown. Readers who present the theory as though class struggle were its mechanism have therefore inverted the true structure: class struggle is the arena, the bias is the claim, and the mechanism would be whatever produces the bias. Naming that difference clearly is the single most clarifying act a teacher can perform on this material.

## The September Group and the sociology of a school that argued with itself

The institutional setting of the debate deserves a section of its own, because the objection's origin inside the defence's own seminar room is not a decorative detail but a causal part of the story. The group that began meeting in September 1979, and took its informal name from the month, gathered scholars dissatisfied with the state of Marxist theory in the same specific way: Cohen the philosopher, Elster the social scientist, Roemer the economist, Erik Olin Wright the sociologist, and their colleagues and successors, united by the conviction that the tradition's claims should be stated with the clarity of the disciplines each member practised elsewhere. The group acquired a jocular second name, the No-Bullshit Marxism Group, which its members repeated with pride because it stated the admission standard: no claim admitted on the authority of the tradition, no obscurity mistaken for depth, no conclusion protected from the argument that threatened it. The standard explains the debate's otherwise puzzling civility at white heat. Elster's objection was not an attack on the group's enterprise but an application of it, delivered to the enterprise's founder, in the forum the founder had built for exactly such deliveries. Cohen's concession, two decades later, was the same standard applied by the founder to himself. Institutions are usually judged by their products; this one should be judged by its procedure, since its most famous products remain a theory and the demonstration that the theory was unproven, and the procedure generated both with the same impartial machinery.

The school's broader fate, surveyed in the series guide to analytical Marxism, includes migrations into rational-choice sociology, comparative historical analysis, and normative political theory, and the migrations share the pattern this profile has traced in Cohen alone: the tradition's questions survived its answers, and its methods survived its doctrines. A school that dissolves into the disciplines it borrowed from has not necessarily failed; it may have succeeded at the only task it ever set itself, which was to make Marxism answerable to the standards of inquiry practised elsewhere. The September Group's members went on to distinguished careers in philosophy, economics, and sociology, carrying the standards with them, and the group's dissolution as its members' interests diverged is better read as graduation than as collapse. Cohen's chair at Oxford, his election to fellowships, and the migration of his later work to the centre of political philosophy all belong to that graduation. The theory of history did not survive the school's standards. The standards survived everything, and they are the school's bequest to any reader of this series who goes on to argue about anything.

## Against the other Marxism of the nineteen seventies: Cohen and the structuralist alternative

The defence's polemical context included a rival reconstruction of Marx that dominated the continental discussion of the period, and the contrast fixes Cohen's distinctiveness more sharply than any summary of his own position. Louis Althusser's structural Marxism also explained social elements by their place in a whole, treating individuals as the supports of structures and history as a process without a subject, and it also relied, at bottom, on functional connections: the elements of a social formation exist in their forms because those forms reproduce the formation. Cohen's analytical reconstruction differed from the structuralist one in every methodological particular. Where structuralism prized a vocabulary of its own, whose terms resist translation into ordinary claims, Cohen insisted on statements a hostile reader could test. Where structuralism treated the demand for mechanisms as a humanist prejudice, Cohen's school made the demand its own and eventually applied it to Cohen. Where structuralism's functionalism was protected by the doctrine that the whole is present in its effects, a formula that can absorb any evidence, Cohen's functionalism was exposed, by design, to the evidence that eventually wounded it. The two reconstructions thus offer a controlled comparison in the methodology of theory defence: the protected version survives as a period style, citable and inert, while the exposed version generated a debate whose results the whole field uses. Readers tempted to regard Cohen's fate as a warning against rigour have the comparison backwards. The warning the comparison issues is against protection. A theory protected from its own standards does not live longer in any sense that matters; it merely takes longer to be recognised as dead, and it teaches nothing on the way.

Cohen's distance from the humanist Marxism of the period was equally deliberate and is worth a paragraph, because the two distances together locate him. The humanists centred the young Marx of the manuscripts, alienation, praxis, and the critique of reification, and they treated the theory of history's determinism as an embarrassment to be softened by dialectics. Cohen regarded the softening as evasion. His Marx was the mature theorist of Capital and the 1859 preface, his questions were the theory's truth rather than its inspirational value, and his attitude to the humanists resembled his attitude to the structuralists: both, in his judgement, had arranged for Marxism never to be wrong, the one by making it a vision and the other by making it a structure, and a doctrine that cannot be wrong cannot be known to be right. The analytical school's founding wager was that Marxism deserved the dignity of refutability. The wager's outcome, in the flagship case, was a refutation of sorts delivered from inside, and the school's honour consists in having paid the wager without suing for its return. Later generations of Marxist scholarship have not always honoured the result; the theory of history continues to be taught in its twilight form in places where Cohen's noon never penetrated. But the noon exists, in print, permanently available, and the existence changes what intellectual honesty requires of anyone who teaches the theory thereafter: the strong statement and its fate must both be mentioned, or the teaching is a protection racket of the kind the comparison condemns.

## Ideology's function, and the explanation Cohen gave of beliefs he rejected

One more province of the defence deserves notice before the closing guides, because it shows the functional device working at the scale where its author thought it most obviously legitimate and because it connects the historical theory to the theory of ideology that the wider series treats elsewhere. Cohen held that ideological beliefs, the distorted representations of social reality that a formation generates, are themselves explained functionally: they persist because they stabilise the relations whose product they are, reconciling the dominated to arrangements that a clear view might make intolerable to them. The explanation has the familiar shape, an item explained by its stabilising effect, and it faces the familiar question, with a twist that partly answers it. Beliefs, unlike relations of production, are carried by minds and transmitted by institutions that can be inspected: schools, pulpits, presses, and their successors, each with identifiable operators and incentives. The feedback loop for ideology runs through the selection of messages by the institutions that benefit from them, a process closer to the purposive and selective mechanisms the objection could recognise. Cohen's ideology functionalism is therefore among the parts of the construction that survive the audit in working order, and its survival pattern confirms the article's sorting discipline from a third direction: the device lives where its loops can be found, and the debate's history is the record of looking for them honestly, province by province, and reporting what the search found in each.

The twist has a limit that Cohen acknowledged and that keeps the province honest. Stabilisation explains why a belief is amplified and transmitted; it does not by itself explain why the belief is believed, since audiences are not blank recipients and a doctrine too flagrantly at odds with lived experience fails however lavishly it is broadcast. The believing requires its own account, in terms of the belief's partial truths, its fit with the believer's situation, and the absence of livable alternatives, and Cohen's discussions of ideology supply those accounts with a psychological realism that the cruder versions of the doctrine lack. The pattern recurs one final time: a functional claim, an elaboration demanded, an elaboration partially supplied through identifiable processes, and a residue honestly marked. A reader who has tracked the pattern through the base, the superstructure, and ideology has learned the debate's whole grammar, and is equipped to apply it to any functional claim encountered anywhere, which is the equipment this article exists to distribute.

## Tendencies, laws, and the modesty the theory learned too late

A word that recurs through the defence and its qualifications deserves separate treatment, since the theory's fate can be written as the biography of that word. The word is tendency. Cohen's claims were tendency claims from the start: the forces tend to develop; relations tend to fit the forces' requirements; fettering relations tend to be replaced. Tendency language is the traditional refuge of historical theories, since a tendency can survive any finite run of counterexamples by pleading interference, and the analytical school's founding suspicion was directed at exactly that refuge. Cohen's achievement in 1978 was to give the tendencies enough structure to be assessable: premises from which the developmental tendency follows, a functional form for the fitting tendency, and historical illustrations organised as evidence rather than as decoration. His difficulty, across the following decades, was that assessment kept returning the same report in different vocabularies: the tendencies are real but weaker than the theory's architecture requires, and their weakness is not a temporary interference but a standing feature of the domain, where counter-tendencies, classes defending fettering rents, states prizing extraction over growth, cultures pricing other goods above output, are not noise around the signal but part of the signal's constitution. A tendency whose counter-tendencies are equally lawful is a description of a contested field, not a motor of history, and the distance between those two things is the distance the retreat travelled.

The philosophy of science has a standard instrument for this situation, the distinction between a research programme's hard core and its protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses, and the instrument was applied to Cohen's programme by friendlier hands than Elster's with the same result. A programme progresses when its auxiliaries predict novel facts; it degenerates when they merely accommodate old ones. The defence's auxiliaries, the scope conditions on the development thesis, the taxonomy of elaborations, the migration between strong and weak primacy, accommodated with great ingenuity and predicted with none that the literature can point to. Cohen's own later standard in the science question, developed elsewhere in this series in the article on whether Marxism is a science or a faith, is that a theory's defenders must be able to say what would count against it and must count it when it arrives. By that standard the preface of 2000 is not an embarrassment to be explained away but the standard's own application, the moment the programme's architect performed the count in public. The article on the science question and this one are companion pieces in that respect: one states the criterion in the abstract, the other shows the criterion operating on the theory's most rigorous defender, at the cost of the theory he had spent his youth defending. Readers who finish both will possess the cluster's central lesson in its complete form, the criterion and its price.

There is a temptation, in closing a profile, to soften the accounting with the reflection that all grand theories of history have suffered comparable fates, and the reflection is true and should be resisted as a sedative. Cohen's case is not edifying because company makes it ordinary. It is edifying because the fate was documented by the one person with every motive to undocument it, in a sequence of texts that lets any reader audit the documentation. Most theories of history die unrecorded deaths, their adherents drifting away, their claims quietly dropped from syllabuses, their falsity never established because their content was never fixed. Historical materialism, alone among its rivals, was fixed, tested, and reported on by its fixer. The fixity is the bequest. Whatever future theory of history earns the field's confidence will earn it by submitting to the same treatment in advance: claims stated so that mechanisms can be demanded, mechanisms specified so that cases can be checked, and defeaters declared so that the theory's friends and enemies alike know what would settle the matter. The declaration this article has carried, that a specified feedback process with the determinacy of selection would defeat its verdict, is written in that spirit and in Cohen's manner, and it is the proper note on which a profile of him should end its argument.

## Facts and principles: the metaethical turn of the mechanism mind

The last major argument of Cohen's career returns, one final time, to the structure that governed all the others: a distinction insisted upon until a field reorganises around it. The distinction, set out in his paper of 2003 on facts and principles and developed in his 2008 book on justice and equality, is between principles that state what justice is and the facts that determine how those principles should be applied in rules for actual societies. Cohen argued that a fundamental principle of justice cannot depend for its truth on any fact about the world, since whenever a fact is offered as the ground of a principle, a deeper principle is doing the real work, the principle that explains why that fact matters. If the difference principle is defended by appeal to the fact that incentives elicit effort, the operative principle is not the difference principle itself but a prior commitment about why incentive-generated benefits justify inequality, and the regress ends only at a principle that no fact grounds. The conclusion he drew was austere: facts constrain what justice can be implemented as, through rules of regulation that prudently fall short of it, but facts cannot be constituents of justice itself, and theories that build feasibility into the content of justice have confused the destination with the road conditions.

The bearing on this profile is familiar by this stage, and it is worth drawing explicitly because it completes the portrait of a single intellectual character operating across four decades and three fields. The theory of history had confused a pattern with its mechanism, and the confusion cost the theory its truth. Normative theory, in Cohen's diagnosis, was confusing a principle with its implementation, and the confusion cost it its purity: principles trimmed to fit the motivational facts of the present cease to be standards against which the present can be judged, and become instead the present's self-description wearing the robes of an ideal. In both cases Cohen's remedy was the same operation performed in different materials, separate the levels, refuse the borrowing of credentials between them, and accept the discomfort that separation brings. The discomfort in the historical case was the admission that the pattern lacked its mechanism. The discomfort in the normative case is the admission that justice, unimplemented and perhaps unimplementable under any arrangements human beings can build, still binds as a standard, and that the distance between the standard and every feasible society is a fact to be confessed rather than a defect to be edited out of the standard. The camping trip book is the popular form of exactly that confession: the principles are endorsed, the machinery is missing, and both clauses are printed on the same page.

Critics replied that a justice no society can instantiate is a standard in name only, and that Cohen's fact-insensitive principles purchase purity at the price of guidance, since agents choosing among real institutions need principles already adjusted to the facts those institutions will face. The exchange remains live, and this article's neutrality discipline requires it to be left live, with both positions stated at strength. What can be recorded without taking sides is the exchange's genealogy: the philosopher pressing for the separation of principle from feasibility is the philosopher who watched a theory of history collapse the separation of pattern from mechanism, and who concluded from the collapse that level-confusion is the characteristic intellectual sin of ambitious theory. Whether the conclusion transfers to normative theory is the question his last book leaves open, deliberately, as the earlier debate was left open by a defeater. The habit of leaving questions open in a specified form, with the settling evidence named in advance, is the closest thing Cohen had to a signature, and the signature is legible on every period of the career this profile has surveyed.

## Three cases examined under the requirement: manor, factory, and enclosure

Abstract standards earn their keep on cases, and the mechanism requirement can be given a final concreteness by running it over three transitions that textbooks of the theory habitually cite, asking of each the same question: what process selected the new relations for their service to the forces, and can that process be specified without borrowing the outcome as evidence? The dissolution of the manorial order in western Europe is the friendliest case. Commutation of labour services, the growth of markets for produce, and the demographic shocks of the fourteenth century loosened the old relations over generations, and a defender can point to real discriminating processes: lords who commuted early prospered beside lords who clung to services, peasants voted with their feet toward tenures that rewarded effort, and the relations that spread were plausibly those under which the land's product grew. Yet the same centuries supply the eastern counter-case, where the discriminating processes ran the other way and the second serfdom was imposed by lords whose power, not the land's productivity, decided the outcome, and the pair of cases together state the requirement's verdict on the transition as a whole: the mechanism discriminates in the west because power happened to align with development there, and a mechanism whose operation depends on that alignment has not been specified; the alignment has.

The rise of the factory system is the case where purposive elaboration comes closest to carrying the full weight, and its closeness is instructive. Manufacturers adopted the factory, in significant part, because it developed the productive forces in ways they could perceive and pocket: machinery concentrated, effort disciplined, output multiplied, and the adopters grew rich while hand producers dwindled. Here the feedback loop runs through identifiable agents responding to perceptible benefits, and the functional claim can be cashed into intentions and balance sheets that archives preserve. The case therefore passes the requirement in a local form, and its passing marks the boundary of the purposive route's jurisdiction. What the factory case cannot explain is the prior transformation of the relations within which factories became possible and profitable, the long making of a labour force separated from the means of production and of markets in which its product could be sold, since that transformation was nobody's design and its benefits accrued to a system no participant was steering. The theory's grand claims live on the far side of that boundary, in the jurisdiction where purposive elaboration falls silent, and the case's lesson is that the silence begins exactly where the claims become distinctively Marxist rather than generally economic.

Enclosure, the third case, is the hardest for the defence and the most valuable for the student, because it is the case where the benefit and the process are both visible and visibly different things. Enclosed agriculture did, on the standard account the theory cites, develop the productive forces: consolidated holdings, improved rotations, heavier yields. The process that selected enclosure, however, was not a filter sensitive to yields. It was a political process, running through Parliament and the law of property, in which those who held power over land converted customary rights into exclusive ownership because the conversion enriched them, at yields high or low. The benefit was real and the benefit was not the selector; the selector was a distribution of power that the theory's own categories treat as part of what needs explaining. A defender may reply that power itself tracks productive development in the long run, and the reply returns the argument to the missing mechanism at one remove, the signature regress of the whole debate: each case's gap is filled by appeal to a longer run in which a further unspecified process does the discriminating. The requirement's discipline consists in refusing the regress its indefinite credit. At some point the run must be produced, with its process named and its cases checkable, and three centuries of the theory's scholarship, audited by its ablest defender, did not produce it. That is the finding the cases converge on, stated here in the historiographical register to match the logical register in which Elster first stated it and the autobiographical register in which Cohen finally confirmed it.

## The requirement abroad: institutional economics and the functionalist fallacy

The mechanism requirement's portability, asserted earlier, can be verified in the discipline that had the most to gain from ignoring it. Institutional economics in its older form explained institutions by the transaction costs they economised: property rights, contract forms, and firm boundaries were read off the efficiencies they delivered, in a literature whose logic is Cohen's consequence explanation translated into the key of costs. The translation attracted the same objection in its new key. Efficiency is a benefit; benefits do not install their own causes; and the historical record of institutions is crowded with arrangements that persist despite inefficiency because the powerful profit from them, and with efficient arrangements that never arrive because no coalition can capture their diffuse gains. The field's most distinguished historian of institutions built his later reputation partly on that record, arguing that the political process, with its own logic of power and commitment, selects institutions on criteria that efficiency influences only weakly and intermittently. The convergence with the Marxist debate is not a borrowing in either direction; it is the same logical structure asserting itself in independent materials, which is what entitles this article to present the requirement as a finding about explanation itself, rather than a verdict on one theory. Wherever a social scientist explains a persisting arrangement through the good it performs, the requirement is already in the room, asking for the loop, and the quality of the ensuing work can be graded by how directly the question is faced.

The grading metaphor can be made literal, and making it literal supplies the answer to one of the extra questions this article's study materials pose. Functional explanations come in grades, and the mechanism requirement is the grading scheme. At the top grade stands the explanation whose loop is specified, evidenced, and independently checkable, the biological case and the purposive cases in the superstructure and the factory. Below it stands the explanation whose loop is specified but whose evidence is thin, a promissory note with the collateral identified. Below that stands the explanation whose loop is gestured at through a family of processes none of which is required to discriminate, the condition of the basal primacy claim through most of its career. At the bottom stands the explanation that treats the benefit as self-certifying, the condition Elster diagnosed across the functionalist literature and Cohen's early confidence occasionally approached. The grades are not a verdict on the explainers' intelligence; the bottom grade has been occupied by scholars of genius, because the temptation it represents is built into the grammar of benefit talk itself. The grades are a verdict on the explanations, and the discipline the requirement imposes is simply that an explanation's grade be printed on its face, so that readers know what they are holding. Cohen's career, read whole, is the story of a man who printed the grade on his own explanation and revised it downward in public as the evidence required, which is why the story is told to students who will never open a page of Marx.

## The last book's reception, and the shape of a reputation

Why Not Socialism? appeared in the year of its author's death and was received in a register unusual for a work of political philosophy: affection, openly expressed by reviewers who disagreed with its premises, for the clarity of the question and the candour of the confession that the machinery was missing. The affection has a source this profile can name. Most defences of socialism available to the general reader either promise the machinery, in the form of a plan, a party, or a technology, or denounce the request for machinery as a failure of faith. Cohen's little book does neither, and its refusal reads, to audiences exhausted by both manoeuvres, as respect. The book trusts the reader with the whole position: the principles are attractive, the small-scale evidence says they are workable among people who can monitor one another, the large-scale design problem is unsolved, and the author's lifetime spent on the adjacent problems had not solved it. A reader may reject the principles and still recognise the respect, which is why the book is assigned in courses whose instructors reject them.

Cohen died in Oxford in August 2009, and the assessments that followed converged on the judgement this article has built its profile around: that he was the most rigorous defender Marxism produced in the English-speaking world, that his rigour dismantled more of the doctrine than any enemy had managed, and that his later work on justice would outlast the debate that made his name. The convergence is not a courtesy. It reflects the unusual fact that both halves of the judgement rest on documents anyone can inspect, the defence and the preface, the objection and the concession, the camping trip and the confession, arranged by their author across thirty years into an arc that no biographer needs to embellish. Profiles of thinkers usually argue for their subject's importance. This one has been able to exhibit it, in the subject's own sequence of claims and surrenders, and the exhibition is the strongest form the argument can take. The tradition Cohen examined has produced grander system-builders and more influential organisers. It has produced no one else whose work lets a reader watch, in primary sources of the first quality, the complete life cycle of a great theory under honest examination: its strongest statement, its sharpest internal criticism, its author's itemised retreat, and its transformation into a moral philosophy that carried the same discipline into a second life. That is the One Test answer with which this article began, earned at last rather than asserted: to understand functional explanation, Cohen's defence of it, and the mechanism objection that his own standards forced him to accept, is to understand the most consequential argument analytical Marxism produced, and to possess an instrument that grades every explanation by effects, in any field, that the reader will meet from here on.

## Nagel's translation challenge, and the defence's answer to it

Between Hempel's critique and Elster's objection stands a third philosophical challenge that Cohen's defence had to pass, less famous than either and in some ways the most searching, because it questioned whether functional explanation says anything at all beyond the causal claims it might be translated into. Ernest Nagel, in his systematic treatise on the structure of science, argued that functional statements in biology and sociology are elliptical: to say that an item functions to produce an effect is, when the ellipsis is expanded, to assert a set of causal relations among the system's parts, together with the item's necessity, or at least its contribution, within them. On that view the functional idiom is a shorthand, harmless where the expansion is available and misleading where it is not, since the shorthand's teleological flavour suggests a content the expansion does not contain. Cohen's response exploited a feature of the biological case that the translation programme handles awkwardly. Selection explanations cite a history: the trait is present because of what its ancestors did for their bearers, and the historical citation is not shorthand for any set of relations among the organism's present parts. The past's causal work cannot be translated into the present's wiring diagram. Cohen's consequence explanation claimed the same historical depth for the social case: relations are present because of what relations of their type did, across the past, for the development of the forces. The claim's form was therefore vindicated against Nagel in principle, as genuinely explanatory and irreducibly historical, while remaining exposed to Elster in practice, since the history the form requires is a history of selection, and the selecting remained unexhibited. The three challenges compose a gauntlet that clarifies the defence's exact achievement and its exact failure. Against Hempel, Cohen showed the form needs no indispensability assumption. Against Nagel, he showed it is not shorthand. Against Elster, he could not show the history it needs, and the failure is the more damning for the company it keeps: the form survived every test of its logic and failed the test of its world.

That ordering, logic vindicated and world withheld, explains a feature of the debate's afterlife that puzzles newcomers, the respect in which Cohen's book is still held by the very philosophers who regard its central claim as unestablished. A book can be a permanent contribution by settling the logical geography of a question while losing the question itself, and the geography Cohen settled, the distinctions among pattern, benefit, explanation, and elaboration, among purposive, selective, and reinforcing loops, among strong and weak primacy, is the geography in which all subsequent discussion is conducted, including the discussion that concluded against him. Fields keep the maps drawn by their defeated generals when the maps are better than the victors'. The mechanism requirement itself, the debate's surviving instrument, is stated in Cohen's vocabulary as much as in Elster's, a fact the objector has never begrudged, since the requirement's point was never territorial. Students who grasp the ordering are immunised against the vulgar scorekeeping that asks who won; the accurate answer is that the question won, having been made precise enough to have a loser, and that the loser was its maker is the episode's distinction rather than its disgrace.

## The preface as a genre: what Cohen's sentence did that arguments could not

A short reflection on the documentary form of the retreat is warranted, because the preface to a new edition is a genre with rules, and Cohen's use of it was exact. Prefaces to later editions conventionally perform one of three acts: they celebrate the book's reception, they answer its critics while conceding nothing structural, or they quietly revise the text and say little. Cohen's preface performed a fourth act for which the genre has no convention: it audited the book against the standard the book itself had set, reported the audit's central failure, and left the text standing unrevised as the record of the position its author no longer held with confidence. The decision to leave the text standing matters. A revised edition would have blurred the documentary sequence on which the episode's evidential value depends, replacing the strong claim with its weakened successor and letting the weakening pass as the original's meaning. By reprinting the defence intact beside a preface that withdraws confidence from it, Cohen preserved both terms of the comparison and forced every future reader to hold them together, the strongest statement and its author's verdict, in a single volume. Scholars citing the book must cite the pair, and the pair is the debate in miniature, which is why the preface has been treated here not as a biographical footnote but as one of the episode's primary texts, coequal with the defence and the objection.

The sentence itself, that he was no longer confident the theory is true, rewards the slow reading the brief's discipline encourages. Its modality is confidence, not knowledge: Cohen does not claim to know the theory false, and the modesty is principled, since the objection showed a gap rather than a contradiction. Its scope is the theory, historical materialism as he had stated it, not the tradition, not socialism, not the book's value. Its tense is a report on a state reached through the intervening decades of work, the replies and qualifications this article has traced, so that the sentence functions as the final entry in a ledger the reader has been shown page by page. And its location, at the front of the defence's own expanded edition, guarantees that no reader can meet the defence without meeting the verdict first, an arrangement its author chose and could easily have avoided by publishing the recantation, if recantation it were, in a journal. The arrangement is the last exercise of the book's own method: the claim together with the terms of its assessment, bound in one cover, exposed to any reader's audit. A profile that has followed the brief's instruction, to report the concession from the texts rather than characterise it as a defeat, can end the documentary story there, with the observation that the texts, so arranged, characterise themselves.

## The hand-mill and the steam-mill: the theory's favourite sentence under audit

No sentence of Marx's is quoted more often in this territory than the aphorism from The Poverty of Philosophy, that the hand-mill gives you society with the feudal lord and the steam-mill society with the industrial capitalist, and Cohen's defence can be understood as an attempt to state what that sentence could mean if it were true. Read as a causal slogan, the sentence is false in both directions often enough to embarrass a schoolchild: hand-mills ground corn under many social forms, and steam power was harnessed by slave plantations and state arsenals as readily as by free factories. Read as Cohen read it, as the compressed statement of a functional correspondence, the sentence asserts that the relations characteristic of an epoch are those under which its characteristic productive forces can be deployed and developed, feudal lordship fitting a world of dispersed agrarian technique and industrial capital fitting a world of concentrated mechanical power. The reading rescues the sentence from falsity at the price of the whole apparatus this article has examined: the correspondence must be explanatory, the explanation must be functional, and the functional claim must survive the demand for its mechanism. The aphorism thus became, in the debate's hands, a diagnostic instrument. A reader's attitude to the sentence, slogan, correspondence, or promissory note, is a reliable map of that reader's position in the controversy, and Cohen's willingness to stake the defence on the strongest reading, rather than retreating to the slogan's poetic licence, is once again the founding wager in miniature: the tradition's most quotable sentence, treated as a theory and examined as one.

The audit of the aphorism also displays, in the small compass of a single example, why the mechanism's absence is not a pedant's quibble. Between the steam-mill and the industrial capitalist stand two centuries of enclosures, poor laws, colonial trades, legal revolutions, and wars, every one of them a candidate cause of the correspondence and none of them a filter that selected relations by their service to steam. The correspondence at the end of the process is real; the mills and the capitalists do characterise the same epoch. What the functional reading adds to the correspondence is a claim about why the epoch's relations took the form they did, and the addition is exactly what the intervening centuries, examined closely, decline to underwrite in the theory's currency. They underwrite it in the currencies of power, law, and accumulation, the currencies of the rival explanations. Cohen's late position can be stated as the recognition that the addition, however natural the aphorism makes it feel, is an addition, one the evidence does not compel and the mechanism does not cash. A sentence that had served for a century as the theory's emblem thus ends the debate as its most instructive exhibit: the emblem of a pattern in search of its process, preserved in the preface's amber beside the theory it adorned.

## What the defence's illustrations could and could not show

Cohen's book marshalled historical illustrations with a care that distinguished it from the tradition's anecdotal habits, and the illustrations' logic deserves a final clarification, since students routinely overestimate what they establish. An illustration of the theory shows a case the theory organises: relations arising as forces press against their predecessors, a period of fettering marked by the theory's characteristic pathologies, a resolution in which growth resumes under new forms. The organisation is genuinely illuminating, and a case organised is not nothing; it demonstrates the theory's vocabulary can cut the record at joints a reader recognises. But organisation is compatible with the pattern's being an artefact of the organising, since the historian who knows the theory's script can find its scenes in any sufficiently rich record, and the scenes' rivals, the episodes where relations changed for reasons the theory cannot use or persisted against its predictions, sit in the same record awaiting an organiser of the opposite school. Cohen understood the asymmetry and said so, which is why the book's argumentative weight rests on the philosophy and not on the illustrations. The illustrations were offered as the theory's plausibility, the philosophy as its credentials, and the objection's genius was to accept the plausibility and audit the credentials. Later defenders who respond to the mechanism objection by multiplying illustrations are therefore answering a charge that was never brought, with evidence of a kind the charge expressly discounts, and the multiplication has become, in the decades since, the surest sign that a defence has not understood what it is defending against. The requirement's pedagogical value lies in making that sign legible early, before a student's own work accumulates illustrations in place of the mechanism the question asked for.

## The preface to the Critique as constitution: the interpretive wager beneath the defence

Every reconstruction rests on an interpretive wager, and Cohen's was that the preface Marx wrote in 1859 for A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy should be treated as the theory of history's constitutional document, the place where its author, in a rare mood of summary, stated the whole doctrine in a page. The wager determined the defence's shape. From the preface Cohen drew the correspondence holding between relations and forces, the fettering that turns development's forms into its chains, the epoch of social revolution that follows, and the distinction of base and superstructure with the former's explanatory priority. Critics of the reconstruction have challenged the wager from both flanks: historians of Marx's thought note that the preface was a retrospective self-presentation, written to introduce a work whose actual method is far more concrete and conjunctural than the summary suggests, while theorists of other Marxisms prize different texts, the Theses on Feuerbach, the Grundrisse, the chapters of Capital on primitive accumulation, whose emphases disturb the preface's clean architecture. Cohen met the challenges with the candour that runs through the whole career: the preface's status is a judgement, defended by the coherence of the theory it yields and by Marx's own presentation of the passage as the guiding thread of his studies, and a reader who rejects the judgement gets a different Marx, at the price of a different and, Cohen argued, lesser theory. The wager's interest for this profile is that it shows the defence's foundations to be interpretive as well as philosophical, so that the retreat, when it came, had a precise address: Cohen withdrew confidence from the theory the preface, on his reading, asserts, and a reader who never accepted the reading can consistently hold that what failed was the reconstruction rather than Marx. The school's internal literature contains that position, held by scholars who keep a looser, more conjunctural Marx in reserve. Cohen's response, implicit in the preface of 2000, is that the looser Marx is not a theory of history at all in the sense worth defending, and that rescuing Marx by dissolving his theory into conjuncture repeats the protection the analytical school was founded to refuse. The exchange is the reconstruction debate's final form, and it leaves the reader with a clean choice that this article, under its neutrality discipline, presents without taking: a definite theory, examined and found unestablished, or an indefinite one, unexaminable and therefore unestablishable, with Cohen's career as the argument that the first is the only choice an honest tradition can make.

## The classroom presence: clarity as a moral quality

Accounts of Cohen as a teacher converge on a trait his books display on every page, a treatment of clarity not as a stylistic preference but as a moral quality, the form that respect for a reader takes. The seminar manner his students describe, the insistence that a claim be stated in a form in which it could be false, the patience with objections from any quarter provided they were stated in that form too, and the corresponding impatience, legendary and occasionally theatrical, with formulations designed to be unanswerable, are the classroom originals of the method this article has tracked through the publications. The connection matters for more than colour. A school of thought is reproduced through its pedagogy before its publications, and the September Group's standards survived the group's dissolution partly because its principals taught them, year after year, to students who carried them into other fields. Cohen's Oxford decades placed those standards at the centre of British political philosophy's training at exactly the period when his own migration made him a central figure in that field, so that the mechanism mind's habits, specify, separate, declare your defeater, became part of the professional formation of a generation of philosophers who may never have read a page of the Marxist work where the habits were forged. Intellectual influence of that kind does not appear in citation counts, and profiles that rely on counts understate it. The fairest measure of Cohen's teaching is the number of his students and readers who report the same small experience: an inability, acquired in his vicinity, to let their own favourite claims pass in the vague form in which they first thought them.

The theatrical impatience deserves its honest sentence, since a profile that reports only virtues fails its subject's own standard. Cohen could be a formidable and not always gentle controversialist, and the clarity doctrine, wielded by a master, can become an instrument of domination in a seminar room, where the demand for exact statement is also a demand to fight on the master's ground. Colleagues and students who admired him most record the edge alongside the generosity, the readiness to help any interlocutor state a position better, including positions he intended to demolish, which he regarded as a duty rather than a courtesy. The combination, demolition preceded by assistance, is the seminar form of steelmanning, and its presence in the recollections suggests that the method's ethics were practised and not only preached. A thinker profile in a series governed by neutrality rules can record the combination without adjudicating the temperament, and the record completes the portrait the argument required: the standards that undid his theory were not abstractions he invoked against others but disciplines he submitted to, in print and in person, at the cost his preface itemised.

## Cohen among the cluster's figures: the defender as the school's centre of gravity

The cluster of articles to which this profile belongs treats the analytical school figure by figure, and Cohen's position in the sequence is structural rather than honorary. The school's founder in the order of argument, he supplied its flagship theory and its standard of statement; the objector whose profile follows his in the series supplied the criticism that redefined the school's agenda; the microfoundations debate generalised the criticism into a programme; and the school's survey, which this profile has linked at the points where a reader needs the whole map, records the dispersion of the school's energies into the disciplines from which its members came. Cohen is the centre of gravity because the school's story cannot be told without the defence and the retreat, while the defence and the retreat can be, and in this article have been, told as a self-contained episode with the school as their setting. That asymmetry is the precise sense in which the mechanism debate is the cluster's load-bearing argument. Remove Roemer's models and the school loses a research programme; remove Wright's class analysis and it loses an empirical wing; remove Cohen's defence and its fate, and the school loses its plot, the story of a tradition that asked to be judged by the standards of science and philosophy and was, by its own best member, judged.

The comparison with the classical tradition the school reacted against sharpens the same point from the other side. Classical Marxism's theorists of history, for all their differences, shared a confidence that the doctrine's truth was guaranteed by its place in the movement's life, a confidence that functioned, in the vocabulary of this article, as a refusal to specify defeaters. The analytical school's wager was that the doctrine would be strengthened by exposure. The wager lost in the flagship case and won in the discipline it created, and the school's exam guide, elsewhere in this series, teaches candidates to hold both halves of that result without flinching, since the halves are the answer to the question the school's history poses. Cohen's profile is where the holding is learned in its hardest form, because his is the case where the exposure was total and the result was negative and the man reporting the result was the man who had most to lose by it. A series on Marxism that omitted the episode would be protecting its subject in exactly the manner the episode condemns, and the series' editors have instead placed it near the cluster's head, where it belongs, as the standard by which the cluster's other verdicts should be graded for honesty.

## A note on sources and the verification discipline of this profile

Because this article's brief carries verification flags, its practice should be visible to the reader in a closing note on sources. The load-bearing documents are few and have been cited by their dates and venues throughout: the defence of 1978 and its expanded edition of 2000 with the preface on which the retreat's record rests; the objection in Theory and Society in 1982 and its author's book of 1985; the reply in Inquiry in the same year as the objection; the self-ownership book of 1995; the egalitarian book of 2000 whose title is its author's question to his own class; the papers on the currency of equality and on facts and principles; the justice volume of 2008; and the short book of 2009 with which the career closed. Quotations in this article have been kept short and confined to phrases whose wording is fixed in those documents, the tradition's own terms and its author's recorded withdrawal of confidence chief among them, and figures of the kind the series' protocols treat as contested, print runs, memberships, and the statistics of schools, have been omitted rather than estimated, since the argument needs none of them. A reader who wishes to audit the profile against its sources will find that the audit is easy, which is the point: an article about the demand for checkable claims should be checkable in its turn, and the standard it has applied to a theory of history applies, in miniature, to itself.

The same discipline governed the article's silences. Cohen's private life enters only where the brief required the formation to be sketched, and it has been used to explain nothing about the arguments' validity. His colleagues appear in their argumentative roles, the objector as the objection's author, the school's other members as the programme's co-builders, and no judgement on any living scholar's present work has been offered or is needed. The theory's rivals appear at the strength their best representatives gave them, the demographic and class-power accounts of transition, the liberal egalitarianism of the ethos debate, the libertarianism of the self-ownership confrontation, since a profile of a steelmanner that strawmanned its subject's opponents would betray its subject in the act of praising him. What remains after those disciplines is the article the reader has finished: a defence stated at full strength, an objection stated at full strength, a concession reported from its documents, a verdict delivered with its defeater attached, and a second career presented as the first one's consequence rather than its replacement. The form is Cohen's, borrowed deliberately, and the borrowing is the profile's final judgement on its subject: that his manner of proceeding, claim, objection, concession, defeater, is the part of the inheritance that no future evidence can retire, whatever becomes of the theory it was first used to defend and finally used to surrender.

## Inside the defence: the order of the book's argument and why the order matters

A reader opening Karl Marx's Theory of History for the first time is often surprised by how long the book takes to reach its famous claims, and the surprise is the first lesson the book teaches. The opening movement is definitional and deliberately unglamorous: what the productive forces are, with the exclusions argued one by one; what production relations are, with economic ownership separated from its legal clothing; what it is for one thing to explain another in the materialist idiom. Cohen's wager was that the theory's defenders had always hurried past this movement because its results were inconveniently narrow, and that its critics had hurried past because a theory never stated cannot be refuted to anyone's satisfaction. Only after the definitions does the primacy thesis receive its statement, and only after the statement does the functional interpretation arrive to give the statement its explanatory form, presented not as a flourish but as the solution to a problem the preceding chapters have made unavoidable: correspondence without a connecting claim is a coincidence repeated across millennia, and the theory either asserts the connection or dissolves into chronicle. The middle movement then tests the construction against the superstructure, against the theory's account of social change, and against the rival readings of Marx that the reconstruction displaces, while the closing movement considers what the theory, so stated, predicts about the future of the forces and the relations that house them. The order matters because it is the order of the retreat reversed. Cohen's later qualifications struck the construction in the sequence opposite to its building: the predictions softened first, the superstructural applications were sorted next, the functional centre was held longest, and the definitions, the first thing built, were never surrendered at all. A building dismantled in reverse order of construction is being dismantled by someone who remembers exactly how it was made, and the memory's precision is what distinguishes the retreat from a change of mind.

The book's manner deserves the same attention as its order, since the manner is the school's method in its purest literary form. Cohen writes in numbered distinctions and imagined objectors, pausing to state the misunderstanding before it occurs, conceding the premise that costs him least and contesting the one that costs him most with a patience that reviewers described as relentless. The prose has no interest in the sublime. Where the tradition's classics reach for the imagery of earthquakes and midwives, Cohen reaches for the example of the tribe, the mill, the legal form, each chosen to isolate one variable of the argument and hold the others still. Readers formed on the classics sometimes find the manner airless, and the complaint records a real trade: the defence purchased examinability at the price of the rhetoric that had carried the theory through a century of movements. Cohen's judgement, vindicated by the debate the purchase made possible, was that the theory had been carried far enough by rhetoric and would either survive examination or not survive at all. The judgement's consequence for the series this article belongs to should be plain. A reference work on Marxism written after Cohen owes its readers his manner whether or not it shares his conclusions: distinctions before verdicts, objections before answers, and the conditions of surrender declared in advance. The articles in this cluster attempt that manner as a house style, and the present profile, which has declared its defeater and printed the grades of its subject's claims on their faces, is the house style applied to the man who taught it to the house.

## The exchange over the objection read closely: two papers and a turning point

The debate's documentary core is short enough to be read in an afternoon, and reading it closely, rather than through the summaries that have accumulated around it, repays the effort with a surprise: the two principals agree on more than the folklore of a refutation suggests. Elster's article grants the defence its reconstruction, grants that Marx's theory is functional in form, and grants that functional explanation is legitimate in biology, before asking why the social case should be exempt from the condition the biological case satisfies. Cohen's reply grants the objection its central distinction, grants that elaborations are the explanations' eventual obligation, and grants that Marxists had presumed them too easily, before arguing that the obligation is not a precondition of the explanation's present legitimacy. The disagreement, stripped of folklore, is a disagreement about the terms of a loan: whether a consequence explanation borrows against a mechanism it must eventually produce, with the explanation's legitimacy as the collateral, or whether it holds its legitimacy outright, with the mechanism as a separate asset to be acquired when fortune allows. Everything that followed for twenty years is the loan's history. The defence's qualifications were the interest payments, each preserving the explanation's standing for another season. The preface was the moment the borrower announced that the principal would not, in his judgement, be repaid, and that honesty required the announcement before the collateral was called in by others. Read in that register, the episode loses the melodrama of victory and defeat and gains its actual significance, as a case study in the temporality of theoretical credit: how long a pattern may legitimately stand in for its process, who decides when the standing has lasted too long, and what it looks like when the decision is made by the pattern's own author on the evidence of his own school's search.

The close reading also corrects a persistent error about the objection's scope. Elster's target in 1982 was not the theory of history as a whole but a specific explanatory practice within it, and his article distinguishes the claims he impugns from those he leaves standing, including much of the reconstruction itself. The folklore that remembers a demolition mistakes the objection's precision for breadth, and the mistake flatters neither party: it makes the objector cruder than he was and the defender's concession grander and vaguer than it was. Precision about scope is what allows the concession to be weighed correctly. Cohen did not concede that Marxism was finished, or that the theory of history was worthless, or that the objection had refuted the primacy thesis. He conceded that a specified condition of the thesis's full establishment had not been met and showed no sign of being met, and he withdrew the confidence that the condition's non-fulfilment had previously not shaken. The concession's narrowness is its strength as evidence, since a scholar conceding exactly one point, the point his critic named, after twenty years of testing it, tells the reader precisely where the structure failed. Broader concessions would tell less. The article's verdict has been built to the same narrowness for the same reason.

## A repaired historical materialism: what the defeater would have to deliver

The invitation carried by this article's defeater can be given a final, concrete form by sketching, in neutral terms, what a submission answering it would have to contain, so that future claimants can be measured against the sketch rather than against enthusiasm. The submission would open by naming its process or processes without reference to any transition it hopes to explain, in the way selection is named without reference to any species. It would specify the units on which the process operates, the variants among which it discriminates, and the currency in which success is reckoned, and it would show that the currency exchanges into productive development at rates that are stated and defended rather than assumed. It would identify the timescale of discrimination and reconcile that timescale with the theory's narrative, in which fettering epochs last for centuries that the process must be shown to be working through rather than merely waiting out. It would then be run, by its author before its critics, against the hardest cases in the record: the intensification of serfdom in the east under the same pressures that dissolved it in the west, the long persistence of relations its own account classifies as fetters, the transitions accomplished by conquest whose productive sequel was incidental. A submission that survived its author's own run against those cases would have earned the field's attention, and a submission that then survived the field's would defeat this article's verdict, which stands ready to be defeated in exactly that way and no other. The sketch is demanding because the standard is the one its own defender accepted, and its demandingness is the measure of what the theory claimed. Modest theories need modest mechanisms. A theory of the succession of all epochs needs the mechanism this sketch describes, and the sketch's emptiness, forty years after the demand was first precisely stated, is the finding with which any honest profile of the theory's ablest defender must reckon.

## A candidate from an unexpected quarter: cultural transmission and its limits

One research programme that arose after the debate's main exchange deserves examination under the standard the debate set, because it is the closest the social sciences have come to exhibiting a genuine selection process operating on cultural material, and its distance from the theory's needs is itself instructive. The dual-inheritance literature, associated with Robert Boyd and Peter Richerson and their collaborators, models culture as a second inheritance system running beside the genetic one: practices, skills, and beliefs are copied between people with measurable fidelity, the copying is biased in describable ways, toward the prestigious, toward the majority, toward whatever worked last season, and variants spread or vanish under those biases across generations of learners. Here, at last, are components named in advance of outcomes, operations that can be investigated in laboratories and field sites, and a currency, the frequency of a trait among learners, in which success is reckoned without borrowing the result to be explained. A defender of the old theory might hope that the missing machinery has been found at last, wearing new clothes.

The hope survives inspection only in weakened shape, and the weakening is worth specifying because it shows how exacting the standard remains even when a real process is on the table. Transmission biases explain why a practice spreads among the people exposed to it; they are silent, in themselves, about why the practice serves the growth of productive power, since prestige and conformity copy failure as faithfully as success when the prestigious and the numerous happen to be failing. A technique can conquer a population because a admired chief adopted it by accident, and a productive arrangement can die unlearned because its bearers lacked standing. The currency of cultural selection is uptake, and uptake exchanges into productive development at rates that vary with everything else in the society, which is the original looseness reappearing inside a genuinely specified process. What the programme gives the theory is therefore not the mechanism the defeater demands but a demonstration of what such a mechanism's paperwork looks like: components, biases, rates, and a literature that argues about measurements rather than about whether measurement is the right demand. The contrast with the older debate is quietly devastating. In the neighbouring field, the demand for specification was met by building instruments. In the theory of history, the same demand was met, for two decades, by restating the pattern with increasing elegance, until the pattern's author announced the result.

A second lesson from the comparison concerns scale and the unit of selection. Cultural transmission operates on traits small enough to be copied in a lifetime of watching and being corrected: a knapping technique, a recipe, a rule of etiquette, a way of organising a hunt. Relations of production are not copied in that grain. They are inhabited, enforced, and occasionally overthrown, and a person no more adopts a property regime by imitation than a fish adopts water. The theory's explanandum sits at a level where the candidate process does not grip, and bridging the levels, from copied techniques to constituted relations, requires exactly the intermediate account, of how aggregated trait changes remake the powers persons hold over one another's labour, that the tradition always promised and never delivered. The candidate thus fails the defeater's checklist at the discrimination clause and the unit clause together, while passing the independence clause with honours. Partial passes of that shape are the normal fate of mechanisms proposed for grand theories, and learning to score them, clause by clause rather than by enthusiasm, is among the transferable skills this article exists to teach.

## Practice after Cohen: how working historians absorbed the demotion

The strong theory's demotion did not empty the workshops. Historical practice absorbed the result in the way professions usually absorb a methodological verdict, not by manifesto but by a slow change in what counts as a finished explanation. Studies of transition that a previous generation would have closed with an appeal to the requirements of developing production are expected to close with the agents, organisations, and contingencies that did the work, and the appeal survives, where it survives, as an opening framing rather than as a conclusion. Textbook presentations of the materialist conception have quietly reordered their emphases in the same direction: the correspondence of relations to forces is presented as the theory's organising intuition, the fettering dynamic as its account of crisis, and the question of what drives the correspondence is flagged, in the better textbooks, as the point where the theory's defenders owe more than they have paid. That flag is Cohen's and Elster's joint monument in the pedagogy, more durable than either man's bibliography considered separately. A student can meet the flag in an introductory course without meeting either name, which is how methodological victories usually end: anonymously, as common sense.

Among philosophers the absorption took a different route, through the rehabilitation of explanation talk itself. The decades after the debate saw the philosophy of the social sciences reorganise around mechanisms as its central category, with handbooks, research programmes, and a generation of scholars for whom the demand that a pattern produce its process is the first move in any inquiry. Cohen's debate is one of that reorganisation's founding exhibits, cited by writers who have no stake in Marxism because it presents the demand and the resistance to it in their purest available forms, a first-rate defence of pattern-first explanation and a first-rate insistence on process, conducted to a documented conclusion. The exhibit's purity depends on a feature this profile has emphasised throughout: both parties shared the standards by which the conclusion was reached. Exhibits drawn from quarrels between rival frameworks teach less, because the loser can always blame the framework. Here the loser supplied the framework. That is why the case is assigned, and why it will continue to be assigned after the details of the primacy thesis have become the province of specialists.

For the tradition itself, the absorption is still incomplete, and the incompleteness has a geography worth noting without polemic. Where the theory of history is taught as a living research programme, the debate is central and the demotion is priced in; where it is taught as heritage, the strong claims circulate in their pre-debate form, and students can complete an education in the tradition without learning that its ablest defender audited it and published the audit. The series to which this article belongs has taken the first course throughout, on the principle its subject exemplified: a tradition is honoured by the examination it survives in public, not by the protection it enjoys in private. Readers who proceed from this profile to the cluster's remaining articles will find the same course held there, verdicts delivered with their defeaters attached and concessions reported from the documents that made them, which is the only tribute to Cohen that he would have recognised as one.

## Primitive accumulation as the stress test the framework sets itself

One portion of Marx's own historical writing sits awkwardly inside any functional reconstruction, and the awkwardness is diagnostic enough to deserve a section near the end of this profile. The chapters of Capital on so-called primitive accumulation describe the making of the capitalist order's preconditions: peasants separated from the land by enclosure and statute, colonial plunder funnelling wealth toward the ports of Europe, the slave trade and the plantation system supplying both markets and capital, public debt and the tax system concentrating the proceeds. The narrative's explanatory currency is force, fraud, and legislation, not developmental benefit. Nothing in it suggests that the new relations were retained because they fostered productive growth; they were imposed, at gunpoint and by act of Parliament, by parties who profited from the imposition, and their later service to growth was, on the face of the account, a consequence nobody in the story was selecting for. Cohen's framework has the resources to absorb the narrative, through the distinction this article drew earlier between origins and persistence: primitive accumulation explains where the relations came from, the functional claim explains why, having come, they stayed and generalised while rival arrangements withered. The absorption is legitimate and it is also revealing, because it concentrates the entire weight of the theory onto the persistence claim at exactly the historical site where the record is richest. If the staying and spreading of capitalist relations across the centuries after their violent installation cannot be shown to track their developmental service, through a process that can be pointed to rather than presumed, then the theory's flagship transition is explained, in the end, by the violence that installed it and the power that guarded it, and the functional superstructure of the explanation is decoration on a causal story of the ordinary kind.

The stress test generalises into a habit of reading that researchers can apply to any grand theory with a functional centre. Find the case where the theory's own canonical texts narrate the crucial change in non-functional terms, and ask how much of the theory's claim survives the translation of that case into its official vocabulary. Where the translation is smooth, the theory is healthy. Where the translation requires the origin and persistence distinction to do all the work, and where persistence itself, examined closely, keeps resolving into the decisions of the powerful, the theory is living on the distinction's credit. Cohen, to give the defence its final due, never hid the primitive accumulation chapters from his reconstruction; he treated them as belonging to the theory's account of a particular transition's prehistory, and the treatment is defensible page by page. The objection's question survives the treatment, as it survived every treatment: granted the prehistory, what selected the sequel? The centuries answer with markets, empires, and wars, each a real process, none of them a filter keyed to productive development with anything like the fidelity the consequence law requires. The stress test therefore ends where the whole debate ends, with the pattern visible, the process unexhibited, and the invitation to exhibit it still open on the terms the assessment declared.

## The objector's later career as the objection's confirmation

A brief coda on the objection's author is warranted, not as biography, which belongs to his own article in this series, but as evidence about the objection's character. Elster's subsequent work generalised the demand of 1982 into a philosophy of social science in which mechanisms, understood as frequently occurring causal patterns that can be recognised without yet amounting to laws, became the discipline's proper currency, a programme set out for a wide audience in his later synthesising work on social explanation. The trajectory matters here for one reason: a criticism improvised to wound a rival would not have organised its author's next three decades. The objection organised them, which corroborates the reading this profile has given throughout, that the demand for a feedback process was a methodological conviction applied to Marxism because Marxism's claims were the largest in the room, and applied afterward to every room its author entered, including rooms furnished with his own earlier opinions. Convictions of that durability are the kind that defenders cannot wait out, and Cohen, who knew the man and the conviction from the inside of the same small group, can be presumed to have understood earlier than most readers that the deferral strategy had a terminus. The preface's timing, arriving after the qualifications had exhausted their room, looks in that light less like surrender at a random moment and more like the settlement of an account whose terms both parties had always understood.

The group's two principals thus ended in a symmetry that the folklore of victory and defeat obscures. The defender spent his second career building the normative theory his first career's failure made necessary. The objector spent his building the explanatory methodology his objection had sketched. Each man's later work is the positive form of the position he had pressed against the other: Cohen's insistence that claims be stated exactly became a theory of justice stated exactly; Elster's insistence that patterns produce their processes became a science of mechanisms. The debate between them, considered as a whole, is therefore not a destruction but a division of labour performed in public, in which a tradition's two best instruments were separated, sharpened, and carried into different workshops. Readers of this series meet both instruments again, the defeater discipline in the cluster's critical articles and the mechanism discipline wherever explanations are graded, and the profile of the defender is the right place to record that the instruments were forged in a single exchange, by friends, to standards both of them kept for the rest of their working lives.

## The stagnant empires problem: the development thesis meets its hardest scenery

The tendency at the foundation of the whole construction faced a standing embarrassment that critics raised early and often, and a complete profile should give the embarrassment its paragraph before the closing guides. The great agrarian empires of Asia and the ancient world commanded enormous populations, sophisticated administration, and refined technique, yet their productive methods changed, across many centuries, at a pace that mocks any robust reading of a developmental drive. Irrigation systems were maintained with genius and extended hardly at all; craft knowledge accumulated in guild secrecy and slept there; inventions appeared, paper, printing, gunpowder, the compass, and were absorbed into the existing order as ornaments or instruments of rule rather than as levers of transformation. If scarcity presses and rationality responds and knowledge accumulates, why did the pressure vent so weakly for so long in the places where civilisation was oldest? The question is the development thesis's version of the mechanism problem, one level down: a tendency whose operation varies by orders of magnitude across the very cases it must cover is a tendency whose conditions do the explaining, and the conditions, property, power, and the interests of the strata who administer both, belong to the side of the theory that was supposed to be explained.

Cohen's handling of the embarrassment was characteristic in method and fateful in consequence. He narrowed the tendency's claim: it operates where the social form does not systematically penalise innovation, where producers can capture enough of an improvement's gain to make adopting it rational, where knowledge can circulate beyond the custodians who hoard it. Each condition is plausible, and each is a fact about relations, so the defence of the first thesis ends by borrowing its premises from the second thesis's territory, the territory whose explanation was the theory's central promise. The circularity is gentle rather than vicious, a web of mutual support rather than a loop of petitio, but gentleness did not save the architecture's claim to explanatory order. A theory in which the forces explain the relations, except that the forces' own dynamism is explained by the relations, has traded a hierarchy for a circle, and circles, however virtuous as descriptions of mutual influence, do not deliver primacy. The stagnant empires thus did to the development thesis, slowly and from the evidence side, what the objection did to the functional explanation from the logic side, and the two pressures meet in the preface's single sentence like two tunnels meeting under a mountain. Readers who want to feel the evidence side at full strength should pair this profile with the series' treatment of the theory's record in Asia, and should notice, as they read, how often the explanation of a stagnation or a breakthrough terminates in the decisions of courts, the security of tenure, and the price of labour, the small change of ordinary causal history, rather than in any pressure exerted by technique upon the forms that house it.

The episode carries one more lesson, about the difference between a tendency and a mood. A generation of the theory's expositors wrote as though history's direction were a settled backdrop against which particular inquiries proceeded, the way a novelist assumes the weather. Cohen's premises were an attempt to replace the mood with an argument, and the argument's troubles, exhibited by the empires, are the price of the replacement: premises can be checked, and the checking found them insufficiently general. The mood survives in popular versions of the theory still, untroubled by the audit, because moods cannot be audited. The audit exists in print for anyone who prefers arguments, and its existence, rather than its outcome, is what separates the tradition's scientific pretensions from its literary ones. That separation was Cohen's first gift to his subject. The preface was his last. Between them stands the defence itself, the most instructive failure in the modern literature of Marxism, and the article that closes here has attempted nothing more, and nothing less, than to exhibit the failure at the standard of statement its author set for everyone else.

## Explanation and justification: the two questions Cohen refused to merge

A distinction runs underneath every episode in this profile, seldom named outright, and naming it in the article's final movement lets the whole career snap into a single intelligible shape. Explanation answers the question why something exists or endures. Justification answers the question why something deserves allegiance. The tradition Cohen inherited had fused the two operations into one gesture: the theory of history explained capitalism's transience and socialism's arrival, and the explanation was treated as carrying the justification inside it, the way a diagnosis was once thought to carry its own prescription. Cohen's life work can be read as the patient separation of the fused gesture into its components, and the assignment of each component to the discipline equipped to grade it. Explanation went to the philosophy of social science, where it was audited for its missing process and found, in its grandest instance, incomplete. Justification went to moral philosophy, where it was audited for its unexamined premises and rebuilt, premise by premise, into positions that could be defended without borrowing authority from any prediction. The separation explains the career's otherwise puzzling combination of loss and gain. Nothing was lost that the audit did not take, and the audit took only what had never been established. Everything gained, the egalitarian arguments, the currency distinction, the ethos, the camping trip's honest confession, was gained on ground the separation cleared, ground where a claim's credentials are its arguments and nothing else.

The separation also supplies the fairest one-sentence verdict on the school that formed around him. Analytical Marxism separated what its tradition had fused, submitted each separated claim to the tribunal of the relevant discipline, and published the tribunals' findings without regard to which side of the old fusion they favoured. Some findings were favourable and have been absorbed so thoroughly that their origin is forgotten. The finding at the centre of this profile was unfavourable, and it has been absorbed the same way, as the standing requirement that explanation by benefit produce its process or confess the omission. A reader who has followed the argument to this point possesses both findings in their usable forms, together with the habit that produced them, and the habit, specify the claim, invite the strongest objection, declare what would settle the matter, is the closest thing the episode offers to a transferable inheritance. It is fitting that the inheritance is a practice rather than a doctrine. Doctrines were the episode's casualties. The practice walked out of the wreckage intact, and it is practised, in departments that have never heard the September Group's name, by scholars who would be surprised to learn whose seminar discipline they inherited.

## For students and exam candidates

Begin with the short book and the long one's architecture rather than with summaries of either. Read the camping trip argument first, since it teaches Cohen's manner in miniature: a claim stated exactly, an objection entertained fairly, a confession printed where a slogan would go. Then take the defence in its own order, definitions before theses, and keep the two theses separate in your notes, since most weak answers on this material fail by fusing the development thesis with the primacy thesis and attributing the functional form to the wrong one. Learn the objection as a sequence of three moves, benefit, missing process, asserted connection, and learn the reply as a distinction between an explanation and its elaboration. The contrast with classical Marxism, set out in the article on [analytical versus classical Marxism](/2020/04/01/analytical-vs-classical-marxism/), will show you what the analytical manner was reacting against. In essays, imitate the master's signature move: end every verdict by stating what would change it. Examiners reward the move because it demonstrates that your judgement is a conclusion rather than a posture, and on this topic, where candidates divide into partisans and detractors before they begin, the demonstration is worth more than any additional fact you could memorise.

## For teachers and seminar leaders

The camping trip is your most efficient teaching instrument: a single session can put the principles on the board, let the class endorse them in the trip's small compass, and then spend the remaining time on the machinery question, which students discover for themselves the moment they try to scale the arrangements up and find that monitoring, motivation, and information each fail in a different way. For the historical material, resist the shortcut of presenting the objection before the defence has been felt from inside. Give the defence its hour at full strength, including the consequence law and the drug analogy, and let the class defend it against your questions; the objection then lands with the force its author intended, as a tightening from within standards the class has already accepted. The preface sentence makes a closing exercise of unusual power: distribute it without context, ask whose sentence it might be and what would make a scholar write it, and only then reveal that its author wrote the book the course has been studying. The revelation teaches the episode's lesson about intellectual honesty more durably than any summary, and it sends students to the texts, which is where this material insists on being learned.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

The research value of the episode lies in its documentary completeness, a defence, an objection, two decades of itemised qualification, and a preface, all in print and all citable, which makes it a controlled case for the study of how research programmes actually end: not by falsification in the textbook sense but by the exhaustion of a specified obligation, recorded by the person who owed it. Historians will find the Brenner controversy the indispensable companion case, since it applies the same pressure from the archives that the objection applied from logic, and the pair together let a researcher watch a theory being squeezed from both ends in the same decade. Journalists covering the afterlife of Marxist ideas should note the distinction this profile has maintained throughout, between the theory of history, whose establishment failed, and the moral philosophy that succeeded it, since conflating them produces the standard inaccurate obituary in which a thinker either defended Marx or abandoned him. Cohen did the first, then reported the defence's result, then built the second career on the report. The mechanism requirement, finally, is a portable instrument for any researcher who encounters explanation by benefit in the wild, in institutional analysis, in evolutionary accounts of culture, or in the functionalist idioms of policy writing, and the grading scheme set out in this article, loop specified, loop gestured at, benefit self-certifying, can be applied to a manuscript in an afternoon.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is a functional explanation?

A functional explanation accounts for an item through what it does rather than through the events that produced it. In biology, a trait is accounted for by the contribution its presence makes to survival; in Marx's theory, on Cohen's reconstruction, a set of production relations is accounted for by the contribution it makes to developing the productive forces. The form is legitimate in biology because natural selection connects effect to persistence. Cohen defended its use in the theory of history, arguing that an explanation of this kind can be genuine before the connecting process is known in detail.

### Q: What is the primacy thesis?

The primacy thesis is Cohen's name for the claim that a society's production relations take their character from the level its productive forces have reached. Primacy is explanatory, not chronological or moral: it says where the deepest answer lies when we ask why these relations rather than others prevail. The thesis depends on the functional form, since the connection it asserts runs from what relations do for the forces back to the presence of the relations themselves.

### Q: What is the development thesis?

The development thesis is the claim that productive power, across the long stretches of history, tends to grow. Cohen grounded the tendency in three premises: human beings face scarcity, they are rational enough to recognise and adopt better means, and they can accumulate productive knowledge across generations. The thesis asserts a tendency rather than a guarantee, allowing stagnation and reversal, but it supplies the direction by which relations may be judged as promoting or obstructing, the gradient the primacy thesis then exploits in its explanations.

### Q: Why did Cohen use the analogy with natural selection?

Because biology is the home of successful explanation by effects, and the analogy let Cohen argue that the form itself is respectable rather than a Marxist peculiarity. A trait explained by its contribution to fitness is not accused of teleology, because selection supplies the process connecting contribution to prevalence. Cohen's claim was that an explanation can precede knowledge of its process, as Darwin's did before genetics. The analogy also exposed the weakness his critics pressed: biology possesses the mechanism the social case lacks.

### Q: What is the mechanism objection?

The mechanism objection, pressed by Jon Elster in 1982, holds that explanation by beneficial effects is legitimate only when a feedback process can be exhibited through which the benefit maintains its source, as natural selection does for traits. Historical materialism, the objection runs, has no comparable process that picks out relations fostering the forces and discards relations obstructing them. Without such a process, citing a benefit amounts to noticing a happy coincidence and calling it a cause, which asserts the connection the explanation was meant to establish.

### Q: Did Cohen concede the objection?

Substantially, yes, and on the record. His first reply defended the right of an explanation to stand before its mechanism is known, but across the following years he granted that an elaboration was required and that none had been produced, qualified the supporting theses, and in the front matter of his defence's expanded edition in 2000 recorded that he could no longer affirm historical materialism as true. The concession was specific rather than total: the reconstruction's value, the form's legitimacy, and his socialism were all expressly retained.

### Q: What is the camping trip argument?

The camping trip argument, from Cohen's short book Why Not Socialism?, observes that friends on a camping trip spontaneously organise themselves by socialist principles: goods are shared by need, tasks divided by ability, and nobody leverages strength or ownership into privilege. Most readers endorse the arrangement, which shows that the obstacle to socialism is not human nature. Cohen's conclusion was that the obstacle is design: we lack the machinery for running those principles across an entire society, an unusually honest formulation of socialism's problem.

### Q: Why did Cohen turn to moral philosophy?

Because the retreat from the theory of history removed the guarantee that had let socialists treat moral argument as dispensable. With that guarantee withdrawn, socialism had to be argued for as just, before people entirely free to refuse it. Cohen also argued that the tradition had always condemned capitalism as unjust while officially disavowing moral judgement, and that honesty required stating the standard openly. His later work on self-ownership, equality, and the demands of egalitarian commitment is that statement, pursued with the same rigour as the defence.

### Q: Is the primacy thesis defensible?

In its strong form, as a functional explanation of why relations obtain, it remains unestablished for want of the mechanism its own defender came to require. In a weaker form it survives as a powerful heuristic: productive capacities constrain the menu of possible relations, and the strain when capacities outgrow their relations generates characteristic pathologies that historians ignore at their cost. Many working historians operate with the weaker form. The distance separating the two forms measures what the objection and the concession, between them, accomplished.

### Q: Where should you start with Cohen?

Start with Why Not Socialism?, his short camping-trip book, which contains the mature Cohen in miniature and asks the least prior knowledge. Readers drawn to the theory of history should then take Karl Marx's Theory of History in its expanded edition, reading the preface last as well as first, so that the defence and its author's verdict stay in view together. Those drawn to the justice debates should proceed to the self-ownership book and the essays on equality, where the arguments about what egalitarians owe are developed at full length.

### Q: How does the mechanism requirement grade functional explanation?

It grades by the availability of the loop connecting benefit to persistence. The highest grade belongs to explanations whose feedback process is specified and independently checkable, as selection is in biology or deliberate design often is for legal institutions. The next grade specifies the process but rests on thin evidence. Below that sit explanations gesturing toward a cluster of processes, no one of which must actually discriminate between success and failure. The lowest grade lets the benefit certify itself. The requirement asks only that an explanation's grade be visible on its face.

### Q: What should a student separate between explaining a pattern and explaining its persistence?

A pattern is a regularity: relations of a given type accompany forces at a given level, across many cases. Persistence is the pattern's continuation through time, and explaining it requires a process that keeps the regularity in being against the forces that erode regularities, decay, conquest, drift, and deliberate reform. Cohen's defence established the pattern to many readers' satisfaction while the process behind its persistence remained unspecified. Students who keep the two apart can state the debate in one sentence and can spot, in any field, explanations that offer a pattern where a process was requested.

### Q: Why does an explanation by effects need a feedback process behind it?

Because such an explanation makes the effect part of the reason its own cause exists or endures, and something must carry influence backward from the effect to whatever maintains the cause. In biology the carrier is differential reproduction: benefits are paid in the currency of survival, and survival is the persistence at issue. In social life the benefit, developing the productive forces, is paid in a different currency from the one in which institutions persist, which is woven from power, law, and custom, and no stable exchange between the currencies has been exhibited.

### Q: How does the argument and the retreat table order the defence?

The table sets the defence's components in rows, the primacy thesis, the development thesis, the functional explanation, and the mechanism requirement, and reads each across four columns: the original claim, the objection it met, the concession its author made, and the standing that remains. Ordered that way, the table shows the retreat's direction, from the outer claims qualified early to the functional centre qualified last, and it lets a reader test any summary of the episode, including this article's, against the sequence the documents record.

### Q: What separates a functional explanation with a mechanism from one without?

A specified process that discriminates. With a mechanism, the explanation identifies what eliminates the arrangements that fail to deliver the benefit and retains those that succeed, it can be checked against cases chosen by its critics, and it risks being wrong in ways that teach something. Without one, the explanation redescribes the outcome in the vocabulary of benefit and is compatible with every sequel, including the benefit's disappearance. The first kind explains why the world could not easily have been otherwise; the second kind explains why, whatever happened, a benefit could be found in it.
