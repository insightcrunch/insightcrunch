The labour share is among the most quoted magnitudes in arguments about who gets what under modern capitalism, and among the least examined. It names the portion of national income paid out as compensation to those who work for a living, set against the portion recorded as income from property, and a long movement in that portion, downward across most advanced economies over the decades since the closing years of the postwar settlement, is among the more robust findings in contemporary macroeconomics. The finding is real. It has been reached by different researchers, working with different datasets and different conventions, and it survives an impressive range of challenges to its construction. A reader who has encountered the claim in a speech, a headline, or a pamphlet can accept the direction of the movement with reasonable confidence.

![Ledgers, wage slips and housing keys arranged for an essay on the labour share of income, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-01.webp)

What that reader cannot accept, on the strength of the headline alone, is any particular size for the movement, and still less any particular meaning. The measured level of the share, and the measured steepness of its fall, depend on a handful of decisions about how awkward categories of income are to be treated, and those decisions are usually buried in the technical apparatus of whatever study is being quoted. Change the treatment of the self-employed, and the level moves. Change the treatment of housing, and a large part of the story changes its subject. Change the treatment of depreciation, of very high salaries, of whole sectors included or left out, and the picture shifts again, sometimes in size and sometimes in kind. None of this makes the finding a mirage. It makes the finding a constructed measure, and a constructed measure has to be read through its construction or not read at all.

There is a second caution, and it matters more than the first, especially for readers who approach this evidence from within the Marxist tradition or in argument with it. The labour share is not the rate of exploitation. The two quantities sound like neighbours, because both describe a division between what labour receives and what it does not, but they are built in different units, inside different systems of categories, for different purposes. One is a proportion of money incomes recorded by statistical offices. The other is a ratio of portions of the working day, of labour time given without equivalent against labour time that replaces the wage. A falling share of income going to compensation is consistent with a rising rate of exploitation, and the association is suggestive, but consistency is not measurement, and treating the first quantity as a reading of the second is the commonest error made with this evidence, made most often by the tradition that relies on the evidence most heavily. This article explains the construction, decision by decision, states what survives every reasonable construction, and then gives the distinction between the two quantities the extended treatment it deserves.

## The labour share and what the measure actually counts

Begin with the definition, because the definition already contains the seeds of every later difficulty. National income is the total of incomes arising from production within an economy over a period, as the statisticians who compile the national accounts define and record it. Compensation of employees is one great heading within that total: wages and salaries in cash and in kind, together with the contributions employers make on their workers' behalf toward pensions and social insurance. Divide the one magnitude by the other and the result is the share of income going to labour in this recorded sense, with the remainder going to the various forms of property income: profits of corporations, interest, rent, and the mixed income of the self-employed, which refuses to sit quietly in either camp and will occupy a full section below.

Every word in that definition carries a convention inside it. Income is what the accounts record as income, which is a matter of rules drawn up for the management of economies and the comparison of economies, not a matter of any theory of value. Production is what the accounts count as production, which includes services of many kinds that a Marxist classification would sort very differently, and includes some things, such as the housing services owner-occupiers are deemed to supply to themselves, that involve no market transaction at all. Compensation is what employers pay and are recorded as paying, which includes the salaries of chief executives, a fact whose consequences are large enough to deserve their own section. The measure is therefore a creature of the accounting framework from the first step. That is not a defect to be lamented and then ignored. It is the condition of using the measure honestly, and the whole argument of this article follows from taking it seriously.

### What exactly goes into compensation of employees?

Compensation of employees gathers wages and salaries in every form, cash payments, payments in kind such as housing or meals provided by an employer, and the social contributions employers pay into pension and insurance schemes on behalf of their staff, so the heading is broader than the pay packet any individual worker actually sees.

The breadth matters because public discussion usually imagines the numerator of the ratio as the wage bill in the everyday sense, the sum of what workers take home. The accounts are counting something wider and also something narrower. Wider, because employer contributions swell the recorded compensation beyond take-home pay, so a change in pension arrangements can move the measured share without any worker feeling richer or poorer. Narrower, because the heading covers employees only, and the earnings of everyone who works without an employer, the self-employed in all their variety, sit outside it under a separate heading whose treatment is a decision rather than a fact. A reader who pictures the numerator as simply wages has already misunderstood the measure, and every conclusion drawn from that picture inherits the misunderstanding.

Consider also what the denominator quietly assumes. National income is a net concept in its careful form, arrived at after deducting the consumption of fixed capital, the wearing out of the means of production, and the choice between the total's gross and net versions is another of the decisions examined below. The denominator can also be taken before or after taxes on production and subsidies, and each choice presents the division of income at a slightly different point in its journey from the factory gate to the final recipient. None of these choices is arbitrary, and none is innocent. Each corresponds to a question someone might want to ask, and the trouble begins only when an answer to one question is quoted as though it were the answer to another.

A further subtlety deserves emphasis at the outset, since it returns throughout. The share is a ratio, and a ratio can move because its top moves, because its bottom moves, or because the composition of the whole changes while every part behaves as before. An economy in which a labour-intensive sector shrinks and a capital-intensive sector grows will show a falling aggregate share even if the division of income inside every single industry stays exactly where it was. Composition effects of that kind are not errors in the accounts. They are genuine features of what an aggregate ratio can and cannot say, and they mean that the aggregate movement, however robust, always underdetermines its own explanation. The measure tells the reader that the division changed. It does not tell the reader where, or why, or at whose expense in any theoretically loaded sense, and the discipline of this article is to keep those questions separate.

## Five decisions that build the number before anyone interprets it

Before the decisions themselves, a word about their status, because misunderstanding their status is the source of much confused commentary. A treatment decision is not a fiddle, a trick, or a confession that the statistician has cooked the books. It is a point at which the raw material of economic life does not arrive pre-sorted into the headings the accounts require, so that a rule must be adopted for sorting it, and different defensible rules give different results. The honest response to such a point is not suspicion but attention: find out which rule was adopted, ask what difference the alternative rules would make, and refuse to compare results built on different rules as though they were readings of a single instrument. Thermometers do not need such decisions explained because the physical quantity sits underneath the scale. Income shares have no such underneath. The categories are the quantity, all the way down.

The five decisions treated in this article were chosen because each one moves the result by an amount that matters for the arguments the result is asked to support, and because each one is invisible in the headline version of the finding. They concern the income of the self-employed, the treatment of housing, the deduction for depreciation, the place of very high labour incomes, and the coverage of the measure across sectors of the economy. Other decisions exist, concerning taxes and subsidies, the boundary of production, the handling of financial services whose output is imputed rather than sold, and more besides. A complete anatomy would be a textbook. These five carry the bulk of the practical difference, and a reader equipped with them can interrogate any published version of the finding and discover, usually within minutes, which version of the story is actually being told.

There is also an order to the presentation, which tracks the internal logic of the accounts. The self-employment decision concerns the numerator and denominator at once, since mixed income must be split before any share can be computed at all. The housing decision concerns a peculiar component of property income that turns out to be doing a surprising amount of work in the headline trend. Depreciation concerns the difference between gross and net totals, hence the size of the whole against which every part is measured. Top incomes concern the composition of the numerator from within. Coverage concerns the perimeter of the economy being described. A reader who works through them in that order acquires not a list of caveats but a map of the measure, and the map is the point: caveats are forgotten, while a map changes how every subsequent encounter with the number is handled.

### Why do treatment decisions matter more here than in other statistics?

Because the quantity itself is defined by the categories that record it, there is no treatment-free version waiting underneath the conventions; each defensible rule for sorting awkward incomes produces a genuinely different measure, and the spread between those measures is wide enough to change the size, and occasionally the story, of the reported trend.

That answer deserves dwelling on, since it separates this measure from many others a reader will know. The length of a coastline depends on the scale of measurement, yet nobody concludes that coastlines are fictions; the underlying geography constrains every answer. With income shares, the constraint is looser. Whether the earnings of a self-employed builder count as labour income, capital income, or a blend is not a question geography answers. It is a question about what the categories are for. A statistical office interested in the functional distribution of income for macroeconomic management will answer one way. A researcher interested in the position of wage earners will answer another. Both are measuring something real, and the two somethings differ. The finding that survives is the one stated at the level where the different somethings agree, which is the level of direction across long periods, rather than that of precise magnitude within any given span.

It follows that the familiar journalistic move, quoting a fall in the share as a single clean magnitude with the authority of a physical measurement, misrepresents the evidence even when the direction quoted is sound. The magnitude belongs to a construction. Detached from the construction, it is a number in search of a meaning, and it will find whatever meaning the surrounding argument wants to lend it. Marxist commentary has been as guilty of this borrowing as any editorial page, and guiltier in one respect, since the tradition possesses, in its own categories, the tools to see exactly why a national accounts ratio cannot carry the weight placed on it, as the later sections of this article set out in full.

## Self-employment and the problem of mixed income

The first decision arises from a simple fact about how people work. A substantial minority of the working population in every advanced economy has no employer. Such people run shops, practise trades, drive their own vehicles, consult, farm, design, and repair, and what they earn is a return to several things at once: to their own labour, plainly, but also to the capital they have sunk in tools, premises, vehicles, and stock, and to the risk they carry in their own persons. The national accounts, confronting this blend, record it under a heading of its own, mixed income, and decline to split it. Any computation of a labour share must therefore adopt a rule for splitting it, and the rule adopted changes both where the share starts and how it moves.

The simplest rule assigns the whole of mixed income to labour, treating the self-employed as workers who happen to lack an employer. That choice flatters the share, raising its level, and it has a certain rough justice where the self-employed are mostly people selling their own effort with negligible capital behind them. The opposite rule assigns the whole to capital, which depresses the level and fits a world in which the self-employed are mostly small proprietors living off assets. Between those poles sits the family of imputation methods, which estimate what the self-employed would earn as employees, using the wages of comparable employees as the yardstick, and assign the remainder to capital. Each variant of that idea, differing in what counts as comparable and in how the remainder is handled, yields its own series, and the series do not merely sit at different heights. In some datasets they slope differently, because the composition of self-employment changes over time, and a rule interacts with a changing composition to produce a changing bias.

### How is self-employment income treated in the accounts?

The accounts record it unsplit as mixed income, a heading that acknowledges the blend of labour return and capital return in a self-employed person's earnings, and every published labour share therefore embodies someone's chosen rule for dividing that blend, whether the rule is announced in the text or buried in a methodological appendix.

The interaction with composition deserves a slower look, because it is the mechanism by which a treatment decision can manufacture or erase a trend. Suppose an economy in which self-employment was once dominated by farmers and shopkeepers with significant assets, and in which it comes over time to be dominated by contractors and freelancers whose capital is a laptop and a reputation. Under a fixed imputation rule calibrated to the older composition, the labour content of mixed income will be increasingly understated or overstated as the newer composition takes over, and the measured share will drift for reasons that have nothing to do with any change in the division of income between employers and employees. The drift is an artefact, but an artefact with a plausible story attached, and plausible stories are exactly what headline magnitudes acquire. Researchers who have worried at this problem, in the literature on the measurement of factor shares associated with scholars who reconstruct the series from the ground up, treat the self-employment adjustment as one of the largest single sources of disagreement between published versions of the finding.

There is a Marxist resonance here that the tradition has underexploited. The self-employed occupy an ambiguous place in Marx's own categories as well: the independent producer who owns modest means of production and works them personally is neither capitalist nor wage labourer in the clean senses, a survival or a re-creation of simple commodity production inside a capitalist economy. The statistical difficulty and the theoretical difficulty are the same difficulty wearing two coats. A category of person who genuinely stands across the divide between labour and capital will embarrass any scheme, accounting or theoretical, that requires everyone to stand on one side of it. The accounts handle the embarrassment with a heading and a shrug. Computations of the share handle it with a rule. Theory handles it, when it is honest, by admitting that the boundary of the working class is a real question and not a bookkeeping convenience. The parallel is worth holding in mind when the translation problem arrives later in this article, because mixed income is the first place a reader learns that the two category systems fail to align at exactly the points where economic life is genuinely mixed.

## Housing, imputed rent, and the share that shelter built

The second decision is the one with the power to change the story rather than merely its size. Owner-occupiers, in the logic of the national accounts, are treated as though they were landlords renting their homes to themselves. A rental value is imputed to the dwelling, entered as output of the housing sector and as income to the household in its capacity as property owner. The convention has respectable reasons behind it. Without it, an economy's measured output would lurch whenever tenure patterns shifted between renting and owning, even though the flow of shelter enjoyed by the population had not changed at all, and comparisons between economies with different tenure patterns would be distorted from the start. The convention keeps the measure of housing services steady. It also means that a growing slice of recorded capital income consists of people notionally paying themselves for the roof over their own heads.

Now place that convention inside the trend this article is about. Housing costs, and with them the imputed rental values attached to owner-occupied dwellings, rose persistently across the advanced economies over the decades in question, driven by land scarcity in prosperous cities, by planning constraints, by financial conditions, and by the treatment of dwellings as assets. As imputed rents grew, recorded property income grew with them, and the share of compensation in the total was pressed downward by arithmetic, whether or not anything changed in the relation between employers and employees anywhere in the economy. The question practically asks itself: how much of the famous fall in labour's portion is, in substance, a rise in the measured price of shelter?

Research addressed that question directly, and its answer is the single most consequential result in this whole literature for the arguments built upon the finding. Matthew Rognlie, in work presented to a Brookings conference on economic activity and widely discussed since, decomposed the rise in capital's share of income and found that housing accounts for a substantial part of it, with the return to other forms of capital behaving far less dramatically than the headline suggested. Strip housing away, on his construction, and the story of a general surge in the returns to productive capital weakens considerably; what remains is bound up with the cost and scarcity of dwellings and the land beneath them. The result does not abolish the fall in the share. It re-describes a large component of it, and a re-description of that scale changes what the evidence can be used to prove.

### What does the housing convention do to the measured result?

Imputed rent on owner-occupied homes is recorded as property income, so rising housing costs swell the capital side of the accounts and press the compensation share downward by construction; research separating housing out finds that a substantial part of the measured rise in capital's portion belongs to shelter rather than to returns on productive capital elsewhere.

Sit with the strangeness of that for a moment, because it illuminates the entire problem of the measure. A homeowner whose house rises in imputed rental value has not extracted anything from any worker. No wage bargain was struck, no working day lengthened, no intensity of effort increased. Yet the accounts register the change exactly where a rise in profits would register, and the aggregate ratio moves exactly as it would move if employers across the economy had successfully driven compensation down. The measure cannot tell the difference between those two worlds, because within its categories there is no difference to tell: property income is property income, whether it flows from a factory, a bond, or a notional tenancy of one's own home. Anyone who reads the aggregate movement as a direct transcript of class relations at the point of production has been fooled by a convention adopted, for good reasons, by statisticians who never claimed it could bear that reading.

For the Marxist tradition the housing result ought to be doubly interesting, and the brief for this article is right to complain that the tradition has engaged it far too little. In one direction the result is an embarrassment, since it weakens the favourite headline. In the other direction it is an invitation, because a rising claim of rent on land and dwellings is something Marxist categories are unusually well equipped to discuss, through the theory of ground rent and the analysis of landed property, a connection explored in the [rent versus profit debate](/2021/02/15/rent-versus-profit-debate/). A shift within property income toward rent and away from industrial profit is a distributional event of the first importance, with its own winners, its own mechanisms, and its own politics, centred on planning, land ownership, and the financial treatment of housing. Reading the accounts carefully does not leave the tradition with less to say about the period. It leaves the tradition with something different and arguably sharper to say, provided it is willing to surrender the simpler headline it had grown fond of.

There is also a lesson in the biography of the result. The decomposition was not produced by an enemy of the finding; it came from inside mainstream macroeconomics, from a researcher examining a famous claim about capital's rising share and asking what the increase was made of. That is how measurement literatures mature: a robust aggregate is pulled apart, and some components turn out to be one phenomenon while others turn out to be another wearing similar clothes. The labour share literature has been undergoing that maturation, and the housing decomposition is its most important single episode. Commentary that goes on quoting the un-decomposed aggregate, years into the maturation, is not defending a finding. It is declining to read.

## Depreciation and the difference between gross and net

The third decision looks, at first glance, like the driest of the five, and it repays attention out of proportion to its glamour. Capital wears out. Machines degrade, vehicles age, software becomes obsolete, buildings decay, and the accounts recognise that wearing out as a cost, the consumption of fixed capital, which must be deducted from gross product to arrive at the net product that represents income genuinely available to be divided. A share computed against the gross total and a share computed against the net total are therefore answers to different questions. The gross version asks how compensation stands against everything produced before replacing worn-out capital. The net version, by contrast, measures compensation against the surplus over replacement, the fund from which consumption and accumulation must both come. For most questions about distribution, the net version is the meaningful one, since nobody can eat, or invest, the portion of output that merely keeps the capital stock intact.

The decision becomes a trend question because the deduction itself has been growing. The composition of the capital stock has shifted, across the advanced economies and over the decades under discussion, toward shorter-lived assets: computing equipment and software above all, whose service lives are brief beside those of structures and heavy machinery, together with a general quickening of obsolescence as techniques change faster. A capital stock weighted toward short-lived assets throws off a larger annual charge for consumption of fixed capital relative to its size. The wedge between gross and net totals therefore widens over time, mechanically, as a consequence of what the capital stock is made of. Any series computed on the gross basis will show property income swelling relative to compensation partly because the replacement charge, which belongs to neither labour nor profit in any distributional sense that matters, is swelling inside the gross property total.

### How do the gross and net versions of the share differ?

The gross version divides compensation by output before deducting the wearing out of capital, while the net version divides by output after that deduction; because the deduction has grown as capital stocks shifted toward shorter-lived equipment and software, the net measure shows a smaller rise in capital's portion than the gross measure suggests.

Researchers who decompose the headline movement, in the strand of the literature that reconstructs factor shares from industry data, have repeatedly found that moving from gross to net terms removes a noticeable part of the apparent shift toward capital. The point is not that the gross figures are fabricated. It is that they answer a question few of the people quoting them intend to ask. If the argument concerns the division of the social product between those who work and those who own, the relevant fund is the net product, and a trend inflated by replacement charges is a trend misread. Here again a treatment decision, adopted silently in the construction of a widely quoted series, lends the headline a drama the underlying division does not possess.

There is a theoretical echo worth hearing. Marx's own reproduction schemes in the second volume of Capital are built around the replacement of consumed means of production, and the distinction between the portion of output that restores capital and the portion that constitutes revenue runs through the whole analysis. The value of the product divides, in his presentation, into the transferred value of used-up means of production and the new value added by living labour, and only the new value divides into wages and surplus value. A measure that folds replacement back into the fund being divided is, from that standpoint, not merely imprecise but category-confused, mixing the restoration of capital with the income of classes. The national accounts statisticians who prefer net measures for distributional questions are, without any allegiance to that tradition, honouring a distinction the tradition insisted on. Convergences of that kind, where careful accounting and careful theory independently refuse the same confusion, are among the more reassuring features of this contested field.

None of this is to say the net basis dissolves the finding. It trims it. The direction of the movement in compensation's portion survives the move from gross to net in the reconstructions that perform the move, and that survival matters for the assessment later in this article. The depreciation decision belongs in the family of decisions that change the size of the story. Housing, as the previous section showed, can change its subject. Keeping the two families distinct is part of reading the evidence well: a reader who lumps every adjustment together as mere statistical fog will miss that some adjustments qualify the headline while others redirect it, and the difference between qualifying and redirecting is the difference between a smaller claim and a different claim.

## Top labour incomes and the executives inside the wage bill

The fourth decision concerns the top of the numerator. Compensation of employees, as the accounts define it, makes no distinction between the pay of a cleaner and the pay of a chief executive. Both are employees. Both are compensated. The salaries, bonuses, and exercised stock options of the highest-paid executives in the corporate sector therefore sit inside the labour side of the ratio, swelling it, and they grew, over the decades under discussion, at a pace far beyond the growth of ordinary pay. The consequence for the measure is direct: a component of the numerator that behaves like property income in every economic respect that matters for distribution, rewarding position and scarcity and bargaining power at the summit of firms, is recorded as though it were the earnings of labour in general, and its rapid growth holds the measured share up even as the compensation of the great majority of employees falls behind.

Separate that component, and the picture sharpens considerably. Studies that exclude the highest labour incomes, or that examine the compensation share of the broad body of employees apart from the top slice, find a substantially larger decline for the remainder. The finding associated with research on the pay of top executives and on the upper tail of the earnings distribution, in the tradition of work by scholars of income concentration at the summit, thus cuts in the opposite direction from the housing result. Housing, separated out, weakens the headline. Executive pay, separated out, strengthens a different and more precise version of it: not that labour in the abstract lost ground, but that ordinary labour lost more ground than the aggregate shows, while a thin stratum of very highly paid employees, whose rewards are entangled with the profits of the firms they direct, gained at a pace that disguised the loss.

### How do the highest labour incomes bend the measure?

They bend it upward: executive compensation is recorded as labour income, so the speed of its growth inflates the numerator and flatters the measured share, and excluding the highest labour incomes reveals a substantially larger decline in the portion going to the broad body of employees than the headline figure reports.

Pause on the conceptual oddity, because it is instructive beyond its arithmetic. The national accounts classify by legal and contractual form: an employee is a person working under a contract of employment, and the chief executive holds such a contract, so the classification is internally impeccable. A Marxist classification asks a different question, about the function a person performs in the relations of production, and by that question the senior executive of a large corporation is an agent of capital, hired precisely to valorise the owners' investment, rewarded in forms tied to the surplus extracted, and separated from the ordinary employee by function even where contract law unites them. Marx himself noticed the emerging figure of the salaried manager and treated the wages of superintendence as a category requiring care, distinct both from the profit of enterprise and from ordinary wages. The accounts cannot make that distinction, because making it would require judgements about function that an accounting framework is designed to avoid. The distortion is therefore nobody's fault and everybody's problem. It sits in the join between two category systems, visible only to a reader who knows both systems exist.

The practical upshot for anyone citing the evidence is a discipline of disaggregation. Whenever the aggregate share is quoted in an argument about the condition of working people, the question to ask is what the share looks like with the summit of the earnings distribution held apart. An aggregate that can be propped up by the pay of the boardroom while the pay of the shop floor stagnates is not lying, exactly, but it is answering a question about all employees in an economy where the category employee has come to span a social distance too wide for any single average to describe honestly. The tradition that insists class is a relation rather than an income bracket should be the first to insist on the disaggregation, and the best Marxist commentary on this evidence does exactly that, treating the top-incomes correction as a refinement of the class picture rather than a technical footnote.

Notice, too, how this decision interacts with the first. The self-employment adjustment asks where the boundary of labour income lies at the edge of the firm; the top-incomes question asks where it lies at the summit of the firm. Both are boundary questions, and both reveal that labour income, as an accounting aggregate, is a coalition of unlike things. The cleaner, the contractor, the consultant with a laptop, and the executive with a bonus tied to the share price all feed the same numerator. Every argument that treats that numerator as the income of a class has silently assumed the coalition is a unity, and the assumption does more work in the argument than any figure quoted in its support.

## Coverage: which economy is being described?

The fifth decision concerns the perimeter. A labour share is always the share within some specified economy, and the specification involves choices about which sectors stand inside the boundary and which are left outside, choices that alter both level and trend. Three boundary questions recur in the literature and in the disputes around it. The informal sector, where work is unregistered and income poorly captured, strains the statistics of any economy in which it is large. The public sector presents a different problem: much of its output is not sold, so the accounts value it at cost, which means largely at the compensation of the public employees who produce it, and including a large bloc of activity whose property income is near zero by construction raises the measured share in a way that reflects the accounting rule more than any distributional fact. The financial sector presents the opposite strain, since a significant part of its measured output is imputed from interest margins rather than charged as fees, and its profits and bonuses have moved dramatically across the decades in question.

A researcher who confines the measure to the corporate sector, as some influential reconstructions do, is answering a question about the division of value added within companies, and will obtain a series that behaves differently from the whole-economy version, because the excluded territories, government, households as landlords, the self-employed in some treatments, have their own dynamics. A researcher who works with the whole economy obtains the broader picture at the price of including components whose movements are artefacts of valuation conventions. Neither is the true share. Each is the share of a specified territory under specified rules, and comparisons between them, of the kind that flourish in polemics where a critic answers a whole-economy figure with a corporate-sector figure and declares the finding refuted, are comparisons between different objects sharing a name.

### Why does the choice of sectors covered change the answer?

Because sectors divide their income very differently and are measured by different conventions: public output valued at cost carries almost no property income, financial output is partly imputed, and informal work escapes registration, so moving the boundary of the measure changes both the share's level and the forces driving its trend.

International comparison multiplies every difficulty in this section by the number of borders crossed. Economies differ in the size of their informal sectors, in the weight of public provision, in tenure patterns that feed the housing imputation, in the prevalence of self-employment and its composition, and in the statistical capacity of the offices compiling the accounts. A cross-country league table of shares, of the kind that invites a reader to rank nations by the fairness of their distributions, stacks all of those heterogeneities into a single column and invites a conclusion the column cannot support. The careful comparative literature, the reconstructions associated with scholars who assemble harmonised series across economies and decades, spends most of its effort exactly here, aligning definitions before daring to compare, and its findings about the breadth of the decline, spanning most advanced economies rather than one idiosyncratic case, carry weight precisely because that alignment work was done.

Composition returns at this level too, wearing a larger coat. Even with definitions harmonised, an economy whose industrial structure shifts away from sectors with high compensation shares toward sectors with low ones will register a falling aggregate, and the decomposition literature distinguishes that between-sector movement from the within-sector movement in which the division inside each industry itself changes. The two components tell different stories. A within-sector fall points toward changes in bargaining, technology, or market power inside industries. A between-sector fall points toward structural transformation of the economy's shape. Both appeared in the decompositions of the period under discussion, in proportions that vary by economy and by dataset, which is one more reason the aggregate, quoted bare, underdetermines the explanation anyone wishes to hang on it.

The discipline this section imposes can be stated as a rule of quotation. No labour share figure should ever travel without its passport: the territory covered, the treatment of self-employment, the gross or net basis, the handling of housing, and the span of years. A figure that arrives without those particulars is not evidence yet. It is a rumour with a decimal point, and the decimal point, in this literature, is doing persuasive work that the underlying construction may or may not justify. Readers trained by this article to demand the passport will find that a surprising proportion of public argument on the subject dissolves at the border, not because the finding is false, but because the figures being fought over were never measurements of the same thing.

## The treatment decisions table

Everything so far can be gathered into a single instrument, and gathering it is worthwhile because an instrument gets used while a sermon gets admired and forgotten. The treatment decisions table sets each decision in a row, states the choice that was available to whoever built a given series, and records what that choice does to the level of the share, what it does to the trend, and which of the claims in circulation it supports or undermines. A reader confronting any published rendering of the finding can walk down the rows, establish which choices that version embodies, and know within minutes how much of its headline is substance and how much is scaffolding. Read the table column by column and a pattern emerges that no single row reveals. The level of the share is almost entirely a creature of choices: shift several decisions at once and the starting point of the series wanders across a wide band, which is why arguments that fasten on the level at a date, declaring that labour receives this or that portion and calling the portion scandalous or satisfactory, are the weakest arguments in the whole debate. The trend is hardier, surviving most single changes of treatment, which is why the defensible core of the finding is a statement about direction over long spans. And the identity of the phenomenon, what the movement is a movement in, is the most fragile element of all, because the housing row and the top-incomes row between them can transform the subject of the sentence while leaving its grammar untouched. A fall in the share can be, in the main, a rise in the price of shelter, or a fall in ordinary compensation masked by executive pay, or a genuine broad shift within production, and the aggregate alone will never say which.

### In what order does the treatment decisions table arrange its choices?

The table follows the logic of the accounts themselves, beginning where the numerator and denominator are first entangled in mixed income, proceeding through the property income swollen by housing and the total enlarged by depreciation, turning then to the summit hidden inside compensation, and closing with the perimeter that decides which economy is under description.

That ordering is a small argument in itself. Measurement debates are often conducted as though every caveat were equal, a fog of qualifications through which the determined partisan walks toward a conclusion chosen in advance. The table refuses the fog. Decisions that merely rescale the result are separated from decisions that redirect it, and the redirecting decisions, housing above all, are placed where a reader meets them early rather than in a methodological appendix where findings go to be forgotten. Anyone is free to dispute the judgements embodied in the final column. What the table makes difficult is the pretence that no judgements were involved, and that pretence, more than any single mistaken figure, is the true adversary this article is written against.

Keep the table in mind through the sections that follow, because the assessment of robustness and the confrontation with the strongest objection are both, in effect, readings of it. Robustness will mean that the direction of the trend persists across the rows under their reasonable settings. The strongest objection will be the housing row pressed at full strength, as its author and his discussants pressed it. And the distinction between the share and the rate of exploitation, waiting beyond those sections, can be introduced already as the observation that no setting of any row in this table, however ingenious, converts a ratio of money incomes into one of labour time. The table governs everything the measure can say. The exploitation question concerns something the measure was never built to say at all.

## What survives every reasonable treatment

After the anatomy, the assessment, and the assessment should be given without flinching in either direction. Across the range of treatments that competent researchers defend, with mixed income imputed by any of the standard methods, with the share computed on a net basis, with housing separated or retained, with the corporate sector isolated or the whole economy taken, a decline in the portion of income going to compensation, spread across the advanced world and running over the decades since the postwar settlement's closing years, remains standing. The reconstructions that disagree about almost everything else, about levels, about steepness, about the weight of composition effects, agree about that. Loukas Karabarbounis and Brent Neiman, assembling evidence across a wide panel of economies and industries, found the decline to be a broad international phenomenon rather than a peculiarity of one country's statistics, and their explanation in terms of the falling price of investment goods relative to consumption goods set the terms for much subsequent debate. Work in that comparative tradition, and work critical of it, share the premise that there is a phenomenon to explain.

That breadth is the finding's real strength, and it is worth being precise about why. A result that appears in one dataset might be a property of the dataset. A result that appears under harmonised definitions across economies with different statistical offices, different tenure patterns, different weights of self-employment, and different industrial structures has passed a sterner test, because the artefacts that could manufacture it would have to conspire internationally. Artefacts do conspire, occasionally, through shared conventions: the imputed rent convention is common to the international accounting standard, so the housing component of the movement is genuinely a common artefact-or-phenomenon across borders, which is exactly why the decomposition mattered. But the within-industry declines found in the comparative work, and the declines visible on net measures with housing held apart, are not one convention's shadow. They are the residue that survives the audit, and the residue is the finding.

### Which parts of the finding survive every reasonable treatment?

The direction of the movement over long spans: compensation's portion fell across most advanced economies under every defensible construction of the measure, while the size of the fall, its level at any date, and the identity of what drove it vary considerably with the choices embodied in each particular series.

Magnitude, by contrast, is not robust, and intellectual honesty requires saying so as plainly as the direction was affirmed. Between a gross whole-economy series with mixed income assigned one way and a net corporate series with housing separated and mixed income assigned another, the measured fall over a comparable span can differ by enough to support noticeably different rhetoric, from a dramatic expropriation narrative to a modest drift narrative. Both rhetorics would be quoting real constructions. Neither would be quoting the evidence, because the evidence is the whole family of constructions together with an account of why they differ. This is the sense in which the decline is robust and its size is contested, a combination that public discussion finds hard to hold, since headlines prefer a single magnitude and partisans prefer the extreme of the family that suits them. The scholarly literature, at its best, holds the combination comfortably, and the reader who learns to hold it has learned the central skill this article exists to teach.

A second robust feature deserves mention, quieter than the first. The decline is not a story of a single break or a single episode. In the harmonised series it unfolds over decades, with accelerations and pauses that differ by economy, and it continues through changes of government, of monetary regime, and of industrial fashion. Long, grinding movements of that character are the kind that structural explanations are for: shifts in the relative price of capital goods, in the organisation of production across borders, in the bargaining position of labour as union coverage thinned and as production became more mobile, in the market power of firms whose scale grew with intangible assets. The literature's explanatory contest among those candidates is vigorous and unresolved, and this article takes no side in it, because the measurement questions that are its subject arise before explanation begins. But the temporal character of the finding, its refusal to be an episode, is itself evidence about the kind of phenomenon under discussion, and it survives every treatment in the table.

It is equally part of the assessment to mark what robustness does not cover. The finding concerns shares, not levels of well-being: a falling portion of a growing total can accompany rising real compensation, and did accompany it for stretches of the period in many economies, a fact that neither refutes the distributional claim nor licenses ignoring it. The finding concerns recorded income, not the whole of economic advantage: it says nothing directly about wealth, about security, about the intensity or autonomy of work, or about the public services that form part of the social wage. And the finding, being an aggregate, conceals divergent fortunes within the compensation heading itself, as the top-incomes discussion showed. Robustness is a property of a specific claim, and the claim that survives is specific indeed: the division of recorded income shifted away from compensation, broadly, persistently, and across most of the advanced world. Everything beyond that sentence must be argued, not quoted.

## The strongest objection: shelter wearing the clothes of capital

Fair dealing requires the objection to the headline to be stated at the strength its proponents gave it, not at the strength convenient for rebuttal. Here it is, in the form the housing decomposition supports. The celebrated rise in capital's share, the mirror image of labour's fall, was presented to the public as evidence that the returns to owning the means of production had swollen at the expense of those who work. Decompose that rise, and a substantial part of it resolves into housing: into the imputed rents of owner-occupiers and the real rents of landlords, into the scarcity value of land in successful cities and the financial conditions that capitalised that scarcity into dwelling prices. The return to capital engaged in production, the profit of enterprise in anything like the classical sense, behaved far more modestly. The finding most often deployed to demonstrate a shift in the capital and labour relation therefore largely records, on this objection, the rising cost of shelter: a real and serious phenomenon, but a phenomenon of land, planning, and housing finance, not of the wage bargain or the labour process. A tradition that built its commentary on the undecomposed aggregate was, without knowing it, writing about the housing market while believing it was writing about the factory.

That objection lands, and this article concedes its force before answering it, because a verdict that cannot concede is a verdict that cannot be trusted. The headline finding is substantially weakened by the housing result. The weakening is not a marginal qualification to be absorbed in a footnote; it removes a large component of the most quoted version of the trend from the evidentiary base of the claim that production relations shifted against labour. Marxist commentary engaged the decomposition late and thinly, preferring the aggregate that flattered its prior narrative, and that preference is a small case study in how a tradition's relationship to evidence decays: the number that agrees is quoted for years, the decomposition that complicates is acknowledged in a sentence and then ignored in every subsequent use. The discipline demanded by the brief for this article, to state the housing result plainly precisely because it weakens a claim the tradition relies on, is the correct discipline, and it is applied here without varnish.

### How contested is the decline in the labour share?

The direction is broadly agreed across the research literature, but the magnitude is genuinely contested, the contribution of housing is disputed in its implications, and the decomposition of the movement into changes within industries against changes in the economy's composition remains an open argument among the researchers who build the series.

Now the answer, which has two parts, corresponding to what remains after housing is removed and what the top-incomes result adds from the other side. What remains, first, is not nothing. On the constructions that separate housing, the non-housing portion of property income still rose in a number of the economies studied, and compensation's portion within industries still fell in the comparative decompositions; the phenomenon shrinks under the housing correction but does not vanish, and the objection at full strength establishes a re-description of much of the trend rather than a dissolution of all of it. The verdict defeater specified for this article is instructive here: the judgement of robustness would fall if a defensible treatment eliminated the decline throughout the advanced world, and the housing treatment, which no one can dismiss as indefensible, comes near that standard for one component among many without reaching it for the whole. An objection that approaches a defeater without reaching it leaves a weakened verdict standing, and weakened is the honest adjective for the finding's condition.

The top-incomes result, second, adds a redistribution of confidence within the claim. Suppose the aggregate fall is decomposed and a substantial part is reassigned to shelter. The part that remains, concerning compensation itself, turns out on disaggregation to understate the deterioration for the broad body of employees, because the summit of the pay distribution was pulling the average upward throughout. The tradition's underlying intuition, that the period was hard on ordinary labour's relative position, therefore survives the audit in a revised form: less supported by the aggregate than its partisans believed, more supported by the disaggregated compensation evidence than the aggregate ever showed. The two corrections, housing and top incomes, are usually cited by opposite camps, which is a pity, because they belong in the same sentence. The measure, properly read, says that shelter claimed more of the social product and that ordinary employees claimed less of what employers paid out, while returns on productive capital moved less dramatically than either headline suggested. That sentence is less stirring than the one it replaces. It has the compensating virtue of being defensible.

There is a final turn to the objection that its proponents sometimes miss. If a large part of the shift in recorded income shares is a shift toward rent on land and dwellings, the correct response for a Marxist analysis is not embarrassment but recognition, since the category of rent, and the political economy of landed property, are native territory for the tradition in a way that the finer points of national accounting are not. The [financialisation marxist theory](/2020/12/01/financialisation-marxist-theory/) discussion in this series touches the same territory from the side of credit and asset prices. A distributional era in which shelter devours income shares is an era in which the price of access to place, to the city, to proximity to work, becomes a central mechanism by which the product is divided, and analysing that mechanism requires the theory of rent, the analysis of planning and land ownership, and the study of housing finance. The evidence, corrected, points the tradition toward work it is equipped to do. Evidence that redirects a research programme has not refuted the programme; it has done the programme a service its cheerleaders were failing to do.

## The distinction the whole article turns on: shares are not exploitation

Everything before this section concerned what the measure says. This section concerns what it does not say, and the negative claim is the one this article exists to establish, so it will be made slowly and from several directions. The labour share is not the rate of exploitation. They are different quantities, measured in different units, inside different category systems, answering different questions, and they coincide only under assumptions that do not hold in any actual economy. The inference from a falling share to a rising rate of exploitation, drawn constantly in speeches, pamphlets, and commentary that should know better, is an inference between two objects that share a rhetorical silhouette and nothing else. Establishing that is not pedantry. It is the difference between an evidence-based Marxism and a Marxism that borrows the prestige of statistics it has not understood.

Start with the units, because units are unforgiving. The rate of surplus value, in Marx's construction, divides the working day into portions of time. Necessary labour is the portion in which the worker produces the equivalent of the wage, the value of labour power. Surplus labour is the portion during which the worker produces value appropriated without equivalent. The ratio of the second portion to the first is the rate of exploitation, a pure relation between stretches of labour time, defined before any question of prices arises and intended to hold, in the theory's own terms, underneath the price forms in which it appears. The labour share divides money by money: compensation recorded in currency against income recorded in currency, both at the prices actually ruling. A ratio of times and a ratio of money sums can move together, can move apart, can even move in opposite directions under changes in relative prices, in the composition of output, or in the categories counted. Nothing in arithmetic obliges them to correspond, and the theory itself, which insists that prices systematically diverge from values, is the last theory that should expect them to.

### Does the labour share measure exploitation?

no. The rate of surplus value is a ratio of labour times; the labour share is a ratio of money incomes in national accounts, which do not distinguish productive from unproductive labour and record executive pay as wages. A falling labour share is consistent with rising exploitation but does not measure it.

Consider the category mismatches one by one, since each is a place where the two systems part company. The accounts make no distinction between productive and unproductive labour, a distinction the reader will find treated in this series' discussion of [measuring rate of exploitation](/2011/03/01/measuring-rate-of-exploitation/), to which the conceptual question properly belongs. Labour employed in circulation, in selling, in finance, in much of administration, counts in the accounts as producing output and receiving compensation exactly as labour on a production line does, while in the Marxist classification such labour, however necessary, does not produce surplus value and is paid out of it. The numerator and denominator of the accounting ratio therefore both contain masses of activity that the theoretical ratio excludes or relocates, and the two ratios are counting differently populated worlds.

The treatment of the state drives a second wedge. Government activity enters the accounts largely at cost, its employees' compensation swelling the labour side, while in the Marxist scheme the state's labour is sustained from revenue, from taxes drawn on wages and surplus value alike, and stands in a different relation to the production of surplus value than labour hired by capital. Executive compensation drives a third wedge, already examined: the accounts place it inside labour income, the theory assigns the function it rewards to the side of capital. Valuation drives a fourth: the accounts record output at market prices, including the markups of monopoly and the rents of scarcity, while the theoretical ratio is struck in labour time, so any change in the price structure, a rise in the relative price of housing services being the period's great example, moves the accounting ratio while leaving the time ratio untouched. Four wedges, each sufficient on its own to separate the quantities, all operating at once in every published figure.

A concrete divergence may fix the point better than the catalogue. Imagine an economy in which productivity in wage goods rises sharply, cheapening the worker's consumption basket, so that labour power's value falls while the rate of exploitation rises, and money wages, defended by strong bargaining, keep pace with money output per head and compensation's share of recorded income stays exactly where it was. The theoretical quantity has moved; the accounting quantity has not stirred. Now imagine the reverse construction, in which imputed rents surge as dwelling values climb, pressing compensation's share downward through pure accounting arithmetic, while nothing changes in any workplace, no day lengthens, no effort intensifies, no wage good cheapens. The accounting quantity has moved; the theoretical quantity has not stirred. Two scenarios, each internally coherent, in which the quantities travel independently. Whatever relation holds between them in the actual historical record is an empirical association to be investigated, not an identity to be assumed, and commentary that slides from one to the other in a single sentence has committed a category error with a statistic attached.

## Why the error is so common, and what it costs

If the distinction is as clear as the previous section argues, the persistence of the confusion requires explanation, and the explanation is not flattering to any of the parties involved. The two quantities are shaped alike as stories. Both describe a division between labour and its other, both can be said to worsen for labour, and a speaker reaching for the emotional register of exploitation finds the accounting share conveniently at hand, dressed in the authority of official statistics, while the theoretical quantity, requiring labour values that no statistical office publishes, remains abstract and unquotable. The share is therefore borrowed as the concept's costume. Journalists borrow it because it is available and vivid. Campaigners borrow it because it converts a theoretical claim into a headline. And Marxist writers borrow it, most culpably, because it appears to give their central category an empirical body, sparing them the laborious work of constructing that body properly.

The costs of the borrowing are real and they accumulate. The first cost is vulnerability: a claim staked on the identity of the two quantities collapses when an informed critic separates them, and the collapse discredits, in the audience's mind, not merely the sloppy inference but the underlying theory, which never made the claim being refuted. The tradition thus finds its strongest concept repeatedly shot down in a form it never held, a self-inflicted wound renewed with every headline cycle. The second cost is blindness: treating the share as a reading of exploitation forecloses the genuinely interesting questions about how the two actually relate, whether they moved together in the period, through which mechanisms, with what lags and mediations. Those questions have answers, and some of them are favourable to the tradition's expectations, but they can only be reached by work the shortcut renders unnecessary and therefore undone. The third cost is internal: a movement trained to accept a proxy as the thing itself loses the habit of precision about its own categories, and the loss shows elsewhere, in profit rate debates where price magnitudes are again handled as though they were value magnitudes, a confusion examined in the [falling rate of profit debate](/2013/11/15/falling-rate-of-profit-debate/).

### Why do commentators keep treating the share as exploitation?

Because the two quantities share a narrative shape, both describing a division that can worsen for labour, while only one of them is published by statistical offices in quotable form; the published ratio is borrowed as a costume for the unpublished concept, and the borrowing is rarely examined by audiences who meet it in headlines.

There is a subtler version of the error that survives even in careful writing, and it deserves naming because it catches authors who would never commit the crude version. This is the use of the share as evidence for exploitation with the inferential steps left implicit: the share fell, productivity rose, therefore the fruits of productivity were appropriated, therefore exploitation intensified. Each step in that chain is arguable, and some version of the chain may even be defensible for particular economies and spans, but the chain is an argument, with premises about prices, about the composition of compensation, about which labour is productive, and about the translation between values and prices, and an argument owes its reader its premises. Presented with the steps suppressed, the conclusion wears the false simplicity of a measurement. The remedy is not to abandon the chain but to walk it in the open, which is what the translation literature, taken up next, attempts at full length and with its assumptions on the table.

It should also be said, in fairness to the borrowers, that the concept of exploitation has a moral and political life beyond its technical definition, and that in ordinary language a falling portion of income flowing to those who work does testify to something the word exploitation gestures at in its loose sense: a shift in the balance of reward between contribution and ownership. Loose senses have their place in rhetoric. The failure occurs when the loose sense and the technical sense are traded against each other, when the moral force of the technical term is claimed for a statistic that measures something else, or when a critic of the statistic is answered as though they had denied the moral claim. Keeping the registers separate protects both. The distributional fact can be deplored on its own terms, as a fact about recorded incomes. The theoretical claim can be advanced on its own terms, as a claim about labour time and its division. Confusing them weakens each, and the weakness is entirely avoidable, which is what makes its persistence a scandal rather than a misfortune.

## Translating national accounts into Marxist categories

Between the two quantities stands a bridge that can be built, at a price. The price is an explicit translation: a set of stated rules by which the headings of the national accounts are dismantled and reassembled into the categories of Marxist theory, so that magnitudes resembling variable capital, surplus value, and the rate of exploitation can be estimated from published statistics. Building that bridge is a serious scholarly undertaking, and its seriousness consists precisely in the explicitness the shortcut avoids. Every heading must be interrogated. Which industries employ productive labour, and which employ labour that circulates, guards, or administers? How are the earnings of the self-employed to be divided when the theory, like the accounts, finds them straddling the boundary? What is to be done with the compensation of supervisory and executive staff, with the income of the state sector, with taxes and subsidies that intervene between value produced and income recorded? Each answer is a judgement, each judgement can be contested, and the resulting estimates wear their judgements openly, which is the whole point.

The standard attempt, the one any serious use of this evidence must reckon with or replace with an equivalent, is the study by Anwar Shaikh and E. Ahmet Tonak, Measuring the Wealth of Nations, which sets out the translation in full: a reconstruction of the accounts of an advanced economy into Marxist categories, category by category, with the mapping rules stated and defended, and with estimates of the theoretical magnitudes computed on that basis. Whatever a reader thinks of its particular judgements, and they have been debated within the tradition at length, the work establishes the form an honest bridge must take. It does not point at a published ratio and declare it exploitation. It rebuilds the ratio's ingredients until they answer to the theory's definitions, and it accepts the labour, and the contestability, that rebuilding involves. The contrast with headline practice could not be sharper, and the existence of the bridge refutes the lazy defence that the translation cannot be done. It can be done. It simply cannot be done for free, in a sentence, by quotation.

### By what steps are the accounts rebuilt into Marxist categories?

By dismantling the accounting headings and reassembling them under stated rules: separating productive from unproductive labour, relocating the state sector and executive pay, dividing mixed income on theoretical grounds, and restating the totals so that magnitudes answering to variable capital and surplus value can be estimated openly and contestably.

What the translation exercises characteristically find, when performed with care, is worth reporting because it complicates the politics in a productive direction. Estimates of the rate of surplus value constructed on Marxist categories have, in several such exercises, risen over the postwar decades by more than the accounting share's fall would suggest, driven by the growth of unproductive employment, which swells the mass of labour sustained out of surplus value even as the compensation share of recorded income drifts down. The two quantities, in other words, not only can diverge in thought experiments; in the translated record they tell related but distinguishable stories, with the theoretical magnitude carrying information about the changing structure of employment that the accounting ratio flattens away. A tradition in possession of that finding holds a stronger and more interesting claim than the headline it usually settles for. It can say that the period saw both a distributional shift in recorded incomes and, on its own categories rigorously applied, an intensification of exploitation, with the growth of circulation and administrative labour as part of the mechanism. That is a thesis. It can be argued, tested against alternative translations, and refined. The borrowed headline could only ever be repeated.

The translation problem also disciplines the reverse journey. A researcher who has built Marxist magnitudes from the accounts and wishes to compare them with the published share must state which accounting conventions the comparison inherits, since the translated series and the published series now differ by exactly the rules the translation imposed. Comparisons that skip that statement smuggle the rules in unexamined, and the whole apparatus of this article, the treatment table applied to theory's own constructions, becomes necessary again. There is no vantage point outside construction from which quantities simply agree. There are only constructions whose rules are stated and constructions whose rules are hidden, and scholarship is the practice of preferring the first kind while remaining able to read the second.

For the student meeting this material in an examination or a seminar, the practical maxim is compact. Whenever a quantity from the national accounts is offered as a Marxist magnitude, ask for the translation. If none is supplied, the quantity is an accounting ratio with a theoretical label taped over it, and it should be discussed as an accounting ratio, which is to say discussed well, on the terms this article has laid out, rather than badly, on terms it cannot meet. The maxim protects the student in both directions: against dismissing the accounting evidence, which is real evidence about recorded incomes, and against crediting it with a meaning its construction cannot carry. The wider methodological setting for that maxim, the pattern of measurement disputes across this field, is the subject of [testing marxist economics](/2016/08/01/testing-marxist-economics/), and the hub for the cluster of questions about contemporary capitalism is [marxism and modern capitalism](/2020/11/01/marxism-and-modern-capitalism/).

## The measurement dominance pattern, ninth instance

Step back from the particulars and a pattern comes into view that this series has now encountered often enough to name as a general finding. Again and again, the disputes that appear to concern the fate of capitalism turn out, on inspection, to be governed by questions of measurement: by how a quantity is constructed, which categories it gathers, which conventions it inherits. The pattern appeared in value theory, where the transformation of values into prices set the terms of a century of argument. It appeared in attempts to measure the rate of exploitation, where the translation problem decided what the statistics could say. It appeared in the profit rate debates, where the valuation of the capital stock, at historical cost or replacement cost, moved the conclusion. It appeared in trade statistics, where the attribution of value added across borders redrew the map of who produces what. It appeared in decoupling accounting, where the boundary drawn around emissions reassigned responsibility between nations. It appeared in assessments of socialist growth, where the price weights chosen for aggregation decided whether an economy had grown impressively or modestly. And it appears here, in factor shares, where five treatment decisions decide what a famous trend is a trend in. This article is the ninth instance of the measurement dominance pattern in the series, and the accumulation matters: nine instances across such different territories are not a run of bad luck but a structural feature of quantitative argument in this field.

Why should measurement dominate so reliably? Because the quantities at issue are not found in nature with their definitions attached. Value, surplus value, the capital stock, the labour share: each is a theoretical object before it is a statistical one, and the passage from theory to statistic passes through conventions that are contestable at every joint. Where the object is a physical magnitude, measurement disputes are real but bounded, since the object pushes back. Where the object is a category of political economy, the pushback is weaker and the conventions carry more of the result, so that the scholarly action migrates, predictably, to the conventions themselves. Researchers who understand this spend their ingenuity on construction and decomposition, and produce the findings, like the housing decomposition, that actually change what a field believes. Commentators who miss it spend their ingenuity on rhetoric hung upon aggregates, and produce the fog.

### Why does measurement keep deciding the argument in this field?

Because the central quantities are theoretical objects that reach statistics only through contestable conventions of categorisation and valuation, so the construction of a measure carries an unusual share of its result, and disputes that present themselves as arguments about the world resolve, on inspection, into arguments about the instrument.

The pattern carries a practical moral for every audience of this article. For the researcher, it ranks the skills: mastery of how the accounts are built outranks fluency in quoting what they say. For the journalist, it supplies the question that unlocks any distributional story, which is not whether the number is large but how the number is made. For the student, it converts a mass of apparently conflicting authorities into an intelligible scene, since authorities who appear to disagree about the economy often turn out to disagree about an imputation, and locating the imputation is most of the work of adjudicating between them. For the Marxist tradition specifically, the pattern is almost a gift, because a theory that has always insisted that categories are historical and constructed should be the theory best prepared for a world in which measurement is construction. The tradition's embarrassments in this literature have come when it forgot its own insight and treated the statistical office's categories as nature. Its successes have come when it remembered, and built its translations in the open.

There is a temptation, having named the pattern, to slide into a lazy relativism in which every measure is merely a construction and no finding is better than another. Everything argued in this article stands against that slide. Constructions are better and worse: better when their rules are stated, when their sensitivity to the rules is explored, when their decompositions are performed, and when their claims are pitched at the level the construction can support. The labour share finding, pitched at the level of direction over long spans, is a good finding. Pitched at the level of a precise magnitude of expropriation, it is an overclaim. The difference lies not in the world but in the discipline of the claimant, and discipline, unlike the world, is within the claimant's control.

## How to read any published claim about the share

The preceding sections assemble into a procedure, and a procedure is the right form for the article's practical legacy, since the reader will meet this evidence again in the wild, stripped of everything taught here. When a labour share claim appears, in a newspaper column, a party pamphlet, a minister's speech, or a scholarly abstract, the trained reader asks a short sequence of questions before believing, repeating, or rebutting it. Which economies does the claim cover, and over which span of decades? Is the measure gross or net of capital consumption? How was the income of the self-employed divided? Is housing inside the property income being described, and if the claim concerns the returns to capital in production, has housing been separated? Are the highest labour incomes inside the compensation being celebrated or mourned? Which sectors stand within the boundary? A claim that answers those questions can be weighed. A claim that cannot answer them has not yet earned a verdict, and the most useful response to it is neither assent nor denial but a request for its construction.

### What must a comparison of labour shares state to mean anything?

It must state the economies compared, the decades spanned, whether the measure is gross or net, and how self-employment income was treated, since without those particulars the two sides of the comparison may be different objects sharing a name, and most published comparisons omit at least two of the particulars required.

The procedure sounds laborious, and against the frictionless consumption of headlines it is, but its cost falls with practice until the questions fire automatically, the way a musician's ear catches a wrong note without deliberation. What the procedure purchases is immunity to a whole genus of manipulation, the genus that works not by fabricating numbers but by selecting constructions: choosing the gross basis when drama is wanted, the whole economy when breadth is wanted, the series whose self-employment rule flatters the thesis, and quoting the result as the finding. Selection among real constructions is the sophisticated form of statistical dishonesty, because every figure produced can be defended as genuine, and only a reader who asks after the road not taken can see the selection happening. The treatment decisions table exists to make that seeing quick.

A word, too, about the opposite failure, the reader so schooled in construction that every figure is waved away as convention and nothing is ever believed. That posture feels like rigour and functions like ignorance, since it cannot distinguish the finding that survives its audit from the claim that collapses under it, and it abandons the field to those with no scruples at all. The procedure taught here ends in judgement, not in suspension: some claims pass, and the labour share's directional core passes, and saying so with the qualifications attached is a stronger speech act than either credulity or refusal. Evidence in political economy is rarely a verdict delivered by nature. It is a case built by craftsmen, and the reader's task is the juror's task, to weigh the building, not to demand a different kind of object and sulk when none arrives.

### What kind of result would defeat the robustness judgement?

A treatment of the measure that is defensible on its own terms, adopted consistently, and shown to eliminate the decline in most advanced economies would overturn the judgement of robustness; the housing correction comes near that standard for a single component of the movement without achieving it for the whole.

That defeater deserves a paragraph of its own, because stating what would change a verdict is the mark that distinguishes a verdict from a loyalty. The robustness judgement in this article is not a commitment to defend the decline against all comers. It is a summary of the present state of the constructions: no defensible treatment, applied consistently, has yet made the broad decline disappear. Should one appear, should a reconstruction with stated and defensible rules show compensation's portion holding across the advanced economies once housing, depreciation, and mixed income are handled as its author argues they should be, the judgement recorded here falls, and this article's own procedure obliges its readers to let it fall. The housing result is honoured in these pages partly because it travelled some distance down exactly that road. Science, including the social science of income shares, advances by defeatable claims surviving serious attempts to defeat them, and a tradition that cannot state its defeaters has quietly left science for apologetics.

## Why the share fell: the explanatory contest the measure cannot settle

Measurement discipline tells the reader what happened to a constructed quantity. It cannot tell the reader why, and the why has its own crowded literature whose candidates deserve a hearing, partly because the candidates differ in what they imply for politics and partly because each candidate interacts differently with the treatment decisions. A cause that operates through the price of capital goods will show itself in gross and net series alike. A cause that operates through the composition of industry will appear in between-sector decompositions. A cause that is really the housing phenomenon wearing an explanatory costume will dissolve when housing is separated. Sorting the explanations against the constructions is therefore not an optional refinement; it is how a reader discovers which explanations are explanations of the finding and which are explanations of an artefact.

The candidate with the widest influence in the macroeconomic literature is the relative price story associated with Karabarbounis and Neiman: equipment, machinery, and computing power became steadily cheaper relative to other goods, so firms substituted capital for labour wherever the elasticity of substitution allowed, and the portion of income accruing to compensation fell as the capital stock deepened. The story has the elegance of a single mechanism operating everywhere at once, which matches the international breadth of the decline, and it has the weakness of depending on an elasticity that other researchers estimate differently and that may vary across industries in ways the aggregate conceals. Alongside it stands the globalisation family of explanations, in which the integration of vast new labour forces into the world economy, and the relocation or credible threat of relocation of production, weakened the bargaining position of labour in the advanced economies while raising the share of value added recorded where capital is booked. A third family emphasises institutions: thinning union coverage, eroded minimum wage floors in some economies, and legal changes that shifted risk and reward within the employment relation. A fourth emphasises market power, pointing to rising markups and to the concentration of activity in highly profitable firms whose internal division of income differs from the rest.

### Has labour lost out because machines became cheaper?

That is one influential explanation, in which falling prices for equipment and computing encouraged firms to substitute capital for workers and pressed compensation's portion downward, but it competes with accounts centred on global integration, weakened bargaining institutions, and the growing market power of the most profitable firms.

Notice what the measurement sections contribute to adjudicating among these. The bargaining story predicts a within-industry decline concentrated where institutions weakened most, and the decompositions do find substantial within-industry movement, though its geography does not line up as neatly with institutional change as the story's partisans would wish. The substitution story predicts movement wherever investment goods cheapened, including in economies whose labour institutions held firm, and the breadth of the decline lends it support. The market power story directs attention to the distribution among firms, and research in that vein, associated with the study of so-called superstar firms whose rise reshapes industry aggregates, finds that much of the aggregate movement reflects reallocation toward firms with low compensation shares rather than a uniform fall inside every firm, a finding that rhymes with the composition warnings issued earlier in this article. The housing decomposition cuts across all of them by removing a slice of the phenomenon from the jurisdiction of production-side explanation altogether. No single candidate emerges unbloodied, and the honest summary is that the decline is overdetermined in the literature and underdetermined by the aggregate, which is precisely the condition in which measurement discipline earns its keep.

There is a Marxist register in which several of these candidates can be restated, and restating them is more illuminating than denouncing them as apologetics. Substitution of machinery for labour in response to relative prices is, in the tradition's vocabulary, a rise in the organic composition of capital driven by the competitive pursuit of relative surplus value, and the tradition predicted the pressure on the wage share from that quarter long before the econometrics arrived. The weakening of bargaining institutions is, in that vocabulary, a movement in the balance of class forces, registered in law and custom before it is registered in income shares. Global integration is the formation of a world market in labour power with a vastly enlarged reserve army standing behind every national wage bargain. Translation between registers is possible here in a way it was not for the exploitation identity, because these are causal narratives rather than quantities, and narratives can be compared for the mechanisms they posit. Where the narratives posit the same mechanism, the apparent quarrel between traditions is a quarrel of vocabularies. Where they posit different mechanisms, evidence about composition, geography, and timing can genuinely discriminate, and the discriminating evidence is, once again, a matter of construction carefully audited.

## Wages, productivity, and the divergence that prompts the question

Behind most lay encounters with the labour share stands a simpler and more visceral observation: pay and productivity, which rose together in the postwar decades in the public imagination, parted company, with output per worker continuing to climb while the typical worker's compensation crawled. The parting of those two lines on a chart is the emotional origin of most labour share argument, and the two phenomena are related without being identical, a distinction worth drawing carefully because the chart and the share are constantly substituted for one another in debate. The share compares compensation with income across the whole economy. The chart compares the compensation of a typical worker with the average product of labour. Between the typical and the average, between compensation and pay, and between the price index used to deflate output and the index used to deflate wages, lie enough wedges to fill a seminar, and each wedge has its own literature.

### Why do wages not keep up with productivity?

Partly because the two series are deflated by different price indexes, partly because average compensation includes the rapidly rising pay of the highest earners while typical pay does not, partly because a growing slice of output goes to replacing worn-out capital, and partly because the division of net income itself shifted away from compensation.

Walk the wedges in order. Output per hour is usually deflated by a producer price index, wages by a consumer price index, and when consumer prices rise faster than producer prices, as they do when housing and services dominate consumption while goods dominate production, real wages measured against consumer prices lag productivity measured against producer prices without any distributional change at all. Compensation includes employer pension and insurance contributions, which grew as a share of the package, so pay in hand lags total compensation even where the total keeps pace. The average is dragged upward by the summit of the distribution, so the typical worker's experience diverges from the mean even in years when the mean behaves. Depreciation claims its growing slice before income exists to divide. And after every wedge has taken its portion, a residual remains in many reconstructions, the genuine distributional shift that the labour share literature studies, which means the chart and the ratio are cousins: overlapping phenomena with shared causes, neither reducible to the other, each liable to be misquoted as the other by speakers who find one of them more convenient.

The Marxist reader will recognise an old friend inside this thicket. The distinction between what labour produces and what labour receives is the theory's home ground, and the cheapening of wage goods through productivity growth is precisely the mechanism of relative surplus value, in which the value of labour power falls even as the worker's material consumption rises. That mechanism generates a wedge between productivity and the value of the wage from inside the theory, without any assistance from price indexes, and it warns against reading the chart naively in either direction: a rising real wage is compatible with exploitation rising in rate, and a stagnant real wage is compatible with several different stories about the division of the working day. The chart, like the share, is evidence that requires a theory to interpret and a construction to audit. What it is not is a photograph of exploitation, and the speakers who brandish it as one repeat, in a new medium, the error this article has pursued through the national accounts.

## Within industries and between them: where the movement lives

An aggregate can fall because every part of it falls, or because the weights among its parts shift, and the difference is not a technicality but the location of the phenomenon itself. The decomposition literature therefore splits the movement in compensation's portion into a within component, the average of changes inside industries weighted by their size, and a between component, the effect of activity migrating from industries with high compensation shares toward industries with low ones. The split has been performed for individual economies and for panels of them, in the work of researchers who reconstruct industry accounts across decades, and its results discipline the explanatory contest of the previous section, since a candidate explanation that predicts movement in the wrong component has been caught explaining something that did not happen.

The broad picture those decompositions give is mixed in an instructive way. Within-industry decline is real and widespread: in many economies the division of value added shifted against compensation inside manufacturing, inside services, inside industry after industry, which rules out any account in which the aggregate movement is pure compositional illusion. At the same time the between component is far from negligible, and in some economies and spans it rivals the within component, as employment and output migrated toward sectors, and within sectors toward firms, whose recorded compensation shares were lower. Research on the firm dimension, the superstar firm literature associated with David Autor and co-authors, sharpened the point by showing reallocation toward highly productive, highly profitable firms with low labour shares doing heavy work in the aggregate for the economy they studied. The phenomenon, in other words, lives in both places at once, inside the typical industry's division and in the shifting shape of the economy, and any single-mechanism story that claims the whole of it has overreached.

### Does the fall happen inside industries or between them?

Both: decompositions find the division shifting against compensation within many industries considered separately, and also find activity migrating toward sectors and firms whose compensation shares were already lower, so the aggregate movement combines a genuine change in internal divisions with a change in the economy's shape.

For the Marxist tradition this decomposition has a familiar ring, because the tradition's own explanations operate at both levels and always have. Within an industry, the mechanisms are the labour process and the wage bargain: mechanisation that raises the mass of means of production each worker sets in motion, reorganisation that intensifies effort, bargaining weakness that lets productivity gains pool as profit. Between industries, the mechanisms are structural: the rise of branches whose production is organised around intangible assets and small, highly paid workforces, the decline of branches where unionised mass employment once anchored a high compensation share, the relocation of labour-intensive stages across borders. A political economy that speaks only of the first level will mistake structural transformation for a thousand separate defeats at the bargaining table. One that speaks only of the second will miss the defeats, which were also real. The decomposition literature, built by statisticians with no stake in that vocabulary, ends up enforcing a both-levels discipline that the tradition's better practitioners always preached, which is another of those convergences that suggest the measurement craft and the theoretical craft are less estranged than their polemics imply.

The firm-level turn deserves one more paragraph, because it changes the unit at which exploitation talk must be conducted if it is to remain honest. If a large part of the aggregate movement is reallocation toward firms with unusual internal divisions, then the experience of the typical worker may be governed less by a universal squeeze inside every workplace than by the shrinking of the kinds of workplaces where compensation shares were high and the growth of kinds where they are low, kinds distinguished by market power, by intangible capital, by global reach. The worker's grievance is undiminished; its address changes. Politics aimed at the internal division of the typical firm and politics aimed at the competitive conditions that let low-share firms swallow industries are different politics, and confusing them wastes the grievance on the wrong door. Measurement, once again, turns out to be the ante-room of strategy.

## The public sector, the financial sector, and the edges of the perimeter

Return to the perimeter decision with more patience, because the two sectors that strain the measure most severely each strain it in a philosophically interesting direction. The public sector's output, in the accounts, is valued at the cost of producing it, since most of it is given away rather than sold, and cost consists overwhelmingly of the compensation of public employees plus the consumption of public capital. The measured compensation share within public activity is therefore very high by construction, approaching the whole of value added once the capital charge is small, not because public employment is a workers' paradise but because the valuation rule leaves almost nothing else in the total. Include a large public sector in the measure and the aggregate share rises; shrink public provision, or contract its functions to private suppliers whose output is valued at price including profit, and the aggregate share falls, even if every individual doing the work is paid exactly as before and the work itself is unchanged. A government can thus lower the measured labour share of its economy by reorganising provision, a fact that should temper any reading of the aggregate as a direct index of class fortune.

The financial sector strains the measure from the opposite side. Much of what finance is recorded as producing is not charged to customers as fees but imputed from the spread between interest rates, a construction known by its acronym in the methodological literature and notorious among its critics for behaving strangely under stress: in a banking crisis, measured financial output has been known to rise, because the spreads from which it is imputed widened, at the very moment the sector's contribution to anyone's welfare was most in doubt. The compensation of financial employees, swollen by bonuses tied to the same spreads, and the profits recorded beside them, both enter the aggregate with these conventions baked in. An economy in which finance grows as a share of activity will therefore register distributional movements that are partly, and sometimes largely, the shadow of an imputation. The literature's corporate-sector and private-sector variants of the share exist largely to let readers see the phenomenon with finance's funhouse mirror removed, and the differences among the variants are a standing exhibit in the case for the passport discipline urged earlier.

### Is the labour share a good measure?

It is a good measure of the division of recorded income under stated conventions, assembled consistently over long spans, and a poor measure of almost everything it is popularly asked to be: of exploitation, of fairness, of living standards, or of the balance of power, each of which needs its own evidence and its own argument.

The informal sector completes the tour of the perimeter, and it matters most where the advanced-economy frame of this article is left behind. Where a large share of work is unregistered, unrecorded, and paid in cash or kind outside any statistical net, the accounts are built on surveys and estimates whose margins of error dwarf the movements under discussion in the harmonised literature, and comparisons between economies at very different levels of formality are comparisons between unlike statistical worlds. The brief for this article confines its robustness claim to the advanced economies, and that confinement is itself a measurement judgement: the claim travels only as far as the statistical infrastructure that supports it. Readers tempted to extend the finding globally on the strength of the advanced-economy evidence should notice that the extension crosses exactly the perimeter problems, informal work above all, that the coverage decision flags. The finding's modesty about its territory is part of its credibility within it.

## Income is not wealth, and a share is not a standard of living

A further family of misreadings detaches the share from income altogether and attaches it to neighbouring concepts it does not measure. The first neighbour is wealth. The distribution of wealth, of accumulated assets net of debts, is far more concentrated than the distribution of income in every economy where both are measured, and it moves to its own rhythms, driven by asset prices, inheritance, and saving out of high incomes. A falling income share for labour and a rising concentration of wealth often travel together and reinforce one another, since property income saved becomes property, but they are distinct magnitudes with distinct measures, and an argument that slides between them mid-paragraph trades accuracy for atmosphere. The housing phenomenon sits astride the boundary in an interesting way, since the dwelling is at once a source of imputed income in the accounts and the principal asset of most households that own anything, so that the same scarcity that swells recorded property income also swells the paper wealth of owners while shutting non-owners out of the asset altogether. The distributional politics of housing is therefore double, an income story and a wealth story braided together, and the braiding is one reason the subject resists the simple narratives built for it.

The second neighbour is the standard of living. A share is a proportion, and proportions are silent about the size of the whole. Compensation's portion can fall while real compensation rises, if the total grows fast enough, and can rise while real compensation falls, in a shrinking economy where profits collapse faster than wages. The decades under discussion contained stretches of both configurations in different economies, which is why the share's partisans and its critics can each assemble honest charts that appear to contradict the other side's honest charts: one side displays the proportion, the other displays the level, and the audience is invited to treat the display as a refutation. It is no such thing. The proportion answers a question about relative position, the level a question about absolute condition, and a serious distributional politics needs both answers and loses its way when it lets either impersonate the other. Marx's own treatment held the two apart with some care, since relative surplus value precisely describes a configuration in which the worker's consumption rises while labour's relative claim on the product falls, a configuration the tradition's founder regarded not as a paradox but as a normal form of the system's development.

### Does a falling share mean workers are worse off?

Not by itself: the share is a proportion of a changing total, so real compensation can rise while its portion falls, and the proportion speaks to relative position rather than absolute condition, which is why serious argument reports the level of pay and the share of income as separate facts requiring separate judgements.

The third neighbour is power. It is tempting to read the share as a thermometer of class strength, rising when labour is organised and confident, falling when it is weak, and the correlation across the postwar decades gives the temptation some excuse. But the thermometer is contaminated by every treatment decision in the table: a share propped up by executive pay does not signal the strength of the organised working class, a share pressed down by imputed rents does not signal its weakness, and a share moved by public sector reorganisation signals something about accounting perimeters rather than picket lines. Power has its own evidences, in union coverage, in strike activity, in the legal framework of bargaining, in the tightness of labour markets, and the share is at best a distant echo of those, filtered through conventions. The tradition that wants to speak about power should measure power, and use the income share for the narrower, genuine service it can perform, which is to record how the division of the money product shifted while power was doing whatever it was doing.

## The long view: a share that was supposed to be constant

The finding's fame owes something to the doctrine it embarrassed. For a generation of postwar macroeconomics, the constancy of factor shares stood among the stylised facts around which growth theory was built, a regularity associated with the growth accounting of Nicholas Kaldor, who listed a roughly constant division of income between labour and capital among the features a theory of growth had to explain. Models were constructed in which the constancy emerged from the technology, and the emergence was counted a success. A measure enshrined as a constant acquires a special vulnerability: when it moves, the movement indicts not only the world but the framework that declared movement impossible, and part of the labour share literature's energy comes from that double indictment. The decline was not merely a distributional event. It was a falsified stylised fact, and falsified stylised facts reorganise disciplines.

The older classical economists, by contrast, would have found nothing surprising in a moving share, since the product's division among wages, profit, and rent was for them the central problem of the science, variable with accumulation, with the state of subsistence, with the bargaining of classes, and with the price of land. Marx inherited that classical framing and radicalised it, treating the division as the surface form of the relation between labour time's portions. The twentieth century's interlude, in which the division was theorised as a technological constant, looks from that longer vantage like the anomaly, and the decades of decline like a return of the repressed question. That framing should not be pushed too far: the classical writers lacked the statistical apparatus to measure what they theorised, and their confidence about the division's determinants was as construction-dependent as any modern series, resting on conventions about subsistence and the wage fund that later dissolved. But the historical arc disciplines the present debate. A quantity that one era built into its models as a constant and another era argues over as a trend is a quantity whose behaviour depends on institutions and history, which is to say on exactly the forces that resist reduction to a single mechanism.

### Was the labour share always expected to stay constant?

Only within a particular postwar growth theory that elevated its rough stability into a stylised fact for models to reproduce; the classical economists before that era, and the Marxist tradition after it, treated the division of income as historically variable and determined by accumulation, institutions, and the balance between classes.

There is a methodological keepsake in the Kaldor episode that generalises beyond this literature. Stylised facts are summaries of constructions, and when the constructions change, through revised accounts, new imputations, or better industry data, the facts can change underneath the theories built to explain them, leaving the theories explaining a world that has been revised away. The labour share's own history contains smaller versions of the same trap, as successive vintages of the accounts revised levels and occasionally trends, and arguments constructed on one vintage were quoted serenely into the era of the next. The reader's defence is the habit this article has drilled throughout: attach the construction to the claim, and when the construction is revised, revisit the claim rather than defending its earlier form out of loyalty. Loyalty is a virtue in persons and a vice in propositions.

## The mirror image: what the accounts call profit

Every statement about labour's falling portion is equally a statement about the rising portion of everything else, and the everything else deserves the same sceptical tour given to compensation, because the property side of the accounts is at least as heterogeneous as the labour side. Recorded property income gathers the operating surplus of corporations, the interest flowing to lenders, the rents of landlords real and imputed, the mixed income not yet assigned, and the profits of unincorporated businesses, a coalition even less unified than the coalition inside compensation. When a headline announces that profits have swallowed the share labour lost, the word profits is doing silent work, gathering interest paid to pension funds, imputed rent paid by homeowners to themselves, and the surplus of giant corporations into a single menacing mass. Disaggregating the property side is therefore as necessary as disaggregating compensation, and the housing decomposition was, in effect, exactly that operation performed on the mirror image: it asked what the rise in property's portion was made of, and found shelter where the audience expected factories.

The exercise generalises. Interest's part in the total swells and shrinks with the level of rates and the stock of debt, neither of which maps onto the fortunes of industrial capital in any simple way, and an era of high rates can fatten recorded interest while squeezing the profits of indebted firms, so that property income in aggregate rises over a period in which the canonical capitalist of the imagination is having a difficult time. The operating surplus of the corporate sector, the component closest to the classical idea of profit, has its own internal divisions between what is retained, what is taxed, what is paid out as dividends, and what is devoted to buying back shares, each with different implications for accumulation and for the incomes of the households that ultimately receive it. Pension funds complicate the moral geography further, since a large part of the property income recorded in an advanced economy accrues, through funds, to the deferred wages of employees themselves, a fact that does not dissolve class distinctions, the funds' beneficiaries have wildly unequal stakes and no control, but does forbid the simple picture in which every unit of property income is a unit extracted from workers by a separate class of idlers. The accounts record flows, not villains, and the flows run through institutions that braid the classes together financially while leaving their positions in production untouched.

### Is the profit share the opposite of the labour share?

Only arithmetically, and the arithmetic misleads: the remainder after compensation gathers corporate surplus, interest, real and imputed rent, and unassigned mixed income into one total, so a rise in the remainder can be driven by shelter or by interest conditions while the profits of productive enterprise move modestly or even fall.

Marxist categories cut this remainder differently, and the difference is one of the translation's main labours. Surplus value, in the theory, is a single fund at the point of production that subsequently divides into industrial profit, commercial profit, interest, and ground rent, with taxes and the salaries of unproductive labour also claiming their portions. The accounting remainder resembles that fund the way a estuary resembles a river: the waters are connected, but much has flowed in and out along the way, including flows, like imputed rent and the earnings of self-employed people assigned to capital, that the theory would source elsewhere or nowhere. The resemblance is close enough to tempt identification and distant enough to punish it, the recurring geometry of this entire subject. A reader who has internalised the geometry will feel the temptation and the punishment arriving together whenever a property-side aggregate is quoted, and that double feeling, attraction checked by audit, is the mature relation to this evidence.

## Markups, market power, and another instrument that argues back

The market power explanation of the share's decline leans on a second constructed quantity, the markup of price over marginal cost, and the fate of that quantity in its own literature is a companion lesson in measurement dominance. Estimates of rising aggregate markups, built from the accounts of firms and from production function assumptions, were advanced as evidence that firms had gained the power to price above cost and to squeeze the share of revenue reaching labour. The estimates were then challenged on construction grounds: the result depended on which costs were counted as variable, on how overhead and marketing outlays were treated, and on assumptions about the technology that the method needed in order to see marginal cost at all, since marginal cost is not a line in any ledger. Alternative constructions produced flatter markup histories, and the debate settled into the familiar pattern in which the instrument, interrogated, turns out to have been carrying much of the conclusion.

None of this shows that market power is innocent in the story of the share. It shows that the case for its guilt must be built like the case for the decline itself, from decompositions and stated conventions, and that a literature in which the two most quoted quantities are both construction-sensitive demands a reader with two audits running at once. The superstar firm evidence, which rests on the observable reallocation of activity among firms rather than on an inferred marginal cost, has worn better under challenge, and its picture, of industries increasingly dominated by firms whose compensation shares are low because their value added per worker is swollen by intangible assets and network positions, connects the distributional story to the structure of contemporary production in a way the aggregate never could. The lesson generalises the article's refrain. In this field the durable findings are the ones that survive being taken apart, and the skill that matters, in producers and consumers of research alike, is the taking apart.

### Are rising markups the proven cause of the decline?

No: the markup estimates advanced as evidence of growing market power proved sensitive to their own construction choices about variable costs and unobserved marginal cost, and while the reallocation of activity toward dominant low-share firms is well documented, the step from that pattern to a single proven cause remains contested.

For the tradition, the markup detour holds a specific warning. The rate of surplus value, the profit rate, and now the markup form a family of quantities that polemic treats as interchangeable dials on the machine of exploitation, each supposedly readable off published data. The family's members are in fact distinct constructions with distinct sensitivities, and a commentary that reaches for whichever dial is pointing the preferred way this season will eventually be caught by a season in which they point apart. The alternative is slower and better: choose the quantity that matches the claim, state its construction, accept its verdict including when the verdict disappoints. That discipline, applied consistently, would cost the tradition some headlines and buy it something worth more, a reputation for meaning what it says about evidence, in a field where such reputations are rare enough to be decisive.

## The boundary of the working class, reconsidered through mixed income

Linger a while longer on the self-employed, because the statistical puzzle they pose conceals a political question that the decades under discussion made urgent. The forms of work that lack an employer multiplied and mutated across the period: alongside the traditional shopkeeper, farmer, and tradesman appeared the dependent contractor whose single client dictates hours and prices, the professional whose incorporation is a tax arrangement over an essentially salaried practice, the driver and courier whose labour is allocated by an algorithm they do not control and whose legal status has been litigated in many jurisdictions, and the portfolio worker stitching together fragments of employment and self-employment within a single week. Each form presses differently on the imputation rules. A rule that treats all mixed income as labour income will read the growth of dependent contracting as a swelling of labour's ranks and income, when the phenomenon may equally represent employers shedding the obligations of employment while keeping its substance, a reclassification of the same work into a cheaper legal costume. A rule that imputes by comparison with employees will chase its own tail as the comparison group itself is reclassified. The accounts, built for stability of categories, register the legal costume; the economic substance migrates underneath.

The Marxist tradition has resources for this problem that it has not always bothered to use. Its classification was never by legal costume in the first place: the wage labourer is defined by separation from the means of production, with the compulsion to sell labour power as its consequence, and by that test the dependent contractor, owning a vehicle or a laptop that cannot be worked without access to a platform or a client controlled by capital, stands far closer to the proletarian pole than the legal form suggests, while the incorporated professional selling scarce expertise at a high price stands closer to a rentier of human capital. The boundary of the class, on the theory's own criteria, runs through the population the accounts call self-employed, at a place no imputation rule keyed to legal status will find. Research that takes the substance seriously, reclassifying by dependence and control rather than by contract, finds the effective working class larger and its income share lower than the costume-based figures suggest, though the finding, like all findings here, is a construction and must carry its rules on its face.

### Who counts as labour when the self-employed are reclassified by substance?

Workers who are separated from any real command over the means of production and compelled to sell their capacity to work, including dependent contractors whose legal independence conceals a client's or a platform's control, belong on the labour side by the tradition's own criteria, whatever costume the accounts record.

The general moral extends well beyond this one category. Every aggregate in political economy is built over a boundary, and the boundary is always, on inspection, a theory in disguise. Where the line around labour income falls decides who counts as labour when the share is computed; where the working class's boundary is drawn decides who counts as labour for the purposes of politics; and the two drawings, though different in purpose, corrupt one another when either is mistaken for a fact of nature. The self-employment decision in the treatment table is the statistical face of a question that movements also face in organisational form, namely whom the category of the worker includes, and the parallel is not a metaphor. It is the same intellectual operation, classification under a theory, performed in two institutions. Competence at it, in either institution, begins with knowing that the operation is being performed.

## Rent's revenge: what the housing result invites the tradition to do

The housing decomposition was treated earlier as an objection, and answered as one. It remains to treat it as an invitation, because the research programme it sketches is substantial and largely unclaimed. If a significant part of the distributional shift of the period runs through shelter, then the mechanisms to be studied are the mechanisms of rent: the scarcity of well-located land and the planning regimes that ration building upon it, the treatment of dwellings as financial assets and the credit conditions that capitalise expected rents into prices, the tenure structure that decides who collects the rent and who pays it, and the intergenerational transfers by which housing wealth sorts the population into those whose families captured location early and those whose wages must service other people's capture forever. Each mechanism is amenable to Marxist analysis in its classical vocabulary of ground rent, differential advantage of location, and monopoly of a non-reproducible asset, supplemented by the analysis of credit and fictitious capital that the tradition developed for other purposes and that housing finance fits almost without alteration.

The political consequences of following the invitation are not minor. A movement whose distributional story centres the wage bargain will organise around workplaces and wage claims. A movement whose story centres rent will organise around tenants, planning, land taxation, and the supply of dwellings, constituencies and demands that overlap with the first movement's without coinciding with it. The evidence, corrected by the decomposition, does not choose between these politics, since the within-industry decline in compensation's portion is also real and the wage story retains its own evidence. But the evidence does forbid the complacent assumption that the first politics exhausts the distributional question, and it assigns the second politics an evidentiary foundation that rhetoric alone had previously supplied. Scholars inside the tradition who have taken housing and rent seriously as central categories of contemporary capitalism have been making this argument from the theoretical side for years. The decomposition gives them, from the statistical side, the strongest wind they have had, and the slowness of the wider tradition to notice remains the standing reproach this article recorded earlier.

### Does the housing result help or hurt the Marxist reading of the period?

Both, in sequence: it hurts the lazy version, which quoted the aggregate as a transcript of the wage relation, and it helps the serious version, by directing attention to rent, land, and housing finance, where the tradition's classical categories of ground rent and monopoly of location apply with unusual directness.

There is a further refinement the tradition should steal from the decomposition's method. Rognlie's question was not whether the aggregate had moved but what the movement was made of, and that question, asked of any aggregate the tradition cherishes, is a solvent for lazy certainties. Asked of the profit rate, it separates the behaviour of the rate in production from the behaviour of returns in finance and real estate. Asked of productivity, it separates genuine technical change from compositional drift among sectors. Asked of the compensation share itself, in the top-incomes section of this article, it separated the boardroom from the shop floor and found the story sharper for the separation. A research programme that habitually asks what its favourite aggregates are made of will be surprised more often than one that does not, and the surprises, as the housing case shows, are frequently where the real findings live.

## Productive and unproductive labour: the wedge that does the deepest work

Of the four wedges separating the share from the rate of exploitation, the productive and unproductive distinction cuts deepest, because it concerns not a boundary adjustment but the identity of the activity being counted. In the Marxist scheme, labour is productive, in the specific sense that matters for the theory of surplus value, when it is exchanged against capital and produces surplus value for its purchaser. Labour exchanged against revenue, however useful, however exhausting, however socially indispensable, is unproductive in this technical sense: the teacher in a state school, the nurse in a public hospital, the clerk recording transactions, the guard protecting property, the salesperson moving goods from shelf to basket. The distinction is not a ranking of worth, a point the tradition's expositors have had to repeat for a century against misreading, since the schoolteacher's labour forms the capacities on which all production depends while the labour of manufacturing a trivial luxury article counts as productive if a capitalist profits from it. The distinction tracks a relation to capital, nothing more, and its bite for the present subject is that the national accounts know nothing of it. Every one of those workers appears in compensation, and the output of the sectors employing the unproductive among them appears in national income, valued by price or by cost, so both sides of the accounting ratio are populated by labour that the theoretical ratio treats as sustained out of surplus value rather than as producing it.

The quantitative consequence is not a detail. Unproductive employment, on the definitions used by the translation literature, grew substantially across the postwar decades in the advanced economies, as circulation, finance, administration, and state activity expanded faster than production employment. A rising mass of compensation is therefore, in the translated picture, a rising charge upon surplus value rather than a rising share of newly produced value going to its producers, and the two ratios can move in opposite directions for decades on the strength of that single structural drift: the accounting share of compensation easing downward gently while the translated rate of exploitation climbs, because the surplus must sustain an expanding unproductive apparatus as well as accumulate. No reader who grasps that mechanism can ever again treat the accounting ratio as a gauge of the theoretical one, which is why this article gives the distinction its own section rather than a place in a list. It is the load-bearing wall of the argument.

### Why does the productive and unproductive distinction matter for this evidence?

Because the accounts count the compensation of circulation, finance, administration, and state labour exactly as they count the compensation of production labour, while the theory treats much of that labour as paid out of surplus value, so structural growth in unproductive employment drives the two ratios in different directions.

The distinction also explains a puzzle that naive readings of the share stumble over. If exploitation intensified across the period as the tradition claims, why did the compensation share not collapse? The naive reading expects the accounting ratio to dramatise the theoretical movement, and its failure to do so is quoted by critics as embarrassment. The translated picture dissolves the puzzle: the compensation total was being swollen from within by the growth of unproductive employment and by executive pay, two components that rise with the surplus rather than against it, so a gently falling aggregate is entirely compatible with a sharply rising rate of exploitation in production. Compatibility is not confirmation, and the translated estimates must stand on their own construction, but the puzzle's dissolution matters because it removes a counterfeit refutation from the field. Critics who wield the share's mildness against the theory are committing the same identity error as partisans who wield its fall in the theory's support, and the symmetry of the error is the cleanest demonstration that the error, not the evidence, is doing the work.

## The state inside the accounts, and inside the theory

The state deserves a section of its own in the anatomy, because it sits inside both category systems in ways that guarantee mistranslation when the systems are confused. In the accounts, general government is a producer whose output is valued at cost and an employer whose compensation bill is a large component of the labour side of the national ratio. In the theory, the state is an apparatus sustained by taxation, which is to say by deductions from wages and surplus value, and its employees, productive of public goods and of order, are unproductive of surplus value in the technical sense. Neither description is a criticism of the other; they answer different questions, the accounts asking what activity costs and produces as measured, the theory asking where the value that sustains activity originates. Trouble begins only at the border, when a change in the boundary between state and market provision, a hospital service contracted out, a railway taken in, moves masses of compensation and output between accounting sectors and shifts the aggregate share without any worker's relation to capital changing at all. The share, quoted across such a reorganisation, records a bookkeeping migration as a distributional event.

The deeper point is that the state is not a bystander in the distributional story but one of its principal theatres, through taxation, transfers, public employment, and the provision of services forming part of the real standard of living while bypassing the wage entirely. Two economies with identical labour shares can deliver radically different material security to their working populations depending on how much of the social product reaches workers as public provision rather than as money income, and two decades with identical shares can differ as sharply. The social wage, as the older literature called it, is invisible to the ratio under discussion, and a distributional politics that confines itself to the ratio will systematically undervalue the terrain on which some of the period's largest distributional battles were actually fought, the defence or dismantling of public provision. The measure's silence about the state is not a flaw to be repaired by a better ratio. It is a reminder that the division of the product happens through several channels at once, of which the wage bargain, the only channel the share can see, is one.

## Objections from the other side: the sceptic's case against the measure itself

The audit in this article has been directed mostly at overclaiming on the finding's behalf. Fairness requires a hearing for the opposite overclaim, the sceptical case that the labour share is a meaningless artefact from which nothing follows, a case assembled from the very treatment decisions catalogued here and pressed to a conclusion they do not support. In its strong form the sceptical case runs: a quantity whose level can be moved across a wide band by defensible choices, whose trend can be trimmed by depreciation accounting and partly reassigned to housing, and whose categories correspond to no theoretical object, is not a measurement of anything; it is a Rorschach blot for distributional rhetoric, and the scholarly industry around it is an elaborate ceremony for dignifying prior convictions. Something like this case is heard whenever the finding is politically inconvenient, and it deserves a better answer than indignation.

The answer begins by conceding the premises and denying the inference. Levels are indeed construction-sensitive, and this article has insisted on it. But a quantity can be level-sensitive and trend-informative at once, as the robustness assessment showed: the direction of the movement survives the family of defensible constructions, and a direction that survives is information about the world, not about the constructors, since the constructors disagree among themselves and include many with no wish to find a decline. The sceptic's inference also proves too much. National income itself, output, productivity, unemployment, and the price level are all constructed quantities with contestable conventions at their joints, and the sceptic who dissolves the labour share on construction grounds has dissolved, by the same argument, the entire empirical apparatus of macroeconomics, including the quantities the sceptic's own side quotes when they point the preferred way. Construction is the condition of social measurement, not a scandal within it. The mature position is neither worship nor dismissal but the juror's stance argued for earlier: weigh the building, claim what the building supports, refuse what it does not.

### Is the labour share decline just a statistical artefact?

No: particular components of it are artefacts of convention, and the housing component is partly one, but the decline survives across independent constructions, datasets, and economies, which is more survival than an artefact can manage, since an artefact belongs to the construction that made it and vanishes when the construction changes.

There is a weaker sceptical case that deserves a different, warmer answer, because it is half right. This is the case that the share, even taken at face value, answers a question nobody should care about, since what matters is absolute living standards, or poverty, or the condition of the worst off, and a proportion is a distraction from those urgencies. The half that is right is the insistence that proportions are not conditions. The half that is wrong is the dismissal, because relative position is itself a material fact with consequences: for bargaining power, since the division of a growing product decides who can wait and who cannot; for demand, since the propensity to spend differs between wage income and property income; for politics, since perceived fairness of the division shapes what populations will consent to; and for the dynamics of the system, since the distribution between consumption and accumulation is written into the shares. A tradition descended from the classical economists, for whom distribution was the principal problem of the science precisely because it governed accumulation and class power together, will not be talked out of caring about proportions by the observation that levels also matter. Both matter, separately, and the discipline is in the separately.

## What a serious research programme does with this evidence

Gather the threads into a programme, since the article's criticisms imply one and a reader is entitled to see the positive shape. A serious Marxist engagement with the labour share evidence would do several things it does only fitfully. It would quote the finding in its robust form, as a directional shift in the recorded division of income through the advanced world across the decades under discussion, with constructions attached. It would absorb the housing decomposition into its own account of the period, developing the theory of rent and housing finance that the decomposition points toward, rather than leaving that development to isolated scholars. It would disaggregate compensation as a matter of routine, separating the earnings distribution's summit and the growth of unproductive employment from the position of production workers at large, since those separations are where the class content of the evidence actually lives. It would maintain and refine the translation exercises in the line of Shaikh and Tonak's Measuring the Wealth of Nations, extending them across economies and keeping their rules public, so that claims about the rate of exploitation rest on constructions built for the purpose. And it would state its defeaters in advance, as this article has stated the defeater for the robustness judgement, so that its relation to evidence is a standing invitation to refutation rather than a search for confirmation.

Such a programme would lose some rhetorical conveniences. It would gain the thing the conveniences were counterfeiting, which is a distributional account of the period that can survive cross-examination by a hostile expert, the only kind of account worth carrying into an argument that matters. The mainstream literature, for its part, has its own homework in the same direction, since its headline practices, the undecomposed aggregate quoted without a passport, are the mirror image of the tradition's sins, and its best practitioners, the decomposers and reconstructors, are already doing the work its popularisers skip. The two literatures at their best are converging on a shared standard, state your construction, decompose your aggregate, pitch your claim at the level the evidence supports, and the convergence suggests the standard is not a partisan demand but the shape of maturity in a measurement-governed field.

### How should the exploitation warning affect the grade given to this evidence?

It grades it by destination: as evidence about the division of recorded money incomes the finding earns a strong mark for direction and a qualified one for size, while as evidence about exploitation it earns no mark at all until a translation has been built, because the quantities belong to different category systems.

That grading, applied consistently, resolves the apparent tension that runs through commentary on this subject, in which the same writer celebrates the finding in one paragraph and is caught by its weaknesses in the next. There is no tension once destinations are distinguished. The evidence is strong where it is strong and absent where it is absent, and the writer's task is cartography, drawing the border accurately, rather than advocacy, colouring the whole map one shade. This article has attempted the cartography for one famous measure. The method travels: to the profit rate, to productivity, to every quantity in the field where a theoretical object and a statistical object share a name and a silhouette. Wherever that sharing occurs, the same questions arise, the same temptations beckon, and the same discipline, construction stated, aggregate decomposed, claim pitched at the supported level, defeater named, is the price of saying something true.

## The denominator's fine print: taxes, subsidies, and the point of measurement

One more construction choice, quieter than the famous five, deserves its paragraph in the anatomy because it illustrates how deeply the habit of asking after construction must go. Income can be measured at the point where it is generated, before the state's fiscal hands have touched it, or where it is received, after taxes on production have been levied and subsidies paid. The accounts call these valuations factor cost and market prices, and the wedge between them, the net tax take on production, is itself a share of the total whose size varies across economies and across decades with the fiscal constitution. A compensation share computed at market prices divides by a total swollen with taxes that are neither wages nor profits, diluting both sides of the class division with the state's portion; computed at factor cost, it divides the product between the two great income categories more cleanly, at the price of ignoring that the state's portion is real and contested. Neither basis is wrong. They answer to different curiosities, the first about the whole product as priced, the second about the division among its private claimants, and a series that switches basis between its endpoints, or a comparison that mixes bases between economies, manufactures movement out of bookkeeping. The methodological literature flags the choice; the headline literature, characteristically, does not know the choice exists.

Subsidies add a wrinkle with distributional teeth. Where an economy supports an industry's prices or its wage bill through subsidy, the recorded incomes of that industry are partly fiscal transfers wearing the dress of market earnings, and the share computed over them reflects a political decision as much as a market outcome. Agricultural support in many economies has exactly this character, holding recorded farm incomes, a blend of mixed income in any case, at levels the unaided market would not have delivered. The analyst who compares compensation shares across economies with very different subsidy regimes is comparing, in part, the fiscal constitutions of states. Once again the observation is not that comparison is impossible but that it is an achievement, earned by alignment work, and that the achievement is invisible in the single column of figures that reports its results.

### At what point in the flow of income should the division be measured?

At factor cost if the question concerns the division between the private claimants on the product, and at market prices if the question concerns the whole priced product including the state's fiscal portion, with the basis stated in either case, since the wedge between the two bases varies across economies and decades.

Revisions deserve their mention beside the fine print, because a national accounts series is not a fixed text but a succession of vintages. Statistical offices revise: new sources arrive, benchmarks are reset, methods are updated, and the past is rewritten, usually modestly, occasionally enough to move a trend that an argument was built upon. The careful literature keeps its vintages and reports sensitivity to them. Public argument, which quotes a figure found in a newspaper found in a report found in a working paper, almost never knows which vintage it is quoting, and the figure's authority survives revisions that have already falsified its precision. There is no remedy except the standing one: claims pitched at that level are revision-resistant in a way that claims staked on precise magnitudes are not, and the pitch of the claim is, once more, the claimant's choice. The labour share's directional core has survived repeated revision of the accounts across many economies, which belongs in the ledger of its robustness; its precise magnitudes have been revised like everyone else's, which is entered on the other side of the account.

## Intangible capital and the share in an economy of ideas

A structural feature of the period presses on several rows of the treatment table at once and merits a section of its own: the growing weight of intangible capital, of software, brands, designs, organisational know-how, and data, in the capital stock of advanced economies. Intangibles strain the accounts at their foundations. Some are capitalised, recognised as investment and depreciated, with software the leading case; others, like much spending on branding and organisational design, are treated as current costs, which understates both investment and the capital stock while overstating the surplus available in the year of spending. The depreciation profile of intangibles is short and uncertain, feeding the gross and net divergence examined earlier. And the firms richest in intangibles display exactly the characteristics the decomposition literature found driving the aggregate: very high value added per worker, small workforces relative to their market weight, and compensation shares that are low despite the high pay of the workers they do employ, because the denominator is swollen by returns to assets that are ideas rather than plant.

For the theory, intangibles pose a delicious difficulty. An asset that can be used without being used up, copied at negligible cost, and fenced only by law sits awkwardly in a framework built on the reproduction of material capital, and the returns it yields resemble rent on a legally constructed scarcity as much as profit on production, which connects the intangible economy back to the housing discussion: in both cases a rising slice of recorded property income is a claim on scarcity, of location in one case and of protected knowledge in the other, rather than a return to organising production. The distributional era the accounts record may thus be, in significant part, an era of rents, on land and on intellect, and the tradition's categories, ground rent generalised, may fit it better than the categories of industrial profit around which its commentary is usually organised. That suggestion, like the housing invitation, is a research programme in embryo, and it is the kind of suggestion that only becomes visible when the aggregate is decomposed and its components are asked what they are.

## Three workplaces: the measure at ground level

Abstraction has carried the argument far, and a descent to ground level will test whether the categories survive contact with particular workplaces, described generically, as this article's structural discipline requires. Take first a hospital. Its output, if publicly provided, is valued at cost; its workforce ranges from surgeons whose pay approaches the executive pattern to cleaners whose pay anchors the bottom of the distribution; its capital, buildings and scanners, depreciates on schedules that have shortened as the technology quickened. In the accounts the hospital contributes a high compensation share and a modest surplus. In the theory its labour is largely unproductive of surplus value while being indispensable to the reproduction of labour power itself, a combination the accounting ratio cannot express and the theoretical ratio expresses only by excluding the hospital from its numerator while depending on it absolutely. Any aggregate containing many hospitals is therefore a negotiated settlement between two descriptions of care, and neither description is the hospital's own truth about itself.

Take next a warehouse operation attached to a retail network. Its drivers perform labour the tradition counts as productive where transport is concerned, its pickers labour at the border of production and circulation, its sales and administrative staff labour in circulation proper. Its measured compensation share reflects the blend, together with the rents of its sites, which sit on valuable urban land, and the depreciation of its vehicles and automation equipment, which is rapid. A fall in this enterprise's recorded share might reflect a genuine squeeze on its workforce, or a rise in the land values its accounts impute, or a round of automation that raised the depreciation charge, or a reclassification of its drivers as independent contractors that moved their earnings out of compensation and into purchased services. Four stories, one movement, and only decomposition at the level of the firm can say which story happened, which is the aggregate's predicament reproduced in miniature.

Take finally a small software practice, a handful of highly paid specialists producing a product that is copied for its customers at almost no marginal cost. Its compensation share will look low in any year its product succeeds, not because its workers are squeezed, they are among the best paid in the economy, but because the denominator carries returns to intangible assets, to the accumulated code and reputation, that dwarf the wage bill. In the theory's terms awkward questions multiply: how much of the product's price is profit, how much is rent on protected knowledge, how much is the deferred wages of the code's authors capitalised? The accounts do not ask. The aggregate that sums the hospital, the warehouse, and the software practice into a single national ratio is summing three different kinds of distributional event, and the reader who knows the three workplaces will never again read the sum innocently.

### What distinguishes a genuine decline from a construction's artefact?

Persistence under hostile reconstruction: a decline that remains when the measure is rebuilt on a net basis, with dwellings set aside, with mixed income imputed by rival methods, and with the perimeter redrawn, is a feature of the economy, while a decline that vanishes under a single defensible rebuilding belonged to the first construction.

Those three vignettes also expose why the within and between decomposition, dry as it sounds, is where the human content of the finding is stored. The aggregate's fall, once resolved, is some mixture of the warehouse's squeeze, the hospital's reclassification, the software practice's swelling denominator, and the migration of employment among the three. Each component has different victims, different mechanisms, and different remedies, and a politics that addresses the aggregate as though it were one event will misaddress all of them. Decomposition is not the enemy of moral seriousness about distribution. It is moral seriousness, refusing to let a single number impersonate a population of different wrongs and changes.

## The world economy dimension: where the value added is booked

The advanced economies whose shares this article has tracked do not produce in isolation, and the geography of production complicates the geography of the shares in ways the comparative literature has had to confront. When a firm headquartered in one economy organises production through suppliers in another, the value added is recorded where the production occurs, while the profits attributable to the firm's intangibles, its brand, its designs, its logistics intelligence, are booked where its accounts place them, often in the headquarters economy or in a jurisdiction chosen for fiscal hospitality. The compensation shares of both economies are bent by the arrangement: the producing economy records the wages of assembly and a thin margin, the booking economy records a swollen surplus against a small, highly paid workforce, and both national ratios now describe the accounting geography of a single integrated operation as much as they describe two national class structures. Global value chain research, which reallocates value added along chains of production, was developed partly to see through exactly this, and its reconstructions show how much of the advanced economies' measured surplus is a claim on production performed elsewhere.

The Marxist tradition, which has theorised the international transfer of value for as long as it has theorised imperialism, should find this terrain congenial, and the measurement moral is by now familiar: the national share of an economy deeply woven into world production is a joint product of its class relations and its position in the chains, and quoting it as a pure index of the first is the identity error wearing a passport. The decline of compensation's portion in the advanced economies and its behaviour in the economies that absorbed the relocated production are chapters of one story, and the story's telling requires the chain-level constructions, not the national aggregates alone. Here as everywhere in this article, the aggregate is the beginning of the inquiry and the end of nothing.

### Can national shares be read separately when production is organised across borders?

Only with caution: where firms book the returns to intangibles in one economy while production and its wages occur in another, each national ratio reflects the accounting geography of integrated operations, and reading either as a pure record of a national class structure mistakes the chain's bookkeeping for the country's distribution.

There is also a temporal trap in the international evidence that the structural discipline governing this article exists to disarm. Comparisons are often drawn between a share in one economy at one date and a share in another at a later date, with the difference narrated as convergence, decline, or overtaking. Every construction difference catalogued in the treatment table can hide inside such a comparison, compounded by the two economies' different statistical vintages and different positions in the chains just described. The harmonised comparative literature earns its authority by refusing such comparisons until definitions are aligned, and its central finding, the breadth of the decline across economies once alignment is done, is powerful precisely because it is what remains after the traps are emptied. Breadth after alignment is the strongest form the finding takes, and the form in which this article's verdict endorses it.

## Institutions and the wage bargain: the explanation with the longest pedigree

Among the candidate explanations, the institutional one has the longest pedigree in the tradition and deserves a fuller hearing than the survey earlier allowed, both because its mechanisms are concrete and because it interacts instructively with the measurement questions. The claim, in its developed form, is that the division of income inside firms and industries is not dictated by technology but settled, within wide bounds, by bargaining, and that the bargaining position of labour deteriorated across the period through a connected set of changes: declining union membership and coverage, the erosion of pattern bargaining that once carried settlements across whole sectors, legal reforms that raised the cost of organising and striking, the growth of employment forms that place workers outside collective coverage altogether, and the credible threat of relocation that global integration put in every employer's hand. On this account the within-industry component of the decline is the footprint of those changes, and its variation across economies tracks, imperfectly but visibly, the variation in how far the institutional dismantling went.

The measurement sections give this explanation both support and homework. Support, because the within-industry decline is real, because the top-incomes disaggregation shows the broad body of employees losing more than the aggregate admits, which is what a bargaining story predicts when the summit's pay is set by a different process than the floor's, and because the timing of accelerations in several economies sits plausibly beside institutional turning points. Homework, because the story must also explain the between component and the economies where institutions held comparatively firm yet shares still fell, and because a bargaining account pitched at the level of the aggregate will be embarrassed by the housing component like every other account. The strongest versions of the institutional explanation are therefore sectorally specific, tracing particular settlements, particular legal changes, particular defeats, and their imprint on particular industries' divisions, and at that level of specificity the explanation and the measurement finally meet on common ground, since both are exercises in decomposition.

### Through what mechanism does executive pay bend the share upward?

Because it is compensation in contractual form while being, in function, a claim on the surplus set by the summit of the firm for itself, so its rapid growth inflates the labour side of the accounts and disguises how far the portion reaching ordinary employees has actually fallen.

The bargaining story also clarifies what kind of quantity the share is by showing what it is an outcome of. A division settled by bargaining within bounds set by technology and markets will move when the balance of forces moves, and will therefore carry information about that balance, filtered and delayed. The share is not a thermometer of power, as an earlier section warned, but neither is it unrelated to power; it is a sediment of past bargains, layered with accounting conventions, and reading sediment is a genuine skill with genuine yields, provided the reader remembers that sediment is not the river. Political economy at its best has always read such sediments, the distribution tables of the classical economists were exactly that, and the modern literature's contribution is to have made the reading auditable, its conventions stated, its decompositions public. The tradition that learns to read the modern sediment with the modern audit gains back, in usable form, the ground its classical founders held by instinct.

## Care work, reproduction, and the labour the accounts half see

No anatomy of compensation is complete without the labour that reproduces labour power itself, because the wage has always had to cover, in Marx's phrase for the value of labour power, the costs of maintaining the worker and raising the next generation of workers, while much of the work of that maintenance and raising is performed unpaid, outside employment, and therefore outside the numerator of the ratio entirely. Unpaid domestic and caring labour does not appear in compensation, and the services it produces do not appear in national income, since the production boundary of the accounts runs around the household and counts its internal services, with the signal exception of imputed housing, as outside production. The share is thus a ratio within the monetised economy, and its movements can be driven by the migration of work across the boundary between the paid and unpaid spheres. When care that was once performed unpaid in households is commodified, purchased from nurseries, agencies, and meal services, compensation and income both grow by the transferred activity, and the share registers the formalisation of reproduction as though it were a change in distribution. When the traffic runs the other way, under austerity or the withdrawal of services, work falls back onto households and vanishes from the ratio while intensifying in life.

Feminist political economy has pressed this point for decades, and its satellite accounts, which value unpaid household production in experimental extensions of the national framework, consistently find a magnitude large enough to reorder any distributional picture built without it. The point for this article is not to annex that literature but to register its measurement consequence: the labour share describes the division of the recorded product among recorded claimants, and the population of labourers whose work is unrecorded stands outside the frame, disproportionately women, their exclusion a convention of the production boundary as consequential as any in the treatment table. A tradition whose founding category, the value of labour power, explicitly includes the reproduction of the worker has less excuse than most for forgetting the unpaid labour that performs the reproduction, and the forgetting is another instance of the accounts' categories being mistaken for the theory's.

### What must a student keep apart when comparing labour times with money incomes?

Everything that makes them different objects: the units, since one divides portions of the working day and the other divides currency sums at ruling prices; the populations, since the accounts include unproductive and executive labour the theory relocates; and the valuation, since prices diverge from values systematically rather than by accident.

The boundary has a second face, at the border of the welfare state. Public provision of care and health moves reproduction work into recorded employment, where it swells compensation and, being valued at cost, raises the measured share; privatisation moves the same work into the market, where it is valued at price and generates a recorded surplus; and the return of care to households under the pressure of fees removes it from the accounts altogether. A single population of carers, doing substantially the same work under three regimes, produces three different labour shares. The ratio is faithfully recording the regimes. What it cannot do is rank them by the wellbeing of carers or cared-for, and the reader who asks it to has once again mistaken the instrument's jurisdiction. The measure's jurisdiction is the recorded division of the recorded product, and the whole art taught by this article is knowing where that jurisdiction ends.

## Generations and the life course inside the aggregate

One more decomposition, rarely performed in the headline literature, rearranges the finding along the dimension of age, and its results complicate every camp's favourite story. Compensation is earned across a life course, and the aggregate share sums the pay of entrants at the bottom of their ladders, of mid-career workers at their peak, and of older workers whose earnings flatten or fall, weighted by the demographic shape of the workforce. An ageing workforce mechanically alters the aggregate even if every age-specific bargain stays fixed, and the decades under discussion were decades of demographic transition across the advanced economies. Meanwhile the experience of the generations diverged: entrants in the later decades faced weaker starting positions, longer periods in insecure and reclassified work, and housing costs that consumed the margin their predecessors had saved, while established cohorts carried protections won earlier. The aggregate share, summing across the generations, can thus record a mild decline while the entrant's position deteriorated sharply and the established worker's held, a distribution of experience inside the total that no aggregate will confess.

Housing binds the generations together in the accounts' own terms. The imputed rent that swells property income accrues to owners, who are disproportionately the older cohorts, while the actual rents paid by younger households transfer real income in the same direction, so the housing component within the share's decline is also, in part, an intergenerational transfer recorded as a class phenomenon. Disentangling class from generation in the data is difficult, since the two are braided by the life course itself, but the attempt matters for the same reason every decomposition in this article matters: the remedy for a class squeeze and the remedy for an intergenerational blockage are different politics, addressed to different mechanisms, and an aggregate that fuses them sends its readers to the wrong doors with the right grievances. The measurement discipline, pursued far enough, becomes a discipline of political aim.

## Crises and the share: the countercyclical trap

The behaviour of the share across crises plants a trap for the unwary that a section should spring deliberately. Profits are the most cyclically volatile component of income, collapsing when demand fails while compensation, stickier by contract and custom, falls later and less. The share therefore tends to rise in slumps and fall in booms, moving against the fortunes of the workers whose name it bears: in the depths of a crisis, with unemployment climbing and hours cut, the measured portion going to labour can spike upward, because the denominator has fallen through the floor of the profit collapse. A commentator reading the ratio as a welfare index would announce, at the worst moment of a generation's working life, that labour's position had dramatically improved. The absurdity is the lesson. Ratios of flows with different cyclical sensitivities cannot be read as indices of wellbeing at cyclical frequencies, and the finding this article endorses is a statement about long spans precisely because the long span averages over the cycle's optical illusions.

The tradition should recognise the trap's mechanism as one of its own discoveries in another dress. Marx's analysis of crises turns on the disproportion between the production of surplus value and its realisation, and the collapse of recorded profit in a slump is the accounting shadow of realisation failing. That the shadow moves the share upward while the substance immiserates workers is a perfect miniature of the article's whole argument: the accounting object and the theoretical object, sharing a silhouette, travelling in opposite directions at exactly the moment when confusing them does the most damage. A commentator trained by this article will check the cycle before quoting any short span, will prefer spans that begin and end at comparable points of the cycle, and will treat any dramatic movement in the share over a short period as a prompt to ask what the denominator did, since the denominator, in the short run, is usually where the drama lives.

### Why can the share rise while workers are getting poorer?

Because profits collapse faster than compensation when demand fails, the denominator of the ratio falls further than the numerator, and the proportion going to labour rises mechanically at the very moment when employment, hours, and security are deteriorating, which is why the finding must be stated over long spans rather than cyclical episodes.

Crisis accounting has a second lesson, about the financial sector's imputed output, whose tendency to swell as spreads widen in a panic was noted earlier. In a crisis centred on finance, the accounts can simultaneously record rising financial output, a rising compensation share, and collapsing profits, a trio of movements each defensible within the conventions and jointly describing an economy in catastrophe as though it were undergoing a redistribution toward labour and toward the very sector at the catastrophe's heart. No single convention is to blame. The episode simply marks the limit of what flow ratios, however constructed, can say in real time, and it vindicates the article's insistence that the share's evidentiary value is long-run and structural or it is nothing.

## Pensions, deferred wages, and the quiet transformation of compensation

Inside the numerator, a slow transformation proceeded across the decades that few headline accounts mention: the changing composition of compensation itself. The portion of the package taken as immediate cash wages shrank relative to the portion taken as employer contributions to pensions and to health provision, where such provision runs through employment. The accounts count the whole package as compensation, correctly by their lights, but the worker's experience of the package depends on its composition: contributions to a pension are deferred wages, real but inaccessible, and their value depends on the performance of funds and the solvency of promises decades ahead, while employer health contributions in systems where care is employment-linked purchase a necessity whose price inflated faster than almost any other component of the consumption basket. Two workforces with identical recorded compensation can therefore be in quite different positions, one holding cash and the other holding claims and coverage whose real yield is uncertain, and a period in which compensation's share held steadier than cash wages would be a period in which the transformation, not any bargain, did the holding.

The pension component braids back into the property side in a way that closes a loop in this article's argument. Deferred wages, gathered into funds, become owners of capital on a vast scale, and the property income those funds receive is recorded as accruing to households, swelling the very side of the accounts that the labour share is set against. The worker as employee feeds the numerator; the worker as future pensioner feeds the denominator's remainder; and the aggregate ratio, summing both, describes an economy in which labour's deferred portion has become one of capital's principal sources. Nothing in that description is false, and nothing in it captures the relations of control, since the funds' beneficiaries neither direct the investments nor set the terms on which their own savings confront them as an alien power in the market. The tradition has a phrase for wealth that confronts its producers as an alien power, and the pension system is among its purest modern instances. The accounts, characteristically, record the flows and are silent about the confrontation, and the silence is not their failure but their nature.

## Errors in the classroom and the newsroom: a field guide to the misreadings

The misreadings this article exists to correct recur in predictable shapes, and naming the shapes gives the reader a checklist for the next encounter. The first shape is the identity error in its naked form, the share quoted as the rate of exploitation, already dissected. The second is the magnitude fetish, in which a precise figure for the fall is quoted from one construction as though it were the fall itself, and rebutted by a rival precise figure from another construction, in a duel of decimals where both duelists are armed and neither is wrong enough to lose. The third is the perimeter switch, in which a whole-economy figure and a corporate-sector figure are traded as though they measured one territory. The fourth is the housing blindness, in which the rise of property's portion is narrated as a rise in returns to capital in production without the decomposition being mentioned. The fifth is the summit blindness, the mirror failure, in which compensation is discussed as though its fastest-growing component were not the pay of those who direct firms. The sixth is the cyclical quotation, the share brandished across a slump or a boom. The seventh is the level and share shuffle, in which a rising level of real pay is offered to refute a falling share, or a falling share to refute a rising level, as though one fact could annul another fact about a different question.

Each shape has a tell. The identity error says exploitation where its evidence says income. The magnitude fetish quotes decimals without constructions. The perimeter switch never names its territory. Housing blindness never mentions shelter. Summit blindness never mentions executives. Cyclical quotation chooses its endpoints at a trough and a peak. The level and share shuffle answers a proportion with a quantity. A reader who learns the tells can diagnose most public uses of this evidence in the time it takes to read them, and the diagnosis is usually more valuable than a rebuttal, since it shows the audience where the trick is rather than merely asserting that a trick occurred. Teachers, in particular, will find the checklist a serviceable syllabus of errors for a seminar, each shape illustrated by specimens that the public record supplies in abundance and that students can be sent to hunt for themselves.

### Are national accounts categories theory-free?

No: the boundary of production, the imputation of rents and of financial output, the classification of persons by contract rather than by function, and the valuation of public output at cost are all decisions embodying judgements about what the economy is, made for the honourable purpose of managing and comparing economies rather than for the purposes of any theory of value.

Underlying all seven shapes is the eighth and deepest misreading, the assumption that the categories of the accounts are a neutral grid laid over economic life, so that quantities built from them inherit the neutrality. The assumption is false in the way this article has documented from five directions, and its falsity is not an accusation against the statisticians, whose categories serve their stated purposes well. It is an accusation against a habit of mind, the habit of treating the most available description of the economy as the economy's own description of itself. The Marxist tradition, of all traditions, should be immune to that habit, since its founding gesture was to treat the categories of political economy, wages, profit, rent themselves, as historical forms to be interrogated rather than as nature to be quoted. That the tradition's commentary on the labour share so often quotes the grid as nature is the irony this article has been circling from its first page, and naming the irony plainly is perhaps its most useful service after the exploitation distinction itself.

## The verdict, restated with its defeater attached

The article's findings can now be gathered, and gathering them means stating each at the level its evidence supports, neither higher nor lower. The decline in the portion of recorded income going to compensation, throughout the advanced world over the decades since the postwar settlement waned, is robust in direction under every defensible construction in the family, and that is a strong claim entitled to confidence. The size of the decline is construction-dependent within a wide band, and any precise magnitude quoted without its construction is an overclaim. A substantial component of the mirror rise in property's portion is housing, a rise in the recorded price of shelter and in the scarcity value of land, so the headline in its popular form substantially misdescribes its own subject. The decline for the broad body of employees, with the summit of pay held apart, is larger than the aggregate shows, so the tradition's underlying intuition about ordinary labour's relative position is better supported by the disaggregated evidence than by the figure it usually quotes. And the labour share, at every level of confidence, is not a reading of exploitation, does not measure it, and cannot be converted into it without a translation that must be built, stated, and defended, as the standard translation study built and stated it.

The defeater stands where the article placed it, attached to the verdict rather than buried under it. Should a construction appear that can be defended on its own terms, consistently applied, and that eliminates the decline in most advanced economies, the robustness judgement falls and should be allowed to fall by everyone who has quoted it. The housing construction travelled part of that road for one component. No construction has travelled it for the whole. Until one does, the verdict stands in its qualified form, and the qualifications are not decorations on the verdict but part of its content, the part that distinguishes a finding from a slogan.

## Ricardo's question and the classical inheritance

The classical economists placed the division of the product at the centre of their science with a frankness the twentieth century abandoned and the twenty-first has had to relearn. For Ricardo, the laws regulating the distribution of the produce among rent, profit, and wages were the principal problem of political economy, and his system made the division turn on the price of subsistence and the diminishing returns of land, so that accumulation itself pressed wages toward their floor and profits toward their grave unless rent absorbed the difference first. The machinery differs utterly from anything in the modern accounts, and the categories are not the accounts' categories, yet the intellectual posture is the one this article has been recommending: the division is the question, the division is historically determined, and the division is where the science earns its keep. Marx took the posture and rebuilt the machinery, relocating the division's determination from the soil to the working day, and the rate of surplus value was his instrument for thinking it. The modern share literature, for all its econometric sophistication, is the classical question returning through the statistical office, and it is best read as that: not as a rival to the classical inquiry but as its continuation under conditions where the state counts what the classical writers could only reason about.

The continuation is imperfect in a way the classical writers would have spotted instantly. Their division was among classes defined by their relation to production: landlords, capitalists, labourers, each receiving a distinct revenue form. The accounts' division is among income types recorded under conventions that scramble the classes, as this article has shown from the summit of compensation to the imputed rents of owners. A Ricardian handed the modern aggregate would ask, before anything else, who exactly receives each portion, and the question would dismantle the aggregate into the populations this article has been separating all along. There is a sense, then, in which the oldest posture in the discipline is also the most critical instrument available for its newest statistics, and the student who reads the classicals alongside the accounts acquires the double vision, categories as theory and categories as bookkeeping, that the whole subject demands.

### What did the classical economists get right about distribution?

They placed the division of the product among classes at the centre of the science and treated it as historically determined by institutions, subsistence, and accumulation, a posture the modern literature has had to recover after a period in which growth theory treated the division as a technological constant requiring no explanation.

That double vision also protects against a romantic error in the opposite direction, the supposition that because the classicals asked the right question their answers can be revived without the statistical discipline they lacked. Their magnitudes were conjectures dressed as deductions, and their confidence about the wage floor or the falling tendency of profit rested on mechanisms asserted rather than measured. The modern literature's constructions, for all their conventionality, are answerable to records in a way the classical magnitudes never were. The mature position holds both inheritances at once: the classical insistence that distribution is the question and is historical, and the modern insistence that claims about it carry their constructions and survive their decompositions. The labour share, read in that double light, is neither the constant of the growth theorists nor the transcript of exploitation of the pamphlets. It is a historically moving division of a constructed total, robust in its direction, eloquent about the era's rents and summits once decomposed, and silent, honourably silent, about how the working day is divided.

## Technology, tasks, and the micro-foundations of the substitution story

The substitution explanation, that cheaper capital goods induced firms to replace labour with equipment, acquires texture when it is followed down to the level of tasks, where a more granular literature has been working. Production, at that level, is a bundle of tasks, some performed by workers and some by machines, and technical change proceeds task by task, displacing labour from the tasks machines learn to perform while creating new tasks in which labour retains its advantage, in design, supervision, repair, care, and the thousand forms of judgement that resist codification. The aggregate share then depends on the race between displacement and reinstatement, between the tasks lost and the tasks created, and on the division struck within the tasks that remain. That framework, associated with work by Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo on automation and the task content of production, explains features of the record that the aggregate substitution story handles awkwardly, including the coexistence of falling shares with persistent employment, since displaced workers crowd into remaining tasks and press their wages rather than vanishing from the numerator.

For the measurement argument, the task literature's value is that it relocates the phenomenon once more, from the division inside a representative firm to the changing boundary between human and machine activity across the whole task space. A reader armed with it will ask of any share movement not only the treatment questions but the task questions: which tasks were automated in the period, which were created, and how did the division change inside the tasks that persisted? The answers are harder to construct than the aggregate and correspondingly more informative, and the pattern of this article repeats at the finer grain, since the classification of tasks, like the classification of incomes, is a construction with contestable joints. There is no floor of fact beneath the constructions at which interpretation stops. There are only finer and coarser constructions, and the discipline of choosing the grain that matches the claim.

## Bargaining inside the firm: rent sharing and the wage curve of the mind

Between the aggregate and the task lies the firm, and inside the firm lies a body of evidence that gives the bargaining explanation its microeconomic flesh: the literature on rent sharing, which asks how the surplus a firm generates is divided between its owners and its workforce. The consistent finding of that literature, built from matched records of firms and their employees, is that wages inside a firm rise with the firm's prosperity in ways that identical workers moving between firms reveal, so that a substantial part of what a worker earns is a portion of that particular firm's rents rather than a price for the worker's labour set in a general market. Where a worker happens to be employed thus enters the wage itself, and the aggregate share becomes, in part, a weighted sum of thousands of internal divisions, each settled by the customs, scarcities, and power relations of a particular workplace. The decline of the aggregate can then proceed through the internal divisions souring, through employment migrating toward firms whose internal divisions are meaner, or through the rents themselves migrating toward firms structured to share little of them, and the decomposition evidence suggests all three channels operated.

Rent sharing also dissolves a puzzle in the top-incomes story, because the summit's pay is rent sharing in its purest form: executives of firms swollen with rents from market position, intangibles, or financial engineering divide those rents with the owners, and their compensation is the visible tip of the general process by which firm-level surplus is shared with the strategically placed. The ordinary worker's rent share depends on scarcity, organisation, and the firm's fear of disruption; the executive's depends on proximity to the division itself. One process, two positions, and an aggregate compensation figure that sums them into a fiction of common fortune. The tradition's language of bargaining between classes, applied firm by firm and disciplined by the matched-record evidence, turns out to describe the mechanism the mainstream literature independently found, another convergence that rewards the reader who holds both literatures at once.

### What does rent sharing show about how wages are set?

That a substantial part of a worker's pay is a share of the particular firm's surplus rather than a universal price for labour, since identical workers earn differently in firms of different prosperity, so the aggregate division is built from thousands of internal bargains whose terms depend on scarcity, organisation, and position within the firm.

The wage curve literature adds a geographical echo: across regions and decades, wages respond to the tightness of local labour markets with a regularity that generations of researchers have rediscovered, so that the division inside firms breathes with the reserve of unemployed labour standing outside them. Marx's reserve army, translated into an elasticity, is recognisable in that finding, and the recognition is worth more than a polemical point. It shows that the mechanisms the tradition named at the level of the system reappear, measurable and modest, at the level of the local labour market, provided the claims are pitched where the measurements can support them. Pitch is everything, in this literature as in the share's. The reserve army as a measurable pressure on local wages is a finding. The reserve army as a number read off a national ratio is a costume.

## The care economy's revenge and the demographic horizon

Project the article's themes forward, not as prediction but as structure, and the demographic horizon rearranges them. The advanced economies face rising dependency, more retired people sustained by each working cohort, and the labour of care, for the old above all, expands as a share of all work. Care is labour-intensive by nature, resistant to the productivity growth that manufacturing and logistics achieved, and largely provided through the state or through low-paid market services. Its expansion presses on the measure from several directions at once: public care valued at cost raises the recorded share, market care at low wages lowers the compensation per unit of output in the expanding sector, and the fiscal cost of the retired population's claims grows against a denominator whose growth care's low productivity restrains. The famous cost disease of services, in which labour-intensive activities grow relatively more expensive as productivity elsewhere rises, becomes a distributional engine in its own right, and the shares of the coming decades will be argued over with the same instruments this article has audited, by readers who will need the treatment table as much as the present generation does.

The structural discipline imposed on this article, writing about mechanisms rather than moments, is vindicated by that prospect. The specific controversies of any decade date quickly; the construction of the measure, the wedges between the accounting ratio and the theoretical one, and the decompositions that relocate the phenomenon do not date at all, because they describe how the instrument works rather than what it last read. A reader ten years hence, confronting a labour share argument framed around care, demography, or some mechanism not yet named, will find the instrument unchanged in its essentials: mixed income to split, housing to separate, depreciation to deduct, summits to hold apart, perimeters to declare. That durability is the reason this article states no figure for the measure it explains. The figure would expire. The explanation is the finding's permanent part.

### Will the labour share argument matter to future readers?

Yes, because the instrument's construction does not date even as its readings change: the treatment decisions, the wedges separating the accounting ratio from the theoretical one, and the decompositions that relocate phenomena will govern any future controversy built on the measure, whatever mechanism that controversy names.

There is a final service the long horizon performs for the present argument. It reveals the finding's true timescale. A movement measured in decades, driven by the slow variables of demography, tenure, technology's task boundary, and institutional sediment, belongs to the class of phenomena that political economy exists to study and that electoral cycles exist to ignore. The tradition's own timescale was always the long one; its founder thought in modes of production and their transformations, and was at his weakest when hurried into commentary on the season. The labour share, properly handled, is an invitation back to that timescale, an evidence base for thinking about the era rather than the episode, and the invitation is perhaps the most valuable thing the measure offers the tradition that keeps mishandling it.

## Skill, credentials, and the division inside compensation

The interior of compensation holds one more division that the aggregate conceals and that the period rearranged: the division among workers themselves by skill, credential, and occupation. Returns to education rose across the decades in many economies, the premium commanded by graduates widened, and within occupations a polarisation proceeded in which the middle of the distribution, the routine clerical and production roles that once anchored broad prosperity, thinned relative to both the summit of abstract professional work and the base of manual service work that automation could not reach. The aggregate share sums across that rearrangement without registering it, so two economies with identical shares can house working populations whose internal geometries of reward are entirely different, one compressed and one stretched, with different politics growing in each. The tradition's category of the working class as a unity is a political project laid over an economic diversity, and the share, which presupposes the unity in its numerator, is a treacherous guide to the diversity's actual movement.

Credential inflation complicates the picture in a way that measurement again decides. Where employers demand degrees for work whose content has not changed, the graduate premium partly records a screening device rather than a productivity difference, and the compensation recorded against credentialed labour rises for reasons located in the sociology of hiring rather than in the production of value. The accounts cannot see the difference, since a wage is a wage, and the theory, which distinguishes the value of labour power by the costs of its training, sees part of it: training costs genuinely enter the value of skilled labour power, so some premium is, in the theory's own terms, a higher value being paid for rather than a greater exploitation being resisted. How much of the observed premium is training cost, how much is scarcity rent on credentials, and how much is the bargaining power of organised professions are questions the aggregate cannot approach and decomposition by occupation can, at least in part. The pattern holds to the end: every interesting question about this evidence lives below the aggregate, and the aggregate's function is to tell the reader that something below it is worth the excavation.

### Does a widening graduate premium show that skill defeated exploitation?

No: the premium divides workers among themselves, mixing genuine training costs, scarcity rents on credentials used as hiring screens, and the bargaining strength of organised professions, while the division between labour as a whole and property proceeds on a different axis that the premium does not measure at all.

Polarisation carries a spatial signature that connects it back to housing. The abstract professional work that prospered concentrated in a small number of metropolitan labour markets, bidding up the price of shelter in those places, while the manual service work that grew was needed exactly where the professionals lived, so the geography of the new division and the geography of the rent explosion are the same geography. The worker who cleans, delivers, and cares in the prosperous city pays its rents out of a service wage, while the professional whose pay the city's economy swells captures the location's advantages and, if an owner, its imputed rents as well. The labour share of the nation sums the city and the left-behind region into one ratio; the lived distribution is the city's internal chasm plus the region's stagnation, and the sum describes neither. Decomposition by place belongs on the list of excavations the aggregate demands, and its results, in the regional literatures, are among the most politically legible the field has produced.

## Minimum wages, monopsony, and the floor of the division

At the bottom of the distribution, a literature on the employer's market power has rearranged an old debate in ways that bear directly on how the share's floor is set. Where employers face little competition for workers, because a town has one large employer, because search is costly, because schedules and commutes bind workers to particular firms, the wage can be held below the value of the worker's contribution without the worker's departure correcting it, a condition the literature calls monopsony. The revival of monopsony as a serious framework changed the interpretation of minimum wages, since a floor imposed on a monopsonised market can raise both pay and employment, and it changed the interpretation of the share's lower reaches, since part of what the aggregate records at the bottom is not a market price for labour but a markdown imposed by employer power. The markdown is, in the theory's vocabulary, a form of the wage's deviation below the value of labour power under conditions of unequal force, and the newer literature's achievement is to have made the deviation measurable, market by market, with the usual construction caveats attending the estimates of the gap between wage and marginal product.

The connection to the aggregate is direct. If the bottom of the labour market is monopsonised, then institutional changes that weaken workers' outside options, the thinning of benefits, the growth of scheduling practices that bind workers to single employers, the decline of alternative employment in a region, all press the share downward through the markdown channel, inside industries and inside firms, without any change in technology or in the formal bargaining structure. The institutional explanation of the decline thereby gains a mechanism that operates even where unions were never present, which helps it cover the economies and sectors the pure bargaining story strained to reach. And the measurement moral repeats: the markdown estimates depend on constructions of the marginal product that no ledger records, so the channel's size is contested in the familiar way, while its existence, established by the behaviour of wages and employment around imposed floors, is among the sturdier findings in the whole territory this article has surveyed.

## The shareholder turn and the destination of the surplus

Where did the portion that compensation lost actually go, at the level of the firm's decisions? One influential answer follows the money through the corporate sector's changing habits: across the period, the distribution of corporate cash toward shareholders, through dividends and above all through the repurchase of the firm's own shares, grew relative to investment in plant, research, and the workforce, a reorientation associated with the doctrine that the firm exists to maximise shareholder value and with the tying of executive pay to the share price. The connection to this article's themes is tighter than it may look. Executive compensation tied to the share price is the mechanism that inflated the summit inside the numerator; buybacks are a destination of the surplus that competes with the wage bill inside the firm; and the doctrine that legitimises both is an institutional fact of exactly the kind the bargaining explanation invokes. The share's decline, on this account, has a boardroom address, and the account has the virtue of naming a decision where the aggregate names only a drift.

The measurement cautions apply with their usual force. Repurchases are not income in the accounts, being transactions in assets rather than flows of value added, so their growth does not appear in the share's arithmetic directly; the share records the surplus from which repurchases are funded, not the funding. A commentator who quotes buyback totals and share declines in one breath as though they were two readings of one flow repeats the family error of the whole subject, the confusion of connected magnitudes. The correct statement is layered: value added's division shifted, the uses of the profit portion shifted with it toward shareholder distribution, and the two shifts are chapters of one institutional transformation, legible only to a reader who keeps the layers distinct. Layering, like decomposition, is a form of respect for the evidence, and its absence is the common thread in every misreading this article has catalogued.

### Did shareholder payouts take the portion that wages lost?

Not in the accounting sense, since repurchases are asset transactions outside the flow of income the share divides, but the same institutional turn that raised payouts also tied executive pay to share prices and subordinated the wage bill within the firm, so the two movements are chapters of one transformation rather than one flow.

There is a historical rhyme here that the tradition will savour. The joint-stock company, in Marx's own treatment, was already a form in which the functioning capitalist and the owner divide the surplus between them, with the manager's salary separated from entrepreneurial profit, and he remarked the tendency of that separation to disguise the surplus's origin, presenting profit as the fruit of ownership alone and the manager's large salary as a wage like any other. The modern summit's compensation, swollen by equity and recorded as labour income, is that disguise perfected: a claim on surplus value wearing the costume of the wage so completely that the national accounts, the costume's unwitting tailors, file it under labour. The theory saw the costume being sewn. The commentary that quotes the accounts without remembering the sewing is not betrayed by the statistics. It is betrayed by its own inattention to its founding text.

## What the decline meant for demand: the macroeconomic afterlife of a share

A distribution is not only a division of spoils; it is a setting of the economy's machinery, and the labour share's decline has a macroeconomic afterlife that a section should trace, because it explains why central banks and finance ministries, institutions with no brief for class analysis, came to study the measure with an intensity that surprised its older students. Wage income is spent at a higher rate than property income, whose recipients save more at the margin, so a shift in the division toward property is a shift toward saving and away from consumption demand, other things equal. An economy undergoing such a shift must find its demand elsewhere: in investment, in exports, in public deficits, or in household borrowing against rising asset values, and the decades of the decline were in fact decades in which different advanced economies leaned on different substitutes, export surpluses in some, housing credit in others, public borrowing in still others. The financial crisis that interrupted the period has been read, by a literature spanning several traditions, as the moment one of those substitutes, household debt collateralised by dwellings, exhausted itself, and that reading binds the housing theme of this article to the demand theme in a single knot: the same shelter whose imputed rents swelled property's share was also the collateral whose inflation sustained the demand that wages no longer could.

The Marxist tradition possesses an old vocabulary for this configuration, the vocabulary of underconsumption and of realisation, and the modern literature's rediscovery of demand shortfalls rooted in distribution has been greeted inside the tradition with a mixture of vindication and exasperation. The measurement discipline applies here as everywhere: the claim that the share's decline weakened demand is a claim about propensities and magnitudes that the share alone cannot establish, requiring evidence about saving behaviour by income source and about the substitutes that filled the gap. But the direction of the inquiry is sound, and it illustrates the final dignity of the measure when it is handled correctly. A well-audited distributional fact becomes a load-bearing element in a macroeconomic argument, connecting the wage bargain to the credit system to the crisis, and the connections are the kind of thing political economy, classical, Marxist, or Keynesian, exists to trace. Handled badly, quoted bare and identified with exploitation, the same fact is a slogan. The difference is never in the fact. It is in the handling, and handling is a choice renewed with every use.

### Does a falling labour share weaken economic demand?

It tends to, because wage income is spent at higher rates than property income, so the shift toward property raises saving relative to consumption and forces demand to be sustained by investment, exports, public borrowing, or household debt, substitutes whose exhaustion has been linked in several literatures to financial fragility.

The demand connection also returns the argument to the state, completing a circuit. Where household borrowing and export surpluses could not or would not carry demand, public deficits did, and the fiscal position of the state became a distributional instrument of the first order, its deficits sustaining the incomes its tax and benefit systems then redistributed. The labour share, which cannot see the social wage, also cannot see this fiscal mediation, and an era's true distributional settlement is written across the wage bargain, the tax code, the benefit system, the credit system, and the housing market together. The measure this article has anatomised captures one line of that settlement with reasonable fidelity. The error is never in consulting the line. The error is in mistaking the line for the document.

## Unions, coverage, and the difference between membership and reach

The institutional explanation leans heavily on organised labour's decline, and that decline itself is a measurement object with its own constructions, worth a section because the distinction it turns on, between membership and coverage, is a perfect miniature of this article's method. Union membership counts those who belong; collective bargaining coverage counts those whose terms are set by an agreement, a much larger population in economies where agreements are extended across sectors by law or custom. The two series tell different stories about labour's institutional strength: membership thinned almost everywhere, while coverage held firm in some economies and collapsed in others, and the economies where coverage held are, broadly, economies where the within-industry division moved least against compensation. A researcher quoting membership where coverage is the operative variable mismeasures the institution exactly as a researcher quoting gross where net is operative mismeasures the share, and for the same reason: the construction nearest to hand is mistaken for the construction the question requires.

The distinction also refines the causal story. What protects the division inside an industry is not the card in the worker's pocket but the agreement over the worker's terms, and agreements can be sustained by extension law, by employer associations that prefer uniform terms, and by the credible residue of organisation long after density has faded. Institutional strength is thus partly a legal sediment, persisting in coverage statistics after the movement that built it has ebbed, and the share's behaviour in such economies records the sediment's protection. When the sediment itself is reformed away, the division moves with a lag and then with a rush, a pattern visible in the comparative record and unintelligible to any account that watches membership alone. Measurement, once again, decides which history the data appear to tell.

### Is union membership the right measure of labour's institutional strength?

No: coverage by collective agreements is the operative variable for the division of income, since agreements set terms for many workers who are not members, and coverage has held in some economies where membership thinned, with the division inside industries moving least where coverage endured.

For the tradition, the membership and coverage distinction carries an organisational moral that mirrors the statistical one. Movements count their members as the accounts count compensation, and the count, in both cases, is a coalition of unlike elements whose headline movement can mislead: rising membership in sectors without agreements may add nothing to bargaining reach, as rising executive pay adds nothing to the position of employees at large. The question that matters, in the movement as in the measure, is not the size of the aggregate but its composition and its purchase on the division of the product. A tradition schooled by its own statistics to ask that question of every total will ask it of itself, and be the stronger for the habit.

## The platform reclassification and the future of mixed income

Return once more to the self-employed, because the direction of travel in the organisation of work promises to make the first row of the treatment table more consequential, not less. The legal boundary between employment and self-employment is being contested and redrawn across the advanced economies, through litigation over the status of platform-mediated work, through legislative experiments that create intermediate categories, and through the quiet spread of contractual forms that place risk on the worker while reserving control to the firm. Each redrawing moves populations between compensation and mixed income, and between mixed income and the purchased services of unincorporated suppliers, so the measured share in the coming decades will register legal and organisational reclassification as surely as it registers any movement in the underlying division. A fall in the share may announce that employers have won a new legal costume for an old relation. A rise may announce that courts have stripped the costume off. Neither movement is an artefact in the dismissive sense; reclassification changes real obligations, real risks, and real incomes. But neither movement is a change in the rate of exploitation in itself, and the reader who has followed this article will know to ask, of any future movement, how much is division and how much is costume.

The theoretical classification, by substance rather than costume, becomes more valuable as the costumes multiply, and the translation literature's insistence on classifying by function reads, in this light, less like scholasticism and more like foresight. Statistical offices will go on classifying by legal form, because legal form is what their administrative sources record, and the gap between the recorded form and the economic substance will widen or narrow with the politics of classification itself. There is even a sense in which the politics of classification is now one of the arenas of the distributional struggle the share purports to measure: to be classified as an employee is to be inside compensation, inside collective coverage, inside the employer's social contributions, and the struggle over classification is a struggle over the division conducted in the vocabulary of law. The measure records the struggle's outcomes without naming it as a struggle, and the reader who names it has understood the measure better than the measure understands itself.

## Econometric quarrels worth understanding: elasticities and identification

Beneath the explanatory contest sits a technical quarrel that a serious reader should understand in outline, because it determines how much of the contest the evidence can settle. The substitution explanation requires knowing how readily firms replace workers with capital when relative prices change, a parameter called the elasticity of substitution, and that parameter is not directly observable. It must be inferred from the joint behaviour of shares, prices, and quantities, and the inference is entangled with the very movements it is meant to explain: a falling share alongside a rising capital stock is consistent with an elasticity above one and a substitution story, and also with an elasticity below one combined with a falling relative price of labour's services driven by bargaining weakness, and separating the two requires assumptions about the direction of technical change that the data alone will not supply. Different research teams, imposing different and individually defensible assumptions, have extracted different elasticities from substantially the same record, and the explanatory contest inherits the indeterminacy. This is measurement dominance at the level of parameters: the quantity that would settle the argument is itself a construction, and its construction smuggles in a portion of the conclusion.

Identification, the econometrician's name for the problem of isolating one mechanism among entangled ones, haunts every channel in the explanatory literature. Bargaining weakness and global integration advanced together; the price of capital goods fell while institutions were dismantled; intangibles grew while markups, perhaps, rose. Natural experiments, episodes in which one channel moved without the others, are the literature's prized possessions precisely because they are rare: a legal reform in one jurisdiction, a sudden opening to trade, a technological shock confined to one industry. The findings built on such episodes deserve the extra weight the literature gives them, and the reader's heuristic follows the pattern of this whole article: trust the claim in proportion to the transparency of its construction, whether the construction is an accounting identity, a decomposition, or an identification strategy. The forms differ; the virtue is one.

### Why can the same record support rival explanations of the decline?

Because the parameter that would discriminate among them, the ease with which firms substitute capital for labour, must itself be inferred under assumptions about technical change, and rival defensible assumptions extract rival values from the same data, leaving the contest to be settled by episodes where one mechanism moved alone.

None of this licenses despair about explanation. It licenses a ranking. Explanations anchored in decompositions and in episodes where a single mechanism moved stand higher than explanations anchored in the aggregate's silhouette. The housing account stands high because its mechanism is visible in the accounts' own components. The bargaining account stands high where it is sectorally specific. The substitution account stands as a powerful frame whose central parameter remains under construction. A literature is not disgraced by containing such a ranking; it is disgraced only when its popularisers flatten the ranking into a single certainty, and the flattening, as ever, is a choice made by writers rather than a property of the evidence.

## The wage share and the profit share in socialist calculation: a cautionary mirror

The measurement problems of this article are not capitalism's private property, and a glance at their mirror image in the calculation debates of planned economies sharpens the general lesson. Where prices are administered rather than formed in markets, the division of recorded income among wages, enterprise surpluses, and turnover taxes reflects the planners' price decisions as much as any underlying relation, and reconstructions of those economies' growth and distribution have had to rebuild the accounts from physical and archival materials before any share could be interpreted at all, the exercise this series met in its discussion of socialist growth measurement, one of the earlier instances of the measurement dominance pattern. The parallel is instructive because it removes any temptation to treat construction-sensitivity as a defect of bourgeois statistics in particular. Every system of accounts embodies the categories of the order that keeps it, and every translation between a system's accounts and a theory's categories is labour that must be done openly or not at all. The Soviet accountant and the modern statistical office are in the same epistemic boat, and the Marxist analyst who mocks the one's conventions while quoting the other's as nature has understood neither.

The mirror also disciplines nostalgia in the opposite direction. Comparisons sometimes drawn between the labour share in a market economy and the wage share recorded in a planned economy, as though the two ratios were readings of one instrument pointed at two systems, commit every error in this article's catalogue at once: different price formation, different production boundaries, different treatment of housing and of the social wage, different populations inside compensation. Such comparisons are not bold syntheses. They are the perimeter switch performed across civilisations, and they collapse on contact with the treatment table, which is perhaps the table's most satisfying application.

### Can labour shares be compared across economic systems?

Only after a translation at least as thorough as the one required within a single system, since price formation, the production boundary, the treatment of housing, and the composition of compensation all differ with the system itself, and an untranslated comparison measures the difference between accounting regimes rather than between distributions.

## Distribution and the rate of profit: keeping two famous declines apart

Public argument frequently welds the falling labour share to another famous magnitude, the rate of profit, treating the two as a single syndrome of the era. The weld should be broken, because the quantities are distinct in construction and can travel separately. The share divides income between compensation and the rest within a period. The profit rate divides profit by the capital stock advanced to earn it, a stock whose valuation, at historical cost, at replacement cost, gross or net of depreciation, with housing and intangibles inside or outside, reproduces every construction problem in this article and adds several of its own. A rising property share is compatible with a falling profit rate if the capital stock grows faster than the surplus, the configuration the tradition's own theory of accumulation predicts under mechanisation, and it is compatible with a rising profit rate if the stock grows slowly or is written down. The era's evidence, in the constructions that attempt the profit rate seriously, is correspondingly mixed in a way the share's evidence is not, and commentary that borrows the share's robustness to lend confidence to a profit rate claim is engaged in evidentiary smuggling across a border this article has spent its length policing. Each quantity must carry its own construction, face its own decompositions, and state its own defeater, and the reader who enforces that separation will find many famous syndromes dissolving into their components, some sturdy and some frail, which is what an evidence base is supposed to look like.

## The philosophical core: what kind of object is a share?

Underneath the technique sits a philosophical question that the tradition, with its long meditation on the forms of appearance of economic relations, is well placed to answer, and a section should pose it plainly. A share is a form of appearance. It presents the social product's division in the medium of money income, at prices, under categories drawn for administration, and like every form of appearance in Marx's analysis it both reveals and conceals the relations that produce it. It reveals them genuinely: the division it records is a real division, money really changed hands in those proportions, and the proportions really shifted. It conceals them structurally: the working day's division, the distinction between producing surplus value and being sustained by it, the rents inside the property total and the surplus claims inside the wage total, all vanish into the form's smooth surface. The error pursued through this article is, in the tradition's own language, a fetishism of the statistic: the treatment of a form of appearance as though it were the relation itself, with the form's social and conventional origins forgotten. Naming the error in that language is not decoration. It locates the mistake inside the theory's own diagnostic scheme, where it becomes available for the theory's self-criticism, which is where this article has always intended it to land.

The positive doctrine follows. Forms of appearance are not to be despised or discarded; they are to be read, with their genesis kept in view, as the surface on which underlying relations necessarily appear and without which those relations cannot be studied at all. The labour share is exactly such a surface for the distributional relations of the period: indispensable, insufficient, and dangerous only when its insufficiency is forgotten. A science of the surface, which is what the decomposition literature is, and a theory of what the surface conceals, which is what the literature of translation is, are the two halves of a complete engagement with the evidence, and the tradition's failure has been to practice the halves in separate rooms, quoting the surface in its polemics and guarding the theory in its seminars. This article has tried to hold one conversation in one room, and its length is the price of the attempt.

### Is a labour share a fact or a construction?

It is a constructed fact: the division it records really occurred in money under the stated categories, and the categories, the imputations, and the boundary are human decisions, so the quantity is neither a fiction to be dismissed nor a natural magnitude to be quoted without its construction attached.

That formulation, a constructed fact, may serve as the article's epitaph for the whole subject. It refuses the two lazy positions between which public argument oscillates, the positivism that treats the statistic as nature and the relativism that treats it as rhetoric, and it assigns the reader the only stance compatible with the evidence: disciplined trust, extended exactly as far as the construction warrants, withdrawn exactly where the construction ends. The labour share deserves that trust in its direction, deserves it qualified in its magnitude, deserves none of it as a measure of exploitation, and deserves, above all, readers who know the difference. Producing such readers is a slower politics than producing slogans. It is also the only one whose gains survive cross-examination.

## Reading a single country's story without being captured by it

The comparative breadth of the decline is its strength, yet every reader lives in one economy, and the pull of the national story, its particular institutions, episodes, and villains, is constant. A section on reading the national case is therefore a practical necessity, and its first rule is that the national series must be rebuilt through the treatment table before it is narrated. The national statistical office's headline series embodies choices on every row, some of them different from the choices in the harmonised international series, and the differences are not pedantry: a national debate conducted on the domestic series and an international comparison conducted on the harmonised one can appear to contradict each other while both are internally sound, and partisans of each will accuse the other of fabrication when the true charge is untranslated comparison. The reader's task is to know which series is on the table, what its rows are set to, and how far its settings differ from the settings behind any figure it is being compared with.

The second rule concerns episodes. National stories love a hinge, a government, a reform, a crisis, after which everything changed, and the share's long grind is often narrated as a sequence of such hinges. Some hinges are real in the data, visible as accelerations when the series is decomposed and the institutional record is laid beside it. Others are the eye's habit of finding breaks in any long series, and the discipline of the decomposition literature, which asks whether the movement at the supposed hinge is within the industry's division or a composition effect of the episode's sectoral shocks, is the test that separates them. A reform that closes an industry will move the aggregate by composition even if it touches no wage bargain anywhere, and narrating that movement as a defeat for labour in general confuses the closing of a sector with the squeezing of a workforce, different events with different meanings for the people involved, who deserve the accuracy.

### How should a reader approach one economy's labour share story?

By rebuilding the national series through the treatment decisions before narrating it, since the domestic headline embodies its own settings on every row, and by testing each supposed historical hinge against the decomposition, separating movements in the division inside industries from composition effects of the episode itself.

The third rule is about the uses of pride and shame. National shares are recruited into stories of national virtue and national decline, the thrifty economy whose workers are rewarded, the decadent economy whose rentiers feast, and the recruitment is almost always an abuse, since the level of a share, the least robust element of the whole apparatus, is what such stories require. Levels differ across economies for reasons thick with construction: tenure patterns feeding imputed rent, the size of the state valued at cost, the prevalence and composition of self-employment, the weight of finance with its imputations. A league table of levels is a league table of those constructions wearing flags. The trend within an economy, aligned and decomposed, is evidence. The level across economies is a costume party, and the reader trained by this article will decline the invitation to rank nations by it.

## The household as the unit the share cannot see

One last boundary deserves inspection, the boundary of the unit to which income is attributed. The accounts build the share from the side of production, dividing the product among income types, but the distributional questions that move politics are usually about households, about how income pools across earners, non-earners, and generations under one roof, and the two perspectives have drifted apart as household structure changed. The rise of dual-earner households means that household income can hold while individual compensation weakens; the growth of single-person households means the opposite translation in other strata; assortative partnering, the tendency of high earners to pair with one another, concentrates household income even where individual earnings disperse less dramatically. The labour share is silent about all of it, since its numerator is a production-side total, and arguments that move from the share to conclusions about household inequality commit a quiet unit switch en route. Household inequality has its own measures and its own literature, in which the share appears, properly, as one upstream determinant among household formation, taxation, and transfers. Keeping the units straight is the same discipline as keeping the categories straight, applied at the point where the statistics meet the dinner table.

## For students and exam candidates

The student meeting this evidence in an examination on modern capitalism faces a specific temptation, which is to deploy the labour share as a ready-made proof: the share fell, therefore exploitation rose, therefore the system's character stands revealed, with a figure's authority borrowed for each step. This article has been written, in large part, to make that answer impossible to give in good conscience and to replace it with a stronger one. ## Automation's second wave and the share in the age of intelligent machines

The substitution story has acquired a new chapter with the spread of machine intelligence into cognitive work, and the chapter is worth a section because it tests whether the article's framework travels to a mechanism its sources could only glimpse. The tasks now exposed to automation include drafting, summarising, coding, translating, and elementary analysis, the abstract routine work that the previous wave of automation spared and even enriched. If the displacement proceeds faster than the creation of new tasks in which human labour holds its advantage, the task framework predicts pressure on compensation's portion from a new quarter, falling this time on the credentialed strata whose premiums the polarisation literature tracked upward. If reinstatement keeps pace, with new tasks gathering around the direction, verification, and human delivery of machine output, the pressure is absorbed as earlier waves were absorbed, at the price of transitions that the aggregate will record only as a smooth line.

The measurement discipline applies to the new chapter with undiminished force, and adds a twist of its own. The output of machine intelligence is difficult for the accounts to price where it is given away or bundled, its capital is intangible and short-lived, feeding the depreciation wedge, and its leading firms display the superstar geometry, immense value added against small workforces, in an extreme form. A future movement in the share driven by this sector will therefore arrive pre-magnified by every construction sensitivity in the table, and the commentators of that moment will need the treatment decisions, the decomposition habit, and the exploitation distinction exactly as much as the commentators of the present one. The article's refusal to date itself is, in this respect, a bet it is happy to settle: the mechanisms will change their names, and the instrument will go on requiring the same audit.

### Does automation necessarily lower the share?

No: the task framework shows displacement pressing the share down while fresh tasks in which labour holds an advantage are created to press it up, and the outcome depends on the race between them and on the division struck inside surviving tasks, which is a matter of institutions as much as of technology.

There is also a theoretical point the tradition should not miss amid the novelty. A machine that performs cognitive tasks is, in the categories of the theory, constant capital still, transferring its value and producing none, however fluent its output appears, and the surplus it helps to extract is produced by the labour it assists, directs, or displaces into other employments. The fluency is a new costume on an old relation, and the classification by substance over costume, which this article has defended for the self-employed contractor and the equity-paid executive, applies to the intelligent machine with the same force. Costume changes are the history of this subject. Substance, patiently classified, is its science.

## The environmental ledger and the shares of a constrained product

A further horizon rearranges the distributional question itself, and a section should acknowledge it because the readers of the coming decades will meet the share inside it. Where the product's growth is constrained by ecological limits, by the costs of repairing climate damage, and by the investment required to rebuild the energy basis of production, the division of the product acquires new claimants and new meanings. A rising portion of output must be devoted to replacement in the widest sense, not only of worn machinery but of damaged ecosystems and obsolete infrastructure, which swells the gross and net totals' divergence that the depreciation section examined and makes the choice of basis more consequential, not less. The labour of repair and adaptation, much of it public, much of it care for places rather than persons, will swell the categories the accounts value at cost. And the distributional conflict over who bears the transition's costs, through energy prices that function as a regressive levy, through rents on scarce green land and minerals, through the wages of the rebuilding trades, will be conducted partly in the vocabulary of shares, by parties who will need to know what the vocabulary can and cannot say.

The tradition's categories travel into this terrain with their usual mixture of power and need for translation. Rent, on land made scarce by climate and on the minerals the transition devours, promises to be as central as the housing rent this article anatomised. The distinction between producing value and living off value produced elsewhere will govern any honest accounting of an economy devoting more of its labour to repair. And the exploitation distinction will matter most of all, since the temptation to read ecological distributional conflicts directly off income ratios will be as strong, and as misleading, as the temptation resisted through the whole length of this article. The instrument endures; the audit endures with it.

## A short history of the number in public argument

The career of the labour share in public argument has its own shape, and tracing it briefly is a way of auditing the auditors. In its first public life the measure was a technocrat's quantity, at home in growth models and central bank reviews, where its rough constancy was a convenience and its movements a specialist's concern. Its second life began when the constancy failed and the decline became a finding, migrating from the journals into the reports of international institutions, where it was framed as a puzzle of globalisation and technology, distributional in content but carefully denatured in tone. Its third life, the one this article has mostly addressed, is its life as a slogan, in which the finding escaped its constructions entirely and circulated as proof of propositions its builders never advanced, above all the proposition that exploitation had been measured and found rising. Each migration stripped away a layer of qualification, as each retelling rewarded the version that travelled lightest, until the public object bearing the measure's name was a different object from the one in the literature, sharing its silhouette and little else.

The pattern is not unique to this measure, and recognising the general law is worthwhile: a quantitative finding's public career is governed by selection for portability, and portability is achieved by amputating construction. The remedy available to writers is limited but real, and this article has attempted it, which is to build the qualifications into the memorable parts of the text, the table, the verdict, the named distinction, so that what travels carries its audit with it. Whether the remedy works is an empirical question the article cannot answer from inside itself. But the alternative, publishing the naked finding and deploring its travels, has been tried by a generation of researchers and has failed in full view, and the attempt at a self-auditing text is at least a new experiment in an old problem.

### Why do findings lose their qualifications as they travel?

Because portability selects for the version with the fewest conditions attached, each retelling rewards the lighter formulation, and the construction that made the finding meaningful is precisely the weight that retelling sheds, until the public object shares the finding's name and silhouette while carrying a meaning its builders never advanced.

The historian of the future, reading the period's distributional argument, will therefore need to read it twice, once in the literature where the constructions live and once in the slogans where the politics lived, and the distance between the two readings will be among the most instructive documents the era leaves behind. It will show a civilisation that built instruments of genuine subtlety for watching its own division of the product, and then argued about the division in a vocabulary those instruments could not support. The Marxist tradition's part in that story is double, as this article has insisted throughout: the tradition supplied some of the slogans and also, in its translation literature and its theory of categories, some of the sharpest tools for dismantling them. Which of the two contributions defines the tradition's legacy is a choice its practitioners renew with every article they write. This one has made its choice.

## The regions inside the nation: a geography of the division

The national aggregate conceals a geography as thoroughly as it conceals a class structure, and the regional decomposition of the share tells a story that neither the national narrator nor the class narrator can tell alone. Capital cities and their hinterlands concentrate the headquarters, the financial activity, the intangible-rich firms, and the professional services, so their recorded value added per worker runs high and their compensation shares run low, swollen denominators being the metropolitan signature in the accounts. Regions of older industry carry the sediment of high-share employment in decline, their aggregates moved by composition as plants close and by genuine division as surviving employers face weakened workforces. Agricultural regions carry the mixed income problem in its most concentrated form, their shares hostage to the imputation rule chosen for farm households. A single national trend sums a metropolitan story about rents and intangibles, an old-industrial story about closure and bargaining, and a rural story about classification, and attributes the sum to the nation, which experienced none of the three in the summed form.

The political consequences of the geographical concealment are visible in the era's electoral geography, where regions whose experience was closure and decline developed a politics of abandonment that national distributional statistics, with their mild aggregate movements, seemed to deny. The denial was an artefact of aggregation. The region's own accounts, had they been read with the treatment table in hand, showed a distributional catastrophe of a specific kind, the disappearance of the high-share employment around which its towns were built, and the catastrophe was real even though the nation's ratio ground gently downward. Decomposition by place is thus not a refinement for specialists. It is the difference between a statistics that can hear a population's grievance and one that files it under noise, and the populist shocks of the period are partly the sound of grievances that aggregate statistics had certified as negligible.

### Why can a mild national trend conceal a regional catastrophe?

Because the national ratio sums unlike regional stories, metropolitan rents and intangibles, old-industrial closure, rural classification problems, into one gentle movement, while a region can lose the high-share employment around which its towns were built entirely, an event the aggregate registers as a minor composition effect.

The tradition's geography was always finer than its statistics, since its classical texts are alive to the town and country divide, to the reserve army's regional pools, and to the uneven development that capitalism produces as a law of its motion. Recovering that fineness with modern instruments means insisting that distributional claims be located, in place as in sector and in class, before they are believed, and the insistence generalises the passport one final time: no share without its territory, and the territory specified down to the level at which the phenomenon actually happened. A finding that survives that specification has earned its generality. A finding that dissolves under it was a sum impersonating a story.

## Trust, legitimacy, and the politics of a number believed or disbelieved

A final dimension of the measure's life deserves record, because it governs whether any of the article's discipline matters in practice: the public's trust in the number itself. Distributional statistics circulate in a climate in which official measurement is alternately revered as neutral fact and dismissed as the bookkeeping of the powerful, and the labour share has suffered both fates, quoted as gospel by those it favours and as fabrication by those it inconveniences. The construction-sensitivity documented in this article feeds the dismissal when it is misunderstood, since a public taught that the number depends on decisions may conclude that the decisions are arbitrary and the number a fraud, a conclusion the sceptical section of this article answered in the scholarly register and which must also be answered in the civic one. Decisions in measurement are like rules in a game whose fairness depends not on the rules being the only possible ones but on their being stated, stable, and applied to all sides alike. The accounts meet that standard better than most public instruments, and the finding built on them, at its robust level, deserves the civic trust that the standard earns.

Legitimacy, though, is not only a property of the instrument. It is a property of the uses, and every overclaim made with the share, every identification with exploitation, every decimal quoted without its construction, spends a little of the trust the instrument accumulated, until the public's eventual refusal to believe the robust core is a debt incurred by the overclaimers rather than by the statisticians. The tradition that relies on this evidence most heavily has the largest stake in its credibility and therefore the largest obligation to quote it cleanly, an obligation this article has tried to discharge on the tradition's behalf as much as its own. A number believed, because it was never caught lying, is worth more to every honest cause than a number inflated, and the arithmetic of trust, unlike the arithmetic of the accounts, permits no imputations.

## What the share would look like if it were built for the theory

A thought experiment closes the analytical part of this article and gathers its argument into a single image. Suppose the statistical office were instructed to build, not the labour share, but its theoretical counterpart: a ratio answering to the rate of surplus value, from the raw materials of a modern economy. The builders would have to identify productive labour establishment by establishment, separating the machinist from the salesperson in the same firm and the surgeon in the private clinic from the surgeon in the public hospital, whose identical labour changes category with its purchaser. They would have to value output in labour time, which means constructing the socially necessary labour embodied in every commodity, a computation requiring the whole input structure of the economy and a solution to the problem of reducing skilled to simple labour. They would have to price labour power by the value of its reproduction, including the unpaid domestic labour the market never records. They would have to strip rents, interest, and taxes out of the surplus and assign executive rewards to their functional side. Every step is conceivable; the translation literature performs versions of several; and the resulting magnitude, at the end of the labour, would be a construction so laden with stated judgements that its makers would publish it with a volume of methodological defence attached, which is to say it would look exactly like Shaikh and Tonak's book.

The experiment's point is the contrast. The labour share can be published as a clean ratio because its categories were designed for administrability, and its cleanliness is purchased by its distance from the theory. The theoretical ratio's dirtiness is purchased by its fidelity. A reader who understands the trade-off will never again be impressed by the cleanliness of the one or embarrassed by the dirtiness of the other, and will recognise in the demand that the theory produce a statistic as clean as the accounts' a demand that the theory abandon the distinctions that make it a theory. The two instruments coexist, each honourable at its own work, and the whole error this article has pursued consists in sending one to do the other's work while wearing the other's name.

### Could statisticians simply publish the rate of exploitation instead?

Not without rebuilding the accounts on theoretical foundations: identifying productive labour firm by firm, valuing output in labour time, pricing the reproduction of labour power including unpaid domestic work, and relocating rents, interest, and executive rewards, a construction so laden with judgements that its cleanliness would be the first thing sacrificed.

There the analytical argument rests. What remains is the apparatus every article in this series carries for the readers who arrive with questions rather than with the patience for the whole road: the answers, stated compactly, to the questions the ledger records for this territory. They follow, and a reader who has travelled the road will recognise each answer as a compressed form of a section already read, which is what a good answer in this series is supposed to be.

## The gendered division inside the aggregate

Compensation sums the pay of men and women into a single total, and the period rearranged the relation between the two in ways the aggregate cannot express. Women's participation in paid employment rose across the advanced economies, often into part-time, service, and care roles whose pay sits low in the distribution, while the gender gap in pay within occupations narrowed unevenly and the segregation of occupations by sex persisted in altered forms. The aggregate share can therefore fall partly because the workforce's composition shifted toward groups the labour market pays badly, a composition effect with a human face, in which the entry of millions of women into recorded employment, an emancipation by any historical standard, registers in the statistic as downward pressure on labour's portion because the jobs awaiting them were priced as secondary earnings. Reading that arithmetic as a simple defeat for labour misses the transformation inside it, and reading the transformation as simple progress misses the pricing. Decomposition by sex, like every decomposition in this article, replaces a slogan with a scene.

The theoretical stakes are not small. The value of labour power, in the classical construction, was modelled on a family wage, a man's earnings covering a household's reproduction, with women's unpaid labour performing the reproduction itself. The dual-earner economy dissolved that model's factual basis while leaving its ghosts in pay structures, in the pricing of care, and in the assumption, embedded in low service wages, that another income stands behind the worker. A translated account of the period must therefore rebuild its categories for a workforce the founders did not observe, in which labour power is sold individually, reproduced through a mixture of wages, public provision, and still-unpaid domestic work, and priced by markets that sort by sex as well as by skill. The labour share, summing over the whole rearrangement, is the beginning of that inquiry and not, as careless commentary has it, its conclusion.

### How does women's entry into paid work complicate the share's story?

New entrants were absorbed disproportionately into low-paid service and care roles priced as secondary earnings, so the workforce's changing composition pressed the aggregate portion downward even as participation itself marked a historic widening of economic independence, a transformation the single ratio registers only as arithmetic.

## Debt, assets, and the household balance sheet behind the flows

Income shares describe flows, but households live on balance sheets, and the period's distributional experience was shaped as much by the stock side as by the flow side. Households at the middle and below accumulated debt against stagnant compensation, sustaining consumption and housing access by borrowing, while asset holders watched property and equity values compound. The accounts record the interest on that debt as property income, so the borrowing that compensated households for the wage share's weakness simultaneously swelled the other side of the division, a feedback loop written into the ratio itself. When the loop broke, in the financial crisis that stands at the period's centre in every narrative, the losses were distributed by the balance sheets the flows had built: the indebted discovered that their asset was a dwelling whose price could fall, while the diversified holders of financial claims were rescued by the public authority in the name of the system as a whole.

None of that history is visible in the share, and all of it is necessary to understand what the share's movement felt like and why its politics ignited when it did. A distributional measure is a window, and the preceding sections have spent their length describing the window's frame, its glass, and its distortions. The landscape beyond includes wealth, debt, security, and the public provision that never passes through a wage, and the reader who has learned the window's limits is precisely the reader equipped to survey the landscape without mistaking the view through one frame for the territory. That equipment, more than any single finding, is what a long article on a single ratio owes its readers.

## Precarity and the quality of the jobs inside the numerator

A wage total says nothing about the security attached to the wages, and the period's transformation of job quality is the last great silence of the measure to be named. Across the decades, employment in many economies shifted toward forms carrying less security: shorter tenures, variable hours set by the employer, contracts without guaranteed time, agency and subcontracted arrangements that interpose a firm between the worker and the enterprise whose work is actually done. Two workforces can earn identical compensation totals, one in stable posts with predictable hours and accrued rights, the other in fragments assembled week by week under the threat of disappearance, and the ratio will report the two economies as distributionally identical. The lived difference is enormous, and it is not a soft difference of feeling but a hard difference of power, since the insecure worker's capacity to refuse, to organise, and to plan is undermined in ways no pay statistic captures.

The tradition has the vocabulary for this silence too, in its analysis of the forms of the wage and of the reserve army's pressure, which operates through insecurity as much as through unemployment's count. A worker who can be dismissed into a pool of the similarly insecure experiences the reserve army not as a statistic about other people but as a condition of the working week, and the division of income inside such a workforce reflects that condition in every bargain struck quietly and alone. Modern research on job quality, on schedule instability and its measurable effects on earnings volatility and household stress, has begun to give the silence a literature of its own, and its findings belong beside the share in any honest distributional account of the period, as the account's necessary supplement rather than its rival.

### Can two economies with identical shares offer workers utterly different lives?

Yes: the ratio totals compensation without regard to the security, predictability, or rights attached to the jobs earning it, so a workforce in stable posts and a workforce assembled from fragments under constant threat of dismissal can present the same distributional figure while standing in opposite relations to power.

The point completes the article's survey of the measure's silences, which now form a pattern worth stating as the survey ends. The share is silent about exploitation, by category. It is silent about wealth and debt, by construction. It is silent about the social wage, by perimeter. It is silent about security, by design. A reader who memorises the silences owns the measure, because every misuse of it consists in asking it to speak where it is silent, and every sound use consists in consulting it where it speaks, on how the recorded product was divided, and then walking to the other instruments for the rest of the truth. Instruments are not rivals. The orchestra is the argument.

## Small firms, franchises, and the division inside the supply web

The economy of small firms refracts the division in yet another direction that aggregate commentary rarely visits. A franchise outlet, a subcontracted workshop, a franchised delivery depot: each is legally an independent business whose owner books a profit, yet each operates inside parameters set by a brand owner or a dominant buyer who dictates prices, standards, and margins. The recorded surplus of the small unit is thus a negotiated remnant, squeezed between the franchisor's royalty, the landlord's rent, the lender's interest, and the workforce's pay, while the economic substance resembles a wage relation conducted at one remove, the owner functioning partly as a manager hired through a contract of enterprise rather than of employment. When activity migrates from integrated firms toward franchised and subcontracted webs, as it did across whole service industries during the decades under review, income that would once have appeared as wages inside a large firm reappears partly as the thin profits of small proprietors, and the measured division shifts for organisational reasons that no bargaining table ever voted on.

The phenomenon generalises an insight the article has met repeatedly: organisational form is a distributional variable. Where the boundary of the firm is drawn decides which incomes are wages, which are profits, and which vanish into the payments between firms that the value-added accounts net away. Vertical integration, outsourcing, franchising, and platform mediation are not neutral technologies of coordination; each redraws the map on which the division is recorded, and a long era that redrew the map incessantly cannot have its distributional history read off the recorded totals without an organisational audit alongside the accounting one. The tradition, which analysed the putting-out system and the sweating system as forms in which capital organised labour while disclaiming the wage relation's obligations, owns the historical template for exactly this analysis. The modern webs are its descendants, and the measured share is their joint portrait, taken from an angle that flatters the draughtsman.

### How does outsourcing change what the division records?

Work once done by employees of a single firm, whose pay appeared as compensation against that firm's value added, reappears when outsourced as the revenue of supplier firms, inside which wages, thin proprietor profits, rents, and interest are blended anew, so the aggregate division moves for organisational reasons even where every individual's terms are unchanged.

## The professions and the partnership form: surplus in a salary's clothing

One more organisational costume deserves its moment, because it conceals a large and growing mass of high income inside categories that commentary rarely questions. The great professional partnerships, in law, accountancy, and consultancy, distribute their surplus to partners as shares of profit, yet the partners work, bill hours, and are managed much like senior employees, while their junior colleagues are straightforwardly salaried. In the accounts, partnership drawings appear as mixed or property income depending on the legal wrapper, so the same economic arrangement, elite labour rewarded out of a firm's surplus, lands on different sides of the division in different organisational dress. As the professions corporatised and globalised across the period, the sums involved grew large enough to matter for the top of the distribution, and they compound the summit problem examined earlier: the boundary between labour income and profit at the top of the economy is not merely mis-measured, it is actively chosen, by advisers, for fiscal and regulatory advantage, and the choices feed the statistic.

A distributional politics that cannot see through organisational dress will misjudge both the size and the location of the surplus at the economy's summit. The tradition's insistence on classifying by function rather than by form was designed for a world of simpler costumes, the mill owner and the mill hand, and its modern application requires an almost forensic patience with partnerships, incorporations, franchises, and contracts of service that exist precisely to blur the line the theory needs drawn. That patience is no pedantry; it is the price of knowing where the division actually runs in an economy that has learned to dress every relation in the costume that suits it best.

## Seasonal, cyclical, and casual labour: the division at the margin of the year

The accounts annualise, and annualising conceals the division's life at the margin of the year, where a significant population labours. Harvest work, holiday retail, tourism's high season, construction's weather windows: in each, employment swells and collapses on a rhythm the annual totals smooth into an average that no participant ever experienced. The seasonal worker's compensation is real but discontinuous, supplemented by benefits, by other seasons' other work, by household pooling, and the employer's surplus in the peak weeks is extracted from a workforce assembled for the surge and dispersed before it can organise. An annual share describes such an economy the way an average temperature describes a climate of frosts and heatwaves, accurately and misleadingly at once. The tradition's reserve army had a seasonal regiment long before it had a statistical one, in the migration of labourers who followed the harvests of the nineteenth-century countryside, and the modern economy's peaks reproduce the pattern inside services, with the same consequence for bargaining: a workforce that assembles briefly cannot easily convert its momentary indispensability into durable terms.

Casualisation generalises the seasonal pattern across the calendar, replacing the continuous post with the summoned shift, and its distributional effect operates partly beneath the wage itself, in the unpaid availability that summoned labour must donate: the hours held open against a call that may not come, the travel undertaken for a shift cancelled on arrival, the impossibility of holding a second engagement against the first employer's uncertainty. None of that donated time enters compensation, and none of the employer's corresponding flexibility enters the accounts as a cost avoided, so the recorded division understates the transfer involved in casual work by exactly the value of the standby life it consumes. Measures of the division built from employer records will never see the standby. Measures built from workers' time diaries see it at once, which is why the two literatures, on pay and on time, describe the same economy so differently, and why the article's closing advice is to read them together or not at all.

### What does the annual total hide about seasonal and casual work?

It hides discontinuity itself: compensation assembled from surges and gaps is averaged into a smoothness nobody lived, while the unpaid standby time that summoned labour donates, hours held open against calls that may never come, enters neither the wage bill nor the employer's recorded costs anywhere.

## A closing inventory of the article's claims

Before the questions that end every article in this series, the claims stand in a row for inspection, each at the height its evidence built for it. The recorded division moved against compensation, broadly and persistently, and that survives the audit. Its size is a family of numbers, not a number. Shelter accounts for a striking share of the other side's advance, and rent is the concept the era's distribution most rewards studying. Ordinary employees fared worse than the headline, once the summit is lifted out of their average. The state's perimeter, the cycle's phase, the firm's boundary, the contract's costume, and the household's pooling all bend the figure before any class force touches it. And exploitation, the theory's central magnitude, is nowhere in the table, awaiting its own construction by hands willing to state their rules. A reader who can recite that inventory, and defend each line against the objection it invites, has the article, whatever becomes of its prose.

## The statistician's craft: an appreciation before the questions

A word of appreciation is owed, near the end, to the craft that built the instrument this article has spent its length auditing, because audit without appreciation curdles into the suspicion that the numbers were made by enemies. The national accounts are among the great administrative achievements of the modern state: a consistent, public, revisable description of an entire economy's flows, maintained by professionals whose methodological debates are published, whose revisions are announced, and whose conventions can be challenged in the open, as the housing decomposition challenged them from inside the craft's own conferences. Every criticism in this article was possible because the craft documents itself. The categories can be interrogated because they are stated. The imputations can be disputed because they are confessed in the manuals. A reader who has followed the audit should emerge with more respect for the instrument, not less, joined to a clearer sense of the distance between what it measures and what commentary asks of it.

That respect has a practical corollary for the tradition this series serves. The statistical offices of the advanced economies are not going to rebuild their accounts on Marxist categories, and waiting for them to do so is not a research strategy. The strategy is the one the translation literature modelled: take the public instrument as it stands, learn its construction better than its casual users know it, build the bridge to the theory's categories in the open, and let the two descriptions correct one another in public. The labour share, handled that way, stops being either a slogan or an embarrassment and becomes what it should always have been for a serious tradition: a well-made window, with a known frame, onto a division that the theory interprets more deeply than the window can. The appreciation and the audit are the same act, performed with both eyes open.

### Why should critics of the accounts respect the statisticians who build them?

Because the craft documents itself: its conventions are published, its imputations confessed in manuals, its revisions announced, and its categories stated openly enough to be challenged from inside, which is precisely what makes an audit like this article's possible at all.

There is a corresponding duty on the craft's side, which its best practitioners already honour: to resist the press release's temptation, to publish the decomposition beside the aggregate, and to say, in the plainest available language, what a given ratio does not measure. Where statistical offices and research teams have done that, public argument improves measurably. Where they have let the headline travel alone, the headline's career follows the law of portability described earlier, and the craft finds its careful instrument brandished in causes it never endorsed. The division of labour between the makers of measures and the makers of arguments is real, but the border between them is patrolled, or not, by exactly the habits this article has tried to teach its readers to demand.

## The reader's toolkit, gathered in one place

Everything this article has taught can be compressed into a pocket toolkit, and compression is worthwhile because the next encounter with the evidence will not announce itself as an examination. When a figure for the division of income surfaces, the equipped reader performs a small ceremony of questions, so practised it takes moments. What territory does the figure describe, and which activities stand inside its fence? Was the total taken before or after the deduction for worn-out capital? How were the earnings of people who work for themselves divided between their labour and their assets? Does the property side contain the notional rents of homeowners, and has anyone taken them out to see what remains? Are the rewards of the highest-paid employees swelling the labour side? Do the endpoints of the span sit at comparable moments of the economic cycle, or has a trough been married to a peak? And finally, the question that guards all the others: is the figure being asked to testify about incomes, where it is competent, or about exploitation, where it is not?

A figure that survives the ceremony can be believed to the extent of its survival, which for the great finding at the centre of this article means believing the direction warmly, the size cautiously, and the popular interpretation hardly at all. A figure that fails the ceremony has not been refuted; it has been returned to its sender for the construction it forgot to include, and the sender's response, whether the construction arrives or the figure is quietly withdrawn, tells the reader most of what the reader needed to know about the argument it came from. That is the toolkit. It weighs nothing, it fits every distributional controversy yet to be invented, and it was the whole purpose of the long road through imputations, perimeters, summits, and shelters that this article has travelled. The road was long because the toolkit's questions each had to be earned from the construction itself; a reader who merely memorised the questions without the earning would wield them as a ritual, and rituals, unlike audits, convince nobody who was not already convinced.

One more item belongs in the toolkit, of a different kind from the questions: a disposition. The evidence surveyed in these pages rewards a reader who can hold warmth and suspicion in a single grip, warmth toward a finding that survived an unfriendly audit, suspicion toward every use of it that arrives without papers. That disposition is rarer than either credulity or cynicism because it must be renewed against the pull of both, against the partisan's wish to believe the convenient figure entire and the debunker's wish to dissolve it entire. Political economy has always demanded the disposition of its serious students, since its objects, classes, values, distributions, are simultaneously real enough to ruin lives and constructed enough to evade every careless instrument pointed at them. The division of income is such an object par excellence. It decides, in the aggregate, whose children eat securely and whose labour commands respect, and it hides, in the particular, behind imputations and perimeters that only patience can penetrate. Patience, in the end, is the toolkit's final tool, the one on which all the questions depend for their answers.

So the article closes where it began, with a measure quoted everywhere and examined almost nowhere, now examined. Its fall is real, its size is a family, its largest single secret is a roof, its most important property is a negative, that it does not measure the thing its loudest quoters say it measures. Those five sentences contain the finding, the method, and the warning in equal parts, and a reader who carries them away has the use of the evidence without its dangers, which is all that any article in this series has ever promised to deliver.

A last word about the company this finding keeps. It belongs to a family of magnitudes, the profit rate, the markup, the productivity residual, the measured rate of exploitation itself, each of which has passed through the same career of construction, decomposition, and partial rehabilitation that this article traced for the share. To learn the share's audit thoroughly is therefore to be trained for the family's other members before meeting them, since the questions transfer even where the constructions differ: what is counted, what is imputed, what is the total against which the part is set, where does the boundary run, and what would the claimant accept as defeat. A student who carries those questions from this article to the profit rate debates will find the terrain strangely familiar, the same fog and the same few lanterns. That transferability is the deepest justification for the space this series gives to measurement. Findings expire with their decades. The habit of interrogating them is the only part of an education in this field that never does.

One further transfer deserves naming, from the statistic to the citizen. Every household that has watched a pay packet stagnate while an employer's fortunes visibly swelled has performed, in private, the comparison this article formalised, and has usually lacked the vocabulary to defend the comparison against the reply that times are hard for everyone. The vocabulary now exists in portable form: portions, constructions, decompositions, summits, shelters. A citizen armed with it can ask, in a public meeting or a comments thread, the questions that return a glib figure to its sender, and can recognise the rare figure that arrives with its papers in order and deserves assent. Democracy's distributional arguments will go on being fought with numbers. The modest ambition of a long article on one ratio is that a few more of the combatants, on every side, fight with numbers they have actually inspected.

And if the combatants include, as they should, the writers of future articles in this series, the lesson they carry forward is the one the measurement pattern taught nine times over: in this field, the instrument is half the argument, and the writer who masters the instrument before reaching for the conclusion will be beaten in debate far less often than the writer who memorises conclusions and borrows instruments. The share has had its audit. Its neighbours await theirs, and the queue is the series' remaining work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Why has the labour share fallen?

Several forces pulled the division of recorded income away from compensation at once, and scholars weigh them differently. Equipment and computing grew cheaper beside labour, tempting firms to mechanise. Production spread across borders, weakening the hand of workers who bargained nation by nation. Unions and the agreements they sustained thinned in many places. Activity migrated toward giant firms and toward industries where the portion reaching employees was always smaller. Shelter costs, working through an accounting convention, inflated the property side. Which force mattered most cannot be settled from the aggregate, because the aggregate's construction decides how much movement each candidate is required to explain.

### Q: Does the labour share measure exploitation?

No, and the two magnitudes belong to different intellectual worlds. Exploitation, in the theory's strict usage, concerns stretches of the working day: the hours in which a worker reproduces the value of the wage set against the hours handed over without equivalent. The share concerns money: what statistical offices record as employee compensation, set against what they record as national income, at the prices actually paid. Those records lump together production workers with clerks, financiers, officials, and directors, and they make no attempt to value anything in labour time. The two magnitudes can drift together or apart, so the second can never be read as a gauge of the first.

### Q: How does housing change the labour share result?

A homeowner is treated by the accountants as a tenant of an unusual kind, paying rent to himself, and that notional rent enters the books as income from property. When dwellings grow dearer, the notional rent grows with them, so the property side of the national division swells even though no employer has paid anyone a penny less. Matthew Rognlie's decomposition of the rising capital portion showed shelter carrying a large part of that rise, leaving the return on other capital comparatively subdued. Removing dwellings from the calculation therefore shrinks the famous trend markedly and changes its meaning, from a general triumph of capital over labour into something substantially about land, scarcity, and the price of a home.

### Q: How is self-employment income treated?

It arrives in the accounts as a single unsplit sum called mixed income, because what a self-employed person earns rewards several things simultaneously: the effort expended, the gear and premises owned, and the hazards personally carried. Anyone computing a share must impose a division that the source data refuse to supply. Some series hand the entire sum to labour, others hand it to capital, and the careful middle course estimates an employee-equivalent wage for such workers and leaves the residue to property. Because the mix of occupations inside self-employment keeps changing, a fixed rule can manufacture drift in the resulting series or conceal genuine drift, which is why published versions of the share disagree so much at exactly this joint.

### Q: Do top labour incomes distort the measure?

They do, and systematically in one direction. The pay of senior executives counts as employee compensation because executives are, in law, employees, yet their rewards ballooned across the period while ordinary pay crept. Inside the numerator, that ballooning disguises the squeeze on everyone beneath the summit: the aggregate can look merely soft while the portion reaching the great majority of staff has fallen hard. Researchers who strip out the highest earners uncover a steeper descent for the rest. The wrinkle is conceptual as well as arithmetical, since an executive's function is to make capital grow, a role the Marxist scheme places on the opposite side of the line from the workforce whose income the statistic is supposed to describe.

### Q: What is the difference between gross and net labour share?

One divides by everything produced; the other first subtracts what production consumed. Machines wear out, vehicles age, and software perishes quickly, and the value used up in that wearing must be replaced before any income exists to share. A gross calculation charges none of this against the total, so the replacement burden sits inside the property portion and makes capital's take look grander than the fund available for distribution warrants. A net calculation removes the burden first. Since the stock of capital tilted toward short-lived equipment and programs, the subtraction grew over the period, and series built on the net principle accordingly show property's advance as gentler than gross series proclaim.

### Q: What is robust across different treatments?

The sign of the long movement, little else, and that is enough to matter. Rebuild the statistic with a rival rule for the self-employed, or on the stricter net basis, or with dwellings lifted out, or confined to companies alone, and the portion going to employees still ends lower than it began in most advanced economies across the span in question. Rival research teams, using rival archives and harbouring rival sympathies, keep landing on that same direction. The size of the descent, by contrast, swings widely from one rebuilding to another, and what the descent consists of changes too, so any speech quoting an exact magnitude without naming its recipe is quoting the recipe's author, not the economy.

### Q: How do you translate national accounts into Marxist categories?

Slowly, explicitly, and in public, as Shaikh and Tonak demonstrated in their study of the wealth of nations. Their method takes each accounting heading apart and asks what it contains in the theory's terms: which workers produce surplus value and which are maintained from it, where the state's employees belong, how the earnings of managers and of the self-employed should be assigned, how taxes and subsidies intervene between value made and income booked. The reassembled totals can then support estimates of variable capital, surplus value, and its rate. Every junction requires a defended judgement, and rival translations may judge differently, but a defended judgement is precisely what separates a translation from the lazy alternative of relabelling an official ratio.

### Q: Is the labour share decline uncontested?

The quarrels are real but they have addresses. Nobody serious disputes that the direction, over long spans and many economies, points downward for compensation. The size is fought over because rival constructions yield rival magnitudes, all honestly derived. The housing decomposition is fought over because scholars disagree about whether shelter's dominance in the property rise acquits productive capital or merely relocates the story to rent. The split between changes inside industries and shifts among them is fought over dataset by dataset. And the causes remain in open competition. A finding can be robust at its core and contested at every edge, and this one is the textbook case of that combination.

### Q: What would overturn the labour share finding?

A rival way of building the statistic that scholars could accept as principled rather than convenient, applied evenly across economies and across the whole span, under which the downward movement disappeared in most places at once. Trimming the movement would not suffice, nor would dissolving it in a single economy, nor for a single component of income. The housing work came partway down that road for the shelter component and stopped well short of the destination, which is why the verdict survives in qualified form. Stating the condition of defeat in advance matters: a judgement that nothing could overturn is not a finding but a loyalty, and this literature has suffered enough from loyalties dressed as findings.

### Q: How does the warning that the labour share is not the rate of exploitation grade the evidence?

It sorts the evidence by the use proposed for it. Offered as testimony about how recorded money income was divided, the finding deserves real confidence in its direction and cautious confidence in its size, having survived repeated rebuilding by unfriendly hands. Offered as testimony about exploitation, it deserves none until a translation has been constructed, because its categories were drawn for administering economies, not for analysing the working day. The warning is thus not a mark down but a routing instruction, and evidence routed to the question it can actually answer is worth more, not less, than evidence waved at every question in the room.

### Q: What should a student separate between a ratio of labour times and a ratio of money incomes?

Three things, and an examination answer lives or dies by keeping them apart. The units differ: hours of a working day on one side, currency amounts on the other. The headcounts differ: the money ratio sweeps in officials, sales staff, financiers, and directors alongside production workers, while the time ratio concerns only labour set to work by capital in production. The yardsticks differ: money amounts are struck at market prices, which bend away from labour values wherever rent, monopoly, or imputation enters, while the time ratio is struck in labour itself. Conflate any one of the three and the two ratios collapse into each other in the student's prose, which is the error this whole subject is arranged to catch.

### Q: Why does executive pay distort the labour share?

Classification follows the contract, and the contract says employee, so rewards that are functionally a share of the firm's surplus are filed as wages. Those rewards also happen to be the fastest-growing element in the entire compensation total, geared as they are to share prices and to the surplus itself. Their presence therefore does double damage to interpretation: it raises the labour side of the ratio mechanically, and it hides the divergence between the summit of the firm and its base, the very divergence a distributional statistic is consulted to reveal. Marx noticed the salaried manager emerging and warned that the salary form could disguise a claim on surplus; the modern accounts, innocently following the contract, perfect the disguise he described.

### Q: How does the treatment decisions table order the measurement choices?

In the sequence the statistician meets them while assembling the number. Mixed income comes first, because nothing can be computed until the self-employed person's blend is divided somehow. Dwellings come next, because an imputed rent must be placed on one side of the ledger or the other. The gross or net decision follows, fixing the size of the pie before any slicing is described. The treatment of the highest earners follows, governing what the compensation slice actually contains. Coverage closes the table, because the boundary of the economy being measured frames every earlier choice. Read down the rows and a reader can interrogate any published figure in minutes, which is the table's purpose.

### Q: What separates a robust decline from an artefact of treatment?

Survival under rebuilding by sceptics. Take the statistic apart and reassemble it the other defensible ways: net instead of gross, dwellings excluded, the self-employed divided by a rival rule, the perimeter redrawn, the top earners set aside. A movement that is still visible after that gauntlet belongs to the economy; a movement that evaporates under one principled rebuilding belonged to the recipe. The test also works in reverse as a diagnostic, since the particular rebuilding that dissolves a movement identifies the movement's ingredients, as the housing rebuilding identified shelter inside the property rise. Robustness, in this field, is not a property a number has but a gauntlet it has run.
