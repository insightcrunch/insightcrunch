The general intellect is a phrase that a reader can meet in a lecture, a journal article, or a book about the direction of modern production, presented as though it were one of the settled concepts of Marxist theory, a term with a definition, a history of use by its author, and a place in a finished system. Almost none of that presentation survives a visit to the source. The phrase comes from notebooks that Marx filled in the winter of 1857 and 1858 while drafting the groundwork of his critique of political economy, notebooks he never published, never revised for publication, and never mentioned as the home of a concept he wished his readers to adopt. He wrote the two words in English, inside a manuscript otherwise written in German. He used them on a handful of occasions only. Nothing he published in his lifetime contains them. Yet an entire account of where capitalism has arrived, complete with claims about the law of value and its fate, the migration of production beyond the workplace, and the identity of the decisive productive force in modern economies, has been constructed on those few lines, and the construction is routinely credited to Marx himself, as though the theory were his and the passage merely its clearest statement.

![An open notebook of dense manuscript pages beside translations and commentaries on the general intellect, Insight Crunch](/assets/images/blog/blog-84.webp)

That disproportion between a slender textual basis and a large theoretical edifice is the subject of this article, and the article's service is precision about it. Precision here cuts in two directions at once, and a reader should hold both edges in view from the start. Against careless usage, precision insists that a phrase in an unpublished notebook cannot carry the authority of a doctrine its author printed, defended, and built a life's work around, and that anyone citing the general intellect as Marx's theory of the present owes an account of why notebook material should be granted that standing. Against reflexive dismissal, precision insists with equal firmness that the thinness of a basis does not by itself refute a theory built upon it. Theories are refuted by argument and by evidence, not by bibliography. A reading can rest on three pages and still describe the world accurately, just as a three volume construction can fail. What the textual facts govern is the appeal to authority. They decide whose theory is under discussion. If the strong claims made in the phrase's name are to be believed, they must be believed as the claims of the later theorists who actually made them, argued on their merits, and not inherited as the settled teaching of the notebooks' author.

The passage at the centre of the dispute, the stretch of the notebooks that readers call the fragment on machines, makes a genuine and striking argument, and this article will state it at full strength before grading any interpretation of it. As large scale industry develops, the passage argues, the creation of wealth comes to depend less on the labour time directly expended than on the power of the agencies set in motion during that time, agencies whose effectiveness bears no proportion to the hours spent producing them. Knowledge, and specifically the general state of science and the organisation of social capacities, becomes embodied in the system of machinery confronting the worker, so that social knowledge itself operates directly as a productive force. The individual worker steps beside the process rather than into it, watching and regulating a production whose intelligence sits inside the apparatus, embodied in the accumulated understanding built into it. Anyone can feel the pull of that description toward later conditions in which production visibly rests on organised knowledge. The pull is real. The question that divides interpreters is what the passage licenses a reader to conclude, and the distance between the rival conclusions is the distance between a tendency inside a theory of value and the obituary of that theory.

Three readings organise the field, and this article is built around distinguishing them cleanly. The strong reading treats the passage as anticipation and prophecy: Marx, on this account, saw that knowledge would displace labour time as the substance and measure of wealth, that the law of value would accordingly cease to operate, and that production would overflow the factory into social life as a whole. The counter-reading treats the passage as a note about fixed capital: the machinery system embodies accumulated social knowledge, the worker's relation to that system changes, and nothing in the note disturbs the published theory in which labour time remains the measure of value and the wage relation remains the organising relation of production. The middle position, which this article defends, accepts the tendency the passage identifies, knowledge hardening into apparatus, and any single worker's share growing hard to trace, while denying that the tendency suspends the law it operates within. A table setting the three readings side by side, the three readings table, appears once the textual ground has been laid, and it serves as the article's findable artifact: what each reading claims, what support each can cite, what each predicts, and how the observable record bears on the prediction.

Method matters here as much as conclusion, because the dispute is at bottom a dispute about how texts may be used. The notebooks in question belong to the mass of preparatory writing that stands behind the published critique, and the series in which this entry appears has a settled way of handling such material, visible in its treatment of the [grundrisse readers guide](/2013/08/01/grundrisse-readers-guide/), where the same notebooks are read as a workshop rather than as a scripture. A workshop shows a thinker testing formulations, pushing an idea past the point where his considered doctrine would stop, following a tendency to its limit in order to see its shape. Workshop writing is invaluable to scholarship precisely because it is unguarded. It is also, for the same reason, dangerous to quote as doctrine, since the author never performed the act that turns exploration into commitment, the act of publishing under his name with the expectation of being held to the words. The reception history adds a further complication that this article treats in its own section: the notebooks reached print in German long after their composition, reached English readers in complete form later still, and gathered their most influential interpreters in Italian and French debates that Anglophone readers can consult only in part. A concept whose career runs through that history cannot be understood apart from it.

One boundary should be drawn before the detailed work begins, so that the article's neighbours keep their own territory. The machinery question in its general form, what mechanisation does to labour and to the demand for it, belongs to the treatment of [automation ai and marxism](/2020/12/15/automation-ai-and-marxism/), and nothing here reopens it. The theory whose survival is at stake in the dispute, the law of value itself, is expounded in [labour theory of value explained](/2010/07/01/labour-theory-of-value-explained/), and this article links to that exposition rather than rebuilding it. The application of the strong reading to a particular organisational form of modern business is handled under [platform capitalism marxist analysis](/2020/11/15/platform-capitalism-marxist-analysis/), and the cluster within which the whole argument sits is gathered at [marxism and modern capitalism](/2020/11/01/marxism-and-modern-capitalism/). What remains for these pages is the phrase, its passage, its interpreters, its critics, and the test that can be applied to the rival claims without waiting for further manuscripts to surface. The tradition within which the strong reading arose is mapped in [western marxism complete guide](/2015/11/01/western-marxism-complete-guide/), for readers who want the genealogy before the verdict.

## The phrase and where it stands in the notebooks

A glossary entry usually begins with a definition, and this one can, provided the definition carries its provenance inside it rather than trailing behind as a footnote. The general intellect names, in the passage where it appears, the accumulated knowledge of a society considered as a productive power: the stock of scientific understanding, technical competence, organisational know-how, and cooperative capacity that a population has built up across generations and that production draws upon as a given endowment. Marx's formulation treats that stock as something that has become objectified, fixed in the machinery system and in the arrangement of the labour process, so that it confronts each individual worker as an alien power belonging to capital rather than as the worker's own developed capacity. The definition is intelligible and even elegant. Its provenance is the problem. The words occur in a private working manuscript, in a section devoted to fixed capital and the development of large scale industry, within a much longer exploration of how capital transforms the conditions of labour. They are not introduced with ceremony, not defined against rival usages, not gathered into a summary, and not repeated as a refrain through the notebooks. A reader hunting for a theory of the general intellect in the way one hunts for the theory of surplus value, with its chapters, its distinctions, its worked examples, will hunt in vain. What exists is a formulation, arrived at in passing, inside an argument about machinery.

### Where does the term general intellect come from?

from Marx's unpublished notebooks of 1857 and 1858, in a passage on machinery and social knowledge. He wrote the phrase in English within a German manuscript, it occurs very few times, and it appears nowhere in anything he published in his lifetime.

The setting of the formulation deserves a slower description, because interpreters who quote the lines often lift them out of a train of thought that qualifies them. The notebooks of 1857 and 1858, known to scholarship as the Grundrisse, were written at speed, for self clarification, during a period when their author was assembling the architecture of the critique he would spend the rest of his life building. The manuscript ranges across money, capital, circulation, pre-capitalist forms, and the prospects of the system, frequently changing direction, frequently trying a proposition in a strong form to observe what follows. The machinery discussion belongs to the treatment of fixed capital, where the governing question is how the instruments of production, once they take the form of an automated system, alter the position of living labour within production. It is inside that question, not inside a treatise on knowledge, that the phrase appears. Social knowledge enters the argument because the machinery system is unintelligible without it: the apparatus is congealed inquiry, the deposit of experiments, failures, and refinements contributed by people who will never stand beside the machines their understanding made possible. The passage marvels at that deposit and draws out its consequence for the worker's role. Any reading that converts the marvel into a free standing philosophy of knowledge has already travelled some distance from the page.

Provenance also includes the negative evidence, and negative evidence of this kind is unusually informative. The published critique that grew out of these notebooks appeared in stages over the following decades, including the first volume of Capital in 1867 and the posthumous volumes assembled by Engels from later manuscripts. Across that entire published corpus, through revisions, new editions, polemics, and summaries written for popular audiences, the phrase never resurfaces. The argument about machinery certainly resurfaces, in the famous chapter on machinery and large scale industry, where the displacement of the worker's skill into the mechanism is analysed at length. The vocabulary of the general intellect does not accompany it. An author who believed he had coined a concept of the first importance, one destined to reorganise his theory of value, had repeated opportunities to install it in the works he actually gave the world. The installation never happened. Silence of that duration and across that many occasions is not conclusive proof that the author abandoned the thought, since writers drop formulations for many reasons, but it fixes the burden of proof. Whoever wishes to treat the phrase as a cornerstone must explain why the building erected by its own architect shows no trace of it.

There is a smaller philological point that turns out to matter for the reception, and it concerns the language of the phrase itself. Marx's manuscripts are polyglot in a way that surprises readers who meet him only in translation. He read voraciously in several languages, excerpted in whichever language the source used, and salted his German prose with English and French expressions where those languages supplied the term of art he wanted. The general intellect is one such borrowing, an English phrase embedded in German sentences. Commentators have speculated about its antecedents in English political economy and in the vocabulary of the period, and the speculation is legitimate, but the sober fact is simpler and more useful for a glossary: the phrase was not a German technical term with a settled meaning that translation then blurred. It was an imported expression, used loosely, in a private text, by a writer reaching for a way to name the social character of knowledge as it passes into production. Readers who encounter the phrase in later literatures, capitalised and hyphenated and freighted with doctrine, are meeting something that its first user handled far more casually than its later users have done.

## A notebook is not a book: the status of the source

Every citation carries an implicit claim about the kind of object being cited, and much confusion in this dispute dissolves once the kinds are kept apart. A published work is a public act. Its author chose its arguments, arranged their order, weighed its formulations against anticipated objections, and accepted responsibility for the result before readers equipped to answer back. A notebook is a private instrument. Its author writes to discover what he thinks, permits himself exaggerations that a draft for the press would have to defend, leaves contradictions standing side by side while he works around them, and abandons lines of inquiry without ceremony when they stop paying. Both kinds of writing are evidence, but they testify to different things. The published work evidences a position. The notebook evidences a process, including positions tried and set aside, emphases that reflection later corrected, and formulations whose very vividness marks them as experiments rather than conclusions. Treating a notebook sentence with the deference owed to a published doctrine is therefore not a sign of respect for the author. It is a category error about the artefact in hand.

The notebooks behind this phrase illustrate the distinction with unusual clarity because their author left a record of how he regarded them. They were written as preparatory material, quarried for the published critique, and then superseded by decades of further research, drafting, and redrafting. The first volume of Capital passed through multiple editions in its author's hands, each revision an opportunity to import notebook formulations that still seemed sound. Later manuscripts for the continuation volumes rework the machinery analysis, the circulation analysis, and the analysis of fixed capital in exhaustive detail. A formulation that mattered to the mature theory had every chance to migrate from the private pages into the public ones, carried by an author who was not shy about revising himself. The general intellect did not migrate. The machinery argument did, extensively, which shows the channel was open and in use. What travelled was the analysis of the worker's changing place beside an increasingly automatic apparatus. What stayed behind was the phrase and the sweeping corollary about wealth and labour time that the strong reading elevates into the centre of the theory.

Scholarship on the notebooks has long recognised this asymmetry, and the recognition is not a partisan weapon forged for the present dispute. Editors and textual scholars who devoted careers to the manuscript legacy, including scholars entirely sympathetic to the strong reading's politics, describe the Grundrisse as a laboratory text whose relation to the published critique must be argued passage by passage rather than assumed wholesale. Some laboratory results were confirmed by later work and belong to the theory. Some were transitional devices, ladders the author climbed and then discarded. Some remained unresolved tensions that the published work manages differently. Sorting a given passage into the right class is the ordinary labour of serious commentary, and it cannot be replaced by a blanket rule in either direction. The blanket rule that notebooks outrank books, occasionally heard in the phrase's defence on the ground that private writing is more candid, confuses candour with commitment. A diarist is candid about doubts that a treatise writer has resolved; the candour does not make the doubts the doctrine. Conversely, the blanket rule that only published words count would impoverish the tradition absurdly, since the posthumous volumes and the early manuscripts are indispensable to understanding how the doctrine was reached. The defensible rule is the laborious one: status is a consideration in every citation, weight is assigned case by case, and a claim that contradicts the published architecture bears a burden that a claim harmonising with it does not.

### What separates a passage Marx published from one he left in notebooks?

Publication is an act of commitment in which an author arranges, revises, and stands behind a formulation before critics, while a notebook records inquiry in motion, with trials, exaggerations, and abandoned lines left standing. Both kinds of writing are evidence, but of different things, so their citations deserve different weight.

That difference in weight is exactly what the phrase's career tends to erase. In much of the secondary literature the citation practice is seamless: a sentence from the notebooks is quoted between sentences from Capital, the seam invisible, the composite presented as a single doctrine uttered by a single voice. Readers without the provenance in mind absorb the impression that the general intellect enjoys the same textual dignity as the theory of surplus value or the analysis of the commodity form. Restoring the seam is not pedantry. It changes what a reasonable reader is entitled to demand from the claim. A doctrine published and defended must be answered on its arguments. A formulation explored in private and then dropped may legitimately be asked why it was dropped, and the asking is not an evasion of the argument but a prior question about the argument's standing in the corpus it invokes. This article asks that prior question throughout, while also answering the arguments themselves, since the strong reading deserves both treatments and receives both in the sections ahead.

## Publication, translation, and the shape of the reception

The career of the phrase cannot be separated from the dates on which its source became readable, and to whom, because an interpretation that arises before a text is widely available develops along channels that later availability does not simply widen but often merely confirms. The notebooks were composed in 1857 and 1858 and then lay among their author's papers, known to his literary executors and to a narrow circle, while the published critique defined the tradition for its first generations of readers. Print arrived in the twentieth century and in stages: a first German publication of the manuscript appeared in Moscow in 1939 and 1941, in an edition whose circulation was throttled by war, followed by the East Berlin edition of 1953 that made the text genuinely obtainable for scholars. English readers waited longer. A selection translated by Jack Cohen appeared in 1971 under David McLellan's editorship, and the complete English translation by Martin Nicolaus, published by Penguin in association with the New Left Review in 1973, at last put the whole manuscript, fragment on machines included, into the hands of Anglophone readers in a form they could cite page by page. The passage that concerns this article is quoted in the literature from that Nicolaus translation, and any reader checking a quotation should check it there, in the Penguin Grundrisse, rather than in the looser renderings that circulate in pamphlets and lecture notes.

The staggered chronology explains a feature of the dispute that otherwise looks like a mystery, namely why the strong reading grew up where it did. The most consequential early exploitation of the fragment occurred in Italy, where Raniero Panzieri's essay on machinery as an instrument of capital, published in the journal Quaderni Rossi in 1961, drew on the notebooks' treatment of machinery before the text was common property elsewhere, and where Mario Tronti's book of 1966 built a distinctive current, workerism, around the primacy of working class struggle and the reading of capitalist development as a response to it. That current, and the broader formation later labelled autonomism, worked with the German text and with Italian discussion of it during the very years when English readers had no complete version at all. The interpretive literature then ramified in Italian and in French, in journals, pamphlets, and seminar cultures whose products were translated selectively, belatedly, and sometimes not at all. Antonio Negri's Paris lectures on the notebooks, issued in Italian in 1979 as Marx oltre Marx, reached English only in 1991 as Marx Beyond Marx. Maurizio Lazzarato's 1996 French essay on immaterial labour travelled into English through collections rather than through a book of his own. Whole strata of the debate, including rejoinders, qualifications, and internal quarrels that would matter to a careful reader, remain available only in their original languages.

An English reader is therefore entitled to a plain statement of what can actually be consulted, and this article gives it as part of its brief rather than as an afterthought. In English, a reader can consult the complete notebooks in the Nicolaus translation, with its valuable introduction on the manuscript's place in the author's development. A reader can consult Negri's lectures in the 1991 translation, Paolo Virno's multitude grammar in its 2004 English edition, Michael Hardt and Antonio Negri's trilogy Empire of 2000, Multitude of 2004, and Commonwealth of 2009, Christian Marazzi's Capital and Language in its 2008 English edition, and Franco Berardi's study of the soul at work in its 2009 English edition. A reader can consult the counter-literature in English as well: George Caffentzis's 2013 collection of essays, Michael Heinrich's introductory study of the three volumes of Capital in its 2012 English edition, and a substantial journal literature on value-form theory. What a reader cannot consult in English is the full Italian and French sediment beneath those translations: the journal debates of the 1960s and 1970s in their entirety, the untranslated essays in which positions were first staked and later quietly modified, and the French discussion that mediates between the Italian current and its Anglophone reception. The honest consequence is asymmetry of access. The strong reading's canonical statements are available; much of its fine print is not, and verdicts formed in English are verdicts formed on a partial shelf. That asymmetry does not decide the dispute, but it should temper the confidence with which either side's history of the other is repeated at second hand.

## What the fragment on machines actually argues

Strip away the commentary and the passage itself has a discernible skeleton, which a careful reader can reconstruct in a few moves. It begins from fixed capital in its developed form, the system of machinery in which the production process confronts the worker as an organised apparatus rather than as a set of tools amplifying the worker's own motions. Within such a system, the passage argues, the skill and strength of the individual labourer cease to be the governing factors of output. What governs output is the apparatus, and the apparatus is science made material: the accumulated findings of natural inquiry, the engineering knowledge that embodies those findings in mechanisms, and the social organisation that coordinates the whole. Hence the celebrated formulation that general social knowledge has become a direct force of production, direct because it no longer waits to be applied by a knowing craftsman but operates immediately, built into the conditions under which any labour occurs at all. The worker's posture changes accordingly. Rather than standing inside the process as its chief agent, the worker steps to its side, tending, supervising, and regulating a movement whose productive intelligence is lodged elsewhere.

From that description the passage draws its boldest inference, the one on which everything in the later dispute depends. If wealth creation rests increasingly on the agencies set in motion rather than on the labour time expended in setting them in motion, then labour time, the passage suggests, becomes a miserable foundation for measuring wealth, a shrinking basis for a magnitude that has outgrown it. Capital itself, on this line of thought, works toward a condition in which direct labour dwindles toward a subordinate moment of production while capital goes on insisting that labour time alone measures wealth and sources it, and that insistence is named a contradiction: the system undermines the very basis on which it rests, summoning the powers of science and social combination to reduce necessary labour while clinging to labour time as its standard. The passage frames the outcome in the language of tendency and contradiction rather than of accomplished fact. It describes what capital strives toward and what that striving does to capital's own measure, in a manuscript whose author habitually explored tendencies by following them to their conceptual limit. Whether the limit described is a destination the system approaches, a pressure it perpetually generates and perpetually contains, or a thought experiment exposing an incoherence in the measure itself, is precisely what the rival readings dispute. The lines underdetermine the answer, which is one reason the dispute has lasted.

### How does the passage describe the worker beside the machinery system?

As a watchman and regulator rather than as the process's chief agent, since the productive intelligence of the arrangement resides in the apparatus and in the knowledge built into it. The worker's own skill and strength stop governing output once the system, rather than the hand, carries the production along.

Two features of the argument are regularly flattened in quotation and deserve restoration. The first is that the passage is an argument about capital, not a celebration of knowledge in the abstract. The social knowledge objectified in machinery confronts the producers as the property and power of another, because the apparatus belongs to capital and its knowledge is enlisted on capital's terms. The general intellect, in its textual home, is therefore not a commons flowering outside the value relation. It is a productive power that has been absorbed into fixed capital and set over against living labour, enriching the owner of the system while diminishing the weight of the individual worker within production. Interpretations that quote the passage as a hymn to liberated social knowledge are reading its first movement and skipping its second. The second feature is that the passage's drama depends on a contrast internal to the theory of value. Labour time can appear as a miserable measure only against the background of a system that continues to impose it as the measure. Remove that background and the sentence loses its tension, becoming a bland observation that knowledge matters to production, which no economist of any school has ever denied. The passage is interesting because it stages a collision between the productive forces capital develops and the value form capital preserves. Readings that keep the collision in view, whatever verdict they reach, are reading the passage. Readings that dissolve the collision into a simple succession, first labour ruled and then knowledge replaced it, are reading a summary of the passage at one remove.

## Fixed capital and the knowledge built into it

The safest way to locate the passage is to follow the category it actually belongs to, and that category is fixed capital. In the published critique, fixed capital comprises the instruments and conditions of production whose value is transferred piecemeal to the product across many cycles: buildings, plant, and above all machinery. The notebooks' discussion extends the category by attending to what a developed machinery system contains. A primitive tool contains the skill of its maker in a simple sense; a handloom embodies techniques, certainly, but techniques that remain legible to and partly reproducible by its user. An integrated apparatus embodies something categorically larger, the organised results of sciences the operator does not possess, designs refined across generations of engineers, and a division of labour among designers, builders, maintainers, and operators that no single participant surveys entire. When the passage describes social knowledge operating directly as a productive power, the fixed capital reading hears a precise claim about the composition of the apparatus: the productive power at work is not the dexterity of the person attending the machine but the knowledge sedimented in the machine's construction and arrangement. That claim is strong, original, and fully compatible with the published theory, in which the value of the apparatus is transferred to output gradually while living labour adds new value alongside the transfer.

This reading gains support from the way the published work handles the same phenomena without the notebook vocabulary. The machinery chapter of the first volume of Capital describes the factory system transferring skill from the worker to the mechanism, describes science pressed into capital's service as a power set apart from the labourer and turned against him, and describes the intellectual conditions of production confronting the worker as the property of another. Nothing in those analyses requires, or is accompanied by, a suggestion that the measure of value itself has lapsed. On the contrary, the same volume grounds the value of the machine's output contribution in the labour embodied in the machine itself, and treats the productivity gains of mechanisation as altering the values of commodities by cheapening the labour required to produce them. The continuity is striking. Where the notebooks dramatise the growing disproportion between the labour expended and the agencies set in motion, the published analysis converts the disproportion into a mechanism: competition forces each capital to cheapen production by embodying more knowledge in its apparatus, the embodied knowledge raises productivity, raised productivity lowers the value of each unit of output, and the pressure renews itself. The drama is preserved. The obituary for the measure is not pronounced.

### Why does the fixed capital reading treat the passage as a claim about productive forces?

Because the knowledge the passage celebrates is located inside the machinery system, as a property of the apparatus and of its arrangement, rather than in a new substance of wealth floating free of production. Claims about what the apparatus contains belong to the analysis of productive forces, not to value theory.

There is also a question of what kind of statement the value theory can even accommodate, and the fixed capital reading presses it with some force. To call knowledge directly productive, in the passage's sense, is to say something about the sources of use-values, about how material wealth, the mass of useful things, is generated. The theory of value was never a theory that labour alone generates material wealth in that sense. Its author insisted, against admirers who turned labour into a metaphysical substance, that nature and the accumulated instruments of earlier labour are co-sources of the useful product, and that labour's unique role lies in the social form: under generalised commodity production, the products of private labours are socially validated and measured through exchange, and labour time supplies the measure because it is the common expenditure that the form equalises. Confusing the two registers, the material sources of wealth and the social measure of value, is the oldest error in the reception of this theory, flagged by its author in print during his lifetime. The fixed capital reading charges the strong reading with committing that error in a sophisticated idiom: from the premise that knowledge swells the mass of use-values out of all proportion to the hours worked, it infers that the measure of value has collapsed, when the theory predicts exactly such swelling as the normal consequence of productivity growth within the measure's reign. The charge is serious and the strong reading's best defenders have felt obliged to answer it, which is why the dispute repays attention rather than summary dismissal from either direction.

## The English phrase inside a German manuscript

Small facts sometimes discipline large debates, and the language of the phrase is such a fact. The notebooks were written overwhelmingly in German, the language of their author's philosophical formation and of the scholarly audience he first imagined. Into that German prose, at the moment of naming the social stock of knowledge turned productive power, he set two English words. The detail is documented in the manuscript and remarked by its editors, and it has attracted a weight of interpretation out of proportion to its size, so a glossary article should say plainly what the detail can and cannot show. It can show that the author was reaching for an expression rather than deploying one he owned. Writers borrow a foreign phrase when their own language offers no ready equivalent with the right resonance, or when the borrowed phrase carries associations from a literature they are reading. Marx excerpted English political economy extensively during those years, moved daily among languages, and treated his notebooks as a workbench where formulations from several tongues lay side by side awaiting assignment. An imported label on a workbench is a tool picked up for a job, not a cornerstone laid for a building.

What the detail cannot show is equally worth stating, since ingenuity has been spent on both sides. It cannot show that the phrase translates some German concept that translation then betrayed, because there is no German original behind it to be betrayed. It cannot show that the author regarded the notion as foreign to his project, since he borrowed terms throughout his career for notions he made entirely his own. And it cannot settle, by itself, whether the underlying thought was central or marginal, since authors coin their most casual labels and their most consequential ones in the same borrowed idiom. The value of the fact is narrower and, for that reason, sturdier. It reminds every reader that the phrase entered the world as an improvisation inside a private draft. When later writers elevated the improvisation into the name of an epoch, they performed an act of theoretical construction for which they, and not the notebooks' author, bear responsibility. Owning that responsibility would cost the strong reading nothing in argumentative force. Its best defenders occasionally do own it, presenting their work as development rather than exegesis, and those presentations are the most persuasive the reading has produced.

A related curiosity concerns capitalisation and the definite article. In the manuscript the words sit inside sentences, lower in profile than the monumental form they later acquired, where the general intellect marches through titles and chapter headings as a protagonist of history. The transformation of a passing phrase into a protagonist follows a familiar pattern in the reception of this author, whose corpus contains several expressions that posterity promoted beyond their textual rank. The pattern is not in itself discreditable. Intellectual traditions grow by seizing on fertile suggestions and cultivating them past anything their originator envisaged, and a tradition forbidden to develop its sources would be a museum. The scholarly obligation is bookkeeping: the record should show which growth is the author's and which is the tradition's, so that praise and blame are delivered to the right addresses. Much of the heat in this dispute comes from misdirected delivery, with the strong reading claiming the author's prestige for conclusions he never drew, and its opponents occasionally blaming the author for extravagances he never uttered. The thin basis, stated carefully, corrects the addresses in both directions.

## Counting occurrences and weighing an absence

Philology has a blunt instrument that this dispute needs: count the occurrences. A concept that organises a theory leaves tracks across a corpus, recurring at the joints of the argument, gathering definitions, distinctions, and applications as it goes. Surplus value leaves such tracks. So do commodity, labour power, and the reserve army, each returning hundreds of times across published and unpublished writing alike, each acquiring precision through repetition under varying conditions. The general intellect leaves no such trail. It occurs only rarely, clustered within a single stretch of a single manuscript, and then vanishes from a corpus that its author continued to expand for a quarter of a century, through works in which the machinery analysis, the fixed capital analysis, and the analysis of science's role in production all receive extended treatment. Rarity alone proves little, since a writer may state a decisive idea once and build on it silently. Rarity combined with total absence from the published sequels, in passages where the idea would have been directly serviceable, proves a good deal more. It establishes that the author did not treat the formulation as load bearing, whatever he thought of it during the winter he wrote it down.

The absence has a second dimension that interpreters sometimes miss because it requires reading the published machinery analysis as a replacement rather than as an abbreviation. When the author returned to the factory system in the first volume of Capital, he rewrote the account of knowledge in production with care. Science appears there as a force appropriated by capital, the intellectual powers of production appear as attributes of the collective labourer and of the apparatus, and the worker's separation from the knowledge governing the process is stated as a defining feature of the system. The conceptual work the notebook phrase performed is therefore not missing from the published work. It is present, distributed across analyses that do the work without the label and, critically, without the corollary that the measure of value is failing. A reader comparing the two treatments watches an idea being absorbed into a theory on the theory's terms. Absorption is what mature systems do to promising notebook material. The strong reading reverses the direction of travel, treating the notebook as the radical original and the published absorption as a falling away, a timidity, or an unfinished state. That reversal is a hermeneutic decision, not a discovery, and it should be defended as one, openly, against the simpler hypothesis that the author refined the thought by embedding it and discarding its extravagant corollary.

### Does absence from the published work settle the argument by itself?

No. Authors abandon formulations for many reasons, and a discarded label can hide a retained thought, which is why the absence must be read together with what the published machinery analysis actually does. It performs the notebook's conceptual work inside the theory of value, without the corollary that the measure has failed.

There is a useful parallel inside the same corpus that clarifies the standard being applied, because the tradition has faced this situation before and reached for the same tools. The early manuscripts of 1844, with their vocabulary of alienation and species being, were likewise unpublished, likewise recovered in the twentieth century, and likewise made the basis of a reading that claimed to find the true centre of the author's thought in private pages the published work had supposedly betrayed. The ensuing controversy, which ran for decades, was not settled by denouncing the manuscripts or by enthroning them. It was settled, insofar as scholarship settles anything, by passage level comparison: which early formulations survive into the mature work, in what altered form, carrying which conclusions and dropping which others. Alienation survived as analysis while shedding parts of its early philosophical dress. Exactly that discipline, applied to the fragment on machines, yields the verdict this article defends. The tendency analysis survives, embedded in the treatment of machinery and fixed capital. The corollary about the measure of wealth does not survive, and the social factory built on that corollary finds no footing in the published architecture at all.

## The thin basis stated as a claim

The argument so far can be compressed into the claim this article exists to name, and naming it gives readers a handle they can carry into other corners of the literature where the same pattern recurs. The thin basis: the phrase itself was written by Marx a very small number of times, in English, inside a German notebook he never published, and an entire theory of contemporary capitalism rests on it, which does not make the theory wrong but does mean it cannot claim Marx's authority in the way its proponents commonly do. Each clause of that sentence does work. The small number of occurrences fixes the scale of the textual foundation. The English inside German marks the formulation as borrowed and improvised rather than coined as doctrine. The unpublished status assigns the citation its proper evidentiary weight. The concession that thinness does not falsify protects the claim from the obvious rejoinder and keeps the dispute where it belongs, on argument and evidence. And the closing clause identifies the real stake, which is authority: whose name may legitimately be attached to the theory when the theory is taught, cited, and wielded against opponents.

Authority is not a vanity metric in this tradition; it is a working instrument. Appeals to what Marx showed, demonstrated, or predicted close arguments in seminar rooms and movement publications with an efficiency that no living theorist's name can match, and that efficiency is precisely why the bookkeeping matters. A reader who believes that the obituary of the law of value was pronounced by the author of Capital will approach the claim with a deference the claim has not earned from the published corpus, and will correspondingly undervalue the critics who point out that the corpus teaches the opposite. Redistributing the credit is therefore an intellectual service and not a debating trick. If the strong reading is right, it is right as the achievement of Negri, Virno, Lazzarato, Marazzi, Berardi, and their interlocutors, thinkers who took a suggestion and built a research programme on it, and the programme should be judged the way programmes are judged, by its explanations and its record against the evidence. If the programme fails, its failure belongs to them as well, and the notebooks' author should not be billed for a prediction he never published.

The pattern generalises, and recognising it is part of the article's purpose, since the series has met the pattern before in other dress. A preface of a few pages has been made to carry a complete philosophy of history. A polemical label coined by opponents has been mistaken for a school its supposed members never joined. In each case the remedy was the same unglamorous labour: return to the source, establish its size, its status, its date, and its afterlife in the author's own hands, and then rebuild the assessment on that foundation. Glossary work of this kind rarely wins applause, because it confiscates the excitement of a large claim before the claim has been examined. Yet confiscating unearned excitement is a precondition of examining anything. A reader who knows exactly how much text stands behind the general intellect can still be persuaded by the strong reading, and some passages of this article will state that reading as persuasively as its own authors do. The persuasion will then rest on the reading's merits, which is where the strong reading's serious defenders have always insisted it should rest, even when their popularisers reached for the shorter route of inherited authority.

## The road from the notebooks to workerism

The strong reading did not spring from the passage unaided; it was carried there by a political and intellectual formation with its own history, and understanding that formation explains both the reading's insights and its characteristic exaggerations. Italian workerism arose in the early 1960s among theorists who believed that the official labour movement had misread the direction of capitalist development by treating the working class as a passive object of economic laws. Against that passivity they asserted a Copernican reversal, as Tronti's Workers and Capital of 1966 framed it in substance: capitalist development should be read as a response to working class struggle, with mechanisation, reorganisation, and state policy following the pressure of workers' refusal rather than dictating its terms. Panzieri's earlier essay on the capitalist use of machinery had prepared the ground by insisting, against a neutral view of technology, that machinery under capital is designed and deployed as an instrument of command over labour, a claim he supported from the published critique and from the notebooks alike. Within that formation, the fragment on machines acquired a significance its first readers in other traditions had not assigned it. Here, apparently, was the author himself describing a stage in which direct labour shrinks toward insignificance beside the knowledge embodied in the system, and in which the terrain of conflict accordingly shifts from the individual workplace toward society as a whole.

The political logic of the appropriation deserves to be stated sympathetically, because caricature at this point would weaken the article's later criticism. A movement confronting mass factory labour, and then watching production reorganise in ways that scattered and recomposed that labour, needed a theoretical account of why struggles erupted outside the factory gates, over housing, transport, services, and the organisation of social life generally. The inherited doctrine located exploitation at the point of production and treated the rest of social existence as a superstructural reflection, which left the new struggles theoretically homeless. The fragment offered a home. If production itself had become social in the strong sense, if the productive power at work was the population's general knowledge and cooperation rather than the hands at a bench, then the factory's walls no longer bounded the site of value creation, and struggles across society could be read as struggles within production rather than as echoes of it. The social factory, the name this current gave to the resulting picture, was therefore not a decorative metaphor. It was the theoretical charter for a politics, and its adherents valued the fragment precisely because it seemed to supply the charter from within the master's own workshop.

Historians of the current note a further circumstance that sharpened the reading's edge. Workerism defined itself against the orthodoxies of the established communist parties, whose theoretical establishments guarded the published canon and treated the notebooks, when they treated them at all, as juvenilia or as raw material safely superseded. Seizing on the unpublished text was thus also an act of internal polemic: the heretics claimed a scripture the orthodoxy had neglected, and read it against the orthodoxy's settled doctrines of stages, measures, and the primacy of the factory. That polemical charge helps explain the reading's tone, its preference for the passage's most vertiginous formulations, and its relative indifference to the passage's qualifications, since a weapon text is seldom quoted with its safety catch engaged. None of this discredits the reading. Texts conscripted for live disputes often yield genuine discoveries under the pressure of use, and the workerist attention to the notebooks permanently enriched the tradition's understanding of machinery as an instrument of command. But the genealogy does mean that the strong reading arrived with commitments already formed, and that the passage was asked to ratify a direction of travel chosen on other grounds. A reader weighing the reading's claim to be a discovery about the text should know that it was also, from the beginning, a solution to a problem the text had not posed.

## Negri and the reading at full strength

The strong reading received its most systematic statement from Antonio Negri, whose lectures on the notebooks, delivered in Paris in the late 1970s and published as Marx oltre Marx in 1979, in English as Marx Beyond Marx in 1991, proposed nothing less than a relocation of the centre of the author's thought. Negri's Grundrisse is not a draft of Capital but a rival summit, the text in which the theory of crisis and of the tendency of the profit rate is joined to a theory of the collective worker and of communism as the abolition of the law of value itself. Within that architecture the fragment on machines functions as the keystone. Negri reads the passage as identifying the historical threshold at which labour time ceases to be the measure of wealth because production has come to rest on the general intellect, on the cooperation and knowledge of the socialised worker, and he draws the consequence without flinching: value theory, understood as the theory of labour time's command, describes a passing form, and the present is the epoch of its crisis. The lectures' famous verve, their willingness to treat the notebooks as a book of the future rather than a record of the past, made them the single most influential gateway through which later readers approached the fragment, and any account of the strong reading that omits Negri's role mistakes a tradition for a spontaneous response to a text.

Stated at full strength, the reading has a coherence that its critics are obliged to honour. Begin with the passage's own logic. The author writes that as industry develops, wealth's creation comes to rest less on labour time than on the potency of the agencies those hours set going, and that labour time thereby becomes a miserable measure for a wealth that has outgrown it. Negri asks what intellectual honesty requires of a reader who takes those sentences seriously rather than decoratively. If the measure shrinks toward irrelevance while production swells, then either the sentences are rhetoric, or the theory of value contains a self abolishing tendency that its author glimpsed and his published work, constrained by its polemical and pedagogical tasks, never fully confronted. Negri chooses the second horn and builds. The collective worker of the notebooks, the social individual whose knowledge and cooperation constitute the real productive force, becomes the protagonist of a history in which capital increasingly appears as a parasitic shell around a productivity it did not create and cannot measure, appropriating through command, rent like extractions, and the organisation of circulation a wealth whose production has escaped its metric. On that basis Negri and his collaborators could present the fragment not as an embarrassment to the published theory but as its prophetic supplement, the place where the theory's own development points beyond the theory's letter.

### What does Negri claim the notebooks show about the law of value?

That the law enters a terminal crisis once production rests on social knowledge and cooperation, since labour time can no longer measure a wealth whose real basis is the general intellect. Negri's lectures treat the notebooks as the summit of the author's thought, where that crisis is theorised directly.

The reading's defenders add a methodological plank that any fair hearing must include. Textual hierarchy, they argue, is not a neutral science. The privileging of published over unpublished writing reflects the accidents of an author's circumstances, the censorship and exile and poverty that shaped what could be printed, and the conservatism of literary executors, as much as it reflects considered judgement. The same tradition that demotes the notebooks in this dispute has elsewhere built entire edifices on posthumous material, on the second and third volumes of Capital themselves, assembled by another hand from manuscripts their author left in far rougher condition than the Grundrisse. If rough manuscripts can ground the theory of profit and rent, the objection runs, then rough manuscripts cannot be ruled out of court when they ground an inconvenient corollary. This article returns to that objection in its own section and concedes its force against any crude version of the counter-reading. For the moment it is enough to register that the strong reading, in Negri's hands, is not a slogan but an argued interpretation with a hermeneutic, a politics, and a striking textual exhibit, and that it persuaded a generation of serious readers for reasons that go beyond fashion.

## Virno, the multitude, and knowledge as the productive force

Paolo Virno gave the strong reading its most philosophically elaborated form, and his version deserves separate treatment because it shifts the emphasis in a way that matters for the empirical test later in this article. In A Grammar of the Multitude, published in Italian in 2003 and in English in 2004, and in essays surrounding it, Virno takes up the fragment's figure of the general intellect and asks what social form corresponds to a productive power that consists of knowledge, language, and cooperation held in common. His answer is the multitude: a plurality whose productive capacities are linguistic and cognitive before they are manual, whose labour involves virtuosity, communication, and the exercise of generic human faculties in public, and whose cooperation is not organised by any single employer within a single workplace but precedes and exceeds every particular employment. The general intellect, for Virno, is not primarily the knowledge frozen in machines. It is the living repository of shared competences, the intellect as a public endowment, which particular capitals tap whenever they hire bearers of those competences and which no capital produces for itself. The machinery of the fragment thus becomes, in his hands, one pole of a duality, with the living, linguistic, cooperative intellect as the other and weightier pole.

The shift has consequences that a careful reader should track. Marx's passage locates social knowledge in the apparatus, as fixed capital confronting the worker. Virno's development relocates the emphasis toward knowledge as an attribute of labour power itself, inseparable from the people who carry it, exercised in activities where the product is communication and relation rather than a material object. From that relocation flow the characteristic themes of the literature that followed: the blurring of the boundary between labour time and life time, since the competences exercised in work are cultivated across the whole of social existence; the centrality of language, the most general and least ownable of productive instruments; and a politics centred on the multitude's capacity to withdraw or redirect its cooperation. Virno is also, among the strong reading's authors, the most explicit about the fragment's textual peculiarities, dwelling on the phrase itself and on its status with a philologist's pleasure. His work therefore resists the accusation that the reading depends on ignorance of the source. It depends, instead, on a wager: that the passage's suggestiveness licenses construction in a direction the author indicated but did not travel, and that the value of the construction is to be judged by the light it throws on forms of labour the nineteenth century could not exhibit.

Alongside Virno, the collaborative work of Michael Hardt and Antonio Negri carried the reading to its widest audience. Empire in 2000, Multitude in 2004, and Commonwealth in 2009 successively elaborated a picture in which production has become biopolitical, producing not only goods but social relations and forms of life themselves, and in which the common, the shared stock of knowledge, affect, and cooperation, is both the principal productive resource and the stake of political conflict. The fragment on machines is cited throughout that trilogy as the anticipatory text, the point where the author of Capital allegedly foresaw production's escape from the measurable working day into the unmeasurable expanse of social cooperation. The trilogy's reach means that for many readers the strong reading is simply what the phrase means, encountered first in those pages and only later, if ever, traced back to the notebooks. A glossary article exists for such readers. It does not ask them to abandon the trilogy's questions, many of which are genuine and pressing. It asks them to notice the distance between the questions and the citation, and to hold the trilogy's claims at the valuation appropriate to constructions of the year 2000 rather than discoveries of the year 1858.

## Immaterial labour and the social factory

Maurizio Lazzarato's essay on immaterial labour, first published in French in 1996 and circulated in English through the collection Radical Thought in Italy in the same period, supplied the strong reading with its most influential category for describing work itself. Immaterial labour, in Lazzarato's formulation, names the activities that produce the informational and cultural content of commodities: the competences of communication, the production of images and meanings, the cultivation of tastes and publics, together with the affective and relational work through which social bonds themselves are produced and maintained. The category was offered as a development of the fragment's insight. If the decisive productive force is the general intellect, then the labour that matters most is the labour that produces and reproduces that intellect: education in the broadest sense, communication, the making of symbols and relationships, performed not only in designated workplaces but across the fabric of social life. Lazzarato drew the boundary consequence explicitly. The distinction between production and reproduction, between the working day and the time outside it, loses its grip when the capacities exercised in production are formed everywhere and always, and the factory's discipline gives way to forms of command that operate through the organisation of subjectivity itself.

The social factory is the spatial counterpart of that temporal blurring, and it predates the immaterial labour category, reaching back through the workerist tradition to the claim that the whole of society has been subsumed under the production relation. In its mature form the thesis holds that the distinction between inside and outside the workplace, on which the classical picture of exploitation depended, has been dissolved by the development the fragment anticipated. Value production, on this account, is no longer an event localised in factories and offices during contracted hours. It is a property of social cooperation as such, harvested by capital through mechanisms that owe less to the wage contract than to the ownership of the infrastructures, standards, and channels within which cooperation occurs. The thesis explains the strong reading's characteristic moves in debates far from the notebooks: its suspicion of the wage as the privileged site of analysis, its interest in rent as the form of appropriation appropriate to a capitalism feeding on a common it did not produce, and its political focus on the multitude's latent power to reclaim the common. Each move follows from the fragment once the fragment is read as prophecy. Each move loses its textual charter, though not necessarily its independent merits, once the passage is read as a note on fixed capital.

### What does the idea of immaterial labour add to the fragment?

A description of the work that supposedly embodies the new productive force: activities producing informational and cultural content, communication, and social relations, carried on across social life rather than inside a single workplace. The category converts the passage's tendency into a sociology of contemporary work.

A glossary must also record the internal tensions that the category's critics, including sympathetic ones, identified early. Immaterial labour gathers under one heading activities with wildly different relations to value production: the designer whose labour is organised by an employer under strict time discipline, the performer whose product is inseparable from the performance, the carer whose work reproduces labour power, and the casual contributor whose traces are harvested without any labour relation at all. A category that classifies by the texture of the activity, its immateriality, rather than by the social form under which it is performed, risks repeating at a higher level the error the fixed capital reading identified in the fragment's misuse: confusing the material character of a product with the social determination of the labour producing it. Sewing a garment and composing an advertisement differ in materiality; both may be wage labour producing surplus value, or neither may be, depending on the relations under which they occur. The defenders of the category reply that its point is diagnostic rather than taxonomic, naming a shift in the composition of labour that any adequate politics must register. That reply has force as far as it goes. It does not restore the fragment's authority for the stronger claim, that the law of value has ceased to operate, which requires the taxonomic work the category declines to perform.

## Cognitive capitalism as a period claim

Where the workerist tradition supplied a politics, the literature gathered under the name cognitive capitalism supplied a periodisation, and the distinction matters because period claims invite a kind of testing that political charters evade. Christian Marazzi's Capital and Language, published in Italian in 2002 and in English in 2008, argued that the linguistic and communicative character of the new production had transformed the mechanics of value and crisis alike, drawing on the fragment to authorise a reading of financial turbulence as a symptom of the measure's breakdown. Franco Berardi's The Soul at Work, published in Italian in 2005 and in English in 2009, traced the subsumption of mental and affective capacities, the soul in his deliberately provocative vocabulary, and described the exhaustion and pathology that follow when the general intellect is harnessed to valorisation without remainder. Yann Moulier Boutang's Cognitive Capitalism, published in French in 2007 and in English in 2011, gave the period its most systematic statement, proposing a third form of capitalism succeeding the mercantile and the industrial, in which the accumulation of knowledge and the capture of its externalities displace the accumulation of physical plant as the system's driving logic. Carlo Vercellone, in essays across the same years, connected the period claim back to the wage relation itself, arguing that the crisis of the law of value manifests as a crisis of the wage based society that the measure had organised.

Taken together, these works convert the fragment into the founding document of an epoch. The conversion has a rhetorical structure worth noticing, because it recurs wherever the phrase is used in its strong sense. A tendency identified in a notebook, the growing weight of knowledge within production, is first promoted into a mechanism, then the mechanism is promoted into a period, and finally the period is invoked to explain phenomena, from financial instability to the reorganisation of work, whose connection to the original tendency is asserted by the period label rather than demonstrated case by case. Each promotion is arguable. The accumulation of promotions is what a glossary should flag, since a reader who accepts the period label at the door has unknowingly accepted the whole staircase. The counter-literature's most effective move, examined in later sections, is to descend the staircase one step at a time, conceding the tendency, questioning the mechanism, and refusing the period until the evidence for a broken measure is produced in a form that could, in principle, have come out the other way.

It should also be recorded, in fairness to the period literature, that its authors were responding to real transformations that more orthodox treatments handled clumsily. The growing share of production costs absorbed by research, design, and the cultivation of demand; the strategic centrality of knowledge that firms do not own outright but must continually access; the difficulty of assigning the productivity of a cooperative process to the contributions of its individual members; the restless expansion of the working day into hours once protected from it: these are phenomena, not inventions, and the orthodox vocabulary of the mid twentieth century factory described them badly. The cognitive capitalism literature earned its audience by describing them vividly and by insisting that a theory of capitalism must have something to say about the production of knowledge itself. The dispute is not between observers and deniers of change. It is between two accounts of what the change does to the system's core relation, whether it dissolves the wage relation and its measure from within, or whether it represents the most developed and sophisticated form of their operation. That is an empirical and theoretical question of the first rank, and the fragment, properly weighed, is a contribution to it rather than its settlement.

## The three readings table

The three readings table is this article's findable artifact: the rival interpretations of the fragment set out in parallel, so that a reader can see at a glance what each claims the passage establishes, what textual support each can muster, what each predicts about the observable world, and how the record bears on the prediction. Its rows compress arguments made at length in the surrounding sections, and a reader who disputes a cell should dispute the corresponding argument, since the table asserts nothing the prose has not earned. Its discipline is the discipline of the whole article: the readings are graded separately on textual support and on empirical fortune, because a reading can be textually faithful and empirically empty, or textually extravagant and empirically suggestive, and conflating the two grades is how the dispute usually generates more heat than light.

| Reading | What it claims the passage establishes | Textual support | What it predicts | How the record bears on it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The strong reading | Labour time no longer measures wealth, the law of value no longer operates, and production has overflowed the workplace into social existence generally | A few notebook pages read as prophecy, with the published corpus treated as an unfinished or superseded statement | The wage relation weakens and contracts as knowledge becomes the decisive productive force, and profit comes visibly unmoored from labour employed | The wage relation has extended rather than contracted across the decades the reading covers, and profit remains bound to the employment and discipline of labour, which tells against the prediction |

Reading the table well requires resisting a temptation it inevitably creates, the temptation to treat its verdicts as a scorecard in which the middle row wins by splitting differences. The middle position is not a compromise manufactured for peace. It is the reading that survives two independent examinations: it accounts for the passage's actual location, language, and afterlife in its author's hands, and it accounts for the observable direction of the wage relation across the period the strong reading claims as its vindication. The strong reading fails the first examination on the evidence of the corpus and fails the second on the evidence of the world, while succeeding at something the table has no column for, which is the generation of questions. Some of the most productive inquiries in the field, into the production of knowledge, the organisation of cooperation, and the forms of appropriation that ownership of shared infrastructures makes possible, were provoked by the strong reading's overreach. A table cannot grade provocation. The prose around it can, and this article's verdict, stated fully in later sections, is that the strong reading should be kept for its questions and retired for its conclusions, while its claim on the notebooks' authority should be surrendered in either case.

## The counter-reading from textual scholarship

The counter-reading begins where this article began, with the artefact, and its first move is to insist that the burden of proof sits with whoever promotes a notebook passage over the published architecture it supposedly overturns. Textual scholars who have edited, translated, and annotated the manuscript legacy bring to the dispute a kind of evidence that theorists sometimes underrate: knowledge of how the author actually worked. Those notebooks were written in a burst of nocturnal labour under the pressure of an expected commercial crisis, by a writer racing to get his system onto paper before events overtook him, and they bear the marks of that haste in their compression, their repetitions, and their willingness to state a tendency in the grammar of an accomplished transformation. The same writer then spent years slowing down. He drafted, published a first instalment in 1859 that quietly omitted most of the notebooks' more speculative reaches, drafted again, and produced in 1867 a first volume whose machinery chapter handles the fragment's themes with a discipline the notebooks never attempted. A scholar reading that trajectory sees selection at work, the considered retention of some formulations and the considered abandonment of others, and asks the strong reading a simple question: by what principle is the abandoned formulation enthroned over the retained ones? The principles usually offered, that the notebooks are more radical, more candid, more prophetic, are aesthetic and political preferences dressed as method. They would not be accepted for any other author in the canon, and the counter-reading declines to accept them here.

The textual case has a second, quieter layer concerning the passage's immediate surroundings, which quotation tends to amputate. The fragment sits inside a discussion of fixed capital and of the contradictions of capital's development, surrounded by analyses in which the wage relation, the working day, and the production of surplus value remain the operative machinery of the argument. The sentences calling labour time a miserable measure occur as the climax of a conditional exploration: capital, by developing the productive forces, posits a condition in which its own measure becomes inadequate, and the exploration is part of a larger demonstration that capital is a moving contradiction, a form that generates the premises of its own supersession while reproducing itself through them. Read in place, the passage is about the system's inner tensions, stated in the future oriented grammar the author reserves for tendencies working themselves out across history. Read out of place, as a bulletin about an arrival, it becomes something its grammar does not support. The counter-reading's philological point is that the strong reading systematically misreads tense and mood, converting the author's characteristic subjunctive of tendency into an indicative of fact, and then congratulating the author on his foresight when the converted claim appears to fit later developments.

### What do textual scholars ask the strong reading to explain?

Why a formulation its author never republished, in decades of revision during which the surrounding machinery analysis was repeatedly reworked for print, should govern the interpretation of the theory that the published revisions actually present. Promotion of the discarded over the retained requires a principle, and radicalism is not one.

None of this requires the textual scholars to deny the passage's brilliance, and the honest ones do not. The fragment is among the most dazzling stretches of the notebooks, a place where the author's imagination outruns his system and glimpses a production resting on organised knowledge with a clarity that still startles. Brilliance, however, is a property of passages, not a warrant for doctrines, and the history of this author's reception is littered with brilliant passages that scholarship has learned to handle with care precisely because their brilliance tempts readers to build on them beyond their strength. The 1844 manuscripts are brilliant. The notes on James Mill are brilliant. The Theories of Surplus Value contain brilliant sustained improvisations that the author never organised into the published plan. In each case maturity as a reader consists in holding two judgements together: the passage rewards the closest attention, and the passage cannot be cited as though its author had staked his theory upon it. The counter-reading asks nothing more exotic than that the fragment on machines be granted the same mature handling, and it notes that the request is treated as scandalous only because a living theoretical school has mortgages drawn on the passage's inflated valuation.

## The value-form objection: wealth is not value

The second strand of the counter-reading comes not from editors but from theorists of the value form, and it is the strand that engages the strong reading on its own theoretical ground rather than on the ground of textual status. Michael Heinrich, whose introductory volume on the three books of Capital appeared in German in 2004 and in English in 2012, and whose editorial work on the complete writings gave him an unmatched command of the corpus, states the objection in its cleanest form. The strong reading, Heinrich argues, confuses how the productive forces develop with the transformation of the value relation, treating a change in how wealth is materially produced as though it were a change in the social form that makes products into values and labour into value producing labour. But the theory never claimed that labour time measures material wealth in the sense of the mass of use-values. It claimed that, under generalised commodity production, the social validation of private labours occurs through exchange, and that labour time functions as the measure within that social process for as long as the process lasts. An explosion in the productivity of labour, driven by embodied science, is not an embarrassment to that claim. It is one of the claim's central predictions, worked out where the published work analyses relative surplus value, where the entire point is that capital revolutionises production precisely to cheapen commodities and shorten the portion of the day in which the worker reproduces the wage.

Heinrich's reading of the fragment follows directly. The passage describes the tendency of fixed capital to absorb the productive powers of social knowledge, and its dramatic sentences about the measure express, in the notebooks' speculative register, the contradiction between the forces capital develops and the value form within which it develops them. That contradiction is real and permanent, and the published work knows it well: capital must simultaneously expand production's knowledge basis and confine the producers within the wage relation, must cheapen labour's product while depending on labour as the source of surplus. Nothing in the contradiction implies a historical moment at which the measure quietly expires and a new principle of wealth silently takes office. Measures of this kind do not expire from internal inadequacy. They are abolished with the relations that sustain them, or they persist, generating their characteristic pressures and crises. A reader who announces the measure's expiry on the strength of productivity growth has mistaken a permanent tension for a terminal event, and the value-form literature's patience wears thinnest at exactly this point, since the mistake is the one the published theory most explicitly warns against, in its repeated insistence that wealth and value are different magnitudes governed by different determinations.

### How does the value-form critique restate the fragment?

As a description of a standing contradiction rather than a dated transition: capital develops knowledge based production while preserving labour time as the measure, and the friction between those two facts is the system's normal condition, visible in the published analysis of relative surplus value, not a sign that the measure has lapsed.

The value-form strand adds a methodological rider that this article endorses and applies throughout. Claims about the law of value are claims about a social form, and they cannot be tested by pointing at the technical composition of production, at how much knowledge a process embodies or how few hands it employs, because the form abstracts from exactly those features. The appropriate evidence concerns the form's own phenomena: whether products are still produced for exchange by autonomous producers, whether labour power is still bought and sold, whether the working day still divides into the portions the theory distinguishes, whether profit still behaves as a claim on unpaid labour rather than as a toll on a commons. On that evidence the value-form theorists rest their case, and it is the same evidence this article's empirical sections examine in the strong reading's own preferred terms, the direction of the wage relation and the mooring of profit. The two strands of the counter-reading thus converge from different directions on a single demand: show us the form changing, in the form's own register, or withdraw the obituary and keep the tendency, which nobody disputes.

## Caffentzis and the measure that refuses to die

George Caffentzis occupies a distinctive place in this dispute because he cannot be cast as an outsider defending orthodoxy against a heterodox current he never joined. A philosopher formed within the autonomist tradition itself, a collaborator in its journals and campaigns, he turned the tradition's tools against the strong reading's central claim in a series of essays gathered in In Letters of Blood and Fire of 2013, and the insider's angle of his critique gives it a weight that purely scholarly objections lack. Caffentzis asks the question the strong reading least enjoys: if labour time has ceased to measure value, why does capital continue to behave, with undiminished ferocity, as though time were the substance of its command? The working day remains contested hour by hour and minute by minute across the world economy. Employers invest enormously in the measurement, surveillance, and compression of labour time, in the elimination of pauses, the subdivision of tasks, and the calibration of effort against the clock. Workers resist in the same currency, contesting schedules, speeds, and the boundary of the day. A measure that had genuinely lapsed would not need this apparatus of enforcement, and a ruling class that had transcended labour time would not spend its substance policing it. The persistence of the time struggle, on every continent and in every kind of workplace the strong reading cites as post industrial, is for Caffentzis the standing refutation of the obituary.

His critique has a second edge, directed at the political moral drawn from the fragment by some of its enthusiasts. If value production has escaped the workplace and the measure has collapsed, then the classical levers of working class power, the withdrawal of labour, the contest over the working day, the organisation of producers at the point where surplus is extracted, might seem obsolete, relics of an economy that has dissolved into flows of cooperation. Caffentzis reads that conclusion as a counsel of disarmament dressed as sophistication. The wage relation's extension across the planet, the incorporation of ever more populations into dependence on employment for survival, means that the old levers are attached to more lives than at any earlier moment, and a theory that declares the measure dead while the measured multiply mistakes its own milieu, the knowledge saturated enclaves of wealthy economies, for the world. The fragment's glamour, on his account, flatters the experience of a stratum for whom labour time has indeed become irregular, project based, and hard to clock, and generalises that experience into a world historical verdict that the global evidence contradicts. Coming from within the tradition that produced the strong reading, the charge of milieu generalisation is the hardest for that reading to answer, and its literature has mostly answered by changing the subject to the qualitative novelty of the work its milieu performs.

The exchange clarifies what an empirical test of the dispute would even look like, and this article adopts the clarification gratefully. The strong reading cannot be tested by cataloguing novelties, since every epoch of this mode of production has teemed with novelties and the theory was built to digest them. It can be tested only at the level of the relations it pronounces transformed: the wage relation's extent and intensity, the working day's contested persistence, the dependence of profit on the employment of labour under discipline. Those are the phenomena Caffentzis marshals, and they are the phenomena the table in this article uses as the record against which each reading's prediction is graded. A reading that predicts the weakening of the wage relation and confronts its extension has failed a test it cannot decline without abandoning its claim to describe the world rather than a mood. The sections on the empirical test develop that grading in full. Here the point is attribution: the test is not an external standard imposed by hostile scholarship, but the standard the strong reading's own dissident heirs proposed when they asked what the world would look like if the obituary were true.

## The strongest objection: selective standards for manuscripts

Fairness requires the objection to the counter-reading to be stated in its most damaging form, and this section states it without dilution, because the article's verdict is worth nothing if it survives only the weak version of the other side. The objection runs as follows. The tradition's textual standards are applied selectively, and the selection follows convenience. When posthumous manuscripts support doctrines the scholarly establishment favours, their rough state is no obstacle: Capital's second and third volumes are themselves posthumous constructions, assembled by Engels from drafts of varying completeness, and no one proposes to demote the theory of the falling rate of profit or of rent to the status of notebook speculation on that account. The early manuscripts are celebrated, taught, and built upon by the same institutions that dismiss the Grundrisse fragment as juvenilia. Even within the notebooks themselves, passages congenial to orthodoxy, on pre-capitalist formations or on the method of political economy, are quoted as authoritative without a whisper about their unpublished state. The unpublished objection is therefore not a principle. It is a verdict in search of a rationale, deployed against the one passage whose conclusions embarrass the settled doctrine, and a reader is entitled to suspect that if the fragment had prophesied the eternal reign of labour time, its textual status would trouble nobody at all.

The objection gains strength from a second observation about how canons actually behave. Authors are not always the best judges of their own strongest insights, and the history of thought is crowded with cases in which a writer's discarded draft, private letter, or marginal note contained the idea for which posterity chiefly values him, while the published system he laboured over reads as a monument to a lesser conception. Editors recover such material precisely because the published record is an artefact of circumstance: of publishers' demands, of censorship, of an author's strategic sense of what an audience could bear, of simple exhaustion. Marx's circumstances were extreme by any standard, exile, poverty, illness, and the perpetual deferral of the great work's completion, and the distance between what he published and what he wrote is correspondingly vast and correspondingly unfit to bear the weight of a simple hierarchy. A hermeneutics that mechanically prefers the published would have to prefer, in this corpus, the relatively cautious public statements of a writer who knew his books would be read by police as well as by disciples. The strong reading's defenders are entitled to ask whether deference to publication is deference to the author's judgement or to his constraints.

Stated so, the objection has genuine force, and this article concedes two of its claims outright. The first concession is that selective invocation of textual status is a real vice in the literature, practised by custodians of orthodoxy exactly as charged, and that any counter-reading resting on status alone, without engaging the passage's argument and the world's evidence, deserves the suspicion the objection directs at it. The second concession is that the passage does describe a real tendency, visible to any honest observer of production's development, and that its author's failure to republish the formulation proves nothing about the tendency's existence. Where the objection overreaches is in its implied conclusion, that because status arguments can be abused, status is irrelevant and the fragment may be cited with the full authority of the corpus behind it. The answer to selective standards is consistent standards, not no standards. Applied consistently, the standard this article uses is the one the tradition's best scholarship applies to the 1844 manuscripts and to the posthumous volumes alike: posthumous and notebook material is admitted as evidence of the author's thought, weighed against the published architecture, and allowed to extend or complicate that architecture where it coheres with it, while claims that contradict the architecture bear the burden of demonstrating, from the world and not only from the manuscript, why the architecture should be revised. The fragment passes that test for the tendency and fails it for the obituary, which is why the middle position, and not the counter-reading in its crudest form, is this article's verdict.

## Tendency, contradiction, and the grammar of the passage

Much of the dispute reduces, on inspection, to a disagreement about how to read one author's characteristic grammar, and a section devoted to that grammar earns its place because the same grammar governs passages across the corpus that no one disputes. Marx's analyses habitually proceed by identifying a tendency, following it to the limit at which it would transform the system that generates it, and then identifying the counteracting tendencies and institutional forms through which the system contains, defers, or displaces the transformation. The profit rate's falling tendency is stated together with its counteracting influences in the same breath. The tendency toward the concentration and centralisation of capital is stated together with the credit system and the joint stock form that reorganise its expression. The tendency of machinery to expel workers is stated together with the absorption mechanisms that complicate its employment effects. A reader trained on the published work therefore approaches a notebook passage announcing labour time as a miserable measure with a set of reflexes: ask what the tendency is, ask what contains it, and ask whether the author is describing a destination or staging a contradiction whose two poles persist together. The strong reading, on the counter-reading's account, simply lacks those reflexes, or suspends them for this one passage, treating a limit statement as a timetable.

The passage's own wording supports the reflexes. Its key sentences are framed around what capital does to its own basis: capital strives to reduce labour time to a minimum while positing labour time as the sole measure and source of wealth, and the passage lingers on that self opposition as the interesting fact. A system whose measure had actually lapsed would not exhibit the striving and the positing in the same gesture; it would have settled into whatever succeeds the measure, and the passage would describe the successor. No successor is described. What is described is an intensifying incongruity, production swelling on a basis of social knowledge while the social form continues to reckon in hours, and the incongruity is presented as a spur to crisis and transformation in general rather than as a report that transformation has occurred. Even the passage's most quoted sentence, in the Nicolaus translation, says that the human being comes to relate to the production process as its watchman and regulator, a statement about the worker's posture within production that presupposes the production process, with its capitals, its wages, and its working days, still standing around the watchman. The watchman is employed. The regulator draws a wage. The process being watched produces commodities for exchange. The fragment's world, inspected closely, is the published theory's world with its knowledge content raised to a speculative pitch, not a successor world in which the theory's categories have been retired.

### Why does the passage's grammar matter to its interpretation?

Because its author habitually states tendencies together with the forms that contain them, so a limit formulation in his prose marks a contradiction to be analysed rather than an arrival to be announced. Reading the fragment's superlatives as a timetable mistakes the corpus's grammar at the exact point where the dispute turns.

There is a further grammatical point about the phrase itself that the middle position draws on and the strong reading passes over. The general intellect is introduced as a power that production draws upon immediately, and the sentence's emphasis falls on production, on the generation of use-values, on the material process whose powers have been socialised. The theory of value enters the passage as the contrasting pole, the measure that the swelling production strains against. A reading faithful to the sentence's own distribution of emphasis would therefore develop the passage as a contribution to the theory of the productive forces and of fixed capital, exactly where its author shelved it, and would treat the value theoretic corollary as the passage's speculative horizon rather than its thesis. Horizons in this author are real features of the landscape; his work is unimaginable without them. But a horizon is where the land runs out, and building a periodisation of the present on a horizon, while the surveyed territory of the published work contradicts the building, remains the strong reading's unforced error.

## The empirical test: the direction of wage dependence

Every reading of the fragment that claims to describe the present submits, willingly or not, to an empirical test, and the test's design follows from the readings' own predictions rather than from any standard imposed by their opponents. The strong reading holds that production has escaped the workplace, that the wage relation no longer organises the creation of wealth as it once did, and that labour time has ceased to function as wealth's measure. If that were so, the observable world should show a characteristic signature. Dependence on the sale of labour power for survival should be shrinking as a share of human life, displaced by other modes of subsistence and other sources of livelihood. The working day as an institution should be losing its centrality, its boundaries dissolving not merely at the edges but as a organising fact. Profit should present itself increasingly as a toll levied on cooperation and knowledge held in common, visibly detached from the quantity and discipline of labour that particular capitals employ. The two rival readings predict the opposite signature on the first two counts and a more complicated one on the third: wage dependence extending, the working day persisting as the contested frame of exploitation, and profit remaining stubbornly correlated with the employment of labour even where the knowledge content of production soars. The record, stated at the level of direction and duration that this article's discipline permits, without figures or endpoints, supports the second signature.

Wage dependence has extended across the long arc that the strong reading covers. Populations that once subsisted through agriculture organised by household and community, through customary rights, through petty production for local markets, have been drawn, generation by generation and region by region, into dependence on employment for access to the means of life, in a movement its author analysed under the heading of the separation of producers from the conditions of production and which has continued to widen its geographical and social reach through every transformation in production's technical character. The movement is not a smooth line, and this article claims nothing about its pace in any particular place or period. Its direction over decades is not in serious dispute among historians of any school, and its direction is the opposite of the one the strong reading requires. Nor is the extension a merely formal matter of legal status. It carries the substance of the wage relation with it: subjection to an employer's direction during contracted hours, dependence on the wage for housing, food, and care, exposure to dismissal as a discipline, and the division of the day into the portion that reproduces the wage and the portion that accrues to the employer, whether or not the worker or the employer ever names the portions. Where that relation is still expanding its domain, reports of the measure's death have been, at a minimum, filed early.

### What would the world show if the strong reading were right?

A contraction of wage dependence as other livelihoods displaced employment, a fading of the working day as an organising institution, and profit presenting itself as a toll on shared knowledge rather than as a return bound to labour employed. The observable directions run the other way on the first two counts.

The strong reading's defenders sometimes answer the wage evidence by reclassifying it, arguing that the extension of employment is compatible with their thesis because the employment extended is itself increasingly cognitive, relational, and social, so that the social factory grows precisely by absorbing the wage relation into its expanded definition of production. The answer deserves respect as an attempt to save the thesis from its most obvious difficulty, but a glossary should note its cost. A thesis that predicts the wage relation's weakening, and then, confronted with its strengthening, reinterprets the strengthening as confirmation, has rendered itself immune to the evidence it once invoked. Immunity of that kind is the mark of a framework that has stopped making predictions and started issuing licences for redescription. The middle position incurs no such cost. It expects wage dependence and knowledge intensive production to grow together, since nothing in the published theory sets them at odds, and it is confirmed rather than embarrassed by their joint advance. On the test the readings themselves propose, that asymmetry settles the empirical question as decisively as social evidence ever settles anything.

## The empirical test: profit and the labour it employs

The second arm of the test concerns profit, and it must be handled with care because the surface of modern profit is genuinely confusing and the strong reading's intuitions feed on real features of that surface. Some of the largest and most celebrated profits in the world economy accrue to owners of positions rather than to organisers of production: to holders of rights, standards, reputations, and junctions whose income resembles the rent of a landlord more than the profit of an industrialist, a resemblance the series examines in its own right in its treatment of platform capitalism. The strong reading takes such profits as exhibits for its thesis: here, apparently, is wealth created by social cooperation and knowledge, appropriated by owners whose contribution is a legal title, in a process where the labour employed by the appropriator bears no proportion to the sums appropriated. If profit can detach itself so completely from labour employed, the argument runs, then the mooring the counter-reading insists upon has already slipped, and the fragment's prophecy is being fulfilled in the accounts.

The counter-argument distinguishes, as this tradition always distinguishes, between the production of surplus value and its distribution among claimants. A profit that arrives as rent like income in one set of accounts is a share of a social surplus produced somewhere, and the question the theory asks is never whether the recipient produced it but whether the total it is drawn from arises from the exploitation of labour within the wage relation. Ground rent in the classical analysis already exhibited the pattern: the landowner produces nothing, employs almost no one, and appropriates a portion of surplus value created in production elsewhere, and the existence of that appropriation was never taken, by the theory's author or by anyone else, as evidence that the law of value had ceased to operate in agriculture. It was taken as evidence about distribution, about the claims that property in scarce conditions of production can stake against the surplus. The modern junction owner extends the pattern's reach without altering its logic. That particular capitals can grow rich on titles while employing few is a fact about where the surplus settles. That the surplus exists to be settled, in an economy whose working population labours under time discipline for wages, is the fact the strong reading needs to dissolve and cannot.

There is a further observation, less often made, that cuts against the detachment thesis from inside the firms most often cited for it. The owners of knowledge based positions employ labour on a vast scale, and they manage that labour with an intensity of time discipline, measurement, and performance calibration that the classical factory would recognise as its own. The engineers, moderators, logistics workers, carers of the apparatus, and maintainers of the infrastructures through which the common is tapped are wage workers, hired by the hour or the year, dismissed at will, and organised to extract from their contracted time the maximum of directed effort. An appropriation machine that supposedly floats free of labour time turns out, on inspection, to sit atop an enormous and tightly clocked employment relation, and to depend for its celebrated margins on the difference between what that labour costs and what the positions it maintains can extract from the social surplus. Detachment at the level of the individual firm's accounts coexists with attachment at the level of the system, and the theory of value was always a theory of the system. Grading the readings by the system's behaviour, which is the only grading either side's serious representatives should accept, the test's second arm confirms the first: profit remains moored, through channels the classical analysis of rent and distribution already mapped, to the employment of labour inside that same relation.

## The middle position stated in full

The middle position can now be stated without the scaffolding of the dispute around it, and it is a position with content rather than a refusal to choose. It holds, first, that the fragment identifies a real and consequential tendency: as production develops, the knowledge embodied in its apparatus and organisation grows relative to the direct labour expended, the productive power at work becomes increasingly social in its sources, and the contribution of any individual worker to the joint product becomes increasingly difficult to isolate and attribute. It holds, second, that this tendency operates within the law of value and is indeed one of that law's own engines, since the competitive pursuit of relative surplus value is precisely what drives each capital to embody more knowledge in its processes and cheapen the labour embodied in its products. It holds, third, that the tendency therefore intensifies rather than relaxes the wage relation's grip, because the same development that raises knowledge's weight also separates more producers from independent access to the conditions of production and renders them dependent on employment, while subjecting the employed to forms of measurement and direction that the swelling knowledge basis makes finer and more pervasive. And it holds, fourth, that the fragment's author understood his own insight in something like these terms when he came to write for publication, since the published work preserves the tendency, supplies its mechanism, and omits the obituary, an editorial pattern that the middle position reads as the author's verdict on his notebook's speculation.

Each element of that statement can be illustrated from the phenomena the strong reading itself prizes, which is part of the middle position's strength: it does not need a different world, only a different account of this one. Consider how hard it is to isolate what each individual contributes within a cooperative, knowledge saturated process, a difficulty the strong reading treats as the measure's tombstone. The published theory never supposed that value is computed by timing individuals and summing their contributions. Socially necessary labour time is a social magnitude, established behind the producers' backs through exchange and competition, and it tolerates, indeed presupposes, that no participant can say which portion of a joint product belongs to which pair of hands. The foreman's stopwatch was a device of discipline, not the theory's measuring instrument. That a design team's members cannot apportion their output among themselves therefore tells us nothing about whether their product's value is governed by the labour time socially required for its reproduction, any more than a choir's inability to apportion a chord tells us whether the performance was rehearsed for a contracted number of hours. The middle position absorbs the observation as a commonplace of cooperation under capital. The strong reading must inflate it into a metaphysical crisis of measure, and the inflation is where its argument does the work its evidence cannot.

### What does the middle position concede to the fragment?

A real tendency with real consequences: production rests ever more heavily on socially accumulated knowledge, and the individual's share in a joint product grows ever harder to trace. The concession is generous because the tendency is genuine, and it stops where the evidence stops, before the obituary for the measure.

Consider, likewise, how the line between working time and the rest of life has thinned, the phenomenon Virno and Lazzarato describe with undeniable vividness. The middle position reads that blurring through the published category of the working day's contested extension. Capital has always pressed against the day's limits, lengthening hours, intensifying effort within them, and colonising the worker's time of recovery and preparation, and the forms of that pressure change with the forms of production. That competences are now cultivated across a life and harvested within employment shows the pressure operating on the formation of labour power itself, a development the theory can state in its own vocabulary, as a change in what the wage must reproduce and in where the costs of that reproduction are borne. Stating it so loses none of the phenomenon's critical edge. It keeps the edge attached to a relation, the wage relation, whose abolition would actually end the pressure, rather than to a metaphysics of measure whose alleged collapse leaves the pressure's beneficiaries exactly where they were.

## Relative surplus value: the mechanism that absorbs the tendency

The middle position's claim that the published work absorbs the fragment's tendency is not a gesture; it can be cashed out in a specific mechanism, and naming the mechanism is the strongest single argument this article has to offer. The published analysis distinguishes two ways in which surplus value is increased. Absolute surplus value grows by lengthening the working day. Relative surplus value grows by shortening the part of the day where the worker reproduces the wage's value, and it grows through exactly the development the fragment describes: the revolutionising of production by science and machinery, which cheapens the goods the worker consumes and thereby cheapens labour power itself. Every advance in the knowledge basis of production, every embodiment of social intellect in apparatus and method, enters the published system through that door. The capitalist who introduces a knowledge saturated process does not thereby exit the law of value. He gains a temporary advantage because his individual production time falls below the social standard, pockets the difference until competitors generalise his method, and then watches the standard itself fall, cheapening the commodities involved and resetting the race. The general intellect, translated into the published vocabulary, is the accumulated stock from which those temporary advantages are drawn, and its growth is the normal metabolism of a system governed by labour time, not a solvent leaking into the system's foundations.

This mechanism also explains a feature of the observable world that the strong reading finds anomalous and the middle position expects: the coexistence of soaring knowledge content with relentless time pressure. If knowledge displaced labour time as the system's organising magnitude, the pressure on time should relax as knowledge accumulates, the way pressure on a scaffolding relaxes when the building learns to stand without it. The observed pressure does the opposite. The more knowledge a process embodies, the more finely the remaining labour within it is timed, subdivided, monitored, and compared, because each fragment of necessary labour expelled from the product raises the competitive stakes of the fragments that remain, and because the expensive apparatus itself must be kept in motion across as many hours as can be extracted from the day and the roster. Expensive fixed capital and intense time discipline are not rivals. They are partners, each unintelligible without the other, and their partnership is written into the published analysis of machinery's effects on the working day. A theory that predicted their divorce and confronts their marriage has been answered by the record in the plainest terms available to social inquiry.

There is a corollary about crisis that the mechanism supplies and that the period literature, in its Marazzi strand, attempted to claim for the opposite side. Financial and industrial crises, on the middle position's account, do not signal that the measure has broken down and values have become unmoored from labour. They express the measure working itself out through its contradictions: overaccumulation relative to the exploitable labour available, disproportions between departments of production, and the violent reimposition, through devaluation, of standards that credit had allowed capitals to evade. The appearance of unmooring during a crisis is the appearance the published theory predicts for a system in which claims on future surplus circulate as though they were surplus itself. Nothing in that account requires a broken measure, and the burden remains on the period literature to exhibit a crisis whose mechanics the older account cannot state. That burden, like the burden on the wage question, has been carried mostly by renaming. The mechanism stands, and with it the middle position's central contention: the fragment's tendency is the published theory's engine, misread by the strong reading as the theory's funeral.

## Science as an alien power in the published work

A reader who doubts that the published work absorbed the fragment's insight should be directed to the machinery chapter's treatment of science, where the absorption is visible on the page. There the development of production is described as a process in which the knowledge governing the process migrates steadily out of the worker and into the conditions that confront the worker: into the machine's construction, into the chemical and mechanical principles the process exploits, into the organisation of the factory as a designed system. The intellectual powers of production are said to expand on one side because they vanish on the other, a formulation as stark as anything in the notebooks, and the separation between knowledge and the labourer is named as a characteristic product of the capitalist form rather than as an accident of particular industries. Science itself is described as being pressed into the service of capital, enlisting as a power distinct from and hostile to the worker, its social character appearing in the form of the capitalist's property. Every element of the fragment's picture is present: the socialisation of knowledge, its objectification in the apparatus, its alienation from the producers, its operation as a direct power over production. What is absent is only the corollary, and its absence in a chapter that had every occasion to state it is the loudest silence in the dispute.

The chapter also supplies what the fragment, in its speculative haste, omits: the consequences for the worker's day and the worker's wage that follow from knowledge's migration, worked out as mechanisms rather than as portents. Machinery cheapens commodities, including the commodities that reproduce labour power, and thereby enlarges relative surplus value. Machinery lengthens and intensifies the working day by making the expensive apparatus's idle hours a loss its owner strives to abolish. Machinery recomposes the workforce, drawing in new categories of labour, expelling others, and degrading the price of labour power whose skills the apparatus has absorbed. A reader who works through those mechanisms possesses an account of knowledge based production that explains the phenomena the strong reading gestures at, the changing composition of work, the pressure on time, the strategic importance of embodied science, while retaining the explanatory core the strong reading discards. The middle position's wager is that explanatory retention beats prophetic drama: an account that explains more, with the measure intact, is preferable to an account that explains less while announcing the measure's death, and the preference is methodological rather than sentimental.

There is one more published resource that the dispute underuses, the analysis of cooperation and of the collective labourer in the first volume's middle chapters. There the productive power of combined labour is treated as a power that arises from combination itself, exceeding the sum of individual powers, and appearing, under capital, as a power of capital rather than of the labourers who generate it. The general intellect's living pole, the cooperation and shared competence that Virno emphasises, has its published ancestor in that analysis, which already knew that social combination produces a surplus of productive power and already asked to whom that surplus appears to belong. The notebooks' phrase adds a name and a machinery setting to an insight the published work develops under other headings. Genealogies of that kind are the normal fate of fruitful suggestions in a maturing system, and tracing them deflates the melodrama of the rival summits: the notebooks and the published volumes are not competing revelations but stages of one inquiry, in which the later stage's silence about a label is evidence about the label, not about the insight the label briefly carried.

## Machinery as command: what the workerist reading keeps

A verdict against the strong reading's conclusions should not be allowed to confiscate its genuine discoveries, and the most durable of them is the insistence, descending from Panzieri's 1961 essay on machinery under capital's command, that technology under capital is never a neutral stock of knowledge applied to production but always also an organisation of command. Panzieri argued, from the published texts and the notebooks together, that the design of machinery and the layout of processes embody decisions about the control of labour: about how far skill is removed from the worker and built into the apparatus, about the pace at which effort is extracted, about the visibility of the worker's activity to supervision. The fragment supports that insistence handsomely, since its picture of the worker reduced to watchman beside an intelligent apparatus is a picture of a power relation before it is a picture of a productivity statistic. Later scholarship on the labour process, including currents with no allegiance to workerism, absorbed the point so thoroughly that its origin in this dispute is now easy to forget. The middle position keeps it without reservation. Knowledge embodied in fixed capital is knowledge wielded, and the wielding is a relation between people, enforced through the wage.

Keeping that discovery matters for the article's balance because it identifies what the strong reading's tradition contributed that the counter-reading's purely textual strand cannot supply. Textual scholarship can establish that the obituary was never published. It cannot, by itself, explain why the fragment electrified readers who worked in and studied the great factories, or why its vocabulary proved so apt for describing the experience of production reorganised around systems whose intelligence confronts the operator as an alien design. The workerist answer, that the fragment names the lived subordination of labour to embodied knowledge, survives the collapse of the obituary and should be carried forward into any adequate account of knowledge based production. Where the tradition erred was in supposing that the aptness of the description implied the expiry of the measure. Subordination to embodied knowledge is a form the wage relation takes when the knowledge basis of production rises. It is not a successor to the wage relation, and the workers who experience it experience it as employees, timed, directed, and dismissible, which is why their struggles continue to take the classical forms of contests over hours, pace, staffing, and pay, even when the apparatus beside which they stand embodies the intellect of a civilisation.

### What survives of the workerist reading if the obituary falls?

The analysis of machinery as an instrument of workplace command, of technology as organised power over labour rather than neutral technique, together with the attention to struggles beyond the factory gate. Those contributions stand on the published texts as much as on the fragment, and they lose nothing when the prophecy is retired.

There is a lesson about intellectual traditions folded inside this salvage operation, and the article states it because the same operation will be needed wherever the series meets a fertile overreading. Traditions advance by a rhythm of exaggeration and correction, in which a generation seizes a text, stretches it toward the phenomena its moment exhibits, and generates insights that a later generation retains while relaxing the stretch. The retention is not a compromise between the exaggerators and their critics. It is how a living doctrine normally metabolises, and it requires critics who can tell the insight from the inflation, which is a finer skill than either wholesale defence or wholesale dismissal. The fragment on machines has now passed through one full cycle of that rhythm. The exaggeration produced the social factory, immaterial labour, and the period of cognitive capitalism. The correction, proceeding through textual scholarship, value-form theory, and the evidence of the wage relation's extension, retires the period and the obituary. What remains in the doctrine's hands, the analysis of embodied knowledge as command within a persisting law of value, is more valuable than what either party to the quarrel originally possessed, and the article's remaining sections are devoted to securing that remainder against the misreadings that still surround the phrase in its popular circulation.

## What a broken measure would actually look like

The dispute suffers from a vagueness about its central term that a glossary is well placed to repair, because both sides invoke the operation or non operation of the law of value as though its symptoms were self evident. They are not, and specifying them disciplines the argument. The law of value, in the published theory, names the process by which the labours of autonomous producers are socially equalised through exchange, so that products trade in proportions governed, through all the mediations the later volumes add, by the labour time socially necessary for their production, and so that the aggregate surplus extracted appears as profit on capital. For that law to cease operating, in any sense stronger than metaphor, the phenomena it governs would have to change in kind. Products would have to cease being allocated by exchange among autonomous units. Labour power would have to cease being a commodity bought for a wage and set to work under direction. The working day would have to stop dividing, in the accounts that matter, into the part that replaces the wage and the part beyond it. Profit would have to cease behaving as a magnitude constrained, at the level of the whole system, by the unpaid labour performed within it. A claimant who believes the law has lapsed should be able to point at several of those phenomena and show them transformed. A claimant who can point only at the rising knowledge content of production has shown a change the law predicts and digests, and the difference between those two showings is the difference between the strong reading's rhetoric and its evidence.

Specifying the symptoms also clarifies the status of measurement difficulties, which the strong reading's literature treats as though they were symptoms of the law's failure. It is true that the values of individual commodities are not directly observable, that prices deviate from values systematically as well as accidentally, and that the knowledge saturated character of much production makes the labour embodied in a particular product unusually hard to trace. None of that distinguishes the present from any earlier period of the theory's application. Value was never a quantity read off a dial. It is a social relation inferred from the behaviour of prices, profits, wages, and crises across the system, and its evidence has always been systemic and indirect. The demand that the law produce a meter reading, followed by the announcement that no meter exists and the law has therefore lapsed, applies a standard the theory never claimed to meet and whose application to any earlier epoch would dissolve the law retroactively across the whole history the strong reading treats as its proper domain. Consistency requires the same evidential standard for every period: systemic behaviour, assessed in the round. Assessed so, the present exhibits the law's signature phenomena, competition enforcing socially necessary standards, capitals ruined by costs above those standards, wages pressed toward the value of labour power, and crises reimposing proportions through destruction, with a fidelity that the obituary cannot explain away.

There is a final clarification concerning the word measure itself, whose ambiguity lubricates the whole dispute. In one sense, to measure is to assign a number, and in that sense labour time measures value imperfectly and through mediations, as it always has. In another sense, a measure is the standard that regulates a process, the magnitude whose fluctuations discipline the participants, and in that sense the question is whether labour time still disciplines production: whether capitals that expend labour wastefully are punished, whether the cheapening of labour embodied in products remains the axis of competition, whether the working day remains the frame within which exploitation is organised and contested. The fragment's dramatic sentence trades on the first sense while the theory's claim concerns the second. Wealth's mass may indeed mock any attempt to reckon it in hours, as a cathedral mocks a reckoning in bricks, without the hours ceasing to govern the building trade. Keeping the two senses apart does not settle every question in the dispute, but it dissolves the appearance that a single notebook sentence has refuted a three volume theory, and dissolving false appearances of refutation is a large part of what glossaries are for.

## Misreadings corrected

The phrase's popular circulation has deposited a layer of plain errors over the dispute, and a glossary article clears them in a dedicated place so that the genuine disagreement can be seen without the undergrowth. The first error holds that the general intellect is a concept from Capital, where the machinery chapter supposedly expounds it. The machinery chapter expounds the migration of skill and science into the apparatus, as this article has shown, but the phrase does not occur there or anywhere in the published volumes, and citing it as a teaching of Capital misattributes a notebook formulation to the author's most considered public work. The second error treats the phrase as a developed concept, with a definition its author refined and a role he assigned it in a system. A developed concept leaves the tracks described earlier in this article, recurrence, distinction, application, and the phrase leaves none of them; it is a passing formulation of great suggestiveness, and describing it accurately means describing it as that. The third error supposes that Marx wrote the phrase in German and that its English form is a translator's choice, when the manuscript record shows the opposite: the English words stand in the German text, and translators into other languages are the ones who must decide how to handle an expression their author had already imported.

The fourth error concerns the sociology of the dispute itself, the assumption that the strong reading is the standard interpretation from which dissenters must excuse themselves. In the scholarship of the texts, the strong reading is a minority position associated with a specific and identifiable tradition, the Italian workerist current and its successors, while the mainstream of editorial and value-form scholarship reads the passage within the fixed capital analysis. Popular presentation inverts that distribution because the strong reading is more exciting and because its popularisers write for audiences beyond the scholarship. A student who meets the phrase first in its prophetic dress should know that the dress is not the consensus of those who edit the manuscripts. The fifth error is the mirror of the fourth and matters as much to this article's fairness: the supposition that establishing the thin basis refutes the theories built on the passage. It does not, and this article has insisted on the point from its opening paragraphs. A theory of cognitive capitalism or of the social factory stands or falls by its account of the world, and the textual argument removes only its borrowed authority. Readers who wield the thin basis as a refutation commit the same fallacy as readers who wield the passage as a proof, substituting bibliography for argument in the opposite direction.

### Which misreading does the most damage in teaching?

The attribution to Capital, because it assigns the phrase the authority of the author's most revised public work and thereby forecloses the questions a student should learn to ask about provenance. Correcting the shelf on which a text sits changes every judgement formed downstream of the citation.

Clearing errors has a cumulative effect that goes beyond the individual corrections. A reader who knows the phrase's shelf, its frequency, its language, and its reception acquires a kind of immunity to the rhetorical use of the phrase as an incantation, the move in which naming the general intellect is supposed to settle an argument about the direction of production without the argument being made. Incantations flourish where provenance is vague. They wither where a readership habitually asks the glossary questions: where is that written, in what kind of text, how often, and what did its author do with it afterwards? The series to which this article belongs exists to cultivate exactly that habit across the whole vocabulary of the tradition, and the present entry is among its purest exercises, since here the incantation and the provenance stand in the starkest disproportion in the entire lexicon.

## Neighbouring terms and the borders between them

Precision about one term requires borders against its neighbours, since the phrase travels in company and the company is often confused. The knowledge economy is a policy expression, coined in the literature of economic administration, naming an economy in which the production and use of knowledge occupy a growing place; it carries no theory of value and implies no verdict about any law's operation. Cognitive capitalism, by contrast, is a theoretical periodisation, the strong reading's name for an alleged third age of the system, and it implies the verdict in full. Immaterial labour names a category of activities, defined by the character of their product, and sits inside the strong reading's sociology without itself entailing the period claim. The social factory names a spatial and institutional thesis, that production's site has expanded to coincide with society, and it supplies the period claim with its geography. The general intellect, properly used, names none of those things. It names the stock of social knowledge considered as a productive force, in a phrase of 1858, and every extension beyond that denotation is a construction by later hands that the user of the term should be able to identify as such.

The borders matter practically because arguments in this field often proceed by sliding along the chain of neighbours, establishing an innocuous claim at one end and collecting a dramatic conclusion at the other. That knowledge occupies a growing place in production is the knowledge economy's platitude, true and important. That a growing share of work produces informational and cultural content is the immaterial labour thesis, defensible as description. That production's organisation overflows the individual workplace is the social factory's observation, partly true of the organisation of cooperation in many settings. None of those propositions, nor all of them together, yields the conclusion that hours have stopped measuring value, because that conclusion concerns a social form and not the inventory of activities, yet the slide from the first propositions to the last is the strong reading's characteristic gait in its popular form. A reader equipped with the borders can stop the slide at the joint where description ends and the form claim begins, and can ask for the specific evidence the form claim requires. That is the service a glossary performs for an argument: not winning it, but preventing it from being won by equivocation.

A second set of borders runs between the phrase and the published vocabulary that does its work legitimately. Fixed capital is the category under which the passage's machinery belongs. The collective labourer is the published figure for the combined workforce whose cooperation generates powers no individual possesses. Relative surplus value is the mechanism through which embodied knowledge raises the surplus. The industrial reserve army, the analysis of science as a force appropriated without equivalent, the treatment of cooperation's productive surplus: each of these published instruments handles a portion of the territory the phrase is asked to govern in its inflated usage, and handles it with distinctions the phrase lacks. A reader who masters the instruments discovers that the phrase adds a vivid name and a speculative horizon to an already furnished room. A reader who begins with the phrase and never acquires the instruments mistakes the nameplate for the furniture, and will describe the room's contents in a vocabulary of epochs and ruptures where the tradition's own vocabulary of mechanisms and tendencies would have explained what the epochs merely announce.

## How the phrase is taught, and how it should be

The phrase has become a fixture of syllabuses, where it is commonly introduced through its strongest interpreters and illustrated by the phenomena those interpreters select, with the notebooks' provenance mentioned, if at all, as a colourful detail rather than as a consideration bearing on the claims. Teaching of that shape produces a predictable graduate: one who can recite that Marx foresaw the knowledge economy, who associates the law of value's alleged lapse with the authority of the founder, and who has never been asked to compare the passage with the published machinery chapter or to consider what evidence would distinguish a tendency from a transition. The remedy is not to strike the phrase from syllabuses, since the dispute around it is one of the most instructive episodes in the tradition's modern life, exhibiting hermeneutics, politics, translation history, and empirical method in a single compact case. The remedy is to teach the dispute rather than the slogan, and to teach it in the order this article has followed: text first, with its status and its grammar; then the readings, attributed and at full strength; then the counter-readings, textual and theoretical; then the test, stated as predictions and graded against directions in the evidence; then the verdict, with its defeater attached.

Taught so, the phrase becomes an instrument for training judgement rather than a badge of allegiance, and the training transfers. A student who has learned to ask of the general intellect where it is written, how often, in what language, with what afterlife, and how the world would have to look for the claims made for it to be false, possesses a portable interrogation kit for every charismatic concept in the social sciences. The kit's questions are unglamorous and devastating in sequence. Provenance questions expose borrowed authority. Frequency questions expose promotion beyond rank. Afterlife questions expose the difference between an author's exploration and an author's commitment. Falsification questions expose the difference between a prediction and a licence to redescribe. A syllabus that drills those questions on this case sends its students into literatures far from Marxism with their defences up, which is a larger educational service than any verdict about the fragment itself, and it is the service the teachers addressed in this article's closing sections are urged to perform.

There is also a point about intellectual honesty in citation that teaching should make explicit, because the dispute's literature violates it in both directions and students absorb the violations as professional norms. The strong reading's popular form cites the passage without its status, letting readers assume a published doctrine. The counter-reading's polemical form occasionally cites the passage's rarity as though rarity were refutation, letting readers assume the theories built on it have been answered when only their pedigree has been questioned. Both moves are shortcuts past the reader's judgement, and both are unnecessary, since each side possesses genuine arguments that survive full disclosure. Full disclosure in citation is a small discipline with large effects: name the kind of text, give the translation, attribute the interpretation to its actual authors, and separate what the source says from what the interpreter builds. A literature that observed it would conduct this dispute at half its length and twice its clarity, and a glossary article can do its part by observing the discipline in its own pages, as this one has endeavoured to do from its first section onward.

## The verdict and what would reverse it

A verdict in this series carries its defeater with it, the statement of what would reverse the judgement, and the discipline is nowhere more necessary than here, where both camps have at times written as though no evidence could move them. The verdict of this article is that the counter-reading is the stronger position on the text and that the middle position is the defensible reading overall: the passage describes fixed capital's tendency to embody social knowledge, a tendency that is real and consequential, and it neither abolished nor is abolishing the law of value, whose signature phenomena, the extending wage relation, the contested working day, and profit's systemic mooring in employed labour, persist through the very developments the strong reading cites. The strong reading is retained as the dispute's great provocation, credited with the analysis of machinery as command and with forcing the tradition to theorise the production of knowledge, and retired as an account of the measure and as a claimant on the notebooks' authority.

The defeater is twofold, matching the verdict's two supports. On the textual side, the counter-reading would be defeated by the identification of an equivalent claim in Marx's published work: a passage, in a text he prepared for publication, stating that labour time is ceasing to serve as wealth's measure, or deploying the general intellect or a clear synonym as a category of the theory. Such a discovery would transform the dispute, because it would show the notebook speculation surviving the author's own editorial judgement, and the strong reading's claim on his authority would revive accordingly. On the empirical side, the verdict would be defeated by evidence that the wage relation is contracting rather than extending as the organising form of livelihood, sustained across regions and decades and attributable to the rise of knowledge based production rather than to collapse or exclusion, since a contraction of that character is what the strong reading's mechanism predicts and what the middle position's mechanism excludes. Either defeater, established, would restore the strong reading's standing and oblige this article's author to rewrite it. Neither has been established, and the second has been contradicted in its direction by the long movement of the world economy across the period in question.

### By what standard are the three readings graded?

By assigning each reading the weight its support can bear: the strong reading carries an epoch on a few notebook pages and grades lowest on textual support, the counter-reading rests on the passage's location and the published corpus, and the middle position grades highest overall by surviving both the textual and the empirical examinations.

Stating the defeater has a further function beyond fairness to opponents. It converts the verdict from a posture into a commitment, identifying the observations that its holder is bound to watch. The textual defeater directs attention to the continuing work of editing the complete writings, where new volumes and new readings of familiar manuscripts occasionally do surface formulations that revise settled judgements, and where any scholar's confidence should be indexed to the state of the edition. The empirical defeater directs attention to the long run behaviour of the wage relation, a magnitude about which honest observers can disagree at the margins while agreeing on the direction that would matter. A verdict with its watching brief attached is a live instrument. A verdict without one is a monument, and monuments in this tradition have a way of being quoted long after the ground beneath them has shifted, which is the very pathology this article was written to correct in the phrase's own case.

## Research frontiers the dispute leaves open

A settled verdict on the phrase should not be mistaken for a settled field, and a responsible glossary closes its argument by marking the questions that remain genuinely open, since those are where a researcher's effort will pay. The first concerns the production of knowledge itself, considered as an economic process in its own right. The published theory treats science largely as a free gift to capital, a force appropriated without equivalent once produced, and that treatment, adequate for an epoch in which inquiry was organised in universities and workshops outside the immediate circuit of production, strains against arrangements in which the production of knowledge is itself organised as wage labour within firms and funded as an investment expecting a return. Theorising that organisation, the labour process of inquiry, the forms of its product's appropriation, and the rents and profits that gather around it, is work the tradition needs and has only begun, and it proceeds best, this article suggests, with the published instruments of fixed capital, rent, and relative surplus value rather than with the obituary that would discard them at the moment they are most needed.

The second open question concerns the commons that the strong reading rightly placed at the centre of attention: the shared stock of knowledge, language, and cooperation that no single capital produces and every capital presupposes. The published theory has rich resources for analysing the enclosure and exploitation of such commons in other domains, above all in its treatment of land, and of how producers were parted from their conditions, and the application of those resources to the knowledge commons is a programme with genuine promise. Who maintains the commons, under what relations, and who pays for its reproduction? How are its products fenced, and what claims does fencing stake against the social surplus? What happens to a commons whose bearers are trained at social expense and harvested at private profit? Those questions descend from the fragment's insight without requiring its corollary, and the strong reading's best legacy may prove to be the insistence that they be asked, even as its own answers are set aside.

The third frontier is philological and modest, as befits the article's theme. The reception of the notebooks in Italian and French remains incompletely mapped for English readers, the untranslated strata noted earlier in this article still await their historians, and the editorial completion of the author's works in their historical critical edition continues to refine the textual basis on which judgements like this article's rest. A dispute that turns on provenance is a dispute that scholarship can genuinely advance, unlike disputes that turn on allegiance, and the advance will come from the unglamorous quarters: from translators, editors, and historians of journals, whose work determines what the next generation of readers will be able to check for themselves. The glossary's final counsel to the researcher is therefore double. Use the verdict, since the evidence supports it and teaching requires a position. And support the infrastructure that could overturn it, since a tradition whose verdicts cannot be overturned by its own scholarship has stopped inquiring and started merely repeating, which is the fate the fragment's career, in its incantatory popular form, most vividly warns against.

## The phrase in the wild: citation drift and its correction

Watching how a phrase is actually cited, in the wild of lectures, articles, and examination answers, teaches something that neither the text nor its systematic interpreters can teach: the mechanics by which authority inflates. The typical career of a citation of the general intellect runs through several hands before it reaches the reader who repeats it. A theorist builds on the passage and states the building honestly as construction. A textbook summarises the theorist and compresses the construction into an attribution, writing that Marx argued what the theorist argued from Marx. A lecturer takes the textbook's sentence and adds the warmth of paraphrase, so that the founder foresaw, predicted, or demonstrated the conclusion. A student writes the lecture's sentence into an essay, where it acquires the final polish of the unqualified present tense: Marx shows that labour time no longer measures wealth. At no single step did anyone lie. At every step a qualification evaporated, and the compound effect of the evaporations is a doctrine with an authority its origin never possessed and a certainty its evidence never earned. Citation drift of that kind is the ordinary entropy of intellectual life, and it operates on every tradition's charismatic texts, which is why the correction cannot be a single exposure but must be a standing practice of returning citations to their sources and reattaching their qualifications.

The practice has a technique, and the technique can be taught as concretely as any other scholarly skill. Trace the citation to the earliest accessible statement and compare the wording at each remove. Note where a named interpreter's claim becomes the founder's claim, where a tendency becomes a fact, where a conditional becomes a categorical, and where a translation choice hardens into a doctrine. Keep the comparison in writing, since drift thrives on the vagueness of memory. In the present case the technique yields a particularly clean specimen, because the distance between the first and last forms is so large: a few English words in a German notebook, hedged by their context in the grammar of tendency, against a periodisation of world capitalism taught as the founder's foresight. Few concepts in the lexicon offer so measurable a drift, and teachers who want a demonstration case for the technique could not do better than to set the passage and its popular summary side by side and let a class perform the comparison unaided.

Correction, to be effective, must also be proportionate, and proportionality is where polemical counter-readings sometimes fail their own standard. The drifted citation is wrong about authority and about certainty. It is not necessarily wrong about the world, and the correction that pretends otherwise merely installs a counter drift, in which the founder is made to have refuted in advance theories he never encountered. The founder did not refute cognitive capitalism, a literature separated from him by more than a century of development he did not witness. He left a theory whose categories, applied to that literature's claims, yield the verdict this article has defended, and the distinction between those two statements is the distinction between scholarship and ventriloquism. Keeping it is the whole of the glossary method: the dead are quoted for what they wrote, the living are answered for what they claim, and the two operations are never allowed to impersonate each other.

## Negri on crisis, and the notebooks as a rival summit

The strong reading's architecture includes a theory of crisis that deserves separate notice, because it shows how much construction the fragment is asked to support once the first promotion, from note to prophecy, has been granted. In Negri's lectures the notebooks present a crisis theory centred on the tendency of the rate of profit to fall and on the class struggle that drives the tendency's countertendencies, and the fragment supplies the theory's horizon: as the general intellect becomes the real productive force, the command of capital over living labour loses its material basis in the measurable working day, and crisis ceases to be a periodic malfunction inside the law's operation and becomes that law's permanent condition, the chronic instability of a form measuring a world that has outgrown measurement. Later writers in the tradition extended the thought to financial turbulence, reading the detachment of financial claims from any plausible basis in current production as the measure's breakdown made visible in the sphere of circulation. The construction is elegant and internally consistent. It is also, at every joint, dependent on the initial promotion, since a reader who declines to treat the passage as prophecy will read the same crises through the published theory's account of credit, fictitious capital, and the violent reassertion of proportions, an account written for exactly such phenomena and requiring no broken measure to explain them.

The rival summit claim, the proposal that the notebooks rather than Capital should be taken as the centre of the author's achievement, is the hermeneutic decision on which all the others rest, and it should be examined as a decision rather than absorbed as a discovery. Decisions of that kind are governed by criteria the decider owes the reader. Fruitfulness is one legitimate criterion, and Negri's partisans invoke it honestly: read from the notebooks, the corpus yields an account of the collective worker and of communism's material premises that read from the published volumes it yields only in fragments. Fidelity is another criterion, and it points the other way, since the published volumes represent the author's own selection among his materials under the discipline of anticipated criticism. Explanatory reach over the present is a third, and it is the criterion this article's empirical sections apply, with results unfavourable to the summit claim. A reader is entitled to choose fruitfulness and accept the consequences, which include owning the resulting theory as a construction of the twentieth century rather than a recovery of the nineteenth. What no criterion licenses is the silent slide between summits, citing the notebooks for authority when their speculation is wanted and the published volumes for authority when their rigour is wanted, without announcing that a choice has been made and defended. The slide, more than any single doctrine, is what the thin basis exists to block.

There is a historical irony in the summit dispute that a glossary may record without malice. The notebooks were elevated by a current that defined itself against the custodians of a canon, accusing them of freezing a living theory into scripture. The elevation then proceeded to freeze its own counter scripture, a handful of pages invested with an authority so complete that questioning their status came to mark the questioner as a custodian in turn. Traditions generate orthodoxies the way rivers generate banks, by flowing, and the only known prophylactic is the habit this article has practised throughout: returning to the artefact, stating its dimensions, and grading every claim by the support actually beneath it, including the claims of the graders.

## The rent turn inside the strong reading

An instructive development within the strong reading's own literature is its migration, over time, from the language of value production toward the language of rent, and the migration deserves attention because it tacitly concedes much of what the counter-reading asserts. In the earlier workerist formulations, social cooperation produced value outside the wage relation, and capital appropriated that value by enclosing the sites where cooperation occurred. In many later formulations, the emphasis shifts: capital is said to stand outside production proper, extracting rent from a common produced autonomously by the multitude, much as a landowner extracts rent from a fertility he did not create. The shift was motivated by honest difficulties. Locating value production in unmeasured social cooperation had proved hard to state without dissolving the concept of value altogether, while the rent formulation preserved the moral and political charge, capital as parasite on a creativity it does not organise, with a category the classical theory already possessed. Writers such as Andrea Fumagalli and Carlo Vercellone developed versions of the rent thesis explicitly, proposing that profit in the cognitive age increasingly takes the form of rent on the common.

The counter-reading greets the migration with a pointed welcome. Rent, in the published theory, is a distribution category: it presupposes a surplus produced under the law of value and claims a portion of it on the strength of property in a scarce condition of production. A literature that redescribes capital's take as rent has therefore re-entered the published architecture through the back door, since the rent it describes must be paid out of a surplus whose production it has not abolished, and the wage labour that produces the social surplus returns to the centre of the account whether the account names it or not. The welcome is not a refutation of the rent thesis itself, parts of which this article's treatment of profit has already endorsed as descriptions of where surplus settles. It is a demonstration that the thesis, carefully stated, is compatible with the middle position and incompatible with the obituary that launched the tradition. When the strong reading's descendants describe the present in the vocabulary of rent, enclosure, and distribution, they are writing middle position analysis with strong reading rhetoric, and a reader who notices the substitution can harvest the analysis while discarding the rhetoric at no cost.

### What does the turn to rent concede?

That appropriation can be described as a claim on a surplus produced elsewhere, which is the published theory's own account of rent, and which presupposes the law of value operating in the production the rent draws upon. The concession relocates the dispute from the measure's survival to the surplus's distribution.

The episode carries a general lesson about how theoretical disputes actually end. They rarely end with a capitulation document. They end with a migration of vocabulary, in which the losing side's phenomena are redescribed in the winning side's categories while the losing side's slogans continue to circulate as the redescription's packaging. Observers who track slogans will report the dispute unresolved forever. Observers who track categories can see the settlement forming: the obituary quietly withdrawn, the tendency retained, the surplus's production restored to the wage relation, and the interesting questions relocated to distribution, enclosure, and the commons, where the published theory's instruments of rent and primitive accumulation await the researchers the strong reading trained. A glossary that records the migration performs the service of dating the settlement, so that students are not sent to fight over ground the combatants have already, in their practice, vacated.

## The fragment among the notebooks' other explorations

Placing the fragment among its neighbours inside the notebooks further disciplines its interpretation, because the manuscript is full of explorations conducted in the same grammar of limit and tendency, many of which no reader dreams of promoting into doctrines. The notebooks speculate on the dissolution of forms of production based on exchange value, on the free development of individuality on the basis of the productive forces, on disposable time as the true measure of a future society's wealth, and on the transformation of the individual from a narrow specialist into a many sided participant in social production. Those speculations belong to the manuscript's forward looking stratum, its sketches of what a society beyond capital might make possible, and they share a characteristic: they describe the premises capital itself creates for its own supersession, stated from the standpoint of the supersession rather than of the present. The fragment's boldest sentences belong recognisably to that stratum. Their subject is what the growth of the productive forces makes possible and what it renders obsolete in principle, contemplated by a writer imagining the standpoint from which the obsolescence would be actual. Reading them as bulletins about the writer's own century, or about ours, confuses the manuscript's tenses in a way its first careful editors warned against.

The disposable time speculation is particularly instructive because its relation to the fragment is intimate and its correct reading is comparatively uncontroversial. In the society the notebooks imagine beyond capital, wealth would be measured by the free time available for the development of individuals, and labour time would lose its role as the measure precisely because the social form that imposes it would have been abolished. The fragment's sentence about the measure's miserableness breathes the air of that speculation: it glimpses, from inside capital's development, the standpoint from which the measure would be judged miserable, namely a standpoint after the measure's social basis has gone. The strong reading needs the sentence to describe a process occurring inside capitalism, a decay of the measure while the form persists. The notebooks' own architecture suggests the opposite sequence: the measure persists while the form persists, generating the contradiction the passage dramatises, and lapses only with the form, in a transformation the passage anticipates as politics rather than reports as economics. That suggestion, drawn from the manuscript taken whole rather than from the fragment taken alone, is among the counter-reading's most underused resources, and it illustrates the general rule that a passage's neighbours are part of its meaning.

A reader may test the rule by performing the experiment in reverse. Imagine the fragment had never been excerpted, and that a student encountered it in sequence, in the Nicolaus translation, amid the discussions of fixed capital and disposable time that surround it. Would the student close the book believing its author had announced the expiry of the law of value within capitalism? The question answers itself for most readers who try the experiment honestly, and the answer explains why the strong reading required the excerpting practices, the anthologies, the lecture courses built around the isolated passage, through which the fragment reached its public. Isolation is not falsification, and many great readings begin by isolating a passage from its context the better to hear it. But a reading that depends on isolation owes its audience the information that the isolation was performed, and the thin basis, once again, is simply the demand that the debt be acknowledged in the citation.

## Disposable time and the standpoint of the passage

The neighbouring speculation on disposable time deserves a section of its own, because it supplies the most plausible account of what the fragment's author thought he was doing, and an interpretation that can explain its author's intention in terms of his own project deserves preference over one that must treat the intention as an obstacle. The notebooks return repeatedly to a contrast between two measures of wealth. Under capital, wealth is measured by labour time, and the system's development is driven by the theft and saving of time: surplus labour extracted, necessary labour compressed, circulation accelerated. In a society of freely associated producers, the same development of the productive forces would ground a different measure, the disposable time in which individuals develop their capacities many sidedly, and the notebooks sketch that society's economics with an enthusiasm their author rarely permits himself elsewhere. The fragment on machines stands at the hinge between the two measures. It watches capital create the material premise of the second measure, a production resting on social knowledge in which direct labour shrinks toward a subordinate role, while continuing to impose the first measure with undiminished rigour, and it names the resulting incongruity as the sign that the form has become a fetter on the forces it developed.

Read at that hinge, the passage's sentences fall into place without strain. The measure turns miserable, in the passage's word, not in the sense that employers have stopped counting hours, a claim the author's own century would have refuted at a glance, but in the sense that the measure's inadequacy to the wealth it governs becomes visible as a social fact, generating the pressure toward a different organisation of production. The general intellect becomes directly productive in the sense that social knowledge, objectified in the system of machinery, does the productive work that individual skill once did, a transformation whose beneficiary under the existing form is capital and whose beneficiary under the imagined form would be the associated producers themselves. Nothing in the passage requires the reader to date the measure's expiry. Everything in it requires the reader to grasp the contradiction as a contradiction, a unity of opposed tendencies whose resolution is historical and political rather than automatic and economic. That grasp is the middle position, stated now from the author's side rather than from the evidence's side, and the convergence of the two routes on the same destination is the strongest assurance this article can offer that the destination is where the argument actually leads.

The hinge reading also explains the passage's emotional register, which interpreters often remark without explaining. The fragment is written with an exaltation unusual even in the notebooks, a sense of standing at a vantage from which the whole trajectory of production becomes visible at once. Exaltation of that kind belongs to moments of theoretical discovery, when a writer sees the premises of a future order assembled inside the present one, and it is entirely compatible with the sober recognition that the future order is not the present one. Prophecy, in the strong reading's sense, is a cooler and more journalistic affair, a prediction with a date implied. The fragment's heat is of a different kind: the heat of a thinker who believed he had found, in the machinery system's development, the material proof that his life's critique described a transitory form. Readers who share the belief and readers who reject it can alike recognise the heat's source, and recognising it removes the temptation to mistake intensity of conviction for proximity of arrival.

## Why the strong reading persuades: the experience it names

A verdict that cannot explain its opponent's persuasiveness has not finished its work, and the strong reading's persuasiveness is considerable and grounded in experience that any honest account must register. The reading names something real about the texture of work and life in knowledge saturated production: the sense that the capacities exercised in employment are formed across the whole of existence, in education, in sociability, in the unpaid cultivation of competences that employers then harvest; the sense that cooperation precedes the firm, that workers arrive already equipped with a shared world of language and know how which the wage purchases but did not create; the sense that the boundary of the working day leaks, as the instruments of work follow the worker home and the cultivation of employability colonises the hours nominally free. A theory that tells the bearers of those experiences that their sense of the world is an illusion, and that nothing has changed since the classical factory, will lose them, and deserve to. The strong reading won its audience by taking the experiences as data and building them into a theory of the epoch.

The middle position's response is not denial but relocation. Every element of that experience has a published category waiting for it. The formation of capacities across a life is the reproduction of labour power, whose costs and sites the theory always located partly outside the workplace, in the household and the school, and whose colonisation by capital's requirements is an extension of the wage relation's reach rather than a departure from it. The pre existing cooperation that employers harvest is the collective labourer's power, which the theory always said appears as a power of capital although the labourers generate it. The leaking working day is the day's contested extension, the oldest struggle in the corpus, conducted with new instruments. Relocation is not a verbal trick. It changes the politics implied by the experience, directing attention from a metaphysical event, the measure's collapse, to the relations that produce the experience and could be organised against: the wage, the day, the ownership of the apparatus in which social knowledge is objectified. A worker persuaded that production has slipped the workplace's bounds has been persuaded that the workplace levers are obsolete. A worker persuaded that the workplace now reaches into life has been given a reason to extend organisation along the same reach. The difference between those persuasions is the practical stake of this entire dispute, and it is why a glossary article about a phrase in a notebook can matter beyond the seminar room.

There is a reciprocal lesson for the counter-reading's partisans, which this article delivers with the same directness. A tradition that possesses the correct categories and declines to apply them to new experience, preferring to police citations while the experience goes untheorised, will forfeit its audience to whoever does theorise it, however extravagantly. The strong reading's career is partly a judgement on its opponents' sloth. How knowledge gets produced, how cooperation gets organised, the economics of the commons: these were available as research programmes inside the published theory for generations before the workerists forced them onto the agenda by the scandal of an overreading. Gratitude for the forcing is compatible with rejection of the overreading, and the healthiest posture for the tradition is the one this article has tried to model: take the questions, refuse the obituary, return the phrase to its shelf, and get on with the work the questions define.

## The cooperative substrate: language as the standing example

Among the phenomena the strong reading invokes, one recurs so often and carries so much of the argument's intuitive weight that it merits isolated examination: language. Virno's version of the reading rests heavily on the observation that the paradigmatic productive instrument of knowledge based work is language itself, a faculty no employer issues to the worker, no capital produces, and no contract can specify in its exercise, since linguistic competence is the common endowment of the species, acquired in socialisation and exercised in every act of cooperation. If the decisive instrument of production is a commons of that kind, the argument runs, then the wage purchases access to a productivity whose source lies outside the capital relation altogether, and the fragment's vision of social knowledge as the direct productive force stands vindicated in the most ordinary fact about how people work together. The observation is acute. The inference is where the dispute lives.

The published theory is not embarrassed by instruments that capital did not produce. It begins, indeed, from the general truth that capital presupposes conditions it does not create: a population separated from the means of production, a stock of techniques inherited from earlier modes, natural forces awaiting enlistment, and the cooperative capacities of the species, language emphatically included. The theory's question was never who manufactured the instrument but under what relations the instrument is set to work and to whom the product accrues. A loom woven by no capitalist still weaves surplus value when a wage worker tends it in a mill. A language learned at no capitalist's expense still produces surplus value when its speakers are hired to persuade, instruct, coordinate, and care within an employment relation, and the antiquity and commonality of the faculty alter neither the wage that purchases its exercise nor the division of the day in which it is exercised. What the linguistic turn genuinely adds is an appreciation of how thoroughly the presupposed commons penetrates production at its knowledge intensive end, and that appreciation, like the others catalogued in this article, enlarges the description of the tendency without touching the form.

### Does the common character of language exempt linguistic work from the law of value?

No, since the theory never required capital to have produced the instruments it enlists, only to command their exercise through the wage and to appropriate the product. A faculty acquired in common and exercised under direction for contracted hours stands inside the relation, however social its origins.

There is, however, a sharper point buried in the linguistic example, and fairness requires extracting it, because it identifies a genuine strain that the middle position should acknowledge rather than absorb too smoothly. Linguistic and cooperative capacities are not only presupposed by production; they are also continuously reproduced by activities that shade into production itself, in the conversations, instructions, and improvisations through which workers maintain the shared world their work requires. The boundary between reproducing the capacity and exercising it is genuinely porous in a way that the boundary between maintaining a loom and weaving with it is not. The strong reading deserves credit for forcing that porosity into view. The middle position's answer is that porous boundaries between production and reproduction are old inhabitants of the theory, which always knew that the worker's dinner and sleep reproduce labour power while also being life itself, and that a porous boundary is a problem for accounting, not a dissolution of the form. Acknowledging the strain, and the credit, costs the verdict nothing and buys the article the right to be believed when it says the reading has been taken seriously.

## Measurement without a meter: how the theory is tested

A recurring demand in the dispute, heard from the strong reading's side whenever the empirical test is proposed, is that the counter-reading produce its measurements: if labour time still measures value, let the values be computed and compared with prices, commodity by commodity, or let the claim to measurement be withdrawn as unfalsifiable piety. The demand sounds like scientific rigour and functions as a trap, since it applies to the present a standard that, applied consistently, would acquit the strong reading of ever having to measure anything either, while convicting the published theory of vacuity across the whole of its history. A glossary article should refuse the trap explicitly and state the theory's actual evidential practice, which is neither meter reading nor piety. The theory is tested the way theories of social forms are always tested: by the coherence of the mechanisms it proposes, by the range of phenomena those mechanisms organise without ad hoc assistance, and by the fate of the directional predictions the mechanisms generate. Does competition punish labour wasted above the social standard? Do capitals that cheapen production by embodying knowledge gain at their rivals' expense until the standard resets? Does the working day remain the frame of a contest whose stakes are the division between necessary and surplus labour? Does the system's crisis behaviour exhibit the reimposition of proportions through devaluation that the theory describes? Affirmative answers, sustained across settings, are the measure's evidence, and they are the kind of evidence on which the middle position rests its empirical arm.

The strong reading, held to the same practice, fares worse than its rhetoric suggests, and the comparison should be made without triumphalism since the standard being applied is the one its own best representatives accept in their other work. Its mechanisms are vivid but under specified: the route from social cooperation to appropriated value is asserted through the vocabulary of capture and enclosure rather than traced through the contracts, wages, and accounts where a mechanism would have to live. Its directional predictions, as this article has shown, point against the long movement of the wage relation. Its organising range is wide but purchased by redescription, since any phenomenon whatever can be redescribed as the common at work and capital capturing it, and a framework that organises everything by renaming it has traded explanation for vocabulary. None of that makes the reading worthless. It makes it a research programme in an early and degenerating phase by the standards programmes are judged by, rich in motivating images, poor in mechanisms, and kept alive partly by the genuine phenomena its images first illuminated. Programmes in that condition sometimes revive when a successor supplies the missing mechanisms. The rent turn examined earlier may be the beginning of such a supply, and if the programme's heirs complete it, they will find, as this article has argued, that the completed theory speaks the published vocabulary and has quietly buried the obituary on the way.

For the student reader, the practical residue of this section is a habit of demanding the same currency from every party to a theoretical dispute. Ask the obituary's proponents for their mechanism and their directions. Ask the measure's defenders for theirs. Refuse to let either side pay in portraits of the other side's naivety. The dispute over the general intellect has consumed decades partly because its participants so often paid in portraits, the prophetic school depicting its critics as frightened custodians, the custodians depicting the prophets as intoxicated journalists, while the mechanisms and directions that would have settled the matter sat unexamined between them. An article in a glossary series cannot retroactively settle other people's decades. It can decline to repeat them, and it can hand its readers the currency standard by which the next decade of the dispute, if there is one, might be conducted more profitably.

## The author's other silences and the discipline of comparison

The method this article has applied to the fragment, comparing a notebook formulation with its fate in the author's published revisions, gains credibility from the fact that the corpus offers many parallel cases against which the method can be calibrated, and a brief tour of the parallels protects the verdict from the suspicion that its standard was invented for the occasion. The notebooks and early manuscripts contain a celebrated discussion of the human senses and their historical formation, a theory of the division of labour's origin in the division between town and country stated with a sweep the later work narrows, a vocabulary of alienation whose philosophical dress the mature work largely exchanges for the analysis of the commodity form, and a treatment of money's power to invert qualities that survives into the published work as illustration rather than as doctrine. In every case the scholarly settlement has the same shape as the one defended here: the early formulation is honoured as a discovery of the workshop, its surviving content is traced into the published architecture under the architecture's own terms, and its discarded extravagances are allowed to lapse without being either anathematised or enthroned. No special pleading was required in those cases, and none is required in this one. The fragment is not being subjected to an inquisition devised for it. It is being read the way its author's other workshop pages are read by the scholarship that knows them best.

Calibration also runs in the other direction, against a counter-reading tempted to make publication an absolute sacrament. The corpus contains published statements that their author later revised, qualified, or silently dropped, including forecasts about the sequence of revolutions and judgements about particular nations and movements that no defender now repeats, and the existence of those cases shows that the published word is a weighty consideration rather than an infallible one. The standard defended throughout this article is accordingly comparative and defeasible: published statements outweigh notebook statements of contrary tendency, other things equal, and other things are equal only after the passage's grammar, its neighbours, its mechanism, and the world's directions have all been examined. In the fragment's case every one of those examinations points the same way, which is why the verdict can be stated confidently without being stated dogmatically. Confidence earned by convergence is the only kind a glossary should traffic in, and its signature is the willingness, already formalised in the defeater section, to name the discoveries that would dissolve the convergence and send the verdict back to the workshop.

There is a final comparative case that belongs here because it concerns this author's reception in the mass rather than in the seminar, and because students meet it constantly. The most quoted sentence in the entire corpus, the eleventh thesis on Feuerbach about interpreting and changing the world, was itself a notebook jotting, unpublished in its author's lifetime, recovered from a private manuscript and elevated by posterity into the tradition's motto. Its elevation is generally and rightly accepted, because the sentence summarises a commitment the published work everywhere enacts, extending the architecture rather than contradicting it. The case proves that the standard in use is not hostility to notebook material. Notebook material that coheres with the published project is welcomed at full value by the same scholarship that resists the fragment's corollary, and the difference between the motto's reception and the corollary's resistance is the difference between extension and contradiction. A reader who grasps that difference possesses the whole method of this article in miniature.

## Examination practice: using the phrase without abusing it

Because this series serves candidates as well as scholars, the article pauses to convert its argument into examination practice, and the conversion is straightforward since the dispute's structure is exactly the structure strong answers are built from. A question inviting discussion of the general intellect rewards, first, accurate provenance stated without embarrassment: the phrase's home in the 1857 and 1858 notebooks, its English form inside a German manuscript, its rarity, and its absence from the published work, all in the answer's opening movement, where they establish that the candidate knows what kind of object is under discussion. It rewards, second, the readings attributed to their actual authors, Negri's lectures for the strong reading's architecture, Virno for its philosophical form, Lazzarato for its sociology of work, the textual scholars and value-form theorists for the counter-reading, with dates and titles, since unattributed surveys of opinion read as journalism. It rewards, third, the test stated as a test: the rival predictions, about wage dependence and about profit's mooring, the directions the evidence takes, and the verdict that follows from the comparison rather than preceding it. And it rewards, finally, the defeater, the sentence in which the candidate states what discovery would reverse the verdict, since that sentence is the signature of judgement as opposed to allegiance, and markers are trained to look for it.

The corresponding failures are equally predictable and equally avoidable. The weakest scripts cite the phrase as a doctrine of Capital, repeat the obituary as the founder's own conclusion, and illustrate it with whatever organisational novelty the candidate happens to know, a performance that fails on provenance, on attribution, and on evidence simultaneously. Middling scripts state the strong reading fairly and then dismiss it by pedigree alone, never engaging its argument about the measure or its genuine descriptions of knowledge based work, a performance that markers recognise as the mirror image of the weakest scripts, allegiance with the opposite sign. The distinction between the middling and the strong script is the willingness to let the evidence arbitrate: to concede the tendency, to state the objection about selective textual standards at full strength and concede its force, and then to show, from the wage relation's direction and the published mechanism of relative surplus value, why the concession does not carry the obituary with it. Candidates who can perform that sequence possess the article, and candidates who possess the article in that form can transfer the performance to any contested concept the paper sets, which is the sense in which a glossary entry about two borrowed words inside a German notebook becomes training for an entire discipline.

One specific warning belongs in this section because examiners report the error constantly and because this article's own discipline forbids the decoration that causes it. Candidates should cite no figure for the extent of wage dependence, for the share of employment in knowledge based activities, or for the scale of investment in intangible assets, whether in support of the strong reading or against it. The figures in circulation for those magnitudes are contested in their construction, incomparable across the sources that produce them, and unnecessary to an argument whose evidence is directional and structural. A script that argues from the directions, the extension of the wage relation across decades and the persistence of profit's mooring in employed labour, will outscore a script that decorates the same argument with numbers it cannot defend under questioning, and the habit of declining indefensible numbers is worth more, in the long run of a candidate's education, than any single verdict this article defends.

## The socialisation of knowledge as an old story

One service a long view performs for this dispute is to remind both sides that the socialisation of knowledge within production is not a novelty of any recent epoch but a tendency as old as the mode of production itself, visible wherever the published texts look. The division of labour within the workshop already socialised knowledge in the manufacturing period, decomposing the craftsman's unified competence into partial operations whose overall design resided with the employer, so that the knowledge of the whole confronted each worker as another's property long before any machinery system embodied it. The factory extended the decomposition and mechanised it. The application of science then raised the process to a new power, enlisting organised inquiry as a standing input rather than an occasional improvisation. Each stage intensified the same relation: the productive knowledge at work grows more social in its sources and more alien in its form, while the wage relation through which the producers are set to work persists unchanged in its essentials. A periodisation that announces the socialisation of knowledge as the signature of a new age therefore mistakes a centuries long gradient for a recent cliff, and the mistake is consequential, because a gradient invites analysis of mechanisms and degrees, while a cliff invites the rhetoric of rupture in which the obituary for the measure sounds plausible.

The gradient reading also recovers an explanatory resource the rupture reading wastes, namely the ability to say why the tendency's pace varies and why its effects differ across settings. Where production's knowledge basis rises under competitive pressure, the published mechanism predicts the sequence in detail: temporary advantages for the innovating capital, a resetting of the social standard, cheapening of the products, pressure on the wages and staffing of the laggards, and a renewed search for the next embodiment of knowledge. Where the rise is driven instead by the requirements of coordination, by the sheer complexity of keeping a large apparatus coherent, the same framework predicts a growth of supervisory and integrative labour inside the wage relation, a proliferation of the collective labourer's directing strata, and new contests over the boundary between conception and execution. Those differentiated predictions are the kind a theory lives by. The rupture reading, committed to a single epochal fact, can only repeat the fact in the presence of every variation, and its literature's characteristic flatness in the face of sectoral and national differences is the visible cost of the commitment. The middle position's gradient is less thrilling than the cliff. It is also, on the evidence of the variations themselves, the shape the world actually has.

There is a historiographical moral here that extends beyond the phrase. The tradition's author was himself a gradient thinker about the tendencies he most cared about, describing concentration, centralisation, and the socialisation of labour as processes working themselves out across long durations with countertendencies at every step, and reserving the language of rupture for the political transformations that conclude such processes rather than for the processes themselves. A reading of his notebook that converts his gradient into a cliff, and then dates the cliff's crossing to the reader's own lifetime, has departed from his method at the exact moment it claims his authority. Restoring the gradient is therefore not a pedantic preference for slowness. It is fidelity to the method of the very text being celebrated, and the fidelity, once again, is what the thin basis finally means.

## Who pays for the intellect: the reproduction question

Beneath the dispute's headline claims sits a quieter question that the middle position can ask with more precision than either rival, and it concerns the costs of producing the general intellect itself. Social knowledge is not a free gift of nature, whatever the classical texts sometimes imply when they describe science as a universal labour whose results capital appropriates without equivalent. It is produced and reproduced by labour: by teachers and students, by researchers and technicians, by the vast apparatus of education and inquiry whose costs are borne through some combination of taxation, household sacrifice, student indebtedness, and the unpaid effort of the learners themselves. Who bears those costs, and who appropriates the product, is a distributional question of the first order, and it arises with special sharpness where the knowledge in question passes directly into production as a force employers enlist. The strong reading earns its credit for insisting that the question be asked, since a picture in which capital harvests a commons it did not pay to create captures something real about the arrangement's asymmetry. The middle position's contribution is to state the asymmetry in categories that support analysis rather than only indignation.

Stated in the published vocabulary, the arrangement is a transfer. The value of labour power, as the theory defines it, includes the costs of the training that makes labour power competent, and those costs are supposed, in the theory's normal case, to be recovered through the wage, so that skilled labour power sells at a value reflecting its production costs and creates correspondingly more value in its exercise. Where the costs of producing competence are displaced onto the worker, the household, or the public purse, while the wage fails to rise in proportion, the transfer runs from those bearers to the employers who enlist the competence, and the mechanism deserves the same forensic attention the tradition gives to other displacements of cost. The analysis requires no broken measure and no escaped production. It requires attention to the wage's adequacy against the actual costs of reproducing the labour power being purchased, a magnitude that the swelling knowledge basis of production pushes upward even as competition among the bearers of competence pushes the wage downward. That scissors movement, between rising reproduction costs and contested wages, is one of the characteristic tensions of knowledge based production, and it is invisible to a framework that has declared the wage relation obsolete. Frameworks are judged by what they let their users see. On this question the allegedly superseded framework sees the tension and the allegedly successor framework walks past it, which is as concise a summary of the article's verdict as the empirical sections provide.

### Where do the costs of producing social knowledge fall?

Disproportionately on households, on learners themselves, and on public provision, while the product is enlisted by employers whose wages frequently fail to reflect those costs. The gap between cost and wage is a transfer the published categories can measure in principle, and it widens as production's knowledge basis grows.

The reproduction question also reframes the politics that the strong reading attaches to the commons. If the general intellect is a commons, it is a commons with a workforce, maintained by identifiable labour under identifiable relations, much of it waged and public, some of it unwaged and domestic. A politics of the commons that speaks only of enclosure and capture misses the maintenance, and with it the people whose labour performs the maintenance and whose organisation could defend the commons as a going concern rather than as a memory. The tradition's older vocabulary, which never separated the analysis of production from the analysis of reproduction for long, handles the point natively. Here again the pattern of the whole dispute repeats in miniature: the strong reading's phenomenon is genuine, its framing obscures the labour that sustains the phenomenon, and the published framework, applied without embarrassment, recovers both the phenomenon and the labour in a single account.

## Conception and execution: the division inside knowledge work

The fragment's picture of the worker stepping beside the process has a published cousin that the dispute neglects and that organises a great deal of the experience the strong reading describes: the separation of conception from execution, the division of labour within production between those who design, direct, and understand the process and those who operate within designs they did not make. That separation, analysed in the published work as a tendency of the capitalist labour process and developed by later scholarship into an account of managerial control, reaches a distinctive intensity where production's knowledge basis is high, because the knowledge embodied in the apparatus must be produced by somebody, and the social division between the producers of the embodied knowledge and the operators of the embodiment becomes the organising fault line of the workplace. The designers of the system stand in a relation to the operators that reproduces, inside the sphere of knowledge itself, the relation between capital and labour in its classical form: one side monopolises the conception of the whole, the other side executes a part whose rationale is withheld, and the wage purchases obedience to a design as much as it purchases effort.

Reading the fragment through that fault line yields an interpretation that neither rival emphasises and that this article's verdict gladly adopts, since it explains the passage's enduring aptness for describing knowledge based work without recourse to any obituary. What the passage glimpsed, on this reading, is the generalisation of the conception and execution split to the level of society's knowledge as a whole. The general intellect names the conception side of production socialised and objectified, the accumulated design intelligence of the process, confronting the execution side in the form of machinery, procedure, and management. The worker beside the process is the executor whose own conception has been expropriated into the system, and the expropriation, not any failure of measurement, is the passage's true subject and its true pathos. That reading honours the text's location inside the fixed capital discussion, honours its emotional register, and connects it to the labour process literature that constitutes one of the tradition's most successful research programmes, all without asking the passage to pronounce on the fate of the value form.

The fault line also supplies the middle position with its answer to a question the strong reading poses effectively: if nothing fundamental has changed, why does knowledge based work feel so different from the classical factory, and why do its workers' grievances so often concern autonomy, recognition, and the ownership of their own competences rather than the classical grievances of hours and pay? The answer is that the difference is real and located exactly where the fault line reading puts it, in the labour process and in the composition of the workforce, rather than in the value form. Where conception is the stake, the contests of the workplace take conception as their object: contests over who designs, who may understand, whose knowledge counts as the firm's property and whose remains the worker's, over the right to practise a competence outside the employer's fence. Those contests are new in their objects and classical in their structure, waged by wage workers against the owners of the conditions of their work over the terms on which their capacities are used. A theory that can say that sentence has no need to announce a new epoch in order to take the new contests seriously, and a movement that hears the sentence knows where its levers are.

## The collective labourer enlarged

The published figure of the collective labourer deserves a fuller hearing in this dispute than either side usually gives it, because it is the point at which the published work comes closest to the fragment's vision and therefore the point at which the distance between the vision and the doctrine can be measured exactly. In the first volume's analysis, the development of production transforms the individual producer into a combined workforce whose members perform partial functions at varying distances from the direct manipulation of the object: some work with their hands, some direct, some design, some maintain the conditions under which the others work, and the product is the product of the combination rather than of any member. Crucially, the analysis extends the boundary of the productive collective as production develops, drawing in functions once external to it, and it insists that to be a productive worker under capital it is no longer necessary to work directly with one's hands; it is enough to be an organ of the collective labourer performing one of its functions. The engineers and managers of the process are gathered into the figure on the same page that gathers in the manual workers, and the productive power of the whole is treated as arising from the combination itself.

Set the fragment beside that analysis and the relationship between them looks less like rivalry than like variation. The fragment's general intellect is the collective labourer's knowledge considered at the level of society rather than of the single production unit, and objectified in the unit's apparatus rather than exercised by its members. The strong reading's living pole, that cooperation and shared competence which Virno and Hardt and Negri place at the centre of their construction, is the collective labourer considered at the same enlarged scale. The published figure therefore already contains the germ of the enlargement, and the history of the concept since, in the hands of scholars who never cite the fragment, shows the germ developing healthily inside the doctrine: analyses of combined workforces spanning firms and continents, of productive functions distributed across design centres and assembly sites, of the collective worker of a whole chain of production, all proceed from the published figure without needing the obituary. Where the fragment genuinely exceeds the figure is in its speculation about the measure, and the excess is precisely the part that did not survive. The pattern could hardly be cleaner, and a glossary may be forgiven for dwelling on it: in case after case, the content of the strong reading that proves durable turns out to have a published ancestor doing the same work, while the content that is unique to the reading is the content the evidence refuses.

There is a corollary for the sociology of the dispute that should be stated plainly, since it explains the temperature of the exchanges better than any accusation of bad faith. The strong reading's adherents experience the counter-reading as an attempt to confiscate their tradition's founding insight, the social character of production's real powers, and to return the theory to a factory bound economism they entered the field to escape. The experience is understandable and mistaken. That published figure is not factory bound; it is the doctrine's own engine for following production as it socialises, and the counter-reading's best representatives wield it exactly so. What the counter-reading confiscates is not the insight but the copyright, the claim that the insight was recovered from a neglected notebook against the doctrine's resistance. Insights do not need romantic genealogies to deserve their place. The collective labourer's enlargement stands on the published page, available to any reader, and the fragment's role was to dramatise the enlargement for a later generation in a vocabulary vivid enough to make the generation look. Looking was the service. The doctrine was already there.

## Crisis and the measure once more

The crisis strand of the strong reading, touched earlier through Negri, returns in the period literature as an argument from financial phenomena, and it deserves a dedicated answer because financial appearances are the strongest intuitive evidence the obituary possesses. The argument observes that the valuations reached in financial markets, the claims traded there, and the fortunes assembled and destroyed there appear to bear no stable relation to the labour embodied in any identifiable production, and it concludes that value has slipped its moorings in fact, whatever the doctrine says, with the fragment's prophecy fulfilled in the sphere where modern capitalism most flamboyantly displays itself. Marazzi's work on language and finance gives the argument its most sophisticated form, connecting the new production's linguistic character to the conventional and expectation driven character of financial valuation, so that the measure's breakdown in production and the arbitrariness of prices in finance appear as two faces of one transformation.

The published theory's answer begins by denying that the appearance is new. The third volume's analysis of interest bearing capital and fictitious capital describes, in the nineteenth century's own financial forms, the same detachment of claims from the production that must ultimately service them: titles to future revenue capitalised at rates set by expectation and convention, fortunes assembled from the appreciation of the titles, and crises in which the titles' claims are forcibly reconciled with the surplus actually available. Detachment, on that analysis, is the normal appearance of the credit system at its height, and reconciliation is the crisis's function, brutal and periodic. A reader equipped with that analysis will therefore ask of any financial episode the discriminating question: did the reconciliation occur, were claims destroyed until they fitted the surplus, did capitals perish and labour get repriced in the aftermath? Where the answer is affirmative, the episode confirms the published account's mechanism in its modern dress. The strong reading, to prevail, needs episodes in which claims float permanently free, serviced by no surplus and reconciled by no destruction, and its literature, for all its vividness about the floating, is notably reticent about the reconciliations that follow each float. Reticence is not refutation, but the burden of the obituary is not met by describing the half of the cycle the doctrine already describes.

There is also a point of intellectual hygiene about levels of analysis that this section should record for the student reader. The law, in the published architecture, does not govern the price of every title or the valuation of every asset directly; it governs the production and distribution of the social surplus, through mediations that include rent, interest, and the prices of production, each with its own relative autonomy and its own capacity for spectacular deviation. Arguments that move from the deviation of a mediated magnitude to the death of the governing relation commit a level error, like arguing from the turbulence of weather to the death of climate. The fragment itself, whatever else it does, does not commit that error; its claim about the measure is made at the level of production, where the dispute belongs. Its financialising descendants are less careful, and a glossary does its readers a service by marking the exact point at which the inheritance was squandered, so that the salvageable parts of the tradition, its attention to language, convention, and expectation in economic life, can be reclaimed for an analysis whose levels are kept straight.

## The university and the factory: an old comparison revisited

One of the strong reading's favourite exhibits, the growing resemblance between the sites of knowledge production and the sites of material production, repays examination because the comparison is older than the reading and its older form teaches the correct moral. Observers have long noted that the organisation of inquiry and the organisation of manufacture borrow each other's forms: inquiry acquires laboratories organised like works, hierarchies of direction, divisions between conceiving and routine labour, and outputs measured against schedules; manufacture acquires research departments, design studios, and a stratum of workers whose labour is inquiry. The strong reading takes the convergence as evidence that production has become knowledge production in essence, dissolving the boundary between the two and carrying the law of value away with the boundary. The older and soberer moral is nearly the opposite. The convergence shows the wage relation and the labour process of capital extending their forms into inquiry, subordinating the production of knowledge to the same disciplines of direction, timing, and appropriation that govern the production of goods, which is what a theory expecting the form's persistence would predict as knowledge becomes a direct input. The factory did not become a university in the sense that matters. The university, in its organisation of work, became legible to the factory's categories, and the categories' ability to describe the convergence is evidence of their reach rather than of their expiry.

The exhibit also illustrates a recurrent pattern in the period literature's use of evidence, which a glossary should name once, clearly, since students will meet it in every chapter of that literature. The pattern is the argument from resemblance. Two domains are shown to resemble each other in some striking feature, and the resemblance is then treated as an identity of essence from which large conclusions are drawn: because knowledge work resembles social cooperation in general, production is society; because financial valuation resembles linguistic convention, value is language; because the worker's capacities are formed in life, life is work. Resemblance is a legitimate starting point for inquiry and a notoriously weak terminus for it, since any two complex domains resemble each other in indefinitely many features, and everything depends on whether the shared feature is the one that governs the phenomena under dispute. The published method, at its best, is a discipline against exactly this weakness, insisting that identities be established by examining the relation that produces the phenomena, the value relation in the present case, rather than by contemplating the textures the phenomena display. The fragment, read in its place, observes the discipline: its claim about knowledge is a claim about the composition of fixed capital, established inside the analysis of production. The literature built on it too often abandons the discipline for the pleasures of resemblance, and the abandonment, more than any doctrine, is what a student should learn to detect.

## Skill, deskilling, and the knowledge in the worker

The fragment's account of knowledge migrating from the worker into the apparatus intersects with one of the most empirically developed debates in the tradition, the debate over skill and its degradation, and the intersection offers a useful check on both sides of the present dispute. The deskilling thesis, given classic form in Harry Braverman's Labor and Monopoly Capital of 1974, held that the capitalist labour process tends to separate conception from execution and to degrade the worker's craft knowledge into the property of management and machinery, a thesis drawn from the published texts and confirmed, qualified, and contested by decades of workplace study. The fragment reads, in that light, as an early and vivid statement of the tendency Braverman documented: the general intellect is the name for what the worker loses, the social knowledge that once resided in the producers' own capacities and now confronts them as the apparatus's endowment. Defenders of the strong reading sometimes cite the deskilling literature as an ally, and in one respect it is: both insist that the migration of knowledge is the central drama of production's development. But the alliance is unstable, because the deskilling literature never supposed that the migration abolished the measure of value or the wage relation. Braverman's workers are wage workers, timed and directed, whose degradation is a strategy for cheapening labour power and enlarging relative surplus value, mechanisms stated entirely within the published theory. The drama and the doctrine coexist in that literature without strain, which is evidence that the strain alleged by the strong reading is manufactured by its own corollary rather than discovered in the phenomena.

The subsequent qualifications of the deskilling thesis are equally instructive, because they show a research programme behaving as programmes should. Workplace studies found settings in which new skills arose alongside degraded ones, in which the operation of knowledge saturated apparatus demanded competences the classical picture had not anticipated, and in which workers contested the boundary between conception and execution with partial success. The programme absorbed those findings as complications of a tendency rather than as refutations of a period, distinguishing the direction of managerial strategy from the unevenness of its achievement. The strong reading's literature, confronted with analogous complications, the working day's persistence, the wage relation's spread, the discipline of time in its own exemplary workplaces, has more often responded by enlarging its definitions until the complications count as confirmations. The contrast between the two literatures' habits is a compact education in method, and it is available to any student who reads them side by side: one programme specifies its tendency and its countertendencies in advance, the other redescribes outcomes as they arrive. The fragment belongs, by its author's practice, to the first kind of inquiry. Its most faithful readers, paradoxically, are the ones who decline to read it as its loudest champions do.

## The general intellect and the question of planning

There is a forward looking use of the fragment that both readings neglect and that a complete glossary should recover, because it connects the passage to the oldest question in the tradition: what the socialisation of production makes possible. If production increasingly rests on a stock of social knowledge, objectified in apparatus and organisation, then the coordination of production is increasingly a matter of administering that stock consciously, and the question of who administers it, in whose interest, by what criteria, becomes the political question in its most concrete form. The notebooks' author drew that conclusion in his own register, treating the socialisation of the productive forces as the material premise for a society of associated producers, in which the general intellect would be developed as a common endowment rather than enlisted as capital's property. Later traditions of thought about planning, from the debates over calculation to the literature on the knowledge problems of central direction, are all, whether they know it or not, arguments about the administration of the general intellect: about whether a stock of knowledge dispersed across a population can be gathered, priced, commanded, or democratically directed, and about what is lost in each mode of gathering.

The fragment contributes to those debates a premise both sides should accept: the knowledge that production uses is social in its production, exceeding any single firm's or bureau's authorship, and arriving at the point of use as an inheritance. From that premise, the planning debates derive their characteristic difficulties. A market order claims to administer the inheritance through prices, letting the stock be enlisted wherever its return is highest, and its critics reply that the return registers only what the wage relation and the distribution of property allow to count. A planned order professes to administer the inheritance consciously, and its critics answer that the knowledge's dispersed and tacit character defeats the gathering that conscious administration requires. The fragment's author had his own answer, associating the inheritance's free development with the abolition of the form that enlisted it as an alien power, and the answer's feasibility is a question this article does not pretend to settle. What the article insists on is the premise's compatibility with the middle position. The social character of the knowledge stock is a fact about the productive forces. The form of its administration is a question about relations. The strong reading's error, in this forward looking register, is to suppose that the fact settles the question, that because knowledge is social in its making, its enlistment by capital has already become a hollow shell awaiting only recognition. Shells of that kind have a history of proving load bearing. The question of administration remains open precisely because the form persists, and the fragment, read soberly, is one of the tradition's sharpest statements of why the question matters.

## Reading the passage whole: a guided pass

Students frequently ask for the passage itself to be walked through slowly, in the order its author wrote it, and the request is legitimate even in an article that has deliberately paraphrased rather than anthologised it. A guided pass, in prose, runs as follows. The author is discussing fixed capital and the tendency of production, as it develops, to rest on the system of machinery rather than on the immediate labour of the worker. He observes that the worker's relation to the process changes: instead of inserting a modified natural object between himself and the object of labour as his instrument, he sets the process itself in motion and stands beside it as its supervisor. He then generalises the observation. The productive power at work resides in the agencies the process sets in motion, and those agencies embody the development of the social individual: the state of science, the organisation of cooperation, the accumulated artifice of generations. It is at that point that the English phrase appears, naming the general social knowledge whose development has become the basis of production and of the wealth produced. He draws the consequence for the measure: labour time, the direct expenditure of the individual, shrinks toward a subordinate and, in his charged word, miserable basis for a wealth that now rests on the general state of knowledge and its application. And he closes the movement by returning the whole development to capital's contradiction, since capital both drives the process that undermines its measure and clings to the measure as the condition of its own existence, a clinging he presents as the system's inner limit rather than as an accomplished transition beyond it.

Walked through at that pace, the passage's proportions become visible in a way that excerpting conceals. The machinery analysis occupies most of it. The phrase occupies a sentence. The measure's drama occupies a paragraph whose grammar is the grammar of tendency and self opposition. The return to capital's contradiction, which the anthologies typically omit, occupies the conclusion, and it is the conclusion that determines what kind of claim the whole makes: not a report that the measure has lapsed, but a demonstration that the system develops by intensifying a contradiction between its productive basis and its social form. A reader who has performed the walk possesses the passage, and possesses it in a form proof against the incantatory use, since incantation depends on the excerpt and withers in the presence of the whole. Teachers who set the walk as an exercise, requiring students to mark where each interpretive school's favourite sentences sit within the whole and what the surrounding sentences do to them, report that the dispute largely adjudicates itself in the classroom, which is perhaps the strongest evidence available that the middle position is not an artefact of this article's preferences but a property of the text read entire.

The guided pass also settles, as far as a paraphrase can, the question of translation that students raise about the charged vocabulary. The Nicolaus rendering is the standard for citation in English, and its choices, including the rendering of the sentence about the watchman and regulator and of the phrase's own sentence, are the choices against which looser versions circulating in the secondary literature should be checked. Where a popular account's quotation cannot be found in Nicolaus at the cited place, the discrepancy is itself a finding, usually indicating that the account is quoting an interpreter's paraphrase at one remove. Checking quotations against the specified translation is the smallest unit of the provenance discipline, and the habit, once formed on this passage, protects a reader across the whole contested corpus of the tradition's charismatic texts.

## The phrase in dictionaries and handbooks

A concept's standing can be gauged by watching how reference works handle it, and the general intellect's treatment in dictionaries and handbooks of the tradition is a small museum of the dispute's stages. Older reference works, compiled before the workerist appropriation had travelled, either omit the phrase or gloss it briefly as a term from the notebooks for the social character of knowledge in production, a gloss this article's own definition resembles. Later works, compiled after the strong reading's ascendancy, give the phrase long entries in which the period claims appear as the term's meaning and the notebooks' status is relegated to a subordinate clause, so that a reader consulting the handbook absorbs the construction as the concept. The drift between the generations of reference works is not evidence that the later ones are corrupt; handbooks register the living usage of their moment, and the living usage of the strong reading's decades was genuinely strong reading usage. But the drift does mean that a handbook entry is a source about the reception, not about the text, and students who cite a handbook for what the phrase meant in 1858 are citing the wrong kind of authority for that question. The text, its edition, and its editors are the authorities for 1858. The handbooks are the authorities for what the phrase had become by the time the handbook was compiled, and keeping those questions separate is once again the whole of the method.

The museum has a further lesson about how concepts acquire the appearance of antiquity. A term promoted by a living school, furnished with a genealogy reaching back to a canonical author, and installed in the handbooks within a generation, will present itself to the next generation as a permanent fixture of the tradition, its recent manufacture invisible beneath the patina of the reference shelf. Whole vocabularies have been built that way, and the process is neither scandalous nor avoidable; traditions must coin and promote terms to think with. The scholarly obligation is only that the manufacture remain discoverable, that the record show when the term entered general use, in whose hands, and carrying what construction, so that a reader who wishes to audit the construction can find the workshop. This article's reception history, with its dates of editions, translations, and lectures, is written to keep the workshop findable for this term. A reader who finishes the article knowing that the phrase's public career begins in the Italian 1960s, passes through Parisian lectures in the late 1970s, and reaches the Anglophone handbooks only after the complete translation of 1973 and the English Negri of 1991, possesses the concept's true chronology, and will not again mistake the patina for the provenance.

## Effort, intensity, and the persistence of the working day

The empirical sections of this article graded the readings by the wage relation's extent and by profit's mooring, and there is a third body of evidence, closer to the skin of the working day, that deserves recording because it is the evidence workers themselves cite when the obituary is put to them. It concerns effort and its measurement. Across knowledge based production, the tendency of management is toward an ever finer accounting of the worker's time and attention: tasks subdivided and timed, outputs counted against schedules, pauses and idle intervals hunted down by systems designed for the purpose, the worker's day rendered legible to supervision in a granularity the classical factory achieved only at its most developed. Whatever else such accounting signifies, it signifies that the purchasers of labour power regard the contracted time as the substance of what they have bought and police its expenditure accordingly, which is the behaviour the theory of the working day predicts and the obituary cannot accommodate. A measure whose subjects invest ever more heavily in enforcing it at the level of the minute has not lapsed in any sense that should interest a materialist.

The workers' side of the same evidence is equally telling. The characteristic struggles of knowledge based employment concern the day's boundaries and contents: the length of the contracted week, the ownership of the hours outside it, the intensity demanded within it, the right to disconnect from the instruments through which direction follows the worker home, the staffing levels that determine how much labour each hour must contain. Those are the classical stakes, restated in the idiom of the new workplaces, and their persistence is not a cultural lag awaiting enlightenment by the period literature. They are the form that the contest over surplus labour takes where the labour is cognitive and relational, and they arise with a regularity that suggests structure rather than nostalgia. A theory of the epoch that cannot explain why its exemplary workers fight over hours has mistaken the epoch. A theory that locates the fight where the published work locates it, in how the day divides between replacing the wage and yielding the surplus beyond it, explains the fight and the accounting that provokes it in a single movement.

### Why does the policing of minutes matter to a dispute about epochs?

Because a lapsed measure would not be enforced at rising cost by the parties who supposedly transcended it. The investment in timing, counting, and intensifying labour within knowledge based work is the behaviour of a system for which labour time remains the substance of command.

There is a temptation, in recording evidence of this kind, to treat it as a trump that ends the dispute by ridicule, and the article resists the temptation for the reason given in its fairness sections: the strong reading's authors know about the policing of minutes, and their framework redescribes it as the desperate enforcement of a form whose basis has vanished, capital clinging to a measure it can no longer justify. The redescription is available and even elegant. Its cost, once again, is testability, since a framework for which enforcement and relaxation alike confirm the thesis has withdrawn from the empirical conversation. The middle position's discipline is to state, in advance, which observations would embarrass it, and the defeater section has done so. Symmetry of discipline is all that either side can offer the other, and the side that offers it while the other declines has, by the standards both profess, the better of the exchange.

## Translation as an event in the concept's life

The article has treated translation dates as part of the reception's skeleton, and the point deserves one more degree of emphasis, because the concept's Anglophone career is unintelligible without grasping that its public arrived in a specific and distorting order. English readers met the interpretation before they could easily meet the text. The complete translation appeared in 1973, but the interpretive wave that made the phrase famous broke over the Anglophone world later, through translations of the interpreters themselves: Negri's lectures in 1991, the collaborative trilogy beginning in 2000, Virno in 2004, Marazzi in 2008, Berardi in 2009. A generation of readers therefore encountered the general intellect first as a term of art in a going theoretical concern, fully furnished with its period claims, and encountered the notebooks, if at all, afterwards, reading the passage through the construction they had already absorbed. The order of arrival matters because first furnishings are sticky. A reader who meets the text first asks what the passage says. A reader who meets the construction first asks where the passage fits, and the fitting, however honestly performed, subordinates the text to the frame.

Other traditions of the same author's reception show the same order reversed, with instructive results. Where the texts arrived before their famous interpreters, as with the first volume of Capital and its nineteenth century readers, the interpretive schools formed around a passage stock the readership held in common, and disputes could be adjudicated by appeal to pages every party had read. Where the interpreter arrived first, adjudication degenerates into competing loyalties, since the parties do not share the text in the same way; one party's evidence is the other party's conclusion. The general intellect dispute has exhibited that degeneration in its polemical phases, and the remedy proposed by this article's whole method, the return to the artefact with its dimensions stated, is precisely the remedy for an interpreter first reception: rebuild the common stock, passage by passage and date by date, until the parties are arguing about the same object again. Glossary work is sometimes derided as preliminary to the real business of theory. In receptions of this shape it is the real business, since without it the theory is conducted between parties who have never met on common ground.

The translation point has a final refinement concerning the phrase itself. Because the author wrote it in English, the Anglophone reader enjoys an accidental intimacy with it: the words on the page of the translation are, in this one instance, the author's own words, unmediated by any translator's choice. That intimacy flatters the reader into a sense of direct contact that the surrounding text does not warrant, since every other word of the passage arrives through Nicolaus's decisions, and the two English words sit in sentences whose grammar, mood, and emphasis are translation all the way down. A reader who builds on the phrase's exact English resonance, its suggestion of a faculty or a collective mind, is building on the one spot where the author happened to write in the reader's language, and the accident should be registered as an accident. The German sentences around the phrase are about social knowledge become a productive power. The English words name that power in a borrowed idiom. Precision consists in hearing the sentences and not only the name.

## The social factory revisited: inside and outside

The social factory thesis, the claim that production's site has expanded until it coincides with society, is the strong reading's most quoted export and the one whose plausibility varies most with the reader's vantage, so it earns a final, careful pass. The thesis draws its force from real dissolutions. The walls of the classical plant bounded a population, a shift, and a discipline that could be pointed to; the organisation of production across chains of firms, households, and public infrastructures bounds none of those so neatly, and the activities that feed production, from the raising of workers to the maintenance of the cities that house them, have always overflowed the plant in ways the classical picture understated. Feminist scholarship within the tradition made that point decisively decades ago, insisting that the reproduction of labour power is labour, socially necessary and largely unwaged, and that a theory of production which begins at the factory gate has defined its object by an act of blindness. The social factory thesis inherited that insistence and generalised it: if reproduction is production's hidden half, and if knowledge and cooperation are production's rising content, then the factory is everywhere and its outside has vanished.

The middle position accepts the insistence and declines the generalisation, and the distinction between them is worth stating with care because it is the distinction on which the whole article turns, applied to space rather than to time. Reproduction is necessary to production, and its labour is real labour, exploited in its own registers. It does not follow that reproduction is value production in the theory's strict sense, any more than the necessity of sleep to the worker makes sleep a shift. The strict sense exists to mark a specific relation, labour exchanged against capital and set to work under its command, and the marking is not a ranking of worth. The feminist critique, at its strongest, demanded that the unwaged half be made visible and its costs counted, not that the categories distinguishing the halves be dissolved, since the dissolution would erase exactly the specificity, who commands, who is paid, who may refuse, that the critique existed to expose. A social factory in which everything is production is a picture in which the wage relation's particular coercion becomes unseeable, dissolved into the general sociality of life under capital. The middle position prefers the picture in which the halves remain distinguishable and their articulation is the object of study: how the waged sector draws on the unwaged, how the costs are shifted between them, how the boundary is policed and occasionally moved. That picture is less sublime. It supports more questions, and the questions, unlike the sublime, can be answered.

## What the examiners of this dispute agree on

Beneath the polemics, the dispute's serious participants agree on more than their exchanges suggest, and a glossary performs a service by inventorying the agreement, since students often mistake the loudest disagreements for the whole terrain. All sides agree that the passage exists, that it is striking, and that it identifies a real feature of developed production: the rising weight of socially accumulated knowledge relative to direct labour. All sides agree that the phrase is rare and unpublished, though they differ on the weight of the fact. All sides agree that the production of knowledge and the organisation of cooperation are central problems for any contemporary statement of the theory, and that the tradition neglected them for too long. All sides agree, finally, that the wage relation and the working day remain massive facts of the world economy, though they differ on whether those facts are the system's essence or its lingering shell. An inventory of that shape relocates the disagreement to its true, narrower ground: the interpretation of a grammar, the weight of a textual status, and the reading of a direction in the evidence. Narrow grounds are good news for scholarship, because narrow grounds can be worked.

The inventory also suggests the form a settlement would take if the dispute were ever formally settled, and the article offers the sketch because settlements imagined in advance have a way of arriving sooner. The strong reading's school would retain its research programme and its canonical authors, redescribed as developers of a tendency the notebooks identified, with the obituary withdrawn and the period claim softened into a claim about the rising salience of knowledge, cooperation, and rent like appropriation within a law of value that persists. The counter-reading's schools would concede the programme's questions as legitimate and partly as their own, ceasing to treat the passage's citation as an offence and confining their policing to the authority claim. The handbooks would rewrite their entries to the pattern of this article's definition, provenance inside the gloss. Nothing in that settlement requires any participant to recant a life's work, which is usually the sign that a settlement describes where a field has already, in practice, arrived. The rent turn documented earlier, the value-form literature's engagement with the knowledge question, the textual scholars' absorption into curricula: the pieces are moving. A glossary's privilege is to say so while the slogans are still catching up.

## A note on sources, editions, and verification

Scholarly hygiene requires the article to close its argument by declaring the basis on which its own factual claims rest, in the same spirit of disclosure it has demanded from the readings it grades. The passage under dispute is cited throughout from the Penguin Grundrisse of 1973, Martin Nicolaus translating, in the stretch of the notebooks given over to fixed capital and to how large scale industry develops, which is where the machinery discussion and the celebrated phrase both occur. The manuscript's composition dates, its first appearance in German dress in Moscow across 1939 and 1941, and the 1953 Berlin edition that established the text for postwar scholarship are the standard facts of the edition history as the translation's own editorial apparatus gives them, confirmed in the scholarship surrounding the complete writings. The English wording inside the German prose, the handful of occurrences, and the silence of everything the author himself published are likewise matters of editorial record, checkable against the volume and its indexes by any reader holding a copy, and the argument has deliberately been built so that its central claims can be verified at that level, without trust in the article itself.

Attribution in the article follows a standing rule: a position is attached to a named author, a titled work, and a year at the point where the position is stated. The workerist formation is represented by Panzieri's machinery essay of 1961 and by Tronti's book of 1966. The strong reading's architecture is Negri's, from the Paris lectures that Italian readers received in 1979 and Anglophone readers in 1991. Its sociology of work descends from a 1996 essay by Lazzarato. Its widest statement is the trilogy that Hardt wrote with Negri across the first decade of the new century, and its philosophical form is Virno's grammar of the multitude, Englished in 2004. The period literature speaks through Marazzi on language, Englished in 2008, through Berardi on the soul at work, Englished in 2009, and through Moulier Boutang's systematic statement, Englished in 2011. Against them stand Caffentzis, whose collected essays of 2013 press the internal critique, and Heinrich, whose introductory study of Capital, Englished in 2012, carries the value-form objection along with an editor's command of the whole corpus. Where a position is compressed into a sentence, the compression is the article's summary and should be checked against the cited work by any reader proposing to rely on it, which is the series' standing rule for summaries and the final application, to this article itself, of the discipline the article has preached.

## The intellect in the singular: a word about the word

A glossary may end its analytical work with the smallest unit of all, the word intellect itself, whose connotations in English do quiet work for the strong reading that a reader should notice. Intellect suggests a faculty, something a mind has, and in the singular with the definite article it suggests a single great faculty, a collective mind thinking through the society that hosts it. The phrase's career owes some of its glamour to that suggestion, since a collective mind is a protagonist in a way that a stock of knowledge is not, and protagonists attract theories. The German prose surrounding the phrase is less glamorous and more exact: it speaks of general social knowledge, of the social individual's development, of science and its application, a vocabulary of stocks, capacities, and processes rather than of a singular thinking subject. Translators into languages where the phrase must be rendered rather than quoted face the choice openly, and their choices, toward knowledge, toward intellect, toward mind, tilt the reception in each language before any interpreter intervenes. English readers, spared the choice by the author's own English words, are also denied the warning the choice would have given them, that a metaphor is in play.

The warning matters because the metaphor, taken literally, smuggles a conclusion. A collective mind, if such a thing existed as the passage's subject, might plausibly be imagined to have outgrown the petty measure of individual hours, and the obituary would follow from the image. A stock of social knowledge, embodied in apparatus and organisation, raises no such suggestion; stocks are accumulated, maintained, and enlisted under relations that persist or change for reasons the stock itself does not dictate. Keeping the less glamorous paraphrase in mind while reading the glamorous phrase is a small exercise with large protective value, and it generalises across the lexicon: wherever a tradition's vocabulary personifies a process, the reader should translate the personification back into the process and check whether the argument survives the translation. Arguments that survive are about the world. Arguments that evaporate were about the metaphor, and the general intellect literature contains more of the second kind than its reputation admits, alongside the genuine contributions this article has been careful to preserve.

## Precarity and the shape of the wage at knowledge's frontier

One phenomenon the period literature claims as its home ground, the insecurity and fragmentation of employment in knowledge based work, deserves a hearing under the middle position's categories, both to test those categories and to deny the period claim its easiest exhibit. The facts of the phenomenon are not in dispute: engagements shortened and project based, careers assembled from successive contracts, income interrupted by gaps the worker must bridge, the risks of fluctuating demand transferred from the employer to the employed, and the apparatus of self presentation and perpetual availability through which the worker markets a competence that must never be allowed to date. The strong reading interprets the pattern as the wage relation dissolving into a general precarious cooperation, the social factory's labour form, in which the classical employment contract appears as a survival. The middle position interprets the same pattern through the published analysis of the wage's forms and of the reserve army, and finds the pattern legible without remainder. Piece wages, casual engagement, and the shifting of risk onto the worker are among the oldest instruments in the corpus's account of how capital cheapens labour power and disciplines its bearers, and their application to cognitive work is an extension of the instruments to new material, not the birth of a new relation.

The discriminating evidence, as throughout this article, is directional and structural. The precarious worker's insecurity is insecurity within dependence on selling labour power, not emancipation from it: the gaps between engagements are feared precisely because the wage is the livelihood, and the perpetual availability is exacted precisely because the next engagement must be secured in a market where the worker competes with others bearing similar competences. A form of life organised around the necessity of selling one's capacities, under competition, to owners of the conditions of work, is the wage relation in one of its historically recurrent costumes, the costume it wore for dockers and seamstresses before it dressed the designers and analysts. Costumes matter to those who wear them, and the analysis of the costume, its specific miseries and its specific possibilities of organisation, is real work that the period literature has sometimes done well. But the costume is not the body, and a glossary that allowed the costume to rename the body would be failing at the only task that justifies its existence.

## What remains to be written about the passage

A glossary article should end its survey by acknowledging the work on its own subject that remains undone, since a verdict that presents its field as closed invites the same suspicion as a reading that presents its text as a prophecy. Three gaps stand out. The first is a full commentary on the fragment in its manuscript context, of the kind the complete edition of the author's writings will eventually make standard, tracing every sentence's antecedents in the excerpt notebooks and its fate in the later drafts, so that the guided pass this article offered in paraphrase can be performed by any reader against an apparatus. The second is a reception history of the phrase in Italian and French that English readers can consult entire, closing the asymmetry of access documented earlier and allowing the Anglophone dispute to be re-run, if anyone wishes, on the full record rather than on the translated fraction. The third is the positive theory whose absence this article noted in its research section: an account, in the published categories, of the production and appropriation of knowledge as a going economic process, covering the labour of inquiry, the reproduction of competence, the rents of enclosure, and the transfers hidden in the wage's inadequacy to training's costs. That theory, when it is written, will owe the strong reading its questions and the counter-reading its instruments, and its title will probably not mention the general intellect at all, which will be the surest sign that the dispute has completed its work.

Until then, the phrase will continue to circulate, and the article's practical hope for its circulation is modest and specific. Let it be cited with its provenance attached, the way a careful writer cites a manuscript: the notebooks written in 1857 and 1858, never issued in print, in the Nicolaus translation, the phrase in English in the author's hand. Let the readings be attributed to their authors and centuries, Negri's construction to Negri, the period to its periodisers, the counter-reading to the scholars who built it. Let the tendency be granted and the obituary be argued rather than inherited. A phrase that has survived a century and a half, an ocean of commentary, and the attentions of schools that loved it too well can survive that much honesty, and the tradition that produced it, at its best, has never asked for more than honesty about its texts as the price of using them.

## The watchman and the regulator: an image examined

The passage's most durable image, the worker as watchman and regulator of a process that runs itself, deserves scrutiny as an image, because images in this dispute do argumentative work that their users do not always notice. The image suggests passivity: the human figure stands at the edge of an intelligent system, intervening occasionally, contributing attention rather than effort, a supervisor of a production that would proceed in the figure's absence with only a modest loss of vigilance. The strong reading leans on that suggestion when it argues that direct labour has dwindled toward a subordinate moment. But the image, inspected against the workplaces it is used to describe, misleads in a specific way. Watching and regulating, where the watched process is fast, integrated, and unforgiving of error, are forms of labour with their own intensity, their own discipline, and their own exhaustion, and the watchers are hired, timed, rotated, and dismissed under the same relation as the hand workers they supposedly succeed. The image's stillness is a property of the metaphor, not of the work. A regulator who must respond within seconds, whose lapse idles an apparatus worth a fortune, whose attention is itself monitored by the systems being watched, is not a figure who has stepped outside production's discipline. The figure is discipline's modern portrait, and the passage's author, who never saw the workplaces in question, can be forgiven an image whose inadequacy only their arrival exposed.

There is a second suggestion folded into the image that cuts the other way, in the counter-reading's favour, and it is worth extracting since this article aims to leave no part of the passage unexamined. A watchman presupposes something worth watching over, and a regulator presupposes a process whose regulation matters to an owner. The image is unintelligible outside a world of valuable apparatus, employed attendants, and production for exchange, which is to say outside the published theory's world. The passage does not image a commons flowering beyond the wage relation. It images the wage relation at a high development of the productive forces, with the worker's posture changed and the worker's subordination intact, indeed deepened by the value of the apparatus the worker's attention protects. Readings that quote the image as an emancipation scene are reading its foreground and ignoring its furniture. The furniture is capital's, and the passage knows it, which is why its conclusion returns to capital's contradiction rather than to the watchman's liberation.

## Surplus value from knowledge: a confusion dissolved

A popular version of the strong reading, below the level of its serious authors, holds that knowledge based production creates a puzzle for the theory of value because the knowledge employed costs nothing to reproduce: an idea, a design, or a body of code, once produced, can be used again without being used up, so that the wealth it generates appears to arise from nothing and the labour theory collapses for embarrassment. The puzzle dissolves under distinctions the published work already draws, and dissolving it in a glossary is worthwhile because the popular version reaches more readers than Negri ever will. The first distinction is between the cost of producing the first instance of a knowledge product and the cost of reproducing it, a distinction the theory handles under the general rubric of any product whose development costs are high and whose unit costs fall with scale, and which affects the value of each unit without touching the form of value itself. The second distinction is between the knowledge as a condition of production, enlisted like a natural force once it exists, and the labour that produced and maintains it, which was and is paid for in the currencies the theory recognises: wages for researchers and teachers, public expenditure raised by taxation on the social surplus, and the unpaid time of learners. Nothing in the arrangement arises from nothing. The appearance of something from nothing is the appearance created by drawing the boundary of the account at the fence of the firm that enlists the knowledge, and the tradition's oldest instruction to its students is to draw the boundary around society as a whole, where the costs reappear and the miracle evaporates.

The confusion has a respectable ancestor that explains its persistence. The theory's author himself remarked that science costs the capitalist nothing, meaning that the owner who enlists an established finding pays no equivalent for the generations of inquiry behind it, and the remark is quoted in the popular literature as a concession that value leaks out of the theory at the point where knowledge enters. Read in place, the remark belongs to the analysis of how capital appropriates the free gifts of social development, alongside its appropriation of natural forces and of the cooperative powers of combined labour, and it functions in that analysis as an explanation of profit's sources within the law of value, not as an embarrassment to it. A gift to capital is a transfer within the system, and transfers presuppose the system whose magnitudes they redistribute. The popular puzzle therefore inverts its source: the passage it cites as the theory's difficulty is the theory's own account of why knowledge based production is so profitable to those positioned to enlist its results, an account that requires the measure to be working perfectly in order to state what is being gifted and to whom.

## The phrase and the philosophy of history

At the deepest level of the dispute lies a disagreement about the philosophy of history that the rival readings presuppose, and surfacing it explains why the argument has proved so resistant to the evidence both sides ostensibly share. The strong reading presupposes a succession of stages driven by the growth of productive powers, in which each stage's social form corresponds to the forces' character and yields when the forces outgrow it, so that the swelling of knowledge within production can be read as the material signature of a stage ending. That presupposition has textual support in the tradition's most famous summary statements, and its holders are entitled to it as an interpretation. The counter-reading presupposes a different motor: forms persist through the forces' development because the forms are relations among people, defended by interests and enforced by institutions, and they end by political transformation rather than by technical obsolescence, so that no development of the forces, however spectacular, announces a form's expiry by itself. On the second presupposition, the fragment's drama is the permanent drama of a form developing forces it cannot comfortably house, and the drama's resolution is a task, not a timetable.

The article does not need to settle the philosophy of history to settle the glossary question, and it does not pretend to. But it is worth recording that the second presupposition fits the author's own practice more closely, since his analyses of transitions, between modes and within them, consistently assign the decisive role to the relations and their human bearers, treating the forces' development as the premise and pressure of change rather than as its sufficient cause. It is also worth recording the practical consequence of the first presupposition when it is held carelessly. A successionism that reads forms off forces will repeatedly announce the expiry of relations that are in fact consolidating, because the forces never stop developing and the announcements never stop being available, and a movement guided by such announcements will repeatedly mistake the terrain. The fragment's career is the case study: half a century of announced expiry, during which the wage relation extended itself across the planet. Whatever the philosophy of history's ultimate merits, a reading with that track record owes its audience a revised confidence, and the revision is what the middle position supplies.

## A last distinction: anticipation and prediction

The vocabulary of the strong reading's praise contains a word that a glossary should audit before closing, the word anticipation, with its cousin prediction. To say that a writer anticipated a later development can mean three things of very different strengths. It can mean that the writer described a tendency whose later unfolding resembles the description, which is true of the fragment and of many passages in the corpus. Or the claim may be that the writer foresaw the specific forms the development would take, which is false of the fragment, whose author imagined the knowledge basis of production in the idiom of the machinery system of his own century and foresaw none of the organisational shapes through which the tendency later worked. Or it may be that the writer's theory entails the development as a consequence, so that the development confirms the theory, which is the strong reading's operative claim and the one this article has examined and rejected, since the entailment runs, if anywhere, in the other direction: the published theory entails the tendency's continuation inside the law of value, and the record confirms the entailment. Keeping the three meanings apart strips the praise of its equivocation. The fragment anticipated the tendency in the first sense, honourably and vividly. It did not predict the forms, and it does not entail the obituary, and a literature that slides among the senses to borrow the honour of the first for the claims of the third is trading on the ambiguity the glossary exists to remove.

The audit of praise words generalises, and the article ends its analytical work by handing the generalisation to the reader as the final instrument. Traditions keep their charismatic texts in circulation through a vocabulary of foresight: the founder foresaw, predicted, prophesied, saw in advance. Each term in that vocabulary should trigger the same three questions applied here. Which sense is meant? What exactly was described, and in what kind of text? What did the unfolding actually confirm, the description or the construction built on it? A reader who asks those questions habitually will find that many reputations for prophecy resolve into reputations for having identified real tendencies, which is honour enough for any writer and a firmer honour than prophecy, since tendencies can be checked. The notebooks' author identified, in the fragment on machines, one of the deepest tendencies of the system he spent his life analysing. The identification stands. The prophecy was manufactured later, by readers who needed it, and returning the manufacture to its makers is the last act of the precision this article set out to perform.

## Guilds, workshops, and the long prehistory of embodied skill

Embodied knowledge did not wait for the factory to begin confronting producers as an alien endowment, and a brief excursion into the prehistory sharpens the article's claim that the tendency is a gradient rather than a rupture. The medieval guild guarded its mysteries jealously, transmitting technique through apprenticeship while fencing the transmission against outsiders, so that the knowledge of a trade existed as the collective property of a corporation and confronted the journeyman who had not yet earned mastery as a power over his working life. The putting out system loosened that fence, scattering production among households while the merchant who coordinated them monopolised the knowledge of markets, designs, and the whole into which the scattered parts would be assembled. Manufacture then gathered the parts under one roof and decomposed them, and the decomposition, as the published analysis shows, transferred the intelligence of the process from the hands that performed its fragments to the head that designed the whole. At every stage the producers' own accumulated competence confronted most of them as something owned elsewhere, and at every stage the wage or its precursors organised the confrontation. The machinery system intensified the pattern by giving the embodied knowledge a material body of iron and, later, of other substances, in which it could be accumulated, transported, and set to work at a scale no guildhall could house. Nothing in that long story required the measure of value to change its nature, and the story's continuity is the deepest reason for doubting that its newest chapter changed it either.

The prehistory also corrects a sentimental picture that the strong reading sometimes encourages, in which an earlier world of production figures as the reign of the knowledgeable craftsman, whole and sovereign in his workshop, against which the knowledge saturated present appears as a fall into dependence. The craftsman's sovereignty was real but narrow, hedged by guild regulation, by the merchant's command of the market, by the scarcity of the tools and materials his knowledge required, and by the exclusion of the majority, women and the unfree and the merely poor, from the corporation that guarded the mysteries. Dependence on knowledge owned by others is not the present's invention. What distinguishes the developed machinery system is the scale of the stock, the speed of its accumulation, and the completeness with which its ownership is separated from its exercise, differences of degree along dimensions the tradition has always tracked. A glossary that kept the degrees distinct would have less drama to report. It would also have less error, and error, across a tradition's long life of teaching, is the more expensive commodity.

## Standards, protocols, and knowledge that belongs to no one

A form of social knowledge that the fragment's interpreters rarely discuss, and that tests the article's categories in an instructive way, is the standard: the shared specification, measure, interface, or procedure that allows separately owned processes to mesh. Standards are knowledge in its most purely social form. Nobody's labour produces a standard in the way labour produces a bolt; a standard exists by adoption, holds by convention, and yields its benefits to whoever organises production around it, including parties who contributed nothing to its making. The owner of a position defined by a standard can draw an income that looks, in the accounts, like a toll on the common, and the strong reading's literature has made much of such tolls as evidence that appropriation has detached itself from production. The published framework, once again, has the older category ready: a standard functions economically like a scarce condition of production whose control confers a claim on the social surplus, analogous to the control of a fertile plot or a navigable strait, and claims of that kind are the theory's home territory under the analysis of rent. The interesting questions are the rent questions. Who sets the standard, by what authority, and with what power to exclude? How are its terms revised, and who bears the cost when revision strands the investments built on the older terms? What portion of the surplus does control of the standard command, and how does that portion move as adoption widens?

Posing the rent questions about standards also clarifies what the general intellect, soberly used, can still name in such cases. The stock of knowledge that a standard crystallises is genuinely general: it belongs to the society that developed and adopted it, it grows by being used, and its enclosure is a political and legal act rather than a natural fact. A vocabulary that can say that, while refusing to say that the law of value has lapsed because standards exist, is a vocabulary adequate to the phenomenon. The mistake the strong reading makes in this territory is characteristic: it takes a genuine novelty in the forms of enclosure, the fencing of protocols and specifications rather than of fields and mines, for a novelty in the form of value itself. Enclosure has changed its objects many times in the system's history, and each change has been announced, by somebody, as the end of the old economics. The economics survives the announcements because its categories were built at the level of the relations that enclosure serves, and relations change more slowly than fences.

## Envoi: the uses of a small phrase

A small phrase, honestly weighed, turns out to have organised a surprising amount of intellectual history, and the weighing itself is the article's last exhibit. Around two borrowed words in a private notebook there gathered a political current, a shelf of books across three languages, a quarrel about the fate of a theory, and a set of questions about knowledge, cooperation, and command that will outlive every party to the quarrel. None of that history needed to happen quite as it did. With a different edition history, the passage might have been read early, absorbed quietly, and filed where its author shelved it, among the notes on fixed capital. With a different politics in the reading countries, it might have slept. The contingency is worth a moment's attention from any reader tempted to treat concepts as natural kinds, because the lesson generalises: what a tradition thinks with depends on what it happened to read, in what order, in whose translation, under the pressure of which struggles. Genealogy is not a decorative preface to theory. It is part of theory's evidence about itself.

The article closes, therefore, by returning its readers to the artefact one final time, with the instruments assembled across its sections now in their hands. Open the notebooks at the machinery discussion. Read the passage whole, neighbours included. Notice the borrowed English words, the grammar of tendency, the return to capital's self opposition. Then read the interpreters as interpreters, Negri's construction as a construction of genius, the period literature as a wager on an epoch, the counter-readings as the corpus and the record answering back. Grade each by the support beneath it and the directions it forecasts. What survives that procedure is not a slogan but a judgement, defeater attached, and a tendency, real and still unfolding, that the judgement knows how to state. That is all a glossary can give, and for the phrase that has carried more than any phrase should, it is enough.

Students meeting this material in an examination setting should organise their preparation around the article's three portable instruments, since questions on the general intellect reward the handling of evidence rather than the recitation of positions. The first instrument is the provenance itself, which should be learned as a claim with its components attached: the notebooks Marx filled in 1857 and 1858, unpublished in the author's lifetime, the phrase set down in English inside a German manuscript, occurring very few times, absent from everything published, and the whole resting on the Nicolaus translation of 1973 as the citable English text. A marker can distinguish within a paragraph between a candidate who possesses those components and a candidate who possesses a rumour of them, and the distinction governs the script's credibility on everything that follows. Practise the provenance until it can be stated in four sentences under pressure, and practise its significance with it: provenance fixes the weight a citation can bear, and the weight is the question the whole dispute turns on.

The second instrument is the three readings table, which converts the dispute into a procedure an answer can perform in public. ## For teachers and seminar leaders

Teachers will find in this article a teaching asset of an unusual kind: a concept whose entire interest lies in the distance between its textual basis and its theoretical career, which makes it a diagnostic instrument for the quality of a seminar's scholarly habits. The general intellect is the clearest available illustration of what provenance does to authority, because the facts are stark, checkable in a single session with the translation on the table, and resistant to the usual evasions. A seminar that begins by asking what the phrase's users claim, and then opens the notebooks to weigh the claim against the artefact, will surface within the hour every habit the course exists to correct: citation without inspection, attribution drift, the conversion of tendency into timetable, the preference for the exciting reading over the supported one. Leaders should resist the temptation to deliver the verdict first. The thin basis teaches itself, provided the passage is allowed to be read whole before the interpretations are admitted to the room.

The table is the seminar's second instrument, and it works best as an exercise rather than as a handout. Give the room the three readings with their columns blank beyond the claims, and require the textual support, the prediction, and the record to be filled in pairs, with every cell defended from a cited page or a stated direction in the evidence. The arguments over the strong reading's prediction, over whether the wage relation's extension refutes or merely embarrasses it, and over what the middle position concedes, are the seminar's real content, since they force the standards of evidence into the open where they can be examined as standards. A leader who lets those arguments run, intervening only to return the room to the artefact and the directions when it drifts toward allegiance, will find that the dispute has been learned in the only form in which it is worth having, as a procedure the students can carry to concepts the course never mentioned.

The translation history serves a third pedagogical purpose: it teaches the materiality of reception, the dependence of ideas on editions, languages, and the order in which readers meet texts and interpreters. Set the chronology as a problem, asking why the strong reading arose where the German text and the Italian journals were, why the Anglophone dispute took the shape it did given that the interpreters arrived in English before the text was widely read, and what a reader in English still cannot consult. Students trained on frictionless digital access often experience the exercise as a revelation, that a concept's career can be governed by the logistics of paper and translation, and that checking a provenance is therefore not pedantry but the recovery of causes. The transferable skill is the one this series most wants its readers to carry away: the habit of asking, of any confident conceptual claim, where the words stand written, in what sort of text, and what the claimant's author did with them afterwards. Teachers who want the textual ground prepared in advance will find it in the series' readers guide to the Grundrisse, which can be set as the seminar's preliminary reading with profit.

## For researchers, historians, and journalists

Researchers entering this field should begin from the article's asymmetry warning, since it determines what the available evidence in English can and cannot establish. The canonical statements of the strong reading can be read in English, while much of the Italian and French sediment beneath them, the journal debates where positions were first staked and afterwards modified, remains untranslated, and any research design that proposes to settle the reception's history from the English shelf alone has assumed away its central difficulty. The workable designs in the literature share a shape: they reconstruct the reception from the journals and editions outward, using the publication and translation chronology as the skeleton, and they treat the famous books as events within that chronology rather than as self sufficient statements. The same discipline applies to the theoretical dispute itself, where the productive designs reconstruct each reading's mechanism and predictions before grading them, and treat the slogans under which the readings travel as evidence about the reception rather than about the passage.

Historians will find the subject's archive unusually instructive, because the concept's career is documented with a completeness rare in intellectual history: dated editions, dated translations, dated lectures, and a polemical literature that rarely leaves its occasions obscure. The historian's questions give that archive its programme. How did the order of arrival, interpreters before text in English, text before interpreters in Italian, shape the disputes each language conducted? What did the editorial decisions of the complete writings, in their successive editions, do to the passage's availability and framing at each stage? How did the phrase pass from the journals of a political current into the handbooks of a discipline, and what was added and shed at each border crossing? Those are answerable questions with archival answers, and their answers would constitute the first full history of a concept manufactured, in the article's sense, within living memory, a specimen of unusual value for the history of theory generally.

Journalists covering the territory this phrase governs, the changing character of work and the claims made for a new economic epoch, can take from this article a checklist that will distinguish their reporting from the standard announcement of a world transformed. Establish the provenance of any concept doing heavy work in a source's argument, and report it with the same prominence as the argument. Establish whose claim is actually being advanced, the founder's or a living theorist's, and attribute accordingly. Ask the claimant how matters would stand if the claim were false, and report the answer, or the absence of one, as part of the story. Ask which direction the wage relation is moving in the claimant's own account, and whether the account's other commitments survive that direction. And when an epoch is announced, ask what mechanism distinguishes the epoch from the gradient it replaces. Reporting that performs those checks will still be serving its readers when the announcement has been forgotten, because it will have described the structure of the claim rather than merely relaying the claim, and describing structures, as this article has argued throughout, is the whole of the method.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Where does the term general intellect come from?

It comes from private notebooks Marx filled during the winter months of 1857 and 1858 while preparing the groundwork for his critique of political economy, the manuscripts posterity knows as the Grundrisse. The words sit inside a discussion of machinery and fixed capital, at the point where the argument turns to the knowledge built into the productive apparatus. Unusually, he set them down in English amid German prose. They recur only a handful of times, and no work he published carries them. The notebooks reached German print in the twentieth century; the complete English version, translated by Martin Nicolaus, appeared in 1973 and remains the citable text.

### Q: What does the fragment on machines actually say?

The fragment holds that, as industry grows large in scale, how much wealth gets created depends ever less on hours of labour spent directly and ever more on the potency of the forces those hours unleash, because society's accumulated knowledge, built into the machinery system, now works immediately as a productive power in its own right. The labourer's stance alters accordingly: instead of standing within the process as its principal agent, the labourer moves alongside it, watching over and regulating a movement whose intelligence is lodged in the apparatus. Marx presents the upshot as a tension capital cannot escape, straining against labour time as wealth's measure while going on imposing it.

### Q: Is the general intellect discussed in Capital?

It is not. Neither Capital nor any other work issued in the author's lifetime contains the phrase, a negative fact any reader can confirm against the published corpus and its indexes. The first volume's machinery chapter does examine kindred phenomena at length: skill passing from the worker into the mechanism, science enlisted as capital's servant, the mind of production confronting labour as somebody else's property. That chapter does the conceptual labour under different headings, and it never draws the inference that the measure itself has failed. Attributing the phrase to Capital mistakes a workshop jotting for a doctrine of the author's most carefully revised public book.

### Q: What do autonomists claim about the passage?

Autonomists claim that the passage saw, ahead of its time, a condition in which shared knowledge and social cooperation displace hours of labour as production's governing force. Antonio Negri built the claim into an architecture in lectures published in Italian in 1979 and Englished in 1991: the law of value, on his telling, enters terminal crisis, value creation spills beyond the factory across social existence, and the multitude, bearer of the common competences capital feeds on, becomes the decisive historical subject. Paolo Virno gave the position philosophical depth in a work Englished in 2004, dwelling on language and virtuosity. Maurizio Lazzarato supplied its account of work itself, centred on activities producing information, culture, and relationships.

### Q: What is the counter-reading of the fragment?

The counter-reading insists on three connected points. First, status: the words were written in a private notebook, never revised for the press, and so cannot outrank the published theory they are said to overturn. Second, location: the passage belongs to an analysis of fixed capital, and its burden is that the machinery system embodies society's accumulated knowledge, a claim concerning the forces of production. Third, continuity: the published work nowhere repeats the suggestion that the measure has failed, and its account of relative surplus value shows embodied knowledge enlarging the surplus by cheapening labour power, with the law intact. Textual scholars advance the first two points; value-form theorists such as Michael Heinrich press the third.

### Q: Does the evidence support the strong reading?

Judged by the predictions the reading itself generates, it does not. Were production truly escaping the workplace and the measure lapsing, dependence on wages for a livelihood should be shrinking, the working day should be losing its grip as an institution, and profit should float free of how much labour a capital sets to work. The long record shows the reverse directions: wage dependence widening across the world economy over decades, hours remaining the fiercely contested frame of employment and policed in ever finer detail, and profit staying anchored, system wide, to labour employed under discipline, however rent like its division among claimants can look. George Caffentzis, writing from inside the autonomist tradition in a 2013 collection, pressed precisely this test.

### Q: Why did Marx write the phrase in English?

Nobody knows his reason, and the honest answer starts by admitting it. What can be shown is the habit surrounding the choice: the notebooks are polyglot, excerpting authorities in their own languages and reaching for foreign expressions whenever a borrowed term carried the wanted resonance, and this phrase is one borrowing among many. Scholars have guessed at antecedents in the English political economy he was then reading closely, and the guesses may even be right, but they outrun the record. The inference the manuscript does support concerns status rather than motive: a phrase picked up in another tongue, mid draft, has the character of an improvisation, not of a concept coined for a system.

### Q: What is cognitive capitalism?

The expression names a proposed periodisation: a third age of the system, after mercantile and industrial ages, in which accumulating knowledge, and capturing the gains that knowledge spills beyond its producers, supposedly replaces accumulating plant and machinery as accumulation's engine. Its systematic statement is Yann Moulier Boutang's book, Englished in 2011; Christian Marazzi's study of language, Englished in 2008, connects the proposal to financial turbulence, and Franco Berardi's work, Englished in 2009, pursues its effects on mental life. All three lean on the fragment for authority. A careful reader will separate the label's descriptive core, that production's knowledge content keeps rising, from its epochal conclusion, that the law governing value has expired, since the second does not follow from the first.

### Q: Has the law of value ceased to operate?

No body of evidence assembled so far shows that it has. The phenomena the law names remain in place: autonomous producers making goods for exchange, labour power itself traded for a wage, each working day splitting into a stretch that replaces the wage and a stretch that yields a surplus, competition punishing whoever expends labour beyond the socially required standard, and crises periodically destroying claims that outgrew the surplus beneath them. Rising knowledge content, the fact usually offered for the obituary, is something the published theory anticipates and explains, through the cheapening of commodities that embodied science brings. Failure of the law would look like those phenomena changing in kind, and change in kind is what the obituary's literature has not demonstrated.

### Q: Which reading of the fragment is defensible?

The middle position earns that description. It grants the fragment its genuine insight: knowledge does harden into apparatus, production does rest ever more heavily on what society collectively knows, and any one worker's share in a joint product does grow harder to trace. It also keeps the fragment's own frame, in which those facts figure as a contradiction capital lives inside, straining against its measure without shedding it. And it refuses the leap from tendency to obituary, on two grounds that reinforce each other: the published corpus absorbs the tendency into its machinery analysis while omitting the obituary across decades of revision, and the world's long movement, above all the widening reach of wage dependence, runs against the obituary's forecast.

### Q: How does the thin basis grade the readings of the fragment?

It grades them on two separate examinations and refuses to merge the marks. On textual support, the strong reading scores lowest, since it founds an epoch on a handful of unpublished pages; the counter-reading scores highest, resting on the passage's setting inside the fixed capital analysis, on the silence of the published corpus, and on that corpus doing the same work under other headings. On empirical fortune, each reading is marked by its forecasts: the strong reading expects wage dependence to wither and confronts its growth, while the middle position expects knowledge intensity and wage dependence to advance together and is borne out. Combining the examinations leaves the middle position first overall, the counter-reading first on the text alone.

### Q: What should a student separate between a passage Marx published and one he left in notebooks?

The student should separate commitment from exploration. What an author publishes has been arranged, weighed against objections, and owned before an audience entitled to answer back, so it may be quoted as doctrine at full weight. What an author leaves in a notebook records thinking in motion: formulations pushed past their defensible strength to see what follows, contradictions left standing, paths entered and quietly abandoned. Notebooks are precious evidence about how a doctrine was reached, and the tradition would be unintelligible without its posthumous volumes and early manuscripts. But weight follows status, and a notebook sentence set against the published architecture must carry the burden of the contradiction, not enjoy the presumption of it.

### Q: Why does the phrase's rarity matter to the strong reading?

Because the strong reading asks the phrase to carry a cornerstone's load, and cornerstones in this corpus are recognisable by their recurrence. Surplus value, labour power, the reserve army: each returns across hundreds of pages, gathering definition and application, in published and unpublished writing alike. The phrase under discussion appears a handful of times in a single manuscript stretch, then disappears from twenty five further years of work during which its author repeatedly revised the very analyses, machinery, fixed capital, science in production, where it would have served him directly. A single statement can of course carry a decisive idea. Silence maintained across that many republication opportunities is evidence that its author set no such store by the statement, and promotion must answer the evidence.

### Q: How does the three readings table order the interpretations?

The table refuses the usual orderings, by fame or by chronology, and substitutes an ordering by examination. Its rows give each interpretation one line: the strong reading, the counter-reading, and the middle position. Its columns then put the same four questions to every row: what the interpretation says the passage establishes, what support in the text it can show, what it leads one to expect in the world that can be observed, and how the evidence treats that expectation. Reading down a column compares the rivals on a single test; reading across a row shows a single rival's whole case. The arrangement matters because the dispute's confusion usually comes from grading one reading on its textual fidelity and another on its suggestiveness, and the table makes that substitution visible at a glance.

### Q: What separates the strong reading from the defensible one?

One inferential step, taken or declined. Start where both readings start, with the tendency the passage undeniably describes: wealth creation leaning ever less on direct hours and ever more on the organised knowledge set in motion alongside them. The strong reading converts that tendency into an accomplished transition, announcing that the measure has given out and the law has lapsed. The defensible reading stops at the tendency and asks the two questions the strong reading skips: whether the author's considered work ratified the conversion, which it did not, and whether the world displays the conversion's signature, a retreating wage relation, which it does not. The step is small enough to miss in a summary and large enough to carry an epoch, which is why the article isolates it.
