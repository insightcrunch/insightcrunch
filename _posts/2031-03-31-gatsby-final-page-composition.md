The composition history of the final page of The Great Gatsby is the record of how the most celebrated closing in American fiction came to exist, and the record quietly contradicts the romance that surrounds it. The romance holds that the ending arrived whole, that Fitzgerald wrote the closing meditation in a single inspired pass and that the last page of the novel never needed anything more than transcription. The textual record tells a less romantic and more useful story. Across the surviving stages of the novel's making, from the early drafts through the intermediate typescript that Cambridge published in 2000 under the title Trimalchio to the galley proofs, the ending was drafted, revised, repositioned, and adjusted. Material was relocated into the closing from elsewhere in the narrative. The placement of the closing meditation was settled through revision rather than fixed from the first attempt. The famous close that readers memorize was assembled, not found.

That claim is the spine of this article, and it deserves to be stated plainly before the evidence is laid out. The greatest ending in American fiction was assembled through documented revision. The manuscript and proof evidence reveals the choices that made the last page what it is: choices about where the closing meditation should sit, about which material belonged on the final page and which belonged earlier, and about how the ending should balance the particular story of Jay Gatsby against the larger reflection that closes the book. None of those choices was made once and for all at the start. Each was tested, moved, and remade as the novel passed through its documented stages. To study the composition history of the final page is to watch a masterpiece being built in the only way masterpieces are ever built, by work that leaves a record.

The complication this article must address honestly is the assumption it argues against. Most readers meet the ending as a finished surface, and a finished surface hides its making. The closing reads as inevitable, and inevitability is the hardest effect to see through, because the mind confuses how an ending feels with how it was produced. An ending that feels inevitable must have been written that way, the assumption runs, in one continuous motion of inspiration. The textual evidence refuses that assumption. It shows an ending reshaped across stages, with the closing meditation relocated into its final position through revision and with the proofs still carrying alterations to the novel's closing machinery. This article argues the assembled case from that evidence, stage by stage, and it does so under a strict discipline: it attributes the textual evidence accurately, states only what the documented record supports, and keeps its claims conservative where the specifics of the record do not permit more.

The galley proofs document the final phase, where Fitzgerald was still making changes, and the record of that phase includes the documented revisions to Gatsby's biography and the middle chapters, proof that the proof stage was a working stage and not a formality. These three anchors, the draft stages, the Trimalchio text, and the proofs, are the ground this article stands on. ## The Composition History of the Final Page: The Record at a Glance

The composition history of the final page can be summarized in a single paragraph, and the summary is worth carrying through the longer argument. The ending of The Great Gatsby exists in more than one documented state. In the early draft stage it appears in provisional form, tied to an arrangement of the narrative that the published novel would later abandon, with the closing meditation not yet settled in its final position. In the Trimalchio typescript stage it appears in an intermediate form, accessible through the 2000 Cambridge edition, in which the closing passage sits in a different arrangement from the published close and the ending has not yet undergone the reshaping that the last rounds of revision would bring. In the proof stage it appears under final adjustment, with alterations still being made as the novel moved through galleys. The famous closing is the product of all three stages, not of any single one.

That summary matters because it reframes what the final page is. A reader who knows only the published novel sees the ending as a destination the author reached. A reader who knows the composition history sees the ending as a decision the author kept making. The difference is not academic trivia. It changes how the closing meditation reads, because a passage that was relocated into position carries a different charge from a passage that was always meant to be there. It changes how the last page's authority reads, because authority built through revision is authority earned by testing alternatives. And it changes what the ending teaches about writing, because the lesson of an assembled ending is that revision is not the enemy of inspiration but its instrument. The stages that follow fill in that summary with the evidence the record supports, and the composition table gathers the stages into a single view.

### What did the earliest surviving form of the ending look like?

The earliest surviving form of the ending was provisional rather than finished: the closing meditation had not yet settled into its published position, material destined for the last page still sat elsewhere in the narrative, and the ending was keyed to an arrangement of events the published novel would later abandon. The record shows a closing still being decided.

The first thing to understand about the early draft stage is what an early state of a novel's ending actually is. It is not a rough version of the famous close with the same sentences in a messier hand. It is a different ending to a different arrangement of the book, because the early drafts of The Great Gatsby carried a different architecture from the published novel. The chapters were ordered differently, episodes sat in different places, and the narrative built toward its close along a different path. The ending that belonged to that architecture could not have been the published ending, because the published ending answers a structure that did not yet exist. When scholars speak of the early form of the ending, they mean the close that fit the book as it stood at that stage, with its own emphases and its own proportions, before the restructuring of the novel demanded a new close.

That restructuring is the key to everything the early stage shows. A novel's ending is not detachable from the chapters that lead to it. It is the final term in a sequence, and when the sequence changes, the final term must change with it. The early draft stage of Gatsby shows an ending calibrated to a book in which the middle chapters did different work, in which Gatsby's biography was distributed differently across the narrative, and in which the balance between Nick's story and Gatsby's story had not yet found its published proportions. The record of the galley stage documents how substantially Gatsby's biography and the middle chapters were still being revised late in the process, and that late revision is itself evidence of how much the architecture was still moving. If the middle of the book was still being reworked at the proof stage, then the early ending was written for a middle that no longer existed by the time the book was finished, and the final page had to be recomposed to meet the book the novel had become.

The second thing the early stage shows is the unsettled position of the closing meditation. The meditation that now closes the novel, the reflective passage that lifts the ending from the particular story of Gatsby to the larger vision with which the book ends, did not begin in that position. The textual record documents its placement as the result of revision, which means that at the early stage it sat elsewhere or existed in a form not yet assigned to the ending's work. This is one of the most consequential facts in the composition history of the final page, and it deserves to be dwelled on rather than hurried past. The passage that readers experience as the natural culmination of the novel, the moment the book opens outward, was not the culmination at the start. It became the culmination through the author's decision to move it there, or to reshape material into that function, across the stages of revision. The inevitability readers feel is therefore a manufactured inevitability, and the manufacturing is the story.

There is a temptation, when describing early draft states, to fill in the gaps with imagined sentences, to describe what Fitzgerald must have written before he wrote what he wrote. The discipline this article follows forbids that temptation. The record supports certain claims about the early ending and does not support others, and an honest composition history says plainly which is which. What the record supports is the stage-level account: the ending existed in provisional form; its architecture belonged to an earlier arrangement of the novel; the closing meditation was not yet fixed in its published position; material that would end up on the last page was still distributed elsewhere. What the record does not support, at the level of specificity a reader might wish for, is a sentence-by-sentence transcript of every early alternative, because the surviving record is partial and the stages are the unit of evidence. A composition history that respects its evidence describes the stages faithfully and refuses to invent the missing sentences.

That refusal is itself a lesson in how to read composition evidence, and it is worth making explicit because readers new to textual scholarship often expect the wrong kind of detail. The manuscript record of a novel's ending is not a film of the author at work. It is a set of surviving states, each one a snapshot of the text at a documented moment, with gaps between the snapshots where work happened that left no direct trace. What belongs here is the consequence for the final page: the ending's earliest surviving form shows a closing that had not yet become the famous close, attached to a novel that had not yet become the published novel, and the distance between that early form and the published ending is the measure of how much revision the last page required.

A student working with this material can make the early stage concrete through a simple exercise that requires no access beyond the published record. Take the published ending and read it against the architecture of the novel as it stands, noting every point where the closing depends on something established earlier: the green light, the geography of the eggs, the accumulated weight of Nick's judgments. The exercise makes visible what the record shows at the stage level: an ending cannot be revised in isolation, because every change to the chapters before it changes what the ending must do. The early form of Gatsby's ending looks the way it does because the early form of Gatsby's novel looked the way it did, and the two changed together through the stages that followed.

The early stage also establishes the baseline against which every later change must be measured. Without a provisional starting point, revision is just a word; with one, revision becomes a visible process of decisions. The closing meditation moves into position. Material relocates from the body of the narrative to the ending. Emphasis redistributes between the particular and the reflective. Each of these movements has a before and an after, and the early draft stage supplies the before. The early stage is where the assembly begins, and its provisional character is the first proof of the claim this article makes: the famous close was built, stage by stage, and the building started here.

### How did the closing passage change between the Trimalchio text and the published novel?

Between the Trimalchio typescript and the published novel, the closing passage was reshaped rather than merely polished: the record shows the ending in an intermediate arrangement, with the closing meditation positioned differently from its published place and emphasis redistributed across the last page. The changes of this interval moved the ending decisively toward its famous form.

The Trimalchio stage is the most instructive of the three for a reader who wants to see revision as a process rather than a rumor, because it is the stage the public can actually read. The 2000 Cambridge edition of Trimalchio preserves the intermediate typescript of the novel, the text as it stood between the early drafts and the published book, and that preservation turns an abstract claim about revision into a concrete comparison. A reader can hold the Trimalchio ending and the published ending side by side and watch the distance between them. The discipline of the comparison matters as much as the result: the point is not to declare one version better and the other worse, but to identify what moved, what was repositioned, and what was redistributed, and then to ask what each movement accomplished for the final page.

What moved, at the level the record supports, was the architecture of the close. The Trimalchio text carries the ending in a different arrangement from the published novel, and the differences are structural before they are verbal. The closing meditation, the reflective passage that now lifts the last page beyond Gatsby's particular story, does not yet occupy the position it would come to hold. The record documents its placement as the outcome of revision across stages, and the Trimalchio stage is the middle term of that revision: later than the early drafts, where the meditation's position was still provisional, and earlier than the published novel, where the placement had been settled. To read the Trimalchio ending is therefore to read the closing meditation in transit, present in the text but not yet assigned to the work it would do on the final page. That in-transit quality is exactly what makes this stage precious to composition history, because it catches the famous close in the act of becoming itself.

The redistribution of emphasis across the last page is the second change this interval shows. An ending balances two demands that pull in opposite directions: it must close the particular story, settling the fates the narrative has raised, and it must open the story outward, giving the particulars a resonance larger than themselves. The Trimalchio stage shows that balance in a different calibration from the published close. The record supports the stage-level claim that emphasis was redistributed between the Trimalchio text and the novel, with the reflective turn of the ending gaining the position and weight that readers now take for granted. The significance of that redistribution is easy to understate. The famous close is famous largely because of its reflective turn, the passage where the novel stops being about one man's summer and becomes about the country and the dream. That turn was not an original feature of the ending that later drafts preserved. It was an achievement of the revision interval between Trimalchio and publication, and the composition history of the final page is in large part the history of how that turn was built.

The third change is the hardest to describe without overclaiming, and the discipline of this article requires the care. Between the Trimalchio typescript and the published novel, the closing passage was not only repositioned and rebalanced but rewritten at the level of the sentence, and the sentence-level revision is where the author's ear did its finest work. The record supports the claim that the closing was reshaped across this interval; it does not always support a neat pairing of a Trimalchio sentence with its published replacement, because the changes were sometimes structural enough that one-to-one mapping misleads. The honest way to describe it is this: the interval between the intermediate typescript and the published novel contains the heaviest revision the ending underwent, revision that touched position, proportion, and phrasing together, and the famous close emerged from that interval rather than preceding it. A reader who wants the full textual detail of the variants has the proper instrument in James L. W. West III's 2019 Cambridge variorum edition, which documents the relationships among the states. This article stays at the stage level the evidence warrants and points the specialist to the variorum.

The Trimalchio stage also clarifies what revision means when scholars use the word with precision. In casual use, revision means fixing what is wrong. In the composition history of this ending, revision meant something larger: re-conceiving what the ending was for. The early drafts had given the novel one kind of close, fitted to one kind of book. The Trimalchio typescript shows the author between conceptions, with the old architecture loosening and the new one not yet locked. The published novel shows the new conception realized. The movement from Trimalchio to publication is therefore not a story of polishing a fundamentally finished ending. It is a story of re-conceiving the ending's function and then rebuilding the last page to perform it. The closing meditation found its published position because the author's conception of what the ending should do changed, and the position followed the conception.

For a student of composition history, the Trimalchio interval offers the single best exercise the record provides. Read the Trimalchio ending first, without the published close fresh in mind, and note where the emphasis falls and what the last page seems to be doing. Then read the published ending and note the same things. The comparison will show the repositioning and the redistribution at the stage level, without any need for specialized apparatus. The exercise also teaches the central caution of the field: the differences that matter most are not always the most visible. A repositioned meditation changes the ending more profoundly than a reworded sentence, and the reader trained only to notice word changes will miss the revision that made the famous close famous. ### What last alterations did the proofs make to the final page?

The proofs show the final page still under adjustment at the last documented stage: the record of the galley phase confirms that revision of the novel, including its closing machinery, continued past the typescript, with the ending receiving late alterations before the text was fixed. The famous close was still being worked when the record runs out.

The proof stage occupies a special place in the composition history of any novel, because it is the last moment when the author can still change the book. After the proofs, the text is fixed, printed, and bound, and the author's power over it ends. What an author does in proof therefore reveals what the author still considered unfinished when the book was nearly done. In the case of The Great Gatsby, the proof stage was a working stage, not a formality, and the record documents that fact in terms no reader of the composition history can ignore. The galley-stage revisions to Gatsby's biography and to the middle chapters are documented, which means that major structural and expository material was still being reworked when the novel was already in type. An author still revising the biography of his protagonist and the architecture of his middle chapters at the proof stage is an author for whom no part of the book, including the ending, was yet beyond reconsideration.

That context is what gives the proof-stage alterations to the final page their significance. The record shows the ending receiving its last adjustments in the proofs, and the adjustments matter less for their individual weight than for what they prove about the process. They prove that the famous close was not finished when the typescript was finished. The revision interval between Trimalchio and publication, which the previous section described as the heaviest the ending underwent, did not end when the typescript left the author's desk. It extended into the galleys, where the author kept working. The closing that readers memorize is therefore the product of a process that ran to the very edge of the possible, stopping only when the mechanics of publication forced it to stop. There is a sense in which every printed book is unfinished, cut off by the press rather than completed by the author, and the proof record of Gatsby's ending makes that abstract truth concrete.

The nature of proof revision differs from the revision of earlier stages, and the difference illuminates what the last alterations to the final page could and could not do. In the draft stages, the author can rebuild: reposition the closing meditation, relocate material from the body to the ending, redistribute emphasis across the last page. In the proofs, rebuilding is expensive and constrained, because the text is already set in type and large changes ripple through the pages. Proof revision is therefore a revision of adjustment rather than of architecture: fine changes to phrasing, to rhythm, to the exact calibration of a passage, made within a structure already fixed. The late alterations to the final page belong to this kind. They are the last turn of the dials, not the construction of the instrument. But the dials matter enormously on a last page, where every word carries the weight of the whole book and where a small adjustment to rhythm can change how the closing lands. The famous close is famous at the level of the sentence, and the sentence level is exactly where proof revision operates.

There is also a discipline to observe about what the proof record can and cannot show, and it parallels the discipline observed for the earlier stages. What belongs in the composition history of the final page is the consequence: the ending's last documented state is a state of continuing work, and the published close is the point where that work was interrupted by publication rather than the point where it was completed by design. The distinction is subtle but it matters for the claim this article makes. An ending completed by design could still be the product of a single inspiration finished early. An ending interrupted by publication is necessarily the product of revision running to the limit, and the proof record shows the latter.

The proof stage also closes a loop that opened in the earliest drafts. The early stage showed an ending provisional because the book's architecture was still moving. The Trimalchio stage showed an ending in transit between conceptions. The proof stage shows an ending nearly fixed but still being touched, with the author using the last available opportunity to adjust the close. Across all three stages, the pattern is the same: the ending was never a settled thing that the author simply preserved through the process. It was a living part of the manuscript at every stage, revised when the drafts were revised, repositioned when the conception changed, and adjusted when the proofs offered a final chance. The assembled character of the famous close is therefore not an inference from one stage but the consistent testimony of all of them.

A reader who wants to feel the weight of the proof stage can do so through a practical exercise that the record makes possible. Take any sentence of the published closing and imagine it as a proof-stage decision, a choice made when the book was already in type and every change had a cost. The exercise reverses the usual direction of reading, which moves from the finished sentence backward to an imagined ease of composition. Read forward instead, from the constraint to the sentence: the author, with the press waiting, chose these words over the alternatives the typescript offered, and the choice stuck because the alternatives were abandoned, not because they were never considered. The famous close feels inevitable, but the proof record shows the inevitability being manufactured at the last possible moment, one adjustment at a time, until the record runs out and the book goes to press.

### Where did the closing meditation sit before it reached the ending?

Before it reached the ending, the closing meditation sat elsewhere in the narrative's design: the textual record documents its published position as the result of revision, which means the reflective passage that now closes the novel was relocated into the ending's work across the draft stages. It became the culmination through movement, not through original placement.

Relocation is the least intuitive of the revision operations this article describes, and it is the one that most decisively supports the claim that the ending was assembled. Drafting is intuitive: the author writes something new. Revising is intuitive: the author improves what was written. Relocating is stranger, because it means the author takes material that was written for one place in the narrative and moves it to another, where it does different work than it was designed to do. The composition history of Gatsby's final page documents exactly this operation. Material that now belongs to the ending, including the reflective turn of the closing meditation, was repositioned into the closing across the stages of revision. The famous close is therefore not only revised but relocated, and the relocation is part of what makes it famous.

To understand why relocation matters, consider what a closing meditation does in a novel. It is the moment the book lifts its gaze from the particular story it has been telling and speaks, briefly, in a larger register. In the published Gatsby, that lift is the source of the ending's power: the last page moves from Nick's farewell to Gatsby into a reflection that encompasses the American experience, and the movement gives the novel its closing authority. Now consider that this lift, this most characteristic gesture of the famous close, was not originally positioned to do that work. The record documents its placement as settled through revision, which means that at earlier stages the reflective passage existed in a different relation to the narrative, serving a different function or carrying a different emphasis. The author recognized in it a capacity it had not been assigned, moved it into the ending's position, and reshaped the close around it. The passage did not change its nature; it changed its address, and the change of address changed the book.

The significance of that movement extends beyond a single passage. Relocation is evidence of a particular kind of authorial intelligence, the kind that sees material not as fixed to its first use but as available for better uses discovered in the course of revision. An author who only drafts and polishes works within the plan. An author who relocates revises the plan itself, discovering in the manuscript possibilities the original design did not contain. The final page of Gatsby is the beneficiary of that intelligence. Its reflective turn, its balance between the particular and the universal, its sense of arrival at a vantage larger than the story, all of these are the products of material being moved into the ending and reweighted there. The closing reads as though it were conceived for its position because the revision that placed it there was thorough enough to erase the seams. But the record preserves the movement, and the movement is the proof of assembly.

There is a further consequence that touches the meaning of the ending itself. A meditation written for the ending would be an ending's meditation, designed from the start to close. A meditation relocated into the ending carries with it the trace of its earlier function, a generality or a detachment that belonged to its first context and that now, in its new position, reads as the novel's hard-won wisdom. The published closing has exactly this quality: it feels both intimately tied to Gatsby's story and strangely detached from it, as though the novel were seeing its own subject from a great distance. That doubleness is the signature of relocated material, the way a passage written for one purpose and moved to another retains a productive tension between its origin and its destination. Readers have always felt the ending's peculiar authority without being able to name its source. The composition history names it: the authority comes from relocation, from a passage that earned its final position by proving it could do more than its first position asked.

The mechanics of relocation also explain something about the ending's famous economy. The last page of Gatsby is short, and its brevity is part of its power; nothing is wasted, and every sentence seems to carry the weight of the book. Brevity of this kind is rarely achieved by writing briefly in the first draft. It is achieved by moving material until the arrangement itself does the work that extra sentences would otherwise have to do. When the closing meditation was relocated into its final position, it brought with it the resonance of its earlier context, and that resonance allowed the ending to say more with less. The surrounding material, the sentences that lead into the meditation and the sentences that follow it, could then be trimmed to their essentials, because the relocated passage was already doing double duty. The famous compression of the final page is therefore not only a stylistic achievement but a compositional one, the result of material finding, through movement, the position where it means the most.

A student can make relocation tangible through an exercise that mirrors the author's operation. Take a reflective passage from the middle of any long piece of your own writing, a paragraph that generalizes or steps back, and move it to the end. Read the piece with the passage in its new position, and notice two things: how the passage changes meaning by changing neighbors, and how the ending gains a lift it did not have before. The exercise is crude beside Fitzgerald's practice, but it demonstrates the principle the record documents. Position is meaning. The closing meditation of Gatsby means what it means because of where it sits, and it sits where it sits because the author moved it there through revision. The earliest stage showed the meditation unsettled. The Trimalchio stage showed it in transit. The published novel shows it arrived. The movement between those states is relocation, and relocation is the operation that most purely embodies the claim of this article: the ending was assembled, piece by piece, and the most important piece was carried into place.

### How did Fitzgerald's habit of revision shape the famous close?

Fitzgerald's habit of revision shaped the famous close by treating no part of the ending as settled until the process forced settlement: the record shows him repositioning the closing meditation, relocating material into the last page, and adjusting the proofs, applying the same restless reworking to the ending that the galley record documents in the biography and middle chapters.

The habit is the right word, because the composition history of the final page does not show an author who revised the ending as an exception. It shows an author who revised the ending as a matter of course, the way he revised everything else. The documented galley-stage revisions to Gatsby's biography and the middle chapters are the context that makes this point, and the point deserves emphasis: at the proof stage, when most authors are correcting errors, Fitzgerald was still reworking the protagonist's backstory and the architecture of the middle of the book. An author who treats the biography of his title character as revisable in proof is not an author who would treat the last page as sacred. The ending received the same restless attention as the rest of the manuscript, and the famous close is the product of that attention applied to the final page with the same thoroughness it was applied everywhere else.

That thoroughness has a particular character, and the composition history of the ending reveals it. Fitzgerald's revision was not corrective but exploratory. Corrective revision fixes what is broken: a continuity error, a flat sentence, a scene that does not work. Exploratory revision tests what a passage could become: it moves the closing meditation to see what the ending does with it there, it relocates material from the body to the last page to test the new balance, it redistributes emphasis to find the calibration where the close lands hardest. The record of the final page shows exploratory revision at every stage. The early drafts explored one architecture and its fitting close. The Trimalchio interval explored a new conception of the ending's function. The proofs explored the last adjustments of phrasing and rhythm. At no stage does the record show the author merely fixing. At every stage it shows him searching, and the famous close is what the search found.

The search metaphor matters because it corrects the most persistent misunderstanding of revision, the idea that revision is what an author does when inspiration fails. In that misunderstanding, the inspired first draft is the real creation and revision is the cleanup, necessary but secondary. The composition history of Gatsby's ending inverts the hierarchy. The first states of the ending were provisional, tied to architectures the novel would abandon. The inspiration, if that word must be used, arrived during revision: in the recognition that the closing meditation belonged at the end, in the decision to relocate material into the closing, in the late adjustments that tuned the final page. The famous close was not inspired and then revised. It was revised into inspiration, and the record of the stages is the record of that transformation. This is why the claim of this article uses the word assembled without apology. Assembly is not a lesser mode of creation. For this ending, it was the mode of creation.

The habit of revision also explains the ending's most distinctive technical feature, the seamlessness with which its parts fit. A close assembled from relocated material and repositioned meditation could easily feel patched, with visible seams where the pieces were joined. The published ending shows no seams, and the absence of seams is itself evidence of how thoroughly the revision was carried out. Each relocation was followed by the rewriting necessary to integrate the moved material. Each repositioning was followed by the redistribution of emphasis that made the new position feel native. The proofs supplied the final pass of adjustment that smoothed whatever roughness remained. The result is an ending that reads as though it were written in a single movement, and the irony is exact: it reads that way because it was revised until the traces of revision disappeared. The author's habit did not only assemble the ending. It concealed the assembly, and the concealment is part of the achievement.

There is a lesson here for the study of writing that goes beyond this novel, and the composition history of the final page is an unusually pure illustration of it. ### Why does the finished ending feel inevitable when it was assembled?

The finished ending feels inevitable because thorough revision erases its own traces: each relocation was integrated, each repositioning rebalanced, and the proofs supplied a final smoothing, so the assembled close reads as a single movement. Inevitability is the effect of complete assembly, and the record shows the work that produced the effect.

The paradox at the heart of this article deserves a full reckoning, because it is the reason the complication addressed here has been so hard to dislodge. The ending of The Great Gatsby feels inevitable. Readers across a century have felt that the book could not have ended any other way, that the closing meditation belongs exactly where it sits, that the last page completes the novel with a necessity closer to music than to prose. And the composition history shows that the ending was assembled through revision, relocation, and late adjustment, the very opposite of inevitability in its making. Both cannot be true in the naive sense, yet both are true, and the resolution of the paradox is the deepest lesson the final page's composition has to teach.

The resolution begins with a distinction between inevitability as a property of the making and inevitability as an effect of the made. Nothing about the composition history suggests that the ending was inevitable in its making. The early drafts show a different close for a different book. The Trimalchio stage shows the closing meditation in transit. The proofs show the author still adjusting. At every stage, the record shows alternatives: the ending could have kept its earlier architecture, the meditation could have stayed where it was, the emphasis could have fallen elsewhere. The making was contingent through and through, a series of choices among possibilities. But the made thing, the published last page, produces in the reader the feeling that no other choice was possible. That feeling is not a report on the making. It is an achievement of the making, the specific achievement of revision carried far enough that the seams disappear.

The disappearance of the seams is a technical accomplishment with a precise mechanism. When material is relocated, the author's next task is integration: rewriting the surrounding sentences so that the moved passage reads as native to its new position. When the closing meditation is repositioned, the next task is rebalancing: redistributing emphasis across the last page so that the new position carries the weight the ending requires. When the proofs offer a final opportunity, the task is smoothing: adjusting phrasing and rhythm until the assembled close moves as a single gesture. Each of these operations removes a trace of the assembly. Integration removes the trace of relocation. Rebalancing removes the trace of repositioning. Smoothing removes the trace of adjustment. What remains is a surface with no visible history, and a surface with no visible history reads as inevitable, because the reader has no evidence of the alternatives that were considered and set aside. The feeling of inevitability is therefore not the absence of revision. It is the presence of revision completed so thoroughly that its evidence has been absorbed into the result.

There is a further mechanism at work in the particular case of this ending, and it concerns the placement of the closing meditation. A meditation placed at the end of a narrative borrows authority from everything that precedes it. Every chapter, every scene, every accumulated detail becomes, retroactively, the ground on which the meditation stands. The reader, arriving at the last page after the whole of Gatsby's story, experiences the reflective turn not as an addition to the narrative but as its revelation, the meaning that was implicit all along. That experience is powerful, and it is also an artifact of position. The same meditation, placed earlier, would read as a digression or a premature conclusion. Placed at the end, after the full weight of the story has accumulated, it reads as destiny. The author's decision to relocate the meditation into the ending's position was therefore also a decision to harness this positional authority, to let the whole novel do the work of making the closing feel necessary. The inevitability readers feel is, in part, the novel's own mass pressing on the last page, and the composition history shows that pressure being deliberately arranged.

The reader's own psychology completes the effect. Human minds are pattern-completing instruments, and a finished work of art invites the mind to treat its actual form as its necessary form. Confronted with the published ending, the reader's imagination does not spontaneously generate the Trimalchio alternative or the early-draft architecture. It takes the given close and finds in it the coherence that justifies it, the way the closing meditation gathers the novel's themes, the way the last page balances the particular and the universal. That coherence is real, but it is the coherence of the assembled result, not evidence that the result was the only possibility. The composition history supplies what the reader's imagination does not: the record of the alternatives, the stages through which the ending passed, the choices that selected this close from among the closes the material could have supported. To read the ending with its composition history in mind is to hold both truths at once, the felt inevitability and the documented contingency, and to understand the first as the triumph of the second.

### What can the order of the draft stages tell us that a single stage cannot?

The order of the draft stages reveals the direction of the author's thinking, which no single stage can show: one state shows what the ending was at a moment, but the sequence from early draft through Trimalchio to proofs shows what the author moved toward, what was abandoned, and what was carried forward.

This distinction between a state and a sequence is the foundation of composition history as a discipline, and the final page of Gatsby illustrates it with unusual clarity. Consider what a reader learns from each stage in isolation. The early draft stage, taken alone, shows an ending fitted to an earlier architecture, provisional and tied to arrangements the published novel abandoned. The Trimalchio stage, taken alone, shows an intermediate text with the closing meditation in transit and emphasis distributed differently from the published close. The proof stage, taken alone, shows a nearly finished text still receiving adjustments. Each of these is informative, and each corrects the naive picture of an ending written whole. But none of them, alone, shows the movement that is the real subject of this article. The claim that the ending was assembled is a claim about movement between stages, and movement is visible only in sequence.

The sequence shows direction first. Direction is the most basic thing an ordered series of texts can reveal: not just that the ending changed, but which way it changed. The composition history of the final page shows a consistent direction across all three intervals. From the early drafts to Trimalchio, the direction is toward a new conception of the ending's function, with the closing passage reshaped and the meditation repositioned. From Trimalchio to the proofs, the direction is toward refinement within the new conception, with the architecture settled and the adjustments operating at the level of integration and rhythm. The direction is never reversed. The record shows no stage at which the author moved the ending back toward an earlier state, no interval in which the closing meditation was returned to a previous position or the relocated material sent back to its origin. The author's thinking moved in one direction, from the provisional toward the published, and the sequence is the evidence of that one-way movement. A single stage cannot show direction because direction requires at least two points. The three stages supply them.

The sequence shows abandonment second, and abandonment is as informative as achievement. Every revision interval in the ending's history involved not only choosing a new arrangement but abandoning an old one, and the abandoned alternatives are part of the composition story. The early architecture of the novel, with its different ordering of chapters and different distribution of Gatsby's biography, was abandoned in favor of the published architecture, and the early ending went with it. Whatever position the closing meditation occupied before its relocation was abandoned in favor of the ending's position. Whatever calibration of emphasis the Trimalchio close embodied was abandoned in favor of the published redistribution. These abandonments are invisible in the published text, which preserves only the choices that survived. They are visible only in the sequence, where each stage can be read as the rejection of the previous stage's solutions. The famous close is therefore not only the product of what the author chose. It is the product of what the author refused, and the refusals are legible only across the ordered stages.

The sequence shows accumulation third. The ending did not simply change from state to state; it accumulated the results of each interval, carrying forward what worked and reworking what did not. The repositioning of the closing meditation, achieved across the draft stages, was preserved through the proof stage, where it was refined rather than revisited. The redistribution of emphasis, achieved in the Trimalchio-to-publication interval, became the ground for the proof-stage adjustments, which tuned the new balance rather than reimagining it. This cumulative character is what makes the assembly metaphor exact. An assembly is not a series of replacements, each discarding the last. It is a series of additions and integrations, each building on the previous state. The sequence of the final page's stages shows exactly this pattern: each stage inherits the achievements of its predecessor and adds its own, so that the published ending contains, in integrated form, the work of all three phases. A single stage shows the inheritance without the history. The sequence shows the history that explains the inheritance.

The sequence also disciplines the imagination in a way that isolated stages cannot. Faced with a single early state, a reader might imagine the author leaping directly from that state to the published close in a burst of inspiration. The intermediate Trimalchio stage forbids that imagination, because it shows the distance being covered in steps, with the closing meditation in transit and the emphasis only partly redistributed. Faced with the Trimalchio text alone, a reader might imagine the remaining distance as trivial polishing. The proof stage, with its documented late alterations, forbids that imagination too, because it shows the author still working when the text was already in type. Each stage, set against the others, corrects the simplifications the others invite. The sequence is therefore not only informative but corrective, a built-in check against the romance of the single leap. The ending was assembled step by step, and the steps are the stages in their order.

For the student of composition history, the lesson of sequence suggests a concrete practice. Do not study the stages as a gallery of alternatives, each interesting in itself. Study them as a film, running the early draft through Trimalchio to the proofs in order, and watch for the three things sequence reveals: the direction of change, the alternatives abandoned, and the achievements accumulated. The chart will show the author's thinking as a trajectory rather than a set of positions, and the trajectory is the composition history. The single stages are the data. The order is the argument. And the argument is the one this article has been making from its first paragraph: the greatest ending in American fiction was assembled, and its assembly is visible only when the stages are read in the order the author lived them.

### How does the composition history of the ending relate to the composition of the novel as a whole?

The ending's composition history mirrors the novel's as a concentrated instance of the same process: the record shows the ending revised at every stage the book was revised, repositioned when the architecture changed, and adjusted in the proofs alongside the biography and middle chapters. The last page was revised with the book, not separately from it.

The mirror relationship follows from a structural necessity that this article has invoked before and that now deserves its full statement. A novel's ending is the final term of the sequence the novel constitutes, and it cannot be revised independently of the terms that precede it. Every change to the middle chapters changes what the ending must accomplish, because the ending's work is defined by what the earlier chapters have established and left unresolved. Every change to Gatsby's biography changes the weight the ending must carry, because the closing meditation reflects on a character whose dimensions the biography determines. Every reordering of the chapters changes the path by which the reader arrives at the last page, and the path determines what the arrival feels like. The ending is therefore the most dependent part of the novel, the part most sensitive to changes elsewhere, and its composition history cannot be separated from the composition history of the book. To revise the novel was, necessarily, to revise the ending, whether the author touched the last page directly or not.

The documented record confirms the necessity with evidence. The galley-stage revisions to Gatsby's biography and the middle chapters are the key fact, and their relevance to the final page is direct. Consider what a revision to Gatsby's biography does to the ending. The closing of the novel reflects on Gatsby, on the nature of his dream and the meaning of his pursuit. If the biography changes, if the facts of Gatsby's past are redistributed or his origins reweighted, then the object of the ending's reflection changes, and the reflection must change with it. The author revising the biography in proof was therefore also, implicitly, revising the ending, because the ending's meditation had to remain true to the character the biography now described. Similarly, a revision to the middle chapters changes the accumulation of incident and judgment that the reader carries into the last page. The ending's authority depends on that accumulation. Alter the chapters and the ending must be recalibrated, or it will close a book that no longer exists. The proof-stage alterations to the final page, documented in the record, are the visible part of this recalibration. The invisible part is the pressure that the biography and chapter revisions exerted on the close, pressure the author answered by adjusting the ending to meet the book the novel had become.

This interdependence explains why the ending's composition history shows revision at every stage. It was not that the author was uniquely dissatisfied with the last page, returning to it out of special perfectionism while the rest of the book settled. It was that the rest of the book kept changing, and the ending had to keep pace. The early drafts show the ending fitted to an early architecture; when the architecture changed, the ending had to be reconceived. The Trimalchio interval shows the ending reshaped between conceptions; the reshaping answered the reconception of the novel that the intermediate typescript embodied. The proofs show the ending adjusted at the last moment; the adjustments answered the late revisions to the biography and middle chapters that the galley record documents. At each stage, the ending's revision was the consequence of the novel's revision, and the famous close is the final term of a process that ran through the entire manuscript. The assembly of the last page was not a local project. It was the local manifestation of a global one.

The relationship also runs in the other direction, and this reverse influence is less obvious but equally real. An ending, once conceived, exerts a backward pressure on the chapters that lead to it. The author who has decided where the novel is going writes the earlier chapters to earn the arrival, planting the dependencies the ending will need and shaping the accumulation the closing will gather. The repositioning of the closing meditation into the ending's place, documented across the draft stages, is an instance of this backward pressure in action. Once the author conceived the ending as closing with the reflective turn, the earlier chapters had to be shaped to support that turn, to build the particular story up to the point where the universal reflection feels earned rather than imposed. The relocation of material into the ending similarly demanded adjustments upstream, because material moved to the last page left gaps where it had been, and the narrative had to be rewoven around its absence. The ending and the novel revised each other, the chapters demanding a new close and the close demanding revised chapters, in a reciprocity that continued through every stage.

For the reader, the reciprocity has a practical consequence that concerns how the published novel should be read. The famous close is sometimes treated as detachable, a passage that can be lifted from the book and admired on its own, and it is detachable in the limited sense that its sentences are beautiful enough to survive extraction. But the composition history shows that the ending's beauty is not self-sufficient. It is the product of the ending's integration with the novel, an integration achieved through the mutual revision of the close and the chapters across every stage. The closing meditation earns its position because the book was revised to earn it. The last page balances the particular and the universal because the biography and middle chapters were revised until the balance held. To admire the ending in isolation is to admire the result while ignoring the process that made the result possible, and the process is the subject of this article. ## Reading the Composition Record Without Inventing It

The composition history of the final page is only as reliable as the discipline with which its evidence is read, and the discipline can be stated as a set of practices. The first practice is to work from the documented stages and to say plainly when a claim moves beyond them. The record supports stage-level claims: the ending was provisional in the early drafts, reshaped in the Trimalchio interval, and adjusted in the proofs; the closing meditation was repositioned through revision; material was relocated into the closing. The record does not support invented sentences, imagined authorial intentions, or precise claims about lost intermediate states. A reader who respects the boundary writes sentences like "the textual record shows the ending was reshaped across stages" and refuses sentences that the record cannot cash. The boundary is not a limitation on the argument. It is what makes the argument trustworthy.

The second practice is to use the instruments the scholarship provides. James L. W. West III's 2019 Cambridge variorum edition of the novel is the proper tool for anyone who wants to see how the published text relates to the earlier states, because a variorum gathers the variants in a form that lets the reader compare without guessing. The 2000 Cambridge edition of Trimalchio is the proper tool for anyone who wants to read the intermediate stage directly, because it preserves the typescript as a text rather than a rumor. Neither instrument replaces the need for judgment. Both replace the need for invention, which is their value. A student who works from these editions can describe the ending's composition with confidence, because every claim can be tied to a documented state. A student who works from memory of the published novel alone cannot, and the difference between the two is the difference between scholarship and impression.

The third practice is to separate the questions the record can answer from the questions it cannot. The record can answer what changed between stages, because the stages are documented. It can answer the direction of change, because the stages are ordered. It can answer what was abandoned and what was accumulated, because each stage can be read against its predecessor. The record cannot answer why the author made a particular choice in the privacy of his study, because intentions leave no textual trace. It cannot answer what the lost intermediate states contained, because they are lost. It cannot answer whether an abandoned alternative would have been better, because the judgment is counterfactual. An honest composition history keeps these two lists distinct and builds its argument only from the first. The temptation to answer the second list is the source of nearly every invented claim in popular accounts of famous endings, and resisting it is the mark of the serious student.

A model reading plan puts the practices together in a sequence a reader can follow. Begin with the published ending, read slowly, noting where the emphasis falls and what the closing meditation seems to be doing. Then read the Trimalchio ending in the 2000 Cambridge edition, noting the same things, and write down the differences at the stage level: position, proportion, emphasis. Then consult the variorum for the relationships among the states, noting what the edition documents about the intervals. Then, and only then, form a claim about the composition history, and test the claim against the discipline: does it stay within what the stages show, does it attribute accurately, does it refuse what the record does not support. The plan is deliberately slow, because the errors it prevents are the errors of hurry, the invented sentence and the overconfident attribution. The final page rewards the slow reader twice: once in the beauty of the close, and once in the integrity of the account.

A worked example shows the practices in action. Take the single feature of the closing meditation's position and trace it across the three stages, writing one sentence per stage. For the early draft stage, the sentence reads: the meditation is not yet fixed in the ending's position, and the close belongs to an earlier architecture. For the Trimalchio stage, the sentence reads: the meditation is present but repositioned relative to the published close, caught in transit between conceptions. For the proof stage, the sentence reads: the meditation holds its published position while the surrounding close receives final adjustment. Three sentences, each tied to a documented state, each stating only what the stage supports. Now read the three sentences in order and ask what changed between each pair. The answer is the composition history of that feature: unsettled to in-transit to arrived, with the direction, the abandonment of the earlier positions, and the accumulation of the final one all visible in the sequence. The exercise uses no invented material and produces a genuine finding, which is why it is the model for everything the field does.

A final practice concerns the use of this article's own claims. Everything asserted here about the stages, the relocation, and the repositioning is stated at the level the documented record supports, and a reader who repeats these claims should repeat them at that level, with the same attributions. "The textual record documents the closing meditation's placement as the result of revision" is a claim the record supports. "Fitzgerald moved the passage on a particular date for a particular reason" is not, and the upgrade from the first sentence to the second is exactly the kind of invention this article refuses. The discipline is portable. Apply it to any claim about any draft of any novel, and the result will be composition history rather than composition fiction. ## A Note on What Assembly Does Not Mean

The claim that the ending was assembled invites a misunderstanding that should be cleared before the questions begin. Assembly, in this article's usage, does not mean patchwork, and it does not mean that the famous close is a committee product or a stitched-together compromise. Patchwork is what happens when incompatible pieces are joined without integration, and the result shows the seams. The published ending shows no seams, because each relocation was followed by integration and each repositioning by rebalancing, as the stages document. The assembly this article describes is the work of a single author exercising judgment across stages, not the compromise of many hands. The distinction matters because the wrong sense of assembly would diminish the ending, turning a triumph of revision into an accident of accumulation.

Assembly also does not mean that the author's initial conceptions were worthless. The early drafts supplied the material that the later stages relocated and repositioned. The Trimalchio typescript supplied the intermediate state without which the final reshaping would have had nothing to reshape. Each stage contributed something the later stages needed, and the author's first thoughts were the raw material of his last thoughts. To say the ending was assembled is therefore not to say that the early stages failed. It is to say that they succeeded in the way early stages succeed, by giving the author something to work with, and that the famous close is the sum of what every stage contributed. The monument metaphor from the verdict is exact: a monument is not diminished by having been built in courses, and the courses are not failures for not being the capstone.

Finally, assembly does not mean that the process could be repeated mechanically to produce another famous ending. The stages are documented, the operations are describable, and the discipline is teachable, but the judgment that guided the operations belonged to Fitzgerald. Another author, given the same stages and the same operations, would have assembled a different ending. The composition history explains how the famous close was made. It does not reduce the making to a formula, and it should not be read as one. What it offers is better than a formula: a true account of work, stated at the confidence the evidence supports, that replaces the romance of the single inspired draft with the reality of revision carried through every stage to the edge of publication.
