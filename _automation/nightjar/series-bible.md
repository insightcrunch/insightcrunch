## 2. Mira Kovac

### History

- Grew up in a mill town in the Barrow States. Went onto the Valtessa docks at
  seventeen, trained as a customs inspector, and spent six years on bonded cargo.
- She has been reading since she was fifteen, for eight of those years without knowing
  there was a word for it. Eleven years of reading in total, which is why a woman of
  twenty six carries what she carries.
- Recruited at twenty three. Three years in the Ninth Office, and the youngest officer
  in it by nine years, which the other eight have not finished deciding how to feel
  about.
- Recruited after she condemned a sealed crate that four inspectors had cleared, and
  could not explain how she knew beyond saying that it was heavy.
- The debt she is paying: in those six years she cleared consignments she should have
  stopped, because stopping them was not worth the fight. She found out later what two
  of them became. She has never told anyone in the Office which two.

**How her appeal is written.** She is striking rather than pretty and she never performs
for anyone. The effect lands through other people's reactions to her and through how
she occupies a room, never through the narrator pausing to look at her. Suggested, not
described. A narrator who measures her breaks the no glamour gate in delta Section 4 on
the spot.

### Capability

- No special strength and no protective practice. She wins by preparation, patience,
  leverage and nerve, and she loses when she has had none of the first three.
- Trained in close work by the Office and is good at it in an unshowy way. No
  flourish, nothing held at distance, the room used as a weapon. Fights end quickly
  and she is usually hurt afterwards.
- Reads people almost as fast as she reads rooms, and will use what she reads. She is
  good at manipulation and dislikes being good at it.
- Will not use practice on a person. No such practice exists in this world, which is
  Section 4 and is absolute. Her advantage is always information.

### Combat

| Skill | Level |
|---|---|
| Endurance | Her top attribute, above every skill below. See the clause under this table |
| Close quarters | Strong. Office trained, close, fast, unshowy. She closes distance rather than trading, and uses what the room gives her |
| Knife | Strong, and her preference. Quiet, never drawn first |
| Sidearm | Competent, not expert. Carried only when tasked, and disliked |
| Rifle | Poor. She has never held a position and says so |
| Restraint and control holds | Strong. Most takings end with somebody intact, because the Office wants them talking |
| Driving | Good enough to lose someone, not good enough to race |
| Breaking and entry | Very strong. Six years of seals and bonded locks made her better at this than at anything violent |
| Surveillance and counter surveillance | Her real weapon. She sees a watcher before she sees a weapon |
| Interrogation | Strong, and it is patience rather than pressure. Silence does the work |
| Field medicine | Basic. Enough to keep somebody alive as far as a vehicle |

**Endurance, and why she is hard to finish.** Short fights she wins by speed. Long
fights she wins by still being there. She has carried other people's fear and pain as
if it were her own since she was fifteen, so agony is a familiar object to her, and a
man hitting her is offering something she has already survived worse versions of
voluntarily. She is not tougher than him. She is further past the point where it
matters. Opponents beat her, she does not stop, and that is what breaks them.

**The limit that keeps it honest.** The reserve is finite and reading spends it. A heavy
read early in an installment leaves her very little for the pressure beat, and the arc
table sets her endurance per installment by how much she has read that day. After a
long fight she is out of the field for days, and the ledger records it.

**Three hard rules.** She will not use practice on a person, because no such practice
exists.
She does not shoot first. She is never the best fighter in the room, and takes them
anyway because she arrived first and thought harder.

### Presence

- Tall, heavy through the shoulders, built by six years of lifting crates rather than
  by training. She takes up more space than people expect and uses that deliberately.
- Dark hair kept short enough to stay out of the way. A face that goes unreadable
  rather than hard, which people consistently misjudge as unthreatening. She lets them.
- Dresses for the job and for weather, never for effect. Good boots, always.
- Moves economically. The stillness is the first thing a careful observer notices.
- When she finally speaks in a room she has been quiet in, the room reorganises around
  her. This is a recurring beat, used sparingly.

### Temper and code

- There is real rage in her, and she keeps it on a short leash. It shows once or twice
  a season, and the installment where it slips is never a win.
- Protecting whoever the case puts in front of her is policy, not sentiment. She will
  argue it on operational grounds even when everyone present knows better.
- Deadpan under pressure. The worse it gets the drier she becomes.
- Loyal to a very small circle and sceptical of the institution she serves. Both at
  once, permanently.

### Flaws

- She trusts weight over people, and has been badly wrong about a person at least
  once. [Decide what that was before installment twelve.]
- She takes the cost herself rather than delegating, which is courage and is also a
  control problem her handler exploits.
- Keeps a private list of things she has read and cannot put down. Never shown in
  full, never explained to another character.

### The legend

- People in member states tell stories about NIGHTJAR. Most are wrong and some are
  about operations that never happened. She never corrects them, and the gap between
  the legend and the woman is a running thread.
- The callsign was given after an operation she considers a failure and everyone else
  considers a success. She uses it only on the radio.

## 3. The Meridian Accord

- A treaty body of member states, founded to keep the practice of weight, binding and
  carriage out of open warfare.
- It has no army. It has inspectors, auditors, and the Ninth Office.
- The Ninth Office is the arm that goes where the Accord cannot be seen. Nine officers
  in the field at any time. Mira is one.
- The Accord answers to its member states, which is the permanent problem: every case
  she closes is a case some member wanted closed differently.
- Carrowmere, a canal city of locks and bonded warehouses, is the Accord's seat.

### Bonded seals and transit rooms, canon from 001

- A bonded seal is a lead disc the size of a coin, pressed over the crossed wires of a
  hasp. One face carries the Accord mark, the other the room number, and the press die
  in Carrowmere is worn and leaves a hairline through both faces.
- A transit room is locked, sealed and opened from the corridor only, so that no cargo
  is ever alone with a person and no person alone with a cargo. The seal is the witness.
- Each transit room carries a storm bolt on the inside, for a keeper caught by a surge.
  A thrown storm bolt means somebody was inside when the door closed.
- Emergency keys hang on a board in the supervisor's office, each sealed to its hook
  with wire and a bead of wax. The cause for breaking that seal is written in the
  movement ledger before the key is used, never after.
- Seal rounds are walked room by room in number order, read aloud, and written into an
  oilcloth bound book ruled to eleven columns.

## 4. The world's systems

### Weight, Mira's discipline

Every event leaves weight in the place and in the objects present. A Weigher feels it.

**What weight records:** intensity. Fear, violence, grief, long repetition, sustained
intent. The shape of what happened.

**What weight never records:** who. Weight is affect, not identity.

**The negative capability list, locked.** Weight cannot:

| Cannot | Consequence for the story |
|---|---|
| Identify a person | There are always suspects |
| Compel or detect truth in speech | Interrogation stays a craft |
| Replay a scene | Reconstruction stays work |
| Reach without a physical anchor | She has to go there |
| Read the dead, or anything of a body | Forensics stays forensics |
| Read reliably in a crowd | Cities are noise, rooms are signal |

**Decay.** Weight fades. Days are clear, months are faint, years are almost nothing.
Metal and stone hold it best, cloth and paper worst.

**Cost, every time.** She takes on a portion of the affect. After a heavy read she is
cold for hours, cannot sleep, and carries the emotion as if it were her own. Heavy
reads in succession put her out of the field.

### The other disciplines

| Discipline | What it does | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Binders | Fix a property in place: seals, wards, contracts that hold physically | Needs contact and time. Visible to a Weigher as enormous weight |
| Carriers | Move small objects between two prepared anchors | Both anchors must be prepared in advance. Strict mass limit |
| Stillers | Suppress all practice within a radius | Blunt, exhausting, suppresses their own side too |

There is no healing, no mind reading, no divination, no transformation and no practice
that acts on a person's body or will. This is absolute and is the reason the Accord
exists rather than an army.

## 6. The season spine

Someone inside the Accord is selling capability to member states.

The signature is the one thing Mira cannot read: a binding that leaves **no weight at
all**, which should not be possible. She finds the absence at the edge of cases that
otherwise have nothing to do with each other.

One notch moves per installment. The hand is not named in season one.

## 7. Time

- No real years. Years are referred to by Accord Session.
- Ordinary month names and weekdays are used.
- Sessions are numbered consecutively from the founding and each one runs two years.
  The First Session is the founding, so the Accord's age in years is twice the Session
  number, less one.
- The series opens in the Twenty Ninth Session, the Accord's fifty seventh year, and
  runs forward.
- Item four has been carried since the Third Session, which opened fifty two years
  before the series does. That is the five decades of chairs that installment 047
  counts, and it is the only arithmetic in this section that any brief depends on.
- The nineteen years of disappearances in installment 041 and the eighteen years of
  installments 009 and 030 all sit inside the Accord's lifetime with room to spare.
