# Nightjar, arc table and deep briefs, season one

**Version: 2026-11-01.7.** Commit to `_automation/nightjar/arc-table.md` before the
first run.

Covers installments 001 to 050. Read the row and the brief for the installment being
written, plus Part 4, before drafting. Everything in a brief is binding. Nothing in a
brief overrides the series bible.

There is no locked date table. The publish date is the run date. The arc table locks
the slug, the title, the case type, the outcome and the spine notch, and those five
are never chosen by the run.

## Part 1. How to use this file

1. Find the next installment number in the story ledger.
2. Read that row in Part 3 and that brief in Part 5.
3. Read Part 4 once per run. It is the reason readers come back.
4. Write to the beat spine in delta Section 5. The brief gives you the content of
   every beat, so the structural thinking is already done.
5. Anything the brief does not name is yours, provided it does not contradict the
   bible. Anything the brief does name is not negotiable.
6. If a brief needs a world fact the bible does not carry, stop and report under task
   Section 7. Do not invent canon.

## Part 2. The shape of season one

Season one is fifty installments in five arcs of ten. Each installment is a complete
case. Each one moves the spine by exactly one notch.

| Arc | Installments | Title | What the reader learns across it |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 001 to 010 | The Clean Room | Absences exist. Mira is not imagining them, and the Office does not want a register of them |
| II | 011 to 020 | The Alloy | The absence is a technique, not a phenomenon. It has materials, a decay curve, and a cost to whoever applies it |
| III | 021 to 030 | The Requisition | A member state is buying, and the paperwork runs through the Accord's own requisition chain |
| IV | 031 to 040 | Teshkar | The weakest member is the proving ground. The method requires something taken from a person |
| V | 041 to 050 | The Charter | Weighers have been disappearing for years. The authorisation was lawful from the beginning |

The hand is not named in season one. At installment 050 the reader knows what is being
done, how, to whom, and under what authority, and does not know who signed first.

### The absence ladder

One rung per installment, listed in the spine notch field of every brief. The ladder
is the series. It is what the reader is actually following, and it moves at a constant
rate so that the reader is always learning and never finished.

Never skip a rung, never deliver two rungs in one installment, and never deliver a
rung that a later installment is scheduled to deliver. This last clause is the one most
easily broken by a good scene: installment 001 originally solved its case with the
Stiller comparison that rung five exists to deliver, and the brief was rewritten so
that Rask declines to name the method. If a collapse beat needs a later rung to work,
the collapse is wrong, not the ladder.

### The failure budget, placed

Nine of fifty are not full successes: 004, 009, 016, 022, 027, 035, 044, 048, 050.
Six are partial, three are objective lost, and the three are 009, 027 and 044. The
outcome column in Part 3 is authoritative. The run never chooses this.

## Part 4. The addiction engine

Read this every run. The case is what the installment is. This is why the reader comes
back tomorrow. Eight rules, all of them checkable.

**1. Pay in full, every time.** The case closes. No cliffhanger case, ever. The reader
has to learn that this series always pays, because the trust that builds is what makes
the withheld thread bearable rather than annoying. A serial that withholds everything
trains readers to leave.

**2. One rung, no more and no less.** The spine notch in the row is the entire
spine content of that installment. One new fact about the absence. A reader finishing
installment 027 can say exactly what they did not know at 026. That steady rate is the
compulsion, and a double rung spends tomorrow's reason to return.

**3. The carried question is a concrete object, not a mood.** It is a badge, a name, a
burn on a hand, a missing page, a signature. Never a feeling and never a rhetorical
question. Readers return for things, not for atmosphere.

**4. The cost ledger grows and is never reset.** Every installment adds one named cost
to Mira. Readers keep score whether or not they notice they are doing it, and a
character who visibly accumulates damage is a character people check on.

**5. Somebody to fear for.** Mira absorbs everything, which is why harm to her reads
as routine by installment 030. Reni Vosk, and the recurring minor characters the
briefs seed, are where the real fear lives. Seed a minor character at least four
installments before anything happens to them.

**6. The minor character returns.** Every arc seeds two people who come back later in
the season, named in the briefs. A world where nobody returns is a world with no
memory, and readers do not invest in one.

**7. Last line is an image.** The final beat ends on something seen, held or heard.
Never a question to the reader, never a line of portent, never a narrator comment. The
last image of each installment is specified in its brief.

**8. The legend grows wrong.** Across the season, the stories member states tell about
NIGHTJAR drift further from what the reader has actually watched happen. The reader
holds the true version. That private knowledge is the strongest bond a serial can
build with the person reading it.

This rule has a cadence, a field and a gate, because a rule with none of those is a
wish. A legend beat appears in every sixth installment, 006, 012, 018, 024, 030, 036,
042 and 048, is specified in that brief's legend beat field, is recorded on the
ledger's legend beat line, and is checked by item 18 of the publish task self check.
Every legend beat must sit further from the truth than the one before it. Installments
without the field record none.

## Part 5. The deep briefs

### 001, Nightjar: The Clean Room in the Carrowmere Warehouse

- **slug:** `nightjar-clean-room-001`
- **arc:** The Clean Room, case 1 of 10
- **case type:** Death under impossible circumstances. Closes with the reveal
- **outcome:** full success
- **premise:** A bonded warehouse supervisor is found dead in a locked transit room in
  Carrowmere, and the room holds no weight at all, which Mira has never once
  encountered in eleven years of reading.
- **dramatic question:** Who killed Edran Voss, and why does the room say nothing?
- **cold open:** The night shift at the Carrowmere bonded wharf. A junior clerk,
  Sabel Orry, walks the transit corridor doing a seal check, finds room nine locked
  from inside, and gets it open. Write the discovery through her, the cold off the
  canal, the smell of wet rope and oil. She is the installment's first eyes and she
  returns in 019.
- **tasking:** Leyden gives it to Mira at the Office before dawn and names the cost up
  front: three reads in nine days already, and she has not slept since the second.
- **three live possibilities:** a smuggling partner inside the bonded chain; a Binder
  hired to make the room secure and paid to leave it unsecure; the dead man's own
  arrangement gone wrong.
- **false lead:** The absence of weight reads as a Stiller having worked the room, and
  Teshkar has the only Stiller on the wharf roster that week.
- **the collapse:** The Teshkar Stiller was signed off the wharf eleven hours before the
  door was last opened, and the gate book proves it. The suspect is wrong. Rask says
  only that whatever did this is not his trade, and will not say more.
- **pressure:** The decision the reader watches: Mira chooses to re-enter the room and
  read the corridor outside it rather than take the sleep Leyden ordered. The set
  piece is what she walks into when the corridor read puts her in the path of a man
  who has come back for something left behind. Unbroken, entry to resolution.
- **the silence:** The reader has the seal log, Rask refusing to name the method, and
  the fact that the corridor is heavy where the room is empty. Four hundred words
  before she speaks.
- **close:** The reveal. The killer is the bonded chain's own assessor, and the room
  was emptied to hide not the death but a seal that should not exist. He is taken.
- **weight, two demonstrations:** the corridor read, which gives her the direction of
  flight and costs her the cold and the shaking; the read of the assessor's own
  gloves, which costs her a night of someone else's fear.
- **limit that bites:** weight never records who. She has the shape of the act and
  cannot put a face on it, which is the whole case.
- **cost paid:** She carries Voss's last ten minutes for the rest of the installment
  and the reader is told she still has them at the end.
- **damage carried:** In: nothing. Adds: Voss's last ten minutes, still with her at the close.
- **endurance:** entering reduced, three reads in nine days behind her. Low at the pressure beat.
- **establish early:** the gate book; the seal log; the assessor's presence at the first
  interview; the gloves.
- **spine notch:** Rung one. A room where something certainly happened holds no weight
  at all.
- **characters:** Mira, Leyden, Rask, Sabel Orry (new), the assessor Henrik Dall
  (new, returns in 026).
- **findable artifact:** The transit room seal log for the night, as a table of nine
  rooms, seal state, and assessor initials.
- **Case File seeds:** what bonded transit is and why the Accord cares; the Ninth
  Office's standing in a member port; how a Weigher's field read is recorded; the
  Carrowmere wharf system; what a Stiller's suppression leaves behind.
- **cross-links:** none, this is installment one.
- **carried question:** The room. Not the killer, who is caught, but the fact that the
  room was empty and nobody in the Office will say that is strange.
- **last image:** Mira standing in room nine after it has been released, hands flat on
  the wall, feeling nothing, and the wall feeling like a wall.
